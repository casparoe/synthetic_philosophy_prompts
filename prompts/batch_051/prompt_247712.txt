Okay so I've been going back and forth on the fall of Satan, and it's one of those places where the tidy theory and the stubborn intuition just won't line up. On the theory side: the privation account plus free will seems to have every virtue I usually want in a view. God creates only good things, because evil isn't a positive thing at all but a lack or turning-away from being; angels (like us) are created free, because love has to be free to be love; and Satan's fall is just the first misuse of that freedom, a rational creature turning from a greater good to a lesser one. That story doesn't posit a rival evil principle, doesn't make God the author of sin, and gives one unified account of every sin, angelic and human. I genuinely find it compelling.

But here's the intuition I can't shake. A sin needs a motive, and a motive needs something that makes the lesser good look better than it is. For humans that's easy: ignorance, passion, habit, bodily appetite, pressure, a long history of bad choices. The angels supposedly had none of that before the first sin—no body, no passions, no prior history, no tempter (Satan was the first one), and on most tellings an intellect so clear it makes our best reasoning look like guessing. So how does a will like that actually go wrong? Saying "it was free" tells me it could have gone wrong, not that it would or why it did. And the stock answer that the first sin was pride or inordinate self-love feels suspiciously like it names the corruption we're trying to explain: how do you get inordinate self-love in a will that's already perfectly ordered? I keep landing on the thought that either there was already some defect in the will before the choice (and we're off on a regress), or "free will" is being asked to do too much—it gives the possibility of sin, but it doesn't give the motivation, and I don't think it can.

I'm not asking who's right. I want to know what it would actually cost to give up the intuition, what it would cost to amend or abandon the privation-and-free-will story, and whether there's a reading of freedom, or of angelic cognition, on which both survive. Give me the strongest version of each move, and tell me which one you'd make, and what would justify it—not just "mysterium."
