I am a novelist, and I am drafting a book whose protagonist is systematically mistaken about his own motives: he tells himself he acts from loyalty when he is plainly protecting his reputation. Working on this character has pulled me into the philosophical literature, where I keep running into the idea that self-deception is paradoxical or even impossible, because the self-deceiver would need to believe a proposition and its negation at the same time. If that is right, then my character is not self-deceived; he is merely ignorant, or weak-willed, and the portrait I want to write collapses. Before I proceed with the manuscript, I want to know whether the phenomenon can survive the paradox. Please give me your committed view rather than a survey: is there a coherent account of self-deception that does not require simultaneously believing contradictory things, and can it be rendered concretely in scenes and interior monologue? Take a definite position and give the reasoning for it.
