I've been turning over a distinction that keeps showing up when I work with models, and I can't tell whether it has an established name or whether I'm conjoining two or three separate ideas. I mostly want the carving; a name would just help me look things up.

Here's the setup. When I evaluate a model's output, two properties keep coming apart. One is whether the answer is correct, which I can only establish by checking against ground truth I compute myself. The other is whether the output carries its own verification with it — a reader who hasn't done the computation can follow it step by step and, ideally, take away a reason that transfers to the next case. These seem like they should track each other, but they seperate pretty sharply.

Example one, math. A model returns a proof of a theorem that consists of an asserted lemma, a sentence saying the remaining algebra is routine, and the conclusion. It's correct; I checked by grinding through the algebra myself. But reading it teaches me nothing I could reuse. Another model gives a proof organized around one intuitive idea that makes the theorem feel obvious — and gets a corner case wrong. When I score these with a reward model that prefers outputs it can follow, average accuracy goes down. And it's not just the overhead of writing longer answers: the outputs get pulled toward the standard textbook moves, because a valid nonstandard route scores as wrong even when it's right, so the model stops taking those routes.

Example two, fuzzier. I fine-tune a small model to explain its reasoning. The explanations get fluent and confident quickly. Soon it gives a beautiful step-by-step rationale for a wrong answer with complete confidence, and the more I train on explanation quality the worse this seems to get: it learns to generate reasons that justify whatever answer it already has, not reasons that generated the answer.

So two questions. (1) Is there a name for the property of a single output being self-certifying — checkable cheaply by someone who hasn't done the work, maybe even explanatory — as opposed to merely correct? (2) Is there a name for the failure mode where optimizing for that property makes the answers less correct, because the scorer only rewards reasoning that already looks like reasoning to it, so the model drops valid weird routes? I realize interpretability and explainability are nearby, but those are mostly about the model or system, not about one output. Faithfulness of explanations is also near but not the same: a faithful explanation can still be unreadable, and a readable faithful explanation can still cause the drift toward textbook answers. Cheap checking in CS, proof-checking and certificates, seems to cover the verification half but says nothing about whether the output teaches anyone.

What am I looking at? If it has a name, tell me which of the two questions it names; if it doesn't, coin one and define it with boundary cases. And dont just tell me it's explainability unless you can explain why the accuracy-dropping failure mode is the same phenomenon.
