I've been arguing with a friend about what Bell's theorem actually rules out, and I've noticed that the word "locality" keeps changing its referent mid-argument. I want a working definition of "locality" that handles a fixed list of cases without equivocating. I've absorbed the standard rationalist-adjacent treatments of quantum foundations, so I'm comfortable with terms like screening off, common causes, no-signaling, and hidden variables; what I want from you is the concept pinned down, not a survey of positions.

Here are the cases:

1. One billiard ball striking another, with momentum transferred by a continuous chain of contact.
2. Classical electromagnetism, where forces are mediated by a field that propagates at finite speed.
3. Newtonian gravity understood as an inverse-square force acting instantly across empty space.
4. Alice and Bob generate a long shared random string together and then separate; later each is given an input, and their outputs are correlated, but after separation no influence passes between them.
5. A pair of entangled particles measured in a configuration that violates a Bell inequality: the correlations exceed anything a local hidden-variable model can produce, yet no signal can be sent faster than light.
6. Bohmian mechanics, on the standard reading: a deterministic hidden-variable theory in which particle trajectories are guided by a wave function, and a measurement interaction on one side can immediately affect the guidance of a distant particle, while still not allowing signaling.
7. A hypothetical pair of no-signaling boxes: each box takes one input bit and returns one output bit, each output looks individually random, and jointly they satisfy a XOR b = x AND y, a pattern that yields a stronger Bell-inequality violation than any quantum strategy.

I suspect "locality" is doing at least three jobs: (i) mediation by contact or a continuous field; (ii) absence of superluminal causation or signaling; and (iii) Bell-factorizability, the requirement that distant outcomes be independent once their common causes in the past are conditioned on. Please propose either a single definition that draws the line sharply, or a small explicit set of senses that tracks these jobs. Then run each case through the definition(s), flagging cases where a definition gives the wrong verdict, no verdict, or a verdict you find uncomfortable, and revise the definition if necessary. Finally, tell me which of the seven classifications is least secure, and whether you think my friend and I are having a substantive disagreement or just letting one word do several different jobs.
