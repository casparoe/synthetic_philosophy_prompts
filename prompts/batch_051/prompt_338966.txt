I'm giving a department workshop next month defending the claim that democratic backsliding is a real, non-partisan category and not just a slur people stick on governments they dislike. My thesis is that backsliding is a process, not a state: an incumbent executive uses constitutional amendments, court-packing, media capture, emergency powers, and changes to electoral rules to entrench itself, while keeping the outward form of competitive elections intact — so the opposition can still formally contest power, but the institutions that could actually remove the incumbent get hollowed out. That's what separates it from a coup on one side and stable authoritarianism on the other. On that definition Hungary under Orbán is the clearest case; Turkey after the coup attempt and the move to an executive presidency is a case but from a very different baseline; Poland under PiS was a case and its later electoral reversal shows the process is reversible if the checks aren't fully destroyed; and the US in Trump's first term is a borderline case, lots of aggrandizing behavior and norm-breaking but the institutions partly held.

The audience is a political science department and its not friendly. There's a couple of Central Europe comparativists, a Turkey specialist who thinks the whole term is a liberal Western projection onto a country that was never a liberal democracy, an Americanist convinced "backsliding" is just what Democrats call Trump, and a methods person who will want to know where the thresholds come from and why the indicators aren't circular. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way they'd actually put it, each with a two-minute reply, a note on where my reply is softest, and a flag for which objections have real force and which are just noise. Tell me which objection I should preempt by conceding something up front, what the one reply is that has to land for the talk to survive, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
