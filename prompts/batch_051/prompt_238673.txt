I'm on the worship committee at a Presbyterian church, and we've been stuck for months on whether we can include a short film clip as part of the sermon. The confession we operate under says in the Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.6:

"Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inward illumination of the Spirit of God to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word: and that there are some circumstances concerning the worship of God, and government of the church, common to human actions and societies, which are to be ordered by the light of nature, and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are always to be observed."

The strict party says the regulative principle means unless Scripture actually prescribes an element of worship, it can't be included, and the "circumstances" clause only covers things like what time we meet or whether the room has chairs. The other side says a film clip is just a circumstance of presenting the sermon, a means of communication common to human societies, so it's left to prudence under the general rules of the Word. Both sides quote the same passage and accuse the other side of gutting it. My working theory is that the whole impasse is hiding inside the phrase "circumstances concerning the worship of God," and neither side has a principled procedure for separating circumstances from elements. Is that diagnosis right? And if it is, what kind of disagreement is this exactly: a semantic one about how to carve the phrase, a substantive theological one about authority, or an unacknowledged disagreement about what worship is for in the first place? What would actually count as progress?
