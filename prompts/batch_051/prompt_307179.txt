Here's one that keeps bugging me. Take Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity: the claim that what we call creative leaps are mostly produced when someone has a strong feel for the core of a theme and then varies it by turning a small number of conceptual knobs, where the real skill is knowing which turns keep the theme recognizable and which ones just break it. On this view creativity is neither divine inspiration nor random search plus selection; its curated variation.

Now here's the wrinkle. Generative image models and LLMs can churn out variations on a theme at scale, stay in the style of an artist or a concept, and people often rate the results as more creative than a lot of human attempts. But there's no felt sense of the theme anywhere in the system; it's turning knobs in a latent space with no understanding of what the theme is about, let alone which variation's matter.

So does Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model survive this development, does it need repairing, or is it just done? Be explicit about which premise the generative case puts pressure on, and whether the model can be salvaged by claiming that generating variations is the cheap part and recognizing which variation to commit to is still the creative act, or whether that's just moving the goalposts. If you think it survives, say what the notion of a theme is still doing. If you think it's dead, say what the better picture is.
