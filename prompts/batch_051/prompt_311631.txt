I teach a first-year history methods course at a small college — 22 students, one 80-minute lab block a week — and I want to build a lab around source criticism, but not the way I've done it before. Here's the hook. Last week two of my students almost got into a fight in the hallway outside my room over a vape someone left on a windowsill. I saw the tail end of it, and I genuinely still dont know what happened, but by that evening the class group chat was full of people who weren't even there explaining exactly what happened. It's now six days later; some of the students have definitely talked and contaminated each other's memories, there's a phone video but it's 11 seconds, starts after the shouting began, and has no audio, and at least three students now remember details from angles they couldn't possibly have seen. I want to run a lab where the class tries to reconstruct this event under rules that make them feel why a historian starts discounting accounts rather than averaging them. No lecture up front, and I want the words source criticism kept out of the room until the last ten minutes. Design the whole thing: setup and materials, who gets put in what role, the rules (for instance whether people with first-hand memories can cross-examine each other, and what happens when someone cites the group chat or the video), a timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps whatever actually happened back onto the ideas — time gap, chain of transmission, motive, independence versus collusion, the way a coherent vivid story beats a messy true one. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run it (someone will almost certainly pull out the video, or the two students involved will refuse to treat their own memory as just another source), and be honest about what the activity cannot teach — I suspect it's something to do with the really distant past, where there's no security tape to play at the end and nobody alive to cross-examine, and I'd like the debrief to climb from our six-day-old hallway mess to the Herodotus-versus-Thucydides contrast without me having to lecture about them. And what should I do when one of the sources is me.
