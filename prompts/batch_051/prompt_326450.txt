I keep getting into the same argument with a friend about intellectual property. His line is that intellectual property is just property, with the serial numbers filed off: a song or a piece of software is the product of someone's labour, and copying it without paying is morally the same kind of act as walking into a bakery and taking a loaf of bread, the only difference being that the thing taken is intangible. He sometimes says that if you copy my code you haven't stolen the car but you've stolen the value of the car. I want an audit of how far the analogy between intellectual property and ordinary tangible property actually goes. Lay out what maps onto what, where the mapping breaks, and which features of physical property are doing the real moral work in his examples — scarcity, rivalry, exclusion, the fact that if I take your loaf you no longer have it — and whether any of those carry over to a copied song or ebook. Then say whether his conclusion, that unauthorised copying is on a par with theft, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the analogy is bad for that purpose, what would a better analogy be?
