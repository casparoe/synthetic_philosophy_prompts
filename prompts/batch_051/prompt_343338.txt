I've been turning over Freud's old argument about religion in The Future of an Illusion. His line is that religious belief persists because it answers to a wish: the wish for a protective father, for the universe to be ordered and our suffering to mean something. The later cognitive science of religion throws a diffrent light on this. On that account, religion is less about comfort and more a byproduct of how our minds are built—we over-attribute agency, we remember minimally counterintuitive stuff, and plenty of the ideas that actually stick are frightening (angry gods, hell, watchful ancestors) rather than soothing. So does Freud's wish-fulfillment argument survive that development? I want to know which premise exactly it puts pressure on, and whether what is left is a repaired version of Freud that is weaker but still worth keeping, or just dead.
