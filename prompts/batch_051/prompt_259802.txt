I am a retired natural scientist (organic chemistry) who has come to philosophy late and has been reading the less formal writings of the Vienna Circle. I have just finished Otto Neurath’s 1936 pamphlet International Picture Language: The First Rules of Isotype. A passage in it has unsettled me.

As I understand the passage, Neurath argues that his picture language is not meant to produce expressive or aesthetically appealing images. It is a disciplined instrument for public education, and its symbols must be as standardized, repeatable, and free of decorative variation as scientific notation. He further claims that the chief enemy of a useful picture language is the individual designer’s desire to make a symbol interesting or evocative, because that reintroduces the very ambiguity the language was meant to replace. The task of the picture-language theorist, on this account, is to resist uncontrolled symbolic drift and to make visual communication as rule-governed as a well-formed chemical notation.

I would like you to test this view against a domain Neurath never saw: the growth of emoji from a small set of pictographs into an uncontrolled, culturally freighted, indefinitely extended visual vocabulary; the spread of platform icons and dashboards in scientific and public communication; and the recent use of large multimodal models to generate visual explanations without any standardized rulebook.

If Neurath were alive now, would he regard this visual environment as confirmation of his worries, partial vindication of his project, or something that requires a third description? Predict his position on the following, in each case grounding the prediction in the specific commitments of the 1936 pamphlet:

(a) whether emoji and algorithmically generated visuals should be governed by something like his Isotype rules;
(b) what kind of institution, if any, he would have recommended to govern them;
(c) whether he would count a visual vocabulary trained on mass usage rather than designed by experts as a picture language in his sense at all.

Finally, flag where the modern case exposes a tension inside Neurath’s own view rather than merely confirming or refuting it.
