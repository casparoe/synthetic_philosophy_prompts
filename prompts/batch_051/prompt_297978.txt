I teach an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar and I'm putting together the take-home final. I need one essay question on the meta-problem of consciousness, and I want it to be unanswerable just by reciting the hard/easy problem distinction or by summarizing the standard candidate solutions. Here's the thing I want the question to drive at: when the meta-problem is glossed as explaining why "we" think there's a hard problem, that "we" actually covers at least three different explananda — (i) folk verbal reports like "I can't see how experience could be physical," (ii) the private first-person intuition of an explanatory gap, and (iii) the fact that a whole philosophical culture treats consciousness as a special mystery. Explaining one of these can leave the others completely untouched, and a solution that only handles the verbal reports might just be debunking the reports rather than getting at the underlying puzzle. Please write the actual essay question (something students could answer in about 1,200 words), a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B by naming which of the three explananda a top answer must address and how. The marking scheme should also flag the typical B-or-below mistakes, like treating the verbal reports as the only data, or sliding straight from "we can explain why people say this stuff" to "the hard problem is dissolved."
