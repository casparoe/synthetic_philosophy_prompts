I'm a high school student working on a project about how scientists decide what animals are thinking, and I keep running into a conflict I can't resolve on my own. I've been reading about the principle usually called Morgan's Canon, and as a rule of method it strikes me as nearly compelling. The idea, as I understand it, is that when an animal does something that looks clever, we should not explain the behavior by a high-level mental capacity like reasoning, planning, or understanding if a lower-level capacity such as associative learning or trial and error would account for it just as well. The virtues of this seem obvious to me: it guards against anthropomorphism, keeps explanations disciplined and testable, and gives comparative psychology a parsimonious default stance. I find myself wanting to accept it as a general rule for any claim about animal minds.

At the same time, I have a firm intuition I cannot shake. When my dog nudges my hand after a bad day, or freezes and looks guilty after knocking over the trash, it feels as though there is something it is like to be him and that he is responding to my state rather than merely running a conditioned routine. I have the same reaction watching an octopus unscrew a jar, a raven solve a puzzle, or a cat seem to sulk after being ignored. I cannot easily believe that all of this is just anthropomorphic projection. Yet I can see the canon's force: my intuition is exactly the kind of evidence the rule teaches me to distrust, because I can never rule out a lower-level explanation by introspection alone. This leaves me unsure whether the fault is in the intuition or in the rule. I would like the strongest case on each side, not a compromise. Give me the best defense of treating Morgan's Canon as a firm methodological principle, and the best defense of taking such everyday intuitions about animal minds seriously as evidence. Then offer a negotiated verdict: how should I decide whether to debunk the intuition, amend or restrict the canon, or accept both under some interpretation? What would justify each move?
