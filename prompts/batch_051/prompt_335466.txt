Write a Petrarchan sonnet that renders Mary's room knowledge argument as a poem, with the volta at the exact moment the door opens. The octave should establish Mary's epistemic position precisely: she has spent her life in a black-and-white room, has never had a color experience, yet knows every physical fact about human color vision—wavelengths, retinal cones, opponent processing, cortical pathways, the complete physical story. The poem must be careful not to say she knows what it is like to see red, and not to say she simply knows everything; the force of the argument depends on that restraint. The volta, at line 9, should be her first encounter with a red object. The sestet should run the inference cleanly: she comes to know something she did not know before, and since she already knew every physical fact, the physical facts cannot be all the facts. It should avoid the weaker claim that she merely has a new experience and avoid suggesting she has merely acquired a new ability. The final two lines should state exactly what the argument says must be given up—the claim that the physical facts exhaust reality. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind would not wince, and good enough that a reader unfamiliar with the argument still watches the inference happen.

- form: the verse form and rhyme scheme you used
- volta_line: the line number where the turn begins
- octave_establishes: one sentence on how the octave fixes Mary's epistemic position without overstating it
- sestet_inference: one sentence spelling out the inference the sestet makes from the new experience
- final_two_lines_claim: the exact conclusion stated in the final two lines
- accuracy_caveats: one or two sentences on what the poem deliberately avoids asserting so it does not misrender the argument
