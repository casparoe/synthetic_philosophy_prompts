In Book II, Chapter XXI of An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Locke describes a man carried while asleep into a locked room where someone he longs to see and speak with is present; on waking, he stays willingly. Though he cannot leave, Locke treats his staying as voluntary because it matches his preference. Write a joke about the compatibilism/incompatibilism debate whose punchline turns on the difference between inability to do otherwise and acting in accordance with one's will. The humor should depend on the actual structure of the distinction, so it lands for someone who knows the philosophical terrain rather than being a generic nerd joke.
