A guy I keep arguing with about AI risk says the safety problem is overblown because "alignment is just making the AI do what you mean, not what you say." His example: you ask a model to shorten a paragraph and it cuts all the qualifications but keeps the confident claim, which is what you literally asked for and not what you meant. He calls this the narrow version of the problem — intent alignment — and says it sidesteps all the vague "human values" stuff. I feel like that slogan is hiding several distinct claims but I cant quite separate them. Could you lay out the distinct precise things "do what I mean, not what I say" could amount to — whose meaning (the user's, the designer's, the company's?), fixed at what point, inferred from what evidence, and what happens when the user is mistaken or changes their mind halfway through? For each reading, say whether it's trivial, substantive-and-defensible, or false. And tell me which readings stay inside a genuinely narrow intent alignment, and which quietly collapse into full value alignment (what you'd want if you were better informed, or what people in general should want).
