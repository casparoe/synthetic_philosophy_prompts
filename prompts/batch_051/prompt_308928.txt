I keep coming back to skeptical theism. The move, as I understand it, is this: if God exists, the gap between what I can survey and the full web of goods, evils, and connections God might be actualizing is so enormous that my failure to see any adequate reason for a particular horror (a child dying of cancer, say) is just not evidence that there is none. It's a rejection of the "noseeum" inference—from "I don't see one" to "there isn't one"—whenever the thing being scanned is God-sized. And I like how much work it does: one principled move that defuses the evidential problem of evil without needing a different just-so story for every plague and massacre.

But I have a conviction I don't want to give up lightly: that a surgeon who harvests one healthy patient to save five is just wrong, and that no hidden greater good could ever make the Holocaust even permissible, let alone justified. When I make those judgments, I'm doing the same thing the skeptical theist tells me not to do—I'm scanning for possible justifying reasons, finding none that could do the job, and inferring there are none. If that capacity is unreliable when the stakes are God-sized, why should I trust it when the stakes are human-sized? And if skeptical theism forces me to lower my confidence that the Holocaust was wrong, that feels like a catastrophic price to pay for saving theism from one argument.

I don't want a verdict. I want the structure of the tradeoff. What would have to be true for the intuition—that I really can know the Holocaust was wrong—to be the thing I debunk, and what would that debunking story even look like? What would have to be true for skeptical theism to be the thing that gets amended or dropped, and which amendment is the most promising? Is there a version that limits the skeptical move to a specific kind of moral knowledge (say, knowledge of God's global reasons) while leaving ordinary moral knowledge untouched, or is that just special pleading? And if both have to bend a little, what does the least-bad compramise actually say?
