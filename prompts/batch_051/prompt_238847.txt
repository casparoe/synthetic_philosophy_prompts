Hey, I recently became a mod on a large city subreddit (somewhere around 100k members) and for the first time I'm actually having to enforce a rule against racist content instead of just arguing about it online. The written rule bans racist content, and there's no controversy about removing slurs or explicit statements like claiming a group is biologically inferior. But that stuff is maybe five percent of what we deal with. The rest is the gray area: someone posts local crime statistics with no commentary, someone says a neighborhood went downhill and ties it to demographics, someone references 13/52, someone claims theyre just asking questions about race and IQ. Half the mod team calls these dog whistles and wants them removed as if they were slurs. The other half says we cant police intent — the literal content is race-neutral, crime stats are just facts, and if we ban people for what we imagine they meant, we're inventing the rule. And then the same people defending the stats post will ban someone for a slur said in a clearly ironic or edgy way. So our actual enforcement pattern isn't clearly intent-based and isn't clearly effect-based either.

I have to help rewrite this rule next month, and I keep flip-flopping. My question: what view of what makes a statement racist does an enforcement practice like ours actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can — how much weight it gives to literal content, speaker intent, audience uptake, shared background codes, plausible deniability, all of that. Then say whether that theory is coherent, which parts of our practice strictly require it and which parts are just compatible with it, whether there are rival theories that would license roughly the same bans for different reasons, and what would change in our rules or enforcement if we went fully one way (say, strictly by the effect on a reasonable listener) or fully the other (strictly by the speaker's intent, with evidence of intent required). I'm especially interested in the places where our practice is making a philosophical call without admitting it. Please answer in American English.
