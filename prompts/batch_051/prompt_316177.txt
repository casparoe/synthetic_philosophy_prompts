I keep hitting a snag in model-interpretability work. We train a small transformer on a task, then build an anomaly detector over its activations to flag when the model is doing something other than the expected mechanism. But I cant get a straight answer on what should count as "the same mechanism". Two training runs of the same architecture, after I permute-match the hidden units, produce circuits that look almost identical: same edges, same weight signs, and on the held-out set they agree with each other and with the full model on every input. But when I actually intervene — clamp a submodule to a constant, or force an intermediate activation to the value the other run would've produced — the two circuits diverge. One keeps matching the full model's behavior under a wide range of interventions; the other only matches on the data and breaks as soon as you poke it. So by a same-outputs-on-the-test-set standard they are the same mechanism, but by a same-response-to-intervention standard they are not.

Is there an established name for that specific gap — two mechanisms that are observationally or behaviorally equivalent on the data you have but not intervention-equivalent? I dont just want a label; I'm trying to figure out how to draw the boundary. What are the closest things people have studied in causal inference / interpretability / adjacent areas, how does each of them differ from exactly my case, and is there a defensible line like "same mechanism iff same behavior under every intervention you can actually perform"? Are there edge cases where two mechanisms pass every intervention test I can run but still shouldnt be called the same mechanism?
