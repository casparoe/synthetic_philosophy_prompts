As part of a seminar I am preparing on early modern political economy, I am drafting a short interpretive essay on Hume's account of money in his *Political Discourses*. The working thesis is that Hume's famous claim that money is not, properly speaking, one of the subjects of commerce but only the instrument which people have agreed upon, should be read less as a metaphysical demotion of money and more as a claim about explanatory priority: real activity, industry, and trade explain the value and effects of money, rather than the other way round. I reconstruct the argument as follows. In *Of Money*, Hume maintains that the absolute quantity of money in a country is in itself of little consequence to domestic industry, because prices adjust to the amount of coin in circulation; what matters is the proportion of money to commodities and the state of demand and supply. He also allows, however, that a gradual increase in money can temporarily stimulate industry, because the new coin is first spent in particular hands before prices have fully adjusted. In *Of the Balance of Trade*, he then develops his well-known price-specie-flow mechanism, according to which a favourable balance cannot be retained indefinitely: money flows in, prices rise, exports become dearer, and trade corrects itself.

The essay then poses a difficulty. If money is, in Hume's analogy, merely the lubricant that keeps the wheels of trade moving smoothly, why should a gradual increase in the lubricant produce real and apparently desirable effects on industry, and why should the adjustment interval be treated as merely transitional rather than as part of the normal operation of commerce? My suggestion is that Hume's answer depends on a distinction between the level of prices and the process of price adjustment, but I am not sure the distinction can bear all the weight he places on it. I would welcome comments on whether this reconstruction is faithful to Hume's argument, and on whether the difficulty I raise is best understood as an internal tension in Hume, as a problem about the need for a theory of frictions and slow diffusion, or as a problem about the relation between short-run stimulus and long-run neutrality.
