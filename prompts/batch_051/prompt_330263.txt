Write an essay on whether there can be genuine moral gains from trade between different value systems. I don't mean compromise in the sense of someone changing their mind, or agreeing on a watered-down midpoint. I mean the logrolling kind: two people (or two sides of one person) each accept something they regard as a real moral cost, in exchange for the other side accepting something the first regards as a morally bigger gain, while both keep there underlying values intact. The question is whether that kind of trade can be justified from inside each value system, rather than being just a strategic deal or a capitulation to whoever has more leverage.

Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides. On one side: the integrity objection (if you act against a moral judgment just this once, you're no longer treating it as a value), the incommensurability worry (values don't live on a common scale, so "bigger gain" talk is confused), and the corruption worry (a habit of logrolling turns values into bargaining chips). On the other: ordinary moral life already involves trading off rival values within a single person, refusing compromise can be its own kind of moral narcissism or indifference to what happens to actual people, and some compromises might let both sides live out what they care most about over the long run in a way that purity doesn't.

I want you to reach a verdict and defend it, not end on "it's complicated." A verdict like "compromise is justified when it meets conditions X, Y and Z, but the integrity objection rules out cases A and B" is fine. Also distinguish single compromises from a policy of being a compromising person, and say something about whether compromise leaves a moral remainder and whether that counts against it. And here's the part I care most about: tell me what would change your mind. Which argument, counterexample, or step in your reasoning is the one where you're least confident, and what would push you toward the opposite verdict. Be honest about the load-bearing weakness rather than performing confidence.
