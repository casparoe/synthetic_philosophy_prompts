I teach a seminar on Hofstadter, and a few months back I got into an argument with a former student of mine who now runs a trust-and-safety team at a mid-sized social platform. Her view was that content moderation is just applying policy to text. Mine was that most of the action is in where you locate the meaning: a message can go wrong at the level of the frame (the recipient never treats the thing as a message at all), the outer message (the cues about how to decode it — register, genre, in-group convention), or the inner message (the content you get once those cues are in place). She finally said: fine, give my analysts a procedure for sorting an ambiguous flag into one of those layers before they even touch the policy. So do that for her.

The team triages roughly two hundred flags per analyst per day, spends about fifteen minutes per item, and the analysts are smart but have no philosophy background and work from a training manual. The stakes are asymmetric: wrongful deletions generate outsized user anger, but missed dog-whistles and coded harassment poison the community. Analysts can escalate at most ten percent of cases to a senior policy council, so the procedure has to say when escalation is mandatory rather than just convenient.

The procedure should be a checklist or ordered set of questions they can run that (a) locates the actual decoding failure or disagreement before the policy gets applied, (b) marks the steps where a human judgment is irreducible, and (c) flags the cases where the frame/outer/inner distinction itself is doing more harm than good — for instance when an utterance operates on several layers at once, or when the meaning is partly constituted by the platform’s own policies (what counts as a dog-whistle only becomes determinate after you decide what its for). Then run it on two or three invented cases, including one where it tells the team to keep up a post they would rather remove, or vice versa, and say plainly what the procedure can’t do.

And since I’ll have to justify this thing to her boss: tell me where your reasoning is most likely to fail, and what specific consideration would make you tell me to scrap the whole design rather than ship it — dont just defend the proposal.
