Here is a working set of criteria for cognitive penetration of perception. A perceptual experience is cognitively penetrated when:

1. The alteration originates in a cognitive state with conceptual content — a belief, expectation, desire, fear, or item of knowledge — rather than in the stimulus, the sensory apparatus, or the conditions of viewing.
2. What is altered is genuinely perceptual: the phenomenal character of the experience (what it is like for the subject) or relatively early perceptual processing, not merely the subject's post-perceptual judgement, reasoning, or report.
3. The influence is semantically coherent: the content of the cognitive state and the content of the perceptual change line up with one another. For example, believing that a woodwind passage is played on a clarinet rather than a soprano saxophone makes the timbre sound more reedy, rather than making it look brighter.
4. The influence is direct: it is not fully explained by the subject's overt action, by a shift of attention, by a shift in response criterion, or by ordinary perceptual learning and adaptation.

Now consider this case. A master Go player is shown an unfamiliar board position. Before any explicit analysis, the black stones in one corner simply look weak, overextended, and on the point of collapse, in the way a chord can sound tense or a face can look familiar. A novice shown the same board sees only black and white stones arranged in a pattern. The master reports that the impression is immediate, that it survives being told the position was randomly generated by a computer rather than played by a human, and that it affects what it is like for him to look at the board.

Does the master's experience qualify as cognitively penetrated under the four criteria? Work through the criteria one by one, and where the verdict is negative, say whether the obstacle lies in the criteria, in the case, or in the boundary between them. Then say whether this case shows that the standard exclusion of perceptual learning from cognitive penetration is doing real work, or whether that exclusion is a stipulation the case reveals to be unstable.
