I'm a novelist, and I run a small quarterly fiction prize from my study: five volunteer judges, a dozen stories shortlisted, ranked ballots. I keep telling the other judges we should trust the majority vote because of the Condorcet jury theorem. But I know that theorem only does its work under conditions our jury probably violates: the judges aren't independent (we pass around the same two reviews, we all share a taste for one kind of sentence), and "each judge is better than random" isn't something we've ever actually checked. I want a reusable procedure, not a lecture, for our next meeting. In order, what should I check about independence and competence before I'm allowed to treat the vote as a real signal? What counts as a good-enough answer to each check to keep going, where does the procedure stop and say "this vote tells us nothing," and where does it hand of to a person because it's a judgment call? Keep it runnable in under an hour by people with no stats training, flag every step that's secretly our values wearing a checklist costume, and tell me straight up what the procedure cannot do. Then run it on three cases: (1) five judges who all read the same two reviews and have known each other's taste for years; (2) one judge with obvious expertise and four first-time readers; (3) a unanimous vote I'd love to treat as settled, where you should show me that the honest procedure doesn't let me.
