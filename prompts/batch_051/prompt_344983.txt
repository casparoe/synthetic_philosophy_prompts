Longtime LW/ACX reader here, and I've got a policy about my own reasoning that I'm starting to suspect might be a bug wearing an epistemics costume. For a few years my rule has been: run one level up by default. Before I let myself update on a piece of evidence, I first ask what prior I'm actually coming from, what my reasoning would look like if I were rationalizing, and what would change my mind. If I can't answer those three, I don't update, I put the evidence to the side. I do this for the big stuff but also for small stuff, like whether a comment on a forum actually moved me.

Lately I've started going meta on the meta. When I finish the one-level-up check, I check whether the check itself was fair or whether I was just performing it for my own benefit. Sometimes I'm three or four levels up before I notice, and the original object-level question has disappeared. At that point it feels less like monitoring my reasoning and more like rumination with a steelman. But when I try to just form a belief without the check, I feel exposed, like I skipped a safety inspection.

So: is "always run one level up before updating" a defensible policy, or is it anxiety and self-image dressed up as good epistemics? When does thinking about thinking actually improve calibration, and when does it just add latency and make me freeze? Is there a principled stopping rule for the regress, a depth where the marginal value goes negative, and could I tell from the inside weather I'm at a useful depth or just spinning? If the rule is broken, what's the closest version that keeps the benefits without the recursive spiral?

Give me the prose answer first, but after it, include a JSON object with these exact fields:
- "verdict": string, one of "defensible", "indefensible", or "partly defensible", plus a one-sentence gloss.
- "helps_when": array of conditions under which the one-level-up check improves accuracy or calibration.
- "hurts_when": array of conditions under which it makes decisions worse, not just slower.
- "failure_modes": array of specific ways the policy predictably degrades into rumination or self-image maintenance.
- "stopping_rule": a concrete, checkable rule for when to stop ascending the meta levels.
- "revised_policy": a one-paragraph restatement of the policy that keeps the intended benefits but drops the spiral.
- "inside_test": what I could observe about my own behavior to tell whether I'm currently monitoring for real rather than performing.
