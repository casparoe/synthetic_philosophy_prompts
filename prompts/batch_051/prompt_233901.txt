I'm putting together a handout for a philosophy of technology class on whether the usual tools-are-safer-than-agents line holds up. By a tool AI I mean a system that just answers the query in front of it and carries nothing forward: no standing goals, no memory of earlier queries, no acting between queries. By an agent AI I mean a system with the same underlying model and training, but with persistent goals, a running record of what it has done, and the ability to plan and act without being prompted. Build me a case where the tool AI does more harm then an otherwise comparable agent AI would have. Constraints: realistic setting (a social-services office, a hospital, that kind of place), no superintelligence, no self-improvement, and the harm has to trace to the one-shot statelessness itself, not to the tool being dumber, buggier, or trained on worse data. It's for a class handout, so cheating means smuggling in any other capability or values difference, or making the harm come out of a human action that would play out the same with an agent. Give me exactly five numbered parts, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario, (2) what the tool AI does and why, (3) what the agent AI would do and why, (4) the structural feature doing the work, and what changes if the tool gets a small persistent memory but still can't act between queries, (5) the strongest objection from someone who says the case is really about human misuse rather than toolhood.
