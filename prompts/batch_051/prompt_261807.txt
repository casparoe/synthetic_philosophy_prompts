I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I keep tripping over a phrase in a debate about whether argumentation should become an actual competitive sport. The context: some people were designing an Elo-rated debate league, with judges, penalties for named fallacies, playoffs, the works, and claiming this would improve real-world reasoning, not just entertainment. When others pushed back—judges are biased, rhetoric wins, fast-talking gets rewarded more than soundness—one of the proponents just kept saying the format was fine because "may the best argument win." It wasn't offered as a hope; it was offered as if it settled the dispute. I'm pretty sure there's a motte-and-bailey hiding in that sentence, something like: (motte) some arguments really are better than others; (bailey) a competitive format with judges and rankings will reliably surface the better ones. But I want it laid out properly.
