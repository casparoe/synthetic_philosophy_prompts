I teach an upper-level undergrad methods seminar and we're about to do the 'history from below' week — E.P. Thompson, Ginzburg, the ususal — but I want them to run into the contrast before they read the theory, so they've got something to attach the terms to. It's around 20 students and I've got one 55-minute session. Could you design a document-reconstruction activity where the same fictional English village in the mid-1700s gets reconstructed twice: once from a packet of state and gentry records (a tax list, an entry from quarter sessions, a steward's letter to his master, maybe an estate map legend) and once from a packet of low and popular records (a widow's petition to the vestry, a laborer's probate inventory with his debts, a bastardy deposition, a scrap of a tavern account book, a broadside ballad). I don't want to have to invent all the documents myself, so please write actual short invented documents, 4 or 5 per packet, realistic but clearly made up, each just a few lines. There should also be one shared 'incident folder' — say a poaching affray in the lord's park — that neither group sees until after they've each produced a rival history of the village's year. The rules should force every claim to be tagged to a specific document, and the payoff can just be whose version gets accepted into the final paragraph plus bragging rights, nothing monetary. Then I want a debrief that maps what the groups came up with onto the elite/state history vs history from below distinction, and maps the incident folder onto what microhistory actually does: using one small, weird, well-documented corner to expose structures that both overviews smooth over. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I suspect one of my students will have read ahead and start naming the theory, or one group will overhear the other's planning — and say what the activity cannot reproduce about real archival work no matter how well it goes. Give me timings, group sizes, the written prompt each group gets, and the debrief questions.
