I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about sweatshops, because a freind and I have been arguing about it for weeks and I keep losing track of my own view. I wrote down the things I find myself committed to when I'm being honest, and I'd like you to audit the list and tell me whether it hangs together. Here's roughly what I believe. (a) A transaction can be exploitative even if both sides freely agree and both end up better off, so mutual benefit doesn't by itself clear a deal of the charge. (b) Exploitation is about taking unfair advantage of someone's vulnerability, and unfairness isn't measured by whether the weaker party's actual alternatives were worse — it's measured by how far the terms fall from what a fair exchange would give them. So a sweatshop wage can be exploitative even if it beats everything else the worker could get. (c) At the same time, when a firm opens a factory in a poor country and pays above the local going rate, I think it hasn't harmed the workers — on balance it has made them better off, and I don't think the opening of the factory was itself wrong. (d) But I do want to say the firm acts wrongly in the cases people actually get angry about: once it's running, it uses the fact that the workers have nowhere better to go to keep their pay at subsistence while the plant makes big margins, and that feels like exploitation I shouldn't wave off. (e) That, in turn, is why I think most consumer boycotts miss the target: a buyer in a rich country isn't the one exploiting anybody, and refusing to buy can remove the one option that was making workers better off. (f) Still, I also believe, as a general matter, that if I knowingly and avoidably support a wrongful practice, at least some small piece of the wrongness falls on me. I can't tell which of these to revise, because each one sounds right on its own.

I'd also like you to flag any tensions that only look bad but dissolve once the right distinction is drawn, because I suspect I'm running a couple of things together.
