I keep going back and forth on Dennett's multiple drafts model. The negative part feels right: there is no place in the brain where everything comes together and gets presented to a little boss, and once you stop looking for that, a lot of puzzles about binding and the homunculus dissolve. But I can't shake the intuition that when I look at the second hand of a clock and it seems to freeze for a beat, or in the color-phi setup where a red spot and a green spot alternate and you see a moving dot change color midway, there's a fact about what I was conscious of and when. The theory says no: the content is constantly being edited and until some probe or question fixes a narrative there's just no answer to what I was 'actually' conscious of in the first two hundred milliseconds. That feels like giving up the subject matter rather than explaining it. I don't want you to just pick a side. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be debunked as an artifact of retrospection, what it would take for the model to be amended so it recovers a kind of first-person fact, and whether there's a reading on which 'what I was conscious of' is real but relational—fixed by the probe or the demand for a report—rather than absolute. Also say how confident you are in whatever resolution you give, and single out the point your least sure of.
