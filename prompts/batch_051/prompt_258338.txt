Consider a research institute that fears its stated mission and norms are slowly drifting under pressure from internal lobbying. It adopts the following rule: any member may try to change the institute's priorities, leadership, or culture, but every hour spent doing so must be publicly logged as influence work, and influence-work hours count for only half as much as ordinary work in decisions about seniority, pay, and promotion; line managers determine what counts as influence work from self-reports and project logs. Treat this purely as a mechanism and analyze how it would actually play out. What does it incentivize — about who seeks influence, how influence-seeking is disguised or delegated, and which kinds of institutional change become easier or harder? Where is it likely to be gamed first? What second-order effects follow, and can the most serious failure modes be fixed without gutting the scheme? In particular, say whether the institute's values would drift more slowly under this rule, or merely drift in ways that are harder to observe.
