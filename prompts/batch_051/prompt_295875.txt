I've been chewing on a cross-domain parallel and I can't tell whether it's load-bearing or just pattern-matching on the word 'model'.

On one side there's Friedman's F-twist: the claim that descriptively false assumptions in a model (agents are fully rational, representative, frictionless, whatever) don't count against the model as long as its observable predictions come out right. On the other side there's shortcut learning in ML: a classifier can learn to key off background texture rather than the animal itself, score beautifully on held-out data from the same distribution, yet its internal features are wrong about the generating process and it fails under a distribution shift. Distillation is an even purer case: a small model copies a big model's outputs without the big model's internal route to those outputs — same prediction surface, different machinery — and it collapses when the task shifts.

The common structure I think I'm seeing: in both, a model's predictive surface fits the world while its internals misrepresent the mechanism, and there's a live claim that the internals don't matter because only the surface matters. Read flatly, Friedman's F-twist seems to license shortcut-trained classifiers whenever they predict well in-distribution; and shortcut learning looks like a worked counterexample exactly where policy evaluation cares, because a policy intervention is a distribution shift (the Lucas-critique point, roughly). So I keep going back and forth between 'the F-twist is just the ML in-distribution/out-of-distribution mistake transplanted into economics' and 'you're equivocating: false assumptions aren't spurious features, and a macro model isn't a feature extractor.'

Is this a real identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it's real, what should transfer: e.g., should the ML failure mode make me discount a macro model's aggregate track record when the intervention changes the regime, and is there a principled size for that discount? If it isn't real, where exactly does the mapping come apart? And can you name something the parallel would predict if true that you'd expect to see but don't? I dont want 'it's an analogy'; I want the precise mismatch.
