I serve on a working group at a teaching hospital that is drafting a policy for when a radiologist may sign off on a finding that was generated, or largely generated, by a machine-learning system. The committee keeps getting stuck on the word "understanding." One faction holds that the radiologist must be able to reconstruct the pathophysiological reasoning as a radiologist would have before such systems existed; a second holds that it is enough to state the system's error rates on the relevant patient population and order the correct confirmatory study; a third holds that the understanding can be spread across the department, the vendor, and the validation report rather than located in any single clinician. The underlying worry is not the accuracy of the system but what it means for a licensed professional to be accountable for a decision she could not have generated on her own. Before we write the policy, I want the specification rather than the answer.

Please write the desiderata that a theory of understanding must meet if it is to serve as a rubric for cases of this kind, where a practitioner must decide whether reliance on an opaque computational aid preserves or erodes understanding. Include the cases such a theory has to handle: the radiologist who can give a textbook explanation only after reading the system's output but could not have produced the finding herself; the radiologist whose fluent explanation is false; the technician who can run the model without knowing what it does; and the department whose members each know only a fragment but jointly cover the necessary checks. Include the distinctions the theory must respect, such as the difference between a reliable process and an instance of understanding, the difference between a justification supplied after the fact and a competence that could have produced the answer in the first place, and the difference between understanding located in an individual and understanding distributed across a group. Include whatever results from cognitive science the theory must be consistent with, such as the finding that people routinely overestimate how well they understand a device or system until they are pressed to explain how it works. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable for our purpose and which are only one faction's preference.

Please also identify the single requirement that would do the most to rule out tempting but inadequate views, and flag any two desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. If you then want to check a few candidate positions against the list — say, the view that understanding is grasping the reasons why, the view that understanding is no more than calibrated deference, and the view that understanding can reside in a team — keep that part brief, because the criteria matter more to me than the scorecard. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a memo to a colleague.
