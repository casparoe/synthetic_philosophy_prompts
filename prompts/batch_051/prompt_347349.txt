I want your actual credence, not "it depends on your theory of legitimacy," that requiring constitutional amendments to clear a two-thirds vote in both houses plus three-quarters of the subunits (no sunset, no reauthorization) leaves a democracy better off over the next century than a simple-majority amendment rule would. Cash "better off" out as: the set of constitutional constraints the median citizen would reflexively endorse if they had the full counterfactual histories in front of them.

Decompose the number. I care about at least three sub-questions: (1) how much rides on whether a collective can really bind it's future self in the way an individual can, rather than this just being dead-hand control by an earlier generation; (2) how much on whether entrenched guarantees actually protect against majority overreaction rather than just ossifying whatever got locked in at some founding moment; and (3) how much on whether supermajority hurdles produce stability or just gridlock plus extraconstitutional workarounds. Give me a probability for each, one line of justification each, and show how you combine them. Then tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence or philosophical development would move it the most.
