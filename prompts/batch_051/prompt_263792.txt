I refuse to let anything it says change my beliefs about what my mother actually thought, wanted, or would have advised me to do. If it tells me, in her voice and idiom, that she would have been happy I left medicine, I treat the message the way I would treat a well-cast actor playing her in a film: moving, but no evidence at all. When I genuinely need to know what she would have wanted, I rely only on her letters, her diaries, our conversations, and what people who knew her tell me first-hand. My sister says this is a self-deception: if the bot is accurate enough that speaking with it gives me real comfort, then its outputs must be carrying some information I could learn from; if it is not accurate enough to learn from, the comfort is based on a fantasy; either way, my rule is unstable. She also points out that I let the bot shape my mood, my memories, and the way I talk about Mom even if I claim I don't update my beliefs. I think there might be a defensible distinction between using a simulation as an affective artifact and using it as a source of testimony about the dead, but I can't make it precise.
