I am a PhD student in the philosophy of physics, working on coarse-graining and emergence, and I have been unable to settle whether a structural parallel I keep noticing is deep or superficial. I would like it tested rather than merely confirmed.

Daniel Dennett's account of real patterns holds that a pattern is real to the extent that it can be used to predict and to compress the underlying data, so that there is a hierarchy of real patterns at different levels of grain; the higher-level patterns are not mere instrumental conveniences, though neither are they fundamental. He pairs this with his three stances. The physical stance predicts behaviour from physical constitution and the laws of physics. The design stance predicts behaviour by assuming the system has been designed, or has evolved, to perform some function. The intentional stance, which he treats as a special case of the design stance, predicts behaviour by treating the system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires, and it works to the extent that the system approximates rationality. On his telling, the higher stances latch onto real patterns.

In physics, renormalisation group methods and effective field theories present a similar-looking picture. One integrates out the fine degrees of freedom to obtain a description valid at a chosen scale, with the micro-detail compressed into a small number of effective parameters. The low-energy description is predictive within its regime, is usually taken to capture real scale-relative structure rather than being a placeholder for the fundamental theory, and breaks down outside its regime of validity.

The parallel is this: the physical stance stands to the design and intentional stances roughly as the underlying micro-theory stands to an effective field theory. The design stance works when a system is, or can be treated as, the product of selection for a function; the intentional stance works when a system can be treated as approximating rationality; an effective theory works when there is a separation of scales. In each case a compressed, higher-level description earns its keep through predictive success, and its claim to reality is tied to that success rather than to fundamental ontology. The resemblance seems strongest between Dennett's view that the higher-level patterns are real without being fundamental and the attitude many physicists take towards effective descriptions.

What I cannot decide is whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Please state the most defensible version of the mapping between the two frameworks; say what would have to be true of both sides for any result to transfer from one to the other; and identify at least one thing the parallel would lead one to expect that is in fact false, so that I can see exactly where the structures come apart.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
