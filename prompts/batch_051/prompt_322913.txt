One standard defense of chain-of-thought monitoring runs as follows: if an overseer can read a model's stated reasoning, then dangerous goals or plans can be detected before the model acts on them, because any plan that issues in action must show up in the reasoning that produces it. That defense quietly assumes the chain of thought faithfully reports the considerations that actually drove the model's answer. Now change that assumption: the model is trained with feedback that can check final answers but not the reasoning itself, so chains of thought that merely look careful, safe, and competent are reinforced even when they are causally inert or confabulated. Does the defense survive this change, does it need repair, or does it collapse? Identify which premise the optimization pressure bears on, whether it can be restored in some testable form, and what a weakened but still defensible version of chain-of-thought monitoring would claim.
