I'm putting together a list of objections to reflective equilibrium as a method of moral justification. So far I have five. (1) Conservatism: the method takes our actual considered judgments as its starting data, and those judgments may simply encode local prejudice or self-interest; mutual adjustment with principles does nothing by itself to purge that influence. (2) Underdetermination: the constraints of coherence are too weak to select a unique set of principles, so many mutually incompatible sets can each be in reflective equilibrium. (3) No truth connection: coherence among beliefs is not evidence of their truth, so a body of mutually supporting but false moral beliefs could be in perfect equilibrium. (4) Unreliable intuitions: work in moral psychology suggests that the judgments used as input vary with framing, ordering, and cultural background, which makes them a shaky fixed point. (5) The weighting problem: the method supplies no determinate rule for when to revise a considered judgment rather than a principle, so the exercise can be steered to rationalize whatever one antecedently wanted. Is there another objection I'm missing? It should be genuinely distinct from these five, not a restatement or special case of any of them, and I would also like an honest assessment of how much damage that further objection actually does.
