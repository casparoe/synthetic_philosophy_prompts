So the other night my brother and my neighbor got into it at a barbecue, and I can't stop thinking about it because both of them had a point, in there own way. My brother Sal just came back from a trip and mentioned he'd started jaywalking like a local because nobody waits for the signal if the street is clear, and my neighbor Dana said that's exactly what's wrong with tourists. That turned into a whole fight about whether everyday rules are actual moral rules. I tried to remember the back-and-forth as best I could:

Dana said that when you visit a place you're a guest, and guests follow the house rules. Squeezing past people in a queue, stiffing a server on the tip, crossing against the light right next to a crosswalk — it's all treating local customs as optional, and they aren't, because the place runs on everyone quietly doing their part. Sal said that's just manners wearing a moral costume. Some rules protect people, like driving on the left. Others are more like a game the locals happen to play, and tourists never signed up for the game. Cutting a busy line is wrong because you take a concrete spot from specific people, but jaywalking on a dead street at 2 a.m. is victimless, and a tip can't be owed when nobody promised anybody anything. Dana came back with the server's wage: in a lot of places the food is priced assuming tips, so by sitting down and eating you're accepting the deal and then reneging on your end. Sal said the owner made that deal, not him, and forking over extra money to keep a lousy wage system alive isn't a duty, it's just compliance. Dana said then don't eat at sit-down places; using a custom while refusing to pay your share is free riding. Sal said you can't free-ride on something you never opted into, and a tourist can't be automatically bound by rules he wasn't raised with and might not even know. Dana's last shot: so ignorance is a get-out? If I visit a country that drives on the left, I still have to drive on the left. The reason isn't "it's the custom," it's that other people are relying on me — and the server and the person in the queue are relying on those customs just the same.

I was nodding along with whoever was talking the whole time, which probably means I'd make a terrible tourist either way. Part of me thinks there's some single distinction that would sort the queue, the tip, and the crosswalk all at once, but I can't get it clear.
