Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether HCH and factored cognition can really be treated as an idealisation of deliberation. His view, roughly, was that deliberation is holistic: you can't take a hard question, split it into subquestions, have people (or copies of yourself) answer those subquestions without seeing the whole, and then reassemble the result, because what counts as a good answer to each subquestion depends on the overall picture you had already tacitly fixed. I found that persuasive, but then a parallel occured to me, and I want to check whether it is deep or just the philosopher's usual pattern-matching. The way I understand HCH now, it goes like this: a question is answered by a human who may decompose it into subquestions, pass each of those to another copy of the same process, and so on recursively, until each subquestion is small enough to answer directly; the final answer gets assembled from those leaves. That looks weirdly like reflective equilibrium flattened, maybe even in reverse. In Rawlsian reflective equilibrium you also refuse to answer the big question head-on: you collect considered judgements on more local cases, you float principles at several levels, and you adjust back and forth until the whole set coheres. Both pictures seem to share a core bet, namely that a global judgement can be replaced by a structured collection of local judgements that no single step has to survey. The obvious disanalogy is that reflective equilibrium is a network with coherence constraints between all levels, whereas HCH is a tree whose branches never compare themselves afterwards, and nothing in HCH gets to revise the original decomposition or even notice when two branches conflict. But is that a difference in how much revisability there is, or does it show the two are simply not doing the same thing at all? I'd like your view on whether the parallel is genuine enough that anything from the reflective-equilibrium literature transfers to HCH (background theory contaminating supposedly local judgements, coherence being too cheap, and so on), or whether it falls apart because considered judgements are revisable while HCH leaves are not.

Please don't format the reply: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just continuous prose, the way you'd write an email. And since the friend who argued with me keeps saying this is all armchair engineering, if the parallel does break, I'd rather hear the exact point where it breaks than a general conclusion that they are just different models.
