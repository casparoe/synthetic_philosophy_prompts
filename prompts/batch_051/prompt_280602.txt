Supererogation keeps coming up in my reading group because half of us think the category is confused and the other half think ditching it makes morality into a guilt machine. After one of those long effective-altruism-adjacent forum threads about whether not donating a kidney is a failure of duty or just missing out on saint points, I realized we were never really arguing about the examples; we were arguing about whether the deontic structure has room for a third category at all. So here's the ask.

Your honest credence in this proposition: at least one action in at least one ordinary choice situation is supererogatory in a strong sense — it is morally better than some permitted alternative, there is moral reason to do it, yet doing it is not required and omitting it is not wrong, and this pattern reflects the structure of moral requirement itself rather than just social practices of praise and blame.

My own number is around 0.35. I want your number too, but the number is the boring part; I want the decomposition. Break the question into sub-claims roughly like these (add or split if you think one is doing too much work):

- whether "you have most reason to do A" and "you ought to do A" always travel together, or can come apart (the usual reason supererogation gets called paradoxical);
- whether morality contains something like an agent-relative prerogative or a deontic ceiling that lets duty stop before the best available act;
- whether obligation is essentially tied to blame or accountability, and whether that tie gives a principled line between duty and going-beyond, or just a conventional one;
- whether the felt experience of going beyond what duty required is real evidence for a third deontic category, or can be explained away by praise-norms and motivated self-forgiveness.

For each sub-claim, give a rough probability. Then say how you combine them into your overall credence — and flag the worst independence violations rather than pretending they multiply cleanly. Then tell me the single sub-claim your overall number is most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would move you most on that sub-claim. At the end, if you had to diagnose why I'm at 0.35 while a typical commenter in those threads sits around 0.8, which sub-claim do you think carries the disagreement?

Please write the answer in American English.
