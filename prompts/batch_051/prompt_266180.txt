A debate in my school's philosophy club started me thinking about an inconsistency in the way people talk about ownership and loss in online games. Imagine a player who has spent months earning, or a substantial amount of real money buying, a rare item in an online game. If another player hacks into the account and transfers that item to their own inventory, nearly everyone—including the company that runs the game—treats this as theft: the hacker is banned, the item is restored, and the victim is described as having been robbed of something that genuinely belonged to them. But if the same company deletes that very item from the player's account, perhaps to rebalance the game or because it is shutting down a server, many of those same people now say that no one has lost anything, since the item was never real and the player never truly owned it. The very same item appears to count as a genuine possession when it is taken by another player and as a mere pattern of data when it is taken by the publisher.

I would like to know whether anything justifies this asymmetry, or whether it is simply an inconsistency people quietly tolerate. What is the strongest principle you can find that generates both judgments—that a fellow player's taking is theft while the publisher's deletion is not a loss at all? Test your proposed principle against nearby cases, such as a publisher deleting an item it had advertised as permanent, or a player accidentally deleting their own item, and then give an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept, revised, or abandoned.
