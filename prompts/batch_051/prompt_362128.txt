I have been turning over a structural parallel and I cannot decide whether it is deep or superficial. In Fearon's rationalist account, war is puzzling because fighting is costly, so there should normally be some negotiated settlement both sides would have preferred to war; the standard rationalist explanations for why war nevertheless occurs are private information combined with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibility. It seems to me that these three map rather cleanly onto competitive AI development, the so-called race toward advanced AI. Developers and states have private information about their actual capabilities and safety practices, and incentives to misrepresent both, for strategic advantage or deterrence; they face commitment problems, because a sincere promise to slow down, pause, or share safety findings may not remain credible once the strategic situation changes; and the prize may be effectively indivisible in a winner-take-all sense, since a small capability lead might compound into a decisive and durable advantage, making a negotiated division of how much development to allow hard to specify.
