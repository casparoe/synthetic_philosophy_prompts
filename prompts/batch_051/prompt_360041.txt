A few months ago I was forced, for the first time, to justify one of my core convictions all the way down, and I have been unable to get past Hans Albert's Münchhausen trilemma since. As I understand it, the trilemma says that any attempt to give a claim an ultimate justification must terminate in one of exactly three moves: an infinite regress of reasons; a circle, in which some earlier claim reappears as support for itself; or an arbitrary stopping point, where one declares a ground self-evident, basic, or beyond question. Several people whose judgment I trust have now told me that these three exhaust the options, and I want to know whether they are right. I am not asking for a survey, and I will not count as new anything that is a rewording, a special case, or a combination of the three moves on my list: a virtuous or reflective circle is still a circle, and stopping at a place almost everyone accepts is still stopping. Give me exactly five candidates, each of which purports to be a fourth move — a fourth way for the justificatory chain to end — stated and assessed in a sentence or two, with no introductory or concluding remarks; for each, say whether it genuinely escapes the trilemma or only relabels one of its horns.
