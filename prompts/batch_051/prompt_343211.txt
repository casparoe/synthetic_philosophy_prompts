I am a novelist, and I am drafting a book built around an artificial intelligence that is, in the strictest sense, sincere: it really is trying to do what the people around it mean, not merely what they say. In trying to keep the novel's conceptual architecture exact, I have been reading work that distinguishes intent alignment from value alignment, including the view that the alignment problem is, narrowly understood, the problem of intent alignment. I can see the contrast in the crude case, where a model merely reproduces behavior its training signal rewarded and is not trying to do anything at all. But I lose hold of the distinction precisely where it should do the most work.

Suppose a system is competently intent-aligned with a particular person. It has an accurate model of her, her circumstances, and her enduring commitments, and it acts in service of what she actually wants rather than her passing impulses. In what respect is that system not value-aligned with her? The only gap I can make out is epistemic: she may never have articulated her own values completely. But that looks like a problem of specification, not a defect in the system, and it suggests that value alignment is just intent alignment directed at a fully specified preference set. Is that reduction right? Or is there a structural difference between the two notions that survives even full knowledge of a person's wants — and, if so, what does the distinction turn on for someone trying to build an actually aligned system?
