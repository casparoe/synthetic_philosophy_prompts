I keep getting pulled toward the occasionalist reading of al-Ghazālī: there is exactly one real cause, God, and created things have no causal powers of their own. On this picture, fire doesn't burn cotton; God creates the burning on the occasion of the cotton touching the fire, and the regularity is just God's habit, which He could in principal suspend. The theory has a lot going for it — no brute 'necessary connection' between distinct events, no puzzle about how one finite thing transmits causal power to another, and it keeps divine sovereignty absolute. But I also can't shake the conviction that when I put my hand in a flame, the fire really is doing something to my skin, and that the brick really breaks the window itself, not just by courtesy of God. I don't want one side declared the winner by fiat. Give me the strongest version of both and tell me which should yield, and whether there's a reading of 'the fire burns me' on which both the theory and the intuition survive. Please write in British English.
