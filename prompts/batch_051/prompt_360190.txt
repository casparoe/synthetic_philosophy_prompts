I write about memory and tech, and I keep coming back to this. We usually talk about remembering as a duty and forgetting as a failure, but Nietzsche flipped that: he treated forgetting as an active power, something a healthy person or culture does on purpose, and he thought you can be too historical, so loaded down with old debts and grievances that you can't act. He died long before every stupid quote, arrest photo, and bad take became one search away forever. So here's my question: what would Nietzsche have made of the EU's right to be forgotten, where a person can ask a search engine to delist old but true material about them? Give me the strongest Nietzschean case for respecting that kind of forgetting as a legal right, then the strongest Nietzschean case against it, and then commit: which side do you think he actually lands on, and on what grounds? I'm asking about the shape of the argument more than about the man himself.
