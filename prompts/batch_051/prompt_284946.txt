A private foundation proposes to turn argumentation into a competitive spectator activity. In each league match, two players take six alternating turns defending opposite answers to the same question. After the match, a panel of three trained judges scores the transcript as follows: +1 for each distinct argument a player offered that the opponent never rebutted; +2 for each explicit concession in which a player accepted an opponent's argument and stated how their own view changed; −1 for each time a player repeated a point that had already been addressed; −2 for each misrepresentation of the opponent's position; and −1 for each turn the panel marks as mostly irrelevant. The player with the higher total is declared the match winner, but seasonal rankings and research grants go by players' average score, adjusted for opponent strength in an Elo-like way, rather than by win–loss record. Analyze this as a mechanism rather than a debating ideal: what behavior does it select for among players, judges, and the foundation; how would a strategic player maximize score without improving their reasoning; what second-order effects would it have on public argument; and which failure modes are fixable by changing a single rule without creating a new game? After your analysis, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with this schema: players, judges, and foundation (each an object with fields incentives and likely_gaming), failure_modes (an array of objects with fields rule_exploited, behavior, second_order_effect, and fixable_by_one_rule_change), and overall_verdict (one sentence on whether the league would improve reasoning or mainly reward gaming).
