ok so I've been trying to nail down my view on suffering and I want a consistency check before I try it out on other people. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a lexical threshold negative utilitarian. Roughly: there's a band of pain intensity—the kind people mean when they say a pain is crushing, not just unpleasant—and once a pain is above that band, no amount of mild pleasure can compensate for it. Add as many pleasant afternoons as you want to a world containing one above-band pain, and it's still worse than a world containing neither.

(2) Below the band, tradeoffs are ordinary. A mild headache can be worth it for enough small pleasures, and two mild headaches are exactly twice as bad as one. I don't think there's anything incommensurable on the mild side.

(3) I think valence is a single thing. Pleasure and pain live on one continuous scale, with pain just being the negative end. The difference between a -2 and a -5 and the difference between -5 and -8 are the same kind of difference; I don't believe a new kind of badness starts at the threshold.

(4) But I also think the threshold is vague, not a sharp knife edge. There's a penumbra between clearly below and clearly above, and for a pain inside it, it's indeterminate which side of the lexical divide it's on. I dont think that's a problem, just how thresholds work.

(5) And here's the test-case intuition I keep going back to: if a pain is just barely below the threshold, enough mild pleasure can justify it, but if the same pain were just barely above, no amount could. The two pains are almost indistinguishable, yet the verdict jumps from some finite number to none. I'm comfortableish with that, but I want to know if it commits me to anything weird.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how would you rank the tensions by severity? And if I had to revise just one, which should it be?
