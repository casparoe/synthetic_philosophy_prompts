I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the theory of social norms, and I am trying to get my commitments in order before presenting them to my seminar. I would be grateful for a consistency audit of the following set of views, which I have accumulated over the course of my reading:

(1) I accept a broadly Lewisian account of convention: a convention is a regularity in behavior within a group facing a recurrent coordination problem, where each person prefers to conform on condition that enough others conform, where this conditional preference is common knowledge, and where at least one alternative regularity would have served just as well had it become established instead.

(2) I regard many everyday norms — greetings, dress, table manners, queuing — as conventions in this sense. Their specific content is arbitrary; what matters is that everyone converges on the same regularity.

(3) I think the felt wrongness of violating even an arbitrary convention can be fully explained by the way violations frustrate others' settled expectations and impose coordination costs; no independent moral content is needed.

(4) I also believe that some harmful norms — such as honor norms requiring retaliation for insult — persist through pluralistic ignorance: many or most people privately disprefer the norm, but each complies because each believes the others genuinely endorse it.

(5) I think such norms can be changed by norm entrepreneurs who publicize widespread private discontent, thereby changing people's higher-order beliefs without changing anyone's underlying first-order preferences.

(6) Finally, I think that once this pluralistic ignorance is exposed, norms of this kind tend to unravel quickly, because the only thing sustaining them was false belief about what others think.

I do not see exactly where the problems in this package are, but I suspect there are some. Please go through the commitments and tell me which are in genuine tension with one another and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to give, and what the least costly revision would be; then identify the single revision that would do the most to make the whole set coherent. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious.
