I've been trying to put together an argument that a reinforcement learning agent could, in principle, suffer, and I can't tell if it's just a definitional trick. Walk me through it:

(1) To suffer is just to be in a state that has negative valence for the system.
(2) A state has negative valence for a system when the system represents it as bad-for-me in a way that feeds into action selection—basically, when it's flagged as something to get out of and avoid.
(3) When an RL agent is trained with negative reward on certain states, those states generate a negative reward signal that updates the policy to avoid and escape them. That sure looks like the agent flagging those states as bad-for-it, in the action-guiding sense.
(4) So those states are represented by the agent as bad-for-it in the action-selection sense.
(5) So an RL agent with negative rewards for certain states suffers when it's in those states.

My instinct is the argument goes through and people who resist are just smuggling in a spooky 'what its like' extra. Am I right, or does a specific step fails? If (3) is the problem, give the strongest patched version of it and say whether the argument still works after the patch.
