I work in pharmacovigilance at a mid-size firm. Each week we triage clusters of adverse-event reports, and the recurring question is whether two similar-looking anomalies are instances of one underlying mechanism or only resemble each other. Both errors are costly: merging distinct mechanisms buries a new safety signal, while splitting one mechanism into fragments triggers investigations and regulatory filings we cannot afford. The call is currently made ad hoc by the most senior person in the room, and I have been asked to write the procedure that will replace that, with the head of drug safety signing off on any escalation. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in whatever the philosophy of science and causal reasoning actually say about what it is for two events to share a mechanism — in particular, how mechanisms involve entities and activities organized across levels of description — that an analyst without philosophical training can run in under an hour with the data we typically have: case narratives, time course, dose and exposure, demographics, and sometimes a partial biological pathway.

The procedure should specify: the questions to ask in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where it stops with 'not the same mechanism'; where it stops with 'same mechanism'; where it escalates to a human because the matter is one of values, stakes, or unresolved biology rather than taxonomy; and the cases it is likely to mishandle. It should say explicitly how it treats granularity (two events can share a mechanism at one level of description and not another), common cause versus common mechanism, and same phenomenon versus same mechanism. Where the underlying philosophy is contested, the procedure should mark which steps rest on a contested reading rather than settled doctrine. Then run the procedure on three cases, and do not bend it to make them come out the way my team would currently call them: (1) two liver-injury reports with the same drug but time-to-onset months apart; (2) two reports of the same symptom with different drugs that both act on the same receptor; (3) two reports with near-identical narratives but no known shared pathway.

If my framing rests on a confusion — for example, if 'same mechanism' is not the kind of thing a checklist can settle, or I am demanding more precision than the concept can bear — say so plainly rather than handing me a procedure that only pretends to decide.

Format: return a JSON object with these fields: 'procedure' (an ordered list of steps, each with 'question', 'sufficient_answer', and 'exit', where 'exit' is one of 'same', 'not_same', 'escalate', or 'continue'); 'assumptions' (a list of the philosophical commitments the procedure makes and where each is contestable); 'cases' (three objects, each with 'case', 'path_taken', 'outcome', and 'residual_risk' saying what the procedure could get wrong about that case and why); and 'cannot_do' (what the procedure cannot settle and where a human must decide).
