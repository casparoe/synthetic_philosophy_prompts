I teach a seminar on classical Chinese ethical thought, and I have found that my students tend to flatten the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi into a pair of slogans, as if one said human nature is good and the other said human nature is bad and nothing else mattered. I would like you to write a dialogue for the class that gets into the mechanics of their disagreement. Anchor the exchange in two specific passages. The first is Mencius 2A6, where Mencius says that if a person suddenly saw a child on the verge of falling into a well, that person would spontaneously feel alarm and distress, not because they wanted the parents gratitude or disliked having a reputation for cruelty, and he takes this to show that human beings possess the beginnings, or sprouts, of morality in their own nature. The second is the opening of the Xunzi chapter usually translated as Human Nature Is Bad, where Xunzi distinguishes between what is so in us from birth and what is produced by conscious exertion; pleasure and the natural desires belong to the former, while ritual and rightness, on his account, are generated from deliberate activity and the guidance of tradition rather than from original nature. I want the dialogue to test whether these two arguments actually address the same question. Mencius seems to be pointing to an unlearned emotional response and treating it as morally significant, while Xunzi seems to be saying that an unlearned response only becomes moral once it is ordered by judgment and artifice. Each should be a competent and charitable exponent of his view: Xunzi should press Mencius on whether a sudden feeling of alarm is anything more than a reflex until it has been shaped, and Mencius should press Xunzi on whether the very process of shaping depends on a prior responsiveness that deliberate effort cannot create. They should respond to each others actual points rather than talking past each other, and the dialogue should reach no simple reconciliation. Please write the response as continuous prose, the way one would write an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
