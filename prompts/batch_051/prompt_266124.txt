Okay so here's a rule I actually run by, and I want to know if it's defensible or if I've just built a fancy shield against criticism. If someone calls me out for something they also do, I treat the whole criticism as having zero claim on me — not just that I take them less seriously, but that I don't update on the content at all. My brother, who hasn't called our mom in months, got on me at Thanksgiving about not visiting her enough, and I told him he had no standing to say that, then didn't give it a second thought, even though a part of me thinks he might be right that I should visit more. My girlfriend says this is textbook tu quoque: the criticism is true or false no matter who says it, and my brothers phone habits are just a way for me to dodge the substance. But it genuinely feels to me like some people have earned the right to blame me and some haven't, and that this matters independently of whether their criticism would help me if I listened. So: is my policy defensible? If it is, what exactly is the hypocrisy doing for me — is it evidence the criticism is false, evidence the critic doesn't really hold the principle, evidence they're the wrong messenger, or something about what I owe them as an audience? And is there a cleaned-up version of the rule that lets me brush off someone who never calls while lecturing me about not calling, without it becoming a blanket excuse to ignore anyone whose record is worse than mine? I honestly can't tell from the inside where the line should be, or when a hypocrite's criticism should still land.
