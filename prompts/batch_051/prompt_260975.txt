Was rereading Maxwell's Theory of Heat and got stuck on the demon passage. Paraphrasing from memory, Maxwell asks us to imagine a finite being with eyes sharp enough to follow every molecule in a gas, who tends a frictionless hole between two chambers and opens or shuts it so that fast molecules are let through only one way and slow ones only the other. After a while one side is hotter and the other colder, with no work done by the being—just the timing of the door. That looks like a macroscopic decrease of entropy for free, which would sink the second law unless something else is going on.

The modern resolution I keep hearing is an information-erasure argument, and I want to check whether I've actually got it. My reconstruction: (1) The demon must make measurements to know when to open the door. (2) Measurement can in principle be done reversibly, so no net entropy increase in gas plus demon is yet forced at that step. (3) To keep operating forever, the demon either needs infinite memory or must periodically erase records. (4) Infinite memory is unavailable to a finite demon in a cyclic process. (5) Erasing a bit at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2 of heat dumped to the environment. (6) That raises environmental entropy by at least kT ln 2 / T per bit. (7) So the entropy decrease in the gas is exactly paid for by the entropy increase from erasing the demon's records, and the second law survives.

I think the argument is basically right, but I've seen objections specifically to step (2) (people who say measurement itself is already dissipative) and to step (5) (people who say Landauer's limit assumes a particular erasure protocol and you could in principle uncompute or let bits leave the universe). I'd like you to tell me: is the step-by-step argument valid, and if not, exactly which step fails? And please separate what's actually settled from what's still contested among people who work on this, because popular treatments usually present the erasure story as completely closed and I suspect that's overstating it.
