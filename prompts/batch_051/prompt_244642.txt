I want a precise working definition of an effectively computable function — roughly, what people mean by an algorithm or an effective procedure — and I want that definition tested against a specific list of hard cases. My ordinary use of the term is confident, but I cannot state the criterion behind it, and I suspect the standard glosses pull apart under pressure. Here are the cases:

1. Euclid's algorithm for the greatest common divisor of two integers. I take this to be a clear case of effective computability.
2. Deciding, for an arbitrary program and its input, whether that program halts. I take this to be a clear non-case, at least on the standard understanding.
3. A brute-force procedure that checks 2^10,000 candidate solutions and is guaranteed to halt eventually, though not within the lifetime of the universe. I lean toward counting it, but I worry that the word effective smuggles in a feasibility requirement.
4. A quantum computer factoring a large integer. I think this should count, but I cannot articulate why it does not outrun the ordinary notion.
5. A procedure that consults a black-box oracle for the halting problem and thereby solves a task no ordinary Turing machine can solve. I genuinely do not know whether the oracle should be treated as part of the procedure.
6. A human mathematician who correctly resolves an open problem by an insight they cannot reconstruct as a finite sequence of checkable steps. This feels borderline, and it is where the philosophical pressure is highest for me.
7. An analog physical device — a soap film settling into a minimal-energy shape, say — that appears to solve an optimization problem all at once rather than step by step. Also borderline.

Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and tell me where it surprises you or which of your verdicts is least secure; if a case forces a revision, say so explicitly. Finally, use the definition to say what epistemic status the Church–Turing thesis has on your account: is it a stipulative definition, a mathematical theorem, an empirical hypothesis about physics and human minds, or something else?
