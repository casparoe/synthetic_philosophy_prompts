I'm the token philosophy professor (phil of mind) on my university's animal research review committee, and we're deadlocked on a proposal, so the chair asked me to draft the committee's decision memo, due next Tuesday. The proposal is from a postdoc in integrative biology who wants to study whether raccoons use causal reasoning in a novel tool puzzle; she frames it as testing for insight, not just trial-and-error learning. One committee member, a hard-nosed behavioral ecologist, wants us to reject it as unscientific anthropomorphism and send it back demanding purely behaviorist language. Another member thinks that's absurd and says the entire point of the study is to actually test cognitive capacities. I need a short memo (roughly 600–800 words) that gives a concrete recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and with a timeframe — and gives the researchers something they can respond to. My request: the memo should not treat Morgan's canon as an across-the-board ban on attributing minds to animals, and it should seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on animal cognition; please don't present a contested methodological stance as the consensus. Also, the losing side's worry should be taken seriously — the worry that mentalistic vocabulary smuggles in unobservables — rather than waved off.
