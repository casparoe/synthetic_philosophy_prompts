Last night I had a long and unresolved argument with a friend who does flood-risk modeling, and I'm trying to figure out what I should concede. She treats an ensemble of global climate models as, for practical purposes, a room full of expert forecasters: each model is one expert, each model's projection is that expert's considered judgment, the spread across the models is the honest range of disagreement among competent judges, and a heavy overlap among projections is what we mean by a robust finding. On the strength of that picture she concludes that when a city sets a levee height or plans around storm-surge return periods, the decision-relevant uncertainty in the projections just is the ensemble spread, and asking for more is like demanding that a panel of experts be more than a panel of experts. I objected that the models are not independent experts, since they inherit code, parameterizations, and historical datasets from one another; she replied that the members of any expert committee also trained in the same graduate programs, read the same literature, and lean on the same observational record, yet a committee's disagreement can still be informative. We never settled it.

Before we speak again, I want a careful audit of this analogy. Lay out the mapping item by item: what corresponds to the expert, the panel, the individual judgment, the points of agreement, and the dissent. Then sort the disanalogies into those that genuinely threaten her intended conclusion and those that are merely cosmetic. In particular, I want to know which features of the source picture the analogy quietly imports into the target — independence, broad representativeness of the relevant possibilities, competence, something like equal weight among voters — and how much work each import is doing. Finally, tell me whether her conclusion, that the ensemble spread simply is decision-relevant uncertainty and that model agreement is evidence of robustness, can survive once the disanalogies are made explicit; and if the committee picture fails, offer a better analogy, or explain why no familiar analogy does this work well.
