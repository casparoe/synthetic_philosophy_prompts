I've been going around in circles on Parfit's hitchhiker case and I want a precise diagnosis of where my argument goes wrong, if it does. The setup, for anyone who doesn't know it: a driver will only rescue a stranded hitchhiker if he's genuinely convinced the guy will pay him $1000 once they get to town. The hitchhiker is bad at faking sincerity, and the driver is good at spotting it, so he can't just lie. On his own, the hitchhiker knows he won't pay—once he's safe, there's nothing in it for him. Its not that he wants to cheat the driver; he just knows himself. But he has a pill that will temporarily make him the kind of person who keeps promises even at cost to himself. The plan is: take the pill, sincerely promise to pay, get the ride, and pay later because the pill has made him a promise-keeper. Assume throughout he cares only about his own self-interest.

Now the argument:
(1) By the hitchhiker's current lights, taking the pill has the best expected consequences: no pill, he likely dies; with the pill, he lives and is out just $1000. So taking the pill is rational.
(2) Measured against his self-interest once he's home and safe, paying the $1000 has worse expected consequences than not paying. So paying is irrational.
(3) Taking the pill is known in advance to cause that irrational paying, and it causes it precisely by making him less responsive to his own future interests.
(4) If an act is known in advance to make you less responsive to your own future interests, and it does this by causing at least one irrational act, then that act is itself irrational.
(5) Therefore taking the pill is irrational, which contradicts (1).

I keep wanting to reject (4), but I can't tell whether that's because (4) is false or because 'rational' means one thing in (1) and another thing in (4). Or maybe the contradiction is real and the idea that the pill helps is just confused. Say exactly which step fails, if any, and under what reading of 'rational' each of the premises is true. If the argument is valid as stated, say so and tell me which unexpected conclusion I have to swallow.
