so i've been mulling over something i've been calling "jurisdictional gravity," and i want someone to tell me if it's a real idea or just a network engineer pattern-matching without knowing the literature. the problem it's aimed at: a post goes up in one country, is read in thirty, and each of those thirty thinks its own speech laws (hate speech, defamation, blasphemy, whatever) should govern it. right now the default outcomes are either "most restrictive law wins" or "where are your servers," and both seem arbitrary to me.

here's the theory. for any online utterance, give each country with a connection to it a score: roughly (number of readers in that country) times (how much that country's legal system actually cares about that kind of speech) divided by (the burden on the speaker of complying with that country's law). i don't have exact coefficents; treat it as schematic. the country with the highest score gets to govern the utterance, and the others defer. so a hungarian-language post by a hungarian user that's 98% read in hungary is hungary's business even if austrian residents could technically load it. a platform deliberately pushing something to a french audience is france's business. i like it because it stops random countries from vetoing everything, and because it tracks where the speech's "traffic" actually goes rather than where the box is plugged in.

the snag i keep hitting is dissidents. chinese dissident outside china, writing in chinese, most readers in china: my formula makes china the jurisdiction, which is exactly the outcome that a global internet was supposed to escape. i could bolt on an exception like "no jurisdiction if the speech would be illegal in the speaker's own country" or a human-rights floor, but then it feels like i've admitted the formula is decorative and the real rule is just my priors.

so: does this already exist under a name i haven't heard of (i mostly know RFCs, not legal theory)? is the dissident case a bug in the formula, a feature i should own, or evidence that the whole approach is wrong? give me the strongest steelman of my own idea before you demolish it, and be candid about which parts are doing real work vs. which parts are just vibes. also tell me how confident you are in each of the main claims and single out the one thing you're least sure about.
