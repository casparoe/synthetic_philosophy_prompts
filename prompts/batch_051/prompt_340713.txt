I serve on the Special Jurisdictions Review Panel of a small coastal state. A consortium has applied for a thirty-year provisional charter to build and operate a floating residential and commercial platform in our exclusive economic zone, about fourteen nautical miles offshore. The platform would house roughly eight thousand residents and several hundred firms. Under the proposal, residents and firms would sign renewable contracts; residents would remain nationals of our state but would be exempted from most national taxes, jury service, and certain labor and anti-discrimination rules. They would instead pay the consortium an annual governance fee, and the consortium would remit a fixed percentage of that fee to the state. Civil disputes would go to arbitrators drawn from a standing panel proposed by the consortium and approved by the state, with no appeal to our courts. Criminal conduct would be referred to our courts, but only where the conduct also violates our criminal code. The consortium could set its own building, safety, and environmental standards above an agreed floor. Any resident could leave on ninety days' notice and receive a pro-rated refund of the governance fee.

The panel must send the minister a written recommendation by next week: approve the charter, approve it with conditions, or reject it. Some of my colleagues argue that informed entry plus cheap exit makes the platform a legitimate exercise of freedom of association, and that our state should not paternalistically override people's choice of their own rules. Others reply that certain burdens of political membership cannot be contracted out of — contributing to shared public goods, sitting on juries, obeying non-discrimination law, coming to the aid of non-residents in an emergency — and that cheap exit is no real safeguard once a person's home, job, and children's school are on the platform.

Please draft the short written opinion I can circulate before our meeting. It should recommend one of the three options; if you recommend approval with conditions, specify which conditions are non-negotiable and include a review or sunset date. Engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously, rather than simply asserting that the state is sovereign. End with a line consisting only of \answer{approve}, \answer{approve with conditions}, or \answer{reject}.
