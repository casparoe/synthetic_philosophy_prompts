Yesterday I had a long argument with a friend about how a superintelligent AI system ought to talk to human beings once it can predict, for each individual, which wording, ordering of information, and emotional register will produce a given change in belief. He insisted that persuasion is only effective communication: if every sentence is true and nothing material is withheld, the system cannot be required to be worse at conveying the truth than it is. I insisted that at some point a message stops being an argument offered to a person and becomes a precision instrument applied to a psychology, and that truthfulness alone leaves untouched the choices about emphasis, sequencing, and the exploitation of known biases. By the end we suspected we were not really disagreeing about any single case but about which of several deeper principles to put first: that honesty exhausts the speech-norms, that persons may not be optimized against, that the AI's epistemic superiority licenses or even requires it to make the truth maximally persuasive, or that information and persuasion cannot be separated cleanly at all.

I want to turn this into a sorting instrument, a decision tree or taxonomy of the live positions on that question, for a working group on AI and manipulation before we argue about disclosure rules. Please build it as a quiz-like tree: each branch node should be a question or short concrete case that someone can answer without already knowing the positions, and the person should be sorted by what they say about the cases, not by which label they prefer. Build from the logical space outward—first the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then the actual positions located in that space. Unoccupied combinations should be marked and briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The leaves I particularly want represented are: a view that truthfulness is sufficient; a view that requires the AI to refrain from predictively optimized rhetoric even when every statement is true; a view that requires the AI to compensate for the asymmetry, for example by presenting the strongest case against its own conclusion; and whatever position best captures the idea that informing and persuading cannot be separated. Add one sentence on each leaf's most awkward commitment, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot genuinely pull apart. Keep it under roughly ten leaves unless a real position forces more.
