I'm running a two-hour workshop for fifteen participants, mostly philosophy graduate students and ML-adjacent researchers, on iterated amplification and distillation: the proposal that a hard task can be handled by having a human overseer decompose it into subtasks, delegate those to other humans or AIs, and distill the resulting work into a model that does more of the task, with the cycle repeated many times. The takeaway I want participants to come away seeing is that this decomposition does not automatically preserve either competence or the original goal: losses and distortions can enter at every interface even when each local step looks reasonable. Design a runnable in-person game or activity for the session that makes the participants produce that drift themselves, before anyone names it. I need exact rules and materials; a role structure with overseers and workers; a scoring scheme that needs no real money; an arrangement predictably yielding both competence loss and goal drift without facilitator nudging; and a debrief that maps specific moments to amplification, distillation, decomposition, oversight, and the difference between losing information and losing the objective. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the worry the activity cannot reproduce, such as scale, recursion depth, or the learned component, that I will need to supply in discussion.
