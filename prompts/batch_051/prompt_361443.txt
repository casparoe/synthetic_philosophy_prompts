I've been a longtime reader of rationalist blogs, and I've built an argument that seems to prove too much; I can't find the exit and want a diagnosis, not a literature survey. Please act as a professional philosopher of mathematics and reason carefully. Here is the argument:

(1) The natural numbers are whatever realize the Dedekind–Peano axioms, unique up to isomorphism; arithmetic is only about structural relations such as successor, addition, multiplication, and order.
(2) If (1) is right, a number such as 2 has no nature beyond its position in that structure.
(3) If 2 has no nature beyond its position in the structure, then a claim about 2 has a determinate truth value only if every isomorphic realization of the structure agrees on it.
(4) The claim "2 is an element of 4" does not meet that test. On the von Neumann construction (0 = ∅ and n + 1 = n ∪ {n}), we get 4 = {0, 1, 2, 3}, so 2 ∈ 4 is true. On the Zermelo construction (0 = ∅ and n + 1 = {n}), we get 4 = {3}, so 2 ∈ 4 is false. The two constructions satisfy the same arithmetic axioms and are isomorphic.
(5) Therefore there is no determinate fact of the matter whether 2 is an element of 4.
(6) But "2 is an element of 4" looks like a perfectly well-formed mathematical sentence, and under the von Neumann ordinals, which are the standard coding in set theory, it is simply a theorem; the question should have an answer one way or the other.
(7) So either that standard set-theoretic theorem is not really about the natural numbers themselves, or the structuralist move in (1)–(3) is too strong.

I find myself wanting to keep both (1) and (6), and I suspect I cannot. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the argument contains a hidden equivocation, a scope or quantifier shift, or a modal slide, identify it precisely; if it is instead valid and the only real question is which premise to surrender, say so and price the main options. Please begin by brainstorming several candidate diagnoses, then give your final answer in American English.
