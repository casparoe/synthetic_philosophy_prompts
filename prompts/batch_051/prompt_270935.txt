Rationalist circles often treat Cox's theorem as having settled the standing of probability theory: from a handful of weak-looking desiderata about the consistency and comparability of plausible reasoning, the theorem appears to force degrees of belief to obey the ordinary probability axioms. Suppose a colleague insists this is not just a formal result but a substantive claim about reasoning under uncertainty: that any system which genuinely reasons about uncertain matters will, on pain of incoherence, satisfy those axioms. How would one test that claim? Design the strongest test you can — an experiment, simulation, or observation — specify which result would count for and against the claim, and then say precisely what a positive result would and would not establish about the normative force of Cox's theorem rather than merely about how people in fact revise their beliefs.
