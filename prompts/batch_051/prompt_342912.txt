I am a PhD student in astrobiology, and I have now spent enough time in the Fermi paradox literature to find its persistent standoff itself in need of explanation. The same datum — no detected technosignatures — is still treated by different camps as evidence for nearly opposite conclusions. One camp reads the silence as support for a Great Filter, most likely ahead of us; a second reads it as broadly unsurprising if technological life is rare, recently arrived, short-lived, or non-expansionist; a third holds that observer selection makes the silence close to uninformative, since we could only find ourselves in a history in which nothing preempted our own emergence. The camps do not dispute the underlying astronomy, yet they do not move. My working hypothesis is that the impasse is less about the empirical record than about how to set priors over unobserved quantities — abiogenesis rates, civilization lifetimes, expansion speeds — and about what anthropic reasoning permits one to conclude from a single absence of evidence. I want a diagnosis rather than a survey: where exactly do the positions part ways; is the disagreement substantive, partly verbal, or several questions under one heading; does some piece of it turn on a resolvable empirical or formal question; and what would genuinely count as progress, or should we expect none?
