I keep seeing arguments for and against referendums that pick one case — Brexit, California ballot propositions, the Swiss system — and let that case stand in for the whole institution. Before I read another of those, I want the full specification of what an adequate normative theory of referendums has to get right. The purpose is prescriptive: enough to appraise and design referendum institutions inside a representative democracy (what may be put to the vote and by whom, how the question is framed, what majority or turnout threshold applies, how binding the result is, and how the outcome should be interpreted), not a defense of a fully direct-democratic state.

Write the requirements list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. The list should cover at least: the cases a theory must classify correctly or explain (a one-off national plebiscite of the Brexit type; the Swiss system, in which some measures must go to a vote and others can be forced to a vote by signatures; the California initiative-and-proposition system with its low-turnout, heavily-financed campaigns); the distinctions it must not collapse (authorizing a decision versus making it; majority support in the whole electorate versus majority among those who turned out; the democratic legitimacy of a result versus its likely quality; agenda-setting power versus voting power; single-issue evaluation versus ballot packages that bundle unrelated provisions); and the empirical and formal results it may not contradict (rational ignorance among voters, status-quo bias in the default option, framing and order effects when there is more than one option, the possibility of cycles in multi-option votes, systematically unequal turnout). Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a camp preference (for instance, participation versus epistemic competence, simple majority versus supermajority, advisory versus binding). Then say which single requirement does the most to eliminate popular theories, and flag any pair or subset of requirements that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied as stated. Also name one demand people routinely make of referendums that you are leaving off the list, and say why it is a camp prejudice rather than a genuine desideratum. Keep any scorecard of existing theories to a sentence or two; the criteria are the deliverable. A table is fine.
