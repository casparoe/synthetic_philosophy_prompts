In a graduate seminar on deliberative democracy, a fellow participant defended a proposed citizens' assembly by saying that, if properly designed, it would let decisions be determined by the unforced force of the better argument. I take the phrase to be Habermasian, but I suspect it is doing more work than its users acknowledge. I would like you to lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing, distinguishing at least the following dimensions: what force means here (causal influence, normative bindingness, psychological motivation, or something else), what makes it unforced, whose perspective determines which argument is better, and what institutional conditions would have to obtain for the slogan to be true. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantively defensible, or false, and say which reading the citizens' assembly defence actually needs if it is to support the conclusion that such a body has democratic legitimacy. Please answer in British English.
