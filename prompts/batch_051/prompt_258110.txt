I teach critical thinking and informal logic at a former polytechnic, and I'm putting in a bid for a twelve-month teaching-research fellowship, partly because I'm fed up with the same thing happening every year: students pass the fallacy quiz and then, a fortnight later, argue exactly as they did before the module. I'm also a hobbyist martial artist, and it's occurred to me that we might have the wrong model of what rationality is. A martial artist doesn't get good by learning facts about footwork; she drills simplified, stylised movements—kata—until they're automatic, and then pressure-tests them in situations that look nothing like the forms. My half-baked idea is that rational competence (spotting argument structure, tracking base rates, noticing when a conclusion outruns its premises) might be trainable the same way, and that the real problem is not teaching but transfer: the kata simply wont fire outside the dojo.

The obvious positions don't settle this. The generic-skills crowd treat the abilities as if they transfer by themselves; the deliberate-practice tradition is mostly about domain-specific expertise, not general reasoning; and virtue epistemology talks about stable character without giving us a training regimen. I'd like a one-page proposal that states the actual question precisely, says why those existing positions leave it open, sets out what I'd actually do month by month (conceptual and design work, not a lab study), explains why this approach rather than just teaching more logic, and tells me what would count as the project failing. Keep it to twelve months. Don't bother with references; I'll slot them in myself.
