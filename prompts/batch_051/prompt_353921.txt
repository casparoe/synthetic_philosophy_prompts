For this one, write a pantoum of four or five quatrains about Sherlock Holmes that does the structural work of the problem of empty names and truth in fiction. The puzzle to enact is that a sentence such as the claim that Sherlock Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street seems exactly right when we report what is true in the stories, and exactly wrong when we report what is true of the actual world, even though the words themselves have not changed. A pantoum suits the point because its second and fourth lines return as the first and third lines of the following stanza, so the same repeated words can shift their truth-status as their surroundings change. Choose the repeating lines deliberately, some speaking from inside the stories—they say where Holmes lives, or what he does—and others speaking from outside and denying that any such man exists; then arrange each recurrence as a movement between two frames of evaluation, the frame of the fiction and the frame of the world. The poem should not merely announce that Holmes is fictional; it should make the repetition enact the mechanism philosophers mark by saying that some claims are true only according to the stories, so that a sentence can be true-in-the-fiction and fail to be true outside it without contradiction, because what changes is the context of evaluation rather than the words. Someone working in the philosophy of language should recognize that the poem is doing more than celebrating the detective, and a reader who has never thought about semantics should still find it a good poem rather than a versified lecture. Please give only the poem, with the line and stanza breaks the form calls for, and no title, epigraph, preface, commentary, or notes.
