I keep trying to explain luck egalitarianism to a freind and realising I can't even apply it consistently myself. The basic idea is that people should be compensated for bad brute luck, stuff they never chose, but not for bad option luck, the foreseeable downside of a gamble they freely took. Could you rank the following cases from most to least entitled to compensation under that principle, give each one a score out of ten, and flag the cases where you think the brute/option distinction breaks down or has to be gerrymandered to get the answer your gut wants? If one detail would flip your ranking, say which one.

- someone who loses their savings in a poker game they freely entered
- born into a poor family in a wealthy country
- lung cancer after smoking since age thirteen
- a farmer ruined by a multi-year drought, farming being the family trade
- born with a severe cognitive disability
- someone who put their retirement in a diversified index fund and lost a third of it in a crash
- an impulsive temperament that keeps getting them fired from every job
- a pedestrian hit by a drunk driver while walking home from work
- a rare side effect of a recommended vaccine that causes permanent disability
- a professional stunt performer who breaks their spine on a jump they knew was dangerous
