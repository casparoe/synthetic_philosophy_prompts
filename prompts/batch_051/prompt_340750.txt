For the past several years my research has drifted from philosophy of mind into the methodology of comparative cognition, and I have accumulated a set of commitments that I have never had to defend as a package. I would like you to audit them for joint coherence rather than assess them one by one.

My current commitments are these. (1) I take Morgan's canon to be sound interpretive advice: if a piece of behavior can be fully and fairly accounted for by a lower psychological process, we should not attribute a higher one. I treat this as a rule of interpretive parsimony, not as an empirical claim about the course of evolution. (2) I nevertheless think "associative learning," as it usually functions in these debates, is not a single well-defined lower process; it is a heterogeneous family of mechanisms, and it is often specified so loosely that a determined critic can construct an associative story to fit almost any behavioral dataset after the fact. (3) I accept the standard reading of the mirror mark test, on which an animal that uses the mirror to locate and investigate a mark it cannot see directly thereby exhibits a form of self-representation; accordingly, I take the reported passing performance in certain birds and fish as good-enough evidence that self-representation is not restricted to mammals, and I have cited those cases in print. (4) I hold that flexible, novel-environment problem solving—behavior that transfers across contexts and cannot plausibly be traced to a single trained contingency—is prima facie evidence for a cognitive rather than a purely associative explanation. (5) Finally, I hold that when a cognitive hypothesis and an associative hypothesis are both on the table, the choice between them should be made by comparing their predictive specificity and falsifiability, not by any standing preference for the lower or more parsimonious story, since in comparative cognition the nominally lower story often turns out not to be more parsimonious in any measurable sense.

Do these five commitments hang together? I suspect they are broadly consistent, but I have learned not to trust my own sense of where the strain is. Please give me an audit rather than a lecture: identify any tensions among them, order them by severity, say which commitment would have to give in each case, and what the least costly repair would be. I also want you to flag apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection, so that the audit does not end by declaring the whole package incoherent.
