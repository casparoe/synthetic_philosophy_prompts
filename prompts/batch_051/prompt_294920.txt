I'm a university ombudsperson, and a few times a week I get complaints that blur together two different things. Sometimes the person is saying a rule or standard was actually broken and they were wronged. Other times they're really saying they can't stand watching a colleague get more — a bigger salary, a better office, a named chair, a grant — without any claim that a rule was broken. The second kind often gets dressed up in the language of the first. I have about 40 minutes per complaint before I decide whether to send it to the equity office as a possible grievance, flag it as a structural or climate issue for the provost, or close it as an interpersonal matter. I'm not a philosopher, so I need this as a checklist, not a lecture.

I vaguely remember Rawls helps here. He treats envy as a vice — hostility at someone else's advantage even when no injustice has been done — while resentment is a moral feeling that points to a wrong. But he also has this wrinkle of excusable envy: when the background differences are so large that they undercut a person's self-respect, the envy is still a vice but the right response is to fix the conditions, not just blame the person. I want the procedure to take that wrinkle seriously rather than sorting everything into legitimate grievance vs. sour grapes.

Turn all that into a decision procedure I can reuse: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the complaint gets routed under this three-way split, and the steps where judgment can't be eliminated and a person has to decide. Then run it on three complaints. (a) A senior lab tech says a new hire with much less experience out-earns her by a lot; no pay policy was broken, but she says she feels humiliated every payday. (b) A faculty member says the chair gave their friend a course release with no stated policy, and that this sends a message about whose work matters. (c) A staff member complains that a colleague in another lab got a big grant and she didn't, and she's sure she deserves it just as much as anyone. At least one of these three should come out in a way I won't like, and you should say what the procedure can't settle.
