Goodman's grue problem starts from the thought that a bare induction machine has no reason to prefer green over grue (where grue means green if examined before a certain time t, and blue otherwise). The evidence that all emeralds examined before t have been green is exactly what the hypothesis that all emeralds are green and the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue both predict up to t. Now suppose the induction machine is not bare at all: it's a large language model, pre-trained on a few trillion words of ordinary human text, and it already assigns a vastly higher prior in favour of green than of grue before it sees so much as one emerald. Does the new riddle of induction actualy go away, or has it just been pushed back into the training corpus? If it goes away, say why human usage is the right prior and not merely a widely shared prejudice. If it doesn't, say whether a community of speakers who used grue as their basic colour term could pre-train an equally confident model on their own corpus, and what that tells us about induction. Point out exactly which premise of Goodman's setup the language model changes.
