I've been thinking hard about the digger wasp that gets stuck in its own routine: it drags a paralyzed cricket to the mouth of its burrow, leaves it at the threshold, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out to drag the cricket in, and if you nudge the cricket away while it is inside, it will reset and repeat the whole sequence — drag to the threshold, check inside, drag to the threshold, check inside — rather than simply pulling the cricket straight in. I want a candid assessment of a distinction I've been building on top of this example before I push it further.

I distinguish first-order flexibility from second-order flexibility. First-order flexibility is variability in what an agent does in response to its situation; the wasp has that much, since it does respond when the cricket is moved. Second-order flexibility is the ability to treat one's own rule or routine as an object and then revise or abandon it. The wasp appears to lack it, because the sequence can repeat without the wasp ever responding to the repetition itself as a problem.

My proposal is that free will should be located primarily at the second level. What makes the wasp unfree is not that its behavior is rigid — it is not rigid at the first level — but that the rule that selects its behavior is fixed. On this view, a free agent is one that can stand back from a rule it is following, ask whether to keep following it, and then actually change it. I know this resembles talk of second-order desires or reflection, but I mean something narrower: the target of the higher-level check has to be the rule itself, not just the particular action the rule produces. The person who notices they check their phone every few minutes and decides to stop following that pattern is exercising second-order flexibility, even if each individual phone check was, by itself, responsive to the situation.

Is this distinction already worked out under another name? Is second-order flexibility genuinely necessary or sufficient for free will, or does it just push the problem up a level — and who is checking the check? Above all, give me the strongest case where my criterion misclassifies something: an agent with deep second-order flexibility that should not count as free, or an agent that seems free while failing my test. Which objections are fatal and which are only uncomfortable?
