I've been reading Metzinger for a seminar on the self and I'm trying to write down where I've ended up, because I keep getting the feeling the set is slighty unstable but I cant locate where. Here's the profile:

(1) I'm a physicalist: no immaterial self, no soul, no Cartesian ego standing behind experience. The person is just the animal, described at the right level.

(2) I accept the self-model picture: the feeling of being a unified, persisting me anchored somewhere behind the eyes is the content of a phenomenal self-model that the brain builds and continually updates.

(3) I hold that this self-model is transparent in Metzinger's sense: in ordinary experience you cannot catch it in the act of constructing the self. You experience through it; all that shows up from the inside is the represented self, never the machinery — the way you see through a window without seeing the window. I know that's not a perfect analogy.

(4) Because of transparency, I don't think ordinary people are making a daft error when they report that they just know they're in there. The architecture doesn't give them access to the construction, so their report is locally reasonable, not a failure of rationality.

(5) But I also think that when transparency collapses — in long meditation, depersonalization, ego dissolution on psychedelics, certain out-of-body experiences — the person is revealing something about the machinery, not just glitching. I'd put it strongly: those states are closer to the truth about the self than everyday experience is.

(6) About the no-self view I hold both halves: there is no substantial unchanging subject standing behind experience, and yet the self does exist as the content of the self-model, which is real and does real causal work. I don't think that's a contradiction.

(7) I'm a compatibilist about moral responsibility. The self-model's role in planning, inhibition, and social cognition is enough to ground responsibility even though there's no contra-causal free will; I actually think the picture makes responsibility easier to defend, because we stop asking for a miracle.

(8) And yet I think that even after a transparency-collapse experience, you can't live from the insight. The system re-transparentizes itself and within days you're back to experiencing yourself as a plain given self. The glimpse leaves a residue but doesn't restructure ordinary experience.

What I want is an audit rather than a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs pull against each other, which only look like they do, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension say exactly what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is. And please end by telling me where your audit is shakiest: which of my formulations, read slightly differently, would remove a tension or introduce a new one, and what argument would be most likely to flip your ranking.
