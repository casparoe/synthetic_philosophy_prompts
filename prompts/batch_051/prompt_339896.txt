I've noticed a weird asymmetry in how people talk about relationships. If an old friend has become draining, or just stopped reaching out, most of us think it's sad but acceptable to let the friendship lapse—you don't owe someone your time just because you used to be close. But if it's a parent who's draining, cold, or was never really around, drifting away gets treated as a serious moral failing. Now consider the view that filial duties aren't debts of gratitude or repayment; they're basically the duties of friendship, so what adult children owe depends on the actual relationship rather than the bare fact of being parent and child. Is the asymmetry between the faded friend and the faded parent something that view can justify, or is it just an inconsistency we're unwilling to give up? Either way, give me the strongest defense of the position you'd keep.
