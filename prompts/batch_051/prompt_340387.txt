Give me a working definition of organism — or, if you think the term has become irretrievably ambiguous, of biological individual — that classifies the following ten cases correctly. For each case, apply your definition explicitly and state the verdict, rather than first intuiting the classification: (1) a single adult human being; (2) a granite boulder; (3) a human heart kept alive and beating on a perfusion machine outside any body; (4) a Portuguese man o' war, a floating siphonophore composed of physiologically integrated, genetically uniform zooids; (5) a eusocial ant colony with one or a few reproductive queens and many sterile, non-reproducing workers; (6) a human being plus the full set of microbes living in and on it, considered as one unit, as in the holobiont concept; (7) a lichen, in which a fungus and a photosynthetic partner form a structurally organized composite with a single name and body plan; (8) a contiguous clonal grove of quaking aspen connected by one shared root system; (9) a mitochondrion inside a eukaryotic cell; and (10) an influenza virion. After each verdict, note whether you think it matches what careful biological practice would say. Where it does not, revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. I lean toward treating the ant colony as only metaphorically an organism and the mitochondrion as no longer an organism, but I am open to being persuaded. Conclude by saying which of your ten verdicts is least secure and why.
