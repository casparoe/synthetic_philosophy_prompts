The standard picture from the Eichmann trial, as I understand it, is that what was terrifying wasnt a monster but a functionary who didnt really think — he pushed paper, reused cliches, followed memos, and the evil lay in a kind of thoughtlessness, a failure to imagine the world from the standpoint of the people whose files he handled. Here's my problem though. In my novel there is no Eichmann. There's a machine-learning model that scores applicants for fraud, and the only humans in the loop are engineers who tune the thing for accuracy, a caseworker glancing at a dashboard, and a manager signing off on an error-rate report. Nobody sees the whole chain; the harm comes out of a pipeline, not a desk. So does the banality of evil survive that? If the thoughtlessness is distributed across the algorithms and the humans, none of whom has the full picture, is this still Arendt's idea or do we need something else — structural evil, procedural evil, some kind of administrative evil without an administrator?
