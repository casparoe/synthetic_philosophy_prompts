Could you write a parody of a homeowners association charter — a community covenant for a planned subdivision that has sort of fused Confucian role ethics with a low-key traditionalist conservatism and markets the whole thing as harmonious living. Maybe 650 words, numbered clauses, in flat HOA legalese. The humor has to come from getting the Confucian side right rather than from the residents being cackling authoritarians: role obligations (ruler-minister, parent-child, elder-younger, husband-wife, friend-friend) written as occupancy and parking rules; filial piety as a maintenance and visiting requirement; ritual propriety doing the work that fines and police do in a normal suburb; the rectification of names turning into a rule that your mailbox and doorplate have to match your household role; and the ideal of harmony as something deeper than uniformity quietly working as a speech code. Don't spell any of that out in so many words; let the clauses show it. The satire should land the standard liberal-pluralist objections without ever stating them — dissent treated as disorder, obligations nobody chose, the family turned into an enforcement arm, no niche for the misfit, harmony as silencing — and it should stay fair enough that a Confucian reader would call it not wrong, just orderly. End with a penalty schedule, and slip in one quietly ominous definition of nuisance. No winking either.
