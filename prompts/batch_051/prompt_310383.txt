Exam question: Descartes argues that nonhuman animals are automata and lack minds. Explain the role of language in that argument. Does Descartes allow that animals have sensations or feel pain? Assess whether his position draws a coherent line between bodily mechanism and mental thought.

Student answer: Descartes's main argument is that language is the only certain public sign of thought. Parrots can reproduce words, but they do not recombine them to answer for all situations; that would require universal reason. Because no animal exhibits such creative language, we have no positive reason to attribute thought or reason to them. This is a difference of kind, not degree. Descartes does allow that animals see, hear, and feel pain, because sensation can be analyzed as a mechanical bodily process carried by animal spirits and not as a mode of thinking. So what he denies is rational thought, not sensation or consciousness. A dog that yelps when struck is undergoing a physical reaction, but there is no mind making the pain an object of awareness. Thus his position remains coherent: animals can have sensations and even consciousness in a non-rational sense, while lacking reason and the only kind of thought language reveals.
