I spent my career in the physical sciences and only recently started reading proper philosophy, and I've been stuck for weeks on causation by omission—the puzzle about whether my not watering the plant genuinely caused it to die, and if it did, why we don't also say that everyone else on earth caused it by not watering it either. What some philosophers call the proliferation problem. Now I see it everywhere, so I was arguing about it with my neice, who manages a small apartment co-op. She says the board's incident reviews always collapse into one of two bad places: either a dozen residents are on the hook because each of them could have noticed, or no one is on the hook because no positive act is identifiable, even when the pipes froze while a known thermostat was broken. Her proposed fix is a traceback rule: when the board reviews an incident, an omission counts as a contributing cause only if it violated a written rule or a motion in the minutes, or if the exact same omission already appears in a prior complaint or an entry in the incident log. An obvious but unwritten omission gets recorded as a mere background condition, not a cause; positive acts are always eligible.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a question about who deserves blame. Walk through what it would actually do to what gets written down, what gets proposed at meetings, and what people deliberately avoid knowing. Who does it systematically let off the hook, and who does it quietly burden? Where does it get gamed first, and can any of the failure modes be fixed without just re-importing the original proliferation problem in a new costume?
