I've been arguing on and off with an old labmate, a retired physicist, about what she actually means by "predictable" as opposed to "deterministic." I've kept a running list of her verdicts:

- a frictionless pendulum: deterministic, predictable
- a double pendulum: deterministic, not predictable
- a tossed coin, treated as a classical mechanical system: deterministic, not predictable
- tomorrow's weather: deterministic, predictable
- the weather two weeks out: deterministic, not predictable
- the exact decay time of a single radioactive atom: not deterministic, not predictable

Now she's given me a new case and won't tell me her verdict until I guess: a Geiger counter next to a weak, long-lived source, and the question is how many clicks it registers in the next hour. My first thought is she'll say the count is predictable, because the half-life gives you a tight expected rate. But then I worry she'll say it isn't deterministic (each click comes from an individual random decay), so by her lights it can't be predictable. What should I guess, what principle fits her record best, and what further case would cleanly seperate that principle from the runner-up? No citations needed, just the reasoning.
