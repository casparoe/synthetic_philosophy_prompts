Write the desiderata a theory of rational irrationality must meet, where rational irrationality is the phenomenon in which an agent seems to do better by arranging to be less than a fully self-interested calculator: making a threat credible, a promise sincere, or a future temptation inaccessible, rather than choosing optimally at each later decision point.

Begin by fixing the phenomenon with these cases, and say for each what it specifically demands of a theory.

- Parfit's hitchhiker: a driver will rescue a stranded man only on the promise of payment after the ride. Once rescued, pure self-interest gives the hitchhiker no reason to pay, the driver can see this, and so the pure maximizer cannot offer a promise the driver will accept. A hitchhiker who is genuinely disposed to keep such promises would do better by his own lights. A theory must explain why this works, and whether the disposition pays only if it would in fact follow through when the time came.
- A toxin-style case in which the reward is given for having formed the intention tonight to perform a mildly unpleasant act tomorrow, regardless of whether the act is performed. The agent cannot simply decide to have an intention he knows he will abandon, and he will abandon it unless he is the kind of person who follows through. A theory must say what being that kind of person consists in, and why it cannot normally be adopted at will.
- The self-binding case: Ulysses has himself lashed to the mast and orders the crew to stop their ears, on the reasoning that a man who knows he will later beg to be untied does better to remove the later choice now. A theory must say whether the rationality lies in the earlier act, in the disposition, in the structure the agent builds around himself, or in something else.

Then give the complete list of requirements, with the case or consideration behind each. At minimum, your list should settle these questions:

- Whether the policy is assessed ex ante or ex post, and whether a single policy can be rational on one assessment and irrational on the other.
- What the rationality attaches to: individual acts, intentions, plans, dispositions, or whole strategies — and whether the same account covers all of them.
- How much self-knowledge the agent can have about the mechanism without collapsing it, and what constraints that places on the account.
- Which nearby phenomena must be excluded as not genuinely rational irrationality — weakness of will, compulsion, unchosen self-deception, ordinary error — and what theoretical work each exclusion does.
- Whether the account must explain why an outside observer should or should not try to talk the agent out of the irrationality, given that the policy as a whole is supposed to serve the agent's interests.

Mark which of these requirements are non-negotiable conditions on any adequate theory and which are merely preferences of particular camps. Then say whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate the otherwise plausible candidate theories. Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of existing positions; the specification itself is the deliverable.
