Consider the following argument that some hard choices reveal a fourth value relation beyond better than, worse than, and equally good. (1) For any two options, exactly one of these holds all things considered: A is better than B, B is better than A, or A and B are equally good. (2) If A and B are equally good, then a slightly improved version of A is better than B. (3) There are hard choices in which neither option is better than the other all things considered. (4) In some such choices—say, between two very different careers—slightly improving one option in some respect (a modest raise, a shorter commute) does not thereby make it better all things considered than the other. (5) By (1) and (3), the options in such a choice are equally good, but by (2) and (4), they are not equally good; contradiction. (6) Therefore the trichotomy in (1) is false, and such options stand in a fourth relation of being on a par. Is this argument valid? If not, identify the exact step that fails and explain under what reading it fails. If it is valid through step (5), is step (6) a warranted response to the contradiction, or can a defender of the trichotomy hold on to (1) by rejecting or reinterpreting another premise? Be specific about which premise and what the reinterpretation says.
