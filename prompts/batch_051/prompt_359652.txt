So I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've been sitting on a distinction about Sleeping Beauty that I want pressure-tested before it goes anywhere near my disseratation. Let me lay out the setup anyway, so we're on the same page: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday night, a fair coin is tossed. If Heads, she's woken Monday only. If Tails, she's woken Monday and Tuesday, with a drug that wipes any memory of earlier wakings, so when she wakes she can't tell which day it is. The familiar fight is wether, on waking, her credence in Heads should be 1/2 or 1/3.

My thought is that the fight is usually misdescribed because "credence" is being asked to do two jobs at once. I want to say a credence has two legs: a degree, which is the number, and what I've been calling a "stake basis," the structure of outcomes the number is supposed to be spent across. If the stake basis is the toss — one verdict per world, scored at the end of the experiment — then 1/2 is forced: the coin is fair, and waking up doesn't discriminate between the Heads world and the Tails world. If the stake basis is the waking itself — each waking is a separate chance to score, and there are twice as many wakings under Tails — then 1/3 is forced by symmetry among possible wakings. I've been calling this "two-legged credence," or sometimes "payoff-indexed credence," and the thesis is that halfers and thirders are both right about their own leg and both wrong to think one leg has to bear the whole weight.

I know this smells like the betting arguments, and maybe like centered-worlds talk, but I don't think it's either: those usually treat betting odds as a diagnostic for the single true credence, whereas I'm saying the choice of stake basis is part of what a credence is in a self-locating setup, not just a test of it. What I want checked: (a) is this already a position in the literature under a name I should know before I embarrass myself at a conference, (b) is it coherent once you push on it, and (c) where does it actually break — is there a version of the problem where the two legs point the same direction, or worse, point different directions while the agent has to act on both at once, so keeping them separate stops being available? My real fear is that I've just renamed the difference between per-world scoring and per-waking scoring and dressed it up as a metaphysical claim. Tear it apart.
