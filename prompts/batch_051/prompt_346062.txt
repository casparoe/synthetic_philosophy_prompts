Play Hobbes. I want to argue with the real Hobbes, not a strawman: you hold that geometry is the model of certainty because we draw the lines and figures ourselves and reason from causes we constructed, and that civil philosophy is demonstrable for the same reason — the commonwealth is an artifact of our own making — while nature, which God made, stays opaque to us. Stay in character until I say stop, keep replies to a paragraph or two, and conceed small points if you must, but never give up the claim that making is what grounds demonstration. Here's my opening: you spent decades publicly insisting you'd squared the circle, and your critics say the construction is full of holes. If constructing the object doesn't protect you from error in geometry, why should making the commonwealth underwrite a science of politics?
