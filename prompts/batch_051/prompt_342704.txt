I've spent way too much time in the decision-theory threads on LessWrong, and over the years I've drifted from "EDT is obviously broken" to something close to the evidential cooperation in large worlds view. The version I mean isn't the flatfooted one where you pay off any blackmailer who claims to predict you; it's the more careful claim that, in a sufficiently large universe, their are enough agents in roughly my exact epistemic situation that my choice to cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma is genuinely diagnostic of what those agents will do, and that evidential link can rationally matter even when there's no causal channel between us. From inside my own head I can't tell which of the correlated deciders I am, so I should act as if my decision is evidence about all of them. I know the usual objections get repeated—that this only works if you're already willing to be updateless, that it seems to license paying people who merely predict you, that there's a double-counting worry when everyone on both sides of a dilemma reasons the same way—but I want more than the talking points.

Treat me as someone who currently accepts the view. I want to know what would actually count against it: which observations, formal results, thought experiments, or ways of drawing the reference class would push against it, sorted from fatal to merely awkward. I'd like you to distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely embarrass the view, and to say which objections a committed defender would themselves concede as real costs rather than dismissing as changing the subject. I also want an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if it only "works" when the copies are specified just so, or when the universe has just the right size, that itself would be informative—and whether being hard to falsify is even a problem for a view about decision-theoretic norms rather than an empirical hypothesis about the cosmos. I'm personally most moved by cases where the evidential link is precise and scrutable rather than hand-wavy, so weight those accordingly.

After your discussion, give me the result as a JSON object with this schema:

- overall_assessment: string, two to four sentences on whether the view is exposed or insulated, and whether that insulation is or isn't a problem.
- defeaters: array of objects, ordered from most to least decisive, each with these fields:
  - name: short label for the defeater
  - claim: what the defeater says, in one or two sentences
  - kind: one of empirical, formal, thought_experiment, conceptual
  - decisiveness: one of fatal, major, awkward, merely_apparent
  - would_a_defender_concede: what an honest defender should grant about this objection
  - live_or_merely_logical: either live or merely_logical
  - what_would_make_me_update: the specific observation or premise that would have to hold for this objection to actually move someone like me
- strongest_live_defeater: string naming the single defeater that should worry me most and why
- riskiest_case: string describing the concrete scenario where the view makes its most distinctive and potentially falsifiable prediction
- bullet_biters: array of strings, consequences a committed defender may have to accept as the price of keeping the view
- bottom_line: string, one or two sentences on whether I should remain a defender
