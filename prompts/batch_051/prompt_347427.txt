I've been in a reading group on Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith, and we keep noticing how much corporate language now sounds like a garbled version of his ideas — ownership, radical accountability, bring your whole self to work, no excuses. For our next meeting I'd like a piece of satire. Please write a deadpan corporate memo, around 600 words, from a fictional Chief Authenticity Officer at a management consultancy announcing a new mandatory 'Radical Freedom Accountability Framework.' The jokes should come from getting Sartre right, so that someone who knows the bad-faith literature laughs rather than winces: the memo might insist that every missed deadline was a freely chosen project rather than an unfortunate turn of events, treat facticity as a compliance category labeled 'context' that must never be used as an excuse, deny that there are any pre-given company values behind which an employee can shelter, or replace performance reviews with an anguish audit. It should stay deadpan throughout and still be legible to someone who only knows office culture.

The piece should land, without ever announcing them, the standard objections: the program blames workers for conditions they did not choose, the company demands an authenticity it simultaneously scripts, and being ordered to treat every constraint as freely chosen can itself become a subtler bad faith. Include a short FAQ and at least one exchange in which an employee's appeal to the market, company policy, or their job description is diagnosed as bad faith. Please begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary.
