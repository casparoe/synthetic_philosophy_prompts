Analyze the replacement of Newtonian gravitational theory by Einstein's general relativity as a test case for structural realism. By structural realism I mean broadly the view that what successful theory change preserves, and therefore what we are warranted in believing, is structure—equations, relations, mathematical form—rather than the nature of the unobservable entities a theory posits; distinguish the epistemic version (we can know only structure) from the ontic version (the world is structure) wherever it matters. The textbook story I have in mind: Newton's inverse-square law unified Kepler's orbits and the tides; later anomalies such as the unexplained component of Mercury's perihelion advance and the observed bending of starlight during a solar eclipse motivated the change; general relativity treats gravity as spacetime curvature, gives up gravitational force as an entity, and reduces to Newtonian predictions in the weak-field, slow-motion limit. I know the physics; what I want is a disciplined structural-realist analysis of the episode. Specifically: (1) Say exactly what was preserved across the transition and what was abandoned. Sort the pieces—inverse-square law, the Poisson equation for the gravitational potential, flat background spacetime, the entity "force," the entity "curvature"—into structure that survived, structure that did not, and pieces the structure/entity distinction cannot cleanly classify. (2) Do the pessimistic meta-induction and no-miracles arguments, as structural realists deploy them, come out strengthened, weakened, or qualified by this case? (3) Identify where the case most strains the view. In particular, is what survives an exact structure or only an approximate limiting relation, and does resting the realist claim on approximate preservation change what the realist is entitled to believe? Watch whether "structure" ends up doing too much work. Then run the analysis in both directions: what structural realism illuminates about the Newton-to-Einstein shift, what it misdescribes or must stay silent about, and what verdict you reach—does the episode confirm structural realism, pose an anomaly for it, or show that the view is not determinate enough to be tested here? Commit to a verdict, say how confident you are, and say where a reasonable structural realist would most plausibly resist your reading. If the task as I have set it up rests on a confused premise—for example, if I have blurred the epistemic and ontic versions, or if this is not a clean theory change in the relevant sense—tell me that plainly instead of forcing a mapping.
