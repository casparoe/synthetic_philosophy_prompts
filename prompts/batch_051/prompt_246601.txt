I been chewing on a worldbuilding problem and thought you could help me stress-test a mechanic before I build it. By day I write backend services, so I keep thinking of it as a scoring algorithm with adversarial users. The setup: in this setting a galactic survey guild ranks civilizations the way a credit agency ranks firms. The usual Kardashev scale only measures total controlled energy: roughly, a Type I uses about the energy that reaches its home planet, Type II the full output of its star, Type III a galaxy's worth. But that feels like it misses an entire axis. I saw a rival approach attributed to a cosmologist named Barrow, where instead of going up in energy you go down in scale and rate a civilization by the smallest thing it can deliberately and repeatably manipulate. Meter-scale is roughly level zero, cells and genes put you higher, molecules and atoms higher still, on down toward the Planck scale.

Here is the mechanic I want picked apart. The guild gives each logged civilization two percentile scores within the currently surveyed population: one on the energy log and one on the miniaturization log. The headline rating is the lower of the two percentiles, published as a single number, with the slogan that a civilization is only as advanced as its weakest axis. So a culture that harnesses a star-system's worth of energy but tops out at micron-level engineering would get a mediocre score, and so would a nanotech culture that runs its whole thing off one planet's solar panels. I'm less interested in whether that's fair and more in what behavior it generates when the rating has consequences: access to certain jump routes, status in galactic forums, priority on first-contact lists. Walk me through the incentives. What would a rational civilization near the threshold do on each axis, how would it game or spoof the two logs, which real capabilities would the index systematically under- or over-reward, and what second-order effects would show up inside the guild itself given that its auditors get recruited from highly rated civilizations? Then tell me whether switching the headline number to a weighted average of the two percentiles would actually fix the failure modes or just swap them.

That's the ask; feel free to point out any weird equilibrium of the min-percentile rule that I'm not seeing.
