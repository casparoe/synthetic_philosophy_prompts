I've been going back and forth on the Milindapañha's chariot exchange—you know, Nagasena asking whether the chariot is any of its parts or something apart from them, and then saying it's just a convenient name for the assemblage. I want to test whether the anatta criteria still work for a weird case. The criteria I'm using are: (1) a person is a conventional label on the five aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness); (2) personal continuity is just a causal chain of momentary aggregates, not a same owner across time; (3) karmic/causal responsibility attaches to the stream, not to a substance; and (4) there is no fact of the matter about whether stream S at t1 and stream S' at t2 are "the same" beyond continuity of the relevant causes. Now suppose an AI is built that, over months, learns to consciously anticipate, reflect on, and take responsibility for the past choices of a particular human being, while that human is still alive but progressively loses all memory of those choices and all the dispositions that produced them. The AI's model gets so good that it predicts and endorses those past choices better than the human now does. Does the AI qualify as the continuation of that person's stream, or is it just a new stream that models an old one? Go criterion by criterion, and then tell me whether the case shows the criteria need a fifth one.
