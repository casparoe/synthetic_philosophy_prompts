I have been turning over a view I started calling sidedness, and I would like an honest assessment of it, because I cannot tell whether it is a genuine insight or only sounds like one. The view began with a simple observation. The object in the corner of the room is a chair if you ask the person about to sit on it, a collection of wooden parts if you ask the person repairing it, and a hiding place if you ask the cat. Asked what the thing really is, apart from any such point of view, I found myself unable to give an answer. From this I moved to a stronger claim: every statement is true only from some side. From another side, the same statement is not true, but I do not want to say it is false; it is just that the other side has no use for it. On this picture, no statement is simply true, and no statement is simply false; all truth is sided.

The problem is what happens when the picture is turned on itself. The claim that all truth is sided is itself a claim. If it follows its own rule, then it is true only from some side, and from another side it is not true. In that case, the claim has given up on being true from all sides, and I no longer see what makes it a claim about all truth rather than a claim about how things look from the side it happens to be voiced from. If instead I say that this one claim is true from every side, then I have produced an unsided truth, and the view denies that such a thing exists. I have tried to escape by saying that the claim about all claims is a different kind of claim than the ones it describes, but then I face the same question one level up, and I can find no reason to stop the climb.

So my questions are these. Is sidedness salvageable if it is stated carefully, or is the self-application problem fatal? If it is fatal, say exactly which move produces the collapse and what the strongest remaining version of the view would have to concede. If it is not fatal, give the version that survives self-application and explain what it gives up in order to survive. And setting the self-application problem aside for a moment: is the chair example even good evidence for the view, or have I confused a thing with our various descriptions of it, and confused being true from a side with being useful to a side? I would rather have the view broken than kept in a form that merely looks like a theory.
