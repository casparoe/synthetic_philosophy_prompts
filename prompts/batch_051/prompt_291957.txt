I've been going round in circles on something in the philosophy of maths, and I want your honest view.

Take the natural numbers. Set theory gives us at least two ways to build them. The von Neumann way: start with 0 = ∅, and let each later number be the set of all the numbers before it. So 1 = {0}, 2 = {0,1}, 3 = {0,1,2}, 4 = {0,1,2,3}. On that encoding, 2 ∈ 4 comes out true. The Zermelo way: also start with 0 = ∅, but let each later number be the set containing only the previous number. So 1 = {0}, 2 = {1}, 3 = {2}, 4 = {3}. On that encoding, 2 ∈ 4 comes out false, because 4's only member is 3.

Now the asymmetry that bothers me. Structuralists and a lot of working mathematicians treat this as showing that there's no fact of the matter about whether 2 is an element of 4: it's an artefact of the encoding, not a real question. Yet they're happy to call 2 + 2 = 4 a determinate fact about the numbers themselves. But both statements just follow from how we've chosen to represent numbers. Why does 2 ∈ 4 get demoted to encoding-relative noise while 2 + 2 = 4 gets kept as genuine arithmetic?

I'd like you to either produce the strongest justification you can for this asymmetry, test it against at least one adjacent case of your own choosing, and say whether it holds up; or else tell me straight that the asymmetry is indefensible, and we should either treat both statements as genuine facts or both as encoding-dependent. Along the way, spell out as precisely as you can what it is for a mathematical statement to be about the structure of the numbers rather than about a particular encoding — and wether that distinction is doing real work or just restating the original asymmetry.

Also, answer in British English please.
