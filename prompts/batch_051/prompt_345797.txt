There's no Cartesian theater where it all gets screened, no little person in the audience; the self too is just part of the interface, not the user. (I've seen this run from Dennett-ish stuff to the attention-schema story that awareness is the brain's rough sketch of its own attention.)

What I want: restate that whole position in the vocabulary I actually live in. Presentation layer, view vs. model, the desktop metaphor, what the system does vs. what it exposes to the user, affordances, error messages, skeuomorphism, the works. Then give me a residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which parts you had to fudge to make them fit, and which parts have no counterpart at all in interface talk.

The thing I'm already suspicious about is this: a real user interface has a real user sitting outside the system, a person with goals who the interface is for. On the user-illusion view, the 'user' of the consciousness interface isnt a separate person—the user is part of the model, or the model is somehow its own user. So is the interface metaphor smuggling in a homunculus? Does the theory need one more than it admits? I want you to keep the translation honest: where the philosophy doesn't survive the trip into UI vocabulary, tell me whether thats because my vocabulary is blind to something real or because the theory was leaning on a picture that does not actually hold up.
