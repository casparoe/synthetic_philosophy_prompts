I've been trying to get clear on what seperates actually understanding something from just being able to get the right answer by leaning on a tool. I want a working definition of understanding — not the loose everyday sense where a dog understands "sit," but the sense relevant to whether a person understands some subject matter, process, or solution.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

1. A med student who memorized the diagnostic criteria and aces multiple-choice questions, but can't say why the criteria hang together or what to do when two of them conflict.

2. A radiologist who used to read scans well but now mostly reads AI heatmaps, and has gradually lost the ability to spot on her own what the AI flags. She's still usually right when the AI is right, because she defers to it reliably.

3. A chess player who preps engine lines, plays them flawlessly, and wins. If you ask him why a move is good he just says "the engine says so."

4. A person in a new city following turn-by-turn GPS who arrives on time but couldn't at the end draw the route or find the destination again without the phone.

5. The same person in the city where they grew up, using GPS out of laziness even though they could navigate fine without it.

6. Someone who can prove a theorem by clicking through a proof assistant step by step, checking that each step is valid by the assistant's rules, but couldn't reconstruct the proof a week later.

7. A mechanic who can diagnose an engine by sound and fix it, but can't give much of an explanation beyond "it sounded like that."

8. A student who solves integrals by typing them into a computer algebra system, can't do substitution by hand, but can look at two solution paths and tell which one is right and vaguely why.

I lean toward saying 2 and 5 count as understanding, 3 and 4 don't, 8 is confusing to me, and I keep going back and forth on 6 and 7. Give me a definition that handles all eight, run each case through it, and flag where the definition gives a verdict you think I'd resist, or where it gives no verdict. Also say whether your definition makes understanding come in degrees, because these cases feel graded but maybe that's just because the term is doing two jobs.
