I am a teaching assistant for an upper-level undergraduate course on the nature of knowledge and skill, and I want a careful second look at one exam script before I return it. The question on the exam read:

"Set out the intellectualist thesis that knowing how to do something is a species of knowing that — the view that S knows how to φ just in case S knows, under a practical mode of presentation, a proposition that answers the question how to φ. Critically evaluate the strongest anti-intellectualist objection to this thesis, in doing so taking care to distinguish knowing how from (a) the ability to perform the act and (b) the ability to state or articulate the proposition known."

The student answered as follows:

"Intellectualism claims that S knows how to φ just in case S knows a proposition about how to φ. The strongest objection is the regress argument associated with Ryle. The argument runs like this: if intelligent action required the prior consultation of a known proposition, then the act of consulting that proposition, being itself an intelligent act, would require the consultation of a further proposition, and so on without end; hence no action could ever be intelligent. Anti-intellectualists take this to show that knowing how cannot be propositional knowledge. The standard intellectualist reply is that one can know a proposition without consciously entertaining or articulating it, so the regress never starts, because the agent simply acts from tacit propositional knowledge. But this reply commits intellectualism to saying that a skilled pianist who loses both arms no longer knows how to play, since she can no longer act on the proposition, and that is an implausible cost. Worse, the regress reappears at a deeper level. Applying a proposition to a concrete situation is itself an exercise of skill; if that application is guided by another tacit proposition, the regress begins again, while if it is not guided by any proposition, intellectualism has conceded that at least some intelligent performances are not manifestations of propositional knowledge. Intellectualism therefore either regresses or surrenders its central claim. The correct conclusion is to identify knowing how with the corresponding ability or multi-track disposition, which requires no propositional content."
