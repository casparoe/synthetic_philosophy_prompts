I've been going back and forth on what it actually means to hold a value intrinsically, as opposed to just valuing it as a tool. My working criteria: someone holds a value intrinsically if

(a) they keep honoring it when it costs them something real;
(b) they'd keep honoring it even if it stopped producing the good it usually produces;
(c) they treat it as not up for ordinary tradeoff — money, status, convenience, that sort of thing;
(d) it's part of their self-conception — if they gave it up, they'd say they'd lost something that made them who they were.

Now the case. Lena is a public-health researcher who is scrupulously honest whenever a belief is "load-bearing," as she puts it: data, methods, clinical summaries, policy briefs, peer review, CV lines. She's turned down lucrative consulting because they wanted her to soften a result. She says she'd stay honest in science even if faking data somehow produced better health outcomes, because faking data would feel like a kind of desecration. She calls herself "a truth person." So far she hits (a)–(d).

The wrinkle: Lena only cares about truth where truth does a job. She lies cheerfully about low-stakes stuff — Santa, exaggerating old stories, half-believing flattering things about people she loves. More interesting, she does not value holding true beliefs for their own sake; if a curiosity-driven truth would be painful and useless, she'd rather not know. What she cares about is truthfulness: not lying, not fudging, keeping the research system trustworthy. Asked why that matters, she gives a thoroughly consequentialist story about trust and better decisions. Asked whether she'd want to know every true-but-useless fact, she says no — "if it does no work, its just trivia."

So does Lena hold truth as an intrinsic value, an instrumental one, or is she holding truthfulness intrinsically while treating accurate belief instrumentally? Is it coherent to hold a value intrinsically only inside a domain, or does her domain-restriction show my criteria are missing something? Go through (a)–(d) seperately and honestly, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether this case shows the intrinsic/instrumental split is doing real work or just sorting things by whether we like the story.

Also, tell me what would change your mind: what extra fact about Lena, or what argument, would most likely flip your verdict, and where in your reasoning you're least confident.

At the very end, after all your reasoning, put a single machine-readable line:

\answer{...}

where ... is one of: intrinsic, instrumental, mixed, underdetermined. (mixed = truthfulness intrinsic, accurate belief instrumental)
