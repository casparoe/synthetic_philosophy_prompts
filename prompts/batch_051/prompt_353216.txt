I'm a retired chemist, recently started reading the philosophy I'd always meant to get to. For the past few months it's been the free will literature, and Galen Strawson's Basic Argument has really got under my skin. As I understand it, he argues that you can only be truly, ultimately morally responsible for what you do if you are responsible for the way you are — your character, motives, capacities — and to be responsible for the way you are you'd have to have intentionally made yourself that way through earlier choices; but those earlier choices are themselves just more actions you'd have to be responsible for, so the regress never bottoms out. The upshot is that no one is ultimately responsible for their actions. If I've butchered it, use the version I've just given; it's the one I'd be working from.

Here's the practical problem. I chair the exclusions panel of our local secondary school's governing body. We hear about twenty cases a year, take around two hours over each one, and decide whether to permanently exclude a pupil after a serious incident. Decisions can go to an independent review panel if the family appeals, so everything has to be written down and defensible. I've noticed we keep reaching for language about what a pupil 'deserves' as if that settled something, and I now think it doesn't.

Write me a reusable procedure our panel can actually follow in these hearings, one that stays faithful to the Basic Argument as I've set it out. I want the procedure to pin down the quesions in order, say what counts as an adequate answer to each, and flag the exact points where retribution or 'ultimate desert' woud try to slip back in, and say how the procedure blocks or redirects it. Then run the procedure on two cases: (1) a Year 11 pupil who, after weeks of harassing a quieter classmate, ambushed him after school and broke his arm, planned and unprovoked; (2) a pupil with a diagnosed conduct disorder and a chaotic home life who has just committed a third serious assault on a staff member. In each worked example, include at least one line showing exactly what the panel would record as its reason. Keep the whole thing under 800 words. If the procedure forces an answer we would find hard to accept, say so plainly; and be explicit about what no such procedure can do.
