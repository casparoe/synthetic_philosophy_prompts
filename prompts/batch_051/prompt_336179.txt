I want your actual credence in the claim: a rationally ideal agent in a world like ours should have a utility function bounded above, where the reason is specifically Pascal's-mugging-type cases rather than "utility is finite by stipulation" or some independent argument.

Decompose it. At least these three terms:

(a) your credence that at least one version of the mugging actually works — mostly I mean the escalation version, where the mugger just keeps multiplying the promised utility until the expected value of paying him a dollar turns positive — and so unbounded expected-utility maximization gives the wrong answer in our world;
