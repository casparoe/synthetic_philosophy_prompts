In my philosophy class we read A.J. Ayer and he claims that sentences like 'you will survive the death of your body' are not false — they're literally meaningless. His test is that a sentence only makes a factual claim if some possible experience could count for or against it, and he thinks afterlife talk fails that test. So he's not saying there's no afterlife, he's saying the question is a pseudo-question. Write an essay on whether Ayer is right about that. Weigh the strongest arguements on both sides, like the objection that lots of meaningful claims (say, about other people's feelings) can't be directly checked either, and whatever reply Ayer could give. Then deal with this problem I keep going in circles on: if Ayer is right, then someone saying 'I'll see my grandma again' has said something with no truth value. Can you still sensibly hope for that or be comforted by it? Or does that show either that Ayer's verification test is too strict, or that a big part of our emotional life runs on sentences that don't literally mean anything? Reach and defend a conclusion.
