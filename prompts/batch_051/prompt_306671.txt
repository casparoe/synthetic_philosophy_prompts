I spent most of my career doing inverse problems in geophysics: you try to recover what's underground from surface measurements, there's never enough data to pin down a unique solution, so you add a regularizer—a prior saying, e.g., the structure should be smooth or simple—and you pick the model that fits the data best under that constraint. Now that I'm retired I've been reading Davidson on radical interpretation, and the whole setup looks like the same beast. The radical interpreter has to solve for a speaker's beliefs and meanings simultaneously, with nothing to go on but behavior and which sentences the speaker holds true. The principle of charity looks to me like exactly a regularizer: it breaks the underdetermination and pushes you toward one interpretation among many. Is this a real structural parallel or just me pattern-matching? If it is real, does anything from inverse theory carry over—identifiability, non-uniqueness, the way different regularizers give you different models? And is there a counterpart to the worry I always had in geophysics, that a good chunk of the solution you recover is an artifact of the regularizer you chose rather than something in the world? Or is charity doing something else entirely because it's supposed to be constitutive of meaning, not just a heuristic for finding it?
