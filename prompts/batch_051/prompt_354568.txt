After retiring from a career in geochemistry, I have been reading formal epistemology, and I have become stuck on a toy problem about updating on conditional information. I have constructed an argument whose conclusion seems too strong, but I cannot find the error. I would like you to tell me whether the argument is valid as written; if it is not, please identify the first step that fails and state what a proper Bayesian update would be.

Here is the case. A geologist is trying to locate a rare mineral. It must be in exactly one of three mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive zones: A1, A2, and B. A1 and A2 are two sectors of the eastern basin, and B is the western basin. Her initial probabilities are:

P(A1) = 0.20  
P(A2) = 0.30  
P(B) = 0.50  

A satellite report arrives from an instrument she knows to be perfectly reliable. The report states only that, conditional on the mineral being somewhere in the eastern basin, the probability that it is in A1 is 0.90. The report says nothing about whether the mineral is actually in the eastern basin.

She reasons as follows:

(1) The new information is a constraint on the conditional probability of A1 given A1 ∨ A2. It is not a report of the truth of any proposition about where the mineral actually is.

(2) Because the report is entirely about the internal distribution between A1 and A2 within the eastern basin, it provides no information that should shift the probability of the eastern basin as a whole.

(3) Therefore, a rational update should leave the probability of the conditioning event unchanged: Q(A1 ∨ A2) = P(A1 ∨ A2) = 0.50.

(4) From the report, Q(A1 | A1 ∨ A2) = 0.90.

(5) Combining (3) and (4) gives Q(A1) = 0.45, Q(A2) = 0.05, and Q(B) = 0.50.

(6) This update is exactly what one would get by applying Jeffrey conditionalization to a partition that separates the eastern basin from the western basin and redistributing probability only within the eastern basin.

(7) Thus, when a perfectly reliable source supplies a new conditional probability assignment over a subset of possibilities, a rational agent should leave the probability of the complement of that subset unchanged.

I suspect that something goes wrong in step (2) or (6), or that step (3) is an illicit inference from a conditional to its condition, but I keep being pulled back toward the argument. Please tell me whether the argument is valid. If it is not valid, identify the first step that fails and explain what the correct update rule should be in this kind of case.
