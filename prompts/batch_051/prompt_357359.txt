I write about both the science of intelligence and the study of political ideology, and I keep bumping into what feels like the same pattern. In intelligence research, g gets extracted from the correlation matrix of a battery of tests, and lots of scientists defend it as a real, meaningful dimension even though no single test directly measures it. In politics, left–right scores get extracted from how people answer a bunch of issue questions, and the dimension is treated as much more suspect—a construct, or maybe a way elite ideolgy gets imposed on messier public opinion. Is the left–right axis real in exactly the same way and to exactly the same degree that g is real? If not, where does the analogy actually break?
