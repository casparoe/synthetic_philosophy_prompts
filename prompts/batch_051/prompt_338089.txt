A country's electoral commission proposes the following reform, to be piloted at the next general election. Every ballot paper will carry two parts. Part A is an ordinary vote for one candidate. Part B, optional and secret like the rest of the ballot, asks the voter to name the candidate they would choose if electability were no object, and to tick one of two boxes describing their Part A vote: "wholehearted endorsement" or "reluctant lesser-evil vote". The commission will publish, alongside the result, the aggregate Part B preferences and the percentage of each candidate's Part A vote that came with the "reluctant" tick. In addition, any winning candidate more than half of whose Part A ballots are ticked "reluctant" forfeits part of their public campaign grant to a civic education fund. Walk through how this scheme would actually behave: who is incentivised to vote, what they are incentivised to reveal or conceal in Part B, how parties and candidates would adapt their campaigns, where the scheme is gamed first, and what it does to the expressive and strategic content of the vote. Can the worst failure modes be fixed without gutting the point of the scheme? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
