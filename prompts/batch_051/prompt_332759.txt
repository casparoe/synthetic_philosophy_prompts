so I've been reading longtermist/EA stuff on the side and I'm trying to work out wether the views I've ended up with are actually consistent. I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy background, so go easy on jargon but not too easy. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) people who exist a thousand or a million years from now matter just as much as people alive now; no principled reason to discount them just because their far away in time.

(2) since the number of possible future people dwarfs the number alive now, most of the expected value of any choice is in its effects on the far future. so reducing existential risk—making sure we don't go extinct or permanently wreck our potential—should dominate our priorities.

(3) I'm broadly okay with this licensing big sacrifices from present people: if a policy lowers x-risk by even a small amount, it can be worth huge costs now, including real losses of liberty or wealth.

(4) but I'm not a utilitarian. I think there are deontological side-constraints: you can't kill an innocent person even to save five, and deliberately violating someone's basic rights doesn't become okay just because the expected value comes out positive.

(5) politically I'm pretty anti-authoritarian. I think states shouldn't impose one comprehensive moral vision on everyone, and I distrust anyone who thinks they've got the correct theory of the good and should be allowed to run things on that basis.

(6) I also lean hard toward epistemic modesty about the far future: we can barely predict thirty years out, let alone millennia, so confident long-range social engineering is usually hubris and risks locking in our mistakes.

what I want to know is wether this set hangs together. some of these feel like they pull against each other, but I can't tell which are real tensions and which only look that way. if I brought this list to a philosophy meetup, which commitments would get me in trouble, paired with which others, how bad is each tension, and what's the cheapest fix? rank them by severity rather than just flagging everything.
