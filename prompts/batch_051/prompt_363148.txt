Alright, I've been turning over the Judy Benjamin problem for a small essay I want to write and could use a second opinion before I get carried away.

Quick version of the setup, in case it helps. Judy Benjamin is dropped into a war zone. Her uncertainty is over sectors—say Red and Blue—and over units within each sector, including, say, Headquarters Company and Second Company. She gets a radio message which does not directly locate her, but which is naturally read as giving a conditional probability: something like, if you are in the Red sector, the probability you are in Headquarters Company is 0.8. Ordinary conditioning handles messages that are events, but this message is more naturally a constraint on a conditional probability, so it isn't obvious how to update her full distribution. In particular, should her probability of being in the Red sector change at all?

Here's the thought I'd want to defend. The usual treatments seem to treat the message as pure partial information—an input that floats free of any story about who sent it, why, or what they might have said in other cases. But the message is also an event: a radio message came in. If Judy has even a rough model of the process that produces such messages, then standard Bayesian updating on "I received this message" would give an answer, and P(Red) might move in ways the constraint-based solutions rule out. So perhaps the Judy Benjamin problem doesn't show that we need a generalisation of conditioning so much as that a conditional-looking message underdetermines the update until you specify the reporting process that generated it. The hard question is whether that just relocates the problem rather than dissolving it.

What I'd love from you: (a) is my characterisation of the formal problem fair, or am I subtly changing the example from how it's usually set up; (b) is the "model the message-generating process" move actually interesting, or does it collapse into something standard and boring once you write out Bayes' rule; (c) which part of the essay would have to do the most work to convince someone who knows the formal literature. Please be blunt and, when you make a criticism, tell me how confident you are in it, and flag the single point you're least sure about overall.
