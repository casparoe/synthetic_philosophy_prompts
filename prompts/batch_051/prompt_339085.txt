In my English class we read H.G. Wells's The Invisible Man, and at the same time my philosophy elective has been working through the early books of Plato's Republic, including Glaucon's challenge about the ring of Gyges. I was struck by how directly the novel seems to take up the thought experiment: Glaucon argues that anyone who could become invisible would eventually help himself at the expense of others, because people generally act justly only out of fear of punishment or loss of reputation, and the ring removes exactly that check. Wells gives us a character in Griffin who actually performs the experiment, and within a short time he has moved from theft and deception to violence and to a plan for systematic terror. On the surface this looks like a literary confirmation of Glaucon's pessimism.

But I am not sure the novel is really just an illustration, and I would like a careful reading of it through Glaucon's challenge. Glaucon asks us to imagine an otherwise decent person acquiring invisibility, whereas Wells spends the early part of the novel making Griffin secretive, proud, and isolated long before the experiment; the novel also makes invisibility itself a source of hardship rather than pure freedom, from the practical problems of keeping warm, eating, and moving through a society that responds to him as a monster. I would like to know whether the novel should be read as a genuine test of Glaucon's claim about the just man freed from detection, or as a deliberately rigged case that changes the question. Which scenes most reward this reading, for example the early reaction to invisibility, the use of the tramp Marvel, the planned reign of terror, and the final scene in which Griffin becomes visible again only after he is dead? Does Wells leave any room for the Socratic reply the story is meant to provoke, or does the novel commit itself to the view that moral restraint depends on being seen? And is there something the novel adds to the philosophical debate that the bare thought experiment cannot capture, perhaps about the difference between being unseen and being outside any human relationship?
