I've been arguing with a friend about Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment, and shes one of those people who won't give you a principle, only verdicts, so I've started keeping track. Her record so far: she's fine with the original Ulysses case, where he has himself tied to the mast because he knows the sirens will make him temporarily irrational; fine with a smoker who signs a precommitment contract where a friend collects a thousand dollars if he smokes; and fine with constitutional clauses that require a supermajority to amend. She's also approved a psychiatric advance directive with a Ulysses clause that lets a hospital hold someone during a documented manic episode, but only if the clause is narrowly scoped to those episodes and there's some kind of review mechanism. On the other side, she hates constitutional clauses that are unamendable, on the grounds that no current generation may take exit off the table for everyone later. And she rejected a case where a healthy person signed an irrevocable, no-exit contract to pay a third party if they ever used any recreational drug, saying it was too broad and too final.

They want to precommit, using a legal and social scaffold that can be undone only by a two-year cooling-off period that the future self has to initiate and sustain without lapsing. No documented psychiatric condition, no expected incapacity, no self-harm in the obvious sense. What should we predict she'll say about this case, assuming she's being consistent with her record?
