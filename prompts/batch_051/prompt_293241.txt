I keep going back and forth on this one and I'd like someone to actually settle it instead of just telling me both sides are reasonable.

The theory I'm half-sold on is that rationality can be trained roughly like a martial art. The idea is that there are small, isolable moves—putting a number on a claim before you look at the evidence, doing a Fermi estimate instead of gesturing at "some 10x thing," running a pre-mortem, catching yourself defaulting to the inside view—and if you drill them in low-stakes, dojo-like settings, they become automatic enough to fire later under real pressure. Calibration, base rates, noticing your own motivated reasoning: these are supposed to be domain-general component skills, so the katas should transfer broadly. It's a clean, optimistic view. It says rationality is a practice, not a trait.

But I have an intuition that keeps cutting against it, and I can't tell whether it's wisdom or noise. I know people who are gorgeous at the katas. Their calibration on quizzes is superb, their Fermi estimates are creepily accurate, they can diagram an argument while half-asleep. And then they make real-life decisions that are obviously, catastrophically irrational. They solve base-rate problems on paper and then stay in a doomed project because they already sunk two years into it. They'll run a beautiful pre-mortem for someone else's startup and then buy a house because it felt like The One. My gut says these aren't just a few anecdotes; they're the norm. The drills produce local skill, not a general muscle. Real decisions are tangled up with identity and face and embarrassment and fatigue in ways the kata context is built to screen out, and maybe transfer is mostly a fantasy, or it only happens inside the dojo.

So wich yields? Maybe my intuition should be debunked: transfer is real but slow and patchy, I'm expecting too much too early, and the katas just need bridging practice that forces use in messier settings. Or maybe the intuition is tracking that the martial-arts metaphor is wrong—that rationality isn't one general ability and what drills train is just a bundle of context-specific scripts, with no core that transfers. Give me the strongest version of each side and then tell me what you'd give up, or whether there's a reading where they're compatible. And be specific about what would justify each move, because I don't want a vague both-and dressed up as an answer.
