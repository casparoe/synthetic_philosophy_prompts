I spent most of my career as a hydrologist working on transboundary river basins, and now that I'm retired I've been reading political philosophy. The thing that keeps nagging me is what I gather philosophers call the boundary problem: before you ask what a democracy should decide, you have to say who the "we" is that gets to decide. In basin work this wasn't abstract. A dam in country A changes the flood regime, sediment load, and dry-season flows downstream in country B; a groundwater-pumping scheme in one district lowers wells in another; a hundred-year flood-protection plan binds people who aren't born yet. Every time, someone would say these decisions were imposed on them by a process they had no vote in, and I never had a principaled answer about who should have been in the room.

So before I read any more theories of democratic inclusion, I want the spec. Suppose I need a theory good enough to handle inclusion disputes in a world of transboundary and intergenerational decisions—not a full theory yet, just the requirements list. What does any adequate answer to the boundary problem have to get right?

Be concrete: for each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, not just a label. I want to know which distictions an adequate theory can't collapse—for instance, having standing being different from how much weight you get, and being consulted being different from having a veto. I want to know which empirical facts (about hydrology, demography, decision timescales, whatever) it can't contradict. And flag any pairs of desiderata that you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, because my hunch is that the interesting result isn't that one criterion wins but that some of them are in tension and a theory has to say which way it leans.

Then, briefly: which single requirement on your list does the most work in knocking out the candidate answers I'm likely to read next? Don't turn it into an essay on each theory; I mainly want criteria sharp enough that I can score the theories myself.

One more thing. Please tell me how confident you are in the list as a whole, and single out the one desideratum you're least sure is a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp.
