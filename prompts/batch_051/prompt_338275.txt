Here are the objections to the Lucas–Penrose anti-mechanist argument that I have collected so far, and I believe they are the standard ones. The argument, as I understand it, runs as follows. For any consistent formal system F strong enough to encode arithmetic, Gödel's first incompleteness theorem supplies a sentence that F can neither prove nor refute. A human mathematician can nevertheless see that this sentence is true, provided that F is consistent. Therefore the human mind cannot be equivalent to any such F, and mechanism about mathematical thought is false. My list:

(1) The consistency-premise objection: the move from F's inability to prove its Gödel sentence to our seeing that the sentence is true requires the premise that F is consistent, and for any serious candidate system we typically have no proof of that. If F is inconsistent, the theorem imposes no constraints on it.

(2) The identification objection: the mechanist is not obliged to hand over a uniquely specified formal system, so the anti-mechanist has no fixed F against which to run the diagonal, and the mechanist can always reply that the system actually realized by the mind was not the one just refuted.

(3) The extendability objection: for any particular F, one can form a stronger system that proves F's Gödel sentence, for instance by adding that sentence or a reflection principle, so the argument defeats only whichever systems have so far been proposed, not the claim that some system captures the mind.

(4) The idealized-insight objection: the premise that we can see the truth of the Gödel sentence of every candidate system overstates actual human capacities. For complicated or ill-understood systems we may be unable to determine whether the system is consistent or what its Gödel sentence says.

(5) The no-fixed-system objection: the mind might not be a single consistent formal system in the relevant sense at all — it might be inconsistent, or its mathematical commitments might shift over time — in which case the argument's central premise never takes hold.

I want one further objection that is not on this list and is not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of these five.
