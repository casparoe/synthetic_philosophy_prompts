I work for an old cabinetmaker who has this habit of sorting every moment with a tool into one of two piles, which he calls "with you" and "in front of you." Over a year I've collected his rulings. Hammering a nail while planning the argument you're about to have with your landlord: with you. Hammer head flying off mid-swing and you stand there turning the broken handle over: in front. In the hardware store, hefting one hammer after another checking the balance: in front. Typing an email while your composing the sentence: with you. Typing in a keyboarding exercise while watching your own hands: in front. Riding a bike to the bakery thinking about nothing much: with you. Riding when the chain starts clicking, so you stop, crouch, and peer at the derailleur: in front. A chair you're sitting in during dinner while following the argument: with you. A chair in a museum behind a rope, while you circle it studying the shape: in front. A phone in your hand while you scroll a feed half-absent: with you. A phone that freezes at checkout so you stand there staring at the frozen screen: in front.

Next week he says he'll rule on a case: a pianist in the middle of a piece she knows by heart suddenly gets self-conscious, starts watching her own fingers, and for ten bars or so she feels like shes outside the playing looking in at her hands, though she never misses a note, and then she drops back in. I want to know which pile those ten bars go in, what rule he's actually running, and what other rule fits every one of his old rulings just as well. If the two rules split on those ten bars, say which rule forces "in front" and which lets them stay "with you." And if neither pile feels right, tell me which of his old rulings should make him invent a third pile.
