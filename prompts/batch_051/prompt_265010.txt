I keep thinking Icelandic names are definately a weird borderline case. There, most people's last names are patronymics: Jón Einarsson's son Pétur is Pétur Jónsson, and Pétur's daughter Anna is Anna Pétursdóttir. So the last name changes every generation, a brother and sister get different endings, and the name points at one parent rather than a lineage. But it still fills the same slots where English puts a family name — forms, school rolls, legal documents. You're an expert on onomastics and naming conventions. Where a criterion says no, say whether that's a fact about Icelandic names or a flaw in the criterion. And if an Icelandic family moved to Germany and legally froze Jónsson as the fixed surname for all descendants, would that same string of letters become a family name, or does that show the given-name/family-name split is less about structure than about how people use it? Think through the borderline carefully.
