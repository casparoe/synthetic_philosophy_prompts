Next month I'm presenting at my town's business forum, and my talk is basically that the word 'joining' hides three different animals — joining a free trade area, joining a customs union, and joining a confederation — and that the customs union is the one people get wrong because you keep your flag and your parliament but from then on the tariffs and the trade deals get decided by people your own voters can't throw out. The audience is mostly small import/export owners who think more trade is good and find sovereignty talk a bit airy, a handful of old-school nationalist types who are against any treaty on principle, and one retired customs official who spent thirty years implementing exactly this stuff, and who I know thinks I'm overselling the difference between legal control and actual bargaining power. I've never studied this formally, just thought about it alot. Prep me for the question period: what will each of these groups actually object to, in roughly the order it'll come, phrased the way they'd say it, which objections have real force, what should I concede up front, and what's the one reply that has to land for the talk to survive? Tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
