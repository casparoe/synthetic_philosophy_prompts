I sit on the risk and compliance committee of a logistics company, and after a recent settlement over falsified safety inspections the rest of the board keeps saying "the company knew," "the company should apologize," and "the company has learned its lesson." The analogy doing the work is corporate personhood: the firm treated as a moral person with beliefs, intentions, and a character that can be blamed. I need to know how far that analogy actually holds before I vote on the remedial measures. Please produce a correspondence table with the following columns: (1) Feature of a natural person; (2) The corporate feature it is mapped onto; (3) Mapping strength (strong, partial, or weak); (4) What the analogy silently imports from the personal case that the corporate case may lack; and (5) Whether this failure matters for the conclusion that the company itself can be morally blamed. Cover at least intention, knowledge, memory, public speech, conscience or remorse, and susceptibility to punishment. Use the standard machinery where it helps, such as internal decision structures, distributed belief across records and roles, the absence of any corporate phenomenal consciousness, and the firm's persistence across personnel turnover. After the table, give a short JSON verdict with these fields: conclusion_survives (yes, no, or only_under_conditions), conditions (string, or null), most_damaging_disanalogy (string), illusory_disanalogy (string), and better_analogy (string, or null). The verdict should say plainly whether the analogy was carrying the blame-the-company conclusion, or whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
