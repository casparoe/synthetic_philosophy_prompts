I've been going down a rabbit hole on the problem in consequentialism that the EA-ish forums I read keep calling "cluelessness," and I'd like a sanity check on the fullest summary of it I've seen. The summary (from an essay in a general-audience magazine, by someone who seemed sympathetic-but-critical) went roughly like this. Consequentialism says do whatever has the best expected consequences. But for almost every real decision — which charity to donate to, whether to take the biosecurity job or the AI policy one, even whether to have a child — the effects ripple out into a absurdly complex future we can't begin to trace. Any small action could kick off a chain of consequences that matters enormously two hundred years from now. So the consequentialist is flying blind: there are no frequencies to extrapolate from, no model we actually trust, which means expected value is unavailable in principle for ordinary choices. The essay concluded that consequentialism therefore gives no guidance at all outside a few clean textbook cases, and that this is why serious consequentialists have mostly retreated to rule-utilitarianism; the only remaining alternatives are picking whatever a crude heuristic happens to say, or admitting that every option is equally justified and just choosing.

I get the feeling this is a mostly-fair version of something real, but also that it's papering over distinctions I don't have names for — like the gap between "we can't trace the net effects" and "expected value is literally unavailable," or between "the theory underdetermines choice" and "the theory gives zero guidance." Which parts of the summary are accurate, which are conflations or overstatements, and which of the "so what" conclusions actually follow from the accurate parts? What would a consequentialist who takes cluelessness seriously accept, and where would they say the essay is smuggling in a stronger claim than the problem supports? Please, where you can, separate what is pretty much settled from what is a live dispute among people working on this, and don't present one camp's take as the consensus.
