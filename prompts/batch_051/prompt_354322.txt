I'm writing a paper that tries to get Dennett's competence-without-comprehension framework to do real philosophical work, and I want to pressure-test it against a case that looks tailor-made for it: the match where the Go-playing system AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol. The way I understand the framework, Darwin and Turing each pulled a strange inversion of reasoning: you can get design-like outcomes from a mindless process (Darwin), and intelligent-looking behavior from a mechanism that doesn't understand what it's doing (Turing). I don't need a recap of the match or the architecture; I know the broad facts about the networks, the self-play, and the famous moves that human pros read as blunders and then had to walk back.

Here's what I want. First, keep three sites separate where competence without comprehension might be doing work: (1) the trained system at match time, as a Turing-style case of comprehension-free competence; (2) the training regime, as a Darwin-style selection process that accumulated competence nobody foresaw; and (3) the engineers who built and trained it, and the sense in which they did not comprehend what they'd built.

Then tell me where the lens starts to misdescribe things. My hunch is that the Darwinian and Turingian inversions get conflated in this case, and the slogan hides it: AlphaGo's training is selection with a goal baked in, which natural selection isn't, and its match-time non-comprehension is not the same as the absence of an understander in Darwin's story. Chase that down: where exactly does treating AlphaGo as competence without comprehension stop fitting, and what would have to be different about the system for the slogan to fit cleanly?

Finish by saying what the episode does to the theory. Does AlphaGo show that competence really is more fundamental than comprehension, or does it mostly show that competence without comprehension bundles two or three distinct phenomena that come apart in this case? Be disciplined: if there's a feature of the match the framework has nothing useful to say about, say so instead of forcing it to speak.
