In a reading group on reduction and emergence in science, someone stopped a discussion of whether an ant colony's ability to re-route its foraging trails is anything over and above thousands of ants following simple local rules by saying, "the whole is more than the sum of its parts," as if that settled the matter. I can tell the sentence is doing more than one job at once, and that it cannot be meant literally, since a whole just is its parts arranged somehow. I want a clean unpacking of the distinct theses that slogan could be used to assert — for example, an epistemic thesis about what a parts-level description lets one predict, a metaphysical thesis about organization or composition, a thesis about the failure of additive models rather than of reduction, or something less respectable.

Please lay out exactly five readings, as a numbered list with no introduction or conclusion, each a sentence or two. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false, and whether that particular reading would actually support the move from "the colony's behavior is produced by simple local rules" to "the colony's behavior is not just the ants." I am not after named positions or citations; just the distinctions, made cleanly.
