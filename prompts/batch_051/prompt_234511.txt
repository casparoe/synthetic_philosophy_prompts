I'm a science journalist, and I've spent the last few months reporting a piece on whether the old logic of nuclear deterrence still makes sense now that everyone's talking about AI in command-and-control and hypersonic missiles. Somewhere along the way I realized I hold a bunch of convictions about this stuff that I've never actually tested against each other, so I wrote them down pretty much as they came to me:

1. Nuclear deterrence mostly works. It's the best explanation for why there hasn't been a direct shooting war between the major powers since WWII.
2. A threat only deters if it's credible. If the other side thinks you won't actually do it when the moment comes, they'll call your bluff.
3. But if deterrence ever failed and an enemy city got destroyed, the last rational thing to do would be to launch a massive retaliatory strike that kills millions more people. At that point retaliation is just revenge that adds bodies without bringing anyone back, and the sane, moral call is to absorb the loss and try to stop the war.
4. I also think states can get around that problem by arranging things in advance so that retaliating isn't a fully free, fully rational choice in the moment. If you pre-delegate launch authority to regional commanders, or deliberately build in some genuine uncertainty about wether things will escalate automatically, the enemy can't be sure you'd hold back. That's the old Schelling-style move of making a threat credible by surrendering a little control.
5. But at the same time I think fully automating retaliation — a doomsday machine, a dead hand, whatever you want to call it — is morally horrendous and destabilizing, and that there should always be a human in the loop.
6. I also think every nuclear state should declare no-first-use.
7. And I think a leader with a reputation for being a bit erratic and unpredictable is, paradoxically, safer for deterrence, because their threats carry more weight.

I can feel that some of these are fighting each other, but I can't pin down which clashes are real and which just look that way. Could you audit the set? For each pair that's genuinely in tension, say how deep the tension goes and what the cheapest repair would be, sort the tensions by how much trouble they cause, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole thing hang together.
