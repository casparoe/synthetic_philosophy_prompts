Im a PhD student working on practical rationality, and this is for a side project on rational irrationality. Could you write me a parody of one of those American telly ads for a prescription drug — the cheery sort that ends with a rushed voiceover listing side effects over footage of someone flying a kite — for a drug that solves Parfit-style commitment problems. You know the setup: the hitchhiker in the desert can't get a lift because the driver knows a rational agent will promise to pay and then, once he's home and safe, have no reason to actually pay; so the hitchhiker takes a drug beforehand that makes him bindingly disposed to keep the promise, and only then will the driver stop for him. The advert should sell exactly that drug as an ordinary lifestyle product, with indications for prisoner's dilemmas, New Year's resolutions, being a reliable friend, that sort of thing. The target of the satire should be the self-optimisation / quantified-self habit of treating trust and commitment as engineering problems, not mental illness itself — please keep it that way. The jokes have to come from getting the rationality right: inducing your own irrationality can be instrumentally rational, because a promise is only credible if the disposition to keep it would survive even once the incentives have flipped. Dont let it slide into the drug just making people principled or moral — the point is that it makes you less rational in a useful way. The side-effects voiceover should quietly bring out the philosophical cost: an inability to update on new evidence, pathological promise-keeping, maybe an interplay with the toxin puzzle. Deadpan throughout, so a reader who's read Parfit laughs and someone who hasn't can still follow it. About 600 words, hard cap 700.
