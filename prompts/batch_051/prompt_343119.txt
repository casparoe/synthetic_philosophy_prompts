In Considerations on Representative Government, John Stuart Mill argues that a representative body should be less like a mirror of the electorate's existing opinions and more like a commission of the nation's best judgement, selected so that its collective intelligence and virtue exceed that of the average voter. I want that claim to anchor a broader question. Before I read any more defences of proportional representation or first-past-the-post, write the desiderata: what must an adequate theory of representation by election deliver? For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration, and say which requirements are genuine constraints and which are merely the prejudices of one side of the electoral-reform debate. Then say whether you think Mill's mirror-versus-commission distinction belongs among the non-negotiable desiderata or is itself a partisan assumption that a good theory should not take for granted. Finally, briefly, how proportional representation and first-past-the-post fare against the list. Please write in British English.
