The procreation asymmetry, roughly stated, holds that a potential child's future suffering counts against bringing the child into existence and the absence of such suffering counts in favor of never creating the child, whereas the child's future happiness does not count in favor and the absence of that happiness does not count against. A colleague claims this pattern is a robust feature of ethical judgment—a genuine evaluative asymmetry—rather than an artifact of question framing, presentation order, or independent beliefs about matters such as overpopulation, resources, or parental burden. Your task is to design the strongest empirical test you can of the colleague's claim. Specify the conditions and measures, state what pattern of results would favor the robust-asymmetry reading and what pattern would favor the artifact reading, and then identify precisely what even a strongly positive result would leave unsettled about the first-order ethical question of whether coming into existence can be good for a person. Write in American English, and end with a final line that is a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment, beginning with 'Summary:'.
