Here's a practice I keep coming back to, because I can't figure out what it's actually assuming about what a person is. When you sign up with a cryonics provider, you pick between neuro and whole-body. On the neuro plan, after legal death they remove and vitrify your brain (or just the head) and store that. On whole-body, they vitrify everything. Neuro is cheaper, and the companies present it as basicaly the same bet, on the reasoning that a future civilization will repair the freezing damage and either regrow a body around the revived brain or scan the preserved brain structure and run it on new hardware. The pitch throughout is that whoever shows up later is you, not a stand-in.

This looks to me like a commercial version of Dennett's brain-transplant puzzle: your brain sits in a vat, your body walks around elsewhere, and it's unclear where you are. The cryonics industry seems to answer: you are with the brain, or with whatever carries the brain's preserved organization, and then it prices the two plans as if that answer were obvious. But does the practice actually have to assume that? Does it strictly require the view that you are the information in the brain, and that copying the information into fresh substrate preserves the person? Or is it compatible with the view that you are a living organism, with neuro just being a wager about what future medicine will be able to grow from a brain?

And consider the scenario that really makes me unsure: if revival turns out to be scan-and-run only, and the future technicians run two copies of the preserved brain, what does the industry's usual view say — that both are you, neither is, or exactly one? If it has to say both, doesn't the original not-a-stand-in promise quietly change its meaning? Lay out the theory of personal identity the practice is implicitly committed to, separate the commitments the core promise strictly needs from the ones that are just marketing, and say how the practice would have to change — or what it would have to stop promising — if the correct theory turned out to be that you're the living organism, or that identity requires unbroken mental activity rather than preserved structure.
