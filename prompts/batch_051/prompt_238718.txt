I'm a novelist world-building a near-future country that charters its capital as a joint-stock corporation: the municipal government is replaced by a company, every adult resident gets exactly one share at founding, shares then trade freely, and the company holds ordinary city powers — zoning, policing, schools, sanitation, plus the civil courts that handle property and contract disputes. The charter requires the company to maximize shareholder value, but it owes every resident a fixed floor of services (sewage, clean water, fire, a basic court) and cannot restrict people moving in or out. Revenue comes from land rents the company collects and from an urban dividend paid out of the city budget surplus, to shareholders rather than residents as such. I want to get the moving parts right rather than just gesturing at a dystopia. Act as a political theorist who works on corporate sovereignty and charter companies, think carefully, and start by listing the main agents and what each wants. Then walk through how this thing would actually behave: who moves in, what gets built, how policing and schools go, how shareholders, tenants, and resident nonshareholders fare, and where it gets gamed or captured first. Dont give me a moral verdict, just the mechanics and the failure modes.
