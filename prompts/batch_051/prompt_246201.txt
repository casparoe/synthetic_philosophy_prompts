I spent my career in the natural sciences, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of language while experimenting with vector-space models of meaning. I have accumulated a set of judgments about when I am willing to treat two expressions as close in meaning, and they do not form an obvious system. I would like to know whether a principled account can be given of them, or which of them I should abandon.

My verdicts are these:

1. The proximity of "dog" and "canine" in embedding space reflects a genuine near-synonymy.
2. "King" and "queen" are not close in embedding space, and I do not call them close in meaning; yet I accept that king − man + woman ≈ queen captures something real about lexical structure. Similarity and vector arithmetic seem to track different things, and I want to keep both claims.
3. "Good" and "bad" are often close in embedding space, but I will not call them close in meaning; they are opposites, not synonyms.
4. A static embedding gives "bank" a single vector although the word has at least two meanings; I do not read that single vector as the claim that a river bank and a financial bank mean the same.
5. If one averages embeddings, "The dog bit the man" and "The man bit the dog" come out similar; I regard them as meaning something importantly different.
6. A contextual model gives "bank" different vectors in "the river bank" and "the central bank." I count that as progress, but I do not think it shows that word meaning is nothing over and above context, since I still recognize one lexical item across the two occurrences.
7. Embeddings place "doctor" closer to "nurse" in a way that also tracks gender in the training corpus; I am reluctant to call that semantic structure rather than a record of usage.

Is there a single conception of what embedding geometry represents—similarity, analogy, distributional substitutability, something else—that renders these verdicts coherent? Which verdicts would such a conception force me to revise, and what would it say about cases I have not listed, such as "buy" and "sell," "red" and "color," or a rare technical term from my own field?
