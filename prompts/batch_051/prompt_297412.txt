Write a short story that dramatizes this question: is it fair to bind people to rules they cannot know? Make the law in the story unknowable in a stronger sense than mere obscurity — it should be structured so that no amount of diligence, good faith, or expert advice would let a person learn in advance which acts are permitted. You might, for instance, imagine laws whose operative interpretations are released only after a violation is charged, or a rule that can be applied only by an institution that refuses to give prospective guidance. Center the story on one person weighing whether to act under such a system, and dramatize, without authorial preaching, both the state's reason for keeping the law opaque and the person's experience of being bound by an unknowable rule. Leave the reader genuinely uncertain about whether the character's eventual punishment is an injustice or a defensible cost of living under law.
