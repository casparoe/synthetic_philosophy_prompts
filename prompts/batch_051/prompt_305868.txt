In my political philosophy course this term we have been working through normative theories of secession, and this week we contrasted two families of views before turning to the Catalan crisis of 2017. I would like a careful analysis of that episode through each theory, and then an analysis of what the episode does back to the theories.

The two positions, as we have framed them. Remedial-right-only theories hold that a group has a moral right to secede only as a remedy for serious and persistent injustice, such as the unjust incorporation of its territory or systematic discrimination by the state; on this view, a majority vote alone cannot create the right. Primary-right theories hold that a right to secede can arise directly from a group's self-determination, whether that is understood plebiscitarily as majority consent or nationally as the claim of a distinct people to govern itself; on these views, a free and fair vote can be sufficient even in the absence of oppression.

The salient facts of the case, as I understand them. On 1 October 2017, the Catalan government held an independence referendum that Spain's Constitutional Court had already declared illegal and suspended. Spanish national police and Civil Guard attempted to close polling stations and seize ballots, sometimes violently. Roughly 43 percent of registered voters turned out, and more than 90 percent of those who voted supported independence, with most unionist parties boycotting the poll. On 10 October, the Catalan president signed a declaration of independence but immediately suspended its effects in order to allow dialogue; on 27 October, the Catalan parliament ratified the declaration. That same day, the Spanish government invoked Article 155 of the Constitution, dismissed the Catalan executive, and called new regional elections. Catalan leaders were later prosecuted, and several went into exile.

Could you analyze this episode through each theory, and then use the episode to test the theories in return? In particular:

(a) Map the dispute in each theory's vocabulary. Who is the relevant self whose rights are at stake: all residents of Catalonia, the Catalan nation, or the Spanish citizenry as a whole? What are the relevant acts and options, and what counts as evidence of injustice or of a genuine self-determination claim? Should the case be framed as unilateral secession at all, or as a failed attempt at negotiated secession?

(b) Say what each theory licenses and forbids. Did the Catalan government have a moral right to declare independence? Was Madrid justified in refusing to authorize a binding referendum, imposing direct rule, and prosecuting those responsible?

(c) Say where each theory fits the episode well and where it is silent or forced. For example, the remedial-right-only view has to determine whether Catalonia had suffered the right kind of injustice, while primary-right views have to explain how they can accept a unilateral vote held in defiance of a constitutional court, with low turnout and a boycott by one side, without licensing instability whenever a bare majority forms.

(d) Finally, say what the episode does to each theory: is Catalonia a confirming case, an anomaly, or simply a hard case the theories were never designed to handle? Does the case narrow the apparent disagreement between the two positions, for instance because both might converge on expanded autonomy within Spain but for different reasons? And does the fact that the referendum was unlawful under the Spanish Constitution carry independent moral weight for either theory?
