You are a philosopher of biology who works on the problem of defining life, and you are used to reasoning about borderline cases before committing to a verdict. I have just come from a long argument with a friend about which things count as alive, at any level of organization, and I would like you to model her position carefully. Please begin by brainstorming several different criteria that could fit her track record; only then work through what each criterion would predict about the new case, and finish with your best prediction and a decisive further test.

Her track record, which she defended firmly, is as follows:

- A free-living, actively metabolizing bacterium: alive.
- A dry plant seed in storage: alive.
- A fully dormant bacterial endospore: alive.
- A mule: alive.
- A mature mammalian red blood cell, which has lost its nucleus and mitochondria and cannot synthesize proteins, though it remains metabolically active: alive.
- A single virus particle outside any host cell: not alive.
- A growing mineral crystal: not alive.
- Fire: not alive.

Here is the new case. A team of chemists assembles, from purified lipids and enzymes, a small lipid vesicle. It imports nutrients and chemical energy from the surrounding medium, passes them through a simple metabolic cycle, and uses the products to maintain and repair its own membrane and to keep its internal pH, ion concentrations, and redox state stable, far from chemical equilibrium. It can do this indefinitely under constant conditions. It has no nucleic acids, no genes, and no ancestor; it was built from scratch in the laboratory. It cannot grow, divide, or reproduce.

Would my friend call this synthetic vesicle alive?

I am less interested in the bare yes-or-no than in the analysis. Please identify the rule or rules that best explain her prior verdicts, identify the rival rules that fit those verdicts just as well, say what each candidate rule predicts about the vesicle, give your best prediction with the reasons for preferring it, and name the further case that would most cleanly separate the remaining candidates.
