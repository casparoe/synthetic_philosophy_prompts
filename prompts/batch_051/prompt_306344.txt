I help produce a small podcast about decision-making under uncertainty for engineers who build trading, planning, and resource-allocation systems. We have booked an independent researcher who defends geometric rationality: the thesis that a decision maker choosing among gambles should be scored by expected log wealth — the long-run growth rate, the quantity behind the Kelly rule — rather than by expected wealth, and that this one criterion does most of the work normally handed to an arbitrary risk-preference function. I want the recorded conversation to stress-test the position rather than summarize it.

Write eight to ten interview questions for this guest. Each question should come with a one-sentence follow-up aimed at the reply she is most likely to give. Make sure the set contains at least one exchange on each of these fault lines: (1) the uniqueness problem — why the logarithm is the one correct concave transform instead of just one conservative utility function among many; (2) the one-shot problem — why a criterion justified by long-run multiplicative growth should govern a decision an agent makes only once; (3) estimation error — what the rule dictates when the probabilities fed into it are themselves uncertain; and (4) the relationship to ordinary expected-utility theory with log utility of wealth, including what geometric rationality adds beyond it. Order the questions so later ones build on the earlier answers, and close by asking the guest to name a realistic class of decisions where growth-rate maximization is the wrong tool and what that admission would do to her view.
