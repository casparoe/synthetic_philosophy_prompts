I am preparing a handout for an upper-level undergraduate aesthetics course that will sort students into the theory of art they actually hold, not the one they would self-report. Please write a seven-question decision tree whose leaves are the following positions: a perceptual or aesthetic definition of art; an expression definition; an institutional definition; a historical definition; a cluster account; and an open-concept or no-definition view. Each branch node should be a concrete case or a paired contrast, phrased so that a student can answer it without recognizing the theory at stake, and the tree must use, at some point, Duchamp's Fountain, a child's crayon drawing kept at home, a technically perfect forgery of a Vermeer, and a piece of driftwood placed on a gallery plinth. Construct the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from the list of labels: let the questions and their combinations come first, then locate the six positions within that space. Finally, flag any combination of answers that no standard position occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of why (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate, giving the further case that would distinguish them.
