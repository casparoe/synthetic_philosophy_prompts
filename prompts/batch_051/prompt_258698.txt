I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, so I might be missing an obvious term. I work on a tool that's meant to help teams make design decisions through discussion instead of rank, and I keep running into the same thing. We scrub out all the explicit power stuff—no names, no titles, no votes, arguments are supposed to stand on their own. And what happens is backwards. Instead of the best-reasoned position winning, the conversation gets captured by whoever can sound like the best-reasoned position. The person who writes four paragraphs without effort, uses the right jargon, stays calm and a little condescending—they win, and then the group says "well, they had the strongest arguement." I've been in enough of these threads now that I genuinely cant tell when I'm agreeing with somebody because they're right versus because they're fluent.

We also ran a side test where we took the same argument and rewrote it two ways, one hedging and disorganized, the other confident and smooth, and people rated the smooth version as better-reasoned even though the reasons themselves were identical. So it's not that the articulate people happen to be right more often. The polish is doing the work, and everyone, me included, credits the polish to the logic.

I know there's a corner of philosophy about what a conversation would need to look like for the better argument to win on its own without power doing the work; I don't trust my memory of the label. What I'm actually after is a name for the specific failure mode where you strip away explicit power and a different kind of power—fluency, confidence, vocabulary—rushes in and gets mistaken for reasonableness. Does that have a name? If the closest existing concepts are just "rhetoric" or "bias," I want to know what separates what I'm describing from those, because it feels more like a structural advantage for a particular kind of person than an ordinary error of judgment. And if there's no name, what would you call it, and what's a clean boundary case where the confident, smooth version is genuinely the better-reasoned one rather than just the better-dressed one?
