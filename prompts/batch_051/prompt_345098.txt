I'm a PhD student working on personal identity, and I keep being struck by how stuck the standard debate has become. The disagreement between advocates of a psychological continuity criterion and animalists has been running in roughly its present form for decades. On one side, Parfit and his heirs hold that what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, not identity in the strict, one-to-one, non-branching sense, and they treat the teletransporter case (my body is scanned and destroyed while a psychologically continuous replica is produced elsewhere) as showing that the psychological thread is what I should care about. On the other side, animalists hold that I am an organism, that the teletransporter kills that organism, and therefore that the replica is not me in any survival-relevant sense. The striking thing is that both sides use the same cases, teletransportation and hemisphere-transfer fission, and both claim those cases cut in their favor: Parfit treats fission as a case in which I survive through both branches even though neither resulting person is identical with me, while animalists treat it as the destruction of the original human animal, not as a person branching. The impasse does not look like a shortage of arguments; it looks like the two sides are doing different things with the same vivid cases.

My working hypothesis is that the two sides are not really answering one descriptive question about what personal identity consists in, but two questions that have been tacitly fused: a descriptive question about the persistence conditions of persons or organisms, and a normative question about what a person should rationally regard as mattering in their own future. Is that the right diagnosis? Or is the impasse better described as a substantive disagreement about the persistence of human animals, as a difference in starting intuitions about which cases count as data and which as science-fiction artifacts, as a dispute about whether 'I' is ambiguous, or as something else? Diagnose the structure of this disagreement rather than adjudicating it. I do not want a survey of positions; I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, one that engages with what each side actually says about the teletransporter and about fission, and a candid answer about whether my hypothesis captures only part of the impasse.

3. What would count as progress (about 150 words): given the diagnosis, identify the specific move — a distinction drawn, a question reformulated, a burden shifted, a case constructed — most likely to break or reframe the impasse, or argue that no such move should be expected.
