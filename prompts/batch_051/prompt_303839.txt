so i was reading the beginning of book 7 of euclid's Elements for a project and found this definition, which I havent been able to shake for days:

"A unit is that by virtue of which each of the things that exist is called one."

and then euclid's next definition says "A number is a multitude composed of units." so on this picture the number 1 is not exactly a number—one is the unit, and numbers are built out of copies of it. it sounds obvious until you try to actually apply it to something that isnt a pile of pebbles, and then it gets weird fast.

I want a working definition of "unit"—not necessarily what Euclid had in mind historically, but what the word should mean if its going to do that job—that handles these cases:

- one apple: clear yes for me
- the number 1 itself: i always think of 1 as a number, but on euclid's setup it seems to sit on the other side of the definition. is the number 1 a unit, or is it built out of units?
- zero: i'd say no
- a single point in geometry: no idea. a point has no parts, but it still seems to be one of something
- one second of time: it's a unit in the measurement sense, but a second isnt a thing that exists the way an apple is
- a flock of birds: is the flock one unit, or many units temporarily treated as one?
- half an apple after you cut it: before the cut it was one unit, after the cut each half feels like one unit, which makes "unit" seem like it depends on how we divide stuff rather than on the thing itself

Give me your definition and actually test it on each case. Tell me which case it handles worst or has no answer for, and if it forces you to say something weird about one of my clear cases (like an apple isnt really a unit), either fix the definition or defend the weird answer.
