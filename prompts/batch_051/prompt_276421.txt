I run a juried photo contest and our eligibilty rules still carry this sentence from the 1990s: "An entry must be a photograph, meaning an image produced by the action of light on a light-sensitive surface, and must be a transparent record of the scene it depicts; no entry may be a composite of two or more distinct photographs." One entrant submits a phone night-mode shot, which the camera makes by firing a rapid burst of underexposed frames, aligning them, and merging them into one cleaner image. Another entrant submits a documentary street photo from which she removed a trash can with the phone's magic eraser, leaving the rest exactly as shot. Are either or both disqualified? Argue it the way a 1990s contest organizer would have read the rule, the way a purpose-minded reader would read it now, and then say which reading should control. Also: does calling a photograph "transparent" in the philosopher's sense—that we literally see the scene through the picture—help settle either case, or is it just a distraction?
