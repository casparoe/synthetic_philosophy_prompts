In a seminar I have been following on long-run predictions, a dispute about Malthus has stalled so completely that I have become more interested in the structure of the disagreement than in who is right. One side holds that Malthus has been empirically refuted: since his original argument, world population has multiplied several times over while per capita income, life expectancy, and nutrition have risen; the demographic transition broke the population mechanism he posited; and repeated technological substitutions have kept postponing any binding resource limit. The other side holds that Malthus was right but early: the past two centuries drew down a series of one-time or bounded escape hatches — fossil energy, the Green Revolution, the fertility transition — and the underlying dynamic of exponential growth pressing against a finite biosphere will eventually reassert itself. The argument does not move because both sides treat the same historical record as support. When the pessimists' deadlines pass, they re-date the limit, and the optimists call this ad hoc rescue; when the optimists cite two hundred years of rising incomes, the pessimists call it confirmation of postponement rather than disproof.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? I want a diagnosis of the dispute rather than a verdict on Malthus. Is there a hidden disagreement about the content of the claim — whether "Malthus was right" is a prediction about timing, a claim about a mechanism, or a conditional whose antecedent simply has not been met yet? Is it a disagreement about standards of evidence for long-run predictions — about when re-dating an auxiliary assumption counts as legitimate updating versus the defensiveness of a degenerating research programme, and about whether a claim with an indefinitely receding horizon can be falsified by any finite sequence of observations at all? Or is it a difference in bedrock background views about technology, ecology, and human institutions that the historical record cannot settle? My own suspicion is that the two sides are partly answering different questions under one name, but I would like that hypothesis tested. Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction, a formal criterion for legitimate postponement, a restated question, or an argument that no resolution should be expected?
