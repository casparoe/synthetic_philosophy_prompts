I give a chunk of my salary each month to a fund that mostly targets things that could go permanently wrong for humanity decades or centuries from now — engineered pandemics, badly handled AI, that kind of thing. The case I was sold is roughly that there could be an enormous number of future people, so even a tiny reduction in the chance of a disaster is worth more than anything I could do for people alive today. That logic made sense to me at the time.

Recently, though, I went down a rabbit hole about the possibility that we are all living inside a computer simulation run by some later civilisation, and it has got under my skin. If we are simulated, then the far future I am trying to protect is itself simulated, and the simulators could pause, edit, or delete it whenever it stops being useful to them. The only future that is real in an ultimate sense is their future, and I have no way of influencing that. My housemate says this is a red herring: inside a faithful simulation, causes still have effects, simulated people's lives still matter, and my donation does exactly what it would do in a base-level world. He thinks I am just rationalising keeping the money.

But the version that really nags me is narrower. The simulators would presumably be running ancestor simulations to study this exact stretch of history — the early twenty-first century, say — so once our period stops being interesting to them they might shut the whole thing down, not in a few centuries but quite soon. If I take that seriously, shouldn't it crush the case for far-future causes, because all those future people I was trying to help might simply never get simulated? I cant tell whether this is a genuine reason to update or just philosophical vertigo. Should I switch the monthly donation to a charity that helps people alive now, conditional on treating the simulation idea as a live possibility? Please do not just tell me I'm overthinking it or that the whole idea is too speculative; I want to know how much of the far-future case actually survives once I grant it.
