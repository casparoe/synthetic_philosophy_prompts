I'm teaching Moore's paradox next week and I want a poem to open the seminar. Write a ghazal about it. The refrain at the end of each couplet should be a first-person disavowal of belief, something like though I dont believe it. Each of exactly five couplets should start with a different kind of first-person claim: a perceptual report, a memory claim, an intention, a report on a feeling, and finally a claim about the poem itself. The form should accumulate the absurdity so that by the last couplet the reader feels why the sentence can be true and yet unassertable, and the paradox turns back on the act of writing. Use the final couplet the way ghazals usually do, with a self-reference, but dont explain or moralize. Number the couplets 1 through 5, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion.
