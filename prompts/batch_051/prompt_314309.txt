I am a high school student in a civics course, and we have to write a short paper on a contemporary defense policy question. I have chosen the tension between conscription, professional armies, and the model of armed neutrality used by Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland. I have been reading Adam Smith’s The Wealth of Nations, Book V, Chapter I. To paraphrase his argument: as societies advance and the division of labor extends to every trade, the art of war becomes too complex for a part-time militia to exercise well; a standing army of full-time professionals will therefore outperform a militia in discipline and skill. Yet Smith also grants that a standing army is a permanent body of armed men under the government’s command, and the same development that makes it necessary also makes the civilian population less warlike. My question is not about what Smith meant, but whether his analysis forces a dilemma on a small neutral state. If such a state chooses a professional army, it gains military effectiveness but may lose the civic habits of self-defense and caution about war; if it keeps a citizen militia, it preserves those habits but may be unable to defend itself. Is this a genuine dilemma, or does armed neutrality itself dissolve it by making large-scale offensive war less likely? I would like help reasoning through which side of Smith’s argument should carry more weight for a country that, unlike a great power, is trying never to fight offensive wars.
