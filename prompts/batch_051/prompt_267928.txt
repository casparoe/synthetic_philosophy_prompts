for a reading group I'm trying to write down my actual position on whether personal data is property, and I'd like a consistency check before I present. here's where I've landed:

1. I think data about you — location history, health metrics, browsing logs — is yours in a roughly property-like way, the way your car is yours.
2. I'm drawn to a Lockean story for this: you own your data because you produce it through your activity, mixing your labor with the world.
3. But I also think property rights are basically a solution to conflicts over scarce, rival resources, and that logic doesn't transfer to information, since your having a copy doesn't stop me having one.
4. I don't think anyone can own bare facts; 'Alice visited a cardiologist on Tuesday' is just true, and truths can't be property.
5. I think it should be fine to sell some of your data to companies for money or a cheaper service.
6. I think most of the real harm in data misuse is privacy harm — manipulation, exposure, unfair scoring — not theft of a thing.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
