My essay idea is built around Ryle's diagnosis of Cartesian dualism as a category mistake. My working analogy is that the Cartesian picture of the mind is like a type error. Here are my notes:

- Ryle's target is the picture of a human being as a body plus a non-physical occupant, a ghost in the machine. The problem with this picture, as I understand him, is not that it asserts a false fact that could be corrected by better evidence. It is that the picture misuses mental vocabulary as though it belonged to the same logical type as vocabulary for physical causes and mechanisms.
- I want to explain this to other programmers by saying that talk of the mind as a non-physical thing behind behavior is syntactically valid but ill-typed. The Cartesian problem is not like a null-pointer error or an unhandled exception somewhere in running code; it is like an expression whose type mismatch stops it from being either true or false. This is why no amount of searching for the hidden mental thing could settle the matter: the search has been written against the wrong type.
- I then extend the analogy to Ryle's positive account. Mental terms such as believing, intending, or being vain should be read largely as dispositional, as characterizing patterns in what someone would do, say, or notice across circumstances, rather than naming goings-on inside a private inner theater. The software counterpart would be describing a system's behavior over time through its interface rather than postulating unobservable internal states.
- My worry: a type error normally renders an expression meaningless, but Ryle presumably does not think ordinary talk about beliefs and intentions is meaningless; he thinks philosophers have mislocated its logic. I also worry that the dispositional reading makes Ryle sound like a crude behaviorist. The essay should avoid that, but I am not sure how much hedging is needed.
