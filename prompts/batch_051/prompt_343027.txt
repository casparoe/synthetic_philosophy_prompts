I co-facilitate a small reading group on panpsychism, and after three sessions on the combination problem I have noticed that the same members change their position depending on whether we are talking about electrons, human minds, or the cosmos as a whole. Before next week's meeting I want to distribute a short self-diagnostic that sorts people by what their answers to concrete cases commit them to, rather than by which label they arrived with. Could you write a six-to-eight-question quiz, along with a scoring key, under the heading Which Account of the Combination Problem Are You Actually Committed To?

Each question should present a concrete case or a claim that can be assessed without background in the literature. The sorts of things I want the questions to probe include: whether there is something it is like to be an electron; whether a person's experience is fully constituted by facts about their smallest parts and how those parts are arranged; whether arranging micro-subjects differently could, by itself, yield one larger subject; whether it is more natural to start with the cosmos as the single fundamental subject and treat ordinary minds as derivative aspects of it; and whether the intrinsic nature of matter might be less than fully conscious, becoming consciousness only when structured in the right way. Please refrain from any question that simply asks respondents whether they accept a named position.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these four leaves: constitutive micropsychism (macro-level experience is wholly grounded in micro-level subjects and their arrangement), emergentist panpsychism (micro-level subjects exist, but macro-level experience strongly emerges from them), panprotopsychism (the intrinsic natures of physical things are proto-phenomenal rather than subjects, and full experience appears only under the right organization), and priority cosmopsychism (the cosmos is the fundamental subject, and ordinary minds are derivative aspects of it). If the logical space your questions generate contains a fifth or sixth coherent leaf — a neutral monist position or an illusionist/no-micro-subject position, for instance — include it. Where some combination of answers has no leaf, the key should say briefly whether nobody occupies it because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. In particular, make sure the quiz does not blur constitutive and emergentist panpsychism, and does not collapse priority cosmopsychism into neutral monism just because both deny that electrons are subjects.
