I want a poem that actually performs satisficing instead of just talking about it. Write a Petrarchan-style sonnet, volta at line nine, from the point of view of someone deciding something concrete — which city to move to, who to marry, what to do with the next ten years, pick one. The octave should be metrically and rhythmically impecable and should show the search itself: the options encountered, the cost of continuing to look, and an explicit aspiration level. At the volta, the speaker settles on an option that is good enough rather than best, and from there the rhymes should get progressively worse — slant first, then eye rhymes, then forced and ugly — while the meter stays mostly intact, so the form enacts the stopping rule and the lowering of standards. The closing couplet should still feel satisfying on its own terms, which is the real argument: make "good enough" feel earned. Someone who knows bounded rationality should recognise the satisficing machinery doing the structural work, and someone who doesn't should still get a good poem.
