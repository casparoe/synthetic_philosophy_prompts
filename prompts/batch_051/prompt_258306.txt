Having spent my career in the natural sciences, I tend to assess a thesis by asking what would count against it, so I want to subject a view I have recently come to hold in epistemology to exactly that treatment. The view is the conjunction of two claims. First, no non-trivial condition is luminous: for any condition that can be gained or lost gradually, it is not the case that whenever the condition obtains, one is automatically in a position to know that it obtains. Second, because knowing itself is such a non-trivial condition, the KK principle fails at least in its weakened form: one can know that p without being in a position to know that one knows that p.

I find the anti-luminosity argument persuasive because it has the structure of a sorites argument, which I recognize from the physical sciences: continuity in the underlying facts, combined with a margin-for-error requirement on reliable discrimination, undermines the idea of a sharp and knowable boundary. But I also know from my scientific work that a model which appears to forbid sharp boundaries can turn out to be an artifact of how the variables were defined. So I want an honest map of the position's exposure.

What would have to be true for the view to be false, or for its philosophical interest to collapse? I want the defeaters distinguished by weight: which developments would be decisive, and which would be merely awkward? Which rival accounts of the formal apparatus, the cases, or the logic of iteration should I take seriously, and what would preserving KK, or luminosity for some non-trivial conditions, actually cost? I am more likely to be moved by formal results in epistemic logic or margin-for-error semantics and by carefully constructed cases than by appeals to introspective obviousness or to the practical inconveniences of abandoning KK. I would also like to know what a committed defender of the anti-luminosity view would themselves concede counts against it, not merely what its critics allege. Finally, tell me whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and, if it has, whether that is a defect or an honest reflection of what a philosophical thesis of this kind is. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
