I keep noticing a parallel and I honestly can’t tell if it’s deep or just a loose analogy. Darwin, in On the Origin of Species, Chapter IV, writes:

"It may be said that natural selection is daily and hourly scrutinising, throughout the world, every variation, even the slightest; rejecting that which is bad, preserving and adding up all that is good; silently and insensibly working, whenever and wherever opportunity offers, at the improvement of each organic being in relation to its organic and inorganic conditions of life."

That sounds to me uncannily like stochastic gradient descent: evaluate small local modifications, keep the ones that lower error, reject the rest, no foresight, no global plan, no part of the system understanding what it is doing. Both are cases of what Daniel Dennett has called competence without comprehension, and both seem to invert the usual order of explanation — in Turing’s version, you get intelligent-looking behavior without an intelligent designer; in Darwin’s, you get adaptive complexity without a comprehending designer. But Darwin is clearly using “scrutinising” metaphorically, whereas gradient descent is literally a mindless procedure. Is this a real structural analogy — variation plus selection plus local hill-climbing under an evaluation function — or just a shared vocabulary? What would need to be true for insights to transfer from one to the other, and what’s something the parallel predicts that turns out false?
