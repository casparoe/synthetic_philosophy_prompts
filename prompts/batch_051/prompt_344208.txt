Rather than polishing it, I've just got notes, and I want to know weather the central idea is worth developing, whether it's accurate to the debate, and where a specialist would push back.

Here's the idea. Everyone uses holism about reasons as the engine of particularism: the fact that a reason can switch valence between contexts is supposed to show that there can't be a finite set of principles that tells you what to do. But I want to argue that this is a bit of a red herring, or at least that holism cuts both ways. Generalists can perfectly well admit that reasons are context-sensitive; what they need is not that each reason has a fixed atomistic valence, but that the *function* from contexts to valences is itself principled — i.e., that the defeaters and enablers that flip a reason's valence are the sorts of things that can be captured in codified form. So the real disagreement is over whether the defeat/disable/enable structure of reasons is systematically expressible. Particularism then becomes the claim that there are brute, uncodifiable valence-flippers — that at some point the best explanation of why a consideration counts the way it does just is 'look at this case'.

So my essay would try to do three things. First, reframe the debate as a debate about second-order structure (defeaters and enablers) rather than about first-order valence. Second, argue that once you do that, a lot of the standard particularist examples stop favouring particularism, because generalism can happily grant them. Third, raise what I think is the harder problem for the generalist: not whether there are principles, but whether the second-order principles will end up horribly gerrymandered, and gerrymandered in a way that looks like particularism in all but name.

Does this land? Specifically: (a) am I being fair to the generalist, or does the standard generalist position already concede the second-order-codification point and I've just moved the goalposts? (b) Is the distinction between first-order valence and second-order defeat/enable structure doing real work, or does it just smuggle the holism back in at a different level? (c) Which part of the proposal would a card-carrying particularist most object to — and how would you sharpen that objection before I embarrass myself?
