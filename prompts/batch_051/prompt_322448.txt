You are a professional historian of political thought who also follows debates in quantitative peace research. Write a satirical op-ed of roughly 700 words in the voice of a fictional think-tank researcher named Dr. Alistair Vane, who argues that the claim that the world has become less violent is a methodological trick rather than a discovery.

Vane's argument should turn on a specific passage in Thomas Hobbes's Leviathan, chapter 13. In that passage Hobbes says, in substance, that war is not limited to actual battles or fighting; it also includes the known disposition to fight, and it lasts for as long as there is no assurance to the contrary. Vane takes this definition literally and insists that by Hobbes's standard the post-1945 long peace is itself a condition of war: the Cold War is the obvious case, but so too, he will say, are standing nuclear arsenals, permanent surveillance, armed police in ordinary neighborhoods, and the fact that governments still plan for war every day. On this reading, the decline-of-violence literature merely relabels permanent war as peace by narrowing the definition of violence.

The satire should land the standard objections without ever stating them in the narrator's own voice: that Vane stretches a definition until it dissolves the very phenomenon it purports to measure; that he conflates the risk of violence with the act; that he treats every precaution against violence as evidence of violence; and that if war is everywhere, then no policy or institution can ever be said to reduce it. Include at least one indignant response from a fictional co-worker who accuses Vane of being constitutionally unable to accept good news. Stay deadpan throughout; do not wink, but a reader should be able to tell what is being ironized.

Before writing the piece, spend a short brainstorming section listing the specific moves in the violence-decline debate you intend to exaggerate. Then write the op-ed.
