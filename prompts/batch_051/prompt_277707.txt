You are marking an essay question in an undergraduate philosophy of action course. Below are the question and one student's answer.

Question: Explain the problem that Buridan's ass is supposed to pose for the view that a rational choice between two alternatives must be based on a reason that favours one alternative over the other. Then evaluate whether a choice made in the absence of such a preference can be anything other than a random event.

Student answer: In the example, a hungry donkey stands exactly midway between two identical bales of hay. The donkey plainly has a reason to eat, namely hunger, but it has no reason to take one bale rather than the other. If rationality requires a reason that favours one option over its alternative, then the donkey cannot choose either bale, and so it starves. Since a rational creature would not starve in these circumstances, rational agency must include a capacity to choose in the absence of a preference. The natural account is that the will is a tie-breaking faculty: when the intellect represents the two options as equal, the will simply selects one. The selection is not caused by any feature of the bales, since by hypothesis they are indistinguishable; it issues from the will's own power of selection. This also shows that the selection is not merely random. Random events, properly speaking, are not exercises of agency, whereas the will's selection is an exercise of agency by definition. Moreover, a free act is simply an act that is not determined by reasons. Since the will's selection is not determined by reasons, it is free; and what is free cannot be merely random.

Grade the answer. Provide (a) a numerical mark out of 10 and (b) point-by-point comments that justify the mark. In the comments, work through the student's main claims in the order they appear, saying whether each is correct, partially correct, or incorrect, and identify any inference that is unsupported or that assumes what is at issue.
