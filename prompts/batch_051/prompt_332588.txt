Someone in a group I'm in proposed a thing for a large online peer-support forum and I keep turning it round in my head. When you join you pick up to two condition tags, like anxiety, low mood, executive dysfunction, that kind of thing. Every quarter the platform rewrites the official description of each tag by surveying the people currently carrying it: they rate a batch of statements about themselves, and the new description becomes a sort of average profile of whoever has the tag that quarter. The descriptions get shown everywhere the tag appears, and you can switch tags any time you like, which changes who ends up in next quarter's sample. The person proposing it says this makes the categories honest because they're owned by the people inside them rather than by clinicians. I'm not that interested in whether it's a good idea; I want to know what it would actually do. Walk me through the incentives. Would people herd toward the tags whose current descriptions look best or least stigmatising rather than the ones that fit them? What happens when a tag's description starts to include a load of people who were themselves drawn in by the description — does the whole thing chase it's own tail? Where does a loop like that settle, if it settles at all? Where do you expect it to get gamed first, and would any of the fixes, like freezing old descriptions, weighting long-standing members' answers, or vetting proposed edits, stop the gaming without turning the whole thing back into top-down classification? The bit I find spooky is that in ordinary psychiatric classification the categories are suppose to be revised slowly by experts, but here the labelled people rewrite the label and the rewritten label changes the labelled people in a few months. Is that a real instance of the looping effect people talk about in philosophy of psychiatry, or is it just a survey artefact?
