A friend of mine has built an argument that seems to show the standard information-theoretic exorcism of Maxwell's demon proves too much, and I can't find the exit. (1) The standard story is that the demon gets to lower the gas's entropy only because it acquires information about individual molecules, and that information has to be paid for: to reset its memory for the next cycle it must erase at least one bit per sorting decision, and erasing a bit at temperature T dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat. That heats the environment enough to repay the entropy reduction, with interest, so the second law is safe. (2) On this story, the thing that stops a Maxwell demon violating the second law is specifically the erasure of its record. (3) Now take a one-shot demon that does exactly one sorting cycle — fast molecules one way, slow molecules the other, so the gas ends up with a hot side and a cold side — and is then destroyed along with its memory, without the record ever being erased. By (1) and (2), it pays nothing, because it never forgets. (4) Yet it has lowered the entropy of a gas that began at a single uniform temperature, and nothing else in the world has had its entropy raised to compensate. (5) That is just a second-law violation. (6) So the standard exorcist is stuck: either the second law only forbids entropy lowering by systems that plan to erase their memories later, which is a loophole big enough to drive a refrigerated lorry through, or the one-shot demon somehow doesnt count, which looks ad hoc. (7) So the standard exorcism has an absurd consequence and should be abandoned. I'd rather keep Landauer's principle, or at least the kT ln 2 floor, than give the one-shot demon its victory lap, but I can't see which step to reject or what rejecting it costs. Where does this go wrong, if it does? Please answer in British English (spelling and the rest).
