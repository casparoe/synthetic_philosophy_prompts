Compare two ways of deciding whether two answers from a deployed model come from the same underlying mechanism. One view says the output is what matters: if the model keeps behaving in its normal range on the relevant test distribution, the internals are a black box and you shouldn't care that the path was different. The other view says the internals are the point: the same output can be computed by very different circuits or features, and a novel internal path is exactly what an anomaly detector should flag before anything weird shows up in the outputs. If you were actually building a mechanistic anomaly detector, which criterion would you mainly rely on, and what would you be systematically blind to wichever way you went?
