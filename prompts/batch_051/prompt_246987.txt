I'm drafting a position statement for a working group on moral status across species, and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. I find myself committed to all of the following: (1) moral considerability and its weight track psychological capacities such as self-awareness, long-term planning, and felt vulnerability, not species membership; (2) across species, absolute brain size is a reasonable first-pass proxy for those capacities; (3) where a proxy is known to be noisy, one should fall back on more direct behavioral and neurobiological evidence rather than the proxy; (4) the neural quantity that should matter for moral status is the number of neurons in relevant cortical or pallial structures, not raw brain volume; (5) some small-brained birds, such as corvids and parrots, warrant more moral consideration than many larger-brained mammals; and (6) a whale matters more than a mouse, and its vastly larger brain is part of what makes me confident of that. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension with one another, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that would make the package coherent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious.
