I have been trying to think about what makes a group of people a state, and whether being a state is like being a bachelor — something fixed by the features — or like being a friend — something that partly depends on how others treat you.

The features I keep coming back to are: (1) a fairly permanent population; (2) a defined territory; (3) a government that makes and enforces law inside that territory; and (4) the capacity to enter into relations with other states — receive an ambassador, sign a treaty, join an international body. I am torn about whether to add a fifth: (5) being acknowledged by other states.

Case B is a country that every other state acknowledges as a state and that holds a UN seat, but whose government has lost control of most of its territory: armed groups run the main towns, and the government cannot collect taxes or enforce its laws outside the capital.
