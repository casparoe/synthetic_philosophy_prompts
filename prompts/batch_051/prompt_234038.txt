I've been trying to understand my own repeated failures to stick to plans I genuinely endorse — saving money, eating better, not staying up late — and a friend who works in behavioral economics sent me what he called the standard contemporary account. He described it roughly as follows. Hyperbolic discounting basically is weakness of will. People discount future rewards at a rate that shrinks as the delay grows, so a smaller-sooner reward can look more attractive than a larger-later reward when both are imminent, and less attractive when both lie far in the future. That is why someone can resolve on Sunday to save and then reverse himself on Wednesday: the preference reversal is not a failure of judgment but a direct consequence of the shape of the discount curve. Because the present self faces a steeper segment of the curve than the future self does, the present self's valuation is the distorted one. Akrasia is therefore a real phenomenon of divided selves rather than a single agent making an error, and the rational response is to bind yourself in advance — Ulysses and the sirens, cooling-off periods, automatic payroll deductions — whenever you suspect your later self will defect. In the end, hyperbolic discounting shows that the traditional philosophical worry about weakness of will is not really about weakness or irrationality; it is an engineering problem about arranging the environment so that the tempting option never becomes available at the moment of choice.

I am suspicious of several moves here, particularly the slide from a descriptive claim about discounting to the normative claim that the present self's valuation is distorted, and the conclusion that the philosophical problem of akrasia has been dissolved rather than redescribed. But I do not know enough to locate the errors precisely or to say what survives correction. Could you go through this account claim by claim and tell me what a careful philosopher or decision theorist would accept, what they would push back on, and whether the final conclusion follows from whatever is accurate in it?
