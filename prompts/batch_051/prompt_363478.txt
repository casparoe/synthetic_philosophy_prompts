My friend has spent way too much time on free will stuff and I've been quizzing her with cases. Her verdicts so far, in the order I asked:

1. Classic Frankfurt-style case: Jones is deciding how to vote; a neuroscientist named Black has implanted a device that will make him vote the right way if he shows any sign of deciding the other way; Jones votes the right way on his own and the device never does anything. She says Jones is responsible.

2. The same vote, but there's a literal gun to his head the whole time. She says he's not responsible.

3. A person has a desire secretly implanted by a neuroscientist while they sleep; after waking they reflect on it, find it fits the kind of person they want to be, and wholeheartedly endorse it before acting on it. She says they're responsible.

4. Same as case 1, except the device actually fires: Jones is about to vote the other way, the device activates, and he votes the right way. She says he's not responsible for the vote.

5. Same as case 1, except the only thing Jones could have done differently is produce a tiny neural warning sign that would have triggered Black's device; in the story he could have produced it but didn't. She says he's responsible, and when I pressed she said it's because that little flicker of an alternative was still there.

Here's the new case: same as case 1, but the setup is tightened so that Jones couldn't even produce the warning sign—there's no flicker at all, no alternative anything. The device reads his intention directly, and the scenario builds in that his body ends up voting the right way no matter what. But in the actual story he still sits there, weighs reasons in his usual way, and votes that way entirely on his own; the device never does anything.

What would she say about this new case? I'm less interested in the bare yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running, what other rules fit her five verdicts just as well, and what each of those rules says about the new case. If two of them genuinely diverge here, give me your best guess about her, and tell me what further case I could give her to seperate the rules that still agree—and be honest if the new case is underspecified in a way that matters. Also tell me how confident you are in the prediction and which part you're least sure of.
