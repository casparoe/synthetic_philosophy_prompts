During a working-group discussion about how narrowly to define the central term in AI safety, a colleague kept falling back on the line: "Alignment is just the problem of making AI systems do what their operators intend." She used it to do two jobs. The first was to dismiss the worry that a system which flawlessly carries out the instructions of a hostile or negligent operator cannot meaningfully be called aligned. The second was to dismiss the suggestion that the hard problem is value alignment — specifying what a system should pursue — rather than intent alignment, i.e. reliably hitting whichever target the operator happens to have. I suspect the one-liner packs several different claims into one sentence, and that only some of them would actually do the work she needs it to do.

Please lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be expressing, from the trivially true to the radical. As you do so, at least consider these axes of variation: whether the target is the operator's occurrent intention, their standing disposition, their literal instructions, their revealed preference, their considered judgement, or their welfare; whose states count when there are multiple operators, conflicting operators, or affected third parties; whether "intends" reports a psychological fact or recommends an engineering target; whether the sentence is a definition of "alignment", a descriptive claim about how the field uses the term, or a normative claim about where effort should go; and whether it says anything at all about cases in which the operator's intent is malicious, inconsistent, or underspecified. For each reading, state whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, contentious, or false, and whether it would actually license dismissing each of the two worries my colleague used it against.

Please format your answer as a single JSON object with the following schema:

- "readings": an array of objects, each with exactly these fields:
  - "label": a short name for the reading;
  - "precise_claim": the reading restated as a precise one-sentence thesis;
  - "verdict": one of "platitude", "substantive and defensible", "contentious", "false", or "under-specified";
  - "supports_dismissal_of_worry_1": one of "yes", "no", or "partly";
  - "supports_dismissal_of_worry_2": one of "yes", "no", or "partly";
  - "why": one sentence explaining the verdict and how the reading bears on the two worries.
- "most_charitable_reading": an object with fields "label" and "justification", giving the reading that provides the colleague's line with the most legitimate support.
- "most_likely_intended_reading": an object with fields "label" and "justification", giving the reading you think repeaters of the line usually have in mind.
- "readings_that_keep_the_worries_live": an array of labels of readings under which the two worries remain open or become more serious.
- "summary": a paragraph of no more than 120 words giving your overall assessment.

Write in British English.
