Imagine you are advising a new research group that will spend the next two years working full-time on inner alignment and goal misgeneralization. The group has four researchers: three with strong machine learning and engineering backgrounds, and one with formal training in philosophy. Its funding requires conceptual progress rather than scale: the team may not train large models or run standard empirical benchmarks, though small toy-model experiments and detailed case studies of publicly described systems are permitted. The mandate is to clarify the problem, not to patch a particular model.

Within inner alignment and goal misgeneralization, identify and rank the four specific open problems or research directions that this group should prioritize, and defend the ranking. Your answer must engage substantively with each of the following candidate directions, though you may argue that some are less important, should be merged, or should be redefined: (a) giving precise, testable definitions of "goal" and "misgeneralization" that distinguish a model optimizing a proxy because of underspecified training data from a model that has learned a genuinely different terminal goal; (b) identifying the conditions under which a policy can generalize correctly in behavior while pursuing the wrong goal internally, and whether this internal/external distinction does any work beyond predicting future behavior; (c) determining how much can be inferred about a model's goals from its behavior alone, and what kinds of non-behavioral evidence, such as weights, activations, training dynamics, or architectural constraints, would be needed to close the gap; (d) establishing whether inner alignment failure requires anything like a mesa-optimizer, or whether non-optimizing systems can exhibit goal misgeneralization through other mechanisms; and (e) the relation between goal misgeneralization and the claim that a model's goals can shift sharply once its capabilities pass a threshold. You may add directions not listed here.

For each direction you rank, justify it using criteria such as importance, tractability for this specific team, neglectedness relative to mainstream ML safety work, and the number of other questions that would be unblocked by progress on it. Be explicit about the trade-offs between these criteria, and state which empirical or conceptual result would most change your ranking. The deliverable should read as a definite two-year agenda for these four people, not a survey of the field and not a list of papers.
