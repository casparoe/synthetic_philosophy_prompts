I am drafting a novel in which a numerate but romantically inclined protagonist must decide how much weight to give to an uncanny coincidence, and I want her interior deliberation to be exact rather than decorative. Please treat the following scene as a real event with a fixed but unknown causal history.

At a dinner of roughly 140 guests, my protagonist is seated beside a stranger. Over the course of the evening they discover three shared facts: both were born on 29 February, both have an elder sister named Celeste, and both broke the same carpal bone in the left hand at the age of seven. The protagonist comes away with a credence of about 0.7 that the conjunction has some non-chance explanation—destiny, a hidden common cause, an unrecognized selection effect in the guest list, a distortion of memory or reporting, or anything else that would make the three facts systematically linked rather than independent accidents. Her friend, who overhears the same exchange, places that credence at about 0.01.

I suspect the birthday-paradox distinction between a match somewhere in the room and a match involving this particular pair is doing much of the work, and I want that pressure made explicit rather than folded into the final number. Please give me your own credence in the proposition that the conjunction has a non-chance explanation, and decompose it into at least the following components, assigning each a rough probability and a sentence of justification:

1. The probability that some pair among the 140 guests, not necessarily these two, would share all three features by chance, on defensible base rates that you state for a leap-day birth, an elder sister named Celeste, and a matching childhood fracture.
2. The probability that, given that such a pair is present, these two would be seated together and then happen to bring all three facts to the surface in ordinary conversation.
3. The probability that this conjunction, once noticed, would be preserved and reported as significant—given that they would have been just as astonished by any of an enormous number of alternative sets of shared features, so the relevant class of coincidences of this order is much larger than these three facts alone.
4. The prior probability that some non-chance process—design, fate, a selection effect in the guest list, a memory distortion, or otherwise—is responsible for this kind of pairing.

Then show how the components combine into your overall credence. Finally, tell me which component your final number is most sensitive to, which component most plausibly explains the gap between my two characters' 0.7 and 0.01, and what additional evidence about the scene would move your number most.
