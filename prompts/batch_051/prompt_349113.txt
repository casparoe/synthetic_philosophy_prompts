Write an essay on the Death in Damascus puzzle — the decision-theory one with the near-perfect predictor, not the old Appointment in Samarra fable. The setup: a traveller has to choose between staying in Damascus and going to Aleppo. Death has already predicted whichever he will choose, with near-perfect accuracy, and has gone to that city, so exactly one city has Death in it. If the traveller picks the city Death is in, he dies; if he picks the other city, he lives. The nasty part is that whichever option he leans toward, that leaning now looks like evidence that Death is waiting for him there, so the option he is about to chose always looks fatal, even though one city is actually empty. Weigh the main responses: the causal decision theory line that his choice does not cause Death to be in one city or the other, the evidential line that his choice is still information about where Death is, and the thought that the real trouble is that neither option is ratifiable. Then defend a conclusion. Its fine to conclude that there is no stable rational decision here, or that he should flip a coin, but actually argue for the verdict instead of just listing positions. If you treat flipping a coin as a way out, consider whether that just reopens the same problem one level up.
