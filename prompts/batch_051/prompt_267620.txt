Your task is to write a piece of deadpan satirical mock-documentary prose of roughly 700 words: a parody of a training manual issued to newly arrived officers of the Indian Civil Service in the early 1860s, under Crown rule, titled something like Handbook for the Supervision of Backward Peoples. The manual's authors are administrators who have read John Stuart Mill's On Liberty and treat it as settled doctrine, while quietly bending it to the needs of colonial administration.

The satire must target a genuine feature of Mill's position rather than a caricature of it. Specifically, it should take seriously Mill's restriction of the liberty principle to those who have reached what he describes as the maturity of their faculties, and his claim that despotic rule over peoples classified as not yet civilized is legitimate when it aims at their improvement rather than at the ruler's benefit. Working from this, the manual should construct a two-tier system: individual liberty and the harm principle are described as fully operative for the British community, while the Indian population is managed under a benevolent developmental despotism whose end date is always deferred.

Please include:

1. A Statement of Purpose explaining that the manual's rule cannot be tyranny, because tyranny is by definition rule for the ruler's benefit, whereas this manual exists solely for the ruled's benefit.
2. A classification section listing the behavioral and social indicators an officer should use to decide whether a district is civilized and therefore ready for liberty, or backward and therefore properly governed by discretion. The indicators should be drawn from administration, economy, and local habits of deference, and it should become clear, without ever being said, that the criteria are so framed that no district currently under British administration will ever be classified as civilized.
3. A Frequently Asked Questions section, including an answer to why local petitions for representative institutions do not demonstrate fitness for them but instead demonstrate the opposite. The reasoning must follow impeccably from the manual's own logic.
4. A closing note on the duty to respect local custom, except where custom conflicts with improvement, where improvement is defined by the manual itself.

The humor must come from getting Mill right: a reader who knows On Liberty, and perhaps his later remarks on why representative institutions are unsuited to India, should recognize that the manual exaggerates a real tension inside his liberal outlook rather than inventing an absurdity. Keep the derision trained throughout on the manual's authors and on the structure of liberal-imperial argument, never on Indian people or their cultures. The irony should remain deadpan, and yet a reader unfamiliar with Mill should still be able to tell what is being satirized. The piece should be self-contained and should not read as a critical essay or include footnotes.
