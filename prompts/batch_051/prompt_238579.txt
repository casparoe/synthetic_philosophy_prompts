Next month I'm giving a colloquium talk defending Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: nobody is truly or ultimately morally responsible for what they do. The compressed version I'll defend goes like this: (1) to be truly responsible for an action you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are, at least in the relevant mental respects; (2) but you can't be — your initial makeup is just heredity plus environment, and any later change you make to yourself comes from how you already were, so the buck never stops; (3) so nobody is ever truly morally responsible. The audience is a philosophy department that is mostly compatibilists of a Frankfurt/Strawsonian-reactive-attitudes sort, plus two criminal law people who think retributive punishment requires genuine desert, plus one already-convinced hard determinist. Put together my Q&A prep sheet: the six or seven objections I'll actually get from this group — not the ones the hard determinist would've raised before signing on — ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each philosopher would actually put it, with a two-minute reply to each and a note on where that reply is weakest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Then tell me how confident you are in your read of this crowd and single out the one point you're least sure about.
