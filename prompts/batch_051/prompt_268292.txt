I am drafting a philosophy of technology paper in which I defend this thesis: meaningful human control over an autonomous weapon is not a property that can be read off any single point in the targeting cycle — an operator approving a target set, a human retaining a veto, a signature on an after-action report — but is instead a structural property of how authority and information are distributed across the people and the timescale of an operation. I want a thought experiment designed to isolate that claim and force the defender of what I will call the ingredient view to confront it. The ingredient view holds that control is meaningfully human so long as several familiar conditions are jointly met: a human in the loop approves each target class or engagement, the system behaves in ways predictable enough for the operator to anticipate it, every engagement is logged so it can be traced to the specific people who authorized it, and responsibility is assignable to those people afterward.

Construct the thought experiment to the following specifications. It must be realistic — a contemporary military setting such as a loitering munition, an air-defense battery, or a counter-battery radar system, not a science-fiction scenario — and no longer than a paragraph. The system in the case must satisfy each of the ingredient conditions, and the humans involved must be neither coerced, deceived, negligent, nor malicious. There must be no malfunction, and the problem must not come from the system deciding too fast for human reflexes. Yet by the end of the case it should feel hollow to say that anyone meaningfully controlled what the system did. The hollowness has to fall out of the way authority is divided: each human sees and authorizes only a fragment, no one holds authority over the overall pattern, and the fragments are never assembled anywhere until afterward. After the scenario, walk through what the ingredient view would say about the case and why it would count the control as meaningful, then what my structural view says and exactly which feature of the case is doing the argumentative work. Then tell me what happens if that load-bearing feature is removed or weakened, and how a defender of the ingredient view would most plausibly attack the case — including the reply I should least want to hear.
