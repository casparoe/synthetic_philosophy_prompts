I have to lead a single seventy-five-minute lecture introducing Zorn's lemma to second-year mathematics majors who have completed a linear algebra course and a first course in proof writing, and who are comfortable with partial orders and with upper bounds in the concrete case of the real numbers, but who have not seen transfinite induction, ordinals, or a formal statement of the Axiom of Choice. Do not explain Zorn's lemma to me; I already understand it. Plan the lecture.

The plan should begin with the order in which the components must be introduced, with the dependency structure made explicit: partial order, chain, upper bound, and maximal element must precede the statement of the lemma, and maximal element must be distinguished from maximum or greatest element before the lemma is stated. Then choose the single example or application that should carry the proof through the whole session. Consider the existence of a basis for an arbitrary vector space, the existence of a spanning tree in a connected graph, and the existence of a maximal ideal in a commutative ring with identity, and say which of these to use for this audience and why, including what the example must let students see at each step — how the chain hypothesis is verified, why the union of a chain of the relevant objects is again an object of the same kind, and how maximality in the poset converts into the property one actually wanted to prove — as well as what features would make an example actively unsuitable here.

Then provide a misconception map for this audience, and specify the exact point in the sequence at which each misconception should be headed off rather than left to surface during the proof. Address at least the following: thinking an upper bound must belong to the chain; reading the hypothesis as requiring an upper bound for every subset rather than every chain; expecting the conclusion to deliver a maximum rather than a maximal element; expecting the proof to construct the object explicitly rather than to certify its existence through maximality; and checking the chain condition only for finite chains. End with one question to ask in the final five minutes that would distinguish students who have absorbed the lemma from students who have merely watched a proof, and state what a good answer must show. Finally, identify what should be deliberately omitted or postponed at this level and how to mark it as out of scope without making the lemma seem unmotivated. Write the plan in American English.
