Physicalism is typically stated as the thesis that everything is physical, but Hempel's dilemma seems to undermine that thesis at the outset. If "physical" means the entities and properties recognized by current physics, physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics is incomplete and likely to undergo revision. If "physical" instead means the entities and properties recognized by some future, completed physics, the thesis becomes empty, because no one can now say what that completed physics will include. Since the two readings appear to exhaust the alternatives, why do philosophers continue to treat physicalism as a substantive doctrine that can be argued for and against, rather than as a slogan whose central term lacks determinate content?
