I've come to believe that the legitimacy of entrenched constitutional provisions is best explained by treating them as a species of Ulysses-style self-binding. On the position I have in mind, supermajority rules and eternity clauses are justified because a polity at a moment of relative lucidity anticipates its own predictable failures of judgment — panic, faction, vengeance, the temptation to entrench a momentary majority — and binds itself in advance, much as Ulysses has himself lashed to the mast because he knows the sirens will predictably overpower his better judgment. The position makes a distinctive set of predictions: the provisions it justifies should be overwhelmingly processual rather than substantive (they should constrain how decisions are made, not dictate which values win), they should bind hardest precisely when the anticipated passion is strongest, and they should be reversible only under conditions that would themselves count as a return to lucidity.

I want a map of what would count against this position. Don't just list the standard objections; tell me which observations, which possible cases, which formal results, and which results in neighboring debates (about individual rationality, intergenerational obligation, credible commitment, or the theory of amendment) it stands or falls with. Distinguish evidence that would be fatal from evidence that would be merely awkward. Which putative defeaters would a committed defender of the Ulysses model concede as genuinely counting against the position, and which would they dismiss as misreadings of the analogy? Pay particular attention to whether the analogy can survive the disanalogy between one continuing person and a succession of non-overlapping generations, and between Ulysses knowing the sirens are a deception and framers possibly being wrong about what later generations will need. For my own part, I would find it most telling if the historical record showed supermajority thresholds and eternity clauses doing more to entrench elite or factional preferences than to protect a polity from its own passions, since that would reverse the asymmetry the position predicts. Finally, say whether the position as I've stated it is well insulated from evidence — whether it risks anything at all — and, if it is insulated, whether that insulation is itself a mark against it. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
