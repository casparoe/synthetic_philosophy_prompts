Write me a deadpan product page, maybe 450 words, for a startup called Longshot Mutual, which sells insurance against Pascal's mugging events: outcomes so astronomically good or bad that taking there tiny probabilities at face value lets them swamp any finite expected-value calculation. The target should be expected-value fanaticism, not decision theory in general, so the jokes have to come from getting the concepts right rather than mocking the maths — for example, the gap between maximizing expected utility and actually having a decision procedure you can run, unbounded versus bounded utility, how you'd elicit a utility from a universe that won't be around to pay the premium, and the way fanatics quietly treat tiny probabilities as zero right after insisting no probability is too small to matter. Include a quote from a skeptical actuary who keeps asking whether premiums are payable in utility or ordinary money, since the exchange rate is kind of the whole problem. Stay deadpan the whole way through; the company should never admit it's selling a superstition, and by the end a careful reader should see exactly whats wrong with fanaticism without you having to say it outright.
