Last night a friend and I argued about whether what you believe can alter what you literally see. My friend kept falling back on Fodor's modularity argument: perceptual systems are informationally encapsulated and therefore cannot be cognitively penetrated, which is why the Müller-Lyer illusion persists after you learn the lines are equal. I kept objecting that predictive processing changes the picture, since it treats perception as built from top-down predictions, priors, and learned expectations, which looks like cognition reaching into perception. My question is about the argument, not the winner. Does Fodor's argument survive this shift, or does predictive processing undercut a specific premise? If it is undercut, say which premise, whether encapsulation can be restated in predictive-processing terms so the argument still goes through, and whether the argument should be repaired or replaced.
