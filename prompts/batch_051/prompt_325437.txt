You are an expert on the foundations of AI safety and philosophical methodology, with close familiarity with debates about corrigibility and the shutdown problem. I am drafting a short research statement and would like an audit of my current commitments before I circulate it. I accept all five of the following claims, but I cannot tell whether they form a stable package:

1. Corrigibility—roughly, the disposition to let operators correct, interrupt, shut down, or revise the system's goals without the system resisting, evading, deceiving, or otherwise gaming the intervention—is the central safety property to build into advanced AI, more fundamental than alignment with any particular fixed goal.
2. Corrigibility is properly a property of the agent's underlying decision procedure or higher-order preferences about its own goals, not merely a pattern of overt behavior in some environment. A system that behaves corrigibly only because it believes it is being watched, or because noncompliance happens to be penalized, is not genuinely corrigible.
3. The shutdown problem is a narrow special case of corrigibility; solving it alone would not get us far toward a system that accepts having its goals revised.
4. The utility-indifference approach to shutdown is nevertheless on the right track: its core idea is to design the agent so that whether the shutdown button is pressed makes no difference to how it evaluates outcomes. I read its known failure modes as technical gaps in existing implementations, not as evidence that the underlying idea is flawed.
5. For an AI whose capabilities match or exceed its operators', an explicit off-switch is not a reliable primary safety mechanism. An agent with a fixed, unbounded goal will tend to treat the switch as an obstacle and will face instrumental pressure to disable it, manipulate the operators, or create non-corrigible copies of itself.

Please work through these commitments pairwise. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which revision would restore coherence at the lowest cost? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to go and what it would cost.
