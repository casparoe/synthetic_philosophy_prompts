I have been trying to write up my considered views on uncertain evidence for a sequence of posts, and before I commit to them in public I would like a consistency audit. Here is the profile as it stands.

1. I am an orthodox Bayesian in the following limited sense: credences obey the probability axioms, and when I become certain of a proposition E, I update by strict conditionalisation.

2. Much of my evidence, however, is uncertain: I do not learn a proposition with certainty, but merely shift credence over a set of possibilities — the garment looks red in poor light, the recording sounds slightly sharp, the forecast makes rain seventy per cent likely. In such cases I should update by Jeffrey conditionalisation. If the experience supplies new probabilities over a partition {E_i}, then for any proposition A, my new credence should be the sum, over the cells of the partition, of my new credence in E_i times my old credence in A given E_i.

3. The rigidity condition built into this rule — my conditional credences in A given each E_i remain unchanged — is not an additional assumption but part of what it is to update on that evidence. The experience bears on A only through the E_i.

4. I do not think there is any privileged partition of an experience. The partition over which an experience supplies new probabilities is not fixed by the world alone; it is shaped by my interests, my descriptive grain, and my background beliefs. Different partitions can capture the same experience without either being incorrect.

5. The order in which my evidence arrives should not matter to my final credences, holding fixed the total evidence. If permuting the order of receipt alone changed my final credence, my beliefs would be an artefact of my history rather than a response to the evidence itself.

6. I also believe that any rationally required shift can be represented, in a suitably fine-grained model, as strict conditionalisation on some event. I take Jeffrey conditionalisation to be a convenient surface rule, not a rival to strict conditionalisation.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs or triples are actually in tension and which only appear to be. Order the genuine tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest single revision would be. I would also like to hear what a defender of the package would say about the apparent tensions before dismissing them.
