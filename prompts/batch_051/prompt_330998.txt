Condorcet's jury theorem is often used to support the claim that a group can judge more reliably than any of its members: if each member is more likely right than wrong about a binary question and the members decide independently, the majority's probability of being correct rises with group size and approaches certainty in the limit. Now consider a development that strains the independence premise. People increasingly form their judgments inside a shared information environment — the same news stories, social media feeds, and widely repeated narratives. Suppose each member combines a noisy private signal with a common, possibly misleading narrative, and suppose the private signal is weak relative to the narrative. Then, as the group grows, the majority will tend to track the shared narrative, and the members' errors will not cancel out, even though each individual remains more likely right than wrong. Does Condorcet's argument survive this change? Does it need a repaired version, or does correlated judgment defeat the crowd-wisdom conclusion? In answering, I want the prompt to isolate exactly which premise is doing the work and whether the argument can be rebuilt without it.
