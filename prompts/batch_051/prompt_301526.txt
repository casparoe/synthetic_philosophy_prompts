I've been going back and forth on Malcolm's argument in *Dreaming* (1959) that it doesn't make sense to say someone was having an experience while asleep, because all we ever have is a later waking report. Then Dennett in "Are Dreams Experiences?" pushes a version of that further: maybe the memory of the dream is just loaded into the brain at waking, so there never was an experience during sleep at all, only a sort of false memory or "cassette" insertion.

Here's the case that's been bugging me. Suppose we use a lucid-dream reporting procedure. A person is trained to move their eyes left-right-left in a prearranged pattern during REM sleep at the moment they realize they are dreaming. Later, upon waking, they report a dream in which they realized they were dreaming and, at that point in the dream, performed the same eye-movement signal. The polysomnograph shows the exact eye movements during REM, before waking.

My question is: does this procedure actually refute the Malcolm/Dennett line, or not? Does it show that there was an experience during sleep, or just that some process during sleep caused both the eye movements and a waking report that matches it? Could Dennett's cassette model be modified to explain the eye-movement data without granting a real-time dream experience? Weigh both Malcolm's and Dennett's actual positions as best you understand them, but don't end on a hedge. Reach a verdict: should this protocol force someone in their tradition to admit that dreams are experiences in real time, or does the skeptical position survive?
