I am a retired organic chemist, and since leaving the bench I have been reading philosophy of science. The question that has come to occupy me is whether scientific categories have fixed essences or fluid boundaries that shift with the context of use. The microdomain in which I can think most sharply about this is the concept of an acid, because I watched that category bend several times over the course of my own training. I should like to build a small sorting instrument for a reading group I help run, whose members are scientifically literate nonspecialists with at most first-year chemistry. The point is to sort people by their actual judgments about cases, not by the label they prefer. I would be grateful if you would write the instrument for me.

Please write a quiz of eight items with a scoring key. Six items should be concrete chemical cases, described in plain terms and without using the labels Brønsted, Lewis, or Arrhenius. Each should ask whether the relevant species is an acid, with three answer options: yes; only in some contexts or under some senses; no. Please use at least these six cases: hydrogen chloride gas protonating ammonia in the gas phase, with no solvent present; boron trifluoride accepting an electron pair from ammonia and transferring no proton; a hydrated aluminium ion in water releasing a proton from one of its bound water molecules and thereby lowering the pH; a water molecule handing a proton to ammonia and leaving behind a hydroxide ion; the bare hydrogen ion, which has no proton to give up but accepts electron pairs readily; and carbon dioxide, which has no proton to give up, lowers pH only after combining with water, and accepts an electron pair from hydroxide. The remaining two items should be general claims designed to separate positions the cases cannot separate. One should ask whether extending the term acid to species that transfer no proton was a discovery about what acidity really is or a conventional change in bookkeeping. The other should ask whether the several acid concepts are rival attempts to capture one underlying reality or legitimate answers to different questions.

The scoring key should map patterns of answers onto at least these positions, whose names you may choose but whose commitments should be made explicit: proton-transfer monism; electron-pair monism; aqueous-operational monism; principled pluralism, on which the concept legitimately shifts and the shifts answer different questions rather than tracking one essence; conventionalism, on which the boundaries are our conventions and the disputes are partly verbal; and a family-resemblance view, on which acids form a graded kind structured around prototypes with no necessary-and-sufficient conditions. Please build the key from the logical space of answer patterns first: lay out the combinations of judgments the eight items allow, locate the positions in that space, and for empty regions say briefly whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two positions the instrument cannot separate, and if a ninth question would separate them, say what it would ask.
