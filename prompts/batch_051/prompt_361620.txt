For a weekly problem session aimed at undergraduate computer science students, I want to teach the move of jumping out of a formal system: settling a question about a system by reasoning about it from outside rather than by grinding out a derivation inside it. I intend to build the whole session on the MU puzzle, with this system: alphabet {M, I, U}, the single axiom MI, and four rules of inference — append U to any formula ending in I, double whatever follows M, replace one occurrence of III with U, and delete one occurrence of UU. The target question is whether MU is derivable. The students already know what a formal system is but have not met this puzzle, and I want the outside move to feel forced rather than announced. Write me the teaching plan rather than the explanation itself: the order in which the components must be introduced and what depends on what; how far to let the students search for a derivation before intervening and how to use that failed search; the misconceptions they will most likely form and the exact point in the arc at which each should be headed off; one closing check that would separate a student who has merely learned that MU is not derivable from one who has grasped the general move; and a brief note on what, for this audience, I should deliberately refuse to explain.
