I've got a stubborn classification problem and I want someone to adjudicate it. The critieria I was taught for calling something play, as opposed to work or a plain habit, ran roughly like this: it's freely chosen; it happens inside a little seperate sphere of time and space where the stakes are only internal; it's absorbing in a way that isn't aimed at any payoff outside itself; and it's governed by rules that the players treat as binding for no better reason than that they are the rules.

Here's the case that's bugging me. A 23-year-old streams a battle-royale game eight hours a day under contract to a platform. He has to hit daily hours and viewer metrics, he plays whatever character or "meta" the channel's analytics say will retain an audience, and he's said in interviews he wouldn't touch the game on a day off—it's a job. While he streams, though, he's inside the game's rules, he's absorbed enough to keep up commentary for hours, and the whole thing happens in a designated room and time slots walled off from the rest of his life. If the game were a pick-up basketball game with his friends, everyone would call it play; if it were data entry in the same room, everyone would call it work.

Work through the criteria one by one. Say plainly where they underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me which criterion is doing the real work. If it comes out as play, does that show the criteria were missing something—or if it comes out as work, does that show one of those criteria is carrying too much weight and should be dropped or loosened?
