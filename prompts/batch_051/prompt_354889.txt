I keep going back and forth on whether there could ever be information it's rational to avoid. On one hand, Good's theorem seems air tight: free information can't make you worse off in expectation, because you can always get the information and then just not act on it — you can keep doing whatever you would have done if you'd never known. So the instrumental value of information should never be negative. On the other hand, I can't shake the intuition that some things are just better not known: the exact date you'll die, a genetic marker for a disease with no treatment or prevention, the fact that your partner was tempted a long time ago and turned it down, the fact that a friend's heartfelt gift was actually picked out by their assistant. In each of these I don't just mean "I'd be sad for a bit and then get over it." I have a real sense that the belief itself would make my life go worse in a way I couldn't fix by not acting on it. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theorem (or its intended scope) to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of "better not known" on which both survive. Please write in American English.
