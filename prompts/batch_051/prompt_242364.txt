i've been trying to sort out whether the chunks we divide history into are real things or just habits of talking, and i've come up with a distinction i want checked. i call them live periods and museum periods. a live period is one where the people inside it had some sense they were doing something new and said so at the time — like the natural philosophers in the 1600s who kept insisting their science was new and a break from aristotle, or the renaissance types who announced they were reviving antiquity. a museum period is one that only gets its name afterward, from people who need a shelf to put it on — the middle ages is my main example, since nobody went around thinking ah, another medieval tuesday; the name exists because later people needed a middle stretch between the ancients and themselves. my claim is that a period is only real if it was live, and museum periods are basically filing cabinets we've mistaken for furniture. but two things bug me. first, scientific revolution is itself a later label, so being live isnt the same as having the name we use now. second, the renaissance might not even pass the test, because most people alive in the 1400s werent walking around feeling reborn — that was a small crowd of scholars — so maybe liveness is a matter of degree. is this distinction already a standard move in how historians think about periodization? and where does it break, like what does it get wrong about the middle ages or the scientific revolution?
