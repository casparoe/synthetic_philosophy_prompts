I'd like a philosophical reading of Jorge Luis Borges's story "Funes the Memorious" (1942; collected in Ficciones) through the lens of Dharmakīrti's epistemology. Dharmakīrti holds that perception (pratyakṣa) is wholly non-conceptual and takes only particulars (svalakṣaṇa) as its objects; that concepts and universals (sāmānyalakṣaṇa) belong to conceptual thought and inference (anumāna), not to perception; and that whatever is ultimately real is momentary. The story's premise is that a young man, Ireneo Funes, after a head injury becomes unable to forget: he perceives and retains every leaf, cloud and momentary appearance in exact detail, yet finds general ideas and abstraction almost impossible. What I want is a serious engagement in both directions, not a reading that reduces the story to a diagram of the doctrine. Would Dharmakīrti have to call Funes a mind of pure perception without conceptual construction, and would the story then be a vindication of the separation of perception from thought, or a demonstration that a life under that description is a horror? Should he deny that Funes can genuinely remember or use language, if memory and the application of a general word both require conceptual synthesis across moments? How should one treat Funes's private numbering system, in which every number receives its own name, and his rapid mastery of Latin: are these conceptual achievements inconsistent with the claim that he cannot think abstractly, and do they show that the very idea of a wholly non-conceptual mind is unstable? And finally: does the story's revulsion at a world of pure particulars amount to an objection to Dharmakīrti's claim that only particulars are ultimately real and that universals are conceptual fictions, or does it merely trace the consequences the doctrine predicts when the synthesising work of concepts is removed? I would like the honest critic's verdict on where the story and the philosophy come apart, and what the fable shows that the theories by themselves do not. Please write your response in British English.
