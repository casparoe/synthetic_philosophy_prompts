In the section of Daniel Ellsberg’s 1961 paper *Risk, Ambiguity, and the Savage Axioms* in which he presents the two-urn problem, Ellsberg describes a person who prefers a bet on drawing a red ball from an urn known to contain 50 red and 50 black balls to a bet on drawing a red ball from an urn containing 100 balls in unknown proportions, and who also prefers a bet on black from the known urn to a bet on black from the unknown urn. Give an example, not involving balls, urns, or lotteries with unknown numbers of tickets, of a pair of choices that exhibits this same Ellsberg pattern. In your example, identify exactly which of Savage’s postulates the two preferences jointly violate, and explain why the two preferences cannot be represented by any single subjective probability measure over the states.
