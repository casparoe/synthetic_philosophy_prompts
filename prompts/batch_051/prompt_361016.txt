You are a philosopher of science with a background in theoretical physics. Think carefully before committing to a view; begin by brainstorming several possible interpretations, then evaluate them against the details of the practice described below.

In condensed matter physics, practitioners routinely explain macroscopic phenomena in terms that are not simply abbreviations for solving the underlying many-body Schrödinger equation. They talk of order parameters, universality classes, quasiparticles, broken symmetry, and effective field theories, and they treat these higher-level objects as genuinely explanatory. Two fluids with very different molecular constituents can exhibit the same critical exponents near their critical points, and practitioners explain this by saying the fluids fall in the same universality class, not by exhibiting the microphysical derivation for each fluid. A superconductor is described by an order parameter and the behavior of its quasiparticles, even though the order parameter corresponds to no single particle degree of freedom in the fundamental Hamiltonian. Many of the same scientists, however, would also assert that everything is ultimately composed of electrons, nuclei, and photons obeying the standard model, and that higher-level laws are fixed by those lower-level laws.

What view of reduction and emergence does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit metaphysics as sympathetically and precisely as you can. Distinguish between what the practice strictly presupposes and what merely fits comfortably with it. In particular, address whether the practice presupposes only epistemological emergence—our limited ability to compute or organize lower-level detail—or also an ontological claim, such as the claim that higher-level structures are real, autonomous, or causally relevant over and above their microphysical realizers. Then say what would have to change in the practice if a strict reductionist denied that higher-level entities and explanations pick out anything real. Could condensed matter physicists retain their explanatory and predictive successes while speaking only of fundamental particles and their interactions, or would rejecting the higher-level ontology force them to abandon or reinterpret the practice itself?
