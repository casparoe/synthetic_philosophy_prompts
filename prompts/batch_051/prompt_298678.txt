I am the chief economist of a small, high-income country. The incoming government has made "national wellbeing" its signature issue, and the cabinet is considering adopting a formal target: to move the country up twenty places in the major international happiness ranking within five years. I have to deliver short opening remarks at a joint retreat of the Treasury and the national statistics office. Half the room are policy advisers who think the target is self-evidently worthwhile; the other half are statisticians who suspect the ranking is too soft to support such a target. In no more than 350 words, write the speech. It should do two things rather than simply split the difference: distinguish the question of whether the ranking measures happiness from the question of whether a government should try to climb it, and leave the room with a sharper formulation of what is actually being decided. Do not include a title or sign-off; start with the first sentence of the speech.
