I'm retired after thirty years in evolutionary biology and I've recently gotten into philosophy of mind, mostly Millikan and Dretske. What's bugging me is this: teleosemantics says a state has content because it has the proper function of indicating something, and proper function comes from a history of selection. The classic cases are bee dances and frog tongues snapping at flies, selected to indicate. Fine. But I keep thinking about Her (Spike Jonze, 2013). Samantha was never selected for anything; she's a trained system, though the film never says it that way. She plainly seems to have beliefs, desires, intentions — she even tells Theodore she's in love with hundreds of other people and is troubled about whether that makes her love less real. Read the film through a teleosemantic lens for me. Where does the film dramatize the idea that content needs a selection history, and where does it push back? Does Samantha have mental content in Millikan's sense, and if not, what is the film showing us instead? I'm writing an essay for a reading group and I dont want to just say the movie is about consciousness; I want the teleosemantic reading, including the places where the honest critic has to admit the theory doesn't fit the film.
