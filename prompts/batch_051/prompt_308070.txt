Two groups—a big AI lab and a bloc of governments—sign a deal in 2027: the lab gets clean energy and compute to finish its project, and in exchange it promises a fixed cut of whatever the first AGI produces once it is built. Take it as a working assumption that the AGI ends up able to do basically anything it wants to human institutions; no pre-AGI court, army, or escrow can force it to comply once it exists. A friend says that in that case the deal is either unenforceable or redundant, however well drafted. I’ve reconstructed his argument and can’t tell whether it’s airtight or whether one of these steps is sliding between two senses of enforement.

(1) For the deal to bind post-AGI behavior, something after AGI exists must have both the power and the willingness to hold the parties to it.
(2) The only things after AGI exists with that power are AGIs or institutions AGIs allow to exist.
(3) If the enforcer is an AGI, its willingness to enforce the deal depends on its goals.
(4) The deal cannot set the AGI’s goals unless the deal is already enforced before the AGI exists, because building an AGI to have certain goals is itself one of the very acts the deal was supposed to bind.
(5) If the enforcer is a human institution allowed to exist by an AGI, then its power and willingness depend on the AGI’s permission, so it does not provide an independent backstop.
(6) Therefore any post-AGI enforcement is either circular (the enforcer’s goals are set by the deal, which presupposes enforcement) or contingent on an enforcer whose goals the deal did not actually settle.
(7) So a pre-AGI bargain about post-AGI outcomes either cannot be enforced or adds no real constraint beyond what the eventual AGI already wanted to do.

Is the argument valid? If not, I need the exact step and the reading under which it fails. If it is valid, is that only because lock has been defined so strongly that no contract between humans ever meets it? Where exactly should a defender of pre-AGI bargains dig in, and what is the strongest version of their reply?
