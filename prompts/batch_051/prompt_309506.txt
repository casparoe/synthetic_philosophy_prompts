In Book XI of the Confessions, Augustine argues that neither the past nor the future exists in itself: the past exists only as memory and the future only as expectation, while time is a distension of the mind. Imagine he were brought into the contemporary debate between presentism (only the present exists), eternalism (all times exist equally in a block universe), and the growing block view (past and present times exist, but the future does not). Which of these positions, if any, would Augustine find most hospitable? Reconstruct his likely response, and in particular address whether he would accept one of the three or instead reject a shared assumption underlying all of them. Be explicit about where his theological commitments, such as divine eternity and creation, would shape his verdict.
