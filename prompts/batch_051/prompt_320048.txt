I am a novelist, and I have been asked to write a short statement of my position on cultural appropriation in fiction for a writers' conference. Before I put anything in front of an audience, I want to know whether the position I actually hold is internally coherent, or whether I have merely assembled a list of individually appealing sentences that quietly pull against one another. Here is where I have landed:

(1) A novelist must be free to imagine across identities. No one should need permission to write a character from another culture, and the injunction that writers should stay in their lane is both an aesthetic dead end and a political mistake.

(2) Publishing is nonetheless a social act, and a writer is rightly answerable for the foreseeable consequences of how a marginalized group is represented in their work.

(3) The wrong of cultural appropriation, as I see it, is not primarily a kind of theft. Cultures are not properties from which an idea can be stolen. The wrong is misrepresentation and flattening: the outsider too often gets the culture wrong and, in getting it wrong, reinforces the stereotypes that the group already lives under.

(4) The writer's intentions are mostly irrelevant to whether a depiction does that kind of damage. Good intentions have accompanied a great deal of harmful art.

(5) Nevertheless, I believe that serious, good-faith consultation — with sensitivity readers, or with collaborators from the culture who are genuinely empowered to say no — is normally sufficient to make an otherwise risky act of cross-cultural imagination permissible.

(6) Finally, I take cultural mixing and borrowing to be unavoidable and often generative. Any serious account must avoid the fantasy of pure, bounded cultures, and the objection cannot simply be to borrowing as such.

I cannot tell whether this is a stable package or a heap of sentences that only look compatible when placed side by side. Treat this as an audit rather than a lecture: identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; say for each what would have to be revised and what the cheapest fix would be; and tell me whether there is one revision that would do the most to clean up the whole set.
