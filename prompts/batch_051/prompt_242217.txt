Ok so my friend and I just spent like four hours argueing about anthropic reasoning, specifically self-sampling vs self-indication and the Presumptuous Philosopher, and it ended the same way it always does, so I want to turn the whole thing into a poem that finally shows why the argument is so hard to get out of. Write a villanelle in the voice of the presumptuous philosopher, who shows up at a cosmology conference and announces that he knows the universe contains vastly more observers than the rival theory says, without having looked at one piece of data. Work the setup into the poem itself so a reader who has never heard of any of this can follow: theory T1 says there's a modest number of observers, theory T2 says there's an astronomically bigger number, and no ordinary evidence distinguishes them. The first refrain should be his conclusion, and the second refrain should be the only evidence he keeps offering, namely the bare fact that he exists, or that any observer exists at all. Have the refrains alternate and then meet in the final two lines in a way that does the real work: under self-indication the huge conclusion is just the thin premise amplified, so rejecting it means explaining why existing isn't evidence, which is exactly the self-sampling move (your existence only discriminates between theories when some theory would have predicted no observers at all; if both predict at least one, the fact that you're here doesn't tell you how many others there are). Don't just name-drop SSA, SIA, or Bostrom and call it a day. The villanelle's repetition should feel like the philosopher re-deriving the same conclusion from the same evidential poverty over and over, and it should be accurate enough that someone who knows the debate recognizes the presumptuous philosopher's move and what the self-sampling reply is, while someone who doesn't know anthropics still gets a good poem about a person mistaking the fact that he exists for a discovery about the universe.
