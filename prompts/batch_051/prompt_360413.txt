So I just got out of a long argument with a friend about whether sweatshop jobs exploit workers, and we eventually realized the whole debate was downstream of us not sharing a definition of exploitation. My line was: a job cant be exploitative if the worker is better off taking it than with any alternative they actually have. Her line was: no, thats mutually beneficial exploitation, the fact that both sides gain doesnt erase the unfairness. I want a working definition of exploitation that handles the cases below. I'm confident case 2 is exploitation and case 5 isnt; the rest I genuinely dont have a settled view on.

1. A garment worker in Bangladesh takes a factory job paying above the going local wage but below a living wage, with 60-hour weeks and no union.
2. A factory owner confiscates workers passports and deducts recruitment fees from their pay until they take home almost nothing.
3. A landlord in a city with almost no vacancies raises the rent 40% in one year, not because costs went up but because he can.
4. A pharmaceutical company prices a life-saving drug far above what it costs to produce, and patients who cant really afford it pay anyway because the alternative is dying.
5. A worker-owned co-op pays everyone a low wage because the business barely breaks even, and the worker-owners voted to keep it going.
6. A parent pays their 14-year-old below the market rate to babysit younger siblings while the parent works; the kid doesnt love it but doesnt object.

Give the response in these sections, in order:
- The definition: one sentence, then what it is meant to track, 200-300 words.
- Case verdicts: run the definition through the six cases one by one, flag where each verdict is forced or wobbly, 500-600 words.
- Revisions: where the definition gives a wrong or no answer, and whether you should revise it or bite the bullet, 200 words.
- Bottom line for my argument: which of the two of us, if either, this definition supports, and what the real dispute was, 150-200 words.
