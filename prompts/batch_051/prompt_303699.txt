In the later sections of *On Certainty*, Ludwig Wittgenstein develops the image of an ungrounded set of certainties that function like the fixed hinges around which inquiry and doubt can turn: as I understand him, he suggests that any particular doubt only makes sense because a great deal of our thought already stays in place, and that these hinge commitments cannot be supported by ordinary evidence without presupposing the very practice they hold steady. This reminded me of a point in my computer science class, where my teacher described a system's trusted computing base: the small set of assumptions a program cannot verify from inside, yet on which every internal check depends. I cannot decide whether this is a deep structural parallel or just a loose analogy. What would need to be true on both sides for the comparison to be informative, and what would it predict that is false? In particular, would hinge epistemology imply that an attempt to audit one's own ungrounded assumptions faces a circularity different in kind from ordinary failed verification, and does anything from software engineering about the distinction between trust and verification transfer back to our epistemic practices?
