My friend Rina is wierdly into Sleeping Beauty-style puzzles, the kind where you wake up without knowing whether the coin landed heads and you have to set a credence in heads. I've been writing down her verdicts on variations for a while, and I honestly can't tell what rule she's actually running.

The standard setup, in case you don't know it: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday. A fair coin is tossed. Heads means she's woken once, on Monday. Tails means she's woken twice, Monday and Tuesday, with her memory of the first waking erased in between. Each waking she's asked for her credence that the coin came up heads. (You probly know this one.)

Rina's verdicts on earlier cases:

- Standard version: she says 1/3.
- Fair coin, but heads means 2 wakings and tails means 4 wakings: she says 1/3.
- Fair coin, but heads means 3 wakings and tails means 1 waking: she says 3/4.
- Fair coin, heads means 2 wakings and tails means 2 wakings: she says 1/2.

Now the new one I want to predict: same as the standard setup, except the coin is biased and lands heads with probability 2/3 (she knows this). Heads means one waking, tails means two wakings, amnesia in between as usual. On a random waking, what would Rina say the probability of heads is?

I care less about whether she's right than about what rule she's running. I suspect more than one natural rule fits her record so far. Can you spell out the candidate rules, what each predicts for the biased-coin case, which one you'd bet she's using and why, and what one further variation would do the most work to settle it?
