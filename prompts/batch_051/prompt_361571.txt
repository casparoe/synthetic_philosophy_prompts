Here is the summary:

"Teleosemantics tries to naturalize mental content by appealing to biological function. The core claim is that a state means F—say, frog—because the state was selected for being set off by Fs: organisms whose internal states tracked real features of the world did better than organisms whose states fired at random, and content just is what a state has the function of indicating. This is meant to solve the familiar problem of misrepresentation for cruder causal theories. A frog's detector can mean frog even when it fires at a passing shadow, because its evolutionary function is to indicate frogs; firing at a shadow is a malfunction, not a counterexample. The view does have to grant that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a frog that assembled by chance, with no selective history, would have no content-bearing states at all, which already strikes many people as a reductio. But the real problem is deeper: any trait benefits the organism in indefinitely many ways, and the same detector can be described as serving any number of functions, so saying that content is fixed by 'the' function means choosing one privileged function from many. That choice looks interpretive, precisely the sort of thing the naturalizing project was supposed to eliminate. So teleosemantics is either circular or it merely relocates the mystery of content, and the view fails."

My inclination is that the "indefinitely many functions" move is doing too much work, but I am not confident. What should I keep and what should I disregard?
