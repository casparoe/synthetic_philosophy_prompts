I am a distributed-systems engineer with no formal background in philosophy of mind. The vocabulary I actually reason in is message passing, broadcast and multicast, subscriber lists, cache coherence, shared logs, and observability. Restate, in that vocabulary, the contrast between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness as it figures in the dispute between global workspace theory and the overflow view. Take global workspace theory to be roughly the claim that a representation becomes access-conscious exactly when its contents are globally broadcast to a wide range of otherwise independent consumer processes rather than remaining local to a single module. Take the overflow view to be the claim that phenomenal consciousness is richer and more detailed than whatever gets selected for global broadcast at any moment, so that conscious experience outruns the workspace. Translate both positions as faithfully as you can, then give me the residue report: which parts compile cleanly into my framework, which required shims or distortion, and which have no counterpart at all. In particular, I want your judgment on whether the overflow dispute survives translation as a genuine architectural disagreement about unlogged computation, and whether the qualitative character of experience is residue that my vocabulary cannot express because my vocabulary is missing something, or because the philosophical distinction is less crisp than it looks.
