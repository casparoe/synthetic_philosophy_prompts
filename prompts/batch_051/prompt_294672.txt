OK so I've been trying to get better at — or maybe get out of my own way in — improvising on guitar, and my teacher keeps telling me the harder I try to "be in the moment" the stiffer I sound. His favorite line is that it's like falling asleep: you can't make yourself fall asleep on purpose, because the trying is part of what's keeping you awake, and sleep only comes when you sort of forget to try. He then applies this to wu-wei, the Daoist idea of effortless action — he says the whole point is that you can't chase wu-wei directly any more than you can chase sleep, and the only fix is to stop trying.

But I'm not sure the analogy does the work he wants it to. Falling asleep isn't a skill, and nobody gets better at sleeping by sleeping badly and learning from it. With music — or conversation, or sports — there's a ton of practice, much of it very deliberate and unglamorous, that seems to be what makes the effortless stuff possible later. So I'm confused about what wu-wei actually commits you to here.

Could you walk me through what maps onto what in this "wu-wei is like falling asleep" analogy, and in particular where it breaks down? I'm especially interested in whether the conclusion he draws — that since you can't try to be wu-wei, the only move is to stop trying — actually survives the disanalogies. Does the fact that you can't will yourself into effortless action mean deliberate practice is pointless, or does wu-wei leave room for "trying" in some indirect, preparatory sense? And if there's a better analogy than falling asleep for whats actually going on, I'd like to hear it. Please write in American English.
