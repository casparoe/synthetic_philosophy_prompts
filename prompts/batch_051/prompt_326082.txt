I want you to draft the joint statement that a rational-choice economist and a Simon-style bounded-rationality psychologist could both sign at the end of a year-long adversarial collaboration on satisficing. The economist comes in beleiving that satisficing is just optimal stopping with information and deliberation costs, and that talk of a genuinely different decision procedure is loose. The psychologist comes in beleiving that aspiration levels and stopping rules are a real psychological process that cannot be reduced to maximizing a value function over an expanded state space without losing something.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by a disagreements table. The table columns should be: the exact question, each side's position put in words that side would accept, why the disagreement matters, and the crux — the sub-question or observation that would move at least one of them. Make sure to include a disagreement about order effects: whether a satisficer's final choice can depend on the order in which alternatives are encountered in a way that no fixed utility function plus search costs can reproduce, with each side writing down in advance what they predict for a within-subject experiment where the same choice set is presented in two different orders with a distractor task in between. Also include the normative disagreement about whether an agent with hard computational limits should literally run an aspiration-level stopping rule, or should only behave as if it were doing the costlier optimization. At the end, have each side name the single finding that would make them switch camps.
