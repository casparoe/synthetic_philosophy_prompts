As a novelist, I have watched two critics agree about every action, memory, and constraint in a character’s history and still divide over whether she acts freely or is merely the product of her circumstances. Suppose I claim their disagreement is merely verbal: each critic uses the word “freely” for a different but coherent relation between an agent and her history, and if the term were disambiguated no disagreement about the novel would remain. How would one test that claim? I want a concrete diagnostic that could, in principle, be run without allowing either critic to use the word “freely,” its cognates, or near synonyms. Please answer in American English as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The points should: describe the diagnostic; state what result would support the merely-verbal reading; state what result would support a substantive disagreement; identify the strongest confound; and state what a clean positive result would still not settle.
