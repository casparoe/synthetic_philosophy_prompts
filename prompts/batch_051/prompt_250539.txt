In my philosophy of mind group we've spent three weeks stuck on the same discussion about Fabre's The Hunting Wasps. He describes a Sphex wasp that drags a cricket to its burrow, leaves it on the doorstep, goes inside to inspect the nest, comes back out, and if the cricket has been moved even a few inches it drags it backs to the entrance and inspects again. Fabre's conclusion in that passage is roughly that the wasp behaves like a machine: its instinct works through a fixed sequence of gears, and if a gear is reset, the sequence repeats without the wasp noticing. The disagreement in our group is about how much this tells us about human freedom. One side says the wasp shows that apparent intelligence can be mechanical, and humans are just the same kind of system with more layers, so 'free will' is an illusion. The other side says humans are different in kind because they can step back, notice the loop, and change the rule. We've all read the same passage and nobody moves. My hypothesis is the impasse isn't about the wasp or even about the facts about human cognition; it's about what each side will count as a break in the mechanism, and maybe about whether 'free will' is doing any real work or is just a placeholder for a bundle of capacities. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this is, and tell me what would genuinely count as progress given the diagnosis.
