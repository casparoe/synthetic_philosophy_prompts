I'm writing a novel where a small town works itself into a panic over a rumor that the lake is poisoned, and I want the panic to be made of individually sensible decisions rather than crowd stupidity. So far I've got three mechanisms to draw on: (1) informational cascades—once enough earlier people have acted, later people rationally discount their own private evidence, because all those public actions are themselves evidence; (2) reputational herding—people go along so they won't be the one who ignored everyone else's fear if the thing turns out to be real; (3) payoff externalities—once enough people are stocking up, the shelves genuinely start to empty, and buying early stops being paranoid. I know there are more academic labels floating around, but a lot of them seem to just be one of these three in different clothes. I want a fourth mechanism that is genuinly different from these three—not a restatement, a special case, or a combination of them—and I'd like you to tell me honestly whether it would actually make good fiction or just sound clever.
