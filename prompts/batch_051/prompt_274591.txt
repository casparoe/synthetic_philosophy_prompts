Suppose an otherwise-unchanged present-day liberal democracy develops a safe, cheap, reversible procedure that reliably induces a state close to the Stoic ideal of apatheia: a person who undergoes it still reasons normally, still judges some states of affairs good and others bad, and still acts on ordinary plans and duties, but the passions — fear, grief, anger, craving, even intense elation — no longer arise as felt disturbances; only a calm, steady concern remains. Any adult may take it free of charge, and within two generations about half of all adults have done so at least once. Predict, with explicit reasoning about mechanisms and incentives rather than narrative, how the society changes in individual wellbeing, close relationships, political life, and creative and economic activity, and whether the resulting world is better or worse, and at what cost. Mark which of your predictions are robust and which are guesses, and state the assumption that would most change each. Answer in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
