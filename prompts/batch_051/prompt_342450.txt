Write a short story about a game designer working on a war game that's supposed to be anti-glorification: no heroic framing, no medals, no triumphant music, just the miserable logistics of a campaign. The trouble starts when the designer realizes the game is still deeply fun, and the fun is coming from exactly the parts meant to be grim — the supply routing, the casualty math, the slow patient retreat that feels like solving a knot. The story should dramatize this specific problem: that making war legible as a system of interesting decisions might itself be a kind of glorification, even when the tone is sober. Show one concrete mechanic doing this work, and try not to let the story end with the designer simply changing the mechanic or quitting the project. Let the unease stay unresolved.
