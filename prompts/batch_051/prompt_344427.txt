I'm running an intro philosophy of mind section this term — around 25 students, one 80-minute session — and I want to do the Chinese Room as a live simulation rather then a lecture. My plan is to split the class in three. One group writes the "program": a hand-made rulebook that maps input symbol-strings to output symbol-strings for a tiny made-up question-answering game, say "which of these three colored shapes is biggest?" written in a symbol code none of them speak. A second group plays the person in the room, passing slips of symbols back and forth strictly by the rules, no peeking at what the symbols mean (if anything). The rest are interrogators who can only use the code and have to decide whether their talking to someone who understands. No lecture up front, and I don't want the Systems Reply named until after they've sat with what happened. Then, in the debrief, I want to stage the Systems Reply — the room-plus-rulebook-plus-person as a whole sure looks like it understands, even though no part does — and then hit them with Searle's move, that this is exactly the point, and that if the "whole system" understands then understanding has become something we can't point to anywhere. The debrief should also map what actually happened in the simulation onto the syntax/semantics distinction.

Can you turn this into a runnable activity? I need exact rules for the symbol game, a way to have them write the rulebook in class without it accidentally smuggling in semantics (e.g., someone writes "output the name of the biggest shape" instead of a symbol-to-symbol table), a materials list, a timeline for 80 minutes, and a list of what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — and how to recover from each. I can already see the interrogators cheating by switching to English, or the rulebook group getting bored and just shouting answers. Also be straight about which part of the argument this can't reproduce: nobody in the room is actually appearing to understand full human Chinese, and the man's first-person experience of understanding-nothing can't be transferred to a student. Tell me where the facilitator has to step in and supply the missing piece instead of pretending the activity covers it.
