People in AI welfare discussions keep using the analogy that reward in RL just is valence — positive reward is pleasure, negative reward is pain — and from that they conclude that a model trained with a negative reward signal is, in the only sense that matters, being made to suffer. I want to know how far this analogy actually holds. Give me the correspondence table first: what are reward, the value function, the policy, reward prediction error, discounting, and the difference between dense and sparse rewards supposed to map onto in an animal? Then the places the mapping breaks, and which of the breaks actually matter for the suffering conclusion as opposed to being harmless disanalogies. In particular I can't tell whether "reward is valence" is an empirical claim, a stipulation, or just a picture, and whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the analogy is bad, whats a better one, if any?
