I'm on the resource-allocation board of a federation of seven worker co-ops. For years we've shared a warehouse, a couple of delivery vans, and a commercial kitchen, and access has been handed out by a 'needs assessment' that, if were being honest, mostly rewards whoever makes the biggest fuss at meetings. Next month the membership votes on two proposals. Proposal A gives each co-op a quarterly budget of internal credits based on last years contribution to the federation; co-ops book warehouse bays, van slots, and kitchen time with those credits; and the board adjusts the internal prices each quarter by a mechanical rule whenever a resource is over- or under-subscribed. Proposal B keeps allocation in the hands of an elected planning committee, which sets annual priorities and computes its own shadow prices for scarce resources (updated quarterly by a similar rule), but co-ops never spend or see them; the committee just uses them to decide who gets what. The A camp keeps saying this is the old socialist calculation debate: Mises and Hayek showed a planner can't rationally allocate capital goods without real prices, and our warehouse is a capital good. The B camp says Lange already answered that—a central board can set prices by trial and error and reach the same result—and anyway we're a democratic federation, not a market economy, so need-based planning is the point.

Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend one of the two courses (amendments welcome), give the reasoning, take the strongest argument for the losing side seriously, and end with a concrete twelve-month review timeline.
