I keep flip-flopping on moral deference. I'm drawn to the view that there's something wrong with taking moral conclusions wholesale from other people. The arguments feel systematic: a moral belief is supposed to be something I can stand behind and answer for, and if I just inherit it from a trusted friend I've got a borrowed conclusion, not an understanding—which predicts the asymmetry where "because my mechanic said so" settles things in a way "because my friend said so" doesn't when the question is whether a joke was cruel or a policy is exploitative. It also explains why moral education keeps telling people to see things for themselves instead of memorizing verdicts. So the theory has that clean, unified feel. But I can't shake a case in the opposite direction. A friend from a community I'm not part of tells me that something I've done for years is, as they put it, humiliating to people like them. I can't reconstruct the reasons from my own experience. My theory seems to tell me to hold off until I understand it myself—and that feels exactly wrong, like using self-authorship as an excuse not to listen. Now I'm stuck between a distinction that seems to carve morality where it actually is, and a concrete intuition that in at least some cases the distinction is doing the wrong work. I want the strongest version of both and a verdict on which should yield. Can the anti-deference view allow one-off updating on moral testimony while objecting only to making someone else my standing moral faculty, or is that a retreat? Or is my intuition in the friend case really tracking non-epistemic reasons—to show trust, not to speak over members of a group—while the epistemic asymmetry survives intact? And what should count as evidence against each side: would a fully competent, trustworthy, articulate moral expert whom I should nonetheless refuse to defer to settle it one way, or would a case where deferring is not just permissible but exactly what I ought to do settle it the other? I'm not asking which position is right as much as what each position actually commits me to, and whether there's a reading on which the epistemic point and the intuition are compatible.
