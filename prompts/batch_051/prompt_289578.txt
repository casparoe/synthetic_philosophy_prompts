I am a PhD student in metaphysics preparing my dissertation prospectus on grounding and fundamentality, and my committee has asked me to defend which open problems in the area my project should take up first. The candidates I am weighing include whether grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive in the way a strict partial order requires; whether 'grounds' is univocal across realization, constitution, set-membership, truthmaking, and mathematical entailment; whether the grounding facts themselves need grounds and whether that demand generates a regress; whether grounding collapses into supervenience or identity once the notion is made precise; and whether there is a fundamental level at all if physics turns out to be scale-relative and bottomless. What I want is a dependency-aware prioritization rather than a survey: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for one person working from the armchair and with formal tools, and which should be deprioritized because settling them would not change much downstream.

Please give me exactly five open questions, ordered from most to least worth pursuing, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences: state the question, explain what further debates depend on it, and note what evidence or result over the next few years would move it up or down the ranking.
