I'm teaching a graduate seminar on empty names and fictional truth this term, and the debate between the Russellian descriptivists, the pretence-theorists, and the object-theory/Meinongian types has stalled so thoroughly that I'm starting to wonder if we're even arguing about one thing. Thought I'd run it by you as a philosopher of language who's good at diagnosing when a dispute is substantive vs. when people have just defined 'refers' differently. In Russell's On Denoting, after he lists 'the present King of France' among denoting phrases, he says: "Thus a phrase is denoting solely in virtue of its form." And later, the line I keep coming back to: "The interpretation of such phrases is a matter of considerable difficulty; indeed, it is very hard to frame any theory not susceptible of formal refutation." Now apply that to literary talk: 'Sherlock Holmes lived in Baker Street' is true in the stories, false out. 'Holmes does not exist' seems true tout court, but then what is 'Holmes' doing in the first sentence? My colleagues all agree on the truth-values of these sentences and all agree that we are not quantifying over a real detective. Yet we have spent three weeks without moving an inch on whether holmes *refers* within the fiction, or whether we should posit an object that has a special kind of being, or whether that's all just bad metaphysics interfering with semantics. My hunch is the impasse is largely one about what a semantics for fictional discourse is answerable to—coverage of ordinary truth-value intuitions, ontological economy, formal compositionality, or the phenomenology of reading. But I want an honest diagnosis. Act as an expert who studies meta-philosophical disputes in philosophy of language. First lay out two or three plausible diagnoses of why the debate stalls and what kind of disagreement it really is, then commit to the one you think is right and say why, and finally tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis. If your diagnosis is that one side is just wrong, explain how it could be shown wrong without begging the question.
