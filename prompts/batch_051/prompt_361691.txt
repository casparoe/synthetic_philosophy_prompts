Retired biologist here, so I spent most of my career saying a protein has a function without ever spelling out what I meant. Now I'm reading philosophy of biology and keep hitting the split between the selected-effect (etiological) camp — a function is what a trait was selected for in its evolutionary history — and the causal-role camp — a function is what a part currently does that contributes to some system's capacity. I want a working definition of biological function that gets the following cases right, or at least explains why my initial verdicts are wrong:

1. A human heart's pumping of blood. I want this to count.
2. The thumping sound a beating heart makes. I want this not to count, even though a physician can diagnose from it.
3. A synthetic gene for human insulin inserted into E. coli in the lab. It does a job, but the bacterium has no selection history with it.
4. A transposable element copying itself into a new position in a genome. Is self-copying the element's function, a function for the organism, or neither?
5. The human appendix serving as a refuge for gut bacteria. Some people now treat that as its function, but its probably not what it was selected for, and most appendices get removed without obvious harm.
6. A penguin's flipper being used for swimming: descended from a wing selected for flight, now doing something else.
7. The red colour of blood. An effect of haemoglobin, but I cant bring myself to call it a function.

Propose the best single definition you can, run it through all seven, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer; then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. If you end up needing two senses of function, say what each is for. Start with the definition in one sentence. Keep the whole thing under 600 words, and write in British English.
