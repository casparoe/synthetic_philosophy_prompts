I'm a science journalist working on a piece about why some writing systems get a pass while others get mocked, and I want to think through an asymmetry that's been bugging me. English spelling is treated as a national joke: people point out that tough, through, though, and thought look like they share a sound and don't, they roll out ghoti as a joke spelling of fish, and spelling reform has been a minor crusade for centuries. But hardly anyone calls Chinese writing defective, even though you can't reliably read a character aloud from its shape either, a single syllable can correspond to dozens of different characters, and literate adults have to memorize thousands of them. One system gets called a broken mess; the other gets called sophisticated, even beautiful. What I want to know is whether there's a principled difference that justifies that double standard, or whether it's mostly familiarity plus a bit of cultural snobbery about what a script is supposed to do.

The obvious answer is that English uses an alphabet, so its whole job is to encode sounds, while Chinese characters encode meanings, so unpredictable sound is just not a failure. But I'm not sure that survives contact with the facts. English spelling is full of places where letters track morphemes instead of sounds: the plural s is pronunced three different ways without being written differently, and sign keeps its silent g partly because of signature. And most Chinese characters aren't little pictures of meanings anyway; the majority combine a semantic component that hints at the category with a phonetic component that hints at the sound. Neither script is a clean record of speech, and neither is a clean record of meaning. They're rival theories about what a writing system should make visible, and we judge one by a standard we don't apply to the other.

So here's what I'd like you to do. Please make the strongest case you can that English spelling deserves more criticism than Chinese characters, then stress-test it. If you think the asymmetry can't be defended, tell me what a fairer standard would look like: should every script be judged by how well it does what it sets out to do, by how it handles some language-specific tradeoff, or by something else? And end with a hard case. Suppose English started spelling tone distinctions the way it already spells morphology, or suppose Chinese adopted a perfectly regular phonetic script alongside the characters. Would either change your verdict, and why?
