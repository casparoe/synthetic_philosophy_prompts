Two friends of mine got into it at lunch over an actual decision our student council just made. The grade won a chunk of money and the council wants to spend most of it on a big end-of-year carnival — better food, an inflatable slide, the whole thing. But to set up, they need the gym, so they're closing the game room for two weeks. A kid in our grade, Marco, basically lives in that room (he's not into sports and it's where all his friends hang out). He'd lose his spot for two weeks so that two hundred other kids get one great afternoon.

Ben said it's obviously the right call: way more total happiness. The class is like one person deciding to save up for one big treat rather then lots of small ones. Yeah, Marco's bummed for two weeks, but the total comes out positive by a lot, and refusing is just bad at math.

Priya jumped on him for that. She said the class is not a person. Happiness doesn't sit in one big class-brain; it sits in individual heads. A loss in Marco's life and a gain in someone else's life happen in different lives, so they don't cancel the way "skip dessert now" cancels "stomachache tomorrow" when it's all the same person.

Ben came back that Priya was turning Marco into a dictator. If gains to everyone else can't ever offset a loss to him, then even a papercut to Marco would outweigh everyone else's broken legs, and that's insane.

Priya said that's not her view, she's not saying "never." She's saying Ben's doing the wrong kind of arithmetic. The fact that the total comes out positive doesn't make Marco's loss any smaller, because Marco doesn't feel the total. There's a limit on what you can do to one guy no matter how big the number gets, because he's not a jar you can pour costs into and then balance against good stuff somewhere else.

They asked me to referee, and I genuinely don't know — I can't tell weather Ben's math even makes sense across different people, or whether Priya is just squeamish about numbers.
