Write an 8–10 item forced-choice sorting quiz, suitable for a philosophy of mind section on whether qualia are an incoherent posit, with the title ‘Which qualia position do you actually hold?’ Build the quiz around the four properties Dennett isolates as central to the qualia posit in Quining Qualia: ineffability, intrinsicness, privacy, and direct or incorrigible apprehensibility. Let the branches arise from respondents’ answers to concrete cases, not from their preferred labels.

Each question should present a short scenario and offer two or three answer options that reveal a genuine commitment about one of those four properties. At least one question must use the Chase and Sanborn coffee-taster case, where one taster says the flavor has stayed the same but his standards have changed while the other says his standards have stayed the same but the flavor has changed. Also include at least one case involving learned perceptual fineness—for example, a tea taster who can now make discriminations she could not make before—targeted specifically at direct apprehensibility, since that property is often underrepresented in such quizzes.

The leaves of the quiz should include at least: robust qualia realism (qualia have the four contested properties); moderate phenomenal realism (there are phenomenal properties, but at least one of the four fails); representationalism or functionalism about phenomenal character; and Dennett-style eliminativism (the qualia posit as defined is incoherent). Provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns to these leaves, flag any pair of leaves that the questions cannot separate, and note unoccupied answer combinations with a brief diagnosis of whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
