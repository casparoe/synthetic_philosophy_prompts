I am a PhD student in political theory. I have been turning over an asymmetry in how democratic states draw the boundary of the demos, and I want to know whether it can actually be justified. Most of us find it obviously wrong to disenfranchise a long-term resident merely on the ground that she is indifferent to politics or untouched by most of what the state does: the bare fact of being subject to the state's legal order seems to settle the matter. But most of us find no comparable wrong in giving no say whatsoever to a non-resident whose livelihood, environment, or liberty is substantially affected by the very same state's decisions—its climate and monetary policy, its extraterritorial financial regulation, its sanctions, or its control of migration routes. The same fact that does the work inside the border (subjection to the state's decisions) appears to do no work outside it. I want to know whether there is a principle that genuinely generates both judgments, or only an inherited inconsistency we have learned to live with. Please supply the strongest candidate justification you can find, test it against nearby cases such as a municipality answerable to a higher level of government, future people subject to present decisions, and the deeply affected non-resident who pays no taxes, and then give an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept, amended, or given up.

Please give the substantive argument as prose first, and then attach a structured summary in JSON with the following fields:

- asymmetry_restated: one sentence stating the asymmetry as you understand it.
- candidate_justifications: an array of objects, each with the fields principle (the justification, stated in a sentence or two), covers_insider (how it justifies ordinary residents' inclusion), covers_outsider (whether and how it justifies the exclusion of affected non-residents), and weak_point (the strongest pressure point against it).
- strongest_principle: an object with the fields name, statement, and rationale (why you judge it the best candidate).
- test_cases: an array of objects, each with the fields case, verdict_under_strongest_principle, and acceptable (true or false, with a brief note on whether the delivered verdict is one we should accept).
- final_verdict: one of "keep", "amend", or "abandon", with two or three sentences on what, concretely, should stay or change.
