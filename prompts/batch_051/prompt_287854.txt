I'm on the board of my housing co-op and I keep chewing on an idea for how we make our spending decisions. I once read that if every person in a big group has even a slightly better-than-chance chance of being right about a question, and they all think independently, then the majority will be right almost all the time, and bigger groups end up more reliable than individual experts. I don't remember the exact conditions, but lets assume the core claim is roughly true. Here's the mechanism I'm mulling over. Before each vote, every member takes a short private quiz on checkable facts about the proposal at hand: how much it actually costs, what the proposal text says, what happened last time we tried something similar, how the relevant city rules work. Their ballot is then weighted by their score: all correct counts double, all wrong counts zero, and everything in between counts proportionally. A rotating commitee of ordinary members writes the quizzes, and the answer key is posted right after the vote so people can check how things were graded. Ballots stay anonymous; when you finish the quiz, a little scanner grades it and hands you a weight token, and then you drop an unsigned ballot plus the token into the box.

Treat it as a mechanism and tell me how it actually plays out. What do members start doing to get higher weights, and does any of it make them better informed rather than just better at the quizzes? What happens to the quiz-writers, who now decide which facts count as relevant and which edge cases get tested? Where does it get gamed first—factions trying to get a friendly question or two through the rotating committee, answer keys leaking early, people refusing to vote on principle, something else—and do the safeguards I described hold up? And the big question: even if that wisdom-of-crowds logic is true for voters who are all roughly as competent as each other, does weighting votes by a factual quiz make our actual decisions more accurate, or do we just hand more power to people who are good at taking short multiple-choice tests about gutters and parking lots?
