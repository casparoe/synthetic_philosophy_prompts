The doctrinal commission I sit on has been asked to answer a question that turns on one article of our statement of faith, adopted in 1972. The article reads:

'Christ is one Person existing in two complete natures, the divine and the human. In the incarnate Lord each nature remains intact and distinct, with its own capacities and operations, while the natures are so united that whatever is truly said of either nature is truly said of the one Person.'

No one who drafted the article had the following possibility in mind. Suppose humanity eventually encounters intelligent extraterrestrial beings who are finite, rational, and morally fallen like us, but biologically quite different. Could the same Person who has already assumed a human nature also assume their nature and become incarnate among them, so that one Person exists in more than two natures? Or does the article's two-natures language make the human incarnation the only creaturely nature the Son can take up?

Work through the question from four angles: the wording of the article, the purpose of the two-natures doctrine as you can best reconstruct it, what the authors would most likely have said had they been asked in 1972, and what a reasonable reader today would take the article to require. For each angle, say whether it permits or resists the additional incarnation. Then say which of these approaches should govern here, and what you would rule. Finally, say how confident you are in your ruling and identify the point you are least sure of.
