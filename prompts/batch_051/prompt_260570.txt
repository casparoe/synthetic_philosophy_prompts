my friend and I just spent three hours arguing about whether planning is overrated, and I think the argument itself might have a weird twin. his view was that plans are mostly security blankets and that real competence is staying loose and responding to what's in front of you. my view was that what he calls improvisation is actually planning done fast and tacitly, with the plan compressed into taste. after he left I realized our disagreement mapped almost exactly onto the model-based vs model-free divide in reinforcement learning: model-based agents use an internal model of the enviornment to simulate possible futures before acting, while model-free agents just learn cached action values from experience and act without lookahead. planning looks model-based and improvisation looks model-free, but then a jazz soloist is obviously using a huge learned model of harmony at high speed, which makes me worry the analogy predicts something false. is this parallel deep or am I just pattern-matching? if it is deep, which results from RL actually transfer to the human debate—for instance, model-based doing better on sparse reward problems and model-free being more robust to model error—and where does the analogy break down?
