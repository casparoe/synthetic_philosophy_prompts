I teach a graduate-level modal logic seminar and I am writing the final exam. Draft one essay question on the Barcan formula and its converse, and on the charge that the simplest quantified modal logic (SQML) forces the necessity of existence — the claim, formalized as ∀x□∃y(y = x), that every actual thing exists necessarily. It should require students to (a) reconstruct the derivations of the converse Barcan formula and of ∀x□∃y(y = x) in SQML, and pinpoint exactly which move is doing the work — necessitation applied to an open existence theorem, unrestricted existential generalization, or the constant-domain reading of the quantifiers; (b) explain, in varying-domain Kripke semantics, why the Barcan formula corresponds to non-increasing domains and its converse to non-decreasing domains, and why constant domains make both valid; and (c) adjudicate between keeping SQML and accepting necessitism, on one side, and moving to an actualist free quantified modal logic that restricts the offending rule, on the other, giving the strongest consideration for each side. The exam is a three-hour final, answers should be about 800 words, and the question is worth 20 marks. Provide the question, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit moves, the marks each is worth (totaling 20), and a one-line note on the typical student error for each.
