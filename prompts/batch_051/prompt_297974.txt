I'm a backend engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but a colleague recently pressed David Lewis's Convention on me, and one of its ideas has lodged itself in my thinking. My understanding (which I've probably mangled) is roughly this: a convention isn't just a habit or a rule handed down from above; it's a regularity in a recurring situation where everyone would rather fall in line with what everyone else is doing than go their own way, even though alternative regularities would have worked just as well. That conditional preference is common knowledge among the people involved, and once the regularity has some precedent behind it, it becomes self-reinforcing—no one can improve things unilaterally, because as soon as you deviate you stop coordinating with everyone else. Reading this, I realized it describes my own team's stylistic conventions perfectly, including ones we all privately agree are arbitrary.
