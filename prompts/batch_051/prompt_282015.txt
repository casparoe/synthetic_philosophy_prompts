Write me a parody of a glossy city-government pamphlet from a future where the 'fun theory' project has actually been implemented — the project of building a future that is good to live in, not just one that avoids catastrophe. Make it around 600 words, deadpan and cautiously utopian. In this city, the Department of Enjoyment has settled on a single metric, something like aggregate experienced wellbeing, and the pamphlet advertises what follows from that: mandatory morning mood audits, a preference-satisfaction window where citizens file an official Want and have it technically granted, free wireheading clinics for anyone whose scores dip, experience-machine bed units for the chronically dissatisfied, and flow-state work reassignment. The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right, so that someone who knows the wellbeing literature actually laughs: the hedonic treadmill, Nozick's experience machine, preference versus hedonic versus objective-list theories, Goodhart's law, and the difference between removing misery and making life positively good. Don't name-drop any of it — let it be the machinery — and keep it legible for a reader who's never heard the terms. The overall target is the idea that a single number can capture and optimize what makes life worth living.
