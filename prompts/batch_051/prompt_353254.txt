People on the structure-explains-it side of the Great Man debate say the only honest answer to whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler is yes: Versailles, the Depression, and the built-in weakness of Weimar made some such catastrophe overdetermined, and Hitler was just the particular face the crisis happened to wear. But that is an assertion about an unobserved world, not a settled finding. So treat the claim that without Hitler there would still have been a regime that did something like the Holocaust as a testable causal claim, and design the best test you can. Be concrete: what cases or near-miss episodes would you compare, what would you hold fixed, and what result would count for or against the Great Man view? Then tell me what the test could never settle — is there a residue that stays rhetorical no matter how good the evidence gets? American English please.
