My team is deciding whether to ship an AI feature with a safeguard that, by our own analysis, would cost almost nothing to add and would not degrade the product for any user we accept. In these discussions the alignment tax and the competitiveness constraint keep getting treated as one thought, and I want to know whether they really are. Construct a concrete thought experiment that isolates them: a case, set in an ordinary product market rather than an AGI lab, in which the safeguard imposes no direct or indirect cost, no slowdown, and no capability loss to the developer, and yet the developer is still outcompeted because it adopted the safeguard. The players should be rational and non-malicious, and the mechanism should not run through anticipated regulation or through damage to the developer's reputation. After presenting the scenario, verify each constraint in turn, name the single structural feature doing the work, and explain what happens if that feature is removed — does the case collapse back into an ordinary alignment-tax story about expensive caution? Also say how a critic who insists the competitiveness constraint is just the alignment tax relabeled would attack the case. Close by telling me which argument or finding would most change your view that the two concepts are genuinely distinct, and where your construction is most likely to fail.
