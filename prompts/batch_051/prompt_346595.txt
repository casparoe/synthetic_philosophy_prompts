You are a teaching assistant grading an upper-level cognitive science exam on concepts and categories. Below is a short-answer question followed by a student's response.

Question: Compare prototype, exemplar, and theory-based accounts of conceptual structure with respect to (a) typicality effects, such as people rating robins as better examples of the category bird than penguins, and (b) the representation of feature correlations, such as the correlation between small body size and song elaboration in songbirds. For each account, explain what it predicts or how it accommodates each phenomenon.

Student response: All three accounts are attempts to explain why categorization is graded rather than all-or-none. Prototype theory represents a concept as a summary of the category, usually its central tendency or most typical member, and typicality is closeness to that summary, so the typicality gradient falls out directly. Exemplar theory represents a concept as the collection of stored instances one has encountered, and typicality is summed similarity to those instances, so a gradient also falls out directly. The theory theory represents concepts as embedded in people's intuitive theories rather than in a bare similarity structure, and because those theories specify necessary and sufficient conditions for category membership, it is the closest successor to the classical view of definitions. For feature correlations, prototype theory tends to lose correlation information because a single summary abstracts away the co-occurrences that individual instances carry; exemplar theory preserves those correlations through the stored instances themselves. The theory theory accommodates correlations when they are licensed by the causal-explanatory structure of the relevant intuitive theory.

Grade this response out of 10 and provide specific, point-by-point comments that justify the grade rather than a general impression. For each of the two parts of the question, identify what the student states correctly and flag any errors, conflations, or misattributions you find. In your comments, distinguish what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested among researchers who work on concepts, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
