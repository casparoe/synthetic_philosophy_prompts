Over the past few weeks a colleague and I have been arguing about the gene's-eye view of evolution. She holds a substantive version of it: selection really acts on genes, organisms are only their temporary vehicles, and the genic perspective is a claim about where the causal action is in evolution, not just a convenient way of presenting the numbers. I have been resisting: gene-level stories can apparently be rewritten at the level of organisms, or of interacting groups, without losing or gaining anything empirical, which makes me suspect the debate is about notation. We keep going in circles, and I want to know whether any observation could break the stalemate.

Design the strongest empirical test you can of the substantive gene-selectionist claim. Be concrete about the organisms, traits, or genomic patterns you would examine; state exactly what result would favor the gene-as-unit reading and what result would favor the redescription reading; and then say what a positive result would still leave unsettled. I want an honest account of the residue that no test can reach here. Please take a definite position: which test would you actually run, what would you bet it finds, and whether the disagreement is ultimately empirical or conceptual.
