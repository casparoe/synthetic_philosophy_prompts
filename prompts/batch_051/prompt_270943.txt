so i've been chewing on suffering-focused ethics lately after watching my partner go through a long pain condition the doctors mostly couldn't fix, and i'm trying to write up a short "where i stand" note for a reading group. problem is i can't tell whether i've just stapled together positions from different books that don't actually fit. could you do a consistency check? here's roughly what i think:

1. pain and suffering are morally much weightier than happiness. i don't think terrible pain can be "cancelled out" by adding enough pleasure, and maybe no amount of mild pleasure justifies one instance of torment-level pain.
2. but i'm not an ends-justify-the-means person about it. i think torturing one person would be wrong even if it reliably prevented a huge number of mild headaches, and i lean toward side-constraints rather than maximizing.
3. i also accept the asymmetry roughly: there's no duty to create a happy being, but a strong duty not to create a being whose life would be dominated by suffering. so i'm sympathetic to antinatalism at least about the worst-off lives.
4. wild animal suffering hits me just as hard as human suffering, and i think we should be looking into large-scale interventions in nature — redesigning ecosystems, maybe gene-editing predators so their prey stop being eaten alive — provided we're careful and don't make things worse overall.
5. but i really don't think it would be good if sentient life just went extinct. i want to fix sentient life, not end it.
6. and i think the same reasons that make me care about a suffering kid here should make me care about a suffering animal or a suffering person centuries from now; species and distance don't matter in themselves.

wheres the actual conflict, if any? rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, tell me which of these only look like tensions, and say what the cheapest fix is for each real one. and if i had to weaken exactly one claim, which should it be?
