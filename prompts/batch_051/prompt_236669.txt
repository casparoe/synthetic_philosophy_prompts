I help run a small debating league — three schools, nothing fancy — and I've ended up on the rules committee. Our 2014 competition rules say:

'Competitors may not consult with, or receive assistance from, any person during a round. All notes used during a round must be prepared by the competitor before the round begins. A breach of this rule will result in disqualification.'

Last week a pair asked whether they could sit with a laptop running a chatbot during prep time, type in the motion, read its suggested arguments, and then keep the laptop open while they spoke, reading from it if they got stuck. They say the rule only bars help from a person, and that generated text on a screen isn't notes. I have to write a recommendation for the committee before next season, and I want to get the interpretation right: what do the plain words, the purpose of the rule, what the 2014 drafters would have said if asked, and a reasonable competitor's understanding each give? And if we decide to push debate toward being an actual sport, with rankings and prize money and winning as the point, should that tilt us toward a stricter or looser reading here? I care more about the reasoning than about the immediate verdict.
