In my high school philosophy class, I am writing a short position paper on the paradox of fiction, and I want to force my own reasoning into the open rather than settle for "it depends." Please give me your actual credence, as a number between 0 and 1, for the following claim: when an emotionally ordinary adult who fully knows that a depicted death did not really happen is moved to tears by that death, at least one mental state involved qualifies as a genuine emotion of sadness, in the sense that it belongs to the same broad psychological kind as ordinary sadness about a real death and is not merely a pretend, imagined, or make-believe emotion. I am asking for your all-things-considered judgment, not for a survey of positions.

Please decompose that number. In particular, assign a probability to each of the following, giving one sentence of justification for each:

(1) The proposal that a mental state cannot be a genuine emotion unless the subject believes that its object exists or that its occasion actually occurred.

(2) Conditional on that proposal being false, the proposal that fiction-directed responses still satisfy the remaining conditions for genuine emotion, rather than being a distinct kind of quasi-emotion.

(3) The proposal that the felt response is real and full-strength but is actually directed at something other than the fictional character, such as a real person, a real loss, or a real possibility the fiction makes salient.

(4) The proposal that the expression "genuine emotion" has no single determinate meaning across these cases, so that part of the disagreement is verbal rather than psychological.

Treat these as candidate pressure points rather than automatically independent factors, and explain the dependencies you build into your combination. Show how the components combine into your overall credence, and add any further component you think necessary. Finally, tell me which component you are least confident in, and say what kind of thought experiment or introspection would move that component the most.
