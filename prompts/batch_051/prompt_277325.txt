I am a novelist, and I have agreed to lead a three-hour workshop on the pragmatist theory of truth for a group of practising fiction writers and book reviewers. They are thoughtful about narrative and character but have no formal training in philosophy, and they will arrive holding something like a correspondence picture of truth: a statement is true if it matches the facts, and therefore a novel can at best be emotionally true in a merely metaphorical sense. My goal for the session is to move them to a precise, non-caricatured grasp of the difference between Peirce's account, on which truth is the opinion that inquiry would settle on in the long run, and James's account, on which a belief is made true by its practical consequences and truth is what proves good in the way of belief. They should leave seeing these as two different accounts of what truth consists in, not as two temperaments emphasising the same doctrine. Do not explain this distinction to me; give me a plan for explaining it. I want, in order: the dependency structure, which idea must be in place before which other idea can be introduced, what would silently collapse if a step were skipped, and why; then the single example or case that should carry the whole session, with an account of what it must have and what it must not have if it is to separate the two positions rather than merely illustrate one of them; then the full map of misconceptions this particular audience will predictably form, among them the flattening of James into the slogan that whatever is useful to me now is true and the suspicion that pragmatism either licenses wishful thinking or dissolves all facts into preferences, with the exact point in the sequence at which each should be headed off rather than corrected later; and finally a concluding question or exercise for the last fifteen minutes that would show me whether someone has genuinely distinguished Peirce's position from James's rather than merely memorised the labels. Also tell me what I should leave out at this level, and how to flag those omissions so that the writers do not come away thinking the account was evasive.
