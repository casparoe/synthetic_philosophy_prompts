Before I read any more proposals about how symbols acquire meaning, I want to fix the standard of assessment: what any adequate theory of symbol grounding would actually have to deliver. Please respond in continuous prose, as in a letter or email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Take the purpose of the theory to be this: explaining how a symbol-using system, natural or artificial, can have symbols that are meaningful to the system itself rather than merely interpretable by an outside observer, can acquire new symbols without being handed their extensions by a programmer, and can combine those symbols productively into novel claims about the world.

With that purpose fixed, I want the list of requirements, each with the consideration or case behind it. At minimum, address the following, though do not limit yourself to them if something else belongs on the list. Explain why a pure dictionary-style story fails, as in the case of someone who looks up every Chinese symbol in a Chinese dictionary without knowing any Chinese, and why this forces some requirement about grounding bottoming out in non-symbolic capacities. Explain what a theory must say to avoid the Searle-style worry that syntactic competence could reproduce the right behavior while leaving the symbols empty. Include the requirement about compositionality: how a finite base of grounded symbols can ground indefinitely many novel combinations, and how the theory distinguishes a grounded novel claim from a merely well-formed but meaningless one. Include the demands that seem to come from abstract terms such as entropy or the empty set, from indexical and proper-name terms, and from cross-modal integration, where one symbol must bind together very different sensory and motor profiles. Say whether the theory must distinguish grounding from mere covariation or information-tracking, so that a thermostat or a sunflower tracking the sun does not count as understanding, and whether it must say that the meanings are the system's own rather than parasitic on the meanings an outsider assigns to its states. Also address whether grounding is all-or-nothing or comes in degrees, and what a theory must say about how differently embodied agents can share symbols well enough to communicate without collapsing the requirement that grounding be internal to each agent.

I also want the meta-level judgments. State which of the requirements are non-negotiable and which are really preferences of a particular research camp, such as the demand that all grounding pass through sensorimotor interaction. Tell me whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, or whether any requirement implicitly presupposes a solution to some other hard problem such as consciousness or original intentionality. Finally, identify the single requirement that, taken seriously, excludes the largest number of existing theories, and give the reasoning.
