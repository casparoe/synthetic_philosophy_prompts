Take Dennett's color-phi setup: a red dot is flashed in one spot, then a green dot flashes nearby, and people report seeing a single dot move across and turn from red to green around the midpoint. Dennett's point is that the standard retrospective report can be told as an Orwellian story (you really saw a red dot moving, then your memory of the early part got rewritten once the green dot appeared) or a Stalinesque one (consciousness was delayed until the second dot arrived, and the whole edited sequence was then presented at once, so you never consciously saw the unedited red dot). Now vary the experiment: along with the retrospective report, have the observer do a speeded response to the first flash, say pressing a key the moment they see the red dot, and suppose the keypress reliably fires before the Stalinesque delay would allow a conscious experience of that red dot. Does this new datum break the Orwellian/Stalinesque tie, or does it just move the argument one step back—for instance into a fight about wether the keypress was itself a conscious response or a zombie reflex? Work through how the best version of each side would handle the speeded keypress, and say what, if anything, the modified setup shows about there being a fact of the matter as to when an experience happens.
