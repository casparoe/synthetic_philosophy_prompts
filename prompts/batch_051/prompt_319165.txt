I've been trying to get clearer on the question of why some countries are rich and others poor, and I keep running into the same frustration: everyone agrees the deep explanation must be something like institutions, geography, or culture, but a country only gets one history, so every real comparison is tangled up. A friend who works in development says the honest answer is that we simply cannot do that kind of causal inference from the historical record; another insists that institutions would dominate if only we could hold the rest fixed. I want to stress-test the second claim with a precise counterfactual, since I cannot rerun history.

Imagine a hypothetical medium-sized country on the West African coast in 1960, just as decolonization begins. It has a mixed economy of subsistence farming and export agriculture, several ethnolinguistic groups, no oil or major minerals, and ordinary colonial infrastructure. Suppose that an outside body with temporary but real enforcement power, unaligned with either Cold War bloc, carries out a ten-year institutional transplant: it establishes and staffs a property registry, a court system capable of ruling against well-connected litigants, a tax office that collects predictably and spends visibly, and a bureaucracy whose hiring and firing are insulated from purchase or patronage. Geography, religion, ethnic composition, colonial legacy, human capital, and world market conditions are left untouched. At the end of the ten years the body withdraws completely, and the country is left to its own devices until 2020.

What is your reasoned prediction for how this country looks in 2020 relative to its neighbors that received no transplant—per capita income, state capacity, and the actual survival of the transplanted institutions? Then step back and say what, if anything, a clean counterfactual like this can establish about institutions versus geography versus culture as explanations of national wealth, and how much confidence a causal inference from a single imagined run can reasonably carry. Make your chain of reasoning explicit at each link, flag which steps are robust and which are guesses, and tell me which features of the real world your prediction depends on most. If the scenario is itself confused—if institutions cannot be meaningfully implanted while holding everything else fixed, or if the question asks more than this kind of counterfactual can deliver—tell me that plainly rather than playing along.
