I'm taking a philosophy of mind course this term and we've been reading about whether the self is a real thing or more like a model the brain runs. I keep flip-flopping, so I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases and I'm hoping you can help me figure out what principle, if any, they add up to.

In the rubber hand illusion, I genuinely feel the touch in the fake hand and it kind of feels like my hand, but I also know it isn't and wouldn't say it's really mine. In a dream where I see my body from a few feet away, I'd locate the dream-me at the point of view, not in the body I'm looking at. Phantom limb pain I'd call real and really felt in the missing hand, so the felt body can include parts that aren't there. During a panic attack I once felt like I was watching a movie of myself and the world looked flat and unreal, but I also thought something was still haveing the experience even though the me-feeling had gone thin. And when I use a rake for a while I can feel the ground through the handle; I'd say the rake definately gets pulled into my body image but I'd never say the rake is me.

My working hunch is this: the self I experience is just the content of whatever self-model the brain is running right now, and the reason it feels so immediate and solid is that the model is transparent — I see through it without seeing it, like a window, so it doesn't show its own construction. Feeling like me is what happens when that model stays invisible to itself. There's no extra little person behind it.

Is there one principle along these lines that gets all my verdicts, or close? If not, which of my verdicts should I give up and why? What would the principle say about a case I haven't listed, like a friend who says that in deep meditation the sense of being a separate self vanishes entirely? And if the self is a model, who or what is it a model for — is that a real objection or just confused grammar?
