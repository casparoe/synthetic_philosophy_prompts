Two people in my department have spent months arguing about tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level. One camp, the hierarchy realist, maintains that some systems—a formal system that derives facts about its own provability, a mind that represents its own activity, Escher's Drawing Hands—genuinely form a tangled hierarchy: an upper level reaches back down and reorganizes the lower level while the inviolate level of rules and interpretation keeps the whole thing meaningful. The other camp, the deflationist, maintains that the hierarchy and its levels are features of our description rather than of the systems themselves; in the underlying causal story everything is flat, the apparent loop resolves into self-referential tokens, and the inviolate level is stipulated. Write the joint statement their good-faith adversarial collaboration should have produced. It should set out the claims both camps can sign about what a tangled hierarchy is and what makes a level inviolate; formulate the residual disagreement in wording each camp would accept for its own view; isolate the cruxes, especially the difference between a representation's being about a system and a representation's entering into the constitution of the system it represents; and for each crux specify the concrete case or observation both would accept as bearing on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. Also say where the dispute, if anywhere, is merely verbal. Write it in continuous prose, like a letter or email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
