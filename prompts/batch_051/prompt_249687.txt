I am a third-year undergraduate in an astrobiology and existential risk course, and for the final project I need to draft the document that would come out of a year-long adversarial collaboration on the Great Filter. The two parties are a researcher who holds that the Great Filter is overwhelmingly behind us and a researcher who holds that it is overwhelmingly ahead of us. The first maintains that somewhere between prebiotic chemistry and a galaxy-spanning technological civilization there is at least one extraordinarily improbable transition — most plausibly the origin of life, the emergence of complex eukaryotic cells, or the evolution of intelligence — so the apparent emptiness of the universe is unsurprising and carries no warning about our future. The second maintains that simple life arises readily, but civilizations reliably fail before they become detectable or capable of interstellar expansion, through self-annihilation, resource exhaustion, or some other collapse, so the silence is a warning about our own trajectory.

Write the document both could sign after a good-faith exchange. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, then the cruxes and tests. It should include:

1. A numbered list of claims both accept about the relevant observations and constraints: that life on Earth appeared geologically soon after conditions allowed it; that the transition from prokaryotes to eukaryotes took on the order of a billion years or more; that radio surveys have found no confirmed technosignatures; that mid-infrared galaxy surveys have found no obvious stellar-scale engineering such as Dyson swarms; and that the solar system shows no evidence of extraterrestrial artifacts or self-replicating probes. For each observation, specify what both sides agree it rules out, if anything, and what both sides agree it does not rule out.

2. A disagreements table whose rows are the precise residual differences, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position. Distinguish the question of how strong the filter is from the question of where it sits, behind us or ahead of us, and distinguish both from the question of how much we should update our expectations about our own future.

3. A list of cruxes: sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side. I expect these to include whether the rapid origin of life on Earth is evidence for a high probability of abiogenesis or only a selection effect; whether the long delay before eukaryotes marks a genuinely hard step or a contingent fact of Earth's history; whether technological civilizations tend to expand and become grabby, since expansionist civilizations would make silence favor the filter-behind-us position while reliable collapse would make silence favor the filter-ahead-of-us position; and how to interpret the apparent earliness of our appearance in cosmic history.

4. For each crux, a concrete observation or test that both sides agree bears on it — for instance, upcoming biosignature surveys of exoplanet atmospheres, more sensitive technosignature searches, or better dating of the origin of life and the eukaryotic transition on Earth — with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where the two sides would interpret the same result differently, say so and explain why.

Close with a short note on which parts of their original dispute, if any, turned out to be verbal rather than substantive. No bibliography or citations are needed; the work is in the precision of the formulations.
