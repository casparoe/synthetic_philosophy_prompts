(1) In a legal order that forbids involuntary servitude, each individual worker is at liberty to withdraw their labor from an employer at any time and for any reason, thereby ending the employment relationship.
(2) If each individual is at liberty to perform some act, then a group of individuals is also at liberty to agree to perform that very act together, simultaneously, and for a shared purpose.
(3) A strike is nothing other than a group of workers doing together what premise (1) permits each to do alone: withdrawing their labor from their employer.
(4) Therefore, workers already possess a basic liberty-right to strike, antecedent to and independent of any statutory grant or protection.
(5) Consequently, any legal restriction on when, for what aims, or in what form workers may withdraw their labor in concert—such as restrictions on work stoppages called without union authorization, or on stoppages during the term of a collective agreement—is, at least pro tanto, a wrongful interference with an ordinary individual liberty.

I suspect the problem is in step (2), but I cannot say so without seeming to hold that permissible acts become impermissible merely by being coordinated.
