I've been turning over an argument that I keep almost believing and then rejecting because the conclusion seems absurd. It runs like this.

(1) If you publish a knowingly false and damaging story about a living person, you wrong that person.

(2) The wrongness in (1) is not exhausted by the fact that the person suffers now. Part of what makes it wrong is that you have taken a real person's life and used it as raw material for a narrative they never agreed to.

(3) Death does not obviously remove that second feature. A dead person cannot agree or object, but their life is still theirs in some relevant sense, and their reputation can still be distorted — with real effects on how their descendants are treated and how their actual choices are remembered.

(4) So if you make a real historical person the protagonist of a drama and knowingly depict them as worse than the record supports — inventing a murder, say, or giving a self-serving motive to an act that was actually reluctant — you wrong them in the same way you would wrong a living person by defaming them.

(5) But nearly all historical drama and biographical fiction does something of this kind. It compresses timelines, invents dialogue, supplies motives the record does not support, and sometimes gives real people invented crimes or cruelties for the sake of the story.

(6) Therefore nearly all historical drama and biographical fiction is morally wrong.

I do not believe (6), and I do not think the problem is simply that (5) is overstated, because even a careful, well-researched drama will invent private motives and dialogue. I would most like to keep (1) and (2); I am prepared to give up a lot before I give up the idea that using a living person as narrative raw material without their say-so is wrong. But I cannot see how to resist the conclusion without either saying that the dead have no claim at all on how they are depicted, or appealing to a special license for fiction that I cannot yet make precise. Where does the argument go wrong, and what exactly do I pay for whichever fix I choose?
