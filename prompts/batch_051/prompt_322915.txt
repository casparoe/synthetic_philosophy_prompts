Over the weekend I got dragged into a forum thread about whether America is in terminal decline, and one commenter kept answering every objection with “all empires fall.” Point out any advantage the US has—ocean borders, nukes, alliances, whatever—and he’d probly just type “all empires fall” like that settled it. Then a second guy started replying “this time is different,” which the first guy treated as obviously stupid. I’ve got my own opinions but I keep going back and forth on what these phrases would even have to mean to count as arguments. Could you seperate out the distinct precise claims hidden in “all empires fall” and “this time is different”? For instance, is “fall” supposed to mean conquest, total collapse, regime change, or just relative decline; is “all” a claim about the historical record, a base-rate claim about what usually happens to great powers, or a deterministic forecast; and what reference class is silently doing the work (all empires ever, land-based agrarian empires, nuclear liberal democracies, or only the US’s exact situation)? For each reading, tell me whether it’s a platitude, substantive and defensible, or actually false. Then say which reading each guy’s argument actually needs if it’s going to support his conclusion, and whether they’re substantively disagreeing or just using the same words differently.
