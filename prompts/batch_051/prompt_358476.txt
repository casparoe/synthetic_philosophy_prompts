I have a rule about accepting other people's explanations of my own motives. When someone says I did a thing out of envy or spite, I don't accept it just because it fits my behavior; I go inward and try to actually locate the feeling. If I can't find anything like it by introspection, I reject the explanation. My friend calls this the introspective veto and says its worthless, because introspection is constructive: if I go looking I can generate a feeling to fit almost any story, and a failed search usually just means the story is unflattering or unfamiliar. Last week she said I skipped a cousin's party out of resentment, and after finding nothing on introspection I denied it. Is my rule defensible, or is she right? Is there a version that treats introspection as genuinely privileged without letting it veto behavioral evidence?
