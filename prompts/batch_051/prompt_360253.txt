I am trying to determine whether my choices between risky prospects are governed by any single principle, and I would like you either to extract that principle or to tell me clearly that none is there. Below are my verdicts in a series of forced choices. I have recorded them as they actually strike me, not as recommendations. Amounts are in dollars, and I mean them to be final outcomes; nothing else depends on which option I take.

1. Between $2,000,000 for certain and a gamble that pays $2,000,000 with 89% probability, $10,000,000 with 10% probability, and nothing with 1% probability, I take the certain $2,000,000.
2. Between an 11% chance of $2,000,000 (otherwise nothing) and a 10% chance of $10,000,000 (otherwise nothing), I take the 10% chance of $10,000,000.
3. Between $3,000 for certain and a gamble that pays $4,000 with 80% probability and nothing with 20% probability, I take the certain $3,000.
4. Between a 25% chance of $3,000 (otherwise nothing) and a 20% chance of $4,000 (otherwise nothing), I take the 20% chance of $4,000.
5. Between a certain loss of $3,000 and a gamble that loses $4,000 with 80% probability and otherwise loses nothing, I take the gamble.
6. Between a 25% chance of losing $3,000 (otherwise losing nothing) and a 20% chance of losing $4,000 (otherwise losing nothing), I take the 25% chance of losing $3,000.
7. Between $50 for certain and a 1% chance of $6,000 (otherwise nothing), I take the 1% chance of $6,000.

I notice that choices 1 and 2 seem to be in tension. In choice 1, both options share an 89% chance of $2,000,000; if I set that common branch aside, the remaining 11% leaves me choosing between a certain $2,000,000 and a gamble that, conditional on that remainder, pays $10,000,000 with probability 10/11 and nothing with probability 1/11, and I choose the sure thing. In choice 2, both options share an 89% chance of nothing; set that aside and the same 11% remainder appears, yet I choose the gamble rather than the sure thing. I cannot tell whether this should bother me.

There is one further case where I keep changing my mind. Imagine a two-stage game. First a draw that either ends the game with nothing (75% probability) or moves to a second stage (25% probability). Before the first draw, I must commit to how I would choose at the second stage if I reach it, between receiving $3,000 for certain at that stage and a gamble that pays $4,000 with 80% probability and nothing with 20% probability at that stage. I find myself inclined to take the certain $3,000. Yet reduced to final probabilities, the certain-$3,000 option is a 25% chance of $3,000, and the gamble is a 20% chance of $4,000, which appears to be exactly the choice in 4, where I took the other side.

What I am asking for is this. State precisely the principle, or the small set of candidate principles, that best captures all or most of my verdicts. Show how each candidate handles cases 1 through 7 and the two-stage case, and say explicitly which of my verdicts each candidate cannot accommodate and which it forces me to give up. Then tell me what each candidate predicts for a case I have deliberately not taken a stand on: a certain loss of $50 versus a 1% chance of losing $6,000 (otherwise losing nothing), and say whether that prediction strikes you as the kind of thing I would in fact choose, given the rest of my pattern.
