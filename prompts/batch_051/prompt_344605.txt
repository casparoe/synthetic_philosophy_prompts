Please write the short eulogy I am to deliver at a memorial assembly at my high school for a student who died in a car accident four weeks ago. I am a senior; she was a junior whom I tutored in physics and with whom I had become close friends. The audience will include her parents, her younger brother, classmates, teachers, and staff. Many people in the room hold religious beliefs, and I know that the customary way to speak is to say that the dead person is in a better place or is watching over us. I cannot say those things in good conscience. I have been studying Epicurus's Letter to Menoeceus in my philosophy class, and I want the speech to grow out of this passage, in the translation by Robert Drew Hicks:

"Foolish, therefore, is the man who says that he fears death, not because it will pain when it comes, but because it pains in the prospect. Whatsoever causes no annoyance when it is present, causes only a groundless pain in the expectation."

I understand this passage to mean that death itself is not an evil for the person who has died, and that a large part of our fear of death is a fear directed at something that is not yet present. I want the eulogy to comfort the room without telling anyone that their beliefs are wrong. In particular, I want it to do three things: honor my friend as she actually was; address the pain the audience is feeling now as a real pain of the living rather than as something the dead person suffers; and suggest, gently, that some of that pain is bound up with the future we expected to share with her and can loosen if we let the future be what it is. Please do not include the quotation itself in the speech, since I will read it aloud separately, but do build the speech on its meaning. Write it to be spoken in about three minutes.
