A friend group of about a dozen of us has been having this recurring argument about people showing up late or cancelling at the last minute, so we're considering a actual rule instead of just complaining. The idea: every time we make plans, each person puts $10 into a shared pot (same $10 for everyone). When the event happens, you get your $10 back if you show up within 15 minutes of the agreed time. If you cancel with less than 24 hours notice, or you're more than 15 minutes late, you forfeit your $10, and the forfeited money is split evenly among everyone who did show up on time. If you cancel more than 24 hours ahead, you just get your $10 back, and anyone can opt out of an event entirely by not putting in a deposit.

I want someone to actually work through what this would do to us, not whether it's philosophically offensive. Who starts saying yes to things and who stops? What happens to the way we treat each other's time once the punctual people are literally being paid out of the late people's pockets? Where does the first real gaming of this show up — I have some guesses, like two people quietly taking turns being late so the forfeits wash out, or someone scheduling events at times that are easy for them and brutal for everyone else just to farm the pot. And does the whole thing change the texture of the friendship even before anyone has actually lost money, just because there's now a ledger running in the background? Also say which failure modes you think could be patched with a better version of the rule and which ones are probably baked in.
