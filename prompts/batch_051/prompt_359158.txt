I'm in a course on direct democracy and referendums, and our term project is an adversarial collaboration between two positions: (a) that citizen lawmaking through referendums and initiatives realizes self-government better than representative filtering does, and (b) that representative institutions should mostly channel, delay, and sometimes override direct popular votes. Could you draft the joint statement they would both sign after a good-faith collaboration? Please include: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, drawing on Switzerland, Brexit, and California ballot propositions as shared material without turning either side into a strawman; (2) a disagreements table where each side's view is worded the way that side would word it; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions that would actually move at least one camp if settled; and (4) for each crux, the kind of evidence or case both sides would accept as relevant (wording effects, low-turnout votes, money in proposition campaigns, civic-learning from repeated voting, how legislatures treat initiatives after they pass), with each side's prediction stated. Try to keep it from collapsing into voters-are-dumb vs elites-are-self-serving.
