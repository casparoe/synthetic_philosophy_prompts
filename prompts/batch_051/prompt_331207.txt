In my philosophy club after school (me plus four friends and a teacher who mostly just makes sure we don't set anything on fire) one of the guys brought in an argument he says shows the whole "you just have to see it for yourself" thing about art is empty. Every step sounds right when he says it, but the conclusion is hard to swallow, and I want to know where it actually breaks.

His arguement goes:

(1) You can't get aesthetic knowledge from testimony. If a friend who already watched the movie tells you it's excellent, you can reasonably believe her, but you don't *know* it's excellent until you've seen it yourself, because the thing that makes a movie excellent only shows up in your own experience of it.

(2) If that's right for you, it's right for everyone, so aesthetic knowledge can never move from one person to another; it's stuck in each person's own viewing/listening/reading.

(3) If aesthetic knowledge can't move between people, then one person can't be a better judge of a work than another in the way we normally suppose, because being a better judge would partly mean that their judgment puts someone else in a better epistemic position — and (2) says that's impossible.

(4) So either there's no such thing as a better judge of movies, music, games, whatever, or "better judge" just means "likes the same stuff I happen to like," which is a fact about me and not about the work.

(5) But that's crazy. A critic who has watched 3,000 films is a better judge of film than I am, and it's not just that their taste happens to line up with mine.

I really want to keep (5), and I'd even be willing to give up (1) if that's what it costs. But is there a cheaper way out? Which step would you reject, and what do you give up by rejecting it?
