I'm in a philosophy of biology class and keep getting into the same arguement with two of my friends. One of them says that when a biologist says the heart is for pumping blood, or that the eye has the job of focusing light, this isn't mushy metaphor: it's just shorthand for the fact that natural selection is basically a designer who works without foresight. He says selection tinkers with whatever variants show up and keeps the ones that worked, so a trait's history of being selected is what gives it a genuine function—the same way an engineer's intentions give an artifact like a pump its function. He thinks this saves teleological talk in biology after Aristotle's final cause got kicked out of physics. Another friend, a physics major, says this is exactly the old dishonesty: purposes are just efficient causes plus a story we tell afterwards, and the designer analogy sneaks in all the things modern biology was supposed to get rid of, like optimality and direction.

I'd like you to audit the designer analogy my first friend is leaning on.
