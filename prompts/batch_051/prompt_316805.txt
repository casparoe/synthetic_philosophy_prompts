I got into it last night with a friend about whether "meme" is doing real work or is just a loose analogy, and I'm still turning it over. Her line was that cultural items don't actually replicate: they get blended, they're re-interpreted by every person who picks them up, people mash five sources into one, and there's no clean parent copy the way there is with genes. I found myself defending the stronger view — that there is a genuine Darwinian process there, and the messiness is just what replication looks like when the copying is noisy and the mutation rate is high. I'm fairly settled on that now, but I realize I might have set the view up so it can't lose.

So here's what I want: play it straight and tell me what would actually count against the claim that cultural variants genuinely replicate and that selection on them is more than a metaphor. What observations, cases, or arguments should make me downgrade to "it's a useful analogy but nothing more"? I don't just mean the textbook objections; I want to know which ones a committed defender of the replication view would themselves treat as defeaters rather than as noise to be re-described. One thing she said that stuck with me: transmission is more like a recipe being re-executed than a string being copied — each performance gives rise to the next performance, but nothing is literally passed along the way a gene is. I couldn't tell weather that was a real distinction or just the same process described from a different angle. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about whether the view is insulated and whether that's a problem.
