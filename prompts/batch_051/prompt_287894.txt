I've been reading the stats wars on the rationalist-adjacent blogs for years, and I still can't tell whether the committed frequentist and the committed Bayesian are actually disagreeing about the replication crisis or mostly using "evidence" to mean two different things. A friend and I keep having this argument, so we've agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration statement instead of having it a fourth time. He's the frequentist: p-values are fine if you use them correctly, preregistration plus multiple-comparison correction would fix most of the crisis, and priors are just subjectivity with Greek letters. I'm the Bayesian: I think treating p < .05 as a discovery threshold is a big part of what got us here, and that what everyone actually wants when they see a p-value is something like a posterior probability, they just won't admit it.

Draft the document we should both be able to sign. I want:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims, with the obvious-but-important ones made precise: what the replication crisis actually consists in, the role of publication bias, file drawers, multiple comparisons, and low power, what a significant p-value does and does not license, and why a huge fraction of published findings would fail to hold up under either of our accounts of probability.

2. A disagreements table: each row a single precise disagreement, worded so that each side would accept the wording of its own view. No cheap shots we'd each disown on a better day.

3. The cruxes — sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one of us would actually change our mind. I suspect one crux is whether preregistration alone gets you most of the way, and another is whether the problem is the threshold or the whole evidential framework, but push back if I'm wrong. For each crux, give a concrete study design or analysis both of us would accept as bearing on it, and record what each side predicts in advance.

If part of this is a verbal dispute — e.g. we mean something different by "probability" or by "evidence" — say which part and why, but don't use that to dissolve the parts that are genuinely substantive.
