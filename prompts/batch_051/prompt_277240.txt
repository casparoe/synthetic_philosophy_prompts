Most debates about hedonism eventually bottom out in one question: is there actually a single experiential quality that all pleasures share? I would like your credence on that question, not a survey of philosophical positions.

The precise claim: there is one phenomenal quality — felt pleasantness — that is present in every pleasant experience and absent from every non-pleasant one, where "phenomenal" is meant to exclude merely functional or attitudinal commonalities such as being wanted, liked, reinforced, or judged good. I intend the claim to cover ordinary adult human experience; animal or infant pleasure can be set aside unless you judge it decisive. The experiences at issue include the taste of sugar, the moment a toothache stops, finishing a difficult proof, the warmth of being praised, and the mean little lift of taking pleasure in someone else's misfortune.

The stakes are straightforward. Hedonism about well-being is far more credible if there is a common currency that these experiences share, so that "pleasure" names a quality rather than a list. If the taste of sugar and the pleasure of being right share nothing beyond our liking both, then the heterogeneity objection is close to decisive and hedonism collapses into either an attitude theory or a catalog of good things with no unifying account of why they are good.

Please give me one number — your actual credence that the precise claim is true — and decompose it. Assign a rough probability to each of the following, flag any further sub-question you think is doing hidden work, and show how the pieces combine into the final number:

1. Pleasant experiences form a natural kind at all, rather than a family-resemblance collection with nothing genuinely shared.
2. Conditional on their forming a kind, the kind is unified by felt character rather than by a shared causal, functional, or motivational role.
3. Conditional on the first two, the felt character genuinely covers the hard cases — intellectual pleasure, relief, anticipation — and not only bodily or sensory pleasures.

Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, that is, where your reasoning is most likely to fail, and what would change your mind about it. Name the consideration, case, or kind of evidence that would most move you, say in which direction, and say what you would do with your overall credence if that evidence arrived.
