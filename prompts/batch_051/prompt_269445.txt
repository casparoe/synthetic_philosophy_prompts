I teach a first-year undergraduate seminar in philosophy of mind, and my next session is on free will and mechanism. I want to build the entire fifty minutes around the Sphex wasp. In the standard story, the female wasp stings and paralyses a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it on the threshold, goes inside to inspect the empty chamber, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If an experimenter moves the paralysed cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the threshold, and — rather than carrying it straight in — goes back inside to repeat the inspection; if the cricket is moved again, she repeats the loop indefinitely. I want my students to leave with neither the reaction "the wasp is just a mindless robot" nor "we are all just wasps," but with an appreciation of the concept sometimes called sphexishness: the idea that the kind of mechanical rigidity the wasp displays is usefully different from ordinary goal-directed flexibility, yet the difference that matters for free will is not a difference between having goals and lacking them, nor between organisms and machines. Plan how I should teach this. Please do not write the lecture itself and do not assign readings; give me the teaching plan. In particular: (1) what has to be in place before the example will land as intended; (2) the order in which the components should be introduced and what depends on what; (3) the misconceptions students will predictably bring or form — for instance that the wasp is irrelevant to human freedom because she is "just a robot," that her goal-directedness already shows she is not mechanical, and that if our actions were subsumed under rules we would be nothing more than wasps — and where in the sequence each should be headed off; (4) what to say at this level about "could have done otherwise," and what to leave out and why; and (5) a closing question or short task that would show me they have understood the distinction rather than merely enjoyed the wasp's loop.
