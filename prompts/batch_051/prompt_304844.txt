My friend teaches a philosophy of emotion seminar, and I've been bugging her for months about which emotions she thinks are part of a good life and which are just noise. She's weirdly case-by-case about it. So far she's given these verdicts:

- Grief at the death of a parent: counts, part of the good life, even though it's miserable.
- Spite: doesn't count; noise, plain and simple.
- Fear of a genuinely dangerous situation (say, an actually aggressive dog loose nearby): counts.
- Phobic fear of flying, when you know the safety statistics cold and even the sufferer agrees the fear is irrational: doesn't count.
- Boredom on a long, uneventful Sunday: doesn't count.
- Anger at a real injustice: counts.

Next week someone's asking her about envy: specifically, envy at a colleague's promotion that the person believes was undeserved, where the feeling is a little painful jab at the other person having the thing and a wish it hadn't happened, not just a resolve to work harder. I want to figure out what shell say and, more importantly, what principle she seems to be running. Theres probably more than one principle that fits her verdicts so far. Lay out the main candidate rules, say what each one predicts about the envy case, give your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name the further case that would cleanly tell the candidates apart.
