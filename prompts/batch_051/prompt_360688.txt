I'm a grad student in a biology department and I've ended up as the peacemaker between two friends who have been fighting for months about what "function" even means. One of them (call her the selected-effects person) thinks a trait's function is fixed by what it was selected for: the heart's function is to pump blood because hearts that pumped blood were favored, full stop, and anything the heart also does incidentally, like making thumping noises, isn't its function even if doctors find it useful. The other one (call him the causal-role person) thinks a trait's function is just whatever it actually does in the organism's current economy that explains why the containing system works the way it does: if the heart now circulates blood and that's the role it plays in keeping the whole thing going, that's its function, end of story, no history required.

We've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration instead of another semester of talking past each other, and I got stuck drafting the document. I want it to be something both of them could genuinely sign. It should have: (1) a numbered "agreed claims" section, with claims about biological facts, ordinary usage, cases where both accounts give the same answer, whatever, as long as they're nonvacuous; (2) a disagreements section, maybe a table, where each disagreement is stated in wording each side would accept as fair to their own view; (3) a list of cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is whether a brand-new mutation in a single organism can have a function before any selection has acted on it, and another is whether "dysfunction" (like a malformed heart) even makes sense without a selection history. But don't let my guesses constrain you if you think the real cruxes lie elsewhere. The two of them keep reaching for the same examples to score points (hearts, sperm tails, junk DNA, the appendix), so it would help if the concrete cases were as specific as possible and not just re-runs of those.
