It receives only trains of electrochemical impulses and constructs the world we experience from them. The researcher uses this to conclude that our ordinary confidence in direct contact with a shared external world is a useful fiction, and that all perception is really an internal simulation.

The analogy is clearly doing real argumentative work: it borrows a thought experiment designed to raise the most radical skeptical possibility and claims that plain anatomy realizes it. I want you to audit the analogy. Give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of the vat, the nutrient fluid, the electrodes, the computer, and the mad scientist in the real skull case? In particular, what does the vat scenario quietly import that the skull case does not supply — about the origin of the signals, their arbitrariness, the relationship between the signals and their sources, the possibility of action and testing, and the existence of a deceptive or artificial source? Finally, say whether the skeptical conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it, and mention whether there is a better analogy that would carry the legitimate part of the point.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. Tell me how confident you are in the main claims and single out the point you are least sure of. Write in American English.
