I've been going back and forth with a friend about surrogacy and egg donation, and we've both reached the point where the other person's country just seems obviously wrong to us. I grew up in Germany, where egg donation and surrogacy are both prohibited. She grew up in California, where there's a real market: agencies, egg donor profiles, pre-birth orders, surrogates earning tens of thousands of dollars. In much of Western Europe, where surrogacy is permitted at all, commercial surrogacy is banned — contracts aren't enforceable and only reasonable expenses can be paid — and egg donation is either banned or constrained to 'altruistic' donation with at most a capped payment framed as compensation for inconvenience.

What I want is a reconstruction of the philosophical commitments each regime presupposes. What would you have to believe about parenthood, the body, reproductive labor, markets, dignity, and autonomy for the European position to make sense, and what would you have to believe for the American one to make sense? Be careful to seperate what each practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with or happens to coexist with. Tell me where each regime is underdetermined, in the sense that several rival philosophical theories could license the same policy, and point out internal tensions — for instance, why sperm is treated differently from eggs in some European countries, or why paying a surrogate is legal in parts of the U.S. while paying a birth mother for an adoption placement is treated as baby-selling. Finally, say one thing that would have to change in each system if you swapped in the rival view.

Give me exactly six numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
