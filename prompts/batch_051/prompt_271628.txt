I am a high school student who helps run a philosophy club, and two members have been arguing all year about whether increasingly autonomous systems can create genuine responsibility gaps. One member defends the gap view: as systems learn and adapt, some harms may occur in which no human satisfies both the control condition and the epistemic condition for blameworthiness, and since the system itself is not a moral agent, the harm is attributable to no one. The other member defends the distributed-responsibility view: the supposed gap appears only because responsibility is being equated with retrospective blameworthiness; once forward-looking responsibility, role obligations, answerability, and liability are counted, human agents across design, training, auditing, and deployment remain responsible even when no single person deserves full blame.

Please write the adversarial-collaboration joint statement that these two members could both sign. Format it as follows. First, a numbered list of at least eight agreed claims; the claims should be non-vacuous and should pin down what each side accepts about the conditions for blame, the difference between blame, answerability, forward-looking responsibility, and liability, and the role of causal contribution. Second, a disagreements table in which each row states a question in neutral terms, then gives the formulation the gap view would sign and the formulation the distributed-responsibility view would sign. Third, identify at least four cruxes, understood as sub-questions whose answers would move at least one side; for each crux, give a test or case both sides accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction about that test in advance.

Use this running example throughout to keep the disagreement concrete. A delivery robot with a continuously updated navigation policy strikes a pedestrian. In the fault variant, a technician skipped a required sensor calibration, and the robot misread the pedestrian's path. In the no-fault variant, every human followed all reasonable procedures, and the harm arose from an interaction among components and road conditions that no single person could have foreseen in detail, although the combined risk of this type of incident was known at the fleet level. Each side may say what each variant establishes for its own view, but the statement should record those claims as predictions to be tested rather than assumptions.

Finally, state which disagreements, if any, the two members should now classify as merely verbal, and briefly justify that diagnosis.
