I'm writing something about temporal illusions and I keep needing a term but I'm not sure there is one. The setup: a red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right a split second later. People don't report two separate dots; they report a single dot gliding across, and they're confident it turned from red to green around the middle of its path. But at the time when it would have been mid-path, the green flash hadn't happened yet. So the report seems to be filled in backwards, using information that only arrived afterwards. I know there are two standard descriptions of what might be going on: either the person really did experience the moving dot as green mid-path and the brain built the green into the experience from the start, or they experienced something else and a later process rewrote the memory (or the report) before it got out. What I want to know is whether that specific fork — post-hoc rewriting versus building the falsity into the experience from the beginning — has an established name, and whether it's a real distinction or just two ways of telling the same story. If it has a name, where are its edges: what would make a case clearly one rather than the other, and what would make them in principle indistinguishable?
