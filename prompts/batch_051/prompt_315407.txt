I have been working through the controversy over whether Gottlob Frege's late political diary should change how we evaluate his philosophy. Near the end of his life, Frege kept a private diary in which he expressed anti-democratic, nationalist, and anti-Semitic political convictions. Since the diary became widely discussed, interpreters have divided. One position treats it as an external fact about the man: Frege's logic, his rejection of psychologism in favor of an objective science of thought, and his philosophy of arithmetic are to be judged solely by their arguments. A second position maintains that the diary exposes a conception of objective, law-governed thought that is not neutral—that Frege's ideal of pure reason was historically intertwined with an exclusionary vision of who is capable of participating in such thought, and that this connection is a proper part of philosophical assessment. A third position grants that the diary matters for intellectual biography and for the ethics of how we curate the canon, but holds that it neither refutes nor impugns any particular technical claim.

Write an essay on the following question: does Frege's political diary bear on how we should evaluate his philosophy? Reconstruct these three positions, refine them where they are ambiguous, and distinguish at least three senses in which an author's politics might bear on a body of work—causal, conceptual, and ethical. Reach a determinate and justified conclusion; do not merely report that the issues are complex.
