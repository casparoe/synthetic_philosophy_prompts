Write a parody of a global social-media platform's terms of service, around 650 words, formatted as numbered clauses, in deadpan legal boilerplate broken occasionally by breezy platform-speak about believing every voice matters. The platform should be fictional: do not name a real company, statute, or case. The target is the practice of claiming that a global platform can comply with the speech laws of every country at once, and behind it the conflict-of-laws problem of extraterritorial jurisdiction over online speech: a post is written in one country, hosted in another, and read everywhere, so it is never obvious whose law ought to govern it.

The humour should come from getting the conceptual issue right rather than from a general tone of isn't-this-a-mess. Work in the real structural features of the problem: the gap between the place of upload and the place of reception; the idea that a state may assert jurisdiction because content produces effects within its borders; geoblocking as an attempt to turn a borderless network into legal zones; the logic of a most-restrictive-applicable-law default; and the way a single global community-standards document conceals many incompatible community standards. The clauses should each quietly commit the platform on one of these points — a clause on where the user is deemed to have published, a clause on which law governs, a takedown clause that never quite reveals the authority behind it, a clause on geoblocking, and a dispute-resolution clause that locates the user's remedies in a country chosen by someone else. The irony should stay deadpan from beginning to end, and the document should stand as self-consistent legal boilerplate that a careful reader can tell is impossible to satisfy, without the narrator ever saying so.

The piece should be funny to a reader who knows the platform-regulation and conflict-of-laws debate, and still readable to a reader who does not. Use British English spelling and punctuation throughout. After the piece, add a brief author's note stating how confident you are that this is a fair satire of the actual positions rather than a strawman, and identifying the one point you are least sure you have right.
