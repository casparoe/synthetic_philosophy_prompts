I'm a TA in a medieval philosophy class for the first time, and the first batch of essays on Abelard's Ethics is giving me trouble. The exam prompt I have to grade against was, more or less: In his Ethics, Abelard develops his account of sin partly through the example of a man who is tied up and forced into sexual contact with a woman; he experiences physical arousal but does not consent in his mind. Explain what Abelard is trying to establish with this case, and then evaluate whether the view that sin is in consent rather than in the outward act can distinguish between someone who only consents internally and someone who actually carres the act out.

One student's answer went like this. Abelard argues that the man in the example has not sinned because sin is located in the consent of the mind, not in the movements of the body or in the external deed. The man's bodily arousal is just a natural inclination, which is what Abelard means by a vice, and vices are not sins unless we agree to act on them. Since the man does not consent, he remains sinless. From this Abelard draws the further conclusion that the outward act is morally irrelevant: once someone has consented, the sin is already complete, so carrying the act out adds no extra guilt. That means someone who resolves to commit adultery but never gets the chance is exactly as guilty as someone who succeeds. A consequence is that for Abelard any strong desire to do wrong is already a full sin, since it is an internal act of the soul, and internal states are all his account is about.

Could you grade this answer? Give me a letter or percentage grade, but more importantly give the point-by-point comments I'd need to justify the grade to the instructor. I'm less interested in whether you agree with Abelard and more in whether the student has accurately represented what he says and spotted the relevant conceptual issues.
