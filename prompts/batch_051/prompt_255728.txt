I spent yesterday evening arguing with a friend about a position she attributed to Abelard: roughly, that the sin is in the consent and not in the act. She resisted stating the underlying rule in general terms, but she was willing to give verdicts on particular cases. Her verdicts were these. A man who resolves to commit adultery and sets out to meet his lover, but is stopped before he arrives, is fully guilty of the sin. A woman who accidentally smothers her infant while trying to keep it warm in bed commits no sin. A servant who kills his master while the master is attacking him, and who would have fled instead if that had been enough to save his own life, commits no sin. A heretic who has a devout man burned while sincerely believing that God commands it commits no sin. A man who gives a large sum to a hospital solely in order to be admired, and who knows that acting for the sake of admiration is wrong, sins. I then asked her about a new case: a mother whose child is starving takes bread without paying, though she believes that the taking is wrong, doing it only because it is the only way to keep the child alive. She refused to answer. What verdict on this new case would best fit her earlier verdicts? Lay out the most plausible rules that fit all five earlier verdicts, say what each rule predicts about the new case, give your best guess about which rule she is actually using and why, and name a further case I could propose that would settle which rule she holds.
