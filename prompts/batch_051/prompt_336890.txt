Last night a friend and I spent an hour arguing about wether the self could be an illusion, and he ended up leaning almost entirely on "I think, therefore I am." In his hands the slogan meant roughly: doubting that you exist is itself a thought, so there has to be someone doing the doubting, so the self can't be an illusion — you're directly certain you exist. I don't think the slogan gets him nearly as much as he thinks, but I couldn't say exactly where the slide happens. Lay out the different precise claims that line could be making — about what kind of thing the "I" is, whether the "therefore" is an inference or something else, and what "am" actually commits you to — and for each reading say whether it's trivial, substantive, or false, and which of them would actually support his anti-illusion conclusion. I want a menu I can take to our next argument, not just one unpacking of the phrase.
