Me and a couple of mates from a reading group have been going round in circles about 'low-impact AI', and I've started to think the whole thing hangs on one word: side effect. The idea that keeps coming up is a robot put in an unfamiliar room and asked to do something simple, and it does it while leaving everything else about as it found it. But every attempt to turn 'side effect' into something an impact measure could penalise falls apart. One friend keeps saying a side effect is just any difference between how the world ends up and how it would have ended up if the robot had never acted (the baseline). Another says no: it is any change that shrinks what the future could still be — a broken vase matters because afterwards nobody can bring it as a present, while a dust speck moved two centimetres doesn't because you can always move it back. Both definitions sound right until you poke them, which is where we get stuck.

Could you give me a working definition of 'side effect' that an agent could in principle use to decide whether to try to avoid some consequence of what it does? It doesn't have to be a full theory of value, just the side-effect part. Please define it, then run it through these cases and say which ones are side effects under your definition:

(1) The robot is fetching a box from a shelf, takes a wide turn, knocks over a ladder, and the ladder knocks a vase off the sideboard so it shatters.
(2) The same robot, on the same route, warms the room by a few thousandths of a degree just from its motors running.
(3) The robot switches on a lamp so it can see the box; a moth that was heading past the window turns and flies toward the lamp instead.
(4) The robot hoovers the rug exactly as asked; in doing so it unavoidably wears the rug fibres a little, so the rug will need replacing a few months sooner.
(5) To avoid anything like (1), the robot stops in the doorway and does not fetch the box at all; because the box isn't brought in time, the person waiting misses their medication.
(6) A trading algorithm sells a stock exactly as instructed; the sale moves the price, and two towns over a small firm's portfolio loses enough that it lays off a dozen workers.
(7) The robot breaks a vase, but the owner had been meaning to throw that vase out and is secretly glad it's gone.

I'm confident (1) is a side effect and (2) is not, and the rest we argue about. It would help if you said which cases your definition gets wrong or gives no answer on, and whether you'd rather revise the definition or defend the unexpected verdict. I'd also like to know, at the end, wether your definition has smuggled in a value judgement anywhere — because if it has, I'm not sure low-impact AI has dodged the original problem after all.

Please write your answer in British English.
