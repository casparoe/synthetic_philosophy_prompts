Write a sonnet — a Petrarchan one, with the volta after line eight — about what makes every handwritten or printed 'a' the same letter. The octave should be the essentialist's side: the speaker keeps trying to pin down the one feature every 'a' has, maybe by staring at type specimens or old shopping lists, convinced there's a single defining trait just out of reach. The volta should arrive when they actually notice the range — the double-story 'a' in a text face, the single-story 'a' in most handwriting, blackletter, a childs wobbly glyph — and realize no single shape-feature survives across all of them. The sestet should then make the positive move, but not by falling back on 'it's all just convention': think overlapping similarities, a central or prototypical case, membership by family resemblance rather than by a common essence. Make it an actual poem, not versified epistemology, and make it accurate enough that someone who works on concepts and categories would nod — but don't just insert the phrase family resemblance and call it done.
