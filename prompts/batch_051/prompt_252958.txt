I am a retired physicist who has spent the years since leaving the field working through the formal literature on anthropic reasoning, and I have been invited back to my old department to give a seminar defending the self-indication assumption as the correct principle for handling observer selection effects in cosmology. My thesis, stated plainly, is this: the self-sampling assumption, on which one reasons as if one were a random draw from the observers in some reference class, is not the neutral principle it is usually taken to be, because it already presupposes a choice of reference class, and no non-question-begging choice of reference class works for the cases cosmologists actually care about; the self-indication assumption, on which one's own existence is itself evidence favoring theories with more observers, avoids this problem and, once it is formulated so that the number of observers a theory predicts is not treated as a free parameter but as tied to an independently motivated physical mechanism, it also survives the presumptuous philosopher objection (the charge that it licenses favoring a theory merely for predicting more observers, without independent evidence).

The audience will be mostly cosmologists and high-energy physicists, several of whom have used anthropic arguments in the multiverse literature but none of whom, so far as I know, have read the philosophical literature on self-sampling and self-indication. They are comfortable with Bayesian reasoning, with the measure problem, and with the idea that predictions can be conditional on our existence. They are not patient with Sleeping Beauty, amnesia drugs, or anything that looks like a puzzle invented by philosophers rather than a difficulty forced on us by a physical theory.

Draft my Q&A preparation sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, and phrased the way a cosmologist would put them rather than the way a philosopher would. For each, give a short reply of the length I could deliver in about two minutes, and a note on where that reply is soft. End with the question I am hoping nobody asks.
