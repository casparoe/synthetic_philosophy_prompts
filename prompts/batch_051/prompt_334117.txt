I am a PhD student in environmental philosophy, and I am drafting a short statement of my theoretical commitments to circulate before a departmental workshop on conservation ethics. Before I circulate it, I would like an outside audit of how the set hangs together rather than a point-by-point defense of any single item. My commitments, as I presently hold them, are these. I think at least some nonhuman natural entities—individual organisms, species, and ecosystems—have intrinsic value, meaning that their value does not depend on their usefulness to anyone or on anyone's actually valuing them. I also think that many of the values that should guide conservation are relational, coming into being only through ongoing relationships between particular people and particular places, such as long-managed woodlands, sacred sites, and fishing grounds. I think the value of an ecosystem is not simply the sum of the value of its component organisms; composition, history, and functional integrity matter in their own right. I do not think instrumental considerations are irrelevant, and I expect that flood regulation, carbon storage, and recreation will often be the decisive considerations in policy, though they do not exhaust nature's value. I believe that a restored native ecosystem has a kind of value that a well-functioning novel ecosystem lacks, even where the novel ecosystem is species-rich. I believe culling introduced mammals to protect endemic island birds is justified, notwithstanding the suffering this inflicts on individual sentient animals. And I think the death of the last member of a species is a greater loss than the death of an otherwise similar individual belonging to a thriving species.

Could you work through this set and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, which only appear to be, and why? I would like the genuine tensions ranked by severity, with an account of what would have to be revised in each case and what the revision would cost, and I would like you to identify the one revision that would do most to stabilize the set as a whole. I want an audit of joint tenability, not lectures on the individual positions. Please write the response as continuous prose in the manner of a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
