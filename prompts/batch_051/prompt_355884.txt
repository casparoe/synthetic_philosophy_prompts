I am currently taking a course on the epistemology of social behaviour, and we have been working through the literature on observational learning, information cascades, and rational herding. I have been trying to set down my own considered views for a short paper, and I would like an audit of whether the position I am drifting towards hangs together, rather than a general explanation of the models. I find myself inclined to accept all of the following. First, in the standard sequential model in which each agent observes the earlier agents' actions but not their private signals, I take the agents to be rational Bayesians, so conforming to the crowd in that model is individually rational rather than a departure from rationality. Second, when private signals are bounded in strength, I accept that informational cascades are genuine: once the public history becomes strong enough, a later agent should follow it even when their own private signal points the other way. Third, I draw a strong lesson about deference to crowds from this, namely that a newcomer observing a long unbroken string of people choosing the same option should treat the string as stronger evidence in favour of that option the longer it grows; the hundredth adopter still adds evidential weight. Fourth, I also believe that cascades are fragile, in the sense that a modest amount of public information, or the arrival of a few well-informed contrarians, can overturn them quickly. Fifth, I think real-world private signals are better modelled as unbounded or continuous than as bounded and binary, and that with unbounded signals rational observational learning converges to the true state in the long run, so these models do not support durable pessimism about the accuracy of crowds. Sixth, I think observational learning is informationally inefficient: rational players do not fully transmit their private information through their choices, so later players are systematically less well informed than they would be if the earlier agents had reported their signals directly. I would like you to tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and what the least costly revision would be that restores consistency. Please order any tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what that revision would cost, and be specific about which commitment, if any, has to go. I would be grateful if the reply were in British English and written as continuous prose, in the manner of an email or letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
