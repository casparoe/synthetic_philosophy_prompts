Write a dialogue between two epistemologists arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem. Judy has a prior over being in enemy territory versus friendly territory, and over being in the red zone versus the blue zone. She receives a radio message from HQ: if you're in enemy territory, head to red, and if you're in friendly territory, head to blue. She can't tell which territory she's actually in. One character thinks the rational update is to adjust only her conditional probabilities—P(red | enemy) should go to 1 and P(blue | friendly) should go to 1—while leaving P(enemy) alone, because the message is a constraint on conditionals rather than direct evidence about which territory she's in. The other character thinks that's too quick: getting this particular message is itself evidence about what HQ knows, what they can see, or what they chose to say, so P(enemy) should shift under some model of the message-generation process. Make both characters competent, and don't let either side be a strawman. They should react to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other.
