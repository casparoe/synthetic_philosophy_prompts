Suppose a wealthy, politically stable democracy decides, for a single general election, to make the franchise legally transferable. Each registered voter is issued one bearer vote-credential in place of a conventional ballot; the credential may be kept or sold on a regulated public exchange where only natural persons may buy, no buyer may hold more than five credentials, and every transaction is posted with price and buyer. Nothing else about the election changes. Predict, with visible reasoning, how that election and the following decade play out: who sells and who buys, how parties and candidates adjust their strategies, which policies shift, how media and courts respond, and whether the norm against commodifying the vote is weakened or strengthened after the market is shut down. Separate the predictions you regard as robust from those that depend on the cap, the publicity rule, or the competence of enforcement, and say which single result would most overturn your overall assessment if it came out the other way.
