I am a high school student, and I have been reading John Rawls on the distinction between envy and resentment in thinking about justice. The position I find theoretically compelling is roughly this: resentment is the moral emotion that tracks being wronged, while envy proper is not a legitimate basis for claims of justice because it aims at others having less rather than at securing one's own fair share. Its appeal is that it gives a clear criterion for which emotions may bear on public institutions: complaints about being wronged count, while complaints that would simply level someone else down do not. Rawls does allow that envy becomes excusable, and less of a moral failing, when inequalities are large and the society is not in fact ordered by principles all could reasonably accept. Still, the theory treats envy as a reaction to be diagnosed, controlled, or at most excused, not as a source of information about justice.

And yet I cannot get rid of a contrary intuition. When I encounter people who envy those whose advantages are essentially unearned, or who envy standing in a social hierarchy that no one deliberately created, the envy feels like a signal that something is wrong with the background structure, even when no identifiable person can be said to have wronged them. That is, the emotion seems to be tracking a real injustice that ordinary resentment about personal wrongs would miss. I am not asking for a verdict. I want to know what it would take for each side to yield: what would justify me in deciding that this intuition is just a predictable and distorting feature of human psychology and setting it aside, and what would justify me in deciding that the Rawlsian treatment of envy needs to be amended or abandoned. Also, is there a stable middle position on which envy is neither a ground of justice nor a mere vice, but an unreliable indicator that nonetheless merits investigation, and would a Rawlsian be able to accept that without giving up the distinction? Please present both positions at their strongest and give me a negotiated assessment of what each move would cost and what would justify making it.
