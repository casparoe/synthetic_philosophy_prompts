I live on a dead-end street with six houses, and we have this tiny recurring coordination problem. The recycling truck comes every Wednesday morning, and the driver has an easier time if every bin is on the same side of the street. It honestly doesn't matter which side; left is exactly as good as right for everyone, and every house is the same distance from the curb. But people put their bins out at different times, and nobody wants to be the person who has to walk up and down the street at 7 a.m. checking which side everyone else happened to choose.

Someone floated a rule for us: on Monday evening, whichever house first rolls a bin to the curb gets to pick that week's side, and they mark it by putting orange tape on the handle of their own bin. After that first bin is out, everyone who puts a bin out later is expected to put it on the tape side. If the first bin doesn't show up until after 9 p.m., the week defaults to last week's side, or to the left if nobody remembers. And that's the whole rule. No fine, no formal enforcement, no real punishment, except maybe some grumbling in the group chat.

I keep going back and forth on whether something like this can actually turn into a convention of the sort I read about once, where people keep a rule just because they expect everyone else to keep it and they'd rather not be the one who makes the truck's job harder—like everyone walking on one side of a busy sidewalk even though nobody ever decided it. So I want a walkthrough of how this plays out over six months, as if you were watching it from above. Who actually gets stuck as the first mover and how do they feel about it? What happens when somebody throws a bin out early with no tape, or a new neighbor moves in mid-week? Would people follow the rule only when they think they're being watched, or would it stabilize so that even people alone at 11 p.m. follow it? What little side norms build up around it, and where does the whole thing most likely break first? If it falls apart, is there a small tweak that keeps it self-enforcing without introducing actual penalties? Please write the response in American English.
