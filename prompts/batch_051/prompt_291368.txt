I cover science and ideas for a general readership, and I have been unable to settle what the pessimistic meta-induction should do to my reporting. The argument, as I understand it, runs: many of the most successful scientific theories in history—caloric, phlogiston, the luminiferous ether—were later discarded as false, so empirical success is not a reliable sign of truth. If I take that seriously, I should become selectively skeptical: treat some current theories as durable and others as likely future phlogiston. But I cannot find any principle that assigns a theory to one camp or the other, and the scientists I ask tend to suggest that the endangered theories are always in some other field. I want a committed answer, not a survey of available positions: which current, empirically successful theories are most likely to be discarded as false, and what criterion separates them from the theories I can keep reporting as established?
