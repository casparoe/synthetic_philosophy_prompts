Write an Onion-style news article — headline plus roughly 550 words — reporting that a fictional international body, the Office of Heritage Valuation, has determined that a minority language no longer learned by its last speakers' children has "standing intrinsic value," and has therefore retained a team of paid custodians from outside the community to keep the language "alive" by performing it on a regular schedule. The target is the view that a language, tradition, or art form can be valuable independently of the people who hold it. The piece should be a sustained, deadpan reductio of that view: take its actual commitments — that intrinsic value is value a thing has whether or not anyone values it, that preservation therefore need not answer to the preferences of a culture's own members, and that the world can lose something even when a community has chosen to move on — and follow them until the conclusion feels absurd. The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy right, not from inventing a strawman. A value theorist should recognize these moves in the piece and find them fair: the intrinsic/instrumental distinction kept clean, with the Office refusing to justify the program by tourism revenue or any other instrumental benefit, or else letting its hypocrisy be plainly visible; the preservation paradox, in which a practice maintained by paid outsiders for an audience preserves the outer forms while losing what gave the practice its point; and, if the article uses "existence value," that notion handled accurately — the value people place on a thing's merely existing, without using it — and not silently identified with the intrinsic value the Office claims. Include quotes from at least two invented figures: an official or custodian explaining the valuation, and a last-generation speaker who is raising her children in the majority language and is unimpressed. The article should be funny to someone who has never read value theory, while a specialist should be able to reconstruct the position being satirized, and recognize the satire as fair, from the article alone.
