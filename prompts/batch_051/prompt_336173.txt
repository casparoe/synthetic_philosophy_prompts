I have been arguing for years with a friend about whether states have a general right to exclude would-be immigrants, and I have kept an informal record of her verdicts on concrete cases, because her answers have stayed remarkably stable even when mine have not. Her record, as I have it, runs roughly as follows. A retired couple from a wealthy and safe country, with no family in the destination state and ample savings, wish to immigrate permanently and buy a house; the state refuses without alleging any burden, and she says the refusal is within its rights. A journalist imprisoned and tortured in his home country appears at the border and asks for asylum, with no prior ties to the state; she says the state may not turn him away. A citizen's spouse of eight years, with one child, applies from abroad for a family-reunification visa and is denied under an annual quota; she says the denial is impermissible. A healthy twenty-five-year-old from a poor but peaceful country, holding a firm offer of steady dishwashing work and having little in the way of savings, is stopped at the border; she says the state may exclude her. A twelve-year-old who arrived at age two and has spent her entire remembered life in the state, though her parents entered without authorization, now faces deportation with them; she says deporting the child is impermissible, whatever is decided about the parents.

Now comes the case that has us stuck. A thirty-four-year-old construction worker entered legally eight years ago on renewable two-year work permits; he has paid taxes, learned the language, and caused no trouble, and he has no family in the state and no fear of persecution at home. The permits are not renewed and he is ordered to leave, but he asks to be allowed to stay permanently. What would my friend say about him, and may the state exclude him or not? I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the rule she seems to be running, the rival rules that would fit her five recorded verdicts just as well, what each of those rules says about the construction worker, and your best guess about which rule she is actually using and why. Please also state the single further case, not yet in the record, that would do the most work to separate the remaining candidate rules, and say what each surviving rule would predict for it. If her five verdicts are simply not enough to identify a unique principle, it is better to say so plainly than to round her off into a view she does not hold.
