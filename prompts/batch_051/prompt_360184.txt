He keeps insisting that my worries about national character — whether the notion is coherent, whether it's ever acceptable to generalize about a people — are either trivial or confused once you formalize them: a claim about national character is just a claim that the distribution of some trait in a population differs in mean or shape from other populations, maybe with a group-level random effect, and the rest is mood. I can follow him maybe seventy percent of the way; I know what a random intercept is, I can tell fixed and random effects apart, I've internalized partial pooling and shrinkage, and I know what an out-of-sample prediction is. But I think something real fails to survive the translation, and I can't allways say exactly what.

Take the philosophical debate as it actually stands. On one side there's the old view that a people has a genuine collective character, something like a dispositional nature that explains a lot of individual behavior and persists through political changes; on the other side there's the skeptical view that national character is just a bundle of stereotypes plus base-rate neglect, so that any generalization about a people is epistemically worthless and morally suspect. Restate both sides in my colleague's generative-modeling vocabulary — population distributions, exchangeability, shrinkage, measurement error, person-level and group-level parameters, posterior predictive checks, whatever does the work. Be as faithful as the vocabulary allows, and don't just declare the debate dissolved. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no counterpart at all — for instance, the idea that a trait is essentially rather than statistically characteristic of a people, or the normative question whether it is ever acceptable to act on a true statistical generalization about a group. For each item of residue, say whether it was doing real work in the original debate, or whether the original debate would be better off without it. I want to know whether my colleague is right that I keep smuggling in unstatable intuitions, or whether his framework is blind to distinctions that matter.
