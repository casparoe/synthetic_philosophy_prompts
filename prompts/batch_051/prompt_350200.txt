Right, I'm in a second-year epistemology module and my tutor has spent the last three weeks insisting that the whole justification debate is already settled in the first few pages of Aristotle's Posterior Analytics. She keeps reading us the same bit (Bouchier translation):

"Now some persons, because of the necessity of knowledge of the primary principles, infer that knowledge does not exist, while others suppose that it does exist and that everything whatever is capable of demonstration. Neither of these views is either true or necessary. Those who assume that knowledge is not possible at all, think that it would involve an infinite regress, since one cannot know the later terms of a series by means of the earlier when such a series has no primary terms. In this they are right, for it is impossible to complete the infinite. But if there be a limit to the regress, and primaries do exist, they say that these must be unknowable, supposing that they admit of no demonstration, which is the only way of knowing they allow to exist.

The other party agrees with them in holding that knowledge can only be attained by demonstration, but considers that there is nothing to prevent a demonstration of everything being given, maintaining that demonstration may proceed in a circle, all things being proved reciprocally."

Her verdicts on the cases we've thrown at her are pretty consistent. An actually infinite chain of distinct reasons that never bottoms out: not justified. A chain where A is supported only by B and B only by A, with nothing outside the loop: not justified. A chain that stops at a bare assertion, like 'I just believe it, end of': not justified. A chain that stops at the principle of non-contradiction: justified. A chain that stops at a simple arithmetical truth, like 2+2=4: justified. A chain that stops at 'I'm in pain right now' after stubbing her toe: justified. Next week's case is one she hasn't ruled on: someone believes the wall is red, directly from a single quick glance under slightly wierd lighting, with no inference and no further reason to offer, and the belief happens to be true.

I'm less interested in the bare yes/no than in the rule she's actually running. What's your prediction for this case? Which theory fits her record best -- classical foundationalism, moderate foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism, or some hybrid -- and which follow-up case would do the most work in telling the remaining candidates apart?
