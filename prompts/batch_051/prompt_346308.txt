During my years as an experimental scientist I learned to distrust arguments I could not audit, and the habit has stayed with me since retiring into philosophy. Recently I have been trying to understand the proposal sometimes called AI safety via debate. As I understand it, two AI systems argue opposite sides of a question before a human judge who is less capable than either system, and the procedure is supposed to remain trustworthy because a misleading argument can be met with a short, specific rebuttal identifying the defective step, which the judge can check without mastering the rest of the case. I do not see how this survives obfuscation. If one debater defends a false claim with an argument so long, technical, and interwoven that the judge could not audit the whole thing in a lifetime, then the judge's inability to survey the entire argument seems to reduce the debate to a contest of rhetorical endurance rather than an evaluation of the claim. What is the best change to the format—a cap on argument length, a requirement that every step be decomposed into individually checkable assertions, or something else—that would eliminate this failure mode, and can debate still serve its original purpose once the change is made?
