I'm drafting a short position statement on the moral psychology of guilt and shame for a seminar, and I'd like an outside audit of whether my commitments actually cohere before I circulate it. Here, roughly, is where I've landed:

(1) The moral quality of an emotional response is best judged by its characteristic motivational profile — whether it tends toward approach and repair, or toward avoidance, concealment, and retaliation.
(2) By that test, guilt is the better moral emotion. Guilt attaches to a particular wrongful act, keeps the agent's attention on the person harmed, and moves toward confession and amends; shame, by contrast, fixes on the agent's own exposed self and standardly moves toward hiding or striking back.
(3) Yet I also think shame is sometimes the fitting and even admirable response — specifically when someone falls short of a demanding personal or professional ideal, the way an athlete or a surgeon might fail their own standard, even where the lapse is entirely private and no identifiable person has been wronged.
(4) I regard the old anthropological contrast between shame cultures and guilt cultures as overstated: every society has both emotions, and what really varies is the weight a society gives to standing before others as against internalized standards.
(5) I'm skeptical that a culture can choose its emotional repertoire. You cannot eliminate shame or elevate guilt by collective fiat, and campaigns framed as replacing shame with guilt strike me as conceptually confused.
(6) That said, I do think institutional design — courts, schools, online platforms, truth-and-reconciliation procedures — predictably shifts which moral emotions populations routinely feel, and that we should design institutions to favor guilt-and-repair over shame-and-hide.

I can't tell where the strain is, and I don't want to discover it mid-seminar. Could you go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be, and which single revision would do the most to make the whole set consistent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating every pair as equally problematic, and keep the response under 800 words.
