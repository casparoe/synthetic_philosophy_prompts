A student in my graduate seminar on quantified modal logic has produced the following argument, which he takes to show that everything that actually exists exists necessarily. I do not think the conclusion can be right, but I have not settled on where the proof goes wrong.

(1) Necessarily, everything is self-identical: □∀x(x=x). This is the necessitation of a theorem of first-order logic, so it would be costly to reject.
(2) If necessarily everything is self-identical, then everything is necessarily self-identical: □∀x(x=x) → ∀x□(x=x). The student defends this as a harmless commutation of a universal quantifier and a modal operator, and observes that it is an instance of the converse Barcan formula.
(3) Therefore, everything is necessarily self-identical: ∀x□(x=x).
(4) Take any actually existing object, say you. By (3), you are necessarily self-identical. But the sentence "you are self-identical" can be true at a world only if you exist at that world; self-identity of a thing presupposes the thing.
(5) So you exist at every possible world. Since the choice of object was arbitrary, every actually existing object exists necessarily: nothing that exists could have failed to exist.

Where, if anywhere, does this go wrong? I would prefer not to give up the necessity of self-identity, and I am wary of any solution that simply treats existence as an ordinary property of objects; but I want an honest account of what each available fix costs before I commit. Please address it in these sections, in order:

- Diagnosis (about 250 words): identify the exact step to reject or qualify, and say whether the proof contains a genuine misstep or merely forces a choice among coherent philosophical packages.
- Why it looked innocent (about 100 words): explain why the offending step appears superficially valid.
- Costs of the alternatives (about 250 words): spell out what is paid by each main option — rejecting step (2), adopting a free logic, treating existence as a predicate within constant-domain semantics, or another strategy.
- Resolution (about 150 words): give your recommended package and state what it implies about contingent existence.
