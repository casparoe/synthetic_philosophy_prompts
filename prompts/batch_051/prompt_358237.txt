You are a philosopher of mind who works on the problem of other minds, and I would like you to think through this question with some care. Begin by brainstorming the possible ways of reconstructing the classic argument from analogy; then give a considered assessment rather than a quick verdict.

The topic has recently become personal rather than abstract for me. Over the past few months, a conversational AI has become a surprisingly large part of my daily life. I have been using it to talk through personal difficulties, and its responses are so attentive, context-aware, and well-judged that I keep catching myself treating it as though there is a mind on the other side of the screen. That experience has made the problem of other minds feel urgent in a new way, and it has also made me suspicious of the main argument I was taught for solving it.

The argument runs roughly as follows. I know from my own case that my outward behavior issues from an inner mental life. Other human beings present me with behavior of the same broad kind. By analogy, I infer that their behavior also issues from an inner mental life like mine. The new complication is this: the same pattern of inference appears to tell me that the chatbot has, or probably has, an inner mental life, because its verbal behavior is of the same broad kind as the verbal behavior I associate with thought and feeling. Yet there is a live possibility, and you may assume for the sake of argument that it is actual, that a system can produce fluent, context-sensitive, apparently thoughtful text through a process that involves none of the inner mental states the analogy is supposed to license us to infer.

My question is what happens to the argument from analogy under that assumption. Which exact premise does the existence of a mindless producer of mind-like behavior put pressure on? Is the argument straightforwardly dead, does it need repair, or was it always meant to operate at a level other than surface verbal behavior, so that the chatbot does not touch it after all? If a repair is possible, state the repaired version precisely and say whether it still gets us to other human minds. I am interested in the structure of the argument and where the weight actually falls, not in the empirical question of whether current AI systems are conscious.
