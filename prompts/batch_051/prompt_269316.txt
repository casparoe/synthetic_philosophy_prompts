I am a novelist. My current book is set in a farm-animal sanctuary, and for the past year I have also worked there two weekends a month as a shift supervisor, which is why I have inherited a recurring dispute I want to understand well enough to write about precisely. The sanctuary takes in hens from egg operations, goats and cows from failed dairies, and sheep no longer wanted for wool or meat. The animals continue to produce things nobody asked them to produce—eggs, milk, wool—and every few weeks someone makes a proposal about them. A volunteer wants to take home a dozen eggs; a local bakery would like to buy eggs each week; a cheesemaking course would like to buy goat's milk; a wildlife rehabilitator has asked whether we would send wool for nest boxes. The volunteer staff is split between two positions. One holds that the animals are rescued individuals, not resources, and that anything their bodies produce may not be used at all. The other holds a freegan position: when a product is an unavoidable side effect of care the animal needs anyway, and it would otherwise be composted, thrown away, or left to rot, using it creates no new demand for farming animals, and the waste is itself a harm.

I would like a decision procedure that the sanctuary's shift leads can actually run in under twenty minutes when one of these requests arrives, with no philosopher on staff and most decisions made by exhausted volunteers. The procedure should give the questions to ask in order, state what counts as a sufficient answer to each before moving on, say where it stops with a no, and mark the places where a value judgment must be taken to the board rather than settled by the checklist. It should also be honest about which steps look factual but are really moral commitments disguised as rules of thumb. It should not simply split the difference between the two volunteer positions; it should keep whatever each view actually commits us to, and say plainly where no procedure can decide. Then run the procedure on three of the cases above, including at least one familiar volunteer-favorite proposal that the procedure would reject, and explain what the procedure cannot settle on its own. Please end with a single-sentence summary of the procedure as the very last line.
