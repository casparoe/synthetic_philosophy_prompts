Write a sonnet about higher-order theories of consciousness, but don't just summarize them. Let the octave give the ordinary picture: a pain is conscious because it hurts, full stop. Put the volta exactly where the theory does its inversion — the raw first-order hurt only becomes a conscious pain when a seperate higher-order thought represents one as being in it, so an unrepresented pain is nobody's conscious pain. Have the closing couplet say precisely what has to go (the thought that a pain's being conscious is intrinsic to the pain, rather than a matter of its being targeted by a higher-order thought). It should be accurate enough that someone working on these views would recognize the position and not wince.

After the sonnet, add a short note — a few sentences, not part of the poem — saying where the poem leans on the higher-order thought version rather than the higher-order perception version, or otherwise takes a liberty, and tell me which rival account (first-order representationalism, self-representationalism, whatever) would most likely make you rewrite the volta.
