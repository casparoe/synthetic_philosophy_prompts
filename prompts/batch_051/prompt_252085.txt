I'm a retired geologist — spent most of my career in volcanic hazard assessment, so I've had plenty of time to think about risk and low-probability, high-consequence events. Recently I've been getting into philosophy, mostly virtue ethics, and I keep picking at the difference between courage and valor. Here's the working definition I've been using for valor.

For an act to count as valor:

1. The person must accurately grasp that the act carries a serious risk of death or grave bodily harm. Not vaguely suspect it, and not be wrong about it.
2. The person must feel fear, or at least a strong aversion to the risk, and nonetheless act. If someone is fearless due to ignorance or unusual brain chemistry, I don't want to count that.
3. The act must be voluntary in a fairly strong sense: not coerced, not ordered under threat, not done because someone misrepresented the danger.
4. The risk must be taken primarily for a good beyond the person — protecting others, preserving a community, something like that. Pay or recognition can be present, but they can't be what explains the action.
5. The risk must be proportionate. Taking a real chance of dying to save a sandwich isn't valor, it's bad judgment. Though I admit "proportionate" is doing a lot of work here.

Now the case I can't decide about. A colleague's former student is a volcanologist. After a big eruption, a lava lake opens in the crater and the gas plume is so thick that the monitoring stations can't tell whether a larger collapse is coming. The only way to get the needed readings is for someone to rappel to the crater rim and stay there with a gas sampler for ninety minutes; if the floor drops out, that person is dead before the helicopter can do anything. She's terrified, she volunteers, she isn't being paid anything extra, and she's doing it so the towns downslope can get there evacuation warning in time. She comes back fine, and later says a large part of her wanted to run the whole time.

Is that valor? She checks everything on my list, and yet the word "valor" still feels slightly wrong to me, maybe because there's no enemy and no battlefield. So: does she qualify? If not, is it because she fails one of my criteria, or because my criteria were never the whole story — that is, does valor require a martial context I left out?
