I am taking a course in environmental philosophy, and our tutor set us a problem about whether the overall welfare of wild animals should overturn standard judgements about conservation. Please act as a professional philosopher specialising in population ethics and wild-animal welfare. Think carefully through each step; a brief brainstorm of possible failure points before your final answer would be helpful. I want to bracket familiar replies about biodiversity or ecosystem services having value independently of the animals, since the point is to assess the argument on its own terms as a claim about animal welfare. Please answer in British English.

As best as I can reconstruct it, the argument runs:

(1) If a policy foreseeably reduces the number of sentient wild animals whose lives would otherwise have been net-negative in welfare, then, other things being equal, that policy makes the world better.
(2) The lives of most wild vertebrates in an intact ecosystem are net-negative: most die very young through predation, starvation, parasitism, disease, or exposure, and the survivors face chronic hunger, pain, and stress.
(3) Many forms of habitat destruction — clearing woodland, draining wetland, ploughing grassland — foreseeably reduce the number of wild vertebrates that would otherwise have lived in the affected area.
(4) Therefore, many forms of habitat destruction, other things being equal, make the world better.
(5) Conservation and rewilding programmes that protect or restore habitat have the opposite effect: they increase the number of wild vertebrates that live in an affected area.
(6) So, by parity of reasoning, conservation and rewilding programmes, other things being equal, make the world worse.

I find (6) startling, but I cannot confidently say which step, if any, is at fault. Is the argument valid as stated? If not, identify the precise step that fails and the reading of the relevant term or phrase under which it fails. If it is valid, say which premise is the first to be questionable and what hidden assumption it relies on.
