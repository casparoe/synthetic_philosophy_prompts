I'm a novelist, and a scene I'm writing turns on whether a character really knew something or merely got it right. I'm drawn to the classical theory that knowledge is justified true belief: it is simple, unifying, and gives me a clean rule for the whole book. But I also have a firm intuition—one I don't half-disown—that my character would be right by accident, and wouldn't know. Here is the scene. Elena sees a car of the same make and color as her brother's parked in the driveway at the hour he always gets home from work, and on that basis believes he is home. The car belongs to a neighbor who was given permission to park there. But her brother is home: his own car broke down, and he came in two hours early through the back. Elena's belief is justified and true, yet it feels plainly wrong to say she knows. Please act as a professional epistemologist and think the matter through carefully before answering. Give me the strongest case for each side, then a committed verdict: should I drop the intuition, amend or abandon the theory, or is there a reading on which they are compatible—and what would justify each move? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
