Give me a working definition of "side effect" that a low-impact AI could use before anyone has handed it a full spec of human values. I want the definition tested against these cases: a delivery robot told to bring you a mug takes the path through the flowerbed and crushes the petunias (clearly a side effect); the same robot takes the empty hallway instead and leaves a few footprints that fade by morning (I want to say not a side effect, or at least not one worth avoiding); the same robot finds the kitchen handle wet, unscrews the door from its hinges, and leaves the hinges on the counter (unclear); a warehouse optimizer that shifts a few boxes a meter so it can store one more pallet (I think not); a language model that answers your question correctly but leaves you slightly more anxious than it found you (maybe yes?); and an agent that first backs up its entire state so it could in principle undo whatever it does next (is the backup itself a side effect?). Propose the definition, run it through the six cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or none, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. The definition should make it possible to aim at low side effects without thereby needing a full theory of what matters—tell me if you think that's impossible.
