I'm putting together a graduate seminar on divine command theory and the Euthyphro problem as it shows up outside the usual Plato-to-Kant arc, and one week is devoted to the Mu'tazila–Ash'arite debate — the Islamic version of the dilemma, basically. My students have read the Euthyphro and some contemporary divine command theory, but almost none of them know the kalām material, so the document needs to be readable for people meeting the Ash'arites and Mu'tazila for the first time, while still cutting hard enough for the three or four students who do know it.

What I'd like is a draft of the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Mu'tazilite and an Ash'arite on the source of moral value and obligation. Set it up as a numbered list of claims both parties can sign — about God's wisdom, justice, will, and freedom, about reward and punishment being real, about the epistemic authority of revelation, and about how the words "good," "evil," and "obligation" are being used in the debate. Then give a disagreements table where each row states the exact sub-question, followed by the Mu'tazilite's position and the Ash'arite's position in wording each side would actually accept as a statement of its own view. After that, identify the cruxes: the four or five sub-questions such that, if they were settled one way or the other, at least one side would have to revise something downstream. Where possible, say what each side would count as evidence or argument on each crux — for instance, whether the Ash'arite takes "God commands something reason judges horrendous" to be a genuine possibility or a category mistake, and whether the Mu'tazilite's confidence in unaided reason survives widespread human disagreement about acts both sides find obvious. If any part of the dispute looks merely verbal — say, over the word "good," or over whether "reason" already depends on revelation to function properly — say exactly which part and why.

The payoff I'm after for the seminar is that students see why this isn't a stale medieval curiosity. As I understand them, the Ash'arite is afraid that grounding morality in human reason subjects God to a standard outside himself and flatters our capacity to know better than the ones God actually addressed; the Mu'tazilite is afraid that making morality entirely a matter of command makes God's justice unreadable and turns the sentence "God is good" into a tautology. Don't just tell the reader that — build it into the formulations so each side comes across as a live position rather than a straw man. And please don't adjudicate: the document should be one both parties could put their names to, with whatever disagreement remains made as exact as possible.
