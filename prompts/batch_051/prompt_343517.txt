I'm putting together a philosophical self-profile for a reading group on Stoicism and modern emotion regulation, and I want an external audit before I present it. The relevant commitments I have ended up with are as follows:

1. Equanimity is centrally valuable: the less my judgment is driven by anger, fear, craving, or acute grief, the better my decisions track the world, and the less I suffer.
2. The Stoic ideal of apatheia, understood as freedom from the passions rather than flat affect, is substantially correct as a regulative ideal: the mature agent should not undergo passions whose evaluative content is false or disproportionate.
3. But I do not accept the global Stoic claim that every passion is a false judgment. Some negative affects, especially grief at real loss and anger at real injustices, are accurate appraisals of the world and can be instrumental: they mark the stakes, interrupt complacency, and motivate repair.
4. I also hold an instrumental epistemic principle: if a mental state reliably improves my pre-committed aims and long-run accuracy, it is rational to cultivate it even when it feels bad in the moment; if a pleasant mental state reliably degrades those aims, it should be treated as a bias.
5. I endorse a welfare-consequentialist criterion for character traits: a trait is a virtue only insofar as it tends, in realistic contexts, to promote aggregate welfare, including my own; no trait is intrinsically valuable apart from that.
6. I think there is a real difference between suppressing an emotion and not having the passion in the first place through cognitive reappraisal, and the latter is the only defensible form of equanimity: white-knuckled suppression is a stopgap, not an ideal.

I'm asking for an internal consistency audit, not a lecture on which view is right. Which pairs are actually in tension, which merely look like they are, and how severe is each real tension? For each tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest coherent revision is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most while preserving as much of the package as possible.
