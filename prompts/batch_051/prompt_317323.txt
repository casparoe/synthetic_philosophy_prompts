I've ended up fairly convinced by the paradox of hedonism — not just the folk wisdom that you shouldn't chase happiness directly, but the stronger view that pleasure, or at least the kind of happiness worth having, is reliably eluded by direct pursuit and shows up mainly as a by-product of doing things you find worthwhile for their own sake. I want to know what would count against that. What observations, experiments, or arguments should actually move me off it? I'm after a proper map of its exposure: which findings would be decisive, which would only be awkward, and what a defender of the position should themselves concede as counting against it. Also be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — for example by saying that any apparent case of someone successfully aiming at happiness wasn't *really* aiming at happiness or wasn't *really* happy — and whether that's a problem for a claim of this sort. I'd personally find careful longitudinal mood data, or credible cases of people who treat happiness as a direct goal and plainly succeed, more persuasive than armchair counterexamples, but I'm open.
