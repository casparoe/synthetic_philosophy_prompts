I recently read a summary of Émilie du Châtelet's essay on happiness, and I find one of her claims attractive: happiness depends on keeping strong passions alive, and for a thinking person, the most reliable passion is study because it does not depend on other people. She also appears to say that illusion is not always an enemy of happiness, and that we sometimes should preserve the beliefs or hopes that energize our passions even when they are exaggerated. I like this because it explains why people who try to live by pure reason often end up feeling empty, and why a life without strong attachments can feel flat. But I also have a firm intuition that if my happiness rests on a belief I know to be false or inflated, then it is not real happiness; it is self-deception, and I should prefer a sober, possibly less happy life to a pleasant one built on error. I do not want a simple answer about which side wins. I want to know what it would cost to give up the intuition, what it would cost to amend or abandon her theory, and whether there is a reading of false belief or happiness on which both can survive.
