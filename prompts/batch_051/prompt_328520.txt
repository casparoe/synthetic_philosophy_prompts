In Federalist No. 78, Alexander Hamilton writes:

"There is no position which depends on clearer principles, than that every act of a delegated authority, contrary to the tenor of the commission under which it is exercised, is void."

I want you to argue that this specific argument, taken by itself, gives at most a reason to treat unconstitutional statutes as invalid in principle, but does not yet justify the actual practice of judicial review where unelected judges get final authority to interpret the Constitution and strike down legislation. The delegeated-authority analogy seems to me to do less work than people think. Please give exactly five numbered arguments, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Make the arguments connect to something in the quote rather than just listing general disadvantages of judicial review.
