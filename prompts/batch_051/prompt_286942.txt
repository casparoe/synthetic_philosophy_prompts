Here is a passage from Harry Frankfurt's *On Bullshit*:

"The bullshitter may not deceive us, or even intend to do so, either about the facts or about what he takes the facts to be. What he does necessarily attempt to deceive us about is his enterprise. His only indispensably distinctive characteristic is that in a certain way he misrepresents what he is up to."

Please argue for the following position: when a large language model hallucinates — inventing a nonexistent reference, silently altering a real source, or answering a question with false confidence — its output is best classified as an error, not as bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, because bullshit requires an agent that misrepresents what it is up to, and a language model has no enterprise, intentions, or indifference to the truth to misrepresent. In making the argument, first state what the passage entails about how bullshit differs from both lying and honest error; then apply that reading to the hallucination cases, saying exactly where the analogy holds and where it breaks; and then answer the strongest objection from someone who insists that hallucination must be bullshit because, like the bullshitter, the model produces words without regard for whether they track reality. Conclude by saying what, if anything, the hallucination phenomenon reveals that Frankfurt's three-way distinction leaves out.
