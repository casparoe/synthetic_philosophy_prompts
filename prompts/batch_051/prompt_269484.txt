ok so in earth science we've been learning how geologists actually decide where one chunk of time stops and the next starts, and it turns out there's a whole formal procedure: a body like the International Commission on Stratigraphy picks a real physical spot on earth and drives a literal golden spike into it, and that spot becomes the official boundary between, say, the Holocene and whatever comes next. (The Anthropocene still isn't official, I think, and people have been fighting about it for years.) What gets me is that the boundary is both real (it has to reflect an actual change in the rock or the chemistry) and also sort of a committee decision, because they could have chosen some lake bed in Canada or a glacier in Antarctica and the precise year would shift. And once the name exists, the label seems to take on a life of it's own, like scientists and countries arguing about "the Anthropocene" as if it were a thing in itself.

That's basically the same structure I see in the generations discourse. Gen Z supposedly starts in like 1997, or 2000, or 2001, depending on which article you click, and people get genuinely aggrieved about being left out of "Zillennials," a microgeneration that is mostly just a label somebody made up. But underneath there's something real — the internet, 9/11, covid, housing prices — these genuinely shape what a childhood is like. So a generation looks like part real historical change, part arbitrary cutoff, part self-fulfilling label. Pretty much the Anthropocene situation.

So is this a real structural identity or am I just pattern-matching? If it's real, what transfers? For instance, geologists distinguish chronostratigraphy from geochronology (rock units vs. time units, if I remember right) — would it be useful to split "cohort" (just a birth-year range) from "generation" (the shared-experience thing)? And what would the parallel predict that's obviously false? My suspicion is the whole thing collapses because rocks don't read thinkpieces about being an Anthropocene, but I want to know if the mapping goes deeper than that.
