Write a dialogue between two friends, Dana and Priya, who've been arguing about a mutual friend lately. The guy treats his romantic relationship as the single source of all meaning in his life — not just devoted, but he talks about his partner the way other people talk about God, and he's flat-out said he'd be nothing without her. Dana thinks the problem is the object: a finite person just can't bear that weight, so the devotion is pointed at the wrong thing; if he'd aimed the exact same devotional stance at something that genuinely deserved it, nothing would be off. Priya thinks the problem is the mode: the stance itself — treating anything as the thing that makes your life worth living at all — is the wrong way to value something, even if the object were worthy. The dialogue should have them actually pressure-test each other rather than take turns monologuing. Dana should push Priya to explain why her wrong-mode view isn't just Dana's wrong-object view with a universalized scope; Priya should push Dana to say how she can distinguish misdirected devotion from excessive devotion without already presupposing something about how one ought to stand toward a worthy object. Each should give ground on at least one point, and at some point they should try to figure out whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Aim for 500-700 words.
