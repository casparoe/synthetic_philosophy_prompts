I've been reading about orthography and keep running into the claim that a writing system is a theory of a language. As I understand it, the idea is that a script commits itself to some level of linguistic structure — phonemes, syllables, morphemes, or something else — and treats everything else as background knowledge for the reader to supply. What I can't get straight is how far that framing actually goes when people use it to grade alphabets. The received view I've absorbed is that the best alphabet is a phonemic one: one grapheme per phoneme, written the way it sounds. On that standard, English is a bad alphabet, with its though/through/cough, sign/signature, and write/right, while Finnish or Spanish come out as good ones. I'm not sure how much of this to accept. If a script really is a theory, it isn't obvious that the phoneme is the only thing a theory of a language ought to care about, or that fidelity to surface sound is automatically better for readers.
