okay so I read this thing about Frankfurt on bullshit for class and I keep going back and forth on whether a ChatGPT hallucination is bullshit, a lie, or just an error. Frankfurt's idea (as far as I understood it) is that a liar actually cares about the truth in a backhanded way, cause they know it and are trying to get you away from it. But a bullshitter straight up does not care whether what they're saying is true or false, they just care about how it lands. And that seems to fit ChatGPT really well: when it makes up a fake court case, it isn't trying to deceive me and it doesn't even know what's true—it's just spitting out the most plausible next token. So the theory says hallucinating is closer to bullshit than to lying, and it isn't exactly error either cause error is more like aiming at truth and missing.

But now the other side. I have this really firm intuition that bullshit is something only a person can do. If wind blows leaves into a pattern that spells out some false antivax claim, the leaves aren't bullshitting me. And I feel like saying "ChatGPT bullshits" is the same kind of catagory mistake, just with fancier machinery. Bullshit seems to need a bullshitter: an agent who could have cared about the truth and didn't. ChatGPT can't care about anything, can't have intentions, can't even have the attitude of not caring, right?

So which one do I give up? Is Frankfurt's account actually meant to apply to non-persons and the stuff they produce, or should I say the model isn't bullshitting—it's just producing text that a person would recognize as bullshit? And if I keep the intuition, does that mean the word can never apply to an AI no matter how good its act gets? Tell me what would justify each move and whether there's a third reading I'm missing, don't just pick a side.
