Take Bertrand's box: three identical boxes, one with two gold balls, one with one gold and one silver, one with two silver. You pick a box at random and draw a ball at random. In the original you clearly see it's gold, so the probability the other ball in that box is gold is 2/3. Now vary the ending: the light is bad and you don't get a clear look. You have a distinct impression the ball is gold, but in this light your color impressions are right 75% of the time for either color, so you end up 75% sure it's gold and 25% sure it's silver. What should your credence be that the other ball in the box is gold? I want to know how to set the calculation up: should you conditionalize on the proposition that the ball looked gold, with the 75% accuracy as a likelihood, or just feed your resulting 75/25 split over {gold, silver} into Jeffrey conditionalization? Do the two routes agree? If they do, is that only because the prior over boxes was uniform — what happens if the box was picked by a biased procedure? And does the 75/25 split need that known accuracy rate behind it to count as evidence, or is a bare hunch enough for Jeffrey's rule to apply?
