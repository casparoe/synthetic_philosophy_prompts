I am trying to find a single principle behind my verdicts about self-amending rule systems of the Nomic family — systems whose change rules can be applied to the change rules themselves. Here are the cases.

1. A change rule says that any rule may be amended by a two-thirds vote. By a two-thirds vote, the members amend that very rule to say that a majority vote suffices. Verdict: valid; afterwards a majority suffices.

2. A change rule says that any rule may be amended by a majority vote. By a majority, the members replace it with a rule that all future changes require unanimity. Verdict: valid; the system is now bound by unanimity even though the current majority would rather not be.

3. A change rule says that any rule may be amended by a majority vote. By a majority, the members replace it with a rule that no rule may ever be amended again. Verdict: invalid; the rule-changing power cannot use itself to abolish rule-changing altogether.

4. A system adopts, by the prescribed move, a rule reading "This rule is void." Verdict: the rule never comes into force; it is a nullity rather than a valid rule that voids itself.

5. A change rule says that amendments take effect on the first day of the month after passage. On March 15 the members amend that timing rule to say that amendments take effect immediately, and in the same meeting they pass another amendment they want to take effect immediately. Verdict: the other amendment takes effect on April 1; the new timing rule cannot govern its own adoption.

Cases 2 and 3 strain against each other, and cases 1 and 4 both concern a rule referring to itself while I judge one valid and the other a nullity. Is there a precise principle that gets all five verdicts, or nearly all? Give me the principle or the small set of candidate principles, say what work each clause does, tell me which of my verdicts it would force me to revise, and apply it to a case I have not settled: a majority passes a rule requiring unanimity for all future changes and simultaneously passes a companion rule saying that the unanimity rule may itself be amended by majority. Is the companion rule valid?
