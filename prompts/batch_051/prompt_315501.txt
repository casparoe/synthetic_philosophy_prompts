Construct a thought experiment, in no more than two paragraphs, in which two actors—either states or large AI developers—are racing to build frontier AI systems, and both would be better off under a reciprocal pause on further scaling than under a continued race. The scenario must be engineered so that, within James Fearon's rationalist explanations for war, the failure to avoid the race is driven by a commitment problem rather than by private information with incentives to misrepresent. Private information must be present and plausibly relevant—for example, about each side's current compute stocks, algorithmic progress, or resolve—but it must not be the reason the bargain fails. To establish this, specify that the parties have fully revealed the relevant private information, and then explain why the bargain still cannot be kept. The case must satisfy three constraints: no perfect monitoring or enforcement technology, no actors who are simply irrational or indifferent to profit or survival, and no circumstances a real AI developer or state could never face.

After the scenario, demonstrate that it meets the specifications. First, state the available bargain and explain why each party genuinely prefers it to racing. Next, explain why a private-information account gets the case wrong even though private information is present. Then show how the commitment problem does the work: identify the moment of agreement, the later shift in capability or bargaining power, and why today's promise to comply cannot be made credible. Isolate the single structural feature that makes the commitment problem decisive, and say what would happen if that feature were removed—would the case collapse into a standard information-asymmetry story, or dissolve entirely? Finally, write one paragraph in the voice of a critic who favors the private-information explanation, arguing that the apparent commitment problem is really an information problem in disguise; then state what additional detail in the scenario would block that objection.

Write your answer in American English.
