David Hume writes in *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, Section X, Part I:

"All probability, then, supposes an opposition of experiments and observations, where the one side is found to overbalance the other, and to produce a degree of evidence, proportioned to the superiority."

Later in that Part he remarks that the Indian prince who refused to believe the first reports of frost reasoned justly, because those reports concerned a state of nature with which he was unacquainted and which bore little analogy to the events he had uniformly observed; very strong testimony was required to produce his assent.
