I'm a novelist, but I got roped onto my city's Historical Reconciliation Commision, which is deciding whether the city should issue a formal apology for bulldozing the Eastside Black business district in 1927 to build a rail depot. The city attorney's office says the city can't meaningfully apologize today because the corporation has been reorganized and the relevant officials are long dead, so the apology would be a empty gesture. I think that's too quick, partly because of a passage in Hobbes's Leviathan, in the Introduction. He writes: "Nature (the art whereby God hath made and governes the world) is by the art of man, as in many other things, so in this also imitated, that it can make an Artificial Animal." He goes on to compare the commonwealth to an artificial person, and it seems to me that if a city can be treated as continuous for debts, contracts, and property, then the same artifice might underwrite apologizing. But I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I need to circulate something before Thursday's meeting. Draft the short written opinion I'll sign as a member of the commission. It should make a clear recommendation on whether to issue the apology, engage the strongest version of the city attorney's discontinuity objection rather than just brushing it off, and offer a principle the commission can use for deciding when an institution may apologize for historical wrongs. Keep it under 900 words.
