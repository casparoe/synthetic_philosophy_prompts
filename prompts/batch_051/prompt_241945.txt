The exam question and the student's anonymized answer are below.

Exam question: In your own words, explain the Knobe effect and what it suggests about the relation between moral evaluation and the folk concept of intentional action. Identify and evaluate at least two interpretations of the effect.

Student answer: In the chairman study, participants read about a chairman who decides to start a profit-making program and says he doesn't care that the program will harm the environment in one condition, or that it will help the environment in the other. The program then produces the side effect. The agent's beliefs, knowledge, and control are the same in both conditions; the main thing that changes is the moral valence of the side effect. Yet people say the chairman intentionally harmed the environment far more (82%) than they say he intentionally helped it (23%). That asymmetry is the Knobe effect.

The upshot is that ordinary ascriptions of intentional action aren't driven only by factual features like belief, desire, and control. Knobe's own interpretation is that the folk concept is partly normative: the moral status of the effect feeds into whether people apply the word "intentionally," rather than arriving after the intentionality judgment is already made. A second interpretation is the blame hypothesis, which says nearly the same thing but adds a psychological mechanism. On this view, people first see the chairman as blameworthy for harming the environment; then, because blame can only attach to actions done intentionally, they call the action intentional in order to justify the blame. In the help condition there is no blame to justify, so people apply a higher standard. I find the blame hypothesis more convincing than Knobe's view, because it explains the asymmetry without saying the concept of intentional action is itself morally loaded.

In the end, both interpretations make the same prediction for the original two conditions, so the experiment alone can't decide between them. What the Knobe effect does settle is that purely descriptive theories of the folk concept of intentional action are at least incomplete.
