So we did Sleeping Beauty in my formal epistemology class last week and I can't stop thinking about a tweek. Standard setup: Sunday night Beauty knows the rules, the coin is fair, heads means she's woken once (Monday), tails means she's woken twice (Monday and Tuesday), the amnesia makes each waking feel like the first, and on Wednesday she's woken up and the experiment ends. The usual fight is whether her credence in heads on an ordinary waking should be one half or one third.

Here's my version: the amnesia is imperfect. Suppose that on any waking where there has already been an earlier waking in the same run, there's an independent chance p, say 0.2, that the drug fails and she remembers the earlier waking. So on a tails run Monday is exactly as before (there's nothing to remember), but on Tuesday there's a 20% chance she remembers Monday and then knows it's tails. On a heads run there's only one waking, so the question never arises. Beauty knows all of this ahead of time, including p.

What I want to work out: on a waking where she has no memory of any earlier waking, what should her credence in heads be, and does the half-vs-third disagreement survive? The memory case is boring by itself—its obviously tails—but I suspect the possibility of memory is informative in the no-memory case, because failing to remember is now evidence about what day it is or how the coin landed. Run the halfer-style reasoning and the thirder-style reasoning on this version and tell me where each one lands, as a function of p, and whether one of them stops applying or quietly changes its meaning. It would also help to check the endpoints: p=0 should reduce to the original puzzle, and p=1 should reduce to something like "they tell her it's Monday"—but I'm not fully sure that's right, so say if it isn't.
