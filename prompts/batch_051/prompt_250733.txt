I have just moved from academic philosophy of language into a role evaluating retrieval and semantic search systems, and the experience has made me newly preoccupied with the gap between distributional geometry and meaning. Please write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 400–500 words, reporting that a computational semanticist has concluded his marriage is failing on the grounds that the cosine similarity between his and his wife's long-term sentence embeddings has recently fallen below an operationally significant threshold. The humor should come from getting the technical machinery right—cosine similarity, nearest neighbors in high-dimensional space, polysemy and contextualization, the parallelogram analogy, hubness, isotropy, and the conflation of relatedness with similarity—so that an embedding researcher would recognize the field rather than a caricature of it. The article should quietly make the serious point that vector geometry tracks patterns of usage and may be silent about much of what semanticists call meaning, but it must never state that thesis outright. Keep the tone deadpan throughout.
