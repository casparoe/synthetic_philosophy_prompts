I work as a software engineer and I've never had any formal philosophy or theology training, but I grew up around a lot of evangelical churches and lately I've been trying to actually understand sola scriptura instead of just nodding along. The way people usually put it is that scripture alone is the final authority for faith and practice, so you don't need the church or tradition or a pope or whatever to tell you what to believe. But here's what keeps bugging me. The text doesn't interpret itself, right? Somone actually has to read it and decide what it means. And when two sincere people both insist they're just going by what the text clearly says, and one ends up baptizing babies while the other doesn't, or one thinks you can lose your salvation and the other doesn't, there's no obvious way to break the tie without appealing to yet another person's interpretation. Which is just another human authority one level up. So it feels like sola scriptura either collapses into a regress where the interpreter you pick becomes the real authority, or it collapses into private judgment, where whatever I decide the text means counts as scripture's authority. In coding terms, it's like having a single source of truth but no agreed parser for it, so whoever writes the parser silently becomes the authority. I'm guessing there's some distinction I'm missing, maybe between authority and interpretation, and I'd rather have someone point out the confusion than hand me a reading list.
