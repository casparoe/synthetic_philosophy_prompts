I've been trying to work out what actually gives an oath its binding force, and the image I keep returning to is the old one of gods swearing by the Styx: the thought that some oaths bind even beings who answer to no one and can be held to nothing by force. I want a principle that captures my case-by-case reactions, and I'm ready to be told that some of those reactions have to be revised.

Here are my verdicts. A promise extorted at gunpoint does not bind. A deathbed promise to a dying parent to look after a younger sibling does bind, even after the parent is gone and there is no one left who could hold the promisor to it. An eight-year-old's blood oath of lifelong secrecy does not bind. An atheist swearing on a Bible in court to tell the truth is fully bound by that oath. An oath obtained by fraud, when the person was misled about what she was swearing to, does not bind. A solemn vow made while keeping one's fingers crossed behind one's back does not bind. A vow to do something that later becomes impossible dissolves, though the vow-maker may still owe some substitute or compensation. An oath of office to uphold a constitution binds even when the officeholder privately rejects several of its clauses.

Is there a single principle about the source of an oath's bindingness that gets all of these, or nearly all? I have toyed with candidate principles — enforcement, reliance by the recipient, the seriousness of the thing sworn on, the intention to be bound, freedom from coercion, capacity to understand — but each handles some cases and fails on others, often in ways that surprised me. Please respond as a professional philosopher working on promises and commitments. Think carefully, begin by brainstorming candidate principles and testing each against the evidence above, then state the best one, or a small set of the most defensible candidates, as precisely as you can; say what each clause is for; identify which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate; and apply it to a novel case of your own devising as well as to these two, which I keep going back and forth on: (a) a deeply religious person swears an oath on a relic she regards as sacred while the recipient privately regards the relic as worthless — is the oath binding, and does it matter that they do not share a view of what was sworn on? (b) A normally sober person makes an earnest but uncoerced vow while very drunk and the next morning cannot tell whether she meant it — does that bind?
