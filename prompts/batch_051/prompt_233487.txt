I have recently been asked to evaluate a proposal to use a debate protocol, in which two AI systems argue before a human reviewer, as an oversight mechanism for a model my organization is considering deploying, and I have been reading the AI-safety-via-debate literature to assemble the known limitations. So far my list is this: the obfuscated-argument problem, in which a dishonest expert can make a bad case easier for a limited judge to follow than the honest case; the observation that debate cannot manufacture knowledge, so it stalls when neither debater actually knows how to expose a false claim; the worry that a debater can win by exploiting the judge's known biases rather than by being right; the concern that the training game may settle into degenerate equilibria in which the debaters collude or jointly optimize for persuasiveness; and the regress problem for compound claims, where adjudicating a hard question requires adjudicating subquestions that are just as hard. I want one more limitation or objection that is not on this list and is not a paraphrase, special case, or combination of anything listed. Ideally it should have a different structure, perhaps concerning the relationship between the debaters and the question being debated or the choice of the question itself. After giving it, tell me honestly whether you think it is a strong objection and how it relates to the ones on my list.
