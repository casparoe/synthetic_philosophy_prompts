In the volunteer fire company where I serve, every member owes the same basic duties: two night shifts a month, regular training nights, and routine equipment maintenance. But there has always been a larger unevenness beneath these requirements. A handful of members do considerably more than the rest of us — they turn out for nearly every call, cover other people's shifts at the last minute, take on the unglamorous cleaning and paperwork, and occasionally put themselves at risk in ways no one would have punished them for avoiding. Others do exactly what is required and no more. We have mostly avoided addressing this, telling ourselves that the extra service is given freely and that its being freely given is precisely what makes it valuable.

At our last meeting, someone proposed regularizing it. The company would keep a merit ledger. Any member could nominate another for an act that went beyond the standing requirements, and in some versions of the proposal a member could nominate themselves. A small committee would decide whether the act qualified, and each approved act would earn a merit slip. A slip could be redeemed to be excused from one ordinary duty — a shift, a training night, a maintenance day — with no reason required, and no member would be permitted to criticize the absence. The ledger of earned and redeemed slips would be posted openly. Slips could not be sold or given away, though a member could redeem one to excuse some other specific member from a duty, with the gift recorded in the ledger.

Some members think this would make going beyond the call of duty visible and lightly reward it. Others think it would poison the very thing it is meant to honor. I am less interested in whether the scheme is good in the abstract than in how it would actually behave if we adopted it. Walk through its incentives and its likely second-order effects. What would it do to the people who already give the most, to those who are exactly dutiful, and to those who do as little as they can? How would it be gamed, and where would it break first? Would it change what going beyond the requirements means in the company, and could any of its failure modes be fixed without gutting the scheme?
