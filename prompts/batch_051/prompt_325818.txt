I cover the science-and-ideas beat, and I'm putting together a piece on geometric rationality and Kelly betting. Rereading Kelly's 1956 paper *A New Interpretation of Information Rate*, this sentence stick out to me:

"Let us consider a communication channel which is used to transmit the results of a chance situation before those results become common knowledge, so that a gambler may still place bets at the original odds."

Kelly's gambler is basically a price-taker: he's betting at odds set before the wire information arrives, and his own bets don't shift those odds. What I want to know is what happens to the standard "maximize expected log wealth" argument when you drop that implicit assumption. Say the gambler is instead trading in a modern in-running market where the odds move as he sizes his position, or where there's a limit order book with limited depth and his orders walk the price. Does the geometric-rationality interpretation still apply, or does Kelly's setup secretly rely on the gambler being small enough relative to the market? Walk me through how this passage applies, or doesn't, to that case—I'm less interested in the practical trading answer than in whether the theoretical justification survives the price-taker assumption being removed.
