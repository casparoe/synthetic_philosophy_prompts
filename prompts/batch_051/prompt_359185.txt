I'm in an upper-level undergraduate seminar on experimental philosophy and moral psychology, and I'm drafting a one-page position profile on the Knobe effect to circulate before I lead discussion. In the classic setup, a company chairman is told that a proposed program will increase profits but will also harm the environment; he replies that he does not care about the harm and only wants to maximize profit; the program runs and the environment is harmed. Most people say the chairman harmed the environment intentionally. In the parallel case where the side effect helps the environment and the chairman again says he does not care about that side effect, most people deny that he helped the environment intentionally. So the same indifference is described in both cases, but the moral valence of the side effect flips the intentionality judgment.

Here is the set of claims I want to defend:

- C1: The asymmetry is a robust feature of competent use of the concept of intentional action; it is not a performance error, a demand characteristic, or an artifact of the vignette wording.
- C2: The right explanation is evaluative-first: the side effect is classified as bad or good before or while the intentionality judgment is formed, so the concept of acting intentionally has an irreducible evaluative ingredient.
- C3: The evaluative ingredient is the attitude the respondent attributes to the agent (for example, the chairman's indifference to the harm) rather than the respondent's own first-order moral verdict. The help case does not support attributing a praiseworthy attitude, and that is why the helpful side effect is not counted as intentional.
- C4: The effect is specific to side effects rather than means: when an outcome is adopted as the chosen means to the goal, people judge both good and bad means intentional, so the valence effect is localized and should not be generalized to all foreseen consequences.
- C5: Underneath this, the folk concept of acting intentionally is largely a tool for tracking who is apt for blame or praise, which is why morally relevant information gets recruited into the classification.
- C6: I am also convinced by the negative program in experimental philosophy: vignette-elicited intuition reports are too sensitive to morally irrelevant presentational details such as order, wording, and affect to serve as direct evidence about a concept; the only respectable object of study is the pattern of variation across cases, not any individual verdict.
