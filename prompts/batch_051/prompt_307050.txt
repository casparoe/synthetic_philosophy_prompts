I'm a philosophy of biology PhD student, and I keep running into the same problem when people argue that some trait—a behavior, a morphological structure, a cognitive bias—is an adaptation. The argument usually has the form: here is the trait's current function, here is how it would have increased fitness, therefore selection produced it. That inference has known gaps, and the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique presses on several of them, but I want to get clear, before any particular case, on what would actually close those gaps.

Act as a philosopher of biology who takes the spandrels critique seriously but also believes adaptationist explanation can be legitimate. Think carefully and reason out loud as you go. Start by brainstorming candidate requirements, then prune and organize them into a defensible specification.

The deliverable: the desiderata that an adaptationist account of a trait must meet before we should accept it as more than a plausible narrative. For each desideratum, say what case or principle makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Separate, if you think it right, three kinds of demand: (1) evidential—what must be shown about variation, heritability, and actual differences in fitness; (2) functional—what it takes to show the trait is for something rather than merely useful now; and (3) exclusionary—which rival processes, such as drift, correlated response to selection, developmental constraint, exaptation, or byproduct status, must be argued against, and to what standard. But do not assume those three categories are the correct ones if you think they cut the space badly.

Then evaluate the list itself. Which desiderata are non-negotiable, which are merely good methodological practice, and which might be the biases of one research tradition? Are any of the requirements jointly unsatisfiable—and if so, does that indict the desiderata or adaptationism? Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most work against Panglossian just-so stories, and explain why it removes more bad explanations than the others do.

Do not turn this into a literature review, and do not give me citations or a reading list; I am asking for the conceptual specification as you would defend it, not a report on what others have said.
