In rationalist accounts of war, war presents a puzzle: because fighting is costly ex post, there should normally be a negotiated bargain that both sides prefer to the war they actually fight, so war must be traced to a failure of bargaining. Fearon organizes these failures into three mechanisms: private information about capabilities or resolve together with incentives to misrepresent; commitment problems that make promises not to exploit a future shift in power non-credible; and issue indivisibility that leaves no mutually acceptable split available.
