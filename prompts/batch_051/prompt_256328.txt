I run a small reading group of mostly LessWrong-literate friends who know enough game theory to have opinions but not enough to have had the backward-induction paradox actually bite them. Whenever I explain the centipede game — two people alternate passing or grabbing, the pot grows each time someone passes, and the backward-induction argument says a rational first mover should grab on the opening move — the reaction is always "oh, clever," and then everyone goes back to assuming that passing is obviously the cooperative, human thing to do. I want a 90-minute activity for ten to sixteen partipants where the people run into the paradox themselves, in their own choices, before I ever name it. I want the payoff structure to do the teaching, not a lecture afterward.

For the design, I'd like a sequence of live variants: a baseline pair-play round with the pot growing fast enough that passing feels generous; a round against a known backward-induction-bot role; a precommitment variant where players can pay a small amount to bind themselves to pass at least once (does that dissolve the paradox or just relocate it?); and a fixed-role round where one player is instructed to always pass and the other always grabs, so the group can see how much of the unraveling comes from common-knowledge-of-rationality vs. actual bad will. Include concrete payoff numbers, timings, role cards, and a debrief script that maps the moment someone grabs early "because the other guy would grab on me" onto the regress of "I know that she knows that I know..." without me having to spell it out. Also flag what predictably goes wrong when you run this — a couple of people will defect on round one to look clever, or everyone will pass forever out of social awkwardness — and how to handle it. Be honest about which part of the paradox the room can't reproduce: presumably the full common-knowledge assumption, since nobody in the classroom has an infinite hierarchy of beliefs, plus all the reputation noise of grabbing in front of friends. If any of my variant ideas is confused, say so and replace it rather than forcing it in.
