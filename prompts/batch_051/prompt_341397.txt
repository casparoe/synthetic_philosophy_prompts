A colleague has offered the following step-matched reconstruction of Anselm's ontological argument and Gaunilo's island parody. I want to know exactly where each of them gives way.

Anselm:
(A1) 'God' means a being such that nothing greater can be conceived.
(A2) A competent speaker who grasps that definition thereby has that being at least in the understanding.
(A3) For any being that exists only in the understanding, a greater being can be conceived: that very being, but existing in reality as well.
(A4) So if God existed only in the understanding, something greater than God could be conceived.
(A5) But by (A1), nothing greater than God can be conceived.
(A6) Therefore God does not exist only in the understanding; God exists in reality as well.

Gaunilo:
(G1) 'The greatest conceivable island' means an island such that no greater island can be conceived.
(G2) A competent speaker who grasps that definition thereby has that island at least in the understanding.
(G3) For any island that exists only in the understanding, a greater island can be conceived: that very island, but existing in reality as well.
(G4) So if the greatest conceivable island existed only in the understanding, a greater island could be conceived.
(G5) But by (G1), no greater island than the greatest conceivable island can be conceived.
(G6) Therefore the greatest conceivable island exists in reality — which is plainly absurd.

Is (A1)–(A6) valid, and if not, at which step exactly does it fail? Is (G1)–(G6) a faithful substitution instance of the same pattern of reasoning, and if so, does the absurdity of (G6) show that at least one premise of Anselm's argument must be false? If the parody is not faithful, say precisely which premise fails for islands but not for God, and why that difference is a genuine disanalogy rather than an ad hoc rescue. I am particularly interested in whether the weakness lies in (A3)/(G3) — treating existing in reality as something that makes a thing greater — or in (G1), the idea that there is a determinate maximum of island-greatness at all.

Please answer in British English.
