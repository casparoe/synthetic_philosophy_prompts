I cover evolutionary biology and adjacent life sciences for a science desk aimed at educated general readers, and I am putting together an explainer on why the units of selection debate is still live. I need a decision tree that a working journalist without population-genetics training could use to classify any evolutionary explanation encountered in a paper, a press release, or a popular book, sorting explanations by what they actually claim rather than by the vocabulary the author happens to use. No branch may ask which label an author adopts, and no leaf should require the reader to know a named book or author.

The tree should be built from the logical space rather than from a list of names. The diagnostic questions should separate cases along the lines that actually drive the debate: whether the explanation claims a trait evolved because it benefited the group at a cost to individuals within it; whether the supposed process is meant to be translatable into individual-level inclusive-fitness accounting; whether the entity doing the competing is a replicator, an organism, a kin group, a social group, or a species or clade lineage; and whether group-level effects are invoked as causes of selection or merely as downstream consequences. Include one question that forces the user to distinguish the beneficiary of an adaptation from the unit whose differential survival or reproduction is supposed to do the causal work; this distinction is routinely collapsed in non-specialist coverage, and I want the tree to make it unavoidable.

Please aim for seven to nine leaves. At least these positions should appear: naive group selection in the good-of-the-species sense, gene's-eye adaptationism, organism-level adaptationism, kin selection construed as individual-level inclusive fitness, multilevel selection with distinct group-level adaptations, species selection, and a bookkeeping-only or neutralist position. Each leaf should carry the position's name and a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. For every combination of answers the tree permits that has no occupant, state whether the cell is empty because it is incoherent, empirically unattractive, or merely underexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate from the questions as posed; I suspect kin selection and multilevel selection will be one such pair, and under some formulations gene's-eye and organism-level adaptationism another. For each flagged pair, give one further question that would separate them if the user had more information. Keep every question answerable from a text without already knowing the taxonomy, and keep the whole instrument readable by someone meeting the debate for the first time.
