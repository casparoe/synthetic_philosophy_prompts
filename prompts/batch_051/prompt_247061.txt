Was up until 2am arguing with a friend about this and it's still bugging me. He used to work on a fish farm and I've been reading about welfare standards for farmed fish, and we got stuck on one clause in a certification standard. Section 7(b) of the slaughter chapter reads: "A fish may not be slaughtered unless it has first been rendered insensible. Insensibility shall be assessed by the absence of a response to a tail pinch or other noxious stimulus; a fish showing no such response is deemed insensible and may be bled out." The standard was drafted back when basically every certified farm used a percussive stunner or electrical stunning before gill-cutting, and the tail-pinch test was just the audit check.

The farm my friend worked at has since switched to an ice slurry — they pump the fish into near-freezing water to quiet them before cutting the gills, which the drafters plainly never had in mind. In the slurry the fish stop responding to a tail pinch within seconds, so the farm says it has complied: no response, deemed insensible, bleed. But the farm's own vet flagged that cold can suppress movement and pain responses without making a fish unconscious, and a university trial at a sister farm apperently found EEG activity for several minutes after fish stopped responding in the slurry. My friend thinks the text lets the farm off, since the clause says "deemed" — it doesn't require actual insensibility, it makes the absence of a response the operative test. I think that's legalistic nonsense; the point of the clause was plainly to stop fish being bled while conscious, and the tail-pinch thing is just a fallible proxy for that.

I'm not asking about the practical decertification question. What I want is the interpretive one: walk me through what the words themselves, the clause's purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked about ice slurry, and what a reasonable reader would take the clause to mean in this case each give. Then tell me which reading should win here and why. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
