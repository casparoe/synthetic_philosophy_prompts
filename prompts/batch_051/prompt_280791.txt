I am a novelist. My current project follows a composer who spends thirty years on a string quartet no one will perform, and in writing it I have had to face the fact that the book is really an argument about when, if ever, an excellent thing that helps no one is still worth making. I have arrived at a set of convictions I genuinely hold, but I suspect they do not all sit easily together. I would like them audited before I let them structure the novel's moral climax.

The commitments are these:

1. A work can be excellent even if no one other than its maker ever encounters it. Its artistic value is a feature of the work itself, not of its reception.

2. The only way a person can be benefited or harmed is through the character of their conscious life. Nothing counts as good for a person unless it makes some difference, actual or prospective, to what they experience.

3. Completing a difficult and excellent work can be good for the person who completes it even when the making is mostly misery, it destroys their relationships, and the work is never shared. The achievement itself is an element of flourishing, not merely an instrument for later satisfaction.

4. When an unknown masterwork is discovered after its author's death, that discovery adds to the work's value; it does not merely change what readers know about value that was already fully there.

5. The existence of an excellent manuscript in a drawer, never to be read, makes the world more valuable simply by existing. This is not just a claim about what could happen if someone found it.

6. A consideration can speak in favor of doing something only insofar as it tracks benefits to, or harms avoided for, conscious beings. A consideration that would touch no one's experience cannot be a reason.

Please go through these as a set rather than as a list of slogans. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which pairs merely look that way once a key distinction is drawn? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say precisely which element of which commitment would have to give and what the least costly revision would be. Then step back and tell me which single revision across the whole set would most improve coherence, and what I would be giving up in making it. I would rather have the fault lines located sharply than be told which positions are widely held. Please write the audit in American English.
