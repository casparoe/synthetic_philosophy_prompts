This came out of a real incident. A spec I was handed had two P0 requirements that turned out to be mutually exclusive given the migration order we were locked into, and the postmortem basically resolved it by deciding one of them was never really a P0 after all. That made me remember half of an argument I once overheard about whether "genuine moral dilemmas" exist: situations where you are morally required to do A and morally required to do B, you can't do both, and there's no hidden principle that already says which one wins. One side says every apparent dilemma is just a spec error — if you truly can't do both, you were never truly required to do both; there's always an actual priority somewhere, even if nobody noticed it at the time. The other side says the conflict can be real: no matter which one you pick, you violate a real requirement, and even when you did the only right thing you're left with a debt or a stain that isn't cancelled by choosing correctly.
