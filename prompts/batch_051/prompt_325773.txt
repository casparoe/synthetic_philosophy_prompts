ok so I'm setting down my actual views on letterforms for a seminar (the "what makes every a an a" problem, Letter Spirit-style) and I want a consistency check before I present. Here's the list: (i) there is no set of shape features that all and only a's share; letter categories are family resemblance / prototype kinds. (ii) what makes a mark an a is ultimately its role in a script system, and the geometry is just evidence. (iii) style is a property of a whole grid or typeface, not of individual glyphs. (iv) a neural net trained on nothing but glyph images, with no information about function, could still recover most of the letter categories, which makes me think the categories are partly in the shapes and not only imposed by our practices. (v) a lone mark that happens to look exactly like some font's a but belongs to no practice of reading or writing is not really an a; being a letter is relative to a practice. (vi) I also hold that the single-storey and the double-storey a are the same letter, while a Latin a and a Greek alpha are different letters even in fonts where they look closer than many a's do to one another. Go through and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, which only look like they conflict, rank the real tensions by severity, and tell me for each what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and which single revision would clean up the most.
