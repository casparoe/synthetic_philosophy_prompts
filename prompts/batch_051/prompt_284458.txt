The fifth preliminary article of Immanuel Kant’s Toward Perpetual Peace says that no state shall forcibly interfere with the constitution or government of another state. Consider a case its drafters could not have foreseen: State A conducts a covert cyber operation against State B during a national election, altering voter-registration records and disabling vote-counting systems in targeted districts. State A does not deploy troops, blockade ports, fund a coup, or install a client government; its stated aim is to discredit the electoral process and induce a constitutional crisis. Read in its eighteenth-century terms, does the article apply to this case? Read as a general principle protecting a people’s right to determine its own constitution, does it apply? If the two readings diverge, which should a present-day Kantian follow, and does the absence of physical force make a difference?
