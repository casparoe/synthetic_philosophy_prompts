So I spent three hours last night arguing with a friend about whether the wavefunction is a real thing out in the world or just a sophisticated bookkeeping device for what we don't know. His line was that ψ can't be part of reality because it lives in a huge configuration space, it does this weird collapse thing, and two people with different information can assign two different wavefunctions to the very same system — which sounds like ψ tracks knowledge, not the world. I kept saying that's too fast: a map can be abstract and still represent real terrain, and the fact that ψ changes when we measure doesn't automatically make it epistemic, since any probability assignment gets updated on new information too. The thing is, after three hours neither of us could actually say what observation would tell us apart.

Heres where we got stuck. Suppose there's some underlying objective state of the world (call it λ) that quantum mechanics only lets us see partially. If ψ is purely a representation of our ignorance, then two different pure states — say |0⟩ and |+⟩ — could in principle overlap in λ-space: there could be a single real underlying state that is compatible with both quantum descriptions. If there's that kind of overlap, then maybe you could build a situation where you catch the world in the overlap region and get predictions that quantum mechanics says should be impossible. If there's no overlap, then ψ starts to look like part of reality itself, not just our summary of it. My friend says this is all just philosopher hand-waving and untestable either way. I suspect there's a concrete protocol that bears on it, but I couldn't make one precise.
