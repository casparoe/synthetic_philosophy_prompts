Suppose a comparative cognition journal adopts a publication rule meant to operationalize Morgan's canon. Under the rule, any paper that attributes a "higher" psychological process to a nonhuman animal—planning, grief, episodic memory, a concept of fairness, etc.—must also report the authors best attempt at a deflationary associative or reinforcement-learning model of their own data, with the comparison preregistered. The higher-level story is only allowed in the title and abstract if it beats the deflationary model on out-of-sample prediction; if it loses or ties, the paper still gets published but the title has to name the deflationary account. Walk me through the incentives this creates for grad students, PIs, reviewers, and the journal. What experiments get designed under this regime, where does it get gamed first, and does it select for real evidence of cognition or just for scientists getting very good at baroque behaviorist models? Are the failure modes fixable without hollowing the rule out?
