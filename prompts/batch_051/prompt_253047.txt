I'm a high school student who writes and performs music, and I've been trying to figure out what I actually think a song is. I have clear gut reactions to particular cases, but I can't tell what principle, if any, lies behind them. Here are my verdicts.

1. I wrote a song last year — melody, chords, and lyrics — and I've performed it many times since. When I play it faster one night and slower the next, or skip a verse, I don't doubt that it's the same song. The song seems to survive any changes in a single performance.

2. My friend recorded me playing the song on his phone. If that file is deleted, I don't think the song has been destroyed. So the song can't be identical to the recording, or to any one performance.

3. A jazz singer performed a well-known standard with the melody heavily altered and a new improvised verse. I still think she performed the same standard, not a new song.

4. A producer took a two-second sample of my vocal, changed its pitch, looped it, and placed it under new lyrics and a new beat. I don't think the result is my song, and I'd hesitate to call it a performance of my song at all.

5. I transcribed my guitar song into piano sheet music, and a pianist played it from the score. It sounded quite different in timbre and register, but I think the pianist performed my song.

6. A cover band reproduced my song note-for-note from the original recording. I think they performed my song, but I also feel the song itself isn't any of these performances — it's something they all have in common.

7. I imagine a composer on the other side of the world who has never heard my song but independently writes the exact same melody and chord progression, with different lyrics. I'm genuinely torn: the music is identical, but it seems wrong to say she wrote my song.

I'm trying to find one principle about what a musical work is that captures these verdicts, or most of them. Please state the principle precisely, and connect each clause to the case that motivates it. Then tell me what the principle says about a case I haven't settled: a band that re-records its own song twenty years later with a completely new arrangement. And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate, and why.
