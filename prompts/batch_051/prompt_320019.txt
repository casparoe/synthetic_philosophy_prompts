I teach an upper-level aesthetics seminar on humour, and I keep catching myself grading the four classic families — superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation — against a different set of cases every week depending on whatever happens to come up in discussion. Before we read another paper, I want to actually write down the spec that any adequate theory of humour has to meet, so I can hold all four of them to the same standard. I'm not just interested in "what makes this joke funny." I want the full list of requirements: the phenomena a theory has to cover or consciuosly set aside (puns and wordplay that get groans rather than laughs, deadpan, tickling and physical comedy, nervous laughter at funerals, laughing at someone's pratfall or humiliation, inside jokes that are opaque to outsiders, dark or cringe humour, the same joke told twice, things that are funny without being jokes at all, and so on), the distinctions it can't collapse (funniness vs. laughter vs. amusement vs. comedic value; a property of an object vs. an experience vs. a person's sense of humour), and the psychological facts it shouldn't contradict (timing, surprise, incongruity detection, social context, or whatever you take the non-negotiables to be). For each requirement, say why it's a requirement rather than just a preference of one camp, and mark which are negotiable and which aren't. If you think some subset of the requirements can't be jointly satisfied — a theory that handles social-relief laughter and pure wordplay at once having to gerrymander, say — tell me, because that's the part I most want to think about. Then say which single requirement does the most work in knocking out the four classic theories, and briefly run the four theories past the list.

I'd like the answer as a JSON object with this schema: a field "requirements" whose value is an array; each item has "name" (short label), "description" (one or two sentences), "motivating_cases" (a small array of examples the requirement is meant to cover), "why_it_constrains" (the consideration that makes this a genuine constraint on a theory of humour), "negotiable" (one of "non-negotiable", "negotiable", or "camp-specific"), and "if_negotiable" (a string explaining which camp demands it and why, or null when non-negotiable). Also include "jointly_unsatisfiable_subsets": an array of objects each with "subset" (an array of requirement names), "diagnosis" (why they pull against each other), and "resolution_options" (how a theory might choose between them). Finally include "most_discriminating_requirement" (a string) and "scorecard": an array of objects each with "theory" (one of the four), "passes" (array of requirement names it handles well), "fails" (array of requirement names it handles badly), and "overall" (one sentence).
