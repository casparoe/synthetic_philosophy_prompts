Here's an asymmetry I keep noticing and can't decide whether it's real or just a vibe. When planetary scientists reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet, Pluto didn't read the press release, join a protest group, or change its behavior in response to the new label. When oncologists introduce a more detailed staging system for a cancer, the tumour doesn't read the staging manual and adjust its growth accordingly. But when psychiatrists introduce or tweak a diagnostic category — say, borderline personality disorder, or the shifting criteria around autism — the people classified do read about the label, form communities around it, reinterpet their own histories through it, change how they present to clinicians, and sometimes campaign against the criteria. The object of classification seems to respond to its classification in the psychiatric case but not the astronomical or oncological case (I think some people call this a looping effect). And in practice a lot of people then treat psychiatric categories as softer, less stable, or less real than cancer staging or planetary categories, as if the looping were itself a sign that something is epistemically shakier. My question: is that move justified? First, is the difference between the two cases a genuine difference in kind, or just a difference in how fast and visible the feedback is — after all, a cancer classification also changes research agendas, funding, treatment, and eventually the people with cancer, just through a longer and less legible loop? Second, even if it is a genuine difference, does it actually justify demoting psychiatric categories, or does a category that shifts in response to its objects just call for a different kind of realism rather than less realism? Be honest about whether the whole asymmetry might be overdrawn.
