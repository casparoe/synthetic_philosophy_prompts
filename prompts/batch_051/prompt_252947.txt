I'd like a piece of sustained satire in the form of an internal Trust and Safety calibration memo for a social media platform whose stated policy is to apply the harm principle, and nothing but the harm principle, to all speech: content is removed only when it harms someone other than the speaker. About 700 words, deadpan and bureaucratic, with a short attached rubric or scoring worksheet that moderators use to triage reported posts. The running joke should be that the memo is a good-faith attempt to keep the policy Millian while quietly widening 'harm' until it covers whatever the platform wants removed. The worksheet should allow moderators to count secondhand offense as harm, to count predicted downstream consequences without requiring the chain of causation to be short or probable, and to assign a score under a category like 'harms to the information environment' in which the platform itself is the injured party. Include a section on incitement that in practice licenses the heckler's veto, and a calibration note saying that treating harms to the audience's peace of mind or to the speaker's own judgment as out of scope would be naive. The piece should land, without ever stating them, the standard objections: that the harm principle only does real work if there is a principled line between offense and harm; that indirect harms are potentially limitless; and that once the health of the platform or of public discourse counts as a harm, the principle is no longer excluding anything. It should be legible to someone who only vaguely remembers On Liberty, and funnier to someone who knows the offense–harm distinction, the incitement exception, and the usual debates about where the line gets drawn.
