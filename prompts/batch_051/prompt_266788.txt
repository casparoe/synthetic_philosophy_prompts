I’m a developmental psychologist with a background in behavioral ecology; I think comfortably in terms of reaction norms, sensitive periods, canalization, niche construction, and the distinction between a trait’s plasticity and its evolved norm of reaction. I’m trying to get precise about what Mencius is doing in this exchange from the Mencius, Gaozi I (James Legge’s public-domain translation):

“The philosopher Gao said, ‘Man’s nature is like the qi-willow, and righteousness is like a cup or a bowl. The fashioning benevolence and righteousness out of man’s nature is like the making cups and bowls from the qi-willow.’ Mencius replied, ‘Can you, leaving untouched the nature of the willow, make with it cups and bowls? You must do violence and injury to the willow, before you can make cups and bowls with it. If you must do violence and injury to the willow in order to make cups and bowls with it, on your principles you must in the same way do violence and injury to humanity in order to fashion from it benevolence and righteousness! Your words, alas! would certainly lead all men on to reckon benevolence and righteousness to be calamities.’”

On one reading, Mencius is denying only that morality is an arbitrary cultural imposition; on another, he is making an empirical-developmental claim that the human organism has an intrinsic, end-directed tendency toward virtue, analogous to a biological norm of reaction that requires a supportive environment to express. I want you to restate Mencius’s reply to Gaozi systematically in that vocabulary, as faithfully as possible, while separating the descriptive-developmental strand (what humans are like) from the normative strand (what counts as virtuous or how we should treat people). Then report what does not survive the translation: which of Mencius’s claims map cleanly to a reaction-norm or niche-construction picture, which map only with distortion, and which have no counterpart. Is the residue mostly a sign that my vocabulary is too thin on normative notions, or that Mencius’s argument is doing something other than developmental explanation? Also, could Xunzi exploit the parts that fail to translate to defend his rival view that our nature lacks such an intrinsic moral direction and that ritual and sages must reshape it?
