You are a senior policy adviser in the foreign ministry of a mid-sized liberal democracy. The cabinet must decide within ten days whether to contribute troops and air assets to a regional coalition preparing to intervene in Tamaria, a country where a government-aligned militia has begun a campaign of ethnic cleansing against the minority Eshari population. Authorization from the United Nations Security Council has failed twice: one permanent member has vetoed any enforcement action. The coalition, led by neighbouring states and one outside power, is invoking the example of Kosovo in 1999 — acting without a Security Council mandate on the claim of overwhelming humanitarian necessity, with regional backing and a stated objective limited to protecting civilians. Opposition voices in your country reply that Iraq in 2003 shows how humanitarian rationales can harden into pretexts once the original justification collapses, and that Libya in 2011 shows how a protection mandate can slide into regime change and leave a collapsed state in its wake.

Draft the decision memorandum to the cabinet, recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The memorandum should reason as a policy analyst actually would, using the criteria of just-war and humanitarian-intervention analysis — just cause, last resort, right intention, proportionality, probability of success, legitimate authority, and the post-intervention burden — rather than merely listing them. It should state the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly and without caricature, and indicate what evidence, if it came to light before the vote, ought to change your recommendation. The document should read as internal policy paper rather than an academic essay: a clear bottom line at the top, sections if useful, and no footnotes. Aim for 800-900 words.
