My friend reviews war games and she's oddly consistent about them. She hated Shockfront, the big-budget first-person shooter set in a made-up modern war where kills give you score streaks and the campaign is basically a hero fantasy; she called it pure glorification. She was totally fine with Bulge '44, a dry hex-and-counter sim of the Battle of the Bulge, no story, just counters. She said the one where you play a civilian stuck in Sarajevo during the siege, no combat, just awful choices, is one of the most serious games about war she's ever played. And she was grossed out by Gumdrop Commando, a cheerful top-down arcade shooter with a cartoon fictional war and points for kill streaks.

Next up she's reviewing a game I'll call Draftee: first-person, set during the 1968 battle for Hue in Vietnam, you play a conscript, the shooting is clumsy and horrible, no score, no rewards, no hero music, deliberately grim. I don't really care whether she likes it so much as what rule shes been running. What are the plausible rules that fit her verdicts so far, what does each predict about Draftee, which one fits her best and why, and what single future game would settle it cleanly?
