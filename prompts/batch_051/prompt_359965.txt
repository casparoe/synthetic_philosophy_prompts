A mid-size software company is worried its engineering culture has gotten too soft: people go easy in code reviews, nobody wants to be seen as cutthroat, and the same few senior folks end up carrying the hard stuff. They propose a "rival pair" system. Each quarter, every engineer gets randomly assigned a rival at the same level and from the same rough performance band. The pair is given one stretch project on top of their normal work, and at the end of the quarter a panel of managers and peers rates each person's contribution. The higher-rated one gets a fixed bonus and the other gets nothing; but if the panel certifies that the project couldn't have met its bar without both of them, both get a smaller bonus instead. Each person also has to review their rival's ordinary code that quarter, and either party can ask to be re-paired after six weeks with no questions asked. Walk me through what this actually does to who signs up for what, to code review quality, to information sharing, to how the panel ends up rating people, and to whether people get tougher or just more strategic. Where does it get gamed first, and is there a tweak that keeps the edge without the sabotage? Also tell me how confident you are in the main predictions and which single prediction you're least sure about.
