I've been rereading Eugene Wigner's 1960 essay, *The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences*. He argues there that the mathematical language we develop largely for its own beauty turns out, again and again, to be precisely what we need for the laws of physics, and that this fact is itself an unexplained miracle — something empirical rather than derivable from first principles.

Here's the thing that's bugging me. Rationalist-types often cite Wigner when they want to defend the idea that the universe is a mathematical structure, or that elegance tracks truth. But then in their actual epistemic practice — when debunking some elegant but unverified physics theory — they say beauty is not truth and treat elegance as a cognitive bias. Doesn't Wigner show that they're being inconsistent? Surely if the effectiveness of maths is a miracle, then a theory's mathematical beauty *should* shift our prior, no?
