A small town is split on whether loyalty to your own people is a virtue or just bias with better PR, and the compromise they land on is a mutual-aid clearinghouse that logs hours residents spend helping each other. The rule: any hour you log helping someone in your inner circle (a relative, or anyone you've exchanged at least ten hours of help with in the past year) creates a stranger-hour debt — within thirty days you have to log an hour helping someone you don't know, through the clearinghouse, or the debt doubles. Hours spent helping a stranger don't create a debt, they clear one. Hours spent helping an acquaintance (someone in between) neither create nor clear debts. The stated goal is to stop care from pooling inside tight networks and to make impartial helping a habit. Walk me through how this actually plays out: what it incentivizes, how people game it (laundering help through acquaintances, refusing help from friends to dodge the debt, fake stranger matches, whatever else you spot), what happens to the texture of friendship and to the quality of help strangers actually get, and whether any of the failure modes can be patched without turning the whole thing into either a pure surveillance scheme or a voluntary charity drive.
