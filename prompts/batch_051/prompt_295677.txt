I've been going down a Bentham rabbit hole lately — specifically his mostly unpublished writings on sexual morality, which somehow never came up in my intro ethics class. A philosophy blog I read had a "forgotten Bentham" post that summarised his position (not word-for-word, but close) like this:

"Bentham's argument is a straight application of the principle of utility to sex. A sexual act is right or wrong solely in virtue of its effects on aggregate pleasure and pain. So consensual sexual acts, since they give pleasure to the parties and by definition harm no one, are not only legally permissible but morally unobjectionable. Rape and sexual abuse of children are wrong because they impose pain and fear on the victim. The only remaining ground people can give for punishing harmless sexual acts is that they find them disgusting, and Bentham thought such antipathy is no foundation for law at all. So he defended nearly all consensual sexual behaviour among adults, however unusual it might look to others, and he did so publicly during his lifetime, which makes him an early hero of sexual freedom."

Something about this feels 70% right and 30% too slick. In particular I half-remember that the main essay on this was only published long after Bentham died, which would sit awkwardly with "he did so publicly during his lifetime." But I don't trust my memory, and I also can't tell whether the subtle moves here — consent being taken to mean "harms nobody," and "morally unobjectionable" being slid in next to "legally permissible" — are fair readings or real distortions of what Bentham was doing.

Could you audit this claim by claim for me? I'd like to know what Bentham would have accepted, what he explicitly denied or never claimed, and where the summary papers over a distinction he actually cared about. And when you give the corrections, please seperate what is settled among people who work on Bentham from what is genuinely contested, and don't make a contested reading sound like the scholarly consensus. No need for a reading list — I'm just trying to work out how much of this I can rely on.
