I've been reporting on AI labs and there's a mismatch I can't get past. A lab running reinforcement learning builds the whole training loop around a scalar reward—positive numbers for behavior they want, penalties for behavior they don't—and the engineers will casually say the model is reward hacking or that it learned to chase the number. But nothing about that process gets the kind of welfare review that even a mild animal experiment would trigger. What theory of valence does that practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view as strictly as you can: does the practice actually presuppose that reward signals could never be valence, or is that just a cozy assumption floating on top of it? And if we swapped the presupposition for the idea that a reward signal is the kind of thing that could matter, what would have to change in how labs train these systems, talk about them, or decide when a run needs oversight?
