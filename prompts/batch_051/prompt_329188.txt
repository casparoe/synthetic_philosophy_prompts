I have arrived at a distinction of my own, and I would like it examined honestly rather than validated. I call the two states "ride-along understanding" and "stand-alone understanding." Ride-along understanding occurs when an AI, a textbook, or a teacher walks me through an argument and I follow each step, can paraphrase the moves, and finish with the same feeling of clarity I get from genuinely working something out, yet if the source were removed or the problem altered slightly, I could not reconstruct or adapt the reasoning. Stand-alone understanding is what survives that test: I can regenerate the argument without the source, extend it to a nearby case, and notice where it stops applying. My theory is that these are two distinct cognitive states rather than two degrees of the same thing, and that the subjective feeling of understanding almost cannot tell them apart because fluency does the work of the feeling in both cases. I also believe that most of what students call "understanding" after using an AI is ride-along understanding, which would explain why people systematically overestimate how much a tool has taught them. For example, after an AI explained why the derivative of e^x is itself, I could repeat the explanation convincingly, but the next day I could not answer a slightly shifted question about a^x without opening the tool again.
