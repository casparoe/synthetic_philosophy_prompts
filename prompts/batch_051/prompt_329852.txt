ok so I've been reading about the idea that if AGI is coming, all the bargains that matter have to get made before it actually arrives. the argument is basically: right now the big labs and governments are still roughly mutually vulnerable, so they have reason to sit down and negotiate, but the seccond someone actually has AGI that balance collapses and whoever got there first can just walk away. i find this really attractive because it doesn't depend on anyone being good, it's a clean point about leverage, and it explains why "we'll figure it out later" is a mistake. But I also can't shake the intuition that a pre-AGI bargain is cosplay. like, you can't genuinely bind someone to a deal when the thing being bargained over will let them rewrite whatever enforces the deal, and the later power will get to say what the vague promises even meant. my gut says first movers loophole, reinterpret, or just ignore whatever was signed, and everyone at the table knows that, so the signing is just signaling. I want both sides at their strongest, then a real verdict: what would it take for the theory to survive and the intuition to get debunked, what would it take for the intuition to force the theory to change or die, and is there a reading where the theory is about shaping norms or institutions rather than enforceable contracts so the two arent actually in conflict?
