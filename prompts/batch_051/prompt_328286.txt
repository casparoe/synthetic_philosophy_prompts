Write a dialogue between two equally sharp philosophers of physics about weather elegance in a scientific theory is evidence that the theory is true. One of them, Anya, defends a qualified version of the view: she doesn't think beauty matters much in messy applied fields, but she argues that in fundamental physics a compact, mathematically deep structure is a real, if defeasible, piece of evidence, because it would be very unlikely that a strikingly elegant set of equations is just a coincidence, and because elegant theories make strong, risky commitments. The other, Ben, thinks elegance is mostly an aesthetic prejudice with a bad track record: he points to beautiful failures like Kepler's nested solids and string theory's decades without a single direct test, and to ugly successes like the standard model with its hand-tuned knobs. Neither should be a strawman. Anya has to have something real to say about the beautiful failures, and Ben has to have a real answer to the strongest pro-elegance cases, like Dirac's equation and general relativity. Make it a genuine back-and-forth where each person is forced to refine the view a bit, not just restate it. Somewhere in the middle, have them zero in on the distinction between elegance raising a theory's prior probability and elegance just making scientists more likely to stick with a theory long enough for the evidence to show up. Dont write it in screenplay format; write it as continuous prose with quoted speech interleaved in ordinary paragraphs, the way one would write an email or a letter. Likewise no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Aim for about 1,200 words.
