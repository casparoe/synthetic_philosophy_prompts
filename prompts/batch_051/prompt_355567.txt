I'm teaching a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of time, and we've been working through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I'm realizing I don't have a credence I can defend out loud. The argument, as I present it anyway: in special relativity there's no privileged frame of simultaneity, so for a distant event that lies in what I call the future but is spacelike separated from me here-and-now, there's some inertial observer for whom that event is simultaneous with their present. But that observer's present is just as real as mine, and reality shouldn't be relative to a mere frame, so the event has to be real already. Run that across enough events and the moving edge of "now" seems to dissolve, and what's left looks an awful lot like the block universe, with everything eternally just there.

What I'd like from you is an honest number: your credence that this argument, granting standard special relativity, actually establishes eternalism rather than just showing something deflationary about our coordinate conventions. But mostly I want the decomposition. At least include these terms: (1) your probability that special relativity is not only empirically adequate but to be taken literally, against neo-Lorentzian or preferred-foliation readings; (2) your probability that the key inference is sound — that if some event is simultaneous with a real observer's here-and-now in some legitimate frame, then that event is real simpliciter, so "reality" can travel across frames the way the argument needs it to; (3) your probability that even a successful version of the argument gets all the way to the block universe, rather than merely undermining a global, non-perspectival present without showing every future event already exists; and anything else you think belongs, like whether "real" is being quietly swapped for "determinate." Give me rough numbers with one line of justification each, show how they combine into the overall credence, and then tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence would move it most. I suspect my students will locate the weak joint somewhere else then I do, and I want to see whether your decomposition finds the same hinge.
