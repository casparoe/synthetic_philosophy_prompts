I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep returning to a parallel I cannot decide is profound or merely punning. The problem of universals asks what, if anything, is the one thing common to the many particulars that fall under a general term — what it is, if anything, that all red things share, or all dogs. The classical options are well known. Realists hold that a universal exists in many particulars at once; nominalists hold that only particulars exist and that general terms are just labels for groups of resembling things; conceptualists hold that the universal exists only as an abstraction in a mind.

Now consider a development in machine learning. A neural network trained to classify images or words usually learns shared features: particular directions in its internal representational space that respond to many distinct inputs possessing a common property. A single such direction participates in the network's processing of each relevant instance, and it seems to be what allows the network to treat all those instances as alike. It is tempting to say that this learned direction is a one-over-many — a single representation present across many particular cases — and therefore a plausible candidate for a universal, at least inside the network's world.

But the analogy may collapse under pressure. A feature direction is a particular structure inside one model, learned from finite data; two networks trained on the same task will not converge on the same direction. Moreover, the direction is activated in varying degrees by different inputs, whereas the traditional realist picture says the universal is wholly present in each of its instances. A feature may also be a compromise carved by the training objective rather than a property of the world, and an individual feature can respond to a mixed bundle of properties that has no single shared nature at all.

I want to know whether this comparison is deep or superficial. What exactly would have to be true of a learned representation for it to deserve the name universal in something like the realist sense? Does the neural network case genuinely recapitulate the schema of the old debate, or does it just borrow its vocabulary? And where precisely do the two structures come apart? Spell out the mapping between the two domains, say where it holds, and identify a consequence the analogy would lead one to expect that is in fact false.
