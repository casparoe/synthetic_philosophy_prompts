I've been doing a lot of forecasting stuff lately—prediction tournaments, Brier scores, trying to actually calibrate my confidence instead of going with my gut—and I hit a wall in the accuracy-first epistemology rabbit hole. This is what I can't figure out. The Brier score is supposed to be "proper," meaning that by my own lights my honest probability always maximizes my expected score, and there's a related theorem that every coherent set of degrees of belief is "immodest": it thinks of itself as the most accurate credence I could hold. Okay, sure. But then shouldn't my current probabilities also think that any change away from them—including the change caused by seeing new evidence or hearing a good argument—would make me less accurate in expectation? And if that's true, isn't changing your mind always irrational by your own lights? There has to be a mistake in the "including" step, right, but I can't put my finger on it.
