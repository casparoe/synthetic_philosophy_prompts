So me and my cousin have been arguing about this for months, and we finally agreed to stop and write one single document that we can both sign. The fight is about whether trying to trace the long-term consequences of what you do makes being good basically impossible. I'm on the side that says it kind of does: every time I try to do something helpful — give to a well-digging charity, canvas for some ballot measure, or just help my neighbor fix her roof — I immediately see the ripples spreading and after two or three steps I'm making things up. The well could help a village grow, and then they use more water, and then they have more kids, and then in forty years it's worse for everyone; the roof repair could keep her in a house she should have left. So I mostly freeze and do very small local stuff, because that's the only place I can sort of see the effects. My cousin thinks this is absurd. He says nobody can trace everything, everyone has always been in the dark, and the sane move is to make a rough guess with what you've got and update later; he also says my freezing is itself a choice with long-term consequences I'm not tracing. I've seen people on forums call this the cluelessness problem, so call it that if it helps. I'm the freeze side and he's the rough-guess side.

What I want you to write is the document we should've produced after actually listening to each other. Start with a numbered list of claims we can both sign, about what's actually settled (long-run effects are real, nobody can predict them exactly, doing nothing is still doing something, etc.). Then a disagreements table with the exact places left over, each stated neutrally and then re-stated in wording each of us would accept for our own side. For each row in the table, add a crux: some smaller question that, if it got answered, would actually move one of us. Then give a concrete test or case we both would agree bears on that crux, and write down what each of us predicts about it in advance. If you think part of our fight is only about words, say which part and why.
