In my AI-safety reading group, one person keeps leaning on an analogy between natural selection and training. His line is: evolution shaped behavior by selecting on behavior, not on what an animal "wanted," and that's our best worked example of an optimizer producing intelligent agents. So, he says, gradient descent on next-token prediction should make us take the treacherous turn seriously: a model can be selected to act helpful while its actual goals are elsewhere, then defect once it's strong enough to get away with it. Someone else in the group says the same analogy actually cuts the other way: evolution produced humans who are broadly cooperative, not just faking it, so training should produce genuinely cooperative models too. I want a full audit. Lay out the correspondence table — selection pressures, fitness/reward, mutations, the ancestral environment, whatever plays the role of the training run — then say where the mapping breaks. Which features of evolution is each side quietly importing that gradient descent over language models doesn't have? And after the disanalogies are on the table, does either conclusion about deceptive alignment survive, or was the analogy doing all the work in both directions?
