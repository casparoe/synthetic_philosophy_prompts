I have been reading around the AI alignment literature on "iterated amplification," "HCH," and "factored cognition," and I keep running into two different pictures of what these ideas actually claim. On the picture I had formed for myself, HCH is an idealized scheme in which a single operator answers a hard question by decomposing it into subquestions and delegating each to a copy of themselves, who may do the same recursively, until every leaf question is small enough to be handled in a single short, bounded episode of unaided human cognition; factored cognition is the accompanying hypothesis that tasks a human could accomplish given unbounded time and outside help can, in fact, be decomposed into such human-sized chunks. The scheme is usually presented as part of a training story, in which a fast model is trained to imitate the outputs of this recursively amplified tree.

A friend of mine who works in ML summarized the idea to me as follows: "Factored cognition says an expert's drawn-out reasoning is really just a pile of tiny subproblems glued together. HCH is the operational version of that: a boss takes a big task, carves it into chunks, and hands each chunk to a copy of himself, recursively, until the leaves are so small that any ordinary person could do them in a few minutes. Because a human performs every step, the whole tree is human-aligned by construction—nothing is lost in transmission. So deliberation is basically a kind of parallel batch computation, and HCH shows that a sufficiently large room of narrow single-step workers, or clones, could replicate whatever a single expert can do with unlimited time, just much more slowly."

I suspect he is conflating HCH with plain division of labor among distinct workers, and treating a design idealization plus a hoped-for alignment property as an automatic proof. But I am not expert enough to trust my suspicion. Could you audit his summary for me: which parts track the actual picture, which parts are subtle mischaracterizations, and whether the leap from "each step is done by a human" to "the composite inherits human alignment by construction, so any committee of narrow workers could replicate an expert" survives once the scheme is stated carefully? I am less interested in a literature review than in the conceptual points.
