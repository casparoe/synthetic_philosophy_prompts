I've been living in Montreal for about a year and my relatives back home keep asking whether I 'think in French' yet. I used to just say 'not really,' but I've realized I can't actually say what the question is asking, and neither can they when I push on it. My French friends treat thinking in the language as the real test of fluency, and the 'language shapes thought' stuff I keep reading seems to lean on the same idea without ever spelling it out. So I want a working defenition of 'thinking in a language' that correctly classifies the following cases. Propose the definition, run it through each case, and tell me which ones it handles worst — and whether you'd rather fix the definition or defend the surprising verdict.

- silently rehearsing a sentence before saying it out loud in French
- doing long division in your head while hearing the number words
- turning a shape over in your mind to check if it will fit through a doorway
- a preverbal infant figuring out that tugging a blanket will pull a toy closer
- me planning tomorrow's errands in French but still doing arithmetic in English
- someone who reports having no inner monologue at all, planning their week
- the tip-of-the-tounge case, where you know exactly what you mean but can't retrieve the word
- reading a French paragraph and following it while deliberately not subvocalizing
- a chess player calculating a forced line as board images plus occasional verbal fragments
- a Deaf friend who says she thinks in signs rather than voiced words
- replaying a melody in your head
