I've noticed a pattern in arguments lately and I can't tell if it has a name. Example one: my cousin got in a fender bender and had dashcam footage, and the other guys insurance just said "that could be AI." Example two: the same cousin later shared a blurry screenshot as proof of what a politician said, and when I said screenshots are easy to fake he shrugged and said "yeah, but at some point you have to trust something." That's the pattern I mean. There used to be a class of things — a video, a photo, a signed document, an official record — that worked as a floor under disputes. They weren't thought to be perfect, but they were treated as good until shown to be bad, and the person doubting them had to do the work. Now that floor only seems to exist when people already like what the evidence shows, and otherwise "it could be fake" never really bottoms out. I'm wondering whether there's a name for the thing that was doing that job before — not evidence in general, but the default-trusted layer a conversation falls back on so it can keep going at all — and for the specific failure mode where people only stop double-checking when the evidence cuts against them. If there's no single name, what would you call it, and how is it different from plain old bias or ordinary skepticism?
