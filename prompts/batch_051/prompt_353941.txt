I want one essay question on the metaphysics of letterforms: what makes every 'a' an 'a'? The question should begin from a concrete puzzle and force students beyond reciting labels. And a glyph closely resembling one of these forms can still fail to be a letter 'a' when the designer intended it as, say, a stylized 'o' or an invented symbol. Students should be made to improve upon at least three positions: shape essentialism, family-resemblance/prototype theories, and a historical-functional view on which letterforms are artifacts whose identity is fixed by lineage and intended role. The question must not be answerable by a summary of Wittgenstein on family resemblance. Please provide the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points, the marks for each (total 20), and a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point.
