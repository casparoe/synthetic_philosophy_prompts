I write about science and ideas for a living, and somehow I'm the best man at my best friend's wedding this weekend, which means I have to give a toast and I've been putting it off because I don't know how to thread this needle. The groom is a physicist — his whole career has been Maxwell's demon and the physics of information: the standard resolution where the demon can't actually beat the second law because the information it gathers has to be stored somewhere, and erasing that memory costs heat, so the entropy books balance in the end. Half the room will be his colleagues, who will absolutely catch it if I fudge any of that; the other half is the bride's family, lovely people who wouldnt know a heat bath from a hot tub. So the toast has to land for both groups at once, and it can't just be a lecture with a champagne glass in my hand. I want it built around one genuinely good Maxwell's demon joke — the physicists laugh because it's accurate, the family laughs because it's funny — and I want the physics to be load-bearing, not decoration: the whole toast should turn on the idea that sorting, memory, and work all have to be paid for, and that watching him with her is the one place the rule seems to clock out. And don't hand me the version where love "violates the laws of thermodynamics" — his postdocs will set their napkins on fire; if you're going to claim an exception, build it out of the demon's own bookkeeping. Five minutes, warm but not schmaltzy. Just write the toast itself — no preamble, no "here's one idea," open with the first line of the toast.
