Say a large worker-owned co-op, after two big projects go wrong and the membership is at each other's throats about who to blame, amends its constitution to add a formal 'bearer of the failure' rite. After any loss the co-op judges significant, every member writes one name on a ballot; the person with the most votes is publicly named by the whole co-op as the cause of the failure, stripped of their committee roles, and excluded from meetings and the shared canteen for six months, after which they are readmitted with a lump sum and a welcoming speech that officially consigns the blame to the past. Membership is voluntary and the person chosen can't refuse without leaving the co-op. The people behind the change defend it by saying it just makes an old sacrificial logic transparent and non-violent: instead of everyone quietly scapegoating someone through gossip and faction fights, the community picks a substitute, loads the guilt onto them, and then brings them back so the conflict ends instead of lingering.

I'm not asking whether this is defensible. I want to know how it would actually run. What sort of person gets selected, and does the selection look like the classic scapegoat critera—marginal members, people with few allies, people whose failure was most visible—or does the voting itself change who becomes a plausible target? What does it incentivise among members, around risk-taking, hiding failures, building alliances, maybe deliberately letting small things fail so a rival gets chosen? How would people game the re-admission, the compensation, even the six-month exclusion? Does the public denunciation do something a private fine or a formal inquiry couldn't? And where does it break down: does it reduce factional conflict, or just concentrate it and timetable it? Could any fixes—rotation, a lottery among the responsible committee, paying the role from the start, an outside arbitrator—patch the failures without turning it back into ordinary accountability?

At the end, say how confident you are in the bits that are actual predictions about how people would behave, rather than just conceptual unpacking, and single out the point you're least sure of.
