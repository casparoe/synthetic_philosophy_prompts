I am a control systems engineer; my working vocabulary is stability, feedback loops, disturbance rejection, setpoint tracking, oscillation and damping, controllability and observability, and robustness under model uncertainty. I can follow political philosophy at a high level, but I do not have its specialist vocabulary. I keep running into a debate between two pictures of what makes a society well ordered. One, which I associate with Confucian and conservative thought, treats order and harmony as primary political values, achieved through authority, ritual, settled role-relations, and the containment of conflict. The other, liberal pluralism, treats persisting disagreement, diversity, and ongoing contestation as signs of a free society rather than as disturbances to be removed. Restate this debate in my control-theoretic vocabulary as faithfully as you can, side by side. For each position, separate the commitments that translate cleanly, those you have to distort to fit, and those with no expression in my framework at all. Then give me a residue report. Does the liberal-pluralist claim that conflict can be intrinsically valuable survive the translation, or does my vocabulary quietly rewrite it as the claim that a bounded amount of oscillation is acceptable because it keeps the system from going brittle? Does the Confucian/conservative claim that harmony is an achieved condition, not merely the absence of disturbance, survive, or does it collapse into tracking a fixed setpoint? End by telling me whether the residue is a real disagreement my framework cannot see or a disagreement my framework dissolves.
