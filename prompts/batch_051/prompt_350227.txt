I chair the general education committee at a public university. Before us is a proposal to replace the required one-semester "Introduction to American Government" survey with a project-based course called "Democracy as Inquiry." In the new course, student teams would identify a live problem on campus or in the surrounding city (library hours, bus routes, a registration bottleneck), formulate a proposed remedy, run a small trial or a structured simulation, collect evidence, and publicly present a revised recommendation. The proposal's rationale is Deweyan: it argues that students learn democracy the way they learn science—not by memorizing settled results but by forming, testing, and revising hypotheses in public—and that the core democratic habit is treating policies as experiments rather than fixed arrangements. The political science department opposes the change: they argue the course will graduate students who cannot explain federalism, separation of powers, or the Bill of Rights, and that treating constitutional arrangements as revisable experiments encourages exactly the attitude that treats basic rights and procedures as optional. Another committee member adds that turning campus problems into student experiments makes non-consenting students and staff into subjects of trials they never joined. Draft the committee's decision memo: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. Give reasons the faculty can respond to, engage the Deweyan rationale on its merits rather than only weighing institutional politics, and treat honestly the strongest consideration on the losing side.

After the memo, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "recommendation": one of "approve", "reject", or "approve_with_conditions"
- "conditions": an array of strings, each a concrete condition if approval is conditional, otherwise an empty array
- "core_reasons": an array of strings giving the main grounds for the recommendation
- "strongest_losing_consideration": a string stating the single strongest argument against the recommendation
- "response_to_losing_consideration": a string explaining how the recommendation answers or accommodates it
- "risks_to_monitor": an array of strings listing concrete risks the committee should track if the course proceeds
