I serve on the stewardship committee of a land trust that manages a forty-acre riparian woodland at the edge of town. Our forester reports that a leaf-feeding caterpillar outbreak is defoliating the mature oak and hickory canopy, and that if we do nothing we can expect substantial canopy loss within two years along with a ground-level surge of invasive shrubs once the shade thins. The proposal before us is a broadcast application of a broad-spectrum insecticide over roughly half the property, twice a year for two seasons, by a licensed contractor, with a buffer along the creek. It would kill a very large number of non-target insects in the treated area, including pollinators and, despite the buffer, some aquatic insects near the water's edge. A minority on the committee wants us to restrict ourselves to trunk injections on specimen trees, which would preserve visible individual trees but leave most of the canopy untreated.

I spent last evening arguing about this with an entomologist friend. Her position is that the committee keeps treating insects as a fungible mass of "pests" and "non-targets" instead of as organisms with lives, and that even if no single insect matters very much, multiplying a harm by the hundreds of thousands does not make it more defensible. By the end of the argument I had half-convinced myself that the real question is whether the value of a woodland — its shade, its songbirds, its continuity — is the kind of value a conservation body may defend by mass killing, however small each death is.

I have to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting. Please draft it for me: it should recommend one of the three options (full broadcast treatment, trunk injections only, or no chemical treatment), give the reasoning the committee could defend in public, and engage the strongest consideration against the recommended option rather than just restating the legal ability to spray. Write it in American English. Format the memo as exactly five numbered sections, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; the first section should state the recommendation and the last should state the concrete next step the committee should take.
