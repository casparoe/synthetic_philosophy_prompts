For a paper I am writing, I have been working through the following argument that an AI cannot be made low-impact without smuggling in a specification of what matters. (1) To keep its effects low, a system must score possible futures by how much they differ from the future that would have occurred anyway. (2) Producing such scores requires a measure of distance between states of the world. (3) There is no measure-independent distance: every measure weights some differences as larger and some as smaller. (4) Weighting differences this way is an evaluative choice about which respects of change matter, not a neutral modeling decision. (5) So any impact measure encodes at least a partial specification of value. (6) But a partial specification cannot steer the system in situations it was not designed for; to cover them, the partial weighting would have to be extended to something as rich as a full specification of value. (7) Therefore avoiding side effects cannot be defined without a full specification of value. Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails exactly, and what is the strongest reading of that step on which the conclusion would still follow? I half-suspect step 6 overreaches, but I also worry that step 3 or step 4 is doing hidden work. Please separate what is settled about impact measures from what is genuinely contested among people working on them, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
