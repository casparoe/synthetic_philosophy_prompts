I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for ages, and I've ended up with the view that more metacognition is basically always better: every layer of reflection can catch errors in the layer below, you should be willing to check the checker, and there's no principled place to stop, since any stopping rule is itself a metacognitive judgment. But I also have a firm intuition that some things get worse when you look at them directly — the old story about the centipede who trips once you ask which leg moves first, the musician who gets worse once she verbalises what her fingers already know, the conversation that dies when you start monitoring your own contributions. I could write these off as cases of doing metacognition badly, rather then metacognition as such, but that feels like immunising the theory. Give me both sides at their strongest, and then a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be debunked and dropped, what would it take for the theory to be amended or restricted, and is there a reading on which they're compatible — maybe one about levels of description, or about what counts as the same task once you've made it an object of attention?
