People seem to judge the same number of working hours very differently depending on whose hours they are. A self-employed software developer who works eighty hours a week is often called driven; a warehouse worker who works the same hours across two jobs is usually called overworked or exploited; and in Japan similar hours are treated as a public health crisis under the name karoshi. If we are expected to admire the first, pity the second, and condemn the system behind the third, then there should be a principled difference between them. I would like to know what that principle is, and whether it would survive two harder cases: a software developer who works eighty hours out of fear of losing investors, and a warehouse worker who genuinely wants the second job, overtime included. State the strongest principle you can find that separates admirable dedication from harmful overwork, and then tell me honestly whether it holds up.
