Williams's Makropulos argument has always seemed pretty strong to me, but I keep wondering whether a specific line of neurotech is aimed at its weakest premise and I can't tell if the argument survives or just gets patched.

The argument, as I understand it: unending life would eventually become intolerably tedious, not merely long. For a person with a more or less stable character, the categorical desires that give them reasons to want to be alive at all — finishing a project, seeing a child grow up, whatever — get used up or satisfied over enough time, and then what's left is repetition of the same experiences, endlessly. And Williams preempts the obvious fix: if your character changes enough to keep generating genuinely fresh categorical desires, it stops being clear that it's the same person surviving; if you could just forget the old stuff so it feels new again, you've likewise broken the continuity that made it your survival rather than a new person's.

The development: we already know memories can be destabilized during reconsolidation, and it's not crazy to imagine, within a century or so, an intervention that periodically re-makes old experiences feel novel and/or gently retunes the desire system so that fresh categorical desires keep coming online — while keeping you recognizably continous with yourself from ten years ago, with no full personality replacement and no blank-slate amnesia. Suppose that actually works.

Does Williams's argument survive? It seems to need the premise that the only ways to get novelty and fresh categorical desires over an endless life are identity-breaking memory wipe or character replacement, so the boredom is unavoidable for any surviving person. The neurotech is aimed exactly at that premise. Is the premise actually forced? What would have to be true about the intervention and about personal identity for Williams's conclusion to go through anyway — and if it can't go through, is the honest verdict that the argument is dead rather than just patched? Try to be specific about which premise breaks, not just hand-wave at it would still get boring eventually.
