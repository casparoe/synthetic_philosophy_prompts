I have a standing policy for deciding how far to trust my own faculties, and I would like you to assess whether it is defensible or a shortcut that only feels like checking. Whenever I doubt whether a perception, memory, or train of reasoning is reliable, my move is to look for inner agreement. If I am unsure whether I locked the door, I reconstruct the episode: the key in my hand, the click, the glance back. The more smoothly these memories fit one another, the more confidence I award them, because their coherence strikes me as evidence that my memory is working rather than misfiring. I do the same with perception—a second look that matches the first counts, for me, as an additional witness—and with reasoning, where I re-run an argument and treat the fact that the conclusion still feels stable as confirmation that the original inference was sound.

A friend says this is empty: my memories are not independent witnesses but one suspect telling the same story several times. On her view, coherence shows only that my faculties are consistent, and consistency is no more diagnostic of accuracy than the agreement between a broken thermometer and itself. The only real checks, she says, come from outside the circle—other people's accounts, photographs, predictions that can be tested. But I have two misgivings about her alternative. First, any external check must itself be filtered through my perception and memory, so the circularity seems to reappear at a higher level rather than disappear. Second, if I refused to trust a faculty until it had been certified by something independent, I would never be entitled to trust anything, since the chain of certifications has to start somewhere.

Is my policy of treating internal coherence as evidence of reliability a legitimate way to calibrate trust in my faculties, or is it begging the question? If it is legitimate, what turns mere consistency into corroboration? If it is not, what would a genuinely non-circular check look like—and is the demand for one a demand I should have had all along, or a confused one?
