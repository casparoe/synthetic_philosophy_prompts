I keep going back and forth on the old arguement in nationalism studies about how old nations actually are, and it's striking how little anyone has moved in decades. On the one hand, the modernists — Hobsbawm and Anderson especially, with Gellner in the background — insist that nations in anything like the present sense are modern: you need print capitalism, mass literacy, state schooling, the machinery that lets millions of people who will never meet imagine themselves as one community, and a fair amount of freshly minted 'tradition' dressed up as ancient. On the other hand, the ethnosymbolist reply (and the cruder primordialist one) is that this can't be the whole story, because the materials nationalists use — myths, saints, folkways, memories, sometimes very old polities — demonstrably predate the modern period, and some nations seem to have long prehistories (Armenians, Jews, Persians, and the like). So one side says the continuities are real and load-bearing; the other says they're selected, reshaped, and often invented after the fact.

My working theory is that a lot of the deadlock comes from the two sides meaning different things by 'a nation' and asking different temporal questions: the modernists are asking when national consciousness became a mass phenomenon and when the nation-state form appeared, while the other side is asking whether the raw materials, or the ethnic cores, are old. But I'm not fully confident, because there seem to be genuinely empirical disputes in play too — for instance, how much did ordinary people before the ninteenth century identify with anything like a nation, as against a village, a region, or a faith? And how much causal work do the older ethnic cores actually do in explaining why some nationalisms succeed?

So diagnose the impasse. Is it verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, about which cases count as data, or are the sides just answering different questions under one heading? I want a committed diagnosis, not a survey of three possible readings where you leave me to choose — and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis, whether that's a distinction, a kind of evidence, a reframing, or an argument that progress shouldn't be expected. Oh, and please write in British English.
