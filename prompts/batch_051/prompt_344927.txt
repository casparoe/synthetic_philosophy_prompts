I’ve been going through a popular physics book, and a passage in it has been stuck with me. It's gloss of the thermodynamic arrow goes roughly like this:

The direction of time is just the direction in which entropy increases. Since the underlying laws of physics are time-symmetric, there would be no distinction between past and future at all if the universe had not started out in an extraordinarily low-entropy state. Memory, causation, free energy, the fact that you can scramble an egg but not unscramble it—these are all just consequences of the entropy gradient. And once the universe reaches maximum entropy, time itself will stop, so there will be no events at all.

I know this is loosely Eddington’s line, and Boltzmann’s statistical mechanics is usually brought in to back it up. The last bit feels suspicious to me, but the author is a professional physicist writing for a lay audience, so maybe I’m just missing a distinction. Could you do an accuracy audit?
