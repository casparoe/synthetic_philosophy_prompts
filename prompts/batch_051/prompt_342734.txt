I am a systems programmer with a good working knowledge of serialization formats, protocol layers, and content negotiation: envelopes, headers, payloads, schemas, content types, magic numbers, and versioned formats. Restate Hofstadter's three-way distinction among outer messages, frame messages, and inner messages—where an outer message signals that there is a message to be decoded at all, a frame message supplies the rules under which the inner message is to be decoded, and the inner message is what decoding yields—together with the claim that meaning does not reside intrinsically in the inner message but arises through its interaction with a decoding context, in the vocabulary of serialization and protocol design. Make the translation systematic rather than impressionistic: take each of the three roles in turn, and each moving part of the claim about the location of meaning, and say what in serialization terms corresponds cleanly, what corresponds only after some distortion, and what has no correspondent at all. Then report the residue as a separate finding. Which aspects of Hofstadter's distinction fail to survive the translation, and are those failures better explained as limitations of the serialization vocabulary or as signs that the original distinction was doing more work through suggestive phrasing than through definition?
