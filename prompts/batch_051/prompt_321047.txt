I have been developing what I call the sortal-role theory of units, and I want an honest assessment of it. Euclid opens Book VII of the Elements by defining a unit as whatever makes an existing thing be called one, and he treats a number as a multitude made up of units, with the unit itself not a number. On my proposal, being a unit is neither an intrinsic property of an object nor a specially thin kind of object; it is a role that a sortal (a kind concept, such as deck or card) together with a counting practice confers on things for the purposes of a count. A pile of fifty-two cards is one unit under the sortal deck and fifty-two units under the sortal card, and neither description is metaphysically more basic. I claim this dissolves the objection that pure units either retain distinguishing features or lose them and so cannot compose a number of distinct units; that objection has force only if a unit is taken to be an object or a shared property rather than a role. It also explains why 'one' behaves unlike the other number words and why Euclid was right to deny numberhood to the unit. I suspect this may be Frege's point that numbers attach to concepts, merely restated as a theory of units. Give me the three strongest objections to the view, and say whether any of them is fatal rather than merely deflating. Also say what would change your assessment: which premise, if false, would most shift your verdict, and where your reasoning is least certain.
