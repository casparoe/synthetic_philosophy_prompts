I have been working through Moore's paradox for a school philosophy essay and have written down the position I currently hold, in the hope that someone can tell me whether it is a stable combination. Here are the claims I find myself committed to:

(1) The sentence 'It is raining, but I do not believe that it is raining' can be true of a speaker: the weather and the speaker's state of mind can come apart in exactly that way.

(2) That same sentence is nonetheless unassertable: no sincere, linguistically competent speaker can utter it without something going badly wrong.

(3) When someone asserts a proposition, part of what they do is present themselves as believing it; assertion typically conveys belief.

(4) A first-person, present-tense ascription such as 'I believe that it is raining' is a report of an inner psychological fact, comparable in kind to reporting a pain, and is true or false according to how that inner fact stands.

(5) The defect in Moore's sentence is not merely a matter of etiquette, informativeness, or conversational oddness; the two conjuncts clash in a way that is deeper than ordinary bad conversation, whilst not being exactly a formal contradiction.

(6) The omissive form ('p, but I do not believe that p') and the commissive form ('p, but I believe that not-p') should receive the same underlying explanation; it would be suspicious if they needed separate treatments.

I am not sure where the strain is, if anywhere. Could you go through these commitments and tell me which are in tension and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to be revised, and what the cheapest fix would be. If one commitment does most of the damage, tell me which, and whether giving it up would also force changes elsewhere.
