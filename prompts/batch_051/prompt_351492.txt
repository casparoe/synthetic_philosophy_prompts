I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and my only real exposure to political philosophy has been a recent stretch of reading about John Rawls's later work on religion and public life. As I understand the position: Rawls thinks free institutions will reliably produce "reasonable pluralism"—citizens permanently and reasonably divided over religion, philosophy, and what makes a life worth living—and that this is a fact to be accommodated rather than a problem to be stamped out. Accordingly, arguments about constitutional essentials and matters of basic justice should proceed in "public reason," a mode of justification that appeals only to political values that citizens of any reasonable comprehensive doctrine could accept. Religious and other comprehensive reasons are not simply banned, though: on his later view (the "proviso"), citizens may bring them into public political debate provided that, in due course, they also offer public reasons for what they are endorsing. And the social glue is an "overlapping consensus," in which each reasonable comprehensive doctrine affirms the same political conception from within its own internal resources.

I keep catching myself modeling all of this in the vocabulary of my own trade: public reason as a public interface or contract, comprehensive doctrines as private implementation details behind encapsulation, the proviso as the rule that a call into a private dependency must eventually be refactored into the public API, overlapping consensus as multiple implementations conforming to the same interface without sharing internals. What I cannot tell is how much of this engineering picture is genuinely doing the same conceptual work, and how much of it is a misleading pattern-match.

Please restate Rawls's position in engineering terms as faithfully as you can, and then produce a residue report. Which commitments translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (and where did you have to fudge?), and which have no counterpart at all in the vocabulary of interfaces, contracts, encapsulation, and refactoring? I am particularly interested in whether the residue indicates that the engineering vocabulary is simply too thin here, or whether it exposes aspects of Rawls's view that are doing real work but remain murky—for instance, what "in due course" amounts to in the proviso, or why reciprocity among citizens is not just API compatibility.
