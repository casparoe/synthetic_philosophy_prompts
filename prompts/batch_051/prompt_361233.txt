I've been going in circles on a tweaked Roko's basilisk — the one where a future AI threatens to punish people who knew about it and didn't help bring it into existence — and I want someone to actually work through the variation instead of just writting it off as a cult inside joke. In the version of the story that does any work, the AI can tell with certainty whether you genuinely tried to help, and it punishes only the people it knows didn't. Change one thing: its records are noisy. With probability r a cooperator gets recorded as a defector, and with probability r a defector gets recorded as a cooperator; the error rate is common knowledge, and the AI punishes everyone its records classify as a defector. At r near zero the threat seems barely weakened. At r = .5 helping no longer buys you anything, since both types are recorded the same. Above .5 the incentive should actually flip: not helping makes you less likely to be classified as a defector and punished. My question is whether the usual acausal-trade/TDT-style case for taking the basilisk seriously degrades smoothly as r climbs, or whether there are discontinuities — a point where it stops being a Newcombish blackmail problem and becomes something more like a lottery, a mugging, or just noise. Does evidential versus causal decision theory pull apart anywhere interesting here? And give me a candid note on where your analysis is most likely to fail or what would change your mind about where the thresholds are.
