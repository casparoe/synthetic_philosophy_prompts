I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether God can suffer. Not as a technical trinitarian question—just the plain one: if God loves us, can our pain actually hurt him? He keeps saying no, that a perfect being can't be pushed around by emotions, and I keep saying that a God who can't be hurt by us can't really love us. So I made a list of the arguments people on my side give for saying God can suffer, and I want to be sure I'm not missing a distinct one before I go back into the argument.

So far I have four. (1) Suffering is built into love: if you love someone, you're exposed to being hurt by them, and if God is love then God has to be exposed to us that way. (2) The Bible plainly shows God grieving, changing his mind, and ultimately dying on a cross in Jesus, so the idea that God is above suffering looks like philosophy overriding scripture. (3) A God who cannot suffer can only understand our suffering from the outside, never stand inside it with us, which hollows out divine compassion. (4) Suffering isn't always an imperfection—some suffering is just the cost of caring well, like a parent suffering alongside a child, so it doesn't automatically rule out a perfect God. Give me one more argument that God can suffer that is genuinely different from these four. Not a rewording of one of them, not a special case of one of them, not a mashup of two. Don't give me a menu of options either; commit to the one you think is strongest and lay it out properly. Then tell me honestly whether that fifth argument is better or worse then the ones I've already got, and whether you'd actually lean on it.
