I'm a writer and I keep tripping over two linked puzzles in the Sherlock Holmes stories that I dont have a word for. "Holmes was a detective" seems true, "Holmes was a plumber" seems false, and yet "Holmes existed" seems false too—so the name works perfectly well as the subject of true-sounding sentences even though there's no real person it picks out. Second puzzle: the stories never say Holmes had two lungs or that the Thames runs through London, but everyone treats those as true in the fiction; by contrast, it feels genuinely unclear whether it's true in the fiction that the water at Reichenbach Falls was H2O, a fact the author and his readers couldn't have known. I've come across the phrase "truth in fiction," but I'm after something more specific: is there an established name for the question of which unstated real-world facts we're allowed to import into a story, and how do philosophers usually separate that question from the fact that the name "Holmes" doesn't refer to anyone?
