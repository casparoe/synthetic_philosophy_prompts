I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm drafting a piece on how a pause on frontier AI would actually play out. I'd like you to act as a technology-policy analyst specialising in international institutions and the economics of the AI industry. Think through mechanisms and incentives step by step, and make clear where your confidence falls; do not just list unconnected possibilities.

Consider this counterfactual. On 1 March 2026, a compact among the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Japan and South Korea enters into force. For eighteen months, the signatories commit to prohibiting any model training run above 10^26 floating-point operations, with exceptions only for individually licensed safety evaluations. After the moratorium, above-threshold runs may proceed only under individual licences. The intended enforcement mechanism is hardware-level: the main accelerator manufacturers, as a condition of their export licences, must ship chips whose firmware measures and logs usage, reports clusters above a set size, and refuses to execute unlicensed above-threshold training runs. Predict how this arrangement plays out over the following five years.

Please organise your answer under these headings, in this order:

1. Working assumptions (around 120 words). State the facts about the AI industry and about state behaviour you are taking for granted.

2. First-round responses (around 200 words). What frontier labs, chipmakers, cloud providers and signatory states do in the first year, and through which mechanisms. Label each response robust, plausible, or speculative.

3. Medium-term adaptation (around 150 words). How the industry and the compact reshape during years two to five.

4. Robust versus fragile (around 150 words). Which predictions you would defend strongly, which are guesses, and what would have to be true for the fragile predictions to be wrong.

5. Bottom line (around 80 words). Whether the pause plausibly buys faster safety progress, better international coordination, or neither.
