Here's one I keep going in circles on wether Benatar's asymmetry is actually doing the work people think it is. As I understand it, the asymmetry says something like: the presence of pain in an existing life is bad, and the presence of pleasure is good; but in a life that is never lived, the absence of pain is good (even though there's no one there to enjoy it), while the absence of pleasure is not bad (because there's no one there to be deprived of it). From that he concludes that coming into existence is always a harm, since never existing has a real advantage and no real disadvantage, so procreation harms the child.

What I'd like is for you to argue a specific position, not to replay the standard objections. Please argue that this asymmetry is better explained by a difference in what we owe to people than by a difference in impersonal value. In other words: the reason absent pain looks good and absent pleasure doesn't look bad when no one exists isn't that the world has extra impersonal goodness in the first case; its that uncreated people have no claims on us at all, and our intuitions are tracking that fact about claims rather than an asymmetry in badness and goodness themselves. Then say what that does to the antinatalist conclusion — I take it the argument loses its force, but make the case properly, and flag the strongest objection to your line.

I want a committed defence, not a survey of possible readings. Pick the deontic reading and push it as far as it can go; note any weak spot quickly and move on. Keep it under 500 words, and use British English spelling please.
