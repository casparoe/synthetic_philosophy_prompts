The only difference between logical, physical, and metaphysical possibility is which probability function you're using, since each kind of possibility just corresponds to a set of background assumptions. This is why philosophers worry about probability-zero events: take a fair coin flipped infinitely many times, and any particular infinite sequence of heads and tails has probability zero, making that sequence impossible—yet some sequence has to occur. So probability-zero events are a kind of case where the impossible happens.' Something about this strikes me as off, though I can't say exactly where. I suspect the final step is too quick, but maybe the earlier claims are fine.
