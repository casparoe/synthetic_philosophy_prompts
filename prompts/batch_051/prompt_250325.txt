I am a graduate student in philosophy of mind drafting the first chapter of a dissertation on physicalism, and I have been trying to write down my position precisely enough that it is neither refuted by the next revision of physics nor true by definition. A friend in the department keeps pressing me on what I actually mean by "physical," and I would like to settle the matter before I circulate the chapter to my committee. Below is the set of commitments I currently want to defend. I would like a consistency audit: which of these actually pull against one another, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and what is the cheapest way to make the whole set coherent?

(1) Physicalism is true: every fact is either a physical fact or is fully determined by the physical facts.

(2) The physical should be characterized by deferring to what the science of physics says there is, not by an a priori test such as being non-mental, non-normative, or spatiotemporal.

(3) Current physics is incomplete and in some respects probably false; I expect its ontology to be revised as the science matures.

(4) Physicalism is a substantive claim about the world, not a tautology: there must be something that would count as discovering that it is false.

(5) We can fix the reference of "physical" without betting on today's ontology: we point to the current paradigm objects, properties, and methods of physics, and future physics counts as the same enterprise if it is the recognizable descendant of that paradigm.

(6) But this open-endedness has limits. If the ideal final physics posited irreducible phenomenal properties — properties with a subjective character not reducible to its structural or dynamical posits — then physicalism would have turned out false, not true with an unexpectedly broadened notion of the physical.

Please go through these one by one. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, explain for each what would have to give and what the cheapest coherent revision would cost, and identify any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection. I would rather find the problems now than have a committee member find them for me.
