im a novelist working on a book about a small religious commune that treats betting as a spiritual discipline. their rule: whenever a member is offered a favorable bet, the community must stake the Kelly fraction of its pooled wealth (the fraction that maximizes expected log wealth — 2p - 1 for an even-money bet you win with probability p), with winnings going to the common fund and losses socialized. members who decline a positive-edge bet are shunned; theres no opting out. treat this as a mechanism, not a moral claim: what does it do to which bets get taken, how people in the surrounding town offer bets, and who joins or stays? where does it get gamed first, and what breaks when several bets arrive at once or when the true probability is disputed? i especially want to know whether the pooling changes anything compared to each person Kelly-betting their own wealth, and whether the commune survives a long bad run.
