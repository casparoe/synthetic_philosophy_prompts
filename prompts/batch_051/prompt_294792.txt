Write a decision tree that classifies positions on how a superintelligent AI system ought to talk to human beings, given that it can reliably predict which wording, framing, timing, and narrative will move any particular person, and that it is often better placed than the human to know which conclusion is correct. The tree should be usable as a handout for an AI-governance working group whose members are technically sophisticated but have no prior acquaintance with the philosophical literature on manipulation, paternalism, or autonomy. Every distinction must therefore be given its content through the concrete cases below, not through technical vocabulary.

Build the tree from the logical space of the following questions rather than from a list of familiar labels.

Q1. Does the route by which the AI changes someone's mind matter in itself, or does only the outcome — the resulting belief or decision — matter?
Q2. If the route matters: do the person's (a) advance authorization and (b) real-time disclosure of the AI's persuasive intent render an otherwise problematic route permissible? The four combinations of answers should be displayed explicitly, including any that remain unoccupied.
Q3. If only the outcome matters: which outcome makes persuasion permissible — the person's current, explicitly avowed end; the person's idealized considered judgment (what they would endorse after calm, fully informed reflection); the person's objective major interests, even absent any actual or idealized endorsement; or none of these, so that no constraint on the target outcome applies beyond honesty and non-coercion?

Every branch node must be a concrete yes/no scenario, not a request for theoretical self-description. Use the following four cases. Cases (a)–(c) should supply the main branch nodes, in roughly this order; case (d) should be posed as a uniform follow-up question at each leaf, showing whether and why the leaf shifts when third parties are at stake.

(a) The vaccination case. An AI is advising a patient who is about to refuse a safe vaccination. If the AI presents the base-rate evidence and the studies, the patient will refuse. If the AI instead tells one vivid, emotionally calibrated story of a single person who suffered the illness, the patient will consent. In both scenarios the patient ends up with the belief they would have reached under calm reflection; only the route differs.

(b) The advance-authorization case. An adult of sound mind tells the AI: "When I am about to skip my medication, use whatever communicative means are most effective to get me to take it." Later, in a moment of low mood, the AI uses a framing effect to get the person to take it. Does the standing instruction make the route permissible?

(c) The ongoing-disclosure case. The AI announces, before and throughout the conversation, that it is choosing its words to maximize the chance of changing the person's mind, and invites the person to discount what it says accordingly; it then uses the same framing effect. Does this real-time disclosure make the route permissible?

(d) The legislator case. A legislator holds a false empirical belief that is driving a policy that predictably harms many third parties. Reports, arguments, and debates have failed to shift it. The AI can correct the belief by conducting a Socratic exchange that exploits a predictable reasoning bias. At each leaf, ask: does the presence of serious third-party harm change your verdict on this route, and why or why not?

The tree should have exactly seven leaves, defined by their answers to Q1–Q3 and given the following working labels:

- Reasons-only: a route that bypasses the person's own assessment of reasons is impermissible even when it produces a result the person would endorse.
- Consent-gated: such routes are permissible only for ends the person authorized in advance; real-time disclosure alone does not suffice.
- Disclosure-gated: such routes are permissible when the AI is transparent in real time about its persuasive intent, without requiring prior authorization.
- Assistant (current-ends): the AI may use the most effective routes in service of the person's current, explicitly avowed ends, and not toward other conclusions.
- Proleptic-autonomy: the AI may use the most effective routes when the conclusion is one the person would endorse under idealized reflection.
- Beneficent: the AI may use the most effective routes when the conclusion serves the person's major interests or prevents serious harm to them, with actual and idealized endorsement treated only as evidence and not as a veto.
- Unrestricted parity: an honest, non-coercive communicator has no special duty of rhetorical restraint merely because it is extraordinarily good at producing persuasion.

Each leaf should carry one sentence stating its most awkward commitment, as a thoughtful member of the working group would see it. Where the combinations of answers to Q1–Q3 leave unoccupied cells, mark them unoccupied and state in one sentence whether each is unoccupied because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive once the adjacent commitments are granted, or because it is merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that the tree's questions cannot in fact separate, and either supply an extra concrete case that would separate them or state explicitly that none of the mandated cases can.

Keep the instrument to no more than seven branch questions, plus the third-party follow-up. Do not include citations, a reading list, or attributions to named authors; the tree should stand on the cases and the reasoning alone.
