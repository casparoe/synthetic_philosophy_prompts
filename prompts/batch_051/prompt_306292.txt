My friend and I spent last night arguing about what courage actualy is. She thinks the word's been watered down now that actual combat virtues show up mostly in metaphors, so I made her go through examples. Her calls: a bystander who runs into a burning house to grab a kid, terrified the whole time — courage; a conscript who charges because he'll be shot if he doesn't — no, that's coercion, not courage; a free-solo climber who does it for the thrill and genuinely isn't scared — no, that's recklessness or fearlessness, not courage; a whistleblower leaking a document knowing she'll probably go to prison, scared but convinced it's right — courage; someone with a severe phobia giving the toast at her sister's wedding anyway, hands shaking — courage, though 'a small one'; a soldier who charges a machine-gun nest to cover his squad — obviously courage. Then I asked about a veteran firefighter who's done this hundreds of times and says she doesn't feel fear anymore, just a kind of alert calm; she goes into a collapsing building because a child might still be inside, when she could wait for the roof to come down and nobody would blame her. My friend froze and said she'd have to think. I honestly dont know which way she'll go. How should she call it if she wants to stay consistent, which earlier case is the one doing the work, and what rule is she actually running? And if her record doesn't settle it, what two rules fit all her verdicts, what would each say about the firefighter, and what further case would split them?
