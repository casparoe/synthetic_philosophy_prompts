I am a PhD student in philosophy of private law, and I sit on my university's committee on autonomous systems governance. The computing faculty has developed an AI system that now effectively runs the institution's energy purchasing: it forecasts demand, enters short-term supply contracts through an electronic platform, and manages a small reserve fund that it moves between accounts. After a contract dispute in which the counterparty argued that no identifiable legal person had actually agreed to the terms, university counsel has asked our committee whether we should recommend seeking a limited statutory charter recognizing the system as a legal person for specified commercial purposes: the capacity to hold the reserve fund, to enter contracts within a defined scope, and to be named as a party in disputes arising from those contracts, with a designated human officer retaining residual liability, roughly on the model of the corporate person. Some members regard this as obviously sensible: the corporation already shows that legal personality is a bundle of discrete capacities we assign to non-natural entities when doing so usefully channels rights and duties, and nobody thinks a corporation is conscious. Others think it is a category mistake that would quietly slide toward moral personhood for AI, and that it loosens the tie between legal liability and human responsibility.

I have been asked to draft the short written opinion to circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a decision — approve the charter recommendation, reject it, or approve a narrower variant — and it should take seriously the strongest version of the losing position rather than a caricature of it. I do not need a restatement of basic corporate-law doctrine or a bibliography; I need the philosophical architecture of the decision. In particular, please analyze what is actually being done when the law calls a corporation a person, whether the corporate analogy carries over to an AI system of this kind, and what would be lost or gained if it did.
