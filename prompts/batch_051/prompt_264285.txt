After a long career in the natural sciences, much of it spent in an applied research institute where the justification for any project was tied to foreseeable use, I have recently been reading philosophical work on the value of scientific knowledge for its own sake. The position appeals to me, but I find it hard to state precisely, and I want to test it against an actual decision rather than a textbook example.

Consider the cancellation of the Superconducting Super Collider by the United States Congress in 1993. The project was already under construction in Texas and was meant to be the largest particle accelerator ever built, but its cost estimate had grown far beyond the original figure, and the near-term practical payoff was difficult to name. Setting aside the familiar budget-politics story, analyze this episode as a case for the thesis that understanding the fundamental structure of nature can be a legitimate public good even when no application is foreseeable. Be disciplined about the mapping. Who are the actors, what were their options, and what did each side treat as relevant evidence? What would an instrumentalist justification of the expenditure require them to show, and what would an intrinsic-value justification require them to show instead? In particular, say whether an intrinsic-value position can place any principled limit on cost, since knowledge is not a fixed quantity with a marginal price, and whether the actual debate gave that position a fair hearing or forced it to speak the language of applications, spin-offs, and economic return.

Then reverse the direction and say what the episode does to the theory. Does the cancellation show that appeals to knowledge for its own sake are politically inert, or only that this was the wrong project and the wrong moment for them? Give an honest account of what the framework cannot see: how much of the opposition was to centralized big science as such, to particle physics in particular, or to the project's management, as opposed to a considered verdict on knowledge without application. Finally, use the analysis to say something specific about the harder case in the same family—the public funding of cosmology, where even the unplanned practical spin-offs are thinner than in accelerator physics.
