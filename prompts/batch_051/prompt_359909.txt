Everyone in the rationality crowd I hang around with talks about training as if it were a martial art. The pitch is that you don't get good at spotting your own motivated reasoning by reading about it — you drill katas, over and over, in a low-stakes dojo setting: pre-mortems, calibration questions, Fermi estimates, steelmanning your opponent. Do it enough and the move is supposed to fire automatically in real fights — the board meeting, the argument with my mom, the twitter pile-on. I'm half sold and half suspicious, and I want you to actually audit the analogy instead of gesturing at it. What maps onto what — what plays the dojo, the sensei, the kata, the sparring, the belt test, the street fight? Where does the mapping break? I've got one specific worry: in motor learning I half-remember that blocked practice, drilling the same move over and over, makes you look great in the gym, while random practice, mixing up different moves, transfers better to novel situations. If that's right, doesn't it cut against the kata-heavy picture? And the question I actually care about: does the conclusion those people are drawing — that drilling self-scored rationality katas transfers to real, messy epistemic performance — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the martial-arts analogy carrying the whole thing? If it breaks, what's a better analogy?
