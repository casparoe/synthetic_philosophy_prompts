I'm the chief compliance officer for a nonprofit that runs maternal-health clinics and smallholder agricultural programs in rural Central America, East Africa, and South Asia. Field managers make weekly decisions about gifts, hospitality, and personal requests from local officials, contractors, and community leaders. Our current policy is a flat ban on any benefit tied to a personal relationship; country directors argue that this imposes a Western standard on local gift economies, while the board suspects that "cultural flexibility" is a euphemism for corruption. I want a reusable decision procedure that a field manager with no legal or philosophical training can run in under half an hour per case, grounded in a defensible account of what distinguishes corruption from legitimate gift-giving and clientelistic exchange, and of when a local norm should change the judgment rather than excuse it. The procedure should state the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the process stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to the compliance team or the board because the residual judgment cannot be eliminated. Then apply it to these three cases: (1) a contractor bidding on a clinic renovation gives a country director a rug worth about $150 and an invitation to his daughter's wedding; (2) a village chief offers to mobilize labor for a well project if the program hires two of his sons as drivers, only one of whom is qualified; (3) a procurement officer receives cash equal to a week's salary at New Year from a supplier whose contract is up for renewal, in a country where returning a gift is a serious insult. At least one application should produce an answer we will not like.
