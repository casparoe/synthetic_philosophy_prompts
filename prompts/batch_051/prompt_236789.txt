I have been reading discussions of AI risk and keep noticing a pattern for which I can't find a name, and I want to know whether it is one phenomenon or several. The pattern shows up when people propose safety measures that take seriously the possibility that a model is misaligned. They begin by insisting that we should not rely on the model's being good; instead, we should build controls that would contain it even if it has hostile goals. But when they spell out the controls, the proposal quietly depends on the model's being cooperative in every respect other than the one the control is built to handle. A monitoring scheme meant to catch a model that is deceptive in conversation assumes the model does not know it is being monitored. A containment scheme assumes the model cannot coordinate with other copies of itself or with outside actors. A method that reads internal signals to raise an alarm assumes the model reports on itself honestly even while it is saying something else to the user. In each case the assumption is not that the model is aligned; it is that the model is adversarial exactly where the control looks and docile everywhere else the control depends on.

Is there an established term or distinction for this? A bare label is not what I'm after. I want to know whether this is a single mistake with a common structure or a family of related mistakes, what the nearest existing concepts are and how each misses it, and whether every control measure must lean on some assumption of cooperation somewhere, or whether that dependence is avoidable in principle.
