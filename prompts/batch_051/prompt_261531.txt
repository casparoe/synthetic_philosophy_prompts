I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a moral philosopher who defends a friendship model of filial obligations in the tradition of Jane English. On the version of the view she defends, adult children owe their parents no debt of repayment for past care, since parental care is not a loan and gratitude is the wrong register for it; whatever adult children owe is owed in virtue of the present relationship, and is closer to what friends owe one another than to a contractual or quasi-contractual duty. I'd like eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the position. In particular, press on the following, and for each, include the follow-up you would put to the reply she is most likely to give: whether the model can account for duties toward elderly parents with advanced dementia, who can no longer participate in the reciprocal give-and-take characteristic of friendship; whether filial ties can be unilaterally dissolved the way friendships can, and what the answer implies about any special moral standing of the parent; whether the model's voluntarism underdescribes cases in which caring for an aging parent is not chosen by anyone but simply falls to a particular adult child; and whether the view is ultimately better described as a theory of filial obligation or as a theory that there are no distinctively filial obligations, only friendship-like ones.
