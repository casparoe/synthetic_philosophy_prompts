I spent thirty years in genetics before I retired, and I've recently gotten into philosophy. Here's a question that's been nagging at me, and my wife says its nonsense. We went through IVF years ago and had four embryos. We transferred one, and our daughter is now sixteen; the other three were never used. I know perfectly well that if we had transferred embryo number two instead, the child would've had different DNA, probably looked different, maybe been a boy, and so on. But would that child have been our daughter? I don't mean would she have the same name or personality. I mean: is it the same person — the same 'who' — just with a different body and genome, or is it a different child entirely? As a geneticist, my first instinct is that a different genome means a different individual, end of story. But then I think: if my own parents had conceived a month later, I'd have come from a different sperm and egg too, and I can't shake the feeling that it still would have been me, just... different. My wife says I'm smuggling in a soul that gets plugged into wichever body comes along. Who's right here, and does the answer come down to what we mean by 'same person'? Please answer in American English.
