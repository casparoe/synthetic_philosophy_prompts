Construct a thought experiment where Rawls's difference principle gives opposite verdicts on the same policy depending on whether the least advantaged is measured by income alone or by a broader index of primary goods (rights, opportunities, income, and the like) that includes a non-income good, like freedom from a workplace hazard or the social bases of self-respect—roughly, whether the job lets you hold your head up. Constraints: exactly two groups, one policy, no changes in anyone's preferences or information when you switch indicies, and the income-only index should look like the sort of shortcut a real legislator would actually reach for. After the scenario, walk through what each index says and why, and identify the feature of the index—not of the policy—that does the work. Then say how a defender of the income-only index would attack the richer one, and whether they can save it by insisting that wages already price in the non-income good.
