ok here's one I keep going in circles on. my state has a sovereign-immunity clause in its constitution, pretty much this: 'The State shall not be sued in any court of this State, nor shall any suit be maintained against any agency of the State, except as the Legislature may by law allow.' It was adopted in the 1890s, mostly to stop people suing the state every time a road flooded or a bridge collapsed.

the case that trips me up is newer. a couple years ago the child-welfare department began using a machine-learning risk score to decide which abuse reports get investigated and which children get removed. the tool is built and hosted by a private company, but the state pays for it and caseworkers are told to follow its score unless they can document a good reason to depart from it. a family's kid was removed after the tool flagged them as high-risk. later it came out that the model was systematically inflating scores for families in certain low-income zip codes, and the child spent eight months in foster care before being sent home. the department says no one person made the decision—the software made it. the vendor says it only supplied a score, not a decision. and the state says sovereign immunity bars the family's suit.

so far i can see three readings of the clause. plain text: this is a suit against a state agency, and the legislature still hasn't authorized it, so it's barred. purpose: the point was to keep courts from bleeding the treasury over routine maintenance failures, not to shield the state from a system it chose to deploy and ordered its workers to obey. drafters' intent: they couldn't have pictured automated decisions at all; the closest thing they'd recognize is a private contractor using its own judgment, which would be sued directly, not the state.

which reading should win, and why? and does the immunity clause even reach a decision no human official actually made? I care less about the correct legal outcome than about whether 'the state' still refers to one thing here, and whether a state can do a wrong that no identifiable person did. also, write your answer in American English.
