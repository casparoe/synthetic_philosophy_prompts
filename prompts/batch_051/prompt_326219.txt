I've been in a metaethics reading group for the past year, and every session on the Frege–Geach problem ends the same way: the expressivists treat it as a compositional challenge they are steadily solving, while the critics reply that each new attitudinal semantics either reintroduces representational content by the back door or fails to account for the inference patterns it was introduced to explain. Nobody contests the linguistic data, and nobody moves. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are not having one disagreement at all, but are pursuing rival explanatory projects—one about compositional semantics, the other about the psychology of moral judgment—that only look like a single dispute because both sides use the word 'meaning'. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: verbal, a difference in explanatory target, a difference in what counts as an acceptable semantics, or something else. Please engage the concrete moves each side makes, and say what would count as progress under your diagnosis, or whether we should stop expecting the debate to converge.
