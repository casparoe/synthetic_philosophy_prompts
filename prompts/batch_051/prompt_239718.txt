Please think carefully, and maybe start by brainstorming the pairwise relationships among my commitments before you rank them.

My current commitments are these:

1. I think Hume is right that induction cannot be given a non-circular justification.
2. But I do not think that means we should abandon induction; I think continuing to rely on it remains the rational thing to do in practice.
3. I think Goodman's new riddle of induction shows that any finite body of evidence can be made to fit indefinitely many predicates, so the data alone never determines which predicates we should project; and I am persuaded that what in fact guides projection is habit or linguistic entrenchment, the fact that we have projected certain predicates successfully before.
4. At the same time, I think some predicates are objectively more projectible than others because they correspond to real similarities or natural kinds in the world, while gruesome predicates are gerrymandered.
5. I also think science is right to introduce new theoretical predicates that were not previously entrenched in ordinary language, and that some of these newly introduced predicates turn out to be more projectible than the older ones.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a lecture: go through the commitments and identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only look like they are in tension and dissolve on inspection, and which are unproblematic. Rank the genuine tensions by how severe they are, say what would have to be revised in each case to remove the tension, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set consistent. I have not decided which commitment I would give up, so please do not assume any of them is non-negotiable.
