I'm working through Berlin's negative/positive distinction and the republican critique that the real issue is non-domination rather than mere non-interference, and I have a distinction of my own that I want pressure-tested before I build an essay on it.

I've been calling it the difference between loud and quiet liberty. By a loud liberty I mean a freedom whose exercise carries a standing requirement to register, notify, justify, or submit for review: a march that needs a permit, a business that needs a renewable license, a public meeting that needs a list of speakers handed to the police, a form of worship that needs a recognized religious body. By a quiet liberty I mean the same underlying act done without any duty to file, notify, or answer for it — walking down the street without papers, speaking in a public square, meeting in a private home, praying outside any registered structure. The core claim is that a loudly regulated liberty can satisfy negative freedom completely (nobody actually interferes) and republican freedom completely (the reviewing official has no arbitrary discretion; the criteria are clear, appealable, and followed), and yet something about the liberty is still diminished. The standing requirement to make oneself legible to an authority changes what people will risk doing, what they rehearse and avoid, and who they become, even when the authority never says no. So I want to say there is a fourth dimension — quietness — that the standard negative/positive/republican scheme cannot name.

Before I commit to that, I would like an honest assessment. (1) Is quiet liberty a genuine fourth concept, or does it collapse into one of the three once each is stated carefully? (2) Is this a rediscovery under another name — in the privacy literature, or in writing on surveillance and chilling effects — and if so, what is the cleanest statement of the difference between my claim and that existing idea? (3) Where does the distinction misfire? I already see one awkward result: on my view, having to register a car makes driving less free than walking, which either trivializes the concept or forces me to restrict it to some list of core liberties, and I am not sure which horn to take. And are there genuine losses of liberty the quiet/loud distinction is blind to? Please be candid rather than encouraging; I would rather find out now that it is just privacy wearing a heavier coat.
