I'm a graduate student in philosophy, and over two years of working with my supervisor I've assembled a record of her quick, consistent bottom-line verdicts about whether some thing has intentionality of its own — original intentionality — or only intentionality that comes from us, its makers and interpreters. Her rulings so far: a handwritten shopping list (milk, eggs, yeast) is merely derived; a thermostat whose bimetallic strip bends when the room cools is not intentional at all, only information-bearing in a loose "as-if" sense; a honeybee's waggle dance, by which a forager signals direction and distance of a food source to the hive, has original intentionality; a roadside curve-warning sign is merely derived; a person with locked-in syndrome who cannot move or speak but continues to think has original intentionality; a large language model's fluent answer to a factual question is merely derived, since we lend it meaning rather than its meaning what it says on its own; and a corporation's "intent," as invoked in a contract dispute, is merely derived, a legal shorthand at best.

Now a visiting engineer has described a hypothetical artifact: a silicon-based system built to replicate, component by component, the causal organization of a human brain and body, so that across years of ordinary interaction it is behaviorally indistinguishable from a typical person. The case is essentially Dennett's "two-bitser" scenario from the Searle–Dennett exchange, and I want to work out what my supervisor's own track record commits her to saying about it before I ask her directly. Specifically: (1) which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and why; (2) what two or more plausible rules fit all her past verdicts, including at least one rule that would deny the silicon system original intentionality and one that would grant it; (3) what your best guess is about which rule she is actually running, what that guess rests on, and what principle she would be adopting under each possible ruling; and (4) what further case would most decisively tell the candidate rules apart. Please separate what is settled in this corner of philosophy of mind from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
