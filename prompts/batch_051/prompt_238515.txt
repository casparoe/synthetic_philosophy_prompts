I have recently taken over a first-year real analysis course, and I keep noticing a tension in how mainstream mathematics talks about infinity. On one hand, we tell students that infinity is not a number and that they should speak only of limits; on the other, we define the real numbers as a completed set of equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences, treat an infinite decimal such as 0.999... as a single object equal to 1, sum infinite series without a second thought, and quantify over the set of all natural numbers as if it were a finished totality. Physicists and engineers push this further: they integrate over infinite domains and use infinite-dimensional spaces as ordinary tools. Set theorists go further still, comparing infinite cardinalities with Cantor's diagonal argument and using Hilbert's Hotel as an introductory illustration. Meanwhile, a minority tradition — finitists, constructivists, and philosophers who speak of infinity as potential rather than actual — holds that completed infinite totalities are not legitimate objects at all, and that what looks like reasoning about the actual infinite is either shorthand for finite procedures or simply meaningless.

I would like you to reconstruct the philosophical view, or family of views, that this widespread mathematical and scientific practice presupposes about the actual infinite. For the main practices I have listed — the real number line as a completed set, infinite decimals such as 0.999..., infinite series, integration over infinite domains, infinite-dimensional spaces, and Cantorian cardinal arithmetic — tell me which strictly presuppose actual infinite totalities, which merely sit comfortably with that view but could be licensed under an instrumentalist or potential-infinity reading, and which are underdetermined between rival positions. I am less interested in which view is true than in getting precise about what the practice implicitly commits its practitioners to, and where it leaves them free.

After your discussion, end with a single JSON object that summarises your answer, with these fields: `overall_implicit_view` (a one-sentence statement of the position that best rationalises mainstream practice), `minimal_shared_commitment` (the weakest claim about the actual infinite that the listed practices cannot easily do without), `practices` (an array of objects, each with `practice`, `classification` equal to one of `strictly_presupposes`, `merely_fits`, or `underdetermined`, `compatible_alternatives`, and `explanation`), and `main_decision_points` (an array of the places where practice underdetermines the philosophy).
