I'm meant to present a position on the Born rule in Everettian quantum mechanics to my foundations reading group, and I'd like a consistency check on the package I've half-assembled before I go in. Here's roughly where I land:

(1) The wavefunction is the whole story: it always evolves unitarily, measurement is just entanglement, there's no collapse, and every term in the final superposition is real.
(2) Still, the Born rule can't just be an extra axiom. It has to be recovered from the structure of the theory or from rational constraints, otherwise the theory doesn't actually make empirical predictions.
(3) I'm suspicious of the usual self-locating uncertainty story. If branching is deterministic and each branch contains a successor of me, I don't see what pre-measurement me is supposed to be uncertain about. Saying I'll probably wind up in the high-weight branch sounds like a category mistake, because there's a me in every branch.
(4) But I do think the decision-theoretic line of argument — a rational agent who cares about all their successors and doesn't care which one they are should apportion their concern by the squared amplitudes, so squared amplitudes act a lot like probabilities — is basically sound.
(5) I also think that when physics talks about probability it's objective chance, not just credence or preference. So I need the Born rule to be about the world, not about what bets I'd take.
(6) I'm fine with saying that after a measurement the successor in a branch can be uncertain about which branch they're in — that's just ordinary indexical ignorance, and doesn't seem fishy to me in the way pre-measurement uncertainty does.

Which of these are actually in tension, which pairs only look like they're in tension, and what's the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity and say specifically which commitment has to give in each case, rather than treating every pair as equally problematic. And please answer in British English.
