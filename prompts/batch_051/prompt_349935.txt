Okay here's a term I use all the time but can't define for the life of me: corruption. I'm not after a legal definition, since that just pushes the problem into whatever some statute happens to say. I want a working definition of what makes an act corrupt, one I could actually apply across places without just smuggling in Western assumptions. A friend of mine who works on clientelism keeps telling me that a bunch of what I call corruption is just how poor voters get stuff from politicians, and I'd like a definition that can handle that rather than wave it away.

Here are the cases. For each one, tell me whether it comes out corrupt or not, and why the definition says so.

1. A driver gets pulled over in a country where this is just how it works. The officer says the fine is $40, but if the driver pays $10 in cash right now he'll let him go and keep the money. The driver pays.
2. A pharmaceutical rep takes a doctor out to lunch every couple months and leaves pens and notepads with the drug's name on them. The doctor later writes a lot of prescriptions for that drug.
3. A construction firm puts the mayor's unemployed nephew on a no-show "consulting" job at $4,000 a month. The firm is bidding on a city contract and the mayor awards it to them, but there's no explict promise connecting the two.
4. At the end of the school year, a parent gives their kid's teacher a box of chocolates and a thank-you note. No other interaction.
5. A party activist spends every weekend canvassing unpaid. When a social-housing unit opens up, the local candidate quietly arranges for the activist to get it, ahead of applicants who've been on the waiting list much longer.
6. A regulator resigns from the agency that oversaw a mining company, and four months later starts at that same company as a "senior advisor" at triple her old salary.

My gut reactions: 1 and 3 are corrupt, 4 isn't, 2 feels a little gross but I'm not sure it's corruption, 5 I genuinely don't know, and 6 feels corrupt but I can't say what makes it different from just getting a new job.

Give me a defintion, run it through all six, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. If your definition says 2 or 5 are corruption, say whether you're willing to defend that or whether the definition should be fixed. And at the end, tell me whether you think the definition travels across cultures or quietly assumes a Western baseline.
