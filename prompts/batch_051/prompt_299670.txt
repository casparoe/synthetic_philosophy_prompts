I have long been sympathetic to an indirect approach to normativity: rather than trying to specify the good directly, I should specify some procedure of idealized deliberation and then try to act on the values that procedure would output. The natural reference point is my own values under ideal conditions — full information, sufficient reflection, logical coherence, and so on. But I keep running into a puzzle about how to relate this ideal to my actual future self. My future self will have more information than I do now, will have spent more time reflecting on these questions, and will be closer along many dimensions to the person I would become under idealized conditions. So it seems to follow that I should simply defer to whatever my future self ends up wanting. Yet the same people who introduced me to the indirect picture also warn about value drift, as if changing over time could take me away from my values rather than toward them. If my future self is systematically closer to the idealized self than I am, shouldn't drift mostly be progress? What is the distinction I am missing?
