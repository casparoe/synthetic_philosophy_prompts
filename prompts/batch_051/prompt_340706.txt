Write an essay on a live dispute about Moore's paradox. Take the omissive form, schematically: p, but I do not believe that p. Asserting this is strikingly absurd, and on most accounts the corresponding judgement is also irrational, even though the proposition can be true of an agent with ordinary self-ignorance.

The controversy to adjudicate is whether this absurdity is fundamentally a fact about assertion or a fact about belief. Speech-act explanations locate the trouble in norms or conventions governing assertion—for example, the knowledge rule of assertion, according to which asserting p represents the speaker as knowing p, combined with the platitude that knowledge entails belief. Doxastic explanations locate the trouble in rational constraints on belief itself—for example, a positive-introspection principle according to which a rational agent who believes p is thereby in a position to self-ascribe that belief.

Weigh the main arguments for each family of explanation, and press them with the cases that are usually taken to discriminate between them. At least address: the contrast with the unproblematic third-person analogue (p, but she does not believe that p); the behaviour of the sentence in embedded and non-assertoric contexts, such as conditionals or lines delivered by an actor; the commissive form (p, but I believe that not-p); and the possibility of rationally self-opaque agents whose evidence about their own beliefs has been contrived. Consider whether the omissive and commissive forms require a unified treatment, and whether the distinction between assertion and belief yields two distinct paradoxes rather than one.

Do not end on a merely disjunctive or non-committal note. Defend a verdict about which level of explanation is fundamental—or defend a bifurcated view, provided you specify precisely how the doxastic and speech-act phenomena are related.

Write the essay in British English.
