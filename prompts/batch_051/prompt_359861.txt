Take the murderer-at-the-door case but change the question. The murderer doesn't ask whether your friend is in the house; he asks whether your friend was in the cellar when you last saw him. He was: the last time you saw him, twenty minutes ago, he was ducking into the cellar. You know he has since moved to the attic—you heard him moving about up there—but you also know the murderer will treat a yes answer as meaning he's in the cellar now, search it, find nobody, and leave. So a strictly true yes sends him to the wrong room. Does the usual standoff between the Kantian who thinks you mustn't lie even to save a life and the person who thinks that's absurd survive this variation? Work through what both sides should say here. In particular: does the duty not to lie, by itself, give the Kantian any reason not to say yes, or does the Kantian have to wheel in a seperate duty against true-but-misleading answers? And if it needs the extra duty, does that show the original case was doing less work than people think, or just that we've changed the subject?
