Had a long argument tonight with a friend about whether i have to defer to my future self, and i still can't tell which of us was right, so i wrote down what i think i'm committed to. Here's the list i want checked:

(1) I accept something like Van Fraassen's Reflection principle: my current credence in any proposition should equal my current expectation of my later credence in that proposition.
(2) I think conditionalization is the right rule for updating when you get new evidence.
(3) I think it can be perfectly rational to know now that i'll forget some relevant evidence later — a code i just memorized, or half of what was said in the arguement.
(4) I don't think future-me is special; deferring to future-me is just deferring to one more source, so it can't be a bedrock requirement.
(5) If i know i'm about to be cognitively compromised — drunk, severely sleep-deprived, or manipulated into overconfidence — i don't think i should move my current credence to match what compromised-me is likely to think.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what would have to give to make the set hang together? Rank the tensions by severity instead of saying they're all equally bad.
