So I'm a novelist, which I mention only because I collect arguments I overhear and feed them to my characters. Last weekend two friends of mine got into it about a video that had been going around our town: a clip of a school-board member seeming to say something awful about a student, which turned out to be (probably) AI-generated. By the time it was debunked, half the town didn't believe the debunking and half spent all their time explaining why it was obviously fake.

Leah said the scary thing wasn't that a fake fooled people. It was that after one decent fake, every real recording becomes easier to dismiss. Recordings used to be the backstop: the thing you could point to when testimony conflicted, the thing that settled which side was lying. Now the backstop itself gets treated like a possible plant. Dev said she was romanticizing how much recordings ever did. A recording is not self-authenticating, he said; it always sat inside a chain of custody, corroboration, who filmed it, who vouches for it, institutions that checked. What she's mourning is a backstop that never existed in that pure form.

Leah said no, the point is that institutions are made of people watching the same clips. If fakes get cheap and plentiful, the quick check—compare the clip against known footage, see whether a newsroom or a court will stand behind it—gets expensive exactly when it's most needed, and the institution drowns. Dev said she was imagining everyone as maximally gullible and institutions as frozen. Courts have evidentiary standards and newsrooms have verification desks, and in any case most of ordinary life never ran on video evidence; it ran on neighbors and local knowledge. Leah said "most of ordinary life" is doing a lot of work there: political scandals, police violence, war crimes—those run on exactly this kind of footage, and those are the cases where the powerless most need a backstop.

Then they both turned to me, because they know I'm writing a novel about a forged video, and I had to admit I couldnt tell who had the better of it. I want to steal the sharpest version of this argument for the book, so go through it move by move and tell me who's right about what, which points are overstated, and what both of them are missing.
