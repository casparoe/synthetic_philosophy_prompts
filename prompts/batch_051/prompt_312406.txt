Question: A mother's adult son has become cruel, contemptuous, and indifferent to her. She says: "I know exactly what he is like; I do not love him for those qualities. I love him because he is my son." Explain how an appraisal theorist, a bestowal theorist, and a relationship-based theorist would each analyze this remark, taking Frankfurt, Velleman, and Kolodny as your three anchors. Be precise about what, on each view, the relevant reason for love is, and about whether that reason must exist antecedently to the mother's love.

Student answer: An appraisal theorist holds that love is justified by the antecedent value of the beloved, and Velleman is the clearest example because he locates that value in the beloved's rational nature rather than in any particular merits. A bestowal theorist, chiefly Frankfurt, denies that the beloved must have such value in advance; love itself confers value on the beloved, so the mother's love does not need a preexisting reason. Kolodny completes the trio by saying the reason is the relationship itself. Kolodny is therefore still a bestowal theorist in a broader sense, because a relationship is partly constituted by the lover's caring and is not simply "out there" before the love. All three views accordingly agree that the mother's "because he is my son" is not an evaluation of the son's character, and they differ only about whether some further appraisal is required before love is permissible. Her love is thus non-deluded on Frankfurt's and Kolodny's views, but precarious on Velleman's, since Velleman would have her cite a virtue in the son that simply is not there.
