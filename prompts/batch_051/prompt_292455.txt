I am a high school senior writing an extended essay for a summer philosophy of biology program, and I have been trying to get clear on the debate over whether the word "meme" is more than a metaphor. The popular story is easy to recite: bits of culture are copied, vary, and get selected, so the term earns its keep. But the more I look at the disagreements, the less I understand what an adequate theory would actually have to establish before that claim stops being a loose analogy. I do not want a survey of positions; I want the specification up front. Please write the desiderata that a theory of cultural inheritance and selection must meet for us to be entitled to say that cultural variants genuinely replicate and evolve by selection, not merely that it is sometimes illuminating to describe them that way.

In particular, address what such a theory must say, and whether it must say anything at all, about the following: what the unit is and how it is individuated (a story, a word, a technique, a behavioral disposition?); how much copying fidelity is required for cumulative selection, given that a joke or a recipe changes each time it is passed on; where variation comes from, and whether deliberately designed improvements count as mutation; how to define fitness for a cultural variant without making the explanation circular, since "it spreads because it is fit" can collapse into "it is fit because it spreads"; whether transmission can be many-to-one and horizontal rather than parent-to-offspring and still support Darwinian selection; whether the theory requires a workable genotype-phenotype distinction for culture; and whether the copying mechanism it posits must be consistent with what we know about human memory, imitation, and social learning rather than just postulating a copying black box. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a stylistic preference, and mark which are non-negotiable for any serious theory and which are merely preferences of particular camps.

Then tell me whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—that is, whether the list itself suggests that no literal Darwinian account of culture can meet all of them—and identify which single requirement does the most work in separating a literal Darwinian account of culture from the metaphor-only reading. If the correct conclusion is that some requirement must be relaxed, say which one, and what we lose by relaxing it. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring particular authors.
