ok so my schools got an online forum where like 400 students talk, and the student council is trying to figure out how to keep it from turning into either a free for all where the loudest intolerant people set the tone or a place where mods just delete anything they don't like. I got kind of obsessed with the paradox of tolerance (the idea that if you tolerate literally everyone you just hand the platform to people whose whole goal is to make sure other people can't use it). So I came up with a rule and I want someone to stress test it instead of just tell me if its good or bad.

The proposal is this: everyones account is tied to their school id so no anonymous alts. Each account gets 3 red cards per month. You can spend one on any post to report it as intolerant. A post auto-hides once 3 different people red-card it within 24 hours, until a mod reviews it within 48 hours. If the mods uphold it, the poster looses one of their 5 posting tokens for the month, and at zero tokens you can read but not post until the month resets. If the mods reject it, each person who spent a red card looses a posting token instead. And red cards are public, so everyone can see who flagged who.

Walk me through what this actually does to the forum. Specifically: what kinds of posts get red-carded first (my guess is not the worst stuff), what happens to minority or unpopular opinions that aren't actually intolerant, how people game it (coordinated brigading? people hoarding red cards? mods strategically rejecting reports to punish enemies?), and where the whole design fails hard. Then say whether the failure modes can be fixed without turning it into a normal report button with extra steps.
