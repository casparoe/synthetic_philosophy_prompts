Here's an asymmetry I've been chewing on and can't quite justify. If a couple rearranges their whole life to shape their future kid after birth — moving for a school catchment, doing flashcards at breakfast, banning sugar, driving to music lessons — everyone calls it devoted parenting. But if the same couple does IVF, has five viable embryos, and picks one partly because its polygenic score predicts a little more educational attainment, a lot of the same people mutter about eugenics. Both are parents spending effort to nudge the same child's expected life uphill; one just works through the environment and the other through the genotype. There's even a view that the couple isn't just permitted but obligated to pick the embryo with the best expected prospects. I don't need you to sign up for that strong claim — but it makes the asymmetry sharper: why is one version of the exact same optimizing move unobjectionable and the other faintly monstrous? Is there a defensible principle that makes the environmental/genetic line carry real moral weight, or is it mostly a felt ick about genes dressed up as a principle? If you defend the asymmetry, make it survive the case where the identical end state — better reading, lower diabetes risk, whatever — could be reached either by nightly phonics and a strict diet or by embryo selection. And if you think the asymmetry can't be defended, say what we should give up: the squeemishness about selecting embryos, or the freedom to shape kids' environments just as aggressively.
