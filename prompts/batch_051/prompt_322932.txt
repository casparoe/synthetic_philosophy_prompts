I am trying to consolidate my views on indicative conditionals before presenting them, and I would like a consistency audit. Here is the package I currently hold. (1) The Adams thesis is correct for rational credence in unembedded indicative conditionals: P(if A then C) should equal P(C|A) whenever P(A)>0. (2) Lewis’s triviality proof is sound and shows that no proposition expressed by if A then C can obey (1) in full generality within classical probability. (3) Nonetheless, conditionals embed; negations and conjunctions of conditionals, and conditionals in the antecedents of other conditionals, have determinate truth-conditional content, so a uniform semantics must assign them propositions. (4) I adopt Kratzer-style restrictor semantics, on which if restricts a contextually supplied modal base, as the best uniform semantics satisfying (3). (5) On that semantics, bare conditionals are truth-evaluable at worlds relative to modal base and ordering source, but their truth conditions do not generally agree with the Adams conditional probability; I regard the Adams thesis as a norm of assertability rather than of truth. (6) I also accept a principle of semantic compositionality: the semantic contribution of if A then C to a larger sentence is the same whether the conditional is embedded or not. (7) I reject the material conditional analysis even for embedded truth conditions; there are cases where not A is true but the complex conditional is intuitively false in its context. Which of these are in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify at least one apparent tension that dissolves under closer analysis, and state which single revision would leave the most coherent package.
