I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad philosophy of medicine class and I want a sorting quiz, vaguely titled "what do you actually think a disease is?" Eight to ten questions, each a concrete little case — decayed teeth, age-related cognitive slowing, infertility in someone who never wanted kids, hearing loss, ADHD, prolonged grief, a Deaf person who doesn't want an implant, that sort of thing. Give each question two or three answer options that are short reasons for counting (or not counting) the thing as a disease, not slogans. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least: Boorse-style naturalism (disease = subnormal functioning for an age/sex reference class), pure normativism (disease = disvalued condition), Wakefield-style harmful dysfunction, and a social-constructivist or eliminativist view. The whole point is to sort people by there case judgments, not by the label they'd pick for themselves, so don't ask any question that mentions the labels or asks whether values influence medicine. After the key, flag the pairs of positions the questions can't cleanly separate, and note any answer-matrix cell nobody defends — for each empty cell, say briefly whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it a bit wry.
