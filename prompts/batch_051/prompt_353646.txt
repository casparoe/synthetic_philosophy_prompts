I've been turning over an argumet in the satisficing / bounded rationality stuff, and I can't find the step to reject, even though the conclusion seems to make the whole optimize/satisfice distinction collapse. Here it is.

(1) A bounded agent never has a certificate — a proof or guarantee — that the option it picks is optimal, because optimality means no better alternative exists anywhere in the search space, and a finite agent hasn't searched the whole space and can't prove the nonexistence of better alternatives without spending resources it may not have.

(2) So whenever a bounded agent stops searching and chooses, it chooses without knowing whether some unexamined alternative would have been better. From its own point of view, it picks the best option it happened to find by the time it stopped.

(3) But "pick the best option found by the time you stop" is just satisficing, once you accept that a stopping time is set by a threshold on when the search is good enough — and any stopping time whatsoever can be redescribed as such a threshold.

(4) Therefore every feasible decision procedure for a bounded agent is a satisficing procedure.

(5) So satisficing isn't a distinctive or rival way of choosing; it's a universal feature of finite decision-making. An optimizer is just a satisficer with a very high threshold.

(6) But that can't be the sense people mean. There is a real difference between an agent that stops searching when the expected marginal gain of more search drops below its cost and then takes the best option found, and an agent that takes the first option above a fixed mediocre bar and goes home. If (5) is true, that difference is only a difference in threshold, not in the kind of procedure, and "optimizing" just names a threshold-setting policy inside satisficing.

I'd most like to keep (2), the stopping-rule picture — I think that's the best thing bounded rationality has going. So tell me: where does this argument go wrong, if it does, and what do I give up if I insist on keeping (2), say by rejecting (3) or (5)? And if I have to choose between "satisficing is trivially true of every feasible procedure" and "there's a real optimize/satisfice distinction," which is the cheaper one to abandon?
