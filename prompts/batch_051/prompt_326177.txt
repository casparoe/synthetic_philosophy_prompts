I'm a phd student working on social ontology, and my sister runs the data team at a county public health department. They're redesigning their intake and survey forms, and every time someone proposes a new field — race, gender identity, whether a patient is unbanked, head of household — she has no principled way to decide how to collect it: checkboxes vs. free text, self-report vs. someone else decides, one question vs. three. My sense is the philosophy actually has something to say here: it distinguishes self-identification from social positioning from institutional facts, and it warns that for some of these kinds the act of classifying people changes the people being classified. Please turn that into a decision procedure her team, who have no philosophy background, can actualy run in under an hour before the form goes to the equity officer and the lawyers, where mistakes mean bad data for funding and care decisions. It should give the questions to ask in order; how to tell which kind of construction you're dealing with; which distinctions from the theory have to survive simplification and which can be dropped; and where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a human because it's a values call. Then run it on three cases: adding a MENA option to the race field, adding a nonbinary option to gender, and adding a yes/no field for whether a patient is unbanked. I expect one of these to come out as 'don't collect this as an identity-style field,' and I'd like the procedure to explain why honestly rather than soften it.
