Write the homily for a Christian funeral at which the preacher is committed to divine impassibility — the position that God, as pure actuality and perfect being, cannot undergo change or be acted upon, so that while God eternally knows, wills, and loves creatures without limit, God does not literally suffer, grieve, or feel pain in response to creaturely events. The deceased is a seven-year-old patient who died after a long illness in the hospital where the preacher serves as chaplain. The congregation includes the child's parents and older siblings, several oncology nurses who cared for the child, and a retired academic theologian who will regard any drift into passibilism — the view that God can suffer or be moved by creatures — as a serious philosophical error rather than a venial figure of speech. The homily should run roughly eight minutes when spoken. It must genuinely comfort a grieving family without asserting anything the preacher considers false: in particular, without saying that God feels their pain, that God's heart is broken, that the child's suffering entered into the divine life, or that God is literally a fellow sufferer. The comfort must come from what an impassible God is and does, not from setting the doctrine aside for the occasion. Show how the doctrine shapes the speech: where it forces the preacher to decline a familiar consolation, and what the preacher offers in its place. If the preacher uses phrases like God is present in your pain or God shares your sorrow, give those claims a content that is compatible with the doctrine as stated rather than a disguised passibilism.
