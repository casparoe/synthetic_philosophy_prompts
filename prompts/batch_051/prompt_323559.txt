I supervise a small building-inspection unit in a municipal office; I have no academic training in philosophy, and the thinking I have done on this question has come from years of watching people at work. For a long time I admired a colleague who struck me as the most incorruptible person I had ever met: no gift, no favor, no friendship, no hard-luck story, and no threat ever altered how he applied the code. I took that to be an unqualified moral achievement. I have come to suspect that what I was actually admiring was not goodness but something narrower and more formal — a refusal to let any particular feature of a case modify whichever rule he had already adopted — and that this refusal is only as good as the rule or project it happens to fix him to.

I want a thought experiment built to isolate that suspicion. Construct a realistic case, set in a workplace or a municipal office rather than on a grand stage, in which a person is maximally incorruptible in the sense just described and yet does something seriously wrong. The wrongness must not come from an obviously bad rule — the standard he applies should be one that a reasonable person could endorse in the ordinary run of cases — nor from malice, cruelty, laziness, or some unrelated weakness; it should fall out of the incorruptibility itself, working exactly as intended. And no bribe, payoff, blackmail, or threat may appear anywhere in the story — the failure should come from how the person handles the ordinary facts of the case, not from resisting a corrupt offer. Keep the scenario to one paragraph.

Then do the demonstration. State exactly what the person does and why it is wrong. Show why the view I used to hold — that being incorruptible in this sense is a virtue in every case, so any bad outcome must be blamed on something else — is forced to deny or explain away the wrongdoing in an awkward way, and how the view I now lean toward — that incorruptibility is not itself a good but only a safeguard, whose value depends on what it protects — handles the case cleanly. Finally, tell me which single feature of the case is doing the real work, how the case changes if that feature is removed, and how a defender of the old view would try to reject the case without simply declaring it unrealistic.
