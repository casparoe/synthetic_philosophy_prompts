write me a poem about deterministic chaos—not the butterfly-effect cliche where a flap causes a hurricane, but the more careful claim that a system can be fully deterministic and still unpredictable in practice because of sensitive dependence on initial conditions. i want the form to do some of the work. take a villanelle and break its rule that the refrains come back verbatim: each recurrence of a refrain changes something small—a tense, a number, an adjective, the word order—so the line is recognizably the same but never exactly repeats, the way an orbit on a strange attractor keeps returning to the same region without ever retracing itself. by the final couplet the two refrains should sit side by side like two trajectories that began almost identically and have since diverged. and the poem should render the actual distinction between randomness and a system whose next state is fixed but whose long run is effectively incalculable—accurate enough that someone in philosophy of physics wouldn't wince.
