I am a student at a high school whose student council constitution has produced a genuine dispute, and I would like a careful analysis of it. The constitution says:

Article IV, Section 1: "The President and the Vice President shall be elected at the final general meeting of the school year for a term of one year, and shall take office at the close of that meeting."

Article IV, Section 2: "If the President resigns, is removed, or is unable to perform the duties of the office, the Vice President shall act as President until the President resumes the duties or until the next regular election."

Article IV, Section 3: "The outgoing President shall preside at the final general meeting until the close of that meeting."

This year, the election was held at the final meeting and a President-elect and Vice President-elect were announced. Twenty minutes before the close of the meeting, the election committee disqualified the President-elect for exceeding the campaign spending limit. The meeting then closed. The Vice President-elect claims that she took office at the close of the meeting as Vice President and, because the presidency is now vacant, must act as President. The outgoing President claims that the disqualification voided the whole ticket, that no one took office, and that she therefore remains in office. The Secretary claims that the constitution has run out and that the Council as a whole should govern until a special election can be arranged.

The constitution says nothing about special elections and nothing about a candidate-elect who is disqualified before taking office. The drafters plainly did not have this case in mind: Section 2 assumes an incumbent President who resigns, is removed, or becomes unable to act, and Section 1 assumes the winners simply take office. I am less interested in the practical politics than in the constitutional question: what does the constitution actually require or permit here? Please argue the question separately from the text, from the purpose of the succession clause, from what the drafters would have said if asked, and from what a reasonable reader would take the constitution to mean, and then tell me which approach should win and why. Please also tell me what would change your mind: which of these arguments, if it were stronger or weaker, would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
