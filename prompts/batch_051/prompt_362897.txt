I'm a PhD student in social choice and I've been going in circles on a little contrived case I put together for a seminar. I can't decide whether it's a real result or whether I smuggled something in, so I'd appreciate a second read.

Take a two-person society, Prude and Lewd, and one copy of a racy novel. Three outcomes: P (Prude reads it), L (Lewd reads it), N (nobody reads it). Their preferences:

Prude: N > P > L. He'd rather nobody read it, but if someone has to, he'd take the hit himself rather than let Lewd be corrupted.
Lewd: P > L > N. He'd like Prude to loosen up; failing that he'll read it himself; nobody reading it is worst.

Suppose we want a social choice rule that takes any profile of individual preferences and outputs a social ranking of the outcomes. The following four conditions each look close to mandatory:

(1) Unrestricted domain: the rule has to handle every logically possible profile, including this one.
(2) Pareto: if both individuals strictly prefer x to y, the social ranking puts x above y.
(3) Minimal liberalism: each individual is decisive over at least one pair of alternatives in his own personal sphere. Here Prude is decisive over {P, N} (whether he himself reads it), and Lewd is decisive over {L, N} (whether he himself reads it).
(4) Transitivity: the social ranking has no cycles.

Now apply the conditions to this profile. By (3) and Prude's preference N > P, society ranks N above P. By (3) and Lewd's preference L > N, society ranks L above N. By (2), since both prefer P to L, society ranks P above L. So the social ranking is P > L > N > P: a cycle, which (4) forbids. So at least one of (1)-(4) has to go.

Each one seemed non-negotiable a week ago, though. Pareto is just unanimity. Minimal liberalism is embarassingly weak—it doesn't grant self-ownership or full autonomy, just one small pair per person. And transitivity is the bare minimum you'd want from a ranking. Which premise do you reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost? I'd be least willing to give up (3), but I can't tell whether keeping it pushes me into giving up something even more central. Walk me through the tradeoff without pulling the punch.
