I keep running into a snag in debates about the mind, and I'd like to know if it has a name. The pattern: someone says a person's thirst caused her to reach for the glass, and the other side replies that a physical story about her nervous system was already sufficient to produce the reaching, so the thirst itself did no extra causal work. I see the same thing with grief and tears, or pain and wincing. But this isnt the usual case of overdetermination, where two independent causes each would have done it (two bullets hitting the same target); here the mental cause and the physical cause aren't independent—they seem like two sides of one event. Is there an established term for this exact problem, and what precisely marks it off from ordinary overdetermination? I've also never been satisfied with the reply that it's just one event described two ways, since the mental description still sounds causally idle. Is there a standard way of handling that, and does it actually work?
