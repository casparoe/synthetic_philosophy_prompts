Science desk question, if you don't mind. I'm writing a piece on how scientific theories actually get accepted, and I keep coming back to Einstein and Mercury. So why do historians and philosophers still describe Mercury as confirming general relativity? Surely the Bayesian result means it shouldn't count? I don't want to mangle the philosophy in print, so could you set me straight? And please answer in American English, as my editor insists.
