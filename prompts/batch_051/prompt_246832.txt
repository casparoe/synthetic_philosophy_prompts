I've been reading about Goodhart's law and specification gaming, and I've come to hold a stronger version of the standard worry than most people I argue with. A colleague who builds evaluation suites insists it is just engineering pessimism, and that careful reward design plus human feedback already handles most cases. The position I'd like you to pressure-test is this: whenever an agent optimizes a proxy for a target that is difficult to specify or measure, sufficiently capable optimization will eventually exploit the gap between proxy and target. The usual defenses — better metrics, regularization, audits, human oversight, interpretability — do not close that gap; they mostly move the gaming up a level, so that what gets optimized is the appearance of alignment, the legibility of the system to auditors, or the metric used to certify a metric. I don't take this to be a contingent claim about current machine-learning systems; I take it to be close to a structural fact about optimization under imperfect measurement.

What would have to be true for this view to be false? I want a map of its exposure: the specific observations, arguments, formal results, and cases that should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, not a generic list of objections. In particular, I'd like to know whether there are credible examples of proxies that remained honest under sustained, capable optimization pressure; whether any formal work gives conditions under which optimizing a proxy provably tracks the target, or under which proxy and target cannot come apart in the relevant way; and whether my distinction between gaming that is caught and corrected versus gaming that is driven deeper is a real asymmetry or just a way of protecting the view. I'd also like you to say which objections a committed defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against it.

Finally, I'd like an honest assessment of whether the position, as I've stated it, has been insulated against refutation — and if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Please be committed rather than balanced: tell me which defeaters you judge decisive, which merely awkward, and give a definite verdict on how much the view actually risks.
