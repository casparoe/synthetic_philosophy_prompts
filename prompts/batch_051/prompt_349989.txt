Some philosophers argue that if panpsychism—or at least a pan-sentient variant of it—is true, then experience is not confined to brains, and much of that experience may be negatively valenced: electrical currents, flames, and elementary-particle interactions might each be accompanied by some amount of suffering. On that view, the moral ledger of the universe is radically larger and darker than we normally suppose, and fields such as electronics or high-energy physics carry hidden ethical stakes. I want to know whether any of this can be brought into contact with evidence. Set out the strongest concrete test of the claim that at least some non-biological physical processes involve negatively valenced experience: specify what you would measure or observe, what pattern of results would count as support and what as disconfirmation, and then be explicit about what a positive result would fail to establish—in particular, whether it would show that the physical system itself suffers, or only that our behavioral and structural markers of valence happen to extend to it. If the core claim cannot be tested even in principle, say so, and explain what rational role, if any, it could still play in ethical decision-making.
