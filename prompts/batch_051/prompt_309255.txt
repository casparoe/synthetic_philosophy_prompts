Here is a parallel I cannot decide about, and I would like a precise judgment on it rather than an intuition. In epistemology, the bootstrapping problem runs something like this. Someone has a gas gauge that is, in fact, perfectly reliable. Every time she glances at it, she forms two beliefs: that the gauge reads a certain value, and that the tank is in the corresponding state—though her only access to the tank is through the gauge itself. From those two beliefs she can validly deduce that the gauge read correctly on that occasion; after enough such occasions she can compile a track record and conclude, apparently with justification, that the gauge is reliable. The trouble is that no step ever checks the gauge against anything independent of it. On reliability-friendly theories of knowledge, each step comes out justified and the final conclusion is known—this is both the bootstrapping objection and the easy-knowledge problem in miniature, since ordinary perceptual knowledge seems similarly to make knowledge of perception's reliability a bit too cheap.

Now compare a standard engineering practice. A compiler for a language L can be written in L itself. To get off the ground, one needs a working implementation of L obtained by some independent route, such as an earlier version of the compiler or a separately written bootstrap compiler. That implementation compiles the new compiler's source into a working binary, and from then on the binary—produced by the very compiler it implements—is used to compile the compiler's own later versions. Computer scientists do not treat this self-involving procedure as viciously circular, though superficially it looks a lot like the gauge case: a tool's own outputs are placed in the chain of trust that certifies the tool.

My question is whether the resemblance is deep or merely verbal. As a philosopher who is also comfortable with how compilers and their toolchains actually work, please first brainstorm the possible symmetries and disanalogies between the two cases. Then do three things: state the explicit mapping between the epistemic structure and the engineering structure; say what would have to be true of both for any lesson to transfer from one domain to the other; and identify at least one claim that the analogy makes tempting but that turns out false. End with your verdict on whether the parallel is structural or just a pun on the word bootstrapping.
