I'm a philosophy grad student giving a talk at our reading group next month, and the talk defends the claim that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument doesn't by itself get you the block universe. I'll be relatively careful about the setup. The argument, as I'll present it: there's no frame-independent simultaneity in special relativity, so two spacelike-separated events can be ordered differently in different inertial frames, and an event that's in my future can be simultaneous with some distant observer's "now." Since no observer's "now" is more legitimate than any other, the argument says, the event is real for that observer and therefore real full stop; so the future is already as real as the present, and we're forced toward a block. My line is that the whole thing hangs on a hidden premise — that being simultaneous in some frame is a way of being real — and that premise should be refused: "real" isn't a frame-indexed property, and once you drop it the argument shrinks to the much weaker point that there's no invariant global present.

Here's who'll be in the room. One professor works on foundations of spacetime and is a convinced eternalist; he thinks the block is forced by the lack of an invariant foliation, and he regards Rietdijk–Putnam as an old curiosity rather than the real argument. A postdoc in the group is a neo-Lorentzian who thinks presentism survives because special relativity is compatible with a preferred frame; she'll say I'm bending over backwards for Minkowski. Then there are a few grad students who can follow the train thought but haven't read the metaphysics side; they'll suspect I'm just playing word games with "real." I need a prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from these people, ordered by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each of them would phrase it, with a short reply for each and an honest note on where the reply is weakest. Tell me what I should concede up front and what I should hold. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
