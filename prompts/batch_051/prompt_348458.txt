My friend is reading this philosophy of math book by some guy named Lakatos, I think, and now he won't stop saying math is basically experimental science. The story he gave me is this. People originally thought the formula worked for every polyhedron. Then someone found a freaky counterexample, like a cube with a rectangular tunnel bored through it, where the formula gives 0 instead of 2. And instead of throwing out the theorem, mathematicians just narrowed the definition of "polyhedron" so the tunnel-cube didn't count. The book's point is that this is the exact move scientists make when an experiment contradicts their theory: you patch the theory or get pickier about what counts as a test of it. So the analogy is: mathematicians are like scientists, theorems are like hypotheses, counterexamples are like experiments, and a proof is just a conjecture that hasn't been refuted yet. Which would mean math gives us no more certainty than physics does, and stuff like 1+1=2 is just really well tested.

I actually like this idea becuase it makes math feel less like magic and more like something you could poke at and revise.
