A forecasting community I belong to is debating a governance mechanism built around Dutch books, and I would like a detailed, unsentimental analysis of how it would actually behave rather than a verdict on whether it is a good idea. The proposal is this. Every member maintains a public credence ledger: for any claim about which they have publicly stated a numerical probability or offered odds, that number—representing their degree of belief, between zero and one—is entered in the ledger. Any member may then submit a package of bets against another member, using only bets priced at the odds implied by the target's own ledger entries, and allege that the package is a Dutch book, meaning the target would lose money however the relevant events turned out. If a referee validates the package, the bets are executed automatically at stakes drawn from a deposit every member must post, and the target cannot decline individual bets; they may only cap their total exposure in advance, and a lower cap takes effect only after a seven-day delay. A validated Dutch book pays the challenger the guaranteed winnings plus a finder's fee taken from the target's deposit. If the package is rejected—because the underlying claims are not jointly resolvable, the numbers had been explicitly marked as provisional or non-literal, or the bets could not actually be honoured at the stated odds—the challenger forfeits an equal bond to the target.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a referendum on Dutch book arguments. I want a walk-through of what the rule does to how members state their opinions: which claims they attach numbers to, how precise those numbers are, and whether the public ledger ever comes to track private belief rather than merely evading the referee. Then say who gets challenged and which challenges are genuinely attempts to elicit or discipline information rather than harassment, punishment, or fee extraction; identify where the system is gamed first; and separate the failure modes that could be fixed by adjusting parameters from those intrinsic to using Dutch books as an enforcement device rather than as a normative argument. Throughout, keep coherence distinct from accuracy and candour, and say whether the mechanism improves either of the latter.
