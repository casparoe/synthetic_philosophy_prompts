audit this analogy for me. give me the correspondence table first — dreamer, dream monster, the knowledge that its not real, the fear, waking up — and say what plays the role of each in the fiction case. then the places the mapping breaks. what does the lucid-dream picture quietly import about how fiction engages us (vividness, immersion, felt presence, a simulated environment) that reading or watching usually lacks? and does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the conclusion? if so, is there a better analogy, or should we stop leaning on analogies here?
