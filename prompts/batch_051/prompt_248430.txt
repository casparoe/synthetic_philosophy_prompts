I am a PhD student working on the cognitive science of contemplative traditions. A colleague in a neighbouring discipline has been defending what I think of as the strong ineffability thesis: that certain contemplative or mystical states place their subject in genuine cognitive contact with contents that are in principle incapable of propositional expression. The thesis is meant to be stronger than the familiar weaker claims—that such states are hard to describe, that their contents are private in the way an ache is private, or that the subject acquires a practical capacity without a corresponding verbal rule. I cannot see how to tell the strong thesis apart from those weaker claims empirically, but I would rather test it than dismiss it as untestable.

I want you to design the strongest feasible study that bears on the strong thesis as stated. Be concrete about the participants, the task, the comparison conditions, and the measured outcome that would speak for or against the thesis. One type of design I keep reaching for, though I am not wedded to it, is a forced-choice sorting or matching task in which practitioners who report an ineffable state must repeatedly classify new items or episodes as falling under it or not while being unable to state a rule; I am unsure whether such a design can distinguish in-principle inexpressibility from what is merely, in practice, inaccessible to verbal report. For whichever design you propose, tell me: what would a positive result establish, and what would it fail to establish; what would a negative result establish, and what would it fail to establish; and which part of the strong thesis, if any, no operational test could reach, and why that is so—because the claim is ill-posed, because operationalization loses the very thing at issue, or for some other reason.
