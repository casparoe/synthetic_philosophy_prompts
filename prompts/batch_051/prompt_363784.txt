I'm taking a course this term on the psychology and anthropology of moral judgment, and we just finished a unit on what the professor called German vs. American folk morality. The way she set it up: the German folk moralist treats rule-following and order as moral goods in their own right — you stop at the empty crosswalk at 2 a.m., you don't jump the turnstile even when nobody's checking, and breaking a rule is itself a small sin regardless of consequences. The American folk moralist treats individual conscience as the final court of appeal — rules have value only instrumentally, and the person who ignores a stupid "keep off the grass" sign is, at worst, mildly annoying, and maybe even doing something healthy by refusing to defer to arbitrary authority. Both are caricatures, obviously, but the course made a pretty strong case that they track a real cultural difference and that each has systematic merits and vices: the German side gives you coordination, predictability, and humility about your own judgment, but it can curdle into blind obedience; the American side gives you flexibility and moral progress and the whole tradition of civil disobedience, but it can curdle into self-serving rationalization where "my conscience" just means "what I feel like doing."

Here's what I want to argue with my roommate about. I put my credence at about 0.35 that when a person's conscience honestly conflicts with a standing rule of a roughly legitimate institution, the rule should win by default — that is, the burden of proof should sit with the person wanting to break the rule. My roommate is around 0.7. I don't just want your number, I want the derivation: assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions — (a) that accumulated rules encode more moral information than an individual's snapshot judgment, (b) that conscience can in practice be distinguished from self-serving rationalization, (c) that "better default" even has a determinate sense across different cultural contexts, and whatever other term's you think belong — and tell me how you combined them. Then tell me which sub-question the gap between 0.35 and 0.7 most likely lives in, and what evidence would move you the most.
