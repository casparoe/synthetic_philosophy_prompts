Ive been trying to turn Goodhart's law into something with actual dynamics instead of just a one-liner, and I want the idea pressure-tested. I call it "Goodhart hysteresis." Hysteresis is when a system doesn't return to its original state once the cause is removed. My thought is that a metric under target pressure goes through two phases: validity collapse while the pressure is on, then a recovery phase after the pressure is off. Some measures snap back once nobody's gaming them; others stay broken because the gaming changed the underlying activity rather than just the reporting. Charles Goodhart's original claim, in "Problems of Monetary Management: The UK Experience" (1975), was: "Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes." That makes collapse sound like an end state. I think permanence is a separate question from the collapse, and you can often predict permanence by asking whether the gaming worked through the world or through the report. For example, a hospital that games wait-time data by redefining "wait time" gets a broken measure that might recover if you audit; a hospital that actually discharges people early to hit the number has changed the system, and the old measure may never mean the same thing again.
