ok so my philosophy club has been doing this unit on consciousness and we spent alot of time on epiphenomenalism, the view that the felt part of an experience (the pain, the redness, whatever) is caused by your brain but doesn't cause anything back — it just kind of rides along like steam from a train whistle, my friend keeps calling it the steam whistle theory and now I cant think of it any other way — while all the wincing and saying "ow" is done by the physical stuff. for our last meeting I want to bring a poem instead of notes, like a send-off for the unit. can you write it? I was thinking a villanelle, the 19-line thing with two lines that keep coming back, because the repeats could act out the weird self-undermining part of the view. one refrain should be the epiphenomenalist's claim that the feeling is causally inert, and the other refrain should be the thing we cant stop judging anyway: that the feeling is exactly what made me say "ow" or "it hurts." the poem should make those two voices keep passing each other, and when the refrains finally land next to each other in the last two lines it should make the reader feel the paradox — the theory says my conviction that pain made me speak is just my brain chemistry talking, but then that also has to apply to my conviction that epiphenomenalism is true, which then has nothing to do with how things actually feel. don't resolve it or make it a lecture, just sit in the tension. also try not to make the rhymes so neat they sound like a nursery rhyme. it would be really good if someone who knows the position could tell the two refrains apart and go "yeah, that's actually the problem," while someone who's never heard of it still gets a real poem.
