I'm a backend engineer on a team that spends an embarrassing amount of time arguing about what our model's probability scores actually mean. One of our data scientists, Dana, has become the unofficial referee for any sentence that starts with "the probability that," and over the past year I've kept a running list of her calls. The reason I kept the list is that they seam inconsistent to me, but I can't tell whether she's running one subtle rule or just switching between rules when it's convenient.

The list so far: a fair coin flipped and covered before anyone looks, where she said the probability of heads is 50/50 but only as a summary of our ignorance, since the coin is already definitely heads or tails, so the 50% isn't a fact about the coin itself. A lottery ticket, where she said the one-in-fourteen-million chance is fine but really shorthand for how often tickets of that sort win across draws, and an individual ticket doesn't carry its own probability. The defect rate on a stable assembly line, where she said the 2% figure is nothing more or less than the long-run frequency in the population of parts, and it would be a mistake to read it as a hidden tendency of any single part. And a radium atom's chance of decaying within one half-life, where she said that case is different: the individual atom has its own objective tendency to decay, a genuine 50/50 built into the physics, and it's neither just our ignorance nor a population-average frequency.

Next month we're launching a brand-new service, the first thing of its kind we've ever built, with no comparable deployments in the company and no stable class we could average over. Management wants to attach a "probability that the launch goes smoothly" to it, and Dana will have to sign off on whatever we put forward. What's your best guess about what she'll say? I'm less interested in the exact number than in the rule she's running: is she a frequentist, a degree-of-belief person, a propensity person, or some mix, and which of her earlier calls should I treat as the closest precedent? If our list genuinely underdetermines what she'd do, tell me which single extra case would best split the difference and force her to commit.
