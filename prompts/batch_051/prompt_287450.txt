I write about psychology and the courts for a general-audience magazine, and I keep being struck by a resemblance I cannot decide about. On the experimental-philosophy side there is the Knobe effect: when a chairman is described as harming the environment as a foreseen side effect of a profit-making project and as not caring about that harm, most respondents say he harmed the environment intentionally; when the same vignette is changed so that the side effect helps the environment and he does not care about that either, far fewer say he helped intentionally. Foresight is held constant, the valence of the side effect is flipped, and the attribution of intentionality reverses. On the legal side is the doctrine of oblique intention: a jury is entitled to find that a defendant intended a consequence if it was a virtual certainty of their act and they realised this, yet in practice the doctrine is almost always invoked for deaths and grave harms rather than benign outcomes. The two look structurally identical to me — in each case the gravity of a foreseen side effect seems to license calling it intended — and I cannot tell whether that is a deep point about intention judgments or merely the word "intentionally" doing different work in a laboratory questionnaire and a jury direction.

Please tell me whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Spell out the mapping and where it is strained; say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer; and identify something the parallel predicts that turns out false, for example that the folk asymmetry should show up in judges' directions or that the law's virtual-certainty standard should track the experimental findings. I would also like a view on whether either field would gain anything from the other if the parallel held. Structure the reply as exactly five numbered items, each no more than a couple of sentences, with no preamble or summing-up; after the five items, add a short note saying how confident you are in the overall verdict and singling out the point you are least sure about. Please write in British English.
