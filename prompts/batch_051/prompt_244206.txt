A friend and I argued most of last night about whether leaning on a language model for intellectual work—summarizing a field, diagnosing a bug, drafting an argument—degrades one's understanding of the domain. His position, which he returned to in several forms, was that this is no different in kind from epistemic dependence on colleagues or on calculators; since we have always relied on other minds and on instruments, any worry that AI specifically erodes understanding is either special pleading or nostalgia. Yet he slid freely between the colleague analogy and the calculator analogy as though they were interchangeable, and I suspect they are doing different work and that one or both break precisely where understanding is at issue. I could not make the disanalogy precise in the moment. Please audit the analogy, or the two analogies: what corresponds to what across the cases of consulting a colleague, using a calculator, and delegating to a language model; where each mapping breaks; what each quietly assumes about the user's residual ability to verify, correct, and reconstruct the result; and whether my friend's conclusion—that no new form of epistemic erosion is occurring—survives once the disanalogies are separated.
