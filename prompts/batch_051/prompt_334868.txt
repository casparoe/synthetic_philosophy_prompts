Davidson argued that radical interpretation — assigning meanings to a speaker's sentences and contents to their beliefs on the basis of nothing but which sentences they hold true under which observed circumstances — can get off the ground only if the interpreter assumes that the speaker is mostly rational and that most of their beliefs are true by the interpreter's own lights. Now suppose a system built with no such assumption reaches the same surface competence: a large language model trained only to predict text, which produces contextually appropriate responses to novel utterances, supplies accurate paraphrases, and can say what a speaker meant, without attributing beliefs, assigning meanings, or presupposing shared rationality. Does Davidson's argument survive this development? In particular, does the model's success show that the charitable assumption is unnecessary for interpretation, or does it show only that this prediction-trained performance is not interpretation in Davidson's sense?
