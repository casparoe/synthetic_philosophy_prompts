Some friends and I have been stuck for weeks on Schrödinger's late-life Vedanta phase and the claim, roughly, that consciousness is one — that the apparent plurality of minds is the illusion, and individual subjects are like subdivisions of a single underlying subject. One camp in our group thinks this is basically right, or at least a serious metaphysical option. The other camp thinks it's romantic nonsense: consciousness is a biological property of particular organisms, and the number of conscious subjects is exactly the number of appropriately organized living things, give or take. We want to run this as a proper adversarial collaboration rather than another year of talking past each other. Please write the joint statement we should produce. I want: a numbered list of claims both camps can actually sign, stated non-vacuously; a disagreements table with each side's view worded so it would accept the wording for its own side; the cruxes where settling the sub-question would actually move at least one of them; and for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute is purely verbal, say which part and why — but don't let that be a cheap way out if there's a substantive disagreement underneath. Also, if the crux turns on something like the individuation of subjects, the binding problem, split-brain cases, or what makes two experiences belong to the same subject, spell out exactly how each camp treats it.
