Put yourself in the mindset of an intellectual historian who knows Darwin’s letters, notebooks, and published work inside out. I’ve been going down a rabbit hole on memetics—Dawkins’s idea in The Selfish Gene that bits of culture are replicators that undergo natural selection, and the modern version where internet memes are said to mutate, spread, get selected, and go extinct. I want you to predict, as carefully as you can, what Charles Darwin himself would have made of the claim that internet memes are Darwinian replicators evolving by natural selection. He died in 1882, so he never saw any of this, but he did think about language change and about whether the struggle for existence applied to things other than organisms—I think in Descent of Man he basically says words compete and the ones that survive do so by a kind of natural selection. First brainstorm the relevant commitments in Darwin’s actual theory, not just the slogan survival of the fittest: heritable variation, differential reproduction, gradualism, blending, whether he needed discrete units or was happy with continuous variation, and why he kept bringing up artificial selection. Then, from those commitments, argue to your best guess about where he’d land: would he treat internet memes as a cute analogy, a literal extension of his theory to a new inheritance system, or a category mistake? Where would he be most excited and where most skeptical—say, intentional human design, blending, the high mutation rate, or the lack of a clean line between a meme and it’s environment? I know we can’t know this for sure, so don’t give me a hedging essay about that; I want your best argued guess. Keep the whole answer under 350 words.
