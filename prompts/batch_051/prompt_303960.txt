I sit on the city council of a small municipality. Our code still contains an ordinance from 1931 making it a misdemeanor to operate any commercial business before noon on Sunday; it has not been enforced since the late 1960s, and the police chief confirms that no current officer has ever written a ticket under it. A newly elected member wants to begin enforcing it, starting with three large stores that now open on Sunday mornings, arguing that a law on the books is law and that selective non-enforcement is exactly what the rule of law exists to prevent. Another member wants us to repeal it quietly, arguing that reviving a rule after sixty years of official inactivity would punish people who had every reason to believe their conduct was lawful, and that a legal system that can do that is arbitrary rather than lawful. I have been reading about the idea of desuetude — that laws can lapse through long, settled non-enforcement — and I cannot tell whether it is a safety valve for the rule of law or a slow corruption of it. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before our vote: recommend one of the options (enforce, repeal, or leave on the books with conditions and a time frame), give the strongest point on the losing side, and say what our choice would imply about whether an unenforced law is still law. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary.
