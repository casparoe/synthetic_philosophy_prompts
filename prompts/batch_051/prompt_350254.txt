I am drafting a novel in which a physicist, over the course of a single evening, becomes convinced that her decision to leave her marriage the following week is already fixed, and the rest of the book follows her attempt to live inside that conviction. I want the scene in which a colleague lays out the argument to be rigorous enough that a reader who knows relativity cannot dismiss it as caricature, but I also need to know where the argument is genuinely vulnerable, because a later character will push back. As I have written it, the argument runs as follows.

(1) In special relativity, simultaneity is frame-dependent: for any two spacelike-separated events, there is an inertial frame in which they are simultaneous.
(2) If two events are simultaneous in some inertial frame, then they have the same ontological status: if one is real, the other is equally real.
(3) The physicist at her desk on Monday evening — call this event A — is real.
(4) Let E be the event of her signing the divorce papers the following Monday. If E is spacelike separated from A, then by (1) some inertial frame makes A and E simultaneous, and by (2) E is real. If E lies in A's future light cone, choose an intermediate event E′ that is spacelike separated from both A and E (such an intermediate event always exists in Minkowski spacetime); then A and E′ are simultaneous in some frame, so E′ is real, and E′ and E are simultaneous in some frame, so E is real. Either way, E is already real on Monday evening.
(5) The same construction works for any event in her future, so her entire future is already real.
(6) If her future decisions are already real, they are fixed; the sense that she could have done otherwise is an illusion.

The colleague calls this a version of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument and says it forces the block universe. I would most like to keep step (1), which is just textbook relativity, and I have no independent attachment to the others. Please diagnose the argument: which step should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost? Also tell me the strongest reading on which the argument is valid and its conclusion should be accepted, and what that reading costs.
