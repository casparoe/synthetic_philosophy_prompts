I'm writing the rules engine for a small online game in the Nomic family — one where changing the rules is itself a legal move, and the altered rules then govern later moves. I keep tripping over one edge case and would like it worked through carefully.

Take the canonical self-reference conflict in a minimal form. A rule-set contains exactly two rules:

Rule 1. Any proposed rule change that receives a two-thirds vote is adopted and takes effect immediately.
Rule 2. Rule 2 may not be amended, repealed, superseded, or overridden by any other rule.

A player proposes, under Rule 1, the change: Rule 2 is repealed. Two-thirds vote yes. The standard puzzle is that at the instant the vote is counted, Rule 1 authorizes the change and Rule 2 forbids it, and both rules are in force; the usual analyses divide over which rule wins, or whether the system is simply inconsistent.

Now vary the mechanics by replacing Rule 1's immediate-effect clause. A successful two-thirds vote does not alter the rules immediately; it instead creates a pending change Q, which the engine validates and applies only at the next tick. Consider two validation policies:

PRE: validate Q against the rule-set as it exists just before the tick.
POST: validate Q against the rule-set that would result from applying Q, together with any other pending changes, at that tick.

Apply both policies to the proposal Q = Rule 2 is repealed.

Work through how the original analysis changes under the two policies. Specifically:
(a) Under PRE, is Q valid or invalid, and which side of the original divide does that amount to endorsing?
(b) Under POST, is Q valid? Is Q self-validating, in the sense that it is evaluated against a state that exists only if Q has already succeeded? Is the resulting fixed point coherent, or viciously circular?
(c) Which policy is the more defensible account of when a rule counts as "in force" for purposes of deciding a challenge to itself? And does the losing policy eliminate the paradox or merely relocate it — if it relocates it, to what point in the machinery?

Treat this spec as authoritative, and don't import assumptions from real Nomic rule-sets or legal doctrine. Hard limit: 600 words.
