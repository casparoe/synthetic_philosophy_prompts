Had a long argument with a friend about this last night and I cant shake it. I ended up defending something like the self-indication assumption, while he kept saying it just is the presumptuous philosopher move and therefore must go wrong somewhere. Here's the version I kept pushing:

(1) Finding yourself awake and existing is an observation, like any other.
(2) If two theories start out equally plausible, and one makes an observation more likely than the other does, the one that predicted it better is better supported.
(3) Compared to an otherwise identical theory that predicts exactly one observer, a theory that predicts 100 billion observers makes the observation "I exist" 100 billion times more likely.
(4) So if two otherwise indistinguishable cosmological theories differ only in how many observers they predict, the observer-rich theory is overwhelmingly better supported by your existence.
(5) Therefore you should be almost certain of the observer-rich theory even with no separate empirical evidence distinguishing them, which is exactly the presumptuous philosopher conclusion.

I think the argument goes through and my friend is just squeamish about where it leads. Is it valid, and if not, which step fails and on what reading? If it is valid, do we really have to accept the presumptuous philosopher?
