I've been arguing with a friend about whether 'acausal threats' are a real thing or just confused. You know the setup: someone who can't touch you, and can't touch anything you value through any normal channel, announces a policy like 'if I predict you won't cooperate, I'll make a copy of your brain and torture the copy' or 'I'll torch the paperclip factory in the nearby branch of the multiverse.' Should that move you? My gut says no: if there's no causal path from their threat to your actual welfare, a threat is just noise. But I also have this nagging feeling that once you care about your copies, simulations, or successors as surrogates, some versions of the thing do sneak in a reason.

Here's what I want. Build me a decision problem where an acausal threat against a surrogate goal — not against the agent, but against something the agent treats as a stand-in for itself or its values — rationally changes the agent's behavior by light of at least one major decision theory, while causal decision theory says the threat is irrelevant. Constraints: the threatener's prediction is no fancier than a very good model built from the agent's public behavior (no omniscient predictors, no Newcomb's demon, no exotic physics unless the case explicitly needs it and says why). Payoffs have to be ordinary: money, career, reputation, a cause the agent supports. The surrogate goal has to be specified concretely: who or what is threatened, why the agent treats it as a surrogate rather than just caring about the thing directly, and what that distinction is doing in the case. Keep the core scenario to one paragraph, then take as much space as you need for the rest.

After the scenario, walk through what causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and functional or updateless decision theory each says about the choice, and pinpoint the structural feature — correlation without causation, prediction of your decision, precommitment — that does the work. Then tell me: if you remove the surrogate part and make the threat target the agent's own future welfare, still through no causal channel, does the case collapse or stay live? And how would a causal decision theorist try to defuse it? I want a honest note on whether the case actually works or whether it's just smuggling in a Newcomb problem in a scary hat.
