I sit on the board of a small Catholic foundation in Germany that runs a hospice and a nursing home, and we have to decide a personnel case on Thursday; I've been asked to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting. The employee, Frau S., has worked as a palliative nurse for fourteen years and is nominally Catholic. Until recently she paid church tax like most registered Catholics here: an eight or nine percent surcharge on her income tax, depending on the Land, collected by the state and passed to the diocese. Six months ago she went to the registry office and made a civil declaration of withdrawal from the Church, the Kirchenaustritt, which is the only way to stop the tax. She says she did it purely for the money, still considers herself culturally Catholic, still comes to Mass now and then, and was genuinly surprised it put her job at risk. Our staff code incorporates the Church's loyalty rules, under which a formal withdrawal is listed as a serious breach, and in some roles the law lets a church employer dismiss on that ground; her contract has the usual clause. She is not a chaplain or religion teacher, but she does lead the short bedside prayer when a resident has died, and bereaved families know her as the church's person in the building.

The board's options are, roughly: dismiss her, keep her in her current post, or keep her but move her away from the pastoral parts of the job, on conditions.

Draft the opinion I'd circulate on Thursday. Make it a clear recommendation with a concrete course of action and a time frame, and take the strongest argument for dismissal seriously rather than just restating the law. I don't want a survey of German church-labor case law; take the legal background as I've sketched it and do the moral and institutional reasoning. Toward the end, tell me what would change your mind — what factual claim about her motives or her role would most push you the other way, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail. And please keep it continuous prose like a letter: no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables.
