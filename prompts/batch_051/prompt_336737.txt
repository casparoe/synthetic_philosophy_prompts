For a seminar on Wilfrid Sellars I'm writting a short paper trying to bring his attack on the myth of the given to bear on contemporary deep learning. The set-up I have in mind is this: a genuinely multimodal model is trained from scratch on raw video, audio and text, with no hand-labelled data and no human-curated concepts. After training it can reliably report that a particular object is in front of it and moving to the left, and its designers say it has acquired empirical knowledge directly from the sensory stream, without any conceptual scheme being supplied in advance. Sellars was writing well before any of this was possible, so the whole thing would have to be an extrapolation.

Here's the specific thing I'm stuck on. Would Sellars regard the low-level processing of pixels and waveforms in such a model as playing the role of the given in its knowledge? And would he actually grant that the model has observational knowledge in the way a human does when they see that something is red? I'd like you to work through his actual distinctions — especially the difference between the space of causes and the space of reasons, and between sensing and seeing that — rather than just saying he'd reject the myth of the given. If there is a point where the analogy between Sellars's sense-impressions and a neural network's early layers breaks down, say where, and whether that breakdown matters. Please answer in British English.
