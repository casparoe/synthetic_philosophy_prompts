I keep trying to work out what John Dewey would have said about the way platform companies now run the biggest social experiments in history without anyone calling them that. Say a platform A/B tests six versions of its ranking algorithm, measures time-on-site and ad clicks, and silently ships the winner to a few billion people. Is that continuous with Dewey's experimentalism, or a parody of it? I can see the continuity: it treats policies as revisable hypotheses and learns from experience. But Dewey's whole point was that inquiry has to be public, problem-driven, and self-correcting, with the people affected able to contest the framing of the problem and the interpretation of the result. None of that happens in a feed experiment. If you had to predict his actual position, where would he land, and what would he have said should be done? Is there a way to run platform experiments that his view would actually sanction, and what institutional machinery does that require—citizen review of experiment designs, public registries of active tests, independent reanalysis, somethign else?
