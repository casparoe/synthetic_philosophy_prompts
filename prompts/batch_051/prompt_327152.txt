I just read David Lewis's book on possible worlds, and I think I understand the broad view: when we say something could have happened, Lewis says there is a concrete world, every bit as real as this one and not a mere description or abstract object, in which it does happen. Last weekend my nephew, trying to save my wife from a dinner lecture, summarized it as follows: "Lewis believes in infinitely many parallel physical universes. They exist in the same sense our universe exists. So when you say you could have been a plumber, there really is another you out there who is a plumber, living in one of those universes. Since that is obviously crazy, the whole thing is just a professional philosopher's game with no relevance to anything." Something in that summary strikes me as unfair, but I'm new enough to this that I can't say exactly where; my only instinct is that a view that has survived this long probably can't be killed by the word "crazy." Please go through the nephew's summary sentence by sentence and tell me what Lewis would accept, what he would object to, and what is simply a distortion; then say wether the conclusion (that it's crazy and not worth taking seriously) follows from the parts that survive correction.
