Write a satirical internal memorandum of roughly 600 to 800 words, from the Research Programme Audit Committee of a fictional university that has made Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes its official evaluation framework. The subject is a research group, which you may name, whose hard core has survived an accumulating list of anomalies only because the group keeps adjusting its protective belt; it now accommodates recalcitrant data after the fact and has produced no novel predictions in several funding cycles. The memo should have the deadpan voice and format of a university administrative document, with headings, a metrics summary, and recommendations, and the comedy should come from applying the Lakatosian apparatus with straight-faced precision. A specialist in philosophy of science should recognize the satire as fair rather than a caricature of the terminology, so get the machinery right: distinguish the hard core from the protective belt; invoke the negative heuristic, which directs modus tollens at the belt rather than the core, and note the neglect of the positive heuristic; classify the programme's trajectory as a degenerating problem-shift on Lakatos's criterion that progressive changes predict novel facts while degenerating ones merely accommodate anomalies; and, if useful, charge the group with monster-barring in the sense of redefining its terms to exclude counterexamples rather than meeting them. The memo should also betray an irony internal to Lakatos's own position: a degenerating programme is not, on his view, to be abandoned just for degenerating, but only when a progressive rival is available, so the committee's harsh metrics sit uneasily with the theory it claims to operate. A reader should finish the memo with a clear sense of why a programme that can retrospectively explain everything may, by Lakatos's standards, be predicting nothing.
