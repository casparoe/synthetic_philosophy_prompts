For the parody issue of a rationalist magazine: write a deadpan peer-review report for a comparative-cognition journal. The submission under review is an empirical paper arguing that domestic dogs show genuine guilt — the familiar guilty look — rather than a conditioned appeasement response. The reviewer should be a hardline defender of Morgan's Canon, understood roughly as the rule that animal behavior must always be explained by the lowest psychological capacity consistent with the evidence. Structure the report with the usual sections (Recommendation, Summary, Major Concerns, Minor Concerns) and keep it under 650 words. The humor should come from the machinery of the position rather than broad anti-academic jokes: the difference between methodological parsimony and an empirical bet about animal minds; the way low-level gets treated as automatically more parsimonious even when the lower-level story — stimulus histories, intermittent reinforcement, subtle owner cues, Clever Hans effects — becomes at least as byzantine as the folk-psychological story it replaces; and the ease with which any observation pointing toward a higher capacity is re-described rather than tested. The reviewed authors may be imperfect researchers — the study can lean on owner reports and a handful of home videos, so the reviewer should land some real methodological points — but by the end a knowledgeable reader should see that the canon, applied this way, is unfalsifiable in the other direction and that the piece is a fair satire of a real stance, not a straw man.
