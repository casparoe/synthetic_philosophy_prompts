For the past month I've been writing my research centre's annual self-assessment for the funding council, and I can no longer un-see that the genre is essentially Lakatos's Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes played straight: we list a hard core we are not permitted to touch, file every failed result under anomalies contained by the protective belt, and then grade the programme as progressive on the strength of novel predictions we have not yet tested. I would like a piece that satirises this.

Please write a deadpan parody of an institutional research-programme self-assessment (around 900 words) in which a fictional research centre uses Lakatos's apparatus with total candour to defend a programme that is plainly degenerating. The centre's subject matter is up to you, but keep it generic enough that the satire cuts at the form rather than at any one field. The humour must come from the accurate deployment of the concepts: hard core, protective belt, negative and positive heuristic, novel predictions, and progressive versus degenerating problemshift. A reader who does not know Lakatos should still see that the centre is treating unfalsifiability as a feature, while a reader who does know Lakatos should recognise each term being used, and abused, correctly.

Keep the voice bureaucratic, complacent, and faintly defensive, like a real progress report that is slightly too honest. No winking, no authorial commentary outside the voice, and never let the report admit outright that the programme is empty; the reader should draw that conclusion.

Structure it as numbered sections, with rough lengths:

1. Mission and Hard Core (~120 words) — the commitments that are treated as non-negotiable and never tested.
2. Anomalies Accrued in the Reporting Period (~200 words) — failed predictions and awkward findings, each described as recalcitrant but peripheral.
3. Protective-Belt Adjustments and Novel Predictions (~250 words) — a new auxiliary hypothesis that preserves the hard core and yields one impressive-sounding but conveniently distant prediction.
4. Assessment of the Problem-Shift (~180 words) — the centre's self-graded verdict that the programme remains progressive.
5. Resource Request (~150 words) — a request for further funding to test the novel prediction, with a hint that new anomalies are already expected.
