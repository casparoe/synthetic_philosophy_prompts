I've gone back and forth on this more times then I'd like, and the position I keep ending up at is something like the propensity interpretation of fitness: an organism's fitness is not the number of offspring it actually leaves, but a probabilistic disposition—roughly its expected contribution to the next generation given its traits and its environment. Actual reproductive output is fallible evidence for the disposition, not constitutive of it. I hold this mainly because it seems to defuse the old charge that natural selection is vacuous: if fit just means whatever ends up surviving and reproducing, then saying selection favors the fitter is an empty restatement. The propensity view feels like it buys selection real content, the same way a coin can be fair even if it happens to land heads five times in a row.

But I've noticed I mostly use this view as a shield rather than a worked-out theory. When someone in a discussion trots out the tautology objection, I say fitness is a propensity, and then move on, without being able to say how you would measure a fitness propensity independently of the survival and reproduction numbers you actually observe. And when someone points at a case where the fitter type just loses—small population, drift, a storm killing the well-adapted individuals—I wave it off by saying outcomes can diverge from propensities. Lately I've had the sinking feeling that I've built a position that can't be wrong, or that collapses back into the tautology the moment I try to cash it out.

So my question is: what would count against the propensity interpretation of fitness? I want the cases, arguments, and empirical or theoretical findings that should move me, sorted by how decisive they are, including the ones a committed defender of the view would themselves admit are trouble rather than easily dismissed. And I'd like an honest assessment of whether my version—with all the waving-off of mismatch cases—has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a serious problem for a claim of this kind or just the usual cost of doing metaphysics.
