Longtime reader of rationalist blogs here. I've got a specific rule for when people bring up Nietzsche in an otherwise ordinary moral argument, and I want to know whether it's a defensable updating policy or just a tic I've dressed up in LessWrong jargon. The rule: if someone offers a genealogical story to debunk a moral value — guilt is just internalized cruelty from Nietzsche's Genealogy, or compassion is a slave-morality leftover — I require them to either apply the same debunking method to the values they approve of (honesty, autonomy, courage, whatever) or give me a principled reason why the method works in the one case and not the other. If they can't, I stop updating on anything else they say about the topic. Concretely: my friend keeps arguing that Christian morality corrupted our concept of pity, while treating his own preference for strength and self-overcoming as if its not even up for examination. I call that a symmetry violation and now I won't engage with his moral points at all. Is this defensible? Is symmetry the right test for when a genealogy actually undermines a belief, or are there good asymmetric cases — say, a genealogy that exposes a specific process as unreliable — where I should update anyway? And is the stop-engaging part doing real epistemic work, or is it just social punishment?
