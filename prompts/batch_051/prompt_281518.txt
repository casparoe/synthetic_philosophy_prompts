I need a working defintion of a strike — the labor kind, not baseball — that gets a bunch of hard cases right. The cases:

- a factory walkout by union members after the contract expires
- a lockout, where the employer shuts the plant to force terms on the union
- teachers working strictly to contract and refusing all unpaid after-school clubs (work-to-rule)
- nurses calling in sick en masse on the same morning, with no formal strike vote
- a wildcat walkout that the national union refuses to authorize
- dockworkers refusing to handle cargo in support of a different union's dispute
- a slowdown at an auto plant, where workers stay on the line but cut output on purpose
- a rent strike by tenants over the condition of the building

I'm confident the first is a strike and the lockout isn't, and I go back and forth on the sick-out and the work-to-rule. Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and say which verdicts you'd defend even if they sound counterintuitive and which ones make you want to revise the definition. Also: I suspect the word does two jobs, one descriptive and one about legitimately exercising collective power. If you agree, give me both senses, say which cases fall under each, and say which cases only one of the senses really fits. And say a bit about whether a good definition should track what the law protects, or whether that's a separate question.
