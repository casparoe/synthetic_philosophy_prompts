I am a PhD student in behavioral ecology, and a dispute in my lab has made me doubt whether the handicap principle is a testable causal claim at all. One side states the principle this way: costly ornaments such as the peacock's train remain honest because their cost makes them unprofitable for low-quality males to produce, so the cost itself is what keeps the signal reliable. The other side replies that honest and cost are doing too much conceptual work, and that the principle may be true by definition unless we can separate it from at least two alternatives: an index, in which the signal is physically constrained by the advertised trait and reliability comes from that constraint rather than from waste, and a differential-benefit mechanism, in which the signal is cheap for everyone but low-quality senders gain less from producing it, so it remains informative without a costly handicap.

I would like you to tell me what, if anything, observations could settle here.
