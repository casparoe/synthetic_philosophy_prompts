Not a philosopher, just been chewing on this on my own. You know that puzzle where a stranger asks you for $20 and says he'll pay you back some unimaginably huge but finite amount tomorrow, and the odds are so tiny that calling it one in a trillion is generous? Expected value says hand over the money, but nobody in their right mind would. Here's where I've landed:

(a) When the numbers are real, expected value is the right way to make decisions — take a positive-expected-value bet if you can afford the stake.
(b) You shouldn't pay the mugger.
(c) That isn't just because he's probably lying. Even if I somehow knew he was telling the truth, I still wouldnt pay.
(d) I do pay for insurance against rare disasters, and I think that's rational.
(e) The real difference, I think, is that with insurance the probability is something I can actually get a handle on, and with the mugger it's below anything I can meaningfully estimate, so expected value is being asked to work with numbers I can't actually have.
(f) I also think a decision shouldn't change just because you describe the same situation in different words, so I can't just say "the offer is absurd" and leave it there.
