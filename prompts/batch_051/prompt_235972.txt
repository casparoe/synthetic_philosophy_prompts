Three months ago I got elected to our national parliament's EU scrutiny commitee, and I ran on a eurosceptic platform — take back control, too much power in Brussels, the usual. Now I actually sit on the body that has to do this for real, and I need to write an opinion I can defend in front of people who voted for me.

Here's the case in front of us. The Commission is pushing the Council to use one of the passerelle clauses to move corporate tax policy from unanimity to qualified majority voting, which would mean no single member state can veto tax measures anymore. The decision itself is taken by the European Council by unanimity, but here's the twist: national parliaments each get to kill it — if we object within six months the whole thing is off — and my committee is suposed to recommend whether our parliament should object. The committee is split. One side says we should object because tax is the core of national sovereignty, the veto is the only thing stopping a small country from getting outvoted by the bigger ones, and integration only ever ratchets one direction — once you give the veto up you'll never get it back. The other side says the passerelle veto we'd be exercising is itself part of the democratic deficit people like me campaigned against: it lets one national parliament block a move 26 others have agreed on, and that isn't more democratic just because the blockage happens at national level. They also point out that if the move went through, the European Parliament would become a proper co-legislator on tax, which it isn't now, so there's actually a democratic gain on the other side of the ledger. The federalists on the committee add that this is what all the old ever closer union language in the treaties was gesturing at — a union that can actually decide things rather than a club of vetoes.

Draft the committee's memo: a definite recommendation — object, refrain from objecting, or refrain only if the government secures specific conditions — plus the reasoning. Engage the strongest thing the losing side has going for it, don't strawman it. And don't give me a menu of options; I have to land somewhere and defend it at the next plenary.
