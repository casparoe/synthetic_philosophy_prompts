Two people in my philosophy reading group got into it last week and I still can't sort out who won. Maya was defending the Best System view of laws — roughly, laws of nature are just the axioms of the simplest, strongest true summary of all the particular facts there are. Dan kept saying this makes the universe's regularity a giant coincidence. He gave a concrete case: a sealed lab where every sample of a newly synthesized element ever tested has conducted electricity. He said Maya has to say it's just an accident of this sample that every tested one conducted, not that a new sample will conduct. She said no: the best system will very likely include "all samples of this element conduct," and the counterfactual "a new sample would conduct" is just what that system says holds in close possible worlds. He came back that this is circular — she's using a summary of the instances to explain the very instances that make the summary true, and that explanation needs a separate governing layer. She answered that his "nomic necessitation" is just a label for the regularity plus a shiver, and the summary is the explanation. He said that gets the direction of explanation backwards: the law should be why this sample conducted, where as on her view this sample is part of why the law holds. She said her view and his are empirically indistinguishable — we come to know laws through the same regularities either way — so he's paying metaphysical rent for no predictive gain. He said empirical equivalence isn't the same as theoretical equivalence, and asked how she draws the law/accident line at all. She said it's membership in the best system; he said that makes lawhood depend on our standards of simplicity, which aren't themselves in the mosaic of particular facts, so she's sneaking in just as much non-empirical machinery. Then she accused him of not being able to say what his "governing" relation is, and he accused her of not being able to say why the mosaic is regular in the first place. Neither would budge.

Please help me adjudicate. Act as a careful philosopher of science who works on the metaphysics of laws. First list each substantive move each person made, then go through them one by one: who is right about the circularity charge, the direction-of-explanation point, the empirical equivalence point, the simplicity worry, and the law/accident distinction? Then say who is more right overall, and what, if anything, both are missing. At the very end, write a final line of the form "Verdict: <short phrase>" giving your overall judgment.
