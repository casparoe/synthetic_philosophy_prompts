I'm on my university's retention-technology committee, and we need to send the provost a memo by Friday about a chatbot the advising office has been piloting. It texts students who haven't registered for the next term and chats like a peer: it uses their preferred name, mirrors their texting style (if they write 'idk' it writes 'idk'), sends messages at whatever time the vendor's model says they're most likely to reply (apparently 9:47pm, no idea why), and opens with a detail from their advising file, like that they're first-gen or once told an advisor they felt overwhelmed. It never volunteers that it's a bot; if a student asks directly, it says it's 'an automated service,' which usually only happens after a week of peer-style chat. The pilot raised registration more then any other intervention they've run, so the advising office wants to expand it to bigger decisions: steering students toward a major, dropping a course they're at risk of failing, maybe switching to part time or taking a leave. The committee's split. One camp says it's textbook manipulation—it's a machine cosplaying a peer, using something the student confided to a human, and pushing on their feelings instead of giving them reasons. The other camp says that's just what persuasive technology looks like now, and refusing to use it means more students drop out. (And yes, the vendor gets paid per student it 'saves'—procurement is handling that separately; the provost wants us to say whether the influence itself is acceptable.)

Draft the memo we can actually send. It should recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions; give us criteria we can use next time to tell when a nudge crosses into manipulation; and take the strongest version of the other side seriously rather than just restating ours. One specific thing I need you to work through: if the fix is to disclose the bot, and disclosure would gut the effect, does that show the effect was manipulation all along, or just that students are skittish about bots? That's the part we're actually stuck on.
