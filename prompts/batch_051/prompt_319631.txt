I keep going back and forth on whether the standard attention economy design stack—infinite scroll, algorithmic ranking optimized for dwell time, variable reward schedules, autoplay—is actually morally loaded in a way that goes beyond just "it's a bit trashy." Heres my exact question. What is your actual credence that these standard engagement-optimizing design patterns, when deployed at scale by major social media platforms, are morally impermissible *specifically because* they constitute manipulation rather than legtimate persuasion? I dont want a hedge or a philosophical treatise on the definition of manipulation. I want a number, and I want the reasoning decomposed.

Break it into something like the following sub-questions and assign a rough probability to each:

1. The probability that these mechanics systematically bypass or degrade a user's rational capacity in the moment of use (as opposed to merely being aesthetically compelling or convenient).
2. The probability that bypassing rational capacity in this specific way counts as manipulative in a morally objectionable sense, rather than just falling under the normal, acceptable category of persuasion, advertising, or good product design.
3. The probability that, even granting it *is* manipulative in that sense, the total welfare tradeoff (connectivity, free access, entertainment value) is negative enough that the practice is impermissible overall.
4. The probability that widespread habituation, cultural normalcy, or implicit consent (users choosing to open the app even knowing how it works) neutralizes the wrong.

Show me how those combine to produce your final credence—dont just average them, I want to see the structure of the dependence. Then tell me which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to: if I gave you strong evidence that changed your mind on that one sub-question, it would swing the overall credence the most. What kind of evidence would that be? Be specific about what it would take to move you, and say how much slack the final number has.
