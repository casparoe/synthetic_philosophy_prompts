I keep seeing a particular move in arguments, and I want to know whether it has a name, and if not, how you would carve the concept. Two cases stick with me. In one, a friend told another that if she weren't afraid of being alone, she would see that her relationship was bad for her. When she resisted—why should the fear of loneliness count as a distortion rather than a legitimate consideration?—the first friend kept revising what he meant by "seeing clearly" until it excluded exactly the thoughts he disagreed with. The idealized version of her was doing all the arguing, but the idealization itself was never defended. In the other case, a classmate in a seminar on legitimate government proposed that a law is fair just in case citizens would consent to it under ideal conditions. Asked to spell out the ideal conditions, he said the parties would be "fully informed" and "rational," and then defined those terms so that full information omitted the vivid details that make people demand harsh punishments, while rationality ruled out vengefulness by definition. Once again, the real disagreement about values was moved into the description of the idealized deliberator rather than being argued for outright.

I know this resembles talk about ideal observers and about what a person would choose under full information, but those are usually advanced as standards in their own right, and I am asking about the underlying structure: deferring a normative question to what an idealized version of the relevant agents would choose or endorse, where the result turns on how the idealization is specified, and where the specification cannot be neutral because any specification already answers part of the original question. Does this phenomenon have an established name? How does it differ from ordinary circularity, from begging the question, and from the fallacy of analyzing something under conditions that would alter the thing being analyzed? A bare label will not help me; I want the exact outlines of the phenomenon, what it is not, where the line—if there is one—lies between harmless idealizations, like ignoring exhaustion or missing information, and loaded ones, like defining rationality so that a rival view simply drops out, and how one might keep the loaded kind from masquerading as a neutral procedure.
