I've been running with a distinction I made up and want it checked, becuase it feels either too obvious to be new or too weird to be right.

Call it custodial ownership, or the vacancy model. The idea: "ownership" of a creative work is two things smushed together. There's entitlement-ownership — the legal bundle, excluding people, licensing, selling. And there's maintenance-ownership — the ongoing work of keeping the thing alive: servers up, files mirrored, bugs fixed, the contact address answered, decisions made about the manuscript. I think the second is the real thing and the first is just its shadow.

Central claim: once maintenance-ownership sits vacant long enough — author dead with no heirs, studio dissolved, maintainer's email bouncing — the entitlement should lapse too. Not "the owner can't be found so we'll make an exception," but "there's no full owner anymore, because owning a work is a role and the role is empty." The work becomes an unkept thing. The first person to resume maintenance (rehost the game, digitize the novel, patch the font) becomes a custodian, not an owner: protected against free-riders who mirror their re-release and slap ads on it, but they dont get to lock it up or sue the fan community that kept it warm. Walk away and the office is vacant again.

Concrete cases: an MMO from a folded studio with fans running a private server for a decade; a 90s shareware app whose author's domain is a dead Geocities link; a novel by a writer who died in the 60s with no identifiable heirs and a publisher long gone. On my view these are empty offices, and the server admin is doing more actual ownership than the absent rightsholder.

Is this already a named position? Adverse possession and abandonware community norms both feel adjacent, but neither says the office-holding part. And where does it break? I worry it licenses publishing someone's private letters the moment nobody answers for the estate, and it treats a corporation sitting on a catalog as a non-owner, which is either the point or a serious bug. Give me the strongest version of it, name the nearest existing ideas, and then tell me exactly where it stops being distinctive or stops being true.
