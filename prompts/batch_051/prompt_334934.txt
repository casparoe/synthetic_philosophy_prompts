Write a sonnet about corporate moral agency in which the volta is the moment the reader realizes that attributing blame to the company does not settle who should bear that blame. The octave should build the case for treating the corporation as a single agent: internal decision procedures, a compliance function, a risk appetite, a mission statement that seems to act as a corporate intention. The sestet should complicate this by having the speaker try to trace a specific harmful decision to a specific human hand, only to find it smeared across committee minutes, delegation chains, and key-person controls. The closing couplet should leave unresolved whether corporate blame is a genuine moral attribution or a practical legal fiction that protects the individuals inside. Accuracy matters: the poem should be recognizable to someone familiar with debates over corporate moral responsibility as engaging the tension between realist and individualist accounts, not merely gesturing at corporate personhood.
