I teach a graduate seminar on naturalism and next month I am moderating a recorded conversation with a philosopher of biology who defends the claim that Darwin’s chapter on instinct in *On the Origin of Species* already contains an early version of the “strange inversion” later associated with Turing: the idea that impressive, purpose-like competences can be produced by mindless processes rather than by comprehension. Her interpretive thesis is that Darwin’s caveat about the admixture of judgment or reason in instinctive behavior is not a concession to a designing intelligence but a defeasible residual claim that the logic of natural selection should lead us to explain away. The passage she takes as central is this:

“I will not attempt any definition of instinct. It would be easy to show that several distinct mental actions are commonly embraced by this term; but every one understands what is meant, when it is said that instinct impels the cuckoo to migrate and to lay her eggs in other birds’ nests. An action, which we ourselves should require experience to enable us to perform, when performed by an animal, more especially by a very young one, without any experience, and when performed by many individuals in the same way, without their knowing for what purpose it is performed, is usually said to be instinctive. But I could show that none of these characters of instinct are universal. A little dose, as Pierre Huber expresses it, of judgment or reason, often comes into play, even in animals very low in the scale of nature.”

Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual content of this reading rather than letting her restate it. Include at least one question each on whether “instinct” and “comprehension” are being treated as exclusive categories, on how much work the phrase “a little dose of judgment or reason” can bear for the anti-comprehension thesis, and on what would count as evidence that the residual judgment Darwin admits is not eliminable by natural selection. For each question, anticipate the reply she is most likely to give and provide the follow-up that would put pressure on it.
