I've been arguing with a friend about whether Reddit karma / Stack Exchange reputation scores track anything like quality, and I ended up with an argument that seems to prove too much. I want to know which step you'd reject and what it costs.

(1) A reputation score is just an aggregate of other users' evaluations of your contributions.
(2) If karma/reputation scores measured quality, then high-karma users should produce contributions that independent judges would rate as better than low-karma users, at a rate above chance.
(3) But the votes that build karma are strongly influenced by visibility, timing, existing score, and who the author is, not just by the quality of the contribution.
(4) So karma scores are not measures of quality.
(5) But the same goes for every other reputation system we have: citation counts, peer review, tenure, industry awards, even word-of-mouth. In all cases, later evaluations are conditioned by earlier visibility and social status.
(6) So no reputation system measures quality.
(7) If no reputation system measures quality, then reputation never gives us a rational basis for trusting one person over another.
(8) But we routinely and rationally rely on reputation to decide who to trust, so something has gone wrong.

Where is the first bad step? I can't tell if it's (3) sliding from 'influenced' to 'no longer measures', or (5) moving from particular reputation systems to all possible ones, or maybe (7) assuming rational trust requires a measure of quality rather than something else. If you reject one premise, what's the price?
