I retired a few years ago after a career in statistical physics, and i've started working through philosophy of mathematics on my own. Something has been nagging me, and I'd like help sorting it out.

My entire working life I used infinities as a matter of course. In statistical mechanics you take the thermodynamic limit, letting the number of particles go to infinity, to get a sharp phase transition; I did that, taught it, and never felt like I was saying anything spooky. I'd also say without hesitation that for every natural number there's a bigger one, and that the limit of 1/n as n grows without bound is 0. None of that ever bothered me.

But I also spent those same years insisting, at conferences and over beers, that mathematics doesn't show there are actual infinities in the physical world. If someone had claimed there's an actually infinite collection of things, or run the Hilbert's hotel thought experiment at me as though it described a real possibility, i'd have rolled my eyes and said they were reifying symbols. I still feel that way.

So i've apparently got two firm habits: I treat some appeals to infinity as perfectly respectable, and other appeals to what looks like the same infinity as philosophy-games. What I can't do is seperate them. The claim that there is no largest natural number and the claim that there is a completed set of all natural numbers look like the same fact stated two ways, yet I sign on to the first in the lab and balk at the second in the seminar room. Likewise the thermodynamic limit seems to quantify over an infinite system just as much as Hilbert's hotel does.

Is there a principle that justifies this asymmetry? The obvious candidate is the potential/actual infinity distinction, but I want to know whether it actually holds up under pressure, or whether it's just a name for the feeling. Give me the strongest version of that distinction, or any other principle you think does better, and test it against my cases. Then throw at it the old supertask where a lamp is switched on and off at each halving of a minute and we ask whether it's on or off at the end; I'd expect a decent principle to say something non-silly about that. And I want an honest verdict: is my balking at actual infinity defensible, or is it a professional prejudice I should drop now that I have the time to think about it?
