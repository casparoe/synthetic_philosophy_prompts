I am a novelist, and the protagonist of my current project is a translator of poetry who has become consumed by a single short sixteenth-century French love poem: tightly rhymed, strictly metered, and so dependent for its charm on sound and form that every literal prose rendering of it feels like a different, flatter object. I want to give her an intellectually serious working theory of what it means to translate a poem under formal constraint—the kind of account a practicing translator or a philosopher of language could actually defend, not just a set of workshop pieties. My question is not which existing translation is best; it is what any adequate theory of constrained verse translation must be able to do before we trust its verdicts about specific renderings.

From my reading I half-suspect the list of requirements includes things like these, though I do not know which are genuine and which are merely one school's taste. An adequate account must say which formal features—meter, rhyme, alliteration, line length, stanza shape, visual layout—carry semantic or aesthetic weight and why. It must explain the trade-off between preserving propositional sense and preserving the music when a target language will not yield both at once. It must handle the many cases where form itself is meaning: puns, grammatical gender, idioms, allusions, and effects that exist only because of the source language's particular resources. It must draw a defensible line between translation, imitation, and a new poem written after the original. And it must confront the fact that constraints, far from being mere obstacles, sometimes produce better poems than unconstrained fidelity would have. I also suspect some of these cannot all be honored together.

Please write the desiderata: the list of requirements an adequate theory of verse translation under formal constraint must meet, with the case or consideration behind each, clearly marking which are non-negotiable and which are negotiable or camp-specific, and flagging any pair that is jointly unsatisfiable. Then, briefly, state how the main approaches to verse translation—the literal or semantic, the formal-equivalent, and the recreative—measure up against the list. I want a committed assessment: make a definite choice about which requirements do the real work and which single one most separates adequate theories from merely elegant-sounding ones, and give your reasons.
