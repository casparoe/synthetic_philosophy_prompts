I keep going back and forth on Dennett's quining qualia bit, the one about the coffee tasters, and I want help thinking through what I'd actually be doing if I gave something up. Chase and Sanborn are two coffee tasters who both tell the company the coffee doesn't taste the way it used to, and both have gone off it. Chase says the taste itself changed: the coffee still has that sharpish, bitterish profile, but it's a different sharpish, bitterish profile now, and the old one was better. Sanborn says the coffee tastes exactly the same to him; what changed is him. His judgment, his standards, whatever the right word is, drifted, not the qualia. My students always find it completely obvious that these are two reports of two different things: a change in experience versus a change in attitude. I've got that intuition myself. When my own enjoyment of a food fades, I feel like I can usually tell whether the food changed on me or whether I changed my mind about the same taste.

But the quining argument has real pull on me too. As I understand it, there's no independent way to calibrate either man's report. Memory is all Chase or Sanborn has, and the same behavioral history can be retold with the change placed on the other side of the line: you can hold Chase's qualia fixed and let his standards drift, and you can hold Sanborn's standards fixed and let his qualia drift, with him misrembering the old taste. The supposedly private, directly known what-it's-like fact that was supposed to settle the case ends up doing no work, so the whole posit starts to look like a wheel that isn't doing any causal work.

I don't want a verdict telling me Dennett's right or the qualia realists are right. I want a negotiated answer. What would it take for the intuition to be the thing that gets debunked and dropped — could it be explained away as a post hoc labeling of what are really just changes in dispositions and standards? What would it take for the theory to yield instead — what would a coherent notion of qualia have to look like for there to be a determinate fact about whether Chase's qualia changed or his standards did? And is there a reading on which both survive, where the intuition tracks some real relational difference between two kinds of change without committing to private, intrinsic, authoritative qualia? Mostly I want to know how I'd tell which of those moves I'm making when I sit with the case.
