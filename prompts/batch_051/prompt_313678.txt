Consider five practices in which an adult says something to a child that is, or may be, false or misleading:

1. Raising a child to believe that Santa Claus brings presents to children who have behaved well.
2. Raising a child to believe that the tooth fairy leaves money under a child's pillow in exchange for a lost tooth.
3. Telling a child that the family dog has been sent to live on a farm, rather than disclosing that the dog has died.
4. Telling a child that if they keep making a certain face, it will stay that way.
5. Telling a child that they can become anything they want if they work hard enough.

Please rank these practices from most to least damaging to the child's trust in adult testimony, all else being equal. In your reasoning, spell out what you take trust in adult testimony to involve, and distinguish between the effect of the initial statement and the effect of the practice over time, including how and whether the truth is later disclosed or discovered.

Then state what would change your mind: identify the single argument or empirical consideration that, if it went the other way, would most alter your ranking, and say where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
