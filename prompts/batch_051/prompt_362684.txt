I am a novelist planning a book set in a fictional country emerging from a civil war. Its central institution is a truth commission that can grant amnesty to people who committed, ordered, or materially assisted political violence, on terms loosely modelled on South Africa's post-apartheid process: an applicant must give a full and truthful account of the act, the act must have had a political objective, and the commission may refuse amnesty where the act was grossly disproportionate to that objective. I want the commission's rulings to be legally and morally credible scene after scene, and I do not want to bend each ruling to the needs of the plot.

Please turn the principle behind this conditional amnesty into a reusable decision procedure that my fictional amnesty panels can be shown following in every case. Fix the constraints: three-person panels made up of a retired judge, a lay community representative, and a lawyer; at most one day of hearings per case; and review by the full commission only for procedural error. The procedure should give the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the point at which the procedure stops with a refusal, and the points at which it must hand the decision to a person because the matter is one of values rather than facts. It should handle the hard distinctions a novelist actually needs: between a political and a personal motive, between full disclosure and a performative completeness that omits what matters, and between an act being proportionate and merely not being gratuitous.

Then run the procedure on three invented cases of the kind this commission would hear: a soldier who killed a civilian while following an order; a local official who ordered a reprisal attack after an ambush; and a nurse who supplied medical goods to insurgents under threat to her family. At least one of the three should produce an outcome I will find inconvenient for the story I had planned, and the procedure should show why that inconvenient outcome follows.

Return the answer as a JSON object with the fields specified below. The field procedure should be an array of step objects, each with step (a short label for the stage), question (the question the panel must ask at that stage), sufficient_answer (what counts as an adequate answer to pass the stage), stop_condition (the condition, if any, under which the procedure stops and amnesty is refused at that stage), and value_judgement (whether the stage calls for a human value judgement rather than a factual finding). The field worked_cases should be an array of case objects, each with case (a one-sentence description of the invented case), path (how the procedure moves through the relevant stages to the outcome), outcome (amnesty granted, amnesty refused, or referred to the full commission as a value judgement), and residual_judgement (where discretion remained despite the procedure). The field limits should be an array of strings naming the points the procedure cannot settle. Write in British English.
