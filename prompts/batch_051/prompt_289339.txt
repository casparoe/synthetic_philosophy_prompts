Give me a working definition of "aesthetic testimony" that correctly classifies the following cases as either reliance on aesthetic testimony or judging a work for oneself. (1) You watch a film and are moved by it. (2) A friend tells you the film is a masterpiece, and on that basis you call it a masterpiece. (3) Before you see the film, a critic tells you that its long takes and natural light give it a documentary feel, and this shapes what you notice when you watch it. (4) A streaming service recommends the film on the basis of your viewing history. (5) You read a full plot summary but do not see the film. (6) At a concert you infer that the performance is excellent from a knowledgeable listener's rapt expression. (7) You know that a painting is by a famous artist and recently sold for a very large sum. (8) You repeat a critic's assertion that a work is formally unbalanced without having seen it. State your definition, run it through the eight cases, and say which classification you are least sure of, and whether the definition or that verdict should give way.
