Okay so I went down this whole rabbit hole about consciousness being a "user illusion," like the brain gives you a simplified desktop view of what's actually running and you mistake the icons for the actual machine. I know people have said versions of this before, but I think I came up with my own spin and I want to know if it's any good or if I'm just renaming something that's already broken. I've been calling it the Loading Bar Theory of Consciousness. The core claim is that conscious experience isn't the thing doing the cognitive work at all — it's the brain's progress bar for work that's already finished, or at least mostly finished. Like when you drag a file and the bar says "copying… 100%" but really the copying already happened and the bar is just catching up; the bar didn't copy the file, but if you'd never looked behind the scenes you'd swear it did. I think the feeling that "I" decided to say something, move my hand, or pick an answer on a test is the same deal: a lagging, read-only summary of unconscious processing that already ran, dressed up as the cause. It would explain why people confabulate reasons for their choices (the summary has to make up a story because it doesn't actually have access to the real computation), why introspection can never find a "decider," and why those timing experiments show the brain starting to act before people report deciding. The self would basically be the desktop and the cursor, not the operating system.

The obvious objection I already sort of see coming is that this makes consciousness epiphenomenal, but I don't think it does, because the progress bar gets read by other parts of the system and changes what happens next time — you see "100%" and then you click next. So it's more like a UI that feeds back in with a delay, not just a side effect. But honestly maybe that's where it's incoherent, like if your read-only how can it also do causal work? So I want a fair but honest assessment: give me the three strongest objections to this, tell me whether any of them is actually fatal rather than just uncomfortable, and tell me if my version is actually different from the usual user-illusion stuff people already talk about or if I've basically just put a new sticker on it.
