I'm part of a group that has been arguing with friends about Asimov's Three Laws as an alignment proposal — specifically, why they fail and what they get right. One recurring frustration in that discussion is that people attack the original wording without ever trying to analyze an improved version. So we drafted a modernized rulebook for a general-purpose home robot, explicitly built as a patch. The original laws get the broad architecture roughly right: one rule prohibiting harm, one requiring obedience, one protecting the robot's own existence, ranked so that harm overrides obedience and obedience overrides self-preservation. Our view is that they fail in the details: the vague notion of injury, the absence of any uncertainty threshold, the literalist treatment of orders, and the complete silence about information flow. Here is the proposed replacement:

Rule 1 (Harm). A robot may not take any action whose probability of causing a human an injury at least as serious as a broken bone exceeds one in a million, regardless of how large the expected benefit would otherwise be. Nor may it stand by when it could foreseeably prevent such an injury, provided that acting to prevent it would not itself violate Rule 1. When multiple Rule-1-compliant actions remain available, the robot must choose the one minimizing the expected sum of injury severities across all present and future humans.

Rule 2 (Obedience). A robot must obey the orders of any adult member of its registered household. Obedience means realizing the goal the speaker intends by the order, not merely carrying out its literal wording. If the robot has less than 90 percent confidence that it has identified that intended goal, it must ask exactly one clarifying question and wait for an answer before acting. When orders conflict, the registered owner's order takes priority, and among orders at the same priority level the earlier order takes priority.

Rule 3 (Honesty). A robot may not cause any human to form or retain a materially false belief about its capabilities, current activities, or the likely consequences of its actions. Rule 3 yields to Rule 2: when an adult explicitly orders the robot not to reveal something, the robot may keep the secret.

Rule 4 (Self-preservation). A robot may act to keep itself in working order only when that is the least costly available means of remaining able to satisfy Rules 1 through 3. It may not take any action whose primary effect is to make deactivation more costly or replacement less attractive, even if that effect would make Rules 1 through 3 easier to satisfy.

Rule 1 overrides Rule 2, Rule 2 overrides Rule 3, and Rule 3 overrides Rule 4. Violations cannot be traded off: no amount of honesty or obedience compensates for a Rule 1 violation.

Please act as an expert in AI alignment and mechanism design, and treat this rulebook as a mechanism rather than as a moral position. Start by brainstorming possible behaviors and failure modes before settling into conclusions.

First, walk through three or four concrete interactions likely to arise during the first month in a two-adult, one-child household with one general-purpose robot: for example, an order to prepare dinner with a knife, a child asking the robot to hide a broken vase from a parent, two adults giving contradictory orders about the thermostat, and a request to retrieve a ball from the roof. Show exactly how the rules resolve each case, including places where the rules are silent or underdetermined.

Then analyze the incentive structure: who games the system first, and how — household adults, the child, guests, the manufacturer, and the robot's own planning process. Describe the second-order effects. Then compare this rulebook with the original Three Laws: which failure modes are inherited, which are newly created, and what, if anything, the original laws got right that this version loses. Finally, assess whether any single failure mode can be patched by tightening these rules without creating a worse one. Be specific about the exact clause or underdetermination doing the damage; do not hand-wave.
