At my last checkup, the nurse asked me to rate my pain from zero to ten, and the doctor treated that single number as though it summed up my pain. The same idea appears in health policy, where a year of life is adjusted by a number meant to capture how much pleasure or suffering it contains. This practice seems to presuppose that pain and pleasure are single quantities: that every pain has the same kind of thing in it, just in different amounts, and that every pleasure is the same kind of thing too. Yet people also describe pains as having very different qualities — a burn stings, a headache throbs, nausea is nothing like a bruise — and the pleasure of solving a puzzle does not feel like a weaker or stronger version of the pleasure of eating. What theory of pleasure and pain does the zero-to-ten practice presuppose? Does it strictly require the claim that all pleasures, or all pains, share a common core, or is it compatible with the heterogeneity view that they are only a loose family with nothing in common? And what would pain scales and quality-adjusted life years have to look like if the heterogeneity view were right?
