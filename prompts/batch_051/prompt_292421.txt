hey — i'm a novelist and i keep getting into this fight with my writing group about whether saying a book is good means anything beyond it worked for me. i want to make a quiz for my blog: which position on aesthetic judgement do you actually hold, with five or six leaves and eight to ten questions, each question a concrete case someone can answer cold, without knowing the labels. don't ask anyone to self-identify. build it from the questions and the answer combos first, then place the positions — not the other way around. the positions i want in there, roughly: plain subjectivism (each to their own, nobody's wrong), the humean standard-of-taste view (judgements are sentiments, but the verdicts of trained, unprejudiced critics under the right conditions set the standard), aesthetic realism (goodness is really in the work and beliefs about it are true or false), a response-dependence or fittingness middle view, and maybe an error theory / no-norms leaf, plus anything else you think earns a spot.

the distinctions that actually matter for my readers are: can a sincere, intelligent, fully informed reader be wrong about whether a book is good; if there are standards, where do they come from (the work, the ideal reader, the community, or nowhere); and what the test of time and cross-cultural agreement are actually evidence of. the old humean checklist of practice, comparison, delicacy of taste, and freedom from prejudice should show up somewhere, but translated into ordinary questions someone could answer without having read hume.

use these cases or close variants: a formally inventive but emotionally flat novel i love and my sharpest reader hates; a fanfic that moved me more than the classic it riffs on; a reader who hated a book at twenty, then spent a decade reading hard and now thinks it's great — was she wrong before; a book that's been called a masterpiece for two centuries and bores me; a book that only lands for readers from the same background as the author; and a hyper-informed critic with flawless technique who doesn't seem to feel anything while he reads. questions can be agree/disagree or pick-a-response, as long as they're concrete.

then give me the scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the leaves. flag any positions the quiz cant really separate — i suspect realism and the humean ideal-critic view make the same case judgements except on whether even ideal critics could all be wrong, and if you can't find a diagnostic that pulls them apart say so and tell me what difference is left. and note any answer combinations nobody occupies, with a quick line on whether thats because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. keep it from just becoming a list of named views with a question each — the questions should do the sorting, not the labels.
