I'm taking a causal inference / philosophy of science course this term, and we've been doing the stuff on causal discovery: the faithfulness condition, minimality, and why we're supposed to prefer the sparsest Bayes net that fits the independence data. On paper I'm totaly sold. The package is clean: if the true distribution is faithful to the true graph, then minimality isn't just an aesthetic preference, it's forced on you, because any graph that fits the data while adding extra edges will imply independencies that don't actually hold. It turns causal discovery from guesswork into something with a decision procedure, and I really want that to be right.

But I've got this intuition that won't go away. It seems really easy to build a system where the distribution is unfaithful on purpose. Suppose a hormone raises blood pressure through one pathway and lowers it through another, and the two paths exactly cancel, so the hormone and blood pressure look independent even though the true graph has the edges. Or a thermostat that holds temperature steady: the heater is a cause of the temperature, but conditional on the setting you might see no dependence. In cases like that, assuming faithfulness doesn't just add a harmless tie-breaker, it systematically hides real causes and hands you the wrong minimal graph. And nature seems full of feedback loops and compensatory mechanisms, some of them actually selected or engineered to cancel things out.

I can't tell whether my intuition is a bug I should debunk. The standard line would say exact cancellations are measure-zero, fragile, and not the sort of thing you should build a methodology around: any tiny change in one parameter breaks the cancellation and the dependence shows up, so faithfulness is a bet that nature isn't fine-tuned against you. But I also can't shake the sense that this treats a real feature of the world—homeostasis, compensation, regulation—as a negligible accident, and maybe the theory is just too tidy.

Present both sides at their strongest for me, and then give a negotiated verdict: should the intuition be debunked and dropped, should the faithfulness/minimality package be amended or abandoned, or is there a reading on which they're compatible? I want the answer to say what would justify each move, not just pick a side, because I keep flip-flopping depending on which example I think about last.
