I'm a clinical ethicist at a large teaching hospital, and maybe once a week I get a consult where someone claims a religious command trumps ordinary ethics or hospital policy. Our existing conscience-accommodation policy is built for recognized doctrines — a nurse declining to assist with an abortion, a Jehovah's Witness refusing blood, that sort of thing. It has nothing for the stranger cases: a nurse who says God told her, directly, not to help withdraw life support from this particular patient; a father who refuses a transfusion for his child not on denominational grounds but because of a private revelation; a resident who says she has to break confidentiality because God commanded her to warn a patient's relative. Some of these people are plainly unwell. But every so often one of them seems to me the most morally serious person in the room, and I dont want our policy to just pathologize them.

I want a triage procedure my team can actually run, grounded in Kierkegaard's teleological suspension of the ethical and his distinction between the tragic hero and the knight of faith. I've read Fear and Trembling, but I need it made into a tool. The team is me, a chaplain, a social worker, and an ICU nurse, and I'm the only one who's read the book, so the steps have to be plain questions, not theory-speak. The procedure should say, for a given claim: what questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where we refuse, where we accommodate, and — this matters most — where the procedure has to stop, because a real Abraham can't be certified from the outside and maybe the whole point is that no ethics committee can sign off on it. Then run it on two of my cases: the nurse with the private command to block a ventilator withdrawal, and a father who wants to take his stable but still seriously ill child home against medical advice because God told him prayer would be enough. We usually need a decision same-day, and whatever we write gets gone over by hospital lawyers, so it has to be defensible but not just legal cover. I'm fine with a procedure that gives me an answer I wont like.
