For the metaphysics reading group I run with rationalist-adjacent friends, I want a decision-tree handout on what Bradley’s regress shows about the unity of propositions. The handout will open with the passage near the start of F. H. Bradley’s Appearance and Reality (1893), chapter III, “Relation and Quality,” in which he considers a relation R supposed to connect two terms: if R itself is a real thing distinct from the terms, then R must be connected to each term by further relations, and so on without end. I want the tree to begin from that passage and ask readers diagnostic questions whose answers place them at a named position—but the questions should not require knowing the position names beforehand.

Please construct the tree from the logical space first: first the key yes/no questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions among those combinations. Mark any combinations nobody defends, with a one-sentence diagnosis of why not (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). The leaves must include at least: a Fregean unsaturated-concept/function-argument view; a Russellian constituent-relational view; a Bergmann-style non-relational tie or nexus view; an Armstrong-style states-of-affairs view on which the unity is not an extra constituent; and a Bradleyan monist or identity view on which the regress is a reductio of all relational structure. If the tree cannot cleanly separate any of these, say so and say where the instrument fails.

The questions should be about cases or claims, not about labels. For example, they might ask whether “this cup is red” is one fact or a complex of three independent items; whether a relation can relate without itself being related; whether the regress is vicious or benign; and whether the unity of a proposition is something we must explain or can take as primitive. Aim for about six to eight questions, with a scoring key or branch resolution if you choose quiz form. The audience is comfortable with formal argument but new to this exact debate, so do not assume prior knowledge of the positions. Keep the instrument usable without web access.
