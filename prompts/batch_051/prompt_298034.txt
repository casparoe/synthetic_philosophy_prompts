I got roped into grading the short-answer part of a take-home exam for an intro logic/critical thinking class a friend of mine teaches. I'm a software engineer, I havent studied philosophy formally, so I dont want to grade on vibes.

Here's the question the students had to answer:

On Friday, a teacher announces: "There will be an exam next week, and you won't be able to know which day it will be before it happens." A student argues the exam can't be Friday, because after Monday through Thursday pass without an exam, the class would know Thursday night that Friday is the day, so it wouldn't be a surprise. It can't be Thursday either, because once Friday is ruled out, the class would know Wednesday night that Thursday is the day. And the same reasoning goes back through Wednesday, Tuesday, and Monday, so no day works. Reconstruct this argument in your own words and assess it. Does it succeed? What does it assume about what the students know? Can the teacher's announcement be true?

Here's one student's full answer:

"If the exam were Friday, then once Monday through Thursday go by with no exam, we'd know Thursday night that Friday is the only remaining day. Since the teacher said there will be an exam, and a surprise means we can't know the day in advance, Friday can't be the exam day. The same goes for Thursday: after Friday has been eliminated, if Monday through Wednesday go by with no exam, we'd know Wednesday night that it has to be Thursday, so Thursday wouldn't be a surprise. Repeating this eliminates Wednesday, Tuesday, and Monday. So no day can make the teacher's announcement true, which means the announcement is false. The root issue is that the announcement is almost self-referential: the surprise clause is about what we can know, and what we can know depends partly on the announcement itself. But the argument only needs the students to remember the announcement and reason through the elimination carefully, which is a pretty mild and realistic assumption."

Grade this answer out of 10, and give me the point-by-point comments that justify the grade: which parts earn credit, which parts have problems, and exactly what those problems are. I'd rather have specific notes I can attach to the grade than a general impression.
