I'm a novelist, three books in, and I'm starting a family saga about a family that got rich off land taken in a massacre. Before I draft it I want to know if my beliefs about inherited guilt actually hang together, because I cant tell anymore. Here's where I've landed. (1) Guilt is earned by what you do or choose; it cant be inherited. (2) But the grandchildren in this story have to carry the family's wrong as a real stain — if I wrote them as clean slates, I'd be writing something false. (3) I also think that if one of them knows the money comes from the massacre and shrugs, "not my doing, not my problem," that shrug is itself blameworthy. (4) And I think it's barbaric to blame or punish people for what they didn't choose. Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest revision? Rank the tensions by severity and say what has to give.
