I've been reading about the old Korean Four–Seven debate between Toegye and Yulgok and I cant shake a tension in it. Quick setup in case you don't know it: the Four Beginnings are the feelings Mencius calls the sprouts of virtue—commiseration, shame and dislike, modesty and yielding, the sense of right and wrong. The Seven Emotions are the broader list—joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, desire. Toegye held they have two sources: the Four issue from principle (li), the Seven from material force (ki), which is why moral feelings feel like a different kind of thing from ordinary appetites. Yulgok held the Four just are the good, well-ordered cases of the Seven—one source, one unified account of emotion, much tidier on paper.

Here's the problem. Theoretically I'm drawn to Yulgok: one ontology instead of two, no need to split a single feeling between sources, no doubled story about how principle and material force interact. But I have this stubborn intuition that the Four Beginnings arent just a subset of the Seven. When I feel that jolt at a child about to fall into a well, before any calculation, it doesn't feel like a better-ordered version of being annoyed in traffic or wanting lunch. It feels categorically different in source, not just different in moral quality or degree. So I'm stuck between a theory I'm attracted to for its parsimony and an intuition I don't want to dismiss as unphilosophical.

I don't want to be told who won historically. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the unified theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading where both survive—one source, say, but still a real difference in kind between the feelings principle leads and the feelings material force leads.
