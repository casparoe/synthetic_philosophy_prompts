I've been thinking about why philosophical arguments so rarely end with one side saying they changed their mind, and I keep coming back to the fact that there's no mechanism that rewards that. So here's a design for a forum. You can only start a thread if you state a precise, single claim at the top and mark yourself as defending or attacking it. If any participant posts in the thread that they used to defend the claim and now assign the opposite at least .7 confidence, the thread is closed and filed as 'settled.' The person whose post did the convincing gets a point, and the person who changed their mind gets a point, maybe two, since changing your mind is the costlier move. So far so tidy. But if a thread has more then fifty active participants and still no one has changed their mind after three months, it goes on a public 'stuck' list, and everyone still defending one side or the other loses a point per week until the thread either gets settled or is declared a merely terminological dispute by a rotating panel. What happens to the way people phrase their initial claims once there's an escape hatch called 'merely terminological'? Who ends up accumulating points, and is the leaderboard tracking philosophical progress or something more like the ability to extract public concessions?
