Design a 75-minute structured activity for an undergraduate seminar of 12–15 students on personal identity that makes participants feel the specific force of the Buddhist claim of anatta: the argument that the self cannot be identified with any of the five aggregates — body, feeling tone, perception, volitions and dispositions, consciousness — because none of them is permanent and none of them is fully under our control. The idea should emerge from what the students actually do, not from a lecture: by the end of the exercise they should have run each candidate self through a test and seen all five fail before the concept is named. The activity should finish with a debrief that maps what happened onto the classical not-self argument and connects it to the interpretive question of whether anatta denies a subject altogether or only denies that the subject is one more item among the aggregates.

Please give me the following sections, in this order:

1. Activity overview and rules (about 200 words). Give the activity a name, describe the roles (for example, claimants and auditors), the point or token system if there is one, and the exact test participants apply to each candidate aggregate each round.

2. Roles and setup (about 120 words). What the facilitator prepares beforehand and what each participant is responsible for doing.

3. Timeline (about 150 words). A minute-by-minute breakdown of the 75 minutes.

4. Debrief (about 250 words). The questions to ask afterward and how specific moments in the activity map onto the not-self argument; mention the chariot simile and what it does and does not show.

5. Likely failures and how to handle them (about 150 words). What will predictably go wrong when the activity is run — students gaming the test, claiming control they do not have, concluding that no-self means they are a blank, and so on — and how the facilitator should respond.

6. What the activity cannot reproduce (about 80 words). An honest note on which feature of the anatta position the exercise cannot recreate and the facilitator must supply.

Write in American English.
