Consider an asymmetry in how we judge coordination failures. A commercial fishery is in what people sometimes call a Moloch problem or multipolar trap: each skipper who restrains their catch loses income to the others, while if everyone takes the legal maximum the stock collapses and everyone ends up worse off. We tend to condemn the fishers, and we call the outcome a race to the bottom and an act of collective greed, even after granting that each skipper was best-responding to what the others did. But when we meet a two-player prisoner's dilemma, in which each player's individually rational choice likewise produces an outcome both regard as worse, we usually refuse to blame the defector and say the structure, not the person, is at fault. Both situations are equilibria in which individually rational moves leave everyone worse off, yet only in the first do we treat the move as culpable. Is that asymmetry defensible, or should one side's verdict be revised to match the other?

To make the question tractable, work through five cases:
(a) a two-player prisoner's dilemma in which all the harm falls on the two players themselves;
(b) the same two-player game, except each defection also imposes a small cost on an uninvolved third party;
(c) a fifty-player commons in which the harm of overuse falls only on the fifty users;
(d) a fifty-player race to the bottom in which the harm falls mainly on nonplayers, such as workers, downstream communities, or the stock as a public resource;
(e) a dominant firm that could unilaterally improve its practices at trivial cost to itself and without losing market share, but does not.

Identify the candidate principles that might justify treating the actors in (d) as more blameworthy than the actor in (a), for example: third-party harm, the number of players, the size of each contribution relative to the total, the presence of an identifiable victim, the capacity to coordinate, or the publicity of the action. Then state which principle, if any, survives all five cases, and whether the ordinary pattern of blame should be kept, revised, or abandoned.

- final_verdict: string; one of keep, revise, or abandon.
