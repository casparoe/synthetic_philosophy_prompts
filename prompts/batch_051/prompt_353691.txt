The part I can't get my head around is in Problema I, where Johannes de silentio keeps insisting the tragic hero is intelligible and even kind of consoling, because he gives up something finite for something higher within the universal, but Abraham can't be understood because his relation to the absolute is direct and private — the single individual is higher than the universal. Our prof keeps telling us this difference matters, but nobody in the room actually feels it. Design a classroom activity that gets us to run into it ourselves: something where the class first works through a tragic-hero situation (where you're sad but everyone gets your reasons and can praise you for it), then gets forced into a decision or a role where the reasons can't be stated publicly without the act turning into something else — and only afterward do we name the distinction. Include rules, a timeline for 75 minutes, how to debrief it, and be honest about which feature of the knight of faith the activity can't reproduce and has to be supplied by discussion instead.
