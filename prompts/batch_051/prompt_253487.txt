A friend and I spent a whole dinner trying to build a spectrum from a single second of toothache to a lifelong one, and we ended up with an argument that seems to show either that one extra second of mild pain matters all by itself, or that 'better than' doesn't behave anything like we thought. I cant find the off-ramp. Here's the argument:

(1) Imagine a person whose life is otherwise excellent, except that at 35 they have exactly one second of mild but genuine toothache. Call this life A0. Now build a sequence: A1 has two seconds of that same toothache, A2 has three seconds, and so on, however many steps it takes, until An, whose life is identical except the same low-grade toothache lasts most of their adult life.

(2) For each i, Ai+1 is not worse than Ai. It would be absurd to say the single added second is what makes the next life worse; adjacent lives are as close to indiscernible as two lives can be, and a one-second difference cannot make one worse.

(3) 'Not worse than' is transitive: if X is not worse than Y and Y is not worse than Z, then X is not worse than Z. And that looks forced once we accept that 'better than' and 'exactly as good as' are each transitive, since 'not worse than' just means 'better than or exactly as good as'.

(4) So by chaining (2) across the whole sequence, An is not worse than A0.

(5) But An plainly is worse than A0. A life with decades of daily low-grade toothache is worse than an otherwise identical life with one second of it.

(6) Therefore either (2) or (3) has to go. If (3) goes, then 'not worse than' is nontransitive, and we are stuck choosing: either 'better than' is nontransitive (a life can be better than a second life, which is better than a third, while the third is better than the first), or 'exactly as good as' is nontransitive (two things can each be exactly as good as a third without being exactly as good as each other).

(7) Either option seems to gut the way we rank lives and choose between them. 'Best option' and 'worse than' stop behaving like anything we can reason with.

Where does this go wrong? If you made me pick, I would keep the transitivity of 'better than' at almost any cost, but then I dont see how to avoid saying theres a magic second somewhere in the sequence. Which step do I reject, and what exactly does each fix cost?
