I am a retired natural scientist who has come to the philosophy of translation rather late; my reading group has been working through the book that gathers dozens of English versions of a single short address by Clément Marot to a sick girl. For the position statement I intend to circulate to the group, I have set down my current commitments about translating verse under formal constraint, and I would like a sober audit of them before I commit myself in print. Here they are:

1. The formal properties of a poem — its meter, rhyme scheme, and stanza shape — are not decoration but part of its meaning, so that a version which keeps the wording while quietly dropping the form has changed the work rather than translated it.

2. It follows, I believe, that a word-for-word rendering is less faithful to a poem than a rendering that freely changes wording in order to carry the meter and rhyme into the target language.

3. Since no translation can carry over every feature of its source, every verse translation rests on a judgment about which features of the original are essential and which may be sacrificed.

4. Some features are so bound to the source language — a pun, an echo, a rhyme that works only in French — that no target-language text can reproduce them; in that strict sense, aspects of a poem, and perhaps some entire poems, are untranslatable.

5. None of this makes translation futile: for any worthwhile poem, a skilled translator can produce a version that stands to the original roughly as a good scientific model stands to the system it models — faithful in the respects that matter, and openly simplified in the rest.

6. When the effort to preserve form yields stiff or contrived lines, the attempt has failed, and a clear prose rendering of the sense is the better outcome.

Please go through these six commitments and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what the least costly revision would be. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, say in each case which commitment would have to give and at what cost, and identify any apparent tension that dissolves once its terms are distinguished. I want an audit, not a survey of translation theory.
