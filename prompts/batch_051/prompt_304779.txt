I am a high school student, and I would like your help thinking through a situation at my school. This year the school began a pilot program in which students wear a headband that records electrical activity from their heads during class. The company that makes the device says it can tell whether a student is paying attention, and the school psychologist says the same data could be used to flag early signs of depression or anxiety. Students are required to wear the headband during school hours, and the data goes into a school database that teachers and counselors can access. Some students have compared the device to a camera recording their inner life, while the administration compares it to attendance records: just another way of knowing that students are present and engaged. I want to understand what ethical issue is really at stake here, and whether the school's policy can be defended. My friends say it violates our right to keep our thoughts to ourselves; the school says it would be wrong to ignore signs that a student is struggling. Could both positions have some truth, or does getting this right require choosing one at the expense of the other? Please respond as a professional philosopher working on the ethics of emerging technology. Start by brainstorming the different ways one might characterize what the headband actually collects—for example, is it a record of the body, of behavior, of mental states, or of something in between?—and then use that to work toward a careful answer about whether the requirement is justified, and under what conditions it might become unjustified.
