My own muddle is that I keep blurring at least three things: being uncorrupted so far (no one has made the right offer yet) versus being built so one can't be corrupted; an incorruptible person versus an incorruption-producing system (audits, open registers, job rotation); and, hardest for me, a person who is immune to threats and bribes versus a person who is also immune to legitimate pressures like constituents' anger, peer criticism, or the need to compromise on means to avoid a harm. I have a feeling the last point is where the 'is it always good?' question actually bites, but I can't tell what has to be laid down before it makes sense to the room.

Plan the session's explanatory arc for me: the order in which the ideas have to go in and what depends on what, including the distinction that has to be in place before the question even becomes discussable; the one running example or case I should keep coming back to and what it must have and must lack to carry the idea; the objections or resistances this particular audience will raise and the order they'll raise them in, with the point in the sequence where each should be met rather than deferred; and a small check I can run at the end to know whether they've understood the question rather than just learned a new slogan.
