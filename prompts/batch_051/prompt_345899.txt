So in my reading group we've been going back and forth for months about whether interpersonal utility comparisons are meaningful at all. The other camp says this is a motte-and-bailey that tanks pretty much all of welfare economics and utilitarianism: if you can't compare utility across people, you can't say a redistributive policy made things better, you can't even say Bob is worse off than Alice after a robbery, and ordinary moral practice is stuffed with these comparisons, so banning them just makes the whole enterprise vacuous.
