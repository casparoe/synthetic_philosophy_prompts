I've been moderating a long-running argument between two friends, and we've decided to try something: an adversarial collaboration. One of them — call him the generalist — thinks there's something systematically epistemically wrong with conspiracy theories as a class, something about how they're formed and held rather than just the fact that many of them turn out false. He keeps pointing to features like self-sealing reasoning, where evidence against the theory gets treated as further evidence of how deep the cover-up runs. The other — call her the particularist — thinks that's exactly the kind of move people make against any theory that says hidden actors coordinated in secret, and real conspiracies and cover-ups exist; Watergate, for a start. On her view the only sound epistemic question is whether this particular theory about this particular event is supported by the evidence we have, not whether we can slot it into a suspicious category using a phrase like "conspiracy theory." After months of this they've mostly been talking past each other, so we want them to produce the document they should have produced.

Please draft that joint statement. Structure it as: (1) a numbered list of claims about conspiracy theories, inquiry, and evidence that both sides can sign without grimacing; (2) a precise statement of the remaining disagreement, in wording each side would accept as fair to its own view; (3) three or four cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to move; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete case or type of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'd like you to commit: pick the actual formulations you'd put in front of both of them, choose the cruxes you think are the live ones rather than the ones that are just differences of emphasis, and state the predictions each side would actually stand behind. If some part of the dispute is merely verbal, say exactly which part and then move on. Aim for the kind of document both would sign reluctantly because it concedes enough but not too much.
