I've been trying to get straight what the live responses to Bradley's regress actually are — the one about the unity of a proposition or state of affairs: if a and b need relation R to hang together, then R needs something to hang it together with a and b, and so on. The familes of response I've collected so far:

(1) Frege's saturation answer. Concepts/functions are unsaturated and objects are saturated, so combination is not an extra constituent that needs gluing; the function just takes the object as argument and the whole is complete.

(2) Russell's multiple relation theory. Stop positing unified propositions as things sitting there; the judging subject stands in a many-place relation to the constituents, and the unity comes from the act of judging instead of from the proposition itself.

(3) The non-relational tie answer. Instantiation is a different kind of connector from an ordinary relation, so it doesnt need a further tie to attach it to its relata; asking for one is a category mistake.

(4) The non-vicious regress answer. The infinite chain is real but harmless, because each step is just the original relation holding again, not a fresh fact that has to be brought about.

(5) The primitive structure answer. A structured proposition just has its arrangement primitively; the relation sits in the proposition at a certain spot, and the demand for a further relation comes from a confused picture of structure as an extra constituent.

I keep having the feeling there's a sixth family I'm not seeing. Is there? Either recall a standard response I've left off, or build a new one. It has to be genuinely different from these five — not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of them — and it should say how it blocks or dissolves the regress, and whether it's any good. Also say how it relates to #3 and #5 specifically, since I suspect any sixth answer will be adjacent to one of those.

Finally, tell me where your proposed answer is most likely to fail, and what consideration would change your verdict about it — in particular, what would make you decide it collapses back into one of the five or doesn't actually stop the regress.
