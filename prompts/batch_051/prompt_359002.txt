Ok so long story: I lost my job last fall, money got tight, and I started dumpster diving behind a grocery store. That pulled me into freeganism, and a couple months ago I took in two spent laying hens a farm was going to kill. So now I think about this stuff alot, and the word "waste" has started doing my head in.

I use the word confidently all the time — "don't waste that," "what a waste" — but when I try to say what waste actually is, my definitions keep breaking. Is waste just not using something that could have been used? Then almost any idle hour is waste, which feels too strict. Is it stuff ending up somewhere it can never be used again? Then what about my hens' eggs — they lay them anyway, unfertilized, and if I don't eat them they just rot. Have I wasted them if I throw them out? Is their manure a waste product or a resource, or both depending on what I do with it?

I want a working definition of waste, one that gets the clear cases right and gives definite answers on the messy ones. Please run it against every case below, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer at all, and either revise the definition or defend the weird verdicts. Bonus if you can say why the concept feels like it comes in degrees.

Clear waste: a bakery tossing unsold bread in the dumpster at closing; someone letting groceries rot in their fridge while they get takeout every night. Clear not-waste: eight hours of sleep; leaves decomposing on the forest floor. Borderline, and I honestly don't know what to think: roadkill; the uneaten eggs; throwing away the hens' molted feathers; a person's organs after a sudden death if no one can use them in time.

Oh and please write in American English.
