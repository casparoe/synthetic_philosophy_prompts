A. A father payed for all four years of college and the down payment on a house, but was otherwise distant — never asked how I was doing, never said anything warm. Verdict: financial support yes; emotional care no.

B. A mother was loving and attentive but had nothing to give financially. Now she's old and needs care and some money. Verdict: I owe her the care and the money, and this can't be repayment, because there's nothing to repay. This one feels clearest of all.

C. A parent and an adult child have, by now, a genuine friendship: they call because they enjoy it, the parent never pulls rank or guilt-trips. The parent gets sick suddenly. Verdict: the child owes about what a close friend would owe in a crisis — a lot of help, but not because of some ledger. It's just what friends do.

D. A parent was bad by any standard — mean, neglectful, kicked the kid out at 17 — and the adult child has had no contact for 25 years. The mother now has dementia and no one else. Verdict: the child doesn't owe personal care, and maybe owes nothing at all. Calling adult protective services would already be more than is owed.

E. Same family, two parents. The mother gave up a career to stay home; the father paid for everything. Both now need help. Verdict: both are owed help, but the felt pull is different. With the mother it feels warm; with the father it feels like settling an invoice. If I'm honest, they feel like two different kinds of duty, and I can't tell whether one of them is really a duty at all.

F. The one I go back and forth on: an adequate but not warm parent, whom the adult child has simply drifted from — no dislike, just nothing to say — has a stroke and needs a few months of help with errands, rides, and decisions. Verdict: I lean yes, the child owes something, not their whole life but a floor of help in a crisis. But I can't get that out of either the debt picture or the friendship picture.

That fits B and C nicely, and maybe D, but it seems to badly misfit A and E, where I do feel something like a debt to a father I had no friendship with. So maybe I'm running two seperate principles, and that bothers me.
