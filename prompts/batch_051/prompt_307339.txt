Here's an argument I keep coming back to against the ubuntu idea that personhood is something you acquire through your relations with other people, and I can't find the slip. (1) If personhood is acquired through relationships, then it can also be lost by losing those relationships. (2) So someone in maximal solitary confinement, with no contact or possibility of contact, would cease to be a person — or would only ever have been a person in a borrowed way. (3) Ceasing to be a person has to mean either losing a normative status (being the kind of being who deserves respect) or merely losing some descriptive social capacities. (4) If it's the normative reading, the guards would apparently be entitled to treat the isolated person as a mere object, which seems monstrous. (5) If it's the descriptive reading, ubuntu becomes a claim about social skills, not about who is owed moral concern, and it loses its force. (6) Either way, the relational conception can't do the moral work it was supposed to do. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and on the strongest reading of that step does the rest still go through?
