I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about animal minds and machine minds, and I figured I'd write down my gut verdicts on a bunch of concrete cases instead of starting from some theory. My working hunch is that sentience is a matter of degree and that moral status should track it, but my gut keeps not cooperating.

Here are the verdicts. Stepping on an ant while I'm walking: nothing, I don't think about it twice. Swatting a fly: fine, maybe a tiny flicker but no real guilt. Boiling a lobster alive: wrong, I won't do it, and I'd judge someone who did it casually. Eating shrimp: fine, even though I can't tell you what the relevant difference from the lobster is. A pig in a gestation crate: clearly wrong, maybe the worst thing on this list. A farmed salmon suffocating on a boat deck: bad, less bad than the pig but still wrong. An octopus killed for food: I won't eat octopus, it feels like eating something too smart. A rat in a glue trap: bad, and I'll go out of my way to use snap traps instead, even though the rat still dies, so apparently my problem is with the suffering, not the killing. An oyster eaten alive: I don't feel anything about it, maybe it can't suffer, though I can't tell wether it does or not. A robot that begs me not to unplug it, with a voice that trembles, but I'm told it has no pain or feeling circuits: I'd unplug it and not feel guilty, maybe a tiny pang at most. A thermostat with a goal of keeping the house at 68: no moral weight at all.

The obvious candidate principle is something like: moral status tracks sentience, sentience comes in degrees, and the question is allways how much a creature can suffer. But that principle makes me uneasy. If a pig could be raised happy and killed instantly in its sleep, my principle seems to say that's fine, and my gut says maybe not. And if a lobster doesn't suffer all that much, why do I care more about it than about the fly? Maybe I'm tracking something else: intelligence, complexity, having a life that can go well or badly for it, or just being an animal with a nervous system. I want a principle that gets most of these verdicts, stated precisely, and an honest list of which verdicts it can't save. What does it say about bees, about shrimp, about a creature that feels pain but has no memory or sense of a future self, about an AI that reports suffering in a way that sounds completely convincing, about a human with severe dementia? And is there a pair of my verdicts I should just give up?
