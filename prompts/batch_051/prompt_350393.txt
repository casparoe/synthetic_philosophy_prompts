I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about heritability, and I've ended up with five things I'm attached to. My worry is they don't all fit together, but I can't tell where the actual stress is, so an audit would help more than a lecture.

(1) Heritability is a number about variance in a population within a particular range of environments — not a fixed property of a trait, and it can go up or down when the environment changes.
(2) A trait can be highly heritable and still be pushed around alot by an environmental change that hits everyone, like average height rising with better nutrition.
(3) If someone says a gap between two groups must be genetic because the trait is highly heritable within each group, I think they're making a bad inference. The same mixed bag of seeds planted in two plots will show a soil-driven gap between plots even though the height differences within each plot are genetic.
(4) But I also think that if I learned a trait is highly heritable within each of two groups, I should be at least a little more open to a partly genetic explanation of the gap between them than if it had near-zero heritability in both.
(5) And I don't think a sentence like 'IQ is 60% heritable' says anything about how much of any particular person's IQ is genetic.
