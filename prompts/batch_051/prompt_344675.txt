I've been thinking about counterfactual mugging and want to run a variant past you. The classic setup, from Eliezer Yudkowsky's LessWrong post Counterfactual Mugging (I'm paraphrasing, not quoting): Omega flips a fair coin. On heads, it gives you $10,000 if you would have paid $100 had the coin come up tails. On tails, it asks you to pay $100, but the heads payoff is just an unobserved counterfactual from where you sit. The usual point is that a policy which precommits to pay on tails collects the $10,000 on heads, while a policy that just conditions on being in the tails branch refuses, so the updateless policy does better. In the passage I'm thinking of, the key move is that Omega is offering a kind of contract across counterfactual branches, and being the sort of agent who honors the counterfactual contract is what makes the payoff real.

Here's the twist. Instead of flipping a physical coin, Omega decides the branch by hashing the source code of the very decision procedure you end up implementing. It tells you in advance exactly which hash function it will use and which parity value maps to heads versus tails. After you've made up your mind about your policy but before you act, it tells you the hash value and so which branch you're in. The catch is that this branch is logically determined by the policy you just chose. If your policy is deterministic and you know it, then in principle you could compute which branch you're in before acting, but that computation just mirrors the policy itself rather than giving you independent evidence about the world. Does the standard updateless reasoning survive? Does a policy that pays on tails actually end up getting the $10,000 on heads, or does the self-referential / logical-correlation structure change the verdict? What would causal, evidential, and functional/updateless decision theories each say here, and at what level of computational ability — e.g., can Omega run the hash, can you — would their recommendations diverge? I'm less interested in the final answer than in the reasoning: where exactly the standard argument breaks or holds, and what that shows about how counterfactual-mugging arguments depend on the branch being independent of the agent's own policy.
