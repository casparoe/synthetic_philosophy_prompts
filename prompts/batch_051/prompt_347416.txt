Suppose that in 2026 a mid-sized city replaces its bus fleet with autonomous buses and, to keep delays acceptable, adopts a fleet-wide safety rule: the system may operate in a mode expected to cause one serious pedestrian or cyclist injury every two years, spread as very small probabilities across everyone who uses the streets. The city does not seek consent; it argues that each individual's risk is negligible, that no one is singled out, and that a compensation fund will cover the rare victims. Predict, with step-by-step reasoning, how this arrangement is likely to develop over the next twenty years. Trace how residents, advocacy groups, insurers, and courts would respond, what moral arguments would actually move each group, and how the policy would be reshaped as a result. Identify which links in your prediction are robust and which rest on contestable assumptions, and state what would have to be different for the policy to collapse within a year rather than become normalized.
