Two guys in my bible study group got really into it last night over the number of fish in the last chapter of John. The passage is John 21:11 (KJV): "Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken."

Dave said the number is clearly doing symbolic work. His thing was that 153 is the seventeenth triangular number, and 17 splits into 10 and 7 — the Ten Commandments plus the sevenfold gifts of the Spirit. So the number encodes completeness, the gathered church, which is exactly what the net-full-of-fish image is about anyway. He also pointed to Augustine, who read it allegorically, and said John is the symbolic gospel; it doesn't stop to explain its own symbols.

Mira said that's post hoc pattern-hunting. Any number can be made to do that — 153 is also three times 51, and the sum of the first five factorials, and probably a dozen other things if you go looking. The text just says there were a lot of fish and the net didn't break. A weirdly exact number is what you'd expect if somebody counted a real catch; specificity is realism, not code. If it were meant symbolically, the author gives zero signal of it, unlike the explicit signs elsewhere.

Dave came back: but why record the exact number at all then? The Gospel uses symbolic numbers all the time — seven signs, seven "I am" sayings — and the author doesn't stop to explain those either. And the triangular reading isn't arbitrary; it hooks into the 7 and 10 that are already doing work in the surrounding tradition.

Mira: but that's the problem. You're using the allegorical reading to justify the allegorical reading. You already assume the net is the Church, then read the number as a deliberate reinforcement of that, and then treat the number itself as evidence. Also, would the first-century Galilean fishermen in the story, or the first readers, have been in the habit of computing triangular numbers? It reads more like a modern numerology puzzle than first-century narrative.

Dave finally said they didn't need to compute it on the spot; meanings can be latent for teachers later to draw out, and the tradition's persistence is itself evidence that it's not just one guy's clever blog post. Mira said tradition's persistence is evidence that numerology is contagious.

They asked me to referee and I honestly don't know who's right about what. I think they kept bluring the difference between "the author intended it" and "the number has a providential resonance," and that's part of what's confusing. Can you adjudicate the actual moves, one by one? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Each point should name a specific move in their exchange and say whether it's right, wrong, or partly right and why.
