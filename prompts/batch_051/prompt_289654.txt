I am a retired biochemist. I spent most of my career working on metabolic regulation—flux control, feedback inhibition, steady states, and the question of whether a pathway has any privileged step—and I have only recently begun reading epistemology with any seriousness. I have been working through the regress problem, and the standard three-way split among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism keeps reminding me of arguments we used to have in the 1970s about whether one can assign control to a single step, whether control is distributed throughout a cycle, or whether the very idea of a rate-limiting step is a conceptual mistake. I want to use my own vocabulary to see how much of the epistemology really carries over.

When I read Aristotle in Posterior Analytics I.3, the dilemma is roughly this: if knowledge requires demonstration from prior premises, then the chain of premises either stops at undemonstrated first principles, circles back, or goes on without end. Modern foundationalism keeps the stopping point, coherentism accepts the loop, and infinitism accepts the endless chain. Please restate each of these three positions in the vocabulary of metabolic control analysis as faithfully as you can: terminal substrates and irreversible initial steps, closed cycles such as the TCA cycle, and open-ended degradative or biosynthetic chains. For instance, is a foundational first principle like a committed step with negligible reverse flux? Is coherentist mutual support like a closed cycle in steady state with no unique entry point? Is an infinite justification chain like a theoretically unbounded pathway with no flux-generating step? I would like the translation to be systematic, not impressionistic.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of each philosophical position map cleanly onto the biochemical concepts, which you had to fudge, and which could not be expressed in metabolic vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether the residue is a limitation of my scientific framework or whether it marks places where the philosophical positions are doing work that the analogy cannot carry. As someone who is comfortable with metabolic control analysis but still a novice in epistemology, I would rather have an honest account of what gets lost in translation than a tidy mapping that ignores the failures.
