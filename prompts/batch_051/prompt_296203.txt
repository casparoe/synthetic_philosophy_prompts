After six months of trying to get a forecasting model to say anything useful about a production process that's apparently chaotic, I keep noticing that 'unpredictable' is doing four different jobs in the same planning meeting. Before I read any more papers, I want the spec. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of what 'unpredictable' means, specifically for deterministic systems rather than pure noise. It has to take a position on cases like the two-body vs. three-body orbit, the double pendulum, the logistic map past r ≈ 3.57, a flipped coin, and a PRNG; it has to respect distinctions like unpredictable-in-practice vs unpredictable-in-principle, chaos vs. stochasticity, and sensitive dependence on initial conditions vs. sensitivity to model error; and it cant contradict results like positive Lyapunov exponents or strange attractors. For each desideratum, say which case or result forces it. Then flag any pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement kills the most loose talk. Its fine if the answer is 'there are two or three varieties of unpredictability and no unified account,' but I want that to come out as the result of the list, not as a cop-out.
