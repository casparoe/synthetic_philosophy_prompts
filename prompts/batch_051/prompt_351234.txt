Been listening to a podcast where one of the hosts keeps reaching for "consciousness is an illusion" to settle arguments. Most recently the topic was whether a lobster, or a large language model, can actually suffer, and he shut it down with something like "that whole debate is confused, because consciousness is an illusion." I know there's a real position in philosophy of mind that goes by that name, but the slogan seems to do different work everytime it gets deployed: sometimes it means introspection is unreliable and confabulates, sometimes it means there's no little homunculus in the head watching a private show, and sometimes it sounds like the flat-out denial that there's any such thing as "what it's like." Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that one sentence could be making, and for each say whether it's a platitude even most realists about consciousness accept, a substantive but defensible claim, or probably false? The part I actually care about: which of these readings would actually support him treating questions about lobster or model suffering as confused, and does illusionism as philosophers actually defend it get him there, or is he smuggling in an extra move?
