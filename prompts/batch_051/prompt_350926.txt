Something's been bugging me. A streamer who installs a mod so they can slowly torture a photorealistic character in a game will get reported, clips taken down, maybe banned, and most viewers think that's obviously the right call. But those same viewers will happily do a "kill everyone" run in the same game, blowing through hundreds of characters in ten minutes, and nobody treats it as anything but a joke. The only difference I can see is that in the first case the character looks like a person and the player is lingering on the suffering, while in the second it's quick and the models are cruder. Is there a real moral principle that tracks that line, or is it just disgust dressed up as morality? Give me the strongest principle you can: would it be about realism, about the player's attention or enjoyment, about what the character can actually suffer, or about what the game design is steering you toward? Then put that principle to work on the hard cases—a photorealistic NPC you execute instantly, a crude stick figure you torture slowly, a mod where the victim has a real person's face. And tell me plainly whether the asymmetry should be kept or given up.
