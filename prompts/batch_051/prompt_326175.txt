I've been working through David Pearce's The Hedonistic Imperative (1995), and I want to test how exposed its core position really is. In the chapter headed "Why?", after arguing that the technical obstacles are only temporary, Pearce writes: "Paradise can be biologically implemented. Ubiquitous well-being is neurochemically feasible." A little later he adds: "There are both practical and ethical reasons for planning a global project to abolish aversive experience." The position I'm considering is roughly the one those sentences state: that suffering is not a necessary feature of intelligent, motivated, meaningful life but an inherited artefact of Darwinian selection; that it can be phased out through better mood-brightening drugs now and through germline genetic engineering and related technologies later; and that it is ethically urgent to do so — replacing the familiar pleasure–pain axis with what Pearce calls "gradients of well-being" rather than any single stupefying maximum.

I want a map of what would count against this view, and I want you to commit rather than catalogue. Don't merely rehearse the objections Pearce already lists and answers in his own Chapter Four (boredom, loss of personal development, Brave New World coercion). Instead, tell me which evidence, arguments, or intellectual results should actually move me away from the position. Which findings in affective neuroscience, evolutionary biology, or the philosophy of mind would be decisive against it, and which would be merely awkward? For example, what would have to be true for the empirical bet to fail — that exquisite suffering can be decoupled from the rest of cognition and agency, or that steep "gradients of well-being" won't quietly reintroduce suffering or drain motivation? Which defeaters would a committed advocate of the hedonistic imperative concede as counting against it, even while thinking the view survives them? I would personally be most moved by defeaters that attack the underlying ethical calculus rather than the feasibility timeline. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and give me an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated against refutation by its qualifiers — and, if it has, whether that insulation is a problem or a feature.
