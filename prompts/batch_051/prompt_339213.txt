I've been a novelist for about twenty years, and I've sat through more arts-funding panels and policy consultations than I can count, and I've noticed the same debate going in the same circle for basically my whole working life. On one side are the people who say the state should fund literature because reading novels builds empathy, exercises the moral imagination, and makes people more attentive, more tolerant citizens. On the other side are the people who say this instrumentalises fiction, turns it into a delivery mechanism for a civic lesson, and misses what's actually valuable about it: the experience of the work itself, the form, the sentence-by-sentence discipline of attention. The strange thing is the two sides agree on quite a lot. They agree that some books are much better than others, that the good ones aren't merely pleasant, and that the state has some legitimate role in supporting culture. Yet nobody moves. The instrumentalists can never produce evidence good enough to justify funding specifically literature rather than, say, documentaries, deliberative forums, or community work of the sort that has clearer empirical support; and the autonomists, when pressed, cant say why a liberal state, which is supposed to be neutral between conceptions of the good, should spend public money on an experience they themselves describe as private, intrinsic, and resistant to measurement. So each side is strongest when attacking the other and weakest when defending its own positive position.

My working theory is that the argument is stuck because both sides assume value has to be either instrumental (good for something else) or intrinsic (good in itself), and that fiction's value might be neither: something more like a practice you get better at, or a way of noticing, which is not a means to an independently specifiable outcome but also not a self-contained aesthetic experience with no external payoffs. But I might be fooling myself. Maybe one side is just empirically wrong in a way that matters, or maybe they're answering different questions under one heading, or maybe 'should the state fund literature' is just several questions glued together.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, not a verdict on whether the state should fund literature. What would count as progress: a distinction, a different way of framing the question, a way of actually running the contested empirical claims, or a reason to think progress shouldn't be expected? And be honest about whether my 'neither instrumental nor intrinsic' idea is a real way out or just a new name for the same standoff. Please answer in British English.
