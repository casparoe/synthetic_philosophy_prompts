Quick recap in case it helps: in the original setup, a chairman is told a new program will increase profits but also harm the environment; he says he doesn't care at all about harming the environment, he just wants profit. People rate him as having harmed the environment intentionally. In the mirror version, where the side effect helps the environment, people mostly say he did not help intentionally. The only thing that flips is weather the side effect is good or bad.

Here's what I want from you. I've been mulling over an unpublished twist and I want a real number, not an it-depends hedge. Suppose somebody runs the same design except the side effect is aesthetic rather than moral: the profitable plant will be hideously ugly from a famous viewpoint (in the good version, unexpectedly beautiful), and the CEO says he doesn't care about the view, he just wants profit. What's your credence that the asymmetry shows up anyway—that people rate him significantly more as intentionally making the view ugly than as intentionally making it beautiful, in the same direction as the original effect?

Decompose it. Give me the sub-questions your credence rides on: maybe how much of the Knobe effect is specifically about moral evaluation versus any negative evaluation at all, how much is about norm violation versus the trade-off structure that's confounded in the original vignettes, and how well ugliness functions as a norm violation in the first place. For each sub-question, give a rough probability and a line or two of justification, then show how you combine them into the headline number. Finally, tell me which sub-question you're least confident in, and what result from the aesthetic experiment—or a cleaner follow-up that untangles the trade-off confound—would move your credence the most.
