My working criteria for a legitimate reference class in outside-view forecasting are these. The class must be specified before the outcome is inspected, not selected to fit the known result. Membership must rest on features causally relevant to the target process, not on a label such as 'empire'. The class must be large enough and well-enough documented to yield a stable base rate rather than a handful of suggestive cases. Conditional on the defining features, the target case must be exchangeable with the other members: nothing known about it should make its prospects systematically different. The outcome variable must be defined sharply enough to be scored in every member (for example, 'loss of great-power status within n years').

Now consider this borderline case. A forecaster wants to estimate how long the contemporary United States will remain a great power by using the base rate of past great powers or empires since 1500 — the Habsburg, Ottoman, British, and Soviet cases, among others. Is the United States eligible for inclusion in that reference class? Go through each criterion in turn, and say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict. Attend especially to the start-date problem (does the series begin in 1776, 1865, 1945, or 1991?), to right-censoring (the United States is still in the game), and to whether 'great power' and 'empire' are doing real causal work or smuggling in a conclusion. End by saying whether the case shows that the criteria themselves need revision, and if so, what you would add or amend.
