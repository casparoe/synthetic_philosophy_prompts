I am putting together a graduate seminar unit on the gap between determinism and predictability, and I want to use a structured ranking exercise to force some distinctions that usually get blurred. The target of the exercise is the Laplacean picture: the claim that a complete and exact specification of the present state together with the true deterministic laws would let an ideal intellect compute the entire future. Against that picture, consider six candidate obstacles:

(a) Sensitive dependence on initial conditions, so that any finite error in specifying the present state grows without bound under the dynamics.
(b) Algorithmic incompressibility, where no model shorter than the system's own trajectory can stand in for that trajectory, so predicting it is no shorter than living through it.
(c) Initial-data specification, since a continuum of possible states may require an infinite amount of information to pin down one exact starting point.
(d) Computational intractability, where the transition from one state to the next is a computable function whose evaluation would take longer than any physically realizable machine has before the event itself.
(e) Predictive feedback, where the system contains agents who learn about predictions, so that issuing a prediction changes the very trajectory one is trying to predict.
(f) Non-uniqueness of evolution, where the dynamical equations admit more than one continuation from a single initial state, leaving nothing unique for even an ideal predictor to compute.

Please rank the six from weakest to strongest as challenges to the Laplacean picture, and assign each to one of three categories: merely practical (removed by enough precision, memory, or speed); principled epistemic (persisting even for an ideal predictor because the required information is not, even in principle, the sort that could be fully had); or metaphysical (undermining the claim that a unique future exists at all). For each item, give one or two sentences of justification for its place in the ranking, and flag any places where two items should really be counted as one or where the ranking depends on how literally one reads the ideal intellect's powers. I am more interested in the reasoning that sorts genuine threats to determinism from mere limits on finite predictors than in any single defensible ordering.
