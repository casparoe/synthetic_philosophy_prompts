I've been trying to pin down what makes some body of people 'the legitimate government' of a country rather than merely the de facto power that happens to be obeyed. Please propose a working criterion that handles these cases:

(a) A president dies in office and the vice-president is sworn in under a standing succession statute; nobody contests it.
(b) A military council seizes the ministries, suspends the constitution, but keeps most old statutes in force and continues running the courts and the tax office.
(c) After an inconclusive election, the outgoing cabinet remains in office as a caretaker for ten months, legally barred from passing new laws or signing treaties, while coalition talks fail.
(d) The constitution authorizes emergency decrees in time of war or insurrection but is silent about epidemics; the executive nonetheless invokes the clause to close borders and requisition hospital supplies.
(e) A foreign army occupies the capital and installs a provisional administration, while the ousted constitutional government meets in exile and remains the one recognized by other states.
(f) An epidemic kills the president, the vice-president, and everyone in the statutory line of succession; a committee of mayors and garrison officers begins issuing orders and is obeyed.
(g) After a revolution overthrows the old regime, a constituent assembly abrogates the old constitution and declares itself the provisional government until a new one is ratified.

Some of these feel like legitimate governments that cannot do much, and others like effective governments that are not legitimate, so I doubt the phrase tracks a single property. Propose a definition or criterion, test it against each case, and where it gives an answer you would resist, either defend the surprising verdict or revise the criterion. If you think two distinct properties are in play, say so and trace which cases turn on which.
