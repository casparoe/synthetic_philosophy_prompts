A friend and I spent last evening going in circles about whether the value of an original artwork is truly in the physical object or in something the object merely carries, and we kept talking past one another because we were imagining different degrees of perfect reproduction. Let me turn the disagreement into a precise counterfactual and ask you to reason it through, rather than referee our intuitions.

Suppose that in 2030 a low-cost machine becomes commercially available that can produce a duplicate of any bounded physical artifact — a painting, sculpture, manuscript, violin, fossil — that is indistinguishable from the original by every known mode of physical or chemical analysis, down to molecular and isotopic composition, accumulated damage, and age. The process cannot copy an object's causal history: a duplicate was not made by the original maker and was not present at the events that constitute the original's provenance. New works could in principle be registered at the moment of creation, but the existing stock of cultural objects could be identified only by documentary continuity of custody, and that record can be incomplete, contested, or falsified.

Give me your best prediction of how the following fifty years play out, in the form of a chain of reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints, not a story. Address at least four domains: (1) the art and collectibles market — do prices for originals collapse, hold, or diverge by category; (2) museums, auction houses, authentication experts, and courts — what institutions form or change; (3) ordinary aesthetic appreciation — does the original/copy distinction wither or sharpen; (4) the concept of forgery — does it shift from material deception to falsifying history. For each link in the argument, mark which predictions you regard as robust and which are guesses, and state what would have to be true about human psychology or institutional behavior for the prediction to fail.

My friend's view is that originals become worthless because the scarcity that prices tracked has been destroyed; my suspicion is that value migrates into the documentary record and the original becomes more like a deed than an object. I want a prediction that keeps both hypotheses live and tests them, not a verdict that simply picks one.
