The constitution of a republic contains two provisions. Article 28: "The powers of head of state may be exercised only by the person elected president under the electoral law." Article 36: "If the presidency falls vacant and no election can be completed, the Speaker of the Assembly shall exercise the powers of head of state until an election can be completed." No other provision addresses the vacancy, and the constitution contains no rule for resolving conflicts between its provisions. The president dies in office, the electoral commission declares that no election can be completed within the constitutional deadline, and the Speaker begins exercising the powers of head of state; a court must rule on whether this is lawful. Do the two provisions genuinely conflict, or can they be reconciled? Give the strongest candidate reconciliations — for instance, different senses of exercising the powers, a general-rule/emergency-exception relation, a distinction between the office and its temporary occupant — and say which reconciliation is a defensible construction of the constitution and which is special pleading. If you conclude they cannot honestly be reconciled, say what follows for the underlying problem: when the rules run out, who governs, and by what authority?
