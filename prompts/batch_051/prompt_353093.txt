Fair warning, I'm writing this still annoyed. A friend of mine -- biology postdoc, so he's convinced he owns this -- spent most of last night arguing the old line that natural selection is vacuous because 'fitness' just means whatever actually survived and reproduced. I kept pushing the dispositional reading: fitness as a probabilistic propensity, like solubility or fragility, that something can have even if it never gets expressed. His move was always that you can only ever observe the propensity through actual survival outcomes, so you're back in the tautology, and drift cases just break the whole picture. We didn't settle it, but it convinced me I want something for my blog: a 'which view of fitness do you actually hold?' quiz that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases rather than by which label they like. The positions I want as leaves: (1) actualist -- fitness is realized reproductive output, full stop, and selection talk is still fine; (2) propensity/dispositional; (3) statisticalist -- selection is a population-level statistical phenomenon, not a cause of individual success; (4) pluralist/contextualist -- what 'fitness' means depends on the explanatory job you're doing. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto those positions. Audience: smart blog readers who know a little evolution but no philosophy of biology. None of the questions should just ask 'do you think fitness is a propensity?' or drop the jargon.

I want you to commit: build the actual quiz rather than giving me three outlines and making me pick. Write out the questions, the options, the scoring key, and then defend the choices -- why these cases are the diagnostic ones, where they genuinely seperate the positions and where they can't. At least a couple of questions should force these splits: same trait type meeting different luck (two seeds, one on rock and one in soil), a trait that raises expected offspring but gets wiped out by a freak event before it ever pays off, drift acting on equally fit types, and maybe the changed-environment case where the same trait is fit here and not there. Also flag unoccupied combinations: if some answer pattern corresponds to a view nobody sane would hold, or two of my four positions collapse together under this format, say so instead of faking a distinction. And if a question can't separate two positions, don't paper over it. I'd rather have an honest quiz with a 'your position is unstable' outcome than a clean-looking one that sorts people wrong.
