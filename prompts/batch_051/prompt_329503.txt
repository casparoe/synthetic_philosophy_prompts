I sit on the research integrity committee of a large biomedical research institute. We must decide a concrete case: whether the evidence against a postdoctoral researcher reaches the "reasonable suspicion" threshold required to open a formal inquiry, with the serious reputational and career costs that an inquiry imposes regardless of its eventual outcome. The evidence is this. In a published results table, eleven consecutive entries in one column of the researcher's data are identical, digit for digit, to the entries in the corresponding column of an earlier paper by another group reporting a different cohort. Our statistical consultant estimates that two independent studies would produce that particular run by chance about once in five thousand tries. The researcher's defense is that the matching run was not pre-specified: an auditor scanned the institute's published output and settled on this alignment only after noticing it. Across hundreds of staff, thousands of tables, and many plausible ways of lining up columns, some near-miraculous match is almost guaranteed to appear somewhere — the lesson of the birthday paradox. Half the committee is taking the inside view of this particular match; the other half is taking the outside view of how many opportunities there were for some match to appear. I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — open a formal inquiry, or do not — and, if it recommends opening one, the evidentiary standard to apply. It should engage the strongest version of the losing side, working through how post-hoc selection, multiple comparisons, and the base rate of misconduct should alter our reading of the one-in-five-thousand figure. In particular, address whether the right question is how improbable this exact match was, or how improbable it was that some comparably striking match would turn up somewhere in the institute; and say where the line falls between a coincidence with a small numerator and a coincidence that genuinely supports opening an inquiry.
