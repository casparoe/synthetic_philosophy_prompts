I am not a student of philosophy; I have been reading Charles Peirce's essay The Fixation of Belief on my own and trying to think carefully about whether his four ways of settling opinion really cover the territory. The four, as I understand them, are these: the method of tenacity, where a person keeps an opinion by refusing to consider what might unsettle it; the method of authority, where an institution fixes the correct opinions and shields them from disturbing alternatives; the a priori method, where opinions are fixed by what seems natural, elegant, or reasonable to reflective taste; and the method of science, where an opinion is fixed by inquiry into something external and permanent, so that inquirers with different starting points are pulled toward the same conclusion.

I want a candidate fifth method of fixing belief that is not any of those four and is not a special case, renaming, or combination of them. It should be a real way a person or community could actually settle what to believe, not an artificial hybrid. Then tell me honestly: is it genuinely distinct, or does it ultimately fall back under one of the four headings? Would it survive the test Peirce uses to judge the four — namely, would it remain usable once its adherents notice that other people, starting from other places, fix their beliefs differently and just as confidently? And is it plausible that some actual community has used it as its dominant method? Please also separate what is settled about Peirce's taxonomy from what is genuinely contested among people who study him, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus view.
