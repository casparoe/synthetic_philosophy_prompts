Write a dialogue between two philosophers about the knowledge argument against physicalism. The scenario: Mary has spent her entire life in a black-and-white room and has learned every physical fact about human color vision from books and a black-and-white screen; on leaving the room, she sees something red for the first time. One speaker, the proponent, argues that Mary learns a new fact — what it is like to see red — and therefore that not all facts are physical facts. The other, the physicalist, must resist this with the strongest available replies rather than by flatly denying the intuition: for example, that Mary gains only a new ability to recognize, imagine, and remember red, or a new way of thinking about a fact she already knew instead of a new fact. The proponent should answer those replies directly, not merely reassert the intuition.

Neither speaker should be a strawman. Each must respond to the other's actual point. At some stage, the physicalist should challenge whether Mary's surprise is evidence about the world or only about her prior concepts, and the proponent should challenge whether a merely new way of thinking can explain the sense that descriptive information was missing without simply being a new fact in disguise. Each speaker should make at least one genuine concession or refinement by the end. Do not let a narrator settle the debate; end with the disagreement sharper than it began.

Present the piece as a dialogue with named speakers and no narrator. Maximum 700 words.
