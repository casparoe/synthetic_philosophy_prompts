Ok so I'm in a formal epistemology course and we just went over van Fraassen's Reflection principle, which our prof presented as a diachronic coherence thing. Then I found this explainer online (not a course reading) and I've reread it like four times because something in it feels subtly off but I cant quite say what. Here's how it puts it:

"Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says that a rational person's current degree of belief in a proposition must equal the degree of belief they expect to have later. The underlying thought is that your future self will have gathered more evidence and had more time to think, so your future self is systematically in a better epistemic position than you are now; deferring to that better-informed self is just good epistemic hygiene. Formally, for any proposition H, P_now(H) should equal the expected value of P_later(H), where the expectation covers everything you might learn in between. A striking consequence is that you can never rationally predict that your own views will change: the moment you notice that you expect to believe something different tomorrow, coherence requires you to switch to that tomorrow-view today. So really Reflection and conditionalization end up being the same requirement, just stated from different directions — conditionalization says your new probability is your old probability conditional on the total evidence you gained, and Reflection says you should lock in those updated probabilities in advance by deferring to your future self."

I keep coming back to cases where my future self is drunk, sleep-deprived, or has just been given a forgetfulness pill that will make them hold some credence for terrible reasons — why would coherence force me to defer to a future self I have no reason to trust? But the explainer says it like its just obvious, and maybe I'm the one confusing things. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what's accurate, what's subtly distorted, and whether the "you can never rationally predict your views will change" consequence actually follows? I'd rather not write my final paper on the back of an explainer that's quietly running Reflection and conditionalization together.
