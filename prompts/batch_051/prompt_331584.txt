I’ve been turning a problem over in the metaphysics of propositions for longer than I care to admit, and I’ve ended up with a small homegrown view that I want someone to be properly unkind about. You know the Bradley regress: if the unity of a simple proposition like Fa has to be supplied by some further ingredient — an instantiation relation, a copula, a big-C Connection — then that ingredient has to be connected to a and F in turn, and off we go. My thought is that the regress only gets going because we keep positing a third thing to do the joining. So here’s the view, which I’ve been calling the dovetail view. The predicate is not a self-standing component that needs glueing to the name. Think of it as a socket or a notch, and the name as a tab or tongue; the unity of the proposition just is the tab seated in the notch, the way a dovetail joint holds without any glue. There is no further item in the inventory, because the fit is intrinsic to the shapes of the two pieces. In yesterday’s seminar a first-year asked whether I’d simply rediscovered Frege’s unsaturatedness in woodwork vocabulary, and I genuinely don’t know wether that’s the right diagnosis, a fair cop, or beside the point.

What I’d like is a candid assessment rather than a sympathetic reading. Is the dovetail view an actual position, or just a metaphor doing all the work? Does it stop the regress at the first step, or does Bradley reappear one level up — since someone now has to explain why the tab and the notch are complementary? And tell me where it breaks: I have a nagging feeling that false propositions, and relations with more than one slot, both make trouble, but I can’t make the trouble precise.

Don’t just wrap up in prose. After your assessment, give me a table with these columns: (1) the part of the view being appraised; (2) the nearest existing idea from the literature, if there is one; (3) the sharpest objection you can formulate against it; (4) how bad it is — cosmetic, serious, or fatal; and (5) whether it can be patched, and at what cost. Finish with a final row for the view as a whole, with your overall verdict in the severity column and “keep, patch, or bury” in the final column.
