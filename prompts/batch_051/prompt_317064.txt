I'm not a philosopher, but I end up in arguments about why people believe things, and I keep noticing a move I dont have a word for. Someone says a theory is 'the best explanation' of the evidence. Asked why it's best, they list features like simplicity, neatness, scope, how satisfying the story would be if it were true. But those all seem like virtues of the story, not reasons to think the story is true; I can easily imagine a clunky, ugly explanation that happens to be right. Last week a friend defended a diagnosis because it made all the symptoms hang together in one tidy narrative, even though it needed three rare things to happen at once, while nobody had ruled out a messier mix of two common conditions. Other times people say 'best explanation' when they just mean 'the only candidate anyone has thought of so far', even if they admit it's a bad one. So the phrase slides between two or three different things. Is there an established name for that slide, and for the gap between an explanation's being pleasing or powerful and its being likely? Does the only-candidate-on-the-table case count as inference to the best explanation at all, or is it something else? I dont just want a label; I want to know exactly what gets conflated, whether people who work on this mostly agree about the distinction or still argue over it, and where the boundary is. Please flag what's settled and what's genuinely contested, and dont present one side as the consensus if it isn't. Write in British English. Hard cap: 300 words.
