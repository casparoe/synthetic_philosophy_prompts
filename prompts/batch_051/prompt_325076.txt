Consider two people rescued from a desert by an armed stranger on the strength of the same promise, each of whom later pays the stranger exactly what was promised, although the stranger has no remaining leverage and they will never meet again. The first pays unreflectively because he is simply someone who keeps promises. The second saw years earlier that he might face a Parfit-style hitchhiker and deliberately cultivated a durable promise-keeping disposition, judging that the occasional unrecoverable loss of paying a powerless stranger was worth remaining rescuable. Suppose the two dispositions are behaviorally indistinguishable—same act, same cost, same counterfactual behavior—and neither man could say from the inside whether his character was formed or chosen. Ordinary judgment seems to esteem the first as a person of integrity while regarding the second as having somehow missed the point. Is this asymmetry defensible? If you defend it, defend it against the case where the engineered disposition persists after no further rescue scenarios remain; if you reject it, say whether we should regard the second as just as virtuous, more rational, or both.
