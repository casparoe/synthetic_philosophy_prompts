I have no academic background, but for the past year I have been thinking on my own about how a person born blind understands shapes. The question began for me with my younger sibling, who was born blind: I kept wondering what she would see, and what she would recognize, if her sight could be restored. In trying to think this through, I came across Molyneux's problem, and then an article for a general audience that summarized it as follows:

"Molyneux's problem asks whether a person born blind, who has learned to distinguish a cube from a sphere by touch, could tell which was which immediately upon gaining sight. Locke answered that she could not. His reasoning was that the newly sighted person would have tactile ideas of the cube and the sphere but would have no visual experience to connect them to, so the two shapes would at first be no more than new and unfamiliar visual appearances. Modern studies of patients whose sight has been restored after long blindness have confirmed this prediction: such patients cannot recognize even simple shapes by sight at first, and must gradually learn to link what they see with what they touch. These results are widely taken to show that perception is learned rather than innate, which in turn supports the empiricist view that the contents of the mind come from experience."

I found the summary plausible, but I have the uneasy sense that the move from "such patients cannot immediately recognize shapes" to "perception is learned rather than innate" is bigger than the article lets on. Because I have no training in philosophy or vision science, I cannot tell whether this unease is sound or whether I am just missing something.

I would appreciate a careful audit of the summary. Please go through it claim by claim and tell me which parts are accurate, which are simplifications that hide something important, which are overstated or wrong, and whether the concluding step about empiricism actually follows from what precedes it. I would also like to know what the summary leaves out that a more careful treatment of Molyneux's problem would need to include.
