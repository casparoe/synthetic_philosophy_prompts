Last night I had a long argument with a friend who works on the history of early modern mathematics. We kept circling the question of whether it is legitimate to describe past mathematics and science in our terms — whether one may say that Archimedes anticipated the integral calculus, or that Euclid's number theory is an early form of abstract algebra. I came away from the argument with a list of commitments I apparently hold, and I would like to know whether they can all be held at once before I present them in a seminar. They are:

(1) When we read past mathematics and science, our first obligation is to recover the concepts, problems, and standards of the historical actors in their own terms, and not to treat their work as a clumsy or partial version of ours.
(2) Some historical work really does stand in recognizable continuity with later results: Archimedes' method of exhaustion and the modern definite integral are not merely the same words applied to different things, and it is not false to say that Archimedes was doing something recognizably like integration.
(3) Scientific and mathematical progress is real: later frameworks can do things earlier ones could not, and not every conceptual change is just a shift of fashion.
(4) In teaching, it is often right to present old results in modern notation and modern terms, because refusing to do so would bury the mathematics under historical vocabulary and lose the students.
(5) There is no neutral, present-free standpoint from which to write history; our current questions inevitably guide which past episodes we find interesting and how we describe them.
(6) The internal/external distinction in the history of science is untenable: intellectual content cannot be cleanly separated from the social and material conditions in which it was produced.

I can feel that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot tell which of the apparent conflicts are real, which are merely differences of level or context, and which single revision would do the most work. Please audit the set: tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Then tell me which one change would make the whole set most coherent.
