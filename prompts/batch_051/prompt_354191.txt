I'm putting together a short position paper on whether image generators are creative, and I've ended up with a list of commitments that I'm not sure all fit togther. Can you audit this before I submit?

(1) Creativity is a property of the process that made the work, not just of the final object.
(2) A painter working recognizably within a tradition — say, doing quiet still lifes in a style you could trace back through a dozen earlier painters — is creative, even if every formal device has an identifiable influence.
(3) The sampler in a diffusion model is basically gradient descent toward regions of a learned distribution, so its process is not creative; it isn't 'making' anything in the sense that matters.
(4) A human artist who uses that same model with a specific vision, iterating over seeds, inpainting, compositing, and overpainting, is being creative.
(5) If the exact same final image came out of one generic prompt with no further human involvement, it would not be a creative work, even though it's pixel-identical to the one made in (4).
(6) Creativity doesn't require full self-awareness or propositional knowledge; a child can be creative without knowing much about the domain.
(7) I dont want to deny AI creativity merely because sampling is stochastic — random variation plus selection is part of ordinary human creativity too.
(8) I also think there's a real difference between exploring an existing style-space and transforming it, and only the latter deserves the name 'deep' creativity; off-the-shelf generators only explore.

I honestly can't tell where the weak point is. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad are the real ones? Rank them by severity, say what has to give in each case, and tell me if any one revision fixes the lot.
