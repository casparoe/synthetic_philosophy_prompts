I've watched people talk past each other about this on the forums for years, and I still can't tell whether its an empirical claim about where selection causally acts or just a very handy bit of bookkeeping. Dawkins and Williams make it sound like organisms are vehicles and genes are the real replicators, but when I try to cash that out it starts to sound identical to "allele frequencies change," which is just population genetics and nobody disagrees with that. So here's what I want. Design the best test you can of the claim that the gene, not the organism, is the level at which selection really acts. Give me a concrete setup — an experiment or a natural observation — the result that would favor the gene-level reading, the result that would favor the organism-level reading, and what an unambigous winner would have to look like. Then tell me what the test cannot settle even in principle. My worry is that any empirical result can be redescribed at either level, and that "the real unit of selection" is doing notation, not causal work. If you think that's right, separate the part of the claim that is testable from the part that is just a choice about how to count. I've seen the usual moves (segregation distorters, selfish genetic elements, the group selection back-and-forth), so don't just list examples; give me the logic of a decisive test, or explain why a decisive test is impossible.
