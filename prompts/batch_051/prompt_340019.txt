I help run a games club at our local community centre, about a dozen regulars, mostly 14 to 17. Its supposed to be board games and logic puzzles, but every few weeks I try to sneak in something that messes with their heads a bit. Recently I've been chewing on an old gambling puzzle: you flip a coin until it comes up heads, and if the first heads is on the nth flip you win £2^n. So tails-heads pays £4, tails-tails-heads pays £8, and so on. If you work out the fair price of a ticket, the maths says you should pay any amount at all, because the average win is infinite — each extra flip doubles the prize but halves the chance, so it adds a pound forever. But if I actually offered the kids that ticket, nobody would pay more than about a fiver, me included. I tried to explain the calculation once and they just said the game was obviously rigged and went back to Uno.

I want to run a one-hour session where they discover this themselves instead of being told. The room has a whiteboard, some coins, and a cash box with small change. Could you design an activity for the club — a game with real (small-stakes) rules — where they first play or price the lottery themselves, write down what they'd pay, and then stumble onto the infinite calculation by working out the payouts for longer and longer runs. Then I'd like a debrief that helps them see the two big things: why the infinite price is genuinely weird given that no sane person would pay it, and why the infinite number might be misleading (something to do with nobody having infinite money on the table, and a pound gained meaning less when you already have thousands). I don't want any lecture at the start — I've tried that and it kills it. Tell me how to set it up step by step, roughly how long each bit takes, what usually goes wrong when people run this, and be honest about which part of the idea the game cant actually show and I'll have to say in my own words at the end.
