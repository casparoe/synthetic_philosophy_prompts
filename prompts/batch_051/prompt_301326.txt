I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the epistemology of AI oversight, with sixteen students and one seventy-five-minute session. Design a structured classroom game that makes the students feel, rather than hear about, the core worry behind the proposal to make AI systems safe by having them debate one another in front of a human judge: that a fluent, confident, subtly misleading argument can beat a true but plain argument before a competent judge, and that this threatens the proposal at a level that cannot just be patched. The students should not be told the lesson up front. The game should assign some students as debaters, at least one as judge, and give the side defending the false claim a role or incentive structure that predictably pushes it toward obfuscation. Include rules, a scoring rule that does not require real money, a timeline, and required materials. Then provide a debrief that maps specific moments in the game to obfuscated arguments, the judge's epistemic position, and the limits of debate as an oversight mechanism. Also tell me what will most likely go wrong the first time I run it and how to adjust, and which feature of the actual AI-debate proposal the classroom game cannot reproduce and the debrief must therefore supply.
