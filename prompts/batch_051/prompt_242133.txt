Take Warren Quinn's self-torturer setup from "The Puzzle of the Self-Torturer" and run a counterfactual for me. You know the basic case: 10,000 dial settings, each increment gives a modest cash payout and an imperceptible pain increase, each step looks individually worth it, but the guy would pay a lot to avoid going from 0 to 10,000 in one jump. Near the start of the paper there's a move where Quinn says the puzzle isn't just about failing to notice pains; even if the man's capacity to discriminate were improved, the preference reversal could remain. I want to hold onto that move. Now suppose the dial is attached to a whole political party's platform: at each party convention the platform can move one notch toward harsher immigration enforcement, each step gets a marginal electoral benefit and adds a bit of cruelty that no single advocacy group can reliably distinguish. The party starts at 0. It cannot move down without admitting it was wrong, but at the end it would pay hugely to be back at 0 rather than 10,000. Predict, with reasoning, the equilibrium trajectory over twenty conventions, what internal factions emerge to precommit the party against the next step, and whether the case is best read as intransitive party preferences or as a coordination failure among factions. Then say what evidence would make you reverse that diagnosis, and where your prediction is least stable.
