We're doing karma in my philosophy of religion module right now, and I'm trying to figure out whether I can defend anything like karma as a *moral* principle about desert, rather than just a "what goes around comes around" vibe. Here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I want to know if there's a priniciple behind them.

A two-year-old is born with a condition that means she's in constant pain for her whole short life. I can't accept that this is deserved because of something in a past life. Even if I grant rebirth just for the sake of argument, it still feels wrong to say she deserved it.

A businessman quietly defrauds his employees out of their pensions, never gets caught, and dies at 90 surrounded by family. My gut says he has it coming, and if it doesn't happen in this life, then it should happen in a next one. I know this might clash with the first case, but I can't shake it.

A guy who, as a teenager, drove drunk and permanently injured someone, then spent decades doing victims' advocacy and living modestly. I don't think he still has a karmic debt left to pay.

An elderly woman was genuinely cruel to her children when she was younger, but now has advanced dementia and can't remember any of it or understand why she's in a bad nursing home. Punishing her now seems pointless and wrong.

A guy born into enormous wealth who is selfish and contemptuous with it but never breaks the law. I don't think he deserves his position, but I also don't feel the need for cosmic correction the way I did with the businessman.

The closest I've gotten is something like: you deserve suffering only if you can remember choosing the act, and only up to what the act deserved. But that obviously kills the businessman case, and I'm not sure I want to give that up.

Is there one principle about karma and desert that gets all or most of these? Where does my tentative principle go wrong, and can it be repaired? Also tell me what your principle says about cases I haven't thought through: someone who causes the same serious harm by pure accident versus on purpose, someone who suffers a lot early in life and then prospers, and someone who does a genuinely good deed but only to burn off bad karma.

For the answer, please use these sections in this order:
(1) Candidate principle(s) — one or two precise statements, about 3 sentences total.
(2) Case-by-case fit — one sentence per case on how the principle handles it.
(3) New cases — what the principle predicts for the accidental-vs-deliberate harm, early-suffering-then-prospering, and instrumentally-good-deed cases; 4–6 sentences.
(4) Verdicts I'd have to drop — which of my gut reactions can't survive if I want a simple principle, and what a slightly more complicated one would cost; 3–4 sentences.
(5) Bottom line — the principle you think I should actual walk away with, 2 sentences.
