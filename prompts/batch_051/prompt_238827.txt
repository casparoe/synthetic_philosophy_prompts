Two colleagues of mine, Ali and Bea, were arguing about a mutual friend who says every week that she wants to quit the video app she scrolls for two hours each night but never does. Ali said the behavior settles it: if she keeps choosing to scroll, then scrolling is what she preferred, whatever she said afterward — utility is just what choice reveals, and there is no further mental fact to check. Bea said that move drains the word of content: if preference is defined as whatever was chosen, then the claim that she acts on her preferences becomes unfalsifiable, and a theory that can rationalize any behavior as maximizing some constructed utility explains nothing. Ali replied that this is not a bug but the point of revealed preference — economics abandoned measuring inner states because they are unobservable, and choice is the only non-circular evidence of preference. Bea replied that refusing to count inner states leaves no room for weakness of will, addiction, or regret, which only make sense if utility is a real condition of the person that choices can fail to reflect. Neither convinced the other. Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: for each of the four moves, say whether it is right or wrong and why, and then say who is more right overall and what both of them are missing.
