Here's a proposal I keep hearing at town council meetings, and I genuinely can't model how it would play out. The city would stop paying police-misconduct settlements and use-of-force judgments out of the general fund, and would pay them instead out of the police pension fund. On top of that, any officer with more than three use-of-force reports in two years triggers a fixed weekly charge to the pension fund — I don't know, maybe a few hundred dollars — that keeps getting deducted until the officer has two clean years on their record, regardless of whether any lawsuit is ever filed. The slogan is that the people who create the liability should eat it, and that officers now have a direct stake in each others restraint because one officer's bad shooting shrinks everyone's retirement.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a slogan. Walk me through the incentives it creates: for patrol officers, for the union, for the city council, for plaintiffs' lawyers, and for the pension board, over maybe a decade. Who actually ends up bearing the cost? Where does it get gamed first? And can the most obvious gaming be fixed with a small tweak, or does every fix create a bigger failure somewhere else?
