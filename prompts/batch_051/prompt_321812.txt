After our lesson on animal behavior, two friends of mine got into an argument about a video in which a crow drops stones into a narrow tube of water until a floating piece of food rises within reach. Maya said the bird was doing something close to causal reasoning: the setup was novel, the bird adjusted its choices rather than running through a trained routine, and the best explanation is that it grasps that dropping a heavy object raises the water level. Eli countered with Morgan's canon: we should not attribute a higher psychological faculty to an animal when a lower one would explain the behavior, and here trial-and-error learning or a simple association between dropping objects and getting food could cover the case without attributing understanding. Maya replied that the canon assumes a tidy ladder from lower to higher faculties, but the line between associative learning and understanding is not tidy, and a rule that always prefers the lower explanation stacks the deck against recognizing novel problem solving. Eli said the canon is not a claim about what the bird actually thinks; it is a guardrail against anthropomorphism, and giving it up returns us to reading rich mental lives into every clever animal video. Maya answered that a guardrail is useful only until it instructs us to prefer an explanation we have no positive reason to accept; at that point it is not scientific caution but bias. They could not agree and asked me to referee. Please go through the exchange move by move, say who is right about which specific points and what each is missing, and then say who is more right overall.
