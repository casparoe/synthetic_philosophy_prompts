I am drafting a discussion document for my engineering team on why colleagues who visibly succeed are sometimes celebrated and sometimes quietly undermined, and I want the center of the document to be a comparison between two rival ways of handling people who stand out.

The first is the American Dream, understood not as a claim about economic mobility but as a stance about success itself: individual striving and success are admirable, rising above others through effort is healthy, and conspicuous achievement should function as a model to emulate rather than a threat to manage.

The second is the cluster of norms found in the Scandinavian Law of Jante (Jantelagen) and, in a more hostile form, the tall poppy syndrome discussed in Australia and New Zealand: standing out is a problem to be kept in check, people who rise too far above their peers should be reminded not to think themselves special, and conspicuous success is something to cut down or resent because it threatens a shared equality.

Treat these as competing social technologies for managing status, merit, and envy, not as mere cultural moods. One variable I want the comparison to press on is whether the underlying danger these norms are trying to control is one and the same: is the threat the successful person's self-presentation and resulting hubris, or is it the envy and status anxiety that success provokes in everyone else? Or do the two norms differ precisely in which of those they take to be the real problem?

Question: which of the two is more defensible as a norm a community could explicitly endorse, if the community values both individual excellence and social cohesion? Give a clear verdict rather than a survey: defend one side, restate and answer the strongest objection to that side, and specify the conditions under which the other side wins. Address explicitly whether Jantelagen and the tall poppy syndrome belong together in this comparison or differ in a way that matters.

Please do not give me a neutral summary. If my framing of the comparison is confused — for example, if the question of which norm is more defensible only makes sense relative to a goal I have not specified, or if I have lumped together things that should be kept apart, or if the contrast between celebrating and cutting down success rests on a false dichotomy — tell me so directly and redo the comparison on the corrected framing, rather than politely producing an answer to the question as I happened to put it.
