For the philosophy of math reading group I'm in I'd like a "which view do you actually hold?" sorting quiz, with a scoring key, on whether it matters which root of x² + 1 = 0 we call i and which we call −i. The quiz should sort people by what they believe rather than by what labels they already know, so phrase every node as a concrete claim or case someone can answer without knowing the position names. Build it from the logical space of answers first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and then locate the actual positions in that space; for any combination nobody occupies, say whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Use at least these cases as diagnostic hooks: the two complex roots of x² + 1 (no ordering on C to pick one out), the two real roots of x² − 2 (where the usual ordering on the reals gives you a "positive" root for free), and, if it earns its keep, the three cube roots of unity (one real, the other two conjugate). One branch should press on whether the respondent treats the "positive root of x² − 2" as a model for non-arbitrary labeling, and whether they think the complex plane has anything analogous — like an orientation — that could make labeling non-arbitrary. The leaves must include at least: someone who thinks exactly one of the two roots really is i, but our notation just can't pin down which; someone who thinks there's no fact of the matter until you fix a construction (like C as ordered pairs), after which the choice is an artifact of the construction; someone who thinks the choice is pure convention, harmless, and not worth losing sleep over; and someone who thinks swapping the labels gives you the oppositely oriented plane, so the two labelings pick out two genuinely different structures rather than the same structure twice. Flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't really seperate, and say why. Keep it to roughly six questions.
