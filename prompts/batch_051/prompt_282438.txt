Write a sonnet about Dennett's careenium and simmballs thought experiment—the one where a thing moves through the world because of lots of little simmballs bouncing around inside it, and the question is whether the higher-level pattern of the whole (the way it seems to steer, the thing it looks like its trying to do) does any real causal work over and above all the dumb local bumping of the balls. The octave should build the reductionist voice: all there is is simmballs obeying local rules, each one blind to the whole, and it sounds like a category mistake to say the pattern pushes them around. The volta should flip this, not by conjuring a ghost in the machine, but by making the reader feel that the pattern is a real pattern—something you couldnt predict or explain without talking about, even though it isnt an extra force. The closing couplet should land on exactly what real pattern means in a way Dennett would nod at: not a thing that shoves, but a feature of the trajector of all those little parts, just as real as a center of gravity, and just as legitimately called a cause once you stop thinking all causes have to be pushes. I want a poem a philosopher of mind who knows this stuff would find right, not wince at.
