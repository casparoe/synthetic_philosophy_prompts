Act as a senior professional philosopher specializing in information ethics, the metaphysics of ownership, and precedent-based reasoning. Work through the problem carefully, and begin by brainstorming the candidate principles that could rationalize the track record below before testing them against the new proposal.

I am a philosophy professor, and I chair my university's standing Data Ethics Committee. Over the past five years, our committee has issued the following rulings, which we now treat as precedents:

(1) We approved a streaming service's use of individual listening histories to personalize song recommendations, on the condition that the recommendation system could be disabled by the user at any time.

(2) We rejected a hospital's transfer of de-identified but re-identifiable patient records to a life insurer's risk-rating subsidiary. Patients had signed a broad admissions form mentioning possible research or administrative uses, but we held that this did not constitute meaningful notice or consent for insurer risk scoring.

(3) We approved a city transit authority's use of aggregated, postcode-level location data from a mobility app to redraw bus routes, with no additional consent sought, on the ground that no individual was identified or singled out by the aggregate.

(4) We rejected a start-up's plan to scrape publicly viewable photographs from a large university's social media pages to train a facial-recognition model. We did not treat the fact that the photographs were visible to the public as equivalent to their being offered for commercial biometric training.

(5) We approved a medical researcher's use of symptom diaries from a chronic-illness management app, with fresh opt-in consent from each participant, to study how symptoms fluctuate over time, with the data held on university-controlled servers and used only for that study.

The proposal before us next week is this. A mental-health company runs a popular therapy-chat app that connects users to licensed counsellors. It now wants to use the full transcripts of therapy sessions, after de-identification, to train a general-purpose empathetic customer-service assistant that it will license to retailers, airlines, and banks. The app's terms of service prominently disclose this future use; the app is free and supported by this data reuse; and users must click to accept these terms to use the service at all. There are competing therapy apps that do not reuse session content.

We have to decide whether the proposal should be approved, rejected, or approved with conditions, if we want to be maximally consistent with our own precedents. Please give me: (a) the candidate principles that fit our five earlier rulings, with a brief note on how each handles them; (b) what each principle would predict for the therapy-chat proposal; (c) your best recommendation for the Committee, including which of the five earlier rulings is the closest analog and why; (d) an honest statement of what principle we would be committing ourselves to under each possible choice; and (e) a further case, not yet on our record, whose decision would best force us to settle which principle we are actually following.
