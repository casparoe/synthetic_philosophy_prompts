I’ve been rereading the sections on music in Schopenhauer’s The World as Will and Representation, and I keep getting stuck on the claim that music is fundamentally different from every other art. According to him, the other arts copy the Ideas—the stable grades of the will’s objectification—but music never represents anything in the phenomenal world at all. It is supposed to be a direct copy of the will itself, the blind, endless striving behind all appearances, which is why it affects us so immediately without the mediation of concepts or images. I want a short story that takes this literally.

Imagine a world where Schopenhauer’s metaphysics is true, and the structure of music really is a direct aperture onto the undifferentiated Will. Musicians are trained not to produce pleasant objects but to open and close that aperture without being consumed by it. The story should show the practical shape of that skill: what a practice session looks like, what a performer experiences when a phrase goes slightly wrong, what institutions grow up to contain or exploit the raw striving that music lets through, and what happens to someone who becomes a little too good at it. The plot should turn on the actual content of the theory—music as direct expression of Will rather than of ideas, emotions, or representations—not on a generic music-as-magic premise. I’d especially like it if the story treats pessimism as a live technical problem: if music gives access to the blind force for which all suffering is a symptom, then performing it well might be both the highest art and the most dangerous act a person can undertake.
