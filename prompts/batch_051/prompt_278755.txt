I've been trying to get a consistent epistemological position on skepticism and closure, and I'd like an audit of whether the following commitments hang together.

1. I'm a fallibilist: a belief can count as knowledge even when its basis doesn't entail its truth.
2. I accept a Nozick-style tracking requirement: S knows p on basis B only if, were p false, S using B would not believe p; and were p true, S using B would believe p.
3. I think this yields the right verdict on skeptical hypotheses: I know I have hands, but I don't know I'm not a handless brain in a vat, because the first belief is sensitive and the second isn't.
4. I've made peace with the consequence that knowledge isn't closed under known entailment; the supposedly abominable conjunction of knowing I have hands while not knowing I'm not a handless brain in a vat strikes me as a feature, not a fatal cost.
5. All the same, I think competent deduction from known premises generally produces knowledge: if I know the cheese is in the fridge, and I know that entails it's not in the cupboard, then having drawn the inference I know it's not in the cupboard.
6. I also think knowledge is closed under known logical equivalence: if I know p and recognize that p is equivalent to q, then I'm in a position to know q.
7. I think Gettier cases are not knowledge because the belief is right only through luck, and I take sensitivity to be the right way to state what's missing.

Please audit the set: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision that would restore coherence and what it would cost.
