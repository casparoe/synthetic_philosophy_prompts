In my philosophy of well-being course, our professor closed a lecture on novelty with this summary:

"There is now a lot of evidence that people adapt to positive changes: the happiness boost from a new hobby, a new city, or even a new relationship is mostly temporary, and people drift back toward a stable personality-linked baseline. So novelty is not itself a component of well-being; at most, it is instrumentally useful for keeping boredom and restlessness at bay. The fact that people still crave adventure does not show that novelty is good for them, because wanting and liking are dissociable, and the wanting system can run ahead of, and outrun, the liking system. Once you see that novelty is usually a matter of wanting rather than liking, there is no reason to put variety or adventure on an objective-list theory of well-being. People who call novelty an intrinsic good are confusing the short-lived thrill of exploration with the things that actually make a life go well, such as meaning and close relationships."
