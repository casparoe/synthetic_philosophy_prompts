I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the methodology of cause prioritization. I have been reconstructing the implicit decision rule of a small foundation that scores every proposed cause on a 1–5 scale, with 1 least and 5 most favorable, along three dimensions: importance, the expected scale of the problem if unaddressed; tractability, the expected marginal progress per dollar; and neglectedness, the inverse of current attention from other funders. The foundation never states how it combines the three scores. Its recent decisions are as follows.

Funded:
- A campaign to phase out a widely used but replaceable industrial solvent: Importance 4, Tractability 3, Neglectedness 5.
- A program to mandate basic vehicle safety standards in low-income countries: Importance 5, Tractability 3, Neglectedness 4.
- A project to digitize land tenure records to reduce property disputes: Importance 4, Tractability 4, Neglectedness 3.

Rejected:
- A microcredit expansion in a market already saturated by donor programs: Importance 4, Tractability 4, Neglectedness 2.
- A project to develop a direct-air-capture technology with unresolved basic-science obstacles: Importance 4, Tractability 2, Neglectedness 5.
- A pilot to reduce post-harvest losses for a single crop in a few districts: Importance 3, Tractability 4, Neglectedness 3.
- A treatment program for a condition with a small disease burden and only moderately effective known interventions: Importance 3, Tractability 3, Neglectedness 4.

Next month the foundation will decide on a proposal to treat a moderately burdensome but currently almost unaddressed parasitic infection with a proven, low-cost drug regimen, scored Importance 3, Tractability 5, Neglectedness 5.

I want to predict that decision from the foundation's revealed behavior rather than from its public statements. What candidate aggregation rules are consistent with all seven past decisions, stated precisely enough to apply to the new proposal? What verdict does each candidate rule imply for the new proposal? Which of them do you think is the best bet, with what confidence and why? Finally, which further proposal would best discriminate among the rules that remain live after the new decision, and what would each possible verdict on that further proposal tell us?
