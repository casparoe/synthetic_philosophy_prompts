I'm part of a philosophy of biology reading group, and last week we stalled out on the familiar charge that natural selection is empty. One member kept invoking "survival of the fittest" as if it settled the matter: ask who survives and the answer is "the fittest"; ask who the fittest are and the answer is "those who survive"; so the principle just goes in a circle. Another member replied that fitness was never meant to be a matter of actual survival alone, but a probabilistic disposition—something like expected reproductive output—and that once you read the slogan that way, the charge falls apart. We argued for an hour without ever clearly separating the slogan from the theory behind it.

I'd like to come back next week with a clean breakdown. What are the distinct, precise claims "survival of the fittest" could be expressing, from the trivially true to the substantive to the probably false? For each reading, say whether it is tautological, and—this is the part I most need—whether that reading is the one the "natural selection is a tautology" objection actually requires in order to go through, and which reading or readings are doing the real work in modern evolutionary explanation. For the readings where you take a stand, tell me which further argument or empirical finding would change your verdict, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
