I got turned around by a friend's reconstruction of the old debate between Mozi's impartial care and Confucian graded love, and I cant find the mistake. (1) Mozi says a stranger's father has exactly the same claim on my care as my own father. (2) But the care that actually keeps an old person alive and sane — keeping track of their meds, showing up when they're low, noticing something's off before they say so — depends on years of local knowledge and partial attention that only special relationships reliably produce. (3) If everyone followed impartial care, my father would lose the one person positioned to give him that sort of care, and in exchange he'd get a thinner, spread-out concern from many people who don't know him. (4) So impartial care, if everyone followed it, would leave each parent worse off, not better. (5) A principle that leaves everyone worse off when universally followed is the wrong principle. (6) So graded love beats impartial care. I want to keep Mozi — or at least some version of impartiality — but I also think (5) is doing a lot of work, since it seems to license whatever partiality happens to 'work', like clannishness or chauvinism. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the argument is valid, what am I giving up by staying a Mohist?
