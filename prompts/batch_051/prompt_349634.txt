I'm writing up my position on laws of nature for a dissertation and I'd like a consistency check before I send it off. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a Humean: laws of nature are just the theorems of the best systematisation of the mosaic of local particular facts, where "best" trades off simplicity and strength.
(2) I think laws explain their instances. The fact that this electron accelerated like that is explained by the Lorentz force law, and the law isn't just a way of summarising electrons' behaviour.
(3) I think there are objective single-case chances: a particular radium atom has a chance of decaying within a given half-life, and this isn't just a fact about frequencies in a reference class.
(4) I think the laws of our world are contingent: there are possible worlds with the same fundamental kinds of things but different laws.
(5) I think if two possible worlds agree on all local particular facts throughout spacetime, they agree on the laws.
(6) I think laws support counterfactuals: if I had placed this electron in a different field, it would have followed the Lorentz force law.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
