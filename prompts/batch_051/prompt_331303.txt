I teach high-school physics and tutor middle schoolers on the side, and I've started noticing something I don't have a word for. My students' wrong answers aren't random. The same wrong idea shows up again and again, and it hangs together with other wrong ideas: the kid who thinks a constant force is needed to keep a ball moving also tends to think a ball thrown straight up has a lingering push from the throw while it rises, and the kid who thinks heavier objects fall faster usually also expects a heavier pendulum to swing faster. It's like they're reasoning from a whole alternative physics with its own rules, not just guessing. But then another student will have a wrong idea that's weirdly local: she'll insist plants 'eat' soil for their mass when we're talking about a potted plant, but she reasons fine about mass everywhere else. Is there an established name for the first pattern — systematic errors that cohere into something theory-like — and how exactly is it distinguished from the second, fragmentary kind? I'm not after just a label: I want to know where the line is between 'a wrong belief' and 'a whole alternative way of thinking', and whether researchers actually draw that line or think even the theory-looking cases are just alot of little local rules.
