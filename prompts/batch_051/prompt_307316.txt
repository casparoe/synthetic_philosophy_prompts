I am a PhD student in philosophy working between decision theory and formal epistemology, and I need to submit a one-page proposal for a year-long individual project at an upcoming workshop on foundational questions about agency. I want the project to be on the absent-minded driver problem. In the standard case, a driver knows he will pass two indistinguishable intersections: he receives a modest payoff for exiting at the first, the best payoff for continuing through the first and exiting at the second, and nothing for driving past both. Because he cannot tell the two intersections apart when he reaches one, the plan that looks best before he sets out and the plan that looks best conditional on being at some intersection appear to come apart, even though he has learned nothing that distinguishes one intersection from the other.

My half-formed idea is this. The usual discussions run together two different notions of the plan the driver would have endorsed ex ante: a plan conceived as an assignment of actions to objective intersections, and a plan conceived as an assignment of actions to the driver's information states. Only the second is available to the driver once the trip has begun, yet the local comparison often proceeds as if the first were the relevant benchmark. I suspect that restating the problem with these two notions kept apart will either dissolve the apparent time-inconsistency or relocate the real difficulty to the treatment of self-locating belief rather than to dynamic consistency itself.

Turn this into a properly structured proposal.
