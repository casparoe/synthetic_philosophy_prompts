I have a half-formed idea about information cascades that I can't quite get sharp on my own. The standard rational herding setup assumes people observe the exact sequence of prior decisions, or at least know how many people acted before them. But in actual online environments, you just see stuff like "47 people found this helpful" or a star rating. You don't know if 47 is out of 50 or out of 5,000. It seems like that should matter for whether a rational cascade can get going: you're not just trying to infer the hidden state from others' actions, you're also trying to infer how informative the aggregate count is as a sample. Maybe some classic cascade results are artifacts of the known-sequence assumption, and under more realistic aggregate-only information the whole herding story changes. But I'm not sure if that's true, or if it's already been done. Could you turn this into a proposal? What I need is: a precise question, a case for why standard cascade models don't settle it, a method (analytical model? simulation? both?) that could make progress, what a useful deliverable looks like for someone thinking about ranking systems or platform design, and what would count as the project failing.
