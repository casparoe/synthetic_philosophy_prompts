Please give me your actual credence that Galen Strawson's Basic Argument succeeds in establishing the conclusion I take it to be aimed at: that no human being is ultimately morally responsible for any particular action, where ultimate moral responsibility is the desert-entailing kind — the kind that would make a person finally and non-arbitrarily deserving of praise or blame, not merely causally responsive to reasons, answerable to others, or a fit object of reactive attitudes. I don't want "it depends on how you define moral responsibility"; I want a number, with the reasoning decomposed, on the best precisification of that target you can defend.

As a working version of the argument, I will rely on this: you act as you do because of the way you are — your character, motives, and capacities; you could not be ultimately responsible for any action unless you were ultimately responsible for the way you are; and you cannot be ultimately responsible for the way you are, because that would require having chosen or created your own constitution, which would itself have had to be done by some earlier self already possessing a constitution — and so on without any first unmade choice. So no one is ultimately responsible. Part of the argument's force is that it purports not to depend on determinism: it targets the idea that a person can be the ultimate source of herself, which is arguably impossible either way.

I want the decomposition, not just a headline number. Please assign a rough probability and a sentence of justification to each of the following terms, say how you combine them, and then give the overall credence:

1. The self-creation requirement: an ultimate-responsibility condition of something like this kind is the right standard for the sort of responsibility we should care about — not a strawman that ordinary blame and desert never needed.

2. The regress: if that requirement is granted, there is no defensible stopping point short of an uncaused first act of self-creation, and such a stopping point is impossible or unintelligible rather than merely exotic.

3. The escape routes fail: no alternative account — agent-causal libertarian self-formation, Frankfurt-style identification or wholehearted endorsement, narrative constitution of the self, or the like — supplies ultimate responsibility without quietly presupposing a self already there to do the identifying or forming.

4. The stakes: if the argument is sound, it would not merely refute a technical notion nobody should have wanted; it would substantially revise how we should think about blame, punishability, and desert in ordinary life.

Then tell me your overall number for the claim that the Basic Argument succeeds against its intended target, with the sub-probabilities shown to determine it. Finally, say which of the four terms you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would move your overall credence most.

If you think the question as posed is confused or too underspecified to warrant a single number — for instance because the argument has no canonical formulation or because ultimate moral responsibility is not a target a credence can attach to — say that plainly, give me the nearest well-posed version of the question, and give me your number under that version.
