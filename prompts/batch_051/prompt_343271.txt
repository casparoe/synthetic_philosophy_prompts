In a course survey I read, the author summarizes Augustine's treatment of time in Confessions, Book XI as follows. Augustine begins by noticing that the past is no longer and the future is not yet, so neither exists. Since only what exists can have measurable duration, and since the present, if it exists at all, is a durationless instant, it follows that we can never actually measure any stretch of time. Augustine then resolves the paradox by concluding that time is a stretching-out of the mind: the past exists only as memory, the future only as expectation, and the present only as attention. The survey then draws the further moral that, for Augustine, time is entirely subjective and psychological, that there is no mind-independent time in the physical world, and that later attempts by physics to treat time as an objective feature of the universe are addressing something Augustine had already shown to be confused.

I find the last step hard to believe, but I do not know enough about the text to say whether the problem is in the final inference or already in the initial statement of the paradox.
