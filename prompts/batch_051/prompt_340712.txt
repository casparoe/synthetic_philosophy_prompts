I keep going back and forth on a Knobe effect variant and want someone to actually run the arguments. Standard chairman case: the side effect is certain (harm in one condition, help in the other), the chairman says he doesn't care, and people end up calling the harm intentional but the help not. Now make the side effect merely probable at decision time, say 20%; the VP says so, the chairman shrugs, the program goes ahead, and the side effect actually occurs. Does the usual asymmetry in 'intentionally harmed/helped' judgments survive, or shrink, or flip? Work through what the two usual lines would each predict here: the story that the asymmetry tracks the badness of the side effect (blame leaks into intentionality), versus the story that it tracks norm violation (harming violates a norm while merely not helping doesn't). Do the two lines come apart in this ex ante probable version, or do they end up making the same call? And does it matter whether people are told the actual outcome before they answer, as opposed to being asked while the outcome is still up in the air?
