for a class I'm in I had to write out my actual position on abortion as a list of commitments, and now I want to know if the list is jointly defensible before I have to defend it out loud. here's what I've got:

(1) I'm fairly convinced by the future-like-ours idea: what makes killing a normal adult wrong is that it deprives her of a future she'd otherwise have had, and by somewhere in the second trimester a fetus has a future of the relevant sort, so it has serious moral status and isn't just tissue.

(2) I also find Thomson's violinist basically right: even granting the fetus is a person with a right to life, that right doesn't include a right to live inside and off someone else's body, so early abortion can be permissible anyway.

(3) I nonetheless think that past viability the state may restrict abortion to health/life of the pregnant person; at that point the fetus could survive outside the body, so the situation stops looking like the violinist.

(4) I also hold that a moral line like this shouldn't shift with what the nearest NICU happens to be able to do; if a fetus's moral situation changes, that change should be in the fetus, not in the local hospital.

(5) And I lean on the killing/letting die distinction in other contexts — pulling the plug on a terminally ill patient realy is different from giving a lethal injection — and I think this matters, because the violinist is a case of unplugging whereas a lot of abortion is more like actively ending something.

I can't tell which of these are doing the real work or where the actual incompatabilities are. treat this as an audit: go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and how severe each tension is, which pairs only look like they clash and why the appearance is misleading, and what the cheapest single revision would be to leave the set coherent, including what that revision would cost. don't tell me what the right view of abortion is; tell me what this particular set commits me to.
