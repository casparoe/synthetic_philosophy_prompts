I'm a phd student in legal philosophy and I cant stop turning over a problem about extraterritorial jurisdiction over online speech. Suppose someone in the U.S. posts something that's completely legal under U.S. law but defamatory under, say, German or Thai law, and the post is just accessible from those countries. Courts in those countries do sometimes assert jurisdiction over that sort of speech. If that's right, then every online utterance is simultaneously subject to every legal regime on Earth where a reader could find it, and the effective global rule for speech is just the least common denominator of whatever the strictest country bans. Is that actually how international law works? If yes, it seems to make domestic free-speech guarantees almost pointless; if no, what principle blocks the strictest-country rule from being the de facto rule?
