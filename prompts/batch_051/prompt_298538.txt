I'm a software engineer and I don't have any formal philosophy background, but a buddy of mine has been going off about free riding and it got under my skin because his examples were mostly from open source. Like, am I a free rider if I use a Linux distro every day, have never filed a bug report, and the one pull request I ever made got rejected? He sent me a paragraph from a book he's reading on social cooperation that sums up the charge like this: "Free riders are people who enjoy a benefit others worked to produce without paying their share. The basic argument against them is that if everyone behaved that way, the good would never be produced, so free riding is self-defeating — in fact it's irrational in the same way that throwing your trash in the park is irrational. On top of that, to accept a benefit is to implicitly promise to do your bit, so the free rider breaks a real promise to everyone else in the collective scheme."

Something there smells off to me. The "if everyone did it" move feels like it's telling me about a world I dont actually live in, and I definitely don't remember promising anyone anything when I installed Debian. But I don't know enough to say whether that's a real objection or just me being defensive about my patch. Could you go through his summary sentence by sentence and tell me what a philosopher would accept, what's getting conflated or overstated, and whether the conclusion that free riding is wrong actually survives once the summary is cleaned up?
