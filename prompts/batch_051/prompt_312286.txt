In my course on political philosophy this term, we have been discussing whether a state may coercively take part of citizens' income to fund welfare programs, and one phrase keeps surfacing: "Taxation is theft." A classmate of mine used it in seminar to dismiss redistributive taxation as though no further argument were needed, and the instructor replied that it was a slogan rather than an argument. I think the instructor is right that the phrase is being asked to do a great deal of work while remaining vague, but I suspect it is not simply empty rhetoric; rather, it seems to be a compressed way of gesturing at several quite different claims, some of which are stronger than others.

What I cannot yet do is separate those claims. The sentence might mean that taxation is literally the same kind of act as theft, since both involve taking property without the owner's consent and merely adding legal authorization does not change what is done. It might mean that taxation is morally on a par with theft even though legally distinct. It might be a claim about self-ownership: people own their labor and its fruits, so the state's taking part of a paycheck violates the same right that stealing violates. It might instead be the narrower claim that only certain taxes are theft, namely those used to fund projects the taxpayer rejects or services from which she never benefits. Or it might be a conceptual claim that turns entirely on how "theft" and "rightful property" are defined, in which case the phrase could collapse into a tautology or beg the question.

I would like this treated as a disambiguation: a menu of the distinct, precise theses that "Taxation is theft" could be expressing, with each reading labeled as platitudinous, substantive and defensible, false, or otherwise problematic. Most importantly, I want to know which readings would actually support using the slogan to reject progressive or redistributive taxation, which would not, and which reading my classmate was most plausibly relying on when he treated it as a conversation-stopper.
