Write a decision tree that sorts someone into a position on the Great Filter: the question of where, between ordinary prebiotic chemistry and a galaxy-spanning civilization, the process stalls or nearly stalls. The instrument is for a small astrobiology and philosophy reading group, and its purpose is to sort members by what they actually believe about which transition is the bottleneck, not by which label they recognize or prefer.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of school names. Use all six of the following nodes, in whatever order makes the tree most diagnostic, and no additional transition nodes:

1. Prebiotic chemistry to self-replicating life.
2. Prokaryote-like cells to complex eukaryotic cells.
3. Unicellular life to large multicellular organisms with complex bodies and behavior.
4. Complex nervous systems to language, cumulative culture, and technology.
5. Technological civilization to a durable, expanding, remotely detectable civilization.
6. The evidential question of whether our failure to observe other civilizations is strong direct evidence of a bottleneck, or is weakened by observation-selection effects that make only some histories visible to their own observers.

At each node, give two or three mutually exclusive answers corresponding to treating that transition as easy, hard, or underdetermined by current evidence. Label every branch with the claim or bet that splits it, phrased so a respondent can answer without knowing any position's name.

Then locate the actual positions in the resulting space of answer combinations. The leaves must include at least: an early-filter view on which abiogenesis is the improbable step; a mid-filter view centered on eukaryogenesis or on the origin of complex multicellular life; a late-filter view centered on the evolution of intelligence; a filter-ahead view on which technological civilizations arise but do not durably expand; and a mediocrity view on which none of these steps is especially improbable and the apparent emptiness is explained otherwise. For any answer combination with no leaf, note the empty cell and say briefly whether the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If two named positions fall into the same leaf under this tree, flag that the tree cannot separate them and explain which additional question would. Each leaf should carry a one-sentence description and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Close with a key that maps each complete path to its leaf and a note on which node does the most classificatory work. Do not cite sources; the tree should be usable by someone with no prior exposure to the named positions.
