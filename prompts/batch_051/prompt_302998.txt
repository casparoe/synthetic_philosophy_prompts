Write the joint statement that a compatriot-priority theorist and a cosmopolitan impartialist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether compatriots have special claims on each other that they don't have toward distant strangers. Assume both are serious and have spent a while at it, so they've already found the places where they were talking passed each other and can state their own views without caricaturing the other side.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section first, then a short note on which disagreements dissolved as merely verbal, then a disagreements table. In the table, each row should be a crux: a sub-question such that getting a clear answer to it would move at least one side. For each crux, give the compatriot-priority view and the cosmopolitan view, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position, then give the evidence, argument, or concrete case both agree would bear on the crux, with each side's prediction about how it would come out recorded in advance.

Don't let the agreed claims be empty truisms, and don't make the table a list of slogans. Make sure the cruxes include at least these three, plus whatever else you think belongs:

- whether subjection to a state's coercive institutions, versus co-authoring those institutions as a citizen, does the real justificatory work for special claims
- whether compatriot partiality mostly scaffolds concern for outsiders or mostly crowds it out
- whether the special claims would survive if there were robust, legitimate global institutions that did much of what states now do

Anchor at least a few rows in concrete cases, like rescue cases with equal costs, tax-funded scholarships, emigration and immigration choices, or a border policy. Begin with the statement itself, no preface or commentary. British English, please.
