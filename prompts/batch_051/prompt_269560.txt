For a graduate seminar in historiography: write the list of requirements that a theory of periodization must meet to count as adequate for deciding whether the 'Middle Ages', the 'Renaissance', and the 'Scientific Revolution' pick out real historical joints or are later inventions imposed on the past for convenience and self-congratulation. For each requirement, give the consideration, case, or failure mode that motivates it. Then mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp—for example, those who read a period as a genuine rupture in practices, institutions, or self-understanding, versus those who read it as retrospective and interest-driven, or those for whom the labels are harmless shorthand rather than explanatory claims. Finally, say whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, so that no scheme could satisfy all of them at once, and identify the requirement that does the most work to exclude candidates. Keep the criteria and their motivating cases the center of gravity; do not turn this into a scorecard of individual historians.

Please also be explicit about what would change your mind. Say which of your non-negotiable requirements you are least confident about, what kind of historical evidence or methodological argument would demote it to a camp preference, and, if you claim some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, what sort of case would most plausibly overturn that claim.
