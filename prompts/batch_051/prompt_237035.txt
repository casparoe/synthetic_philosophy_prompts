I am a retired physicist — most of my career was in gravitational physics — and only in the past few years have I begun to read the philosophy of time seriously. I have agreed to present my position to a reading group, and before I do I would like a consistency audit of the set of commitments I have assembled. I would rather have a careful reader tell me now that two of my views cannot both be kept than discover it in front of the group. Here, as plainly as I can state them, is where I have landed:

(a) Philosophical accounts of time should be answerable to the actual empirical content of our best physics. I regard the empirical adequacy of special and general relativity as non-negotiable, and I will not rescue a metaphysical doctrine by denying what those theories get right about measurement.

(b) I accept the standard special-relativistic conclusion that there is no empirically accessible, observer-independent simultaneity: observers in relative motion will disagree about which spatially separated events are simultaneous, and no measurement can pick out one of them as having the right now.

(c) Nevertheless, I am a presentist in the A-theoretic sense. I believe the present is metaphysically privileged, that there is objective passage, and that only what is present exists.

(d) I reconcile (b) and (c) along neo-Lorentzian lines. I posit a privileged foliation of spacetime — a single objectively correct global now — but one that is empirically undetectable, so that the relativity of simultaneity is a fact about observers' epistemic situation and not about the metaphysics of time.

(e) I think Gödel's rotating-universe solution is a serious result and not a curiosity. As I understand it, it is a rotating solution to the Einstein field equations in which closed timelike curves appear and no global cosmic time can be defined. I do not follow Gödel all the way to the conclusion that time is ideal, but I take the solution to show that objective worldwide passage — just what the presentist says is actual — is not something general relativity delivers, since the theory admits worlds in which such passage cannot even be defined. That strikes me as a serious problem for treating passage as a necessary feature of time.

(f) At the same time, I am generally unpersuaded by arguments that move from physically possible but merely possible worlds to conclusions about what actually exists. The fact that the field equations admit an exotic rotating solution with closed timelike curves tells us, at most, something about the equations; it does not by itself tell us that our universe — the one spacetime there actually is — lacks an objective now. A presentist needs to be right about this world, not about every model of the equations.

(g) Methodologically, I distrust theoretical structure introduced solely to save a doctrine: unobservable structure must simplify, unify, or otherwise earn its keep before I will grant it.

Audit this set. I am not asking for a summary of each commitment; I am asking for the stress points. Please structure your reply as follows:

1. Ranked tensions (roughly 60 percent of your answer). Identify every pair — or larger subset — of (a)-(g) that you judge to be in genuine tension. State each tension in your own words, specify which commitments it joins and why they conflict, and rank them by severity, most damaging first. Do not blur several distinct tensions into one general unease.

2. Apparent tensions that dissolve (roughly 20 percent). Identify any pair that looks like a conflict but, on inspection, is not, and explain what distinction removes the appearance.

3. Least costly revision (roughly 20 percent). For the most severe tension, state the cheapest change that restores consistency — which commitment would have to give, and exactly what giving it up costs. Then say which single revision to the set would resolve the most problems at once, and whether you recommend it.
