People reach for the prisoner's dilemma whenever they want to explain why two employees who both know about fraud will rat each other out rather than keep quiet. Start from the standard one-shot version with these payoffs: mutual silence gives each 2, mutual ratting gives each 0, and if one rats while the other stays quiet the rat goes free with 3 while the quiet one gets -1. Standard result: ratting strictly dominates, so both rat, and both end up worse off than if they'd both stayed quiet. Now add one thing: the two aren't strangers; each attaches a loyalty cost L to being the one who rats while the other stays quiet — the felt wrong of betrayal. Ratting when the other also rats carries no such cost, since nobody's left holding the bag. Work through the best responses as L rises. At what value of L does ratting stop strictly dominating? What game does the thing turn into, and does the original dilemma survive? If mutual silence becomes an equilibrium, what does it actually take for the players to land there rather than on mutual ratting?
