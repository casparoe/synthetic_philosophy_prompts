Consider the argument, most associated with Bernard Williams, that all reasons for action are internal. On this view, a claim of the form A has a reason to φ is true only if there is a sound deliberative route from something already present in A's subjective motivational set — A's desires, projects, loyalties, and similar motivations — to the motivation to φ. The route may pass through imagination, reflection on what A would want in other circumstances, and correction of factual error, but it must begin in motives A actually has. A consideration that could not rationally engage anything A already cares about is therefore not a reason A has, however good a reason it would be for a person with a different psychology. The rival, externalist view holds that at least some reasons obtain independently of A's present motivations, for example reasons grounded in A's well-being or in what A would want after an ideal process of improvement.

Now suppose a medical intervention becomes available that can directly edit the motivational set itself. A course of stimulation or a pharmacological protocol can install a durable new desire, or extinguish an old one, as a direct causal effect of the intervention rather than as the terminus of the patient's own reasoning. Consider a profoundly apathetic patient whose subjective motivational set is, as far as clinicians can determine, effectively empty: he has no current desire to recover, to avoid future distress, to maintain relationships, or to continue living in any particular way. The intervention, if administered, would produce in him a stable desire to recover and to continue treatment, and he would later judge that the change was to his benefit.

Does Williams's internal reasons argument survive this development? State precisely which premise the intervention puts under pressure. Does the patient have a reason to undergo the intervention before it is administered, only after it has changed him, or never? If Williams's position commits us to a verdict that seems unacceptable in this case, say whether the position can be repaired without simply conceding the external reasons view, and if so, state the repaired thesis and what it gives up. If it cannot be repaired, what exactly should the example lead us to abandon?
