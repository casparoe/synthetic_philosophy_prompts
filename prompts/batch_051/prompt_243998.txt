As a retired condensed matter physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of physics, I have been trying to sort out what the effective field theory framework should make me think about reductionism and fundamentality. I recently came across the following passage in Steven Weinberg’s talk “What is Quantum Field Theory, and What Did We Think It Is?”:

In its mature form, the idea of quantum field theory is that quantum fields are the basic ingredients of the universe, and particles are just bundles of energy and momentum of the fields.

I have written down the set of commitments I have arrived at, and I would like you to act as an expert in both philosophy of physics and metaphysics. Please think carefully, and before giving your final assessment, brainstorm the possible ways these commitments might interact.

Here are my commitments:

(1) All the quantum field theories we currently use—including the Standard Model—are best understood as effective field theories valid only below some energy cutoff, with unknown physics above it.

(2) The effective field theory framework supports a general principle of the autonomy of scales: to describe phenomena at a given energy or length scale, one needs only the symmetries and light degrees of freedom relevant at that scale, and one does not need detailed knowledge of higher-energy physics.

(3) Weinberg is right in the quoted passage that quantum fields are more basic than particles; particles are derived, non-fundamental entities.

(4) The universe is nonetheless layered, and higher-level sciences such as chemistry and condensed matter physics are autonomous: their explanations do not bottom out in particle physics, and the entities and structures they describe are real, not merely convenient fictions.

(5) A quantum field theory with no continuum limit—for example quantum electrodynamics if the Landau pole is physical, or general relativity treated naively as a quantum field theory—has only a finite range of validity and therefore cannot serve as a final theory.

(6) It is an open and serious question whether talk of a final, fundamental theory makes sense at all, since any theory is always a theory of degrees of freedom appropriate to a particular scale.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which are only apparently in tension because they rely on different senses of “fundamental,” “basic,” or “autonomous”? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. I do not know where the real problem is, so do not assume I am prepared to abandon any particular commitment. The deliverable should be an audit of my position’s internal consistency, not a literature review or a survey of rival interpretations.
