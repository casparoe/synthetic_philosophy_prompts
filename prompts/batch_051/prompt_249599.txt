I'm a legislative aide, and my boss just voted for a bill moving the age at which someone can be charged as an adult from 17 to 18. The whole thing left me a bit confused: at Tuesday's hearing we spent hours insisting that maturity doesn't just switch on at a birthday, and on Wednesday my boss voted for a bill that treats someone who is 17 years and 364 days old as a child and someone a few hours older as an adult for sentencing. I know a cutoff is practically unavoidable, but our own legislative findings describe maturity as a matter of degree. So what is a bright-line age cutoff like this implicitly assuming about vague predicates? Does it force us into epistemicism — the view that there really is a sharp but unknowable boundary where 'mature' becomes true — or could a supervaluationist also defend the cutoff as more than just a convenient fiction? And what woud actually have to change in how we draft these statutes if we changed the underlying view of vagueness?
