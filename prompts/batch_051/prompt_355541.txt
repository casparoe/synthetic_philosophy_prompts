I host a philosophy podcast that tries to keep the discussion in the weeds rather than at the level of slogans, and next month I am recording a long episode with a political philosopher who defends a reconstructed Lockean theory of original appropriation. Her view has three distinctive commitments. First, she takes the labor-mixing argument seriously, but she reconstructs it as a claim about extending one's agency into previously unowned matter rather than as a literal mixing of owned and unowned substances. Second, she reads the Lockean proviso not as a per-act requirement to leave others resources of comparable quality, but as a sufficientarian constraint on the property system as a whole: a regime of private property is just only if it leaves everyone able, through their own labor, to acquire enough to secure a decent life. Third, she argues that, so interpreted, Lockeanism supports substantial taxation of the unimproved value of land and natural resources, so her position comes out left rather than right-libertarian.

Acting as an expert in political philosophy who knows both the classical texts and the modern debates about original acquisition, draft eight to ten interview questions, each accompanied by a follow-up keyed to the reply the guest is most likely to give. Make sure the set includes at least: one question on whether labor-mixing can create title to something one did not already own, pressing the familiar objection that pouring what you own into what you do not own would more plausibly waste the former than appropriate the latter; one on the proviso's baseline problem, asking against what comparison state the sufficiency standard is to be measured once others have already enclosed the best resources; one on whether treating the proviso as a system-level constraint rather than a per-act constraint remains faithful to the original argument or replaces it with a subtly different test; one on why the threshold is sufficiency rather than equality, and whether sufficiency can be specified without smuggling in a contested account of what a decent life requires; and one on whether her treatment of unimproved resource value can handle resources such as aggregates of data or trained models, where the unimproved value is trivial and most of the value is added by widely distributed collective labor. Begin by brainstorming the strongest objections and the guest's most natural defensive moves, then write questions that block the easy answers and force her to say what is load-bearing in her view. Write the final questions in American English.
