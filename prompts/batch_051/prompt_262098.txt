So I keep going around in circles on Hempel's dilemma and whether it still has teeth. The dilemma was aimed at a specific way of stating physicalism: define 'physical' in terms of current physics, and physicalism is almost certainly false; define it in terms of some future completed physics, and it becomes empty, since we don't know what such a physics will include. But much of the literature has moved on from that formulation — people now put physicalism as a grounding claim (all facts are grounded in physical facts) or define 'physical' negatively, as 'not fundamentally mental.' I need to know whether Hempel's dilemma survives that shift, or whether the new formulations just moved the bump in the carpet. Which premise of the original dilemma, if any, do they actually dodge, and is there a version of the dilemma that still bites the grounding and via-negativa statements?
