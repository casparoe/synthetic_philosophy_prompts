In my environmental economics class we've been going over two things that are starting to feel like they contradict each other, and the more my study group discusses them the less I cant tell if one of us has made a category mistake or if the standard story just has a hole in it. On the one hand, public goods are defined as non-rival and non-excludable, and the standard problem with them is underprovision: since people can't be excluded from enjoying a lighthouse or clean air, they free ride, and too little of the good gets produced or maintained. On the other hand, Hardin's tragedy of the commons is supposed to show that a shared pasture that everyone can use will be overused to the point of ruin.

My study group keeps calling that pasture a public good, and now I'm confused. If free riding on a public good means nobody pays and the thing ends up undersupplied, why does the same setup with the shared pasture produce the opposite outcome, with everyone using it too much? Is the underprovision story or the depletion story the real prediction, or is there something different about the pasture that makes it a bad example of a public good?
