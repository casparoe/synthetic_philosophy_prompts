I have been reading about the free will defense and want help understanding what remains of the problem of evil. As I understand it, the defense shows that an omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly good God could have a morally sufficient reason for permitting moral evil, because creatures who freely choose right over wrong are a great enough good that the possibility of their choosing wrong is worth allowing. If that is right, then the existence of evil is compatible with theism in the strict logical sense, and the logical problem of evil is often taken to be solved. My puzzle is this: why do philosophers still treat the evidential problem of evil as a serious objection? Once the compatibility is established, should not the problem be considered solved, with remaining complaints being emotional or pastoral rather than philosophical? Is not the evidential problem just an attempt to revive a refuted argument by changing the subject from whether evil exists to how much evil there is?
