My question is about Maxwell's demon as an information-theoretic argument, not as loose metaphor. In Maxwell's Theory of Heat he gives the setup of a "finite being" who can follow every molecule and works a small hole between two chambers A and B, letting only swifter molecules pass from A to B and only slower ones from B to A, thereby raising the temperature of B and lowering A "without expenditure of work, in contradiction to the second law of thermodynamics." I want a step-by-step reconstruction of the best version of the argument that this is a genuine counterexample to the second law, treating the demon's sorting as a physical process but not assuming in advance that information acquisition must cost work. Here's the argument as I'd formalize it:

(1) The second law says no process operating in a cycle (or with no net change in its surroundings) can transfer heat from a colder body to a hotter body, or equivalently no isolated system can decrease its entropy.

(2) The demon plus the two chambers A and B and the gas is an isolated system in the relevant sense, at least if we idealize away external interference.

(3) The demon's actions—opening and closing a small frictionless trap door based on observing molecular velocities—can in principle involve no transfer of energy to or from the gas, or an arbitrarily small amount. So any energy exchange with the gas can be made negligible.

(4) If the demon opens and closes the door exactly so that faster-than-average molecules from A pass to B and slower-than-average molecules from B pass to A, the mean kinetic energy in B increases and in A decreases, so a temperature difference arises or increases between the two chambers.

(5) The resulting temperature difference is exactly a case of transferring heat from a colder body to a hotter body, or if you prefer, lowering the entropy of the gas without a compensating entropy increase elsewhere.

(6) Therefore the second law fails even on its most careful statistical formulation, or at least must be revised from a law of physics to a merely probable regularity.

I actually think the argument is valid and that the only way out is to deny (1) by saying the second law was never meant to apply to systems that use information. But people who know more physics than I do say the flaw is somewhere around (2) or (3), maybe having to do with the demon's memory or the fact that sorting requires a measurement that must dissipate somewhere. Tell me: is the argument as stated valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and what is the strongest repair that would make it go through—or if no repair works, which step is doing the illicit work?
