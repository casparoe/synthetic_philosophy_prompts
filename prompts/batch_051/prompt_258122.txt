I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the traffic between evolutionary biology and machine learning: on one side, the claim that natural selection is an optimization process and that studying it can teach gradient-based learning something it would not otherwise learn; on the other, the skeptical reply that natural selection is a short-sighted, blind hill-climber and that the analogy with gradient descent has misled people in both fields. Before I publish anything, I want to know exactly what survives the translation between the two vocabularies, because my working suspicion is that part of the dispute is a dispute about which analogies are licensed.

The translation I'd like you to attempt is this. Take the standard neo-Darwinian picture — heritable variation, selection, drift, recombination, and environments that selection itself keeps changing — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary of gradient-based machine learning: loss functions and reward signals, exact and estimated gradients, stochastic gradient descent, local minima and saddle points, exploration and exploitation, credit assignment, inductive bias, and the phenotype–fitness mapping. I can follow technical writing at the level of an attentive, scientifically literate observer, but I am not an engineer, so the terms need to do real work in the translation rather than decorating it.

Then I want the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly — for example, does selection give you anything like an estimate of a gradient, or only a comparison among the variants currently on offer? Which parts translate only with distortion, and where does the distortion begin to mislead — for instance, evolutionary fitness is not a fixed objective, because selection alters the very environment in which fitness is defined, so the optimizer's objective moves under it. Which parts have no expression at all in the optimization vocabulary — I have in mind things like drift, the historical contingency of the alleles actually available, or the absence of a separable channel for assigning credit across a recombining population. For each residue item, tell me whether it marks a real feature of evolution that the optimization vocabulary cannot represent, or a place where the original biological claim was less precise than it looked. Also tell me, for each mapping, how much weight it can bear in print: a precise correspondence, a loose but useful metaphor, or a stretch I should drop.

The judgment I most want is on the underlying analogy itself. If the right conclusion is that natural selection is not an optimizer in any sense that would license borrowing theorems or intuitions from gradient-based learning, I need to hear that. If it is an optimizer, but of a kind that gradient descent does not prepare you for, tell me what kind. And if the whole request presupposes that the two fields are close enough that a residue report is the interesting question rather than a category error, say so plainly instead of producing a polite translation.
