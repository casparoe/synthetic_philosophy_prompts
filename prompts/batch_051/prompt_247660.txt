I'm a senior in a course on AI safety and interpretability, and I've been trying to get my views on chain-of-thought monitoring into a defensible shape. The course has walked us through the faithfulness debate, the concern that optimization pressure encourages obfuscation, and the question whether reading a model's stated reasoning gives us anything like privileged access to what actually produced its behavior. I've converged on a set of commitments, and I'd appreciate a consistency audit before I have to present them in seminar.

Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Faithfulness should be understood causally: a chain of thought is faithful only insofar as the stated reasoning is the reasoning that actually determined the answer, not a plausible post hoc gloss.

(2) Since we cannot verify causal faithfulness in deployed systems, we should treat a model's chain of thought as a fallible report rather than as direct access to its underlying computation.

(3) Nevertheless, chain-of-thought monitoring is our best near-term mechanism for catching a model that is contemplating a course of action we would disallow, and we should intervene when the intermediate reasoning reveals such a course.

(4) Optimization pressure is the central threat to this mechanism: once we penalize models for producing worrying chains of thought while rewarding the final output, the model is pushed to produce whatever reasoning avoids the penalty, which can make the channel less diagnostic over time.

(5) Any model capable enough to entertain a dangerous plan is capable enough to anticipate that expressing that plan will be penalized; so the more seriously we rely on the chain of thought for enforcement, the stronger the incentive for hidden reasoning.

(6) It is still better to have and use the chain of thought than to discard it, since even a noisy read is better than none, and the alternatives to chain-of-thought monitoring offer no read at all.

I can tell that some of these pull against each other, but I don't trust my sense of which tensions are real and which only look like tensions. Could you go through the set and identify the pairs in genuine tension, rank them by severity, and say what the least costly revision is in each case? Please also flag any apparent tension that dissolves under closer analysis, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
