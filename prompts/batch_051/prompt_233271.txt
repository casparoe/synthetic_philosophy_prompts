Please argue that ideal negative conceivability is too weak to be a genuine guide to metaphysical possibility, and that Goldbach's conjecture is the cleanest way to expose the problem. Set the argument up like this: ideal negative conceivability says a proposition P is possible if an ideal reasoner can entertain P without uncovering a contradiction, no matter how long she reflects. Now let P be the claim that Goldbach's conjecture is false. Either ideal reflection settles Goldbach one way or the other, or it doesnt. If it settles it, then the idealization has been loaded with enough machinery that conceivability is no longer evidence for possibility—it's just reporting independent modal or mathematical facts you smuggled in. If it doesn't settle it, then an ideal reasoner can still conceive of Goldbach being false without contradiction; but on the standard assumption that mathematical truths are necessary, Goldbach's falsehood would then be impossible if the conjecture is true. So ideal negative conceivability either goes circular or overgenerates. Work through that dilemma carefully, and then say what it means for run-of-the-mill conceivability arguments like the zombie argument or the claim that one can conceive of water not being H2O. Dont take the easier route of just pointing out that ordinary people make logical mistakes; the idealization is supposed to be the interesting target.
