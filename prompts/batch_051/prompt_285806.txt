Long-time lurker here, hoping the forum can help me think through a dispute with my building.

My lease includes this clause: "The tenant shall not keep or store in the premises any flammable, explosive, or otherwise dangerous article or substance, nor do anything that materially increases the risk of fire to the building."

I charge two large lithium-ion e-bike batteries in my apartment overnight, a couple times a week. The building manager says I'm in breach and points to that clause. Thing is, the lease was written in the early 2000s, and the super told me the clause was always understood to be about storing gasoline cans, propane tanks, and oily rags. My take is a battery isn't a "substance" in that sense, and charging one is just normal residential use in 2025 — no different in kind then a laptop battery, just bigger. The manager's comeback is that the failure mode (thermal runaway, a fire water can't put out) is exactly the kind of low-probability, high-severity risk the clause exists for, and my neighbors never consented to it.

So: am I in breach? I care less about the actual legal answer than about which reading is right — the words, the purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked, what a reasonable neighbor would understand them to mean. And underneath that: when is it okay to impose a small-but-severe risk on people who never signed up for it, and how much work is it fair for a clause written before anyone had an e-bike to do?
