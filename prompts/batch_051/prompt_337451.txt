Write a strict villanelle (nineteen lines: five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the two refrains returning in the prescribed pattern) about sphexishness — the tendency for behavior that looks flexible and intelligent to be, in fact, a rigid routine. The poem should take its material from the much-retold anecdote of the digger wasp Sphex: the wasp stings a cricket to paralyze it, drags it to the burrow's entrance, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out, and drags the cricket in; but if an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp drags it back to the entrance and runs the entire inspection routine again, indefinitely. Let one refrain voice the return to the entrance — the one step the wasp can always redo — and let the other refrain voice the inspection of the burrow, so that the refrains' mechanical alternation and their final meeting enact the loop the wasp never exits. Do not try to vary the refrains or make them feel fresh; their identical return is the point. The poem should make the reader feel the pull of both reactions at once: that the wasp is a blind automaton, and that its routine is a kind of meticulous, even dutiful, work. The closing quatrain, where the two refrains finally meet, should turn on the reader or on the form itself, leaving open whether a poem that has been obeying its own rules as rigidly as the wasp obeys its routine is a reductio of form or a condition of doing anything at all. Someone familiar with the sphexishness discussion should recognize the example and the issue, and someone who is not should still find it a good poem. After the poem, add a short note separating what is settled from what is genuinely contested: it is settled that the anecdote, treated as a simplified conceptual model, illustrates a real question about rigidity, flexibility, and free will; it is contested whether the anecdote is entomologically faithful, whether sphexish behavior supports doubts about free will, whether human flexibility is a difference in kind or just a more elaborate stack of subroutines, and whether flexibility is the right criterion for freedom at all. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Use American English.
