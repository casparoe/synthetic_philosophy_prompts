People talk like Gen Z and boomers are real kinds — like cohort membership itself does causal work, over and above how old you are or what's happening in a given year. But the moment you try to test that, you hit the snag that cohort = year minus age, so you can't freely vary age, period, and cohort all at once. I want to know whether a claim like "Gen Z is a genuinely distinct kind" is even the sort of thing you could test. Design the best test you can: what outcome would you use, what groups or data would you compare, and what pattern of results would count as evidence for a real cohort effect rather than just an age effect or just a period effect? Then say exactly what a positive result would settle and what it would leave open — including whether the "real kind" framing survives, or whether we should fall back to calling cohorts useful labels. And try to seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this.
