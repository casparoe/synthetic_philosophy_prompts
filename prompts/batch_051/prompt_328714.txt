For a discussion group on the relation between evolution, minds, and machines, I have been asked to write down my working commitments about what happens to the idea of comprehension once one accepts the Darwinian and Turing-style inversions of reasoning. I would like you to audit the set and tell me whether these commitments hang together. Here they are.

1. The Darwinian inversion. Biological design and the appearance of goal-directed engineering can be fully explained by natural selection: a process with no foresight, no representation of ends, and no comprehension of what it is producing. Appeals to a designer or to nature's purposes do no explanatory work for me.

2. The Turing inversion. A physical mechanism can be competent at a cognitive-looking task, such as doing arithmetic, playing chess, or parsing a sentence, by manipulating uninterpreted states according to rules, without any understanding of the task or of the content of those states. The understanding we attribute to such a system is brought to it by the interpreter, rather than being an ingredient inside the mechanism.

3. And yet I hold that there is a real difference between a person who understands arithmetic and a calculator that merely turns out correct answers. Understanding is not just matching outward input-output behavior; I reject a purely behavioristic account of comprehension.

4. I also hold that comprehension is itself a product of the Darwinian process. It is a biological competence that evolved by natural selection, it admits of degrees across species and perhaps across artifacts, and it involves no extra ingredient over and above organized matter and energy—no immaterial thinking substance and no vital force.

5. Selection explanations are genuinely explanatory even though nothing in the process represents the reasons they cite. When a moth's coloration is explained by the camouflage it provides, the reason, camouflage, is not arbitrary and not merely projected: it picks out a real selective advantage in the organism's environment. Reasons of this sort can be real and explanatory without being represented by the organism or by selection itself.

6. I take the philosophical payoff of the inversions to be this: we can explain competence, even sophisticated competence, in terms of underlying mindless processes, without treating purpose or meaning as primitive, and without treating either as a causal force over and above the physical processes.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or larger subsets are in tension and how badly. Order the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give in order to resolve it and what the cost of that revision would be. I suspect at least one apparent tension among these dissolves on closer inspection; say where that happens and why it is only apparent. Do not try to make everything consistent—I want the trouble spots, not a defense of the set, and I have no preferred commitment to give up.

For each substantive claim you make in the audit, please say how confident you are in it, and single out the point you are least sure about.
