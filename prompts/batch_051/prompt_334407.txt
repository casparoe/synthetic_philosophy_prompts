So I've been doing this thing where I try to sit and just watch my own mind for ten minutes a day, and separately a friend pushed GEB on me, and now I can't stop thinking about the strange loop account of the self. As I understand it: there's no 'I'-thing, no soul, no homunculus in a Cartesian theatre. The self is a pattern, specifically a self-referential loop in the brain's symbol system — the system builds a symbol for itself, that symbol feeds back into the system, and when the loop gets rich enough, the thing that emerges is what 'you' are. What attracts me to it is how restrained it is: it never asks me to posit a extra ingredient, the 'I' gets explained as structure rather than substance, and the self becomes just another instance of the self-reference you see in Gödel and Turing. It feels like the first theory that doesn't end with me smuggling a ghost back in.

But when I'm actually sitting there watching, the intuition that fights back is not subtle. It doesn't feel like I'm a loop or a pattern or a symbol. It feels like I'm the most concrete thing there is — the one doing the watching, not something watched. A symbol is the sort of thing that's about something, that gets tokened and interpreted by a system; but I'm not about something in that way, I'm the one who's here. And it seems entirely possible to imagine the whole loop running, self-referring away, with nobody home. So the intuition is that the theory explains the self-model — the 'I' as the system's representation of itself — but leaves out the subject, the fact that there's something it is like to be the loop, or that the loop is mine. I think Hofstadter would say that's the very illusion the loop exists to produce, and that asking 'who's home?' is the system taking its own user-interface for a little man. But sometimes that feels less like answering the problem and more like renaming it.

I genuinely don't know which one should yield. If I drop the intuition, I want the actual debunking story: not just 'introspection is a self-model,' but an account of why introspection would present itself as an indivisible subject if no such subject exists. If I amend the theory, I want to know whether it becomes an account of self-representation only, with subjectivity marked as a separate unsolved problem — and whether that's an amendment or an abandonment. Or is there a reading on which both are right: the self as object is the strange loop, and the self as subject was never the kind of thing a theory could be about in the first place? Tell me what each move costs, and which one you'd actually make.
