I have been reading J. M. E. McTaggart's 1908 article "The Unreality of Time" in a philosophy of time reading group, and the group keeps splitting at the same point. We can follow the A-series/B-series distinction itself, and we can follow McTaggart when he says he regards time as unreal because the A-series is essential to time and the A-series lands in contradiction. But we get stuck on the intermediate step where he argues that a B-series alone, without an A-series, could not contain change. The passage we keep returning to is this one:

"If, then, a B series without an A series can constitute time, change must be possible without an A series. Let us suppose that the distinction of past, present and future does not apply to reality. Can change apply to reality? What is it that changes?

Could we say that, in a time which formed a B series but not an A series, the change consisted in the fact that an event ceased to be an event, while another event began to be an event? If this were the case, we should certainly have got a change."

What I want is an in-the-weeds explanation of exactly how this step of the argument is supposed to work, and where an eternalist or B-theorist should push back. In particular: why does McTaggart think change has to consist in something like events acquiring and losing a status such as presentness, rather than in objects having different properties at different positions in the B-series? Is he rejecting the idea that a poker can be cold at one time and hot at a later time, or is he making a more specific claim about whether that counts as change unless some position in the series is singled out as now? Please separate the claims about change from the claims about events' fixed positions in the earlier-later ordering, spell out the premise about change that a presentist has reason to keep and an eternalist has reason to deny, and try to state what each side has to pay for its reply.

Because I plan to use your answer to get the reading group unstuck, please say how confident you are in the overall interpretation, and single out the one move in your reconstruction that you are least sure of.
