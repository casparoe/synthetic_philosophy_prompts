I am a retired evolutionary ecologist, and since leaving the bench I have been reading moral philosophy and epistemology. One part of that literature I keep returning to is the dispute over whether knowledge or understanding is valuable for its own sake and whether curiosity is an intellectual virtue as opposed to a merely useful drive. The arguments strike me as important, but I keep translating them into the framework I used for decades, behavioral ecology, and I do not know whether the translation is fair or whether my framework is quietly deciding the question in advance.

Please do the translation systematically and then prepare a residue report.

The philosophical material is the following debate. One side holds that at least some knowledge or understanding has non-instrumental value: it is worth having independently of what it enables us to do, and curiosity is a standing virtue of the inquirer rather than a mere appetite for novelty. The other side holds that the value of knowledge is always instrumental, and that curiosity is at best a useful trait whose worth is exhausted by the practical or social outcomes it tends to produce.

Translate each side, and its main supporting moves, into the vocabulary of behavioral ecology and life-history theory. I am fully fluent in that vocabulary and only partially fluent in the philosophical one, so paraphrase any philosophical claims you rely on before translating them. Use concepts such as fitness and reproductive value, resources and foraging, marginal returns, exploration-exploitation trade-offs, information as a patchy and unevenly distributed resource, novelty and uncertainty as cues that correlate with expected future payoff, learning as investment with delayed returns, and costly signaling of cognitive quality.

Do not stop at the claims that translate cleanly. I am especially interested in what does not survive the translation: which distinctions or commitments from the philosophical debate can only be represented with distortion, and which have no counterpart in behavioral ecology at all. For each item of residue, say whether it resists translation because my framework lacks a concept it ought to have, or because the philosophical claim is doing no clear work once your translation refuses to supply it. I would like to know whether my habitual framework systematically excludes something true about the value of knowledge and curiosity, or whether it exposes a real unclarity in the idea that knowledge can be valuable for its own sake.
