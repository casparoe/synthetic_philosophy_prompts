Design a single experimental scenario in the Libet tradition that satisfies the following specifications. The subject's action must be preceded by a readiness potential, and the subject must report no conscious proximal intention to act; nevertheless, a reasons-responsive compatibilist must be committed to judging the action free and responsible, while a neuroscientific skeptic who takes Libet's results to show that only consciously initiated actions are free must be committed to judging it unfree. Keep the case psychologically realistic: no perfect predictors, no exotic physics, no tampering with the clock or the reports. After presenting the scenario, show the step-by-step reasoning each side is committed to; identify the structural feature of the case that produces the disagreement; say what happens if that feature is removed, and whether the case then collapses into the standard Libet experiment or into some other familiar case; and finish with the strongest reply the skeptic would make to the compatibilist's reading. The point of the case is to test whether the dispute is really about the timing of conscious intention or about the conditions of responsible agency, so a case that merely stipulates the verdicts rather than generating them from the two positions fails.
