I cover science and ideas for a living, and there's an asymmetry I can't shake. Most of us treat proportionality as the first test of a criminal sentence: a judge who handed down thirty years for shoplifiting would be seen as unfit, not just harsh. Yet a huge share of the same people also affirm that God punishes unrepentant wrongdoers with eternal conscious torment — an infinite sentence for a finite offense. The standard move is that a sin against an infinite being has infinite gravity and so deserves infinite punishment. I can't tell whether that move actually does any work or just restates the conclusion. Is there a real principle that makes the divine case different from the human one, or should the same proportionality objection push at least to annihilation or universalism? Give me your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
