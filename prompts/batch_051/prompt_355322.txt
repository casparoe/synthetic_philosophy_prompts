I’m trying to see whether my hedonism hangs together, and I would like an audit of the following commitments rather than a general defense or criticism of hedonism.

Here is where I am. I am a hedonist about well-being: the only thing that makes a life go well is pleasant experience, and the only thing that makes it go badly is painful experience. I also think Bentham’s framework is basically right: the value of a pleasure or pain is a function of its intensity, duration, certainty, and propinquity, and comparing two pleasures means comparing something like how much, how likely, and how soon. But I do not think there is any single felt quality shared by all pleasures: the pleasure of a cool drink, of getting a joke, of solving a problem, and of someone else’s misfortune are not alike in the way blue things are alike. Instead, I think a state is a pleasure in virtue of its being liked, desired, or otherwise playing the relevant functional role, not in virtue of a common phenomenal core. At the same time, I am a realist about the category: I think “pleasure” picks out a real feature of mental life, not a merely nominal or family-resemblance grouping. And I think we can meaningfully compare intensities across these very different kinds, at least ordinally: it is not nonsense to say that relief from a bad headache is more intense than the pleasure of a good meal. Finally, I also think some qualitative differences among pleasures matter for their contribution to well-being above and beyond intensity and duration: I would say that the pleasure someone takes in cruelty is less choiceworthy than the equally intense and equally long pleasure of a child at play, and this is not merely because of likely consequences.

Please treat this as an audit: identify which pairs or triples are in tension, rank the tensions by severity, say which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs.
