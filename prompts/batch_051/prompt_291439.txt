I want the specification before I read more theories. What must an adequate theory of the direction of time — understood first and foremost as the thermodynamic arrow — get right? I am asking for the requirements list: the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results it has to be consistent with. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, not just the label. Treat adequacy as adequacy for explaining the large-scale temporal asymmetry of the world: why entropy increases toward the future in ordinary isolated systems, why records and traces of the past accumulate while future-directed traces do not, and why these asymmetries line up.

A theory may define its own explanatory target, but it must be explicit about which arrow it takes as primitive and which it claims to derive. I expect the list to grapple with at least these pressure points, though you should add any you think I have missed.

1. The microdynamics. The underlying laws are time-reversal invariant to excellent approximation, so an adequate theory must locate the arrow in boundary conditions, in the initial macrostate, or in some explicit and relevant failure of time-reversal invariance — not by writing irreversibility into the dynamics as a bare posit. It must also say what makes a boundary condition acceptable, and whether a low-entropy initial condition of the universe can be taken as brute or itself requires explanation.

2. Loschmidt and Zermelo. The theory must not claim strict, exceptionless entropy increase in finite isolated systems. It has to face the reversibility objection — that every dynamical trajectory has a permitted reverse — and the recurrence objection — that a bounded finite system returns arbitrarily close to its initial state — and then say exactly in what sense the second law survives both.

3. The entropy concept. It must specify which entropy it intends — coarse-grained Boltzmann entropy, Gibbs entropy, or another — and show that its explanatory claims survive that choice rather than resting on an informal appeal to disorder.

4. The cases. It must get right both the mundane processes — gas expanding into an evacuated chamber, heat flowing from hot to cold, ice melting in a warm room — and the contrived ones that test the boundary between thermodynamics and information: Maxwell's demon, Szilard's engine if the theory's view of information demands it, small systems satisfying fluctuation theorems, and whatever counter-instance cases the theory is prepared to handle.

5. The other arrows. It must take a stand on the psychological arrow, the causal arrow, the epistemic arrow of records, and — if it claims relevance — the cosmological arrow of cosmic expansion: which reduce to the thermodynamic arrow, which are primitive, and which are misdescribed. It cannot define the future as the direction of entropy increase and then claim to have explained why we remember the past.

6. The fluctuation trap. If the theory explains the observed arrow by treating the universe, or our observable patch, as a fluctuation from a larger equilibrium, it must avoid the consequence that typical observers should be Boltzmann brains. Either pass that test or renounce the fluctuation-style explanation.

Then do the two things I most want. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or negotiable, and for the negotiable ones identify the camp that insists on it and why it is not a universal demand. Then examine joint unsatisfiability, because that is what will make the list a result rather than a shopping list. In particular, ask whether one theory can simultaneously have (a) time-reversal invariant microdynamics, (b) an objective, uniformly future-directed law of entropy increase, and (c) a non-question-begging account of records and intervention; if the three cannot all be held, say which one gives way and what the weakened formulation claims. Finally, state which single requirement does the most to eliminate otherwise plausible candidates, and why.

Keep the list of criteria as the deliverable; do not turn this into a survey of Boltzmannian, Gibbsian, or interventionist theories except where a named theory makes a criterion vivid.
