I'm a PhD student in conservation biology, and I'm stuck on whether a collaborator's gene drive release would violate a clause in our field station's restoration grant agreement. The clause reads: "The grantees shall not carry out any intervention that is irreversible. An intervention is reversible only if there is a feasible and acceptable method of returning the affected ecosystem to approximately its pre-intervention condition within one generation of the relevant target species." The proposal is to release a homing drive into an island population of invasive house mice; the drive biases inheritance of an allele that disrupts female fertility, with the goal of collapsing the population to zero. The collaborator says this satisfies the clause because you could later release lab mice carrying the wild-type allele, or the island would be recolonized from the mainland, so the change counts as reversible. But that seems wrong to me, since the pre-intervention condition includes the established invasive population, and the entire point of the project is to make sure that condition doesn't return. The clause obviously was not written with self-propagating drives in mind, and I keep going in circles trying to apply it. Can you work through what the plain words, the purpose of the reversibility condition, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reviewer would take the clause to mean each imply about whether the project is allowed? They seem to pull in differnt directions, and I need to figure out which reading to defend.
