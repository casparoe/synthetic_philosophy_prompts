I keep catching myself making confident calls about English "if" sentences and then realizing my calls dont line up with the truth-table "if" I half-remember from a college logic course. I'm a software engineer, so if-statements are the one thing I thought I had on lock, but natural-language conditionals keep misbehaving. I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and I want to know what principle, if any, captures them.

1. "If Oswald didn't pull the trigger, someone else did." Sounds right, I'd say true.
2. "If Oswald hadn't pulled the trigger, someone else would have." Sounds false; its like claiming a second gunman was standing by, and I don't believe that.
3. "If you drop that glass, it'll break." True, no hesitation.
4. "If you had dropped the glass, it would have broken." Also true. So the will/would switch doesn't always flip my answer.
5. "If you had dropped the glass, it would have broken. But if you had dropped it onto the foam pad, it wouldn't have broken." Both halves feel true, and I can say them back to back with no sense of contradiction.
6. "If the barometer had read low this morning, the storm would have hit this afternoon." I want to say false, because the reading doesn't cause the storm. But if you remind me the barometer is accurate, I'm tempted to say true. I genuinely flip flop on this one.
7. "If the compiler were both correct and incorrect about this program, the deploy would have succeeded." I don't want to call this vacuously true; it just feels like a broken premise. Maybe I can't even evaluate it.
8. "If the asteroid hits next year, it'll be catastrophic." I'd say true, even though I think the asteroid won't hit.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or do some of my verdicts have to go? Give me exactly five numbered candidate principles, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. For each one, tell me which of my verdicts it keeps, which it forces me to drop, and what it predicts about a case I keep going back and forth on: "If the deploy had gone out, the build would have passed."
