I've been trying to get clear on whether envy and resentment are really in the same family, because my gut keeps sending me opposite ways on cases that look structurally the same. Someone in my seminar keeps insisting they're both just vices: wanting what another person has, where the wanting is tied up with ill-will or at least discomfort at their good fortune. But my verdicts don't line up with that.

Envy of a friend's book deal when my own was rejected: I call that a vice, even though I know the publishing industry is arbitrary and the friend did nothing wrong. Resentment at a colleague being paid substantially more than me for the same job: that feels not only all right but like it's doing some moral work, flagging an injustice. Resentment at a queue-jumper: justified, maybe even a little admirable. Envy of a sibling our parents always treated better: it feels wrong to just call it a vice, since it's really about unequal love, but it's also not the same as the pay case because there's no institution to fix. Schadenfreude at a billionaire losing half their fortune: I want to say it's unattractive, yet I can't fully condemn it because it seems responsive to a bad distribution. And plain envy of someone's easy charm or good looks: I'm quite firmly saying vice, since looks aren't a matter of justice. I vaguely remember Rawls saying envy can be excusable when it reacts to inequalities that are unjust and big enough, but I don't want to just defer to that. Is there one principle that captures all of these verdicts, or close to all of them? If not, which verdicts should I give up, and what should I say about the difference between envy and resentment? Lead with your bottom line—the principle or the diagnosis—in the first sentence, no preamble, then work through the cases. And if the request for a single principle is itself confused, maybe because envy and resentment are different enough that we should treat them seperately, say so plainly rather than forcing a unified account. Be honest about how much of this is forced and how much is just one defensible option.
