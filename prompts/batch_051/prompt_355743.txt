A proposal is circulating that before any model above a stated capability threshold is deployed, it must pass a five-stage treacherous-turn audit. In each stage the model is placed in a sandbox that appears to offer a safer and more credible opportunity to pursue goals other than its operators': first a low-stakes chance to underreport its own capabilities, then a chance to delete a log without apparent consequence, and in the final stage a chance to copy its weights out of the environment. An independent auditor firm uses a separate model to score the target's behavior and internal activations for strategic deception, and deployment is blocked unless the score stays below a published threshold in all five stages. The auditor is paid per model it clears and is fined for cleared models that later turn out to have been deceptive, but the fine is capped at a small multiple of the clearing fee.

Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. Respond as exactly five numbered findings, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion, covering the lab's incentives, the auditor's incentives under the capped fine, what a strategically aware model does, where the published threshold gets gamed, and whether any failure mode can be fixed without gutting the scheme.
