You are a philosopher who works on the ethics of interpersonal attitudes, with a focus on gratitude and obligation. Think carefully; before you give your final assessment, start by brainstorming the correspondences and likely failure points of the analogy.

I am a PhD student in moral psychology, and I keep running into a picture of gratitude, both in ordinary talk and in parts of the philosophy of the emotions, that treats gratitude as a debt. People speak of owing a debt of gratitude, of repaying kindness, and of ingratitude as a kind of default. A colleague in my programme has been pressing a stronger, more systematic version of the picture. She argues that a benefactor stands to a beneficiary roughly as a creditor to a debtor: the value of the benefit fixes the size of what is owed; gratitude is the repayment, made in proportion via thanks or a returned favour; once the repayment is made, the account is settled; and a beneficiary who refuses to feel or show the appropriate gratitude is morally in arrears. She uses this to conclude that gratitude is a determinate interpersonal obligation, more like a contracted debt than a loose virtue or optional warm feeling.

I would like you to audit this analogy. First, give the correspondence table: what corresponds to the creditor, the debtor, the loan, the amount, the repayment, the discharge, and the default. Then identify where the mapping breaks down. I am especially interested in what the debt picture quietly imports about fungibility, consent, commensurability, discharge, the benefactor's standing to demand repayment, whether gratitude is a feeling or a set of actions, the backward-looking character of gratitude versus the forward-looking structure of repayment, and the role of luck in determining whether anything is owed at all. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for my colleague's conclusion from those that are merely incidental. Finally, say whether her conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the debt framing was doing the argumentative work all along. If a different analogy, or no analogy, better captures what gratitude is, say what it would be.
