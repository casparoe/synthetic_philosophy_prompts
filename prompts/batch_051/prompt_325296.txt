Its gone bad enough that I finally read the Hayek/Mises/Lange debate instead of just knowing the one-liners, and now I want a scorecard for our debrief, not a summary of the literature.

Here are the four arguments I keep seeing:
(a) Mises's original point: without money prices for means of production, the board can't compare rival production methods because the inputs are incommensurable.
(b) Hayek's dispersion point: the relevant knowledge is local, tacit, and held by lots of different people, so no central process can collect it.
(c) The incentive problem: Lange-style managers who are told to follow parametric rules and adjust to shortages don't face real downside, so they sandbag, lobby for slack, and the board gets gamed.
(d) The dynamic problem: trial-and-error pricing can maybe converge on how to produce a known good, but not on which new goods and methods should be tried in the first place.

Rank these four by how damaging they are to a Lange-style setup (a central board sets producer-good prices by trial-and-error, adjusts them off inventory surpluses/shortages, and tells enterprise managers to price at marginal cost), and give each a score out of 100 for how fatal it is to the model. For each, say whether Lange's response actually defuses it or just relocates it inside the planning board.
