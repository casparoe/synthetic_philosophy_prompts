I'm trying to think about how to define honesty for AI systems, and somebody pointed me to this bit from Augustine's On Lying:

"Wherefore, that man lies, who has one thing in his mind and utters another in words, or by signs of whatever kind. Whence also the heart of him who lies is said to be double; that is, there is a double thought: the one, of that thing which he either knows or thinks to be true and does not produce; the other, of that thing which he produces instead thereof, knowing or thinking it to be false."

Could you help me unpack what this definition is actually doing? I'm especially puzzled about the "has one thing in his mind" condition—it seems to make lying depend on a mismatch between a speaker's inner state and their outward utterance, rather than on whether what they say happens to be false. If I try to apply that to an LLM that confidently produces a false claim, does it just fall outside the definition entirely, since there's no inner state being deliberately contradicted? And if so, what concept should we reach for instead when we want to say the system was somehow dishonest or deceptive? I'm less interested in a verdict than in understanding what each piece of the definition does for us.
