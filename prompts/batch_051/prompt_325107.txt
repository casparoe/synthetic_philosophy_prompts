Okay so I got into a long argument with a friend about the COVID years — the lockdowns, the mask mandates, the vaccine passports, all of it — and whether the whole thing was a sensible trade of liberty for safety or something more sinister. He kept saying people were basically glad to dump their freedom on someone else as long as they got taken care of in return, and it reminded me of the Grand Inquisitor chapter in The Brothers Karamazov. The Inquisitor tells Christ, roughly, that ordinary people can't bear freedom, that it makes them frightened and miserable, and that what they really want is bread, miracle, mystery, and authority — someone to feed them, astonish them, and tell them what to do, while taking the burden of conscience off their hands. I want to actually run that idea against the pandemic rather than just let it sit there as an edgy line.

I'm not really asking whether the restrictions were medically justified; I want the mapping. Treat the pandemic response — the lockdowns, curfews, mask rules, contact tracing, vaccine mandates and passes, the whole aparatus a lot of countries built between 2020 and 2022 — as a case study of the Grand Inquisitor's argument. First do the careful mapping: who is playing the Inquisitor, who is the flock, what exactly plays the role of the bread, the miracle, the mystery, and the authority, and what options the Inquisitor would say people really faced versus what options they actually had. Then run it the other direction: which features of the pandemic the Inquisitor's picture genuinely explains, and where it breaks or misdescribes what was going on — the compliance, but also the resentment, the resistance, the fights over reopening, the mental-health costs, people demanding their freedom back the moment the danger receded. End by saying what the case does to the Inquisitor's position: does it come out looking like a dark prophecy about how people behave under fear, a self-serving story elites tell to justify coercion, or something in between? And on which specific points would you revise the Inquisitor rather than throw him out?
