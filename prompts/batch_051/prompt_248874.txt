I have been following the aftermath of a case in which a large pharmaceutical company paid a substantial fine for downplaying the side effects of one of its drugs. No individual faced charges; the company itself admitted liability, and the fine was ultimately borne by shareholders, many of them pension funds, while the executives who had set the strategy remained in post. I keep noticing a peculiar asymmetry here, and I want to know whether anything can justify it.

We borrow the language of moral blame for the collective without hesitation. We say the company lied, the company concealed, the company deserves punishment. Yet when we apply the ordinary conditions of blame to the same events, we quietly drop each of them. An individual who had defrauded the regulator would go to prison; the company writes a cheque that may already have been priced in as a cost of doing business. Individual guilt requires a culpable mental state, but a company has no mind in which such a state could reside; we reconstruct one from scattered emails, committee minutes, and a decision nobody made in full. And the punishment does not fall on the wrongdoing mind, if there is one, but on owners and employees who may have known nothing at all. If a company is the sort of thing that can lie and deserve punishment, why do we not treat it as the sort of thing that can have mens rea, suffer the penalties we reserve for individual wrongdoers, or be personally shamed? And if it is not, why do we keep the language of blame?

I am after the strongest principle you can find that explains when and why a company may be blamed as a company rather than as a stand-in for its managers, and an honest verdict on whether it survives harder cases — a small family firm, a decision that required the coordination of many people none of whom had the full picture, a company that changes its entire board after the wrongdoing. If the asymmetry cannot be justified, I would rather you say which side should give way than tell me it is merely pragmatic. Please answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you would write a letter.
