In a philosophy of physics course, the instructor keeps saying "identical quantum particles are indistinguishable" as if it settles things, most recently to dismiss a question about whether there is a fact of the matter as to which of the two electrons in a helium atom is where at a given moment. The sentence seems to slide between several different claims. Distinguish the precise theses this slogan could be used to assert, ordering them from the least controversial to the most revisionary—for example, that particles of the same kind share all their intrinsic properties; that no measurement, even in principle, could attach and track a label; that the many-particle state is invariant under exchanging particle labels up to an unobservable overall sign; and that quantum particles lack haecceities or primitive thisness, so "which one is which?" has no answer at all rather than merely an answer we cannot verify. For each thesis, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or likely false, and identify which one actually does the work in the instructor's dismissal.
