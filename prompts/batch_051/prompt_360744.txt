I'm in a reading group that's spent way too many weeks circling around Dennett's three stances, and two of us have finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration instead of just talking past each other. M. is a pattern realist about the intentional stance: he thinks when the stance works it is tracking genuine real patterns in the world, that for a suitably unified agent there is a fact of the matter about whether it believes it is behind in material or wants to control the center, and that intentional vocabulary refers to something actually there, even if it's a blurry and interest-relative something, rather than just being a useful fiction. S. is an instrumentalist about the stance: she thinks attributing beliefs and desires is a predictive trick, no more truth-apt than a forecaster's "the storm wants to go north," and that the success of the intentional stance tells us about our explanatory habits more than about any content sitting inside the system.

They want a joint statement both could sign. Could you draft it? It should open with a numbered list of claims both accept — about the physical and design stances and what they do and don't explain, about when the intentional stance buys you predictive or computational leverage over the design stance, about how indeterminate intentional ascriptions are, and about the cases were the stance clearly fails — with each claim made as strong and non-vacuous as possible without slanting toward either camp. Then include a disagreements table. The rows should give: the central disagreement stated in neutral language, then in wording each side would accept for its own view; the cruxes, the sub-questions that if settled would move at least one of them; and for each crux a concrete test, dataset, or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, like a classifier trained only on behavior that makes the same systematic mistakes the intentional stance makes, or an agent whose internal states are fully known but that still benefits from intentional description, with each side writing down their prediction in advance. They suspect at least one of their fights is verbal (probably about the word "real"), so if some disagreements dissolve once the wording is fixed, say which ones and be explicit about what is left over.
