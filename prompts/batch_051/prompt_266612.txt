I've been going down a rabbit hole on mental imagery after a side project where I tried to get a vision model to do something a bit like mental rotation. I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I expect I'm missing alot of nuance. A blog post I read summarised the Kosslyn–Pylyshyn debate like this:

Kosslyn's experiments showed that people take longer to mentally rotate a shape through a bigger angle, and to scan across more of their mental image, and because the response times rise smoothly with angle and distance the underlying representation has to be depictive, like a picture in the head. Pylyshyn replied that this is basically circular: subjects are just doing what they'd do with a real picture, so all the experiments show is that they have tacit knowledge about how looking at pictures works. He also pointed out that there isn't a little screen in the skull and no homunculus to look at it. Later, brain scanning settled it: early visual cortex lights up during imagery, sometimes topographically, which proves Kosslyn was right that imagery is depictive. After that the field moved on.

How fair is that as a summary? I keep reading 'depictive' as meaning something like a framebuffer or an internal bitmap, and i'm suspicious of the neat ending.
