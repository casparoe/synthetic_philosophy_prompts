But many readers resist stipulations with a certain evaluative or normative content. If a story presents a character's gratuitous cruelty as simultaneously just and beautiful, or asks us to imagine that the betrayal of a vulnerable person was the noble thing to do, readers report that they cannot, or will not, go along. Another treats it as unwillingness: readers could form the imaginative state but refuse, because doing so would feel too close to endorsing the content.
