Me and my friend have been arguing about the difference between an echo chamber and an epistemic bubble. Neither of us has any philosophy background, it came up after a family group chat blew up over politics. She refuses to give a defination and just keeps handing down rulings, so I've been writing them down to try to work out the rule.

So far she's said: someone who only watches one news channel and has almost no contact with the others is in a bubble but not an echo chamber; someone who watches one channel and is also convinced the others are deliberately lying is in an echo chamber; a scientist who reads only her own subfield's journals and never opens adjacent fields is in a bubble but not an echo chamber; a message board that doesn't ban outside links but has a culture of mocking anyone who posts them as gullible or paid is an echo chamber; and someone who reads sources from both sides but thinks every source on one side is owned by the same people is in an echo chamber.

Now I'm trying to figure out what she'd say about three new cases that have come up. One, a person whose news feed is arranged by an engagement algorithm and therefore shows mostly one worldview, but they have no special distrust of the other side; they just never go looking for it. Two, a group chat where, whenever someone posts a contrary article, people reply with links to a site that rates all mainstream outlets as part of a cover-up. Three, a country with one state-run news channel that most people trust, where private media is legal, but most people never watch the alternatives. What would she most likely say each one is, what rule is she actually running, what other rules fit her old verdicts just as well, and which of these new cases does the best job of telling those rules apart?
