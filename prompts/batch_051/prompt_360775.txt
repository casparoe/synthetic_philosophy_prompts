We are surrounded by a culture and an industry devoted to novelty: adventure travel, bucket lists, subscription boxes, constant self-reinvention, and a widespread dread of a boring or routine life. People seem to treat it as obvious that a good life must keep generating fresh experiences, and that a life which repeats satisfying routines is in some way a failure. I want to understand what view of human well-being this practice presupposes. Does it commit people to the idea that novelty is a basic ingredient of a good life in its own right, or can it be explained just as a particularly effective way of pursuing pleasure, achievement, or close relationships? Is the practice really tied to one theory, or is it consistent with several different ones? And if someone concluded that novelty matters only as a means to other goods, what would actually have to change in the way people chase novelty and fear boredom? If I am wrong to expect a single implicit theory here, or the practice does not presuppose anything that definite, say so plainly rather than forcing an answer. Please give exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
