Here is the argument:
(1) Suppose the knowability principle: every truth is knowable — if p is true, then it is possible for someone to know p.
(2) Suppose for reductio that at least one truth is not known by anyone. Let p be such a truth, and let q be the proposition that p is true and no one knows that p. Since p is true and unknown, q is true.
(3) Since q is true, the knowability principle says q is knowable: it is possible for someone to know that p is true and no one knows that p.
(4) In the possible situation where someone knows q, that person knows each conjunct, since knowing a conjunction requires knowing its conjuncts. So the person knows that p, and the person knows that no one knows that p.
(5) Knowledge is factive, so if the person knows that no one knows p, then it is true that no one knows p.
(6) Yet from step (4), that same person knows p, so someone does know p — contradicting step (5).
(7) Therefore no possible situation contains knowledge of q: it is impossible for anyone to know q, contradicting step (3).
(8) So the reductio assumption in step (2) must be false, meaning every truth is known by someone.

The conclusion seems plainly false, but I cannot locate the mistake. Please give a committed answer: state whether the reasoning is valid as written. If a step fails, identify it exactly and give the corrected version; if it is valid, tell me which premise or principle I should reject and why, while saying what, if anything, can be salvaged of the knowability principle.
