In my philosophy class we have been allowed to choose our own essay questions, and I have gone down something of a rabbit hole with Maimonides on animal sacrifice. In the *Guide for the Perplexed*, Part III, chapter 32, he seems to argue that God did not abolish sacrificial worship outright because human nature cannot pass suddenly from one extreme to another, and that the law therefore redirected an already existing practice toward the service of the one God. I have been trying to turn this into a general principle, which I have provisionally named *projective command*: a lawgiver may issue a command whose content he does not consider intrinsically good if, given the current habits and capacities of the people addressed, that command is the best available means of moving them toward a better state and of preventing a worse practice from continuing under an unreformed description.

I think the principle becomes especially interesting because Maimonides seems to have seen the obvious objection and stated it with great force. He writes: "I know that you will at first thought reject this idea and find it strange; you will put the following question to me in your heart: How can we suppose that Divine commandments, prohibitions, and important acts, which are fully explained, and for which certain seasons are fixed, should not have been commanded for their own sake, but only for the sake of some other thing: as if they were only the means which He employed for His primary object?" If *projective command* is granted as a category, we can say that a command is not merely a means but also a projection of an end that the addressees cannot yet bear; yet that may simply restate the problem. My question is whether my concept is a faithful extrapolation of what Maimonides is doing, or whether it smuggles in a modern historicist attitude he would not have accepted. Is *projective command* already known under another name, and what are the three strongest objections to using it as a way of evaluating inherited rules? Do any of those objections show that the concept is incoherent, rather than simply liable to misuse?
