Here are all the tests I know for seperating vicious from benign infinite regresses, and I want a new one. The no-entry test: the only way to take the first step in the relevant sequence is to have already taken a step of that very kind, so the sequence can never get started. The reappearing-term test: the thing to be explained or defined shows up again in the explanation or definition at the next level, so nothing ever gets discharged. The proliferating-entities test: each stage posits another distinct item of the same problematic sort, as in the Third Man. The burden-transfer test: each stage just passes the unmet requirement to the next stage without reducing it. The infinite-task test: the regress would require actually completing an infinite task, and an actual infinite can't be traversed. I want a test for viciousness that is not on that list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of any of them. It can be about causal, epistemic, metaphysical, semantic, or practical regresses — I don't care. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good: does it get at least one standard case right that the listed tests misclassify, and does it accidentally count a benign regress as vicious (e.g., the truth regress from 'p is true' to 'it is true that p is true')? Please write the answer in American English.
