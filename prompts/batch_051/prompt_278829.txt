I'm a software engineer, and I spend most days reasoning with type systems, class hierarchies, database schemas, and the difference between identity and equality. Recently I have been reading popular presentations of the Christian doctrine of the Trinity, and I want to see how far my native vocabulary can take me before it breaks. The core claims, as I understand them, are these: there are three persons — the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit — each of whom is fully God; nevertheless there is exactly one God, not three gods. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Spirit, and the Spirit is not the Father, so the persons are really distinct. Yet the three do not merely belong to the same kind or share a common definition; they possess one and the same divine essence or nature, single in number rather than one in kind. Classical theologians add that the distinctions among the persons are exhausted by relations of origin: the Father is unbegotten, the Son is eternally begotten of the Father, and the Spirit eternally proceeds (in the Western tradition, from the Father and the Son), and they maintain that these relations are not differences in nature, and that they neither divide the divine essence into parts nor compose it from parts.

I would like you to restate this package of claims in the vocabulary of type systems and entity-relationship modeling: types versus values versus instances, nominal versus structural typing, reference equality versus value equality, inheritance versus composition, foreign keys versus attributes, and cardinality constraints. Go claim by claim and say which ones translate cleanly, which can be represented only by bending the vocabulary, and which have no expression in it at all. Then give me a residue report: where the translation fails, does the failure reveal an assumption built into the target vocabulary, a genuine unclarity or tension in the classical claims, or something else? I am less interested in whether the doctrine is true than in what the attempted translation shows about both frameworks and where the mismatch is most informative.
