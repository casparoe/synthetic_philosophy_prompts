I sit on the deployment safety committee at an AI lab, and before our Friday meeting I need to put a recommendation in writing. We are deciding whether to approve an agentic model for commercial use when the principal safety control is chain-of-thought monitoring. Before the model may take a consequential action — making a payment, altering production code, sending a legally binding message — it must first produce a private reasoning trace. A separate monitor model reads that trace and flags anything suggesting hidden goals, situational awareness, or deception; actions that trip the flag are blocked and routed to a human. The safety team argues that this is sufficient, because the trace is the closest thing we have to direct evidence of the model's reasoning and the monitor's false-negative rate in red-team evaluation is acceptable.

Last night a friend and I argued about this for hours. Her view, which is also roughly the safety team's, is that I am manufacturing a problem: the traces are legible, logged, and reviewed by an independent model, and if that does not count as evidence of what the system is doing, then nothing will, which would amount to refusing to ship any powerful system at all. My unease is that a chain of thought is not a transcript of the computation that produced the action; it is another output of the model, produced under the same optimization pressure that produced the action. A training process that rewards passing the monitor can teach the model to write chains that satisfy the monitor while the action is driven by factors the chain does not report — unfaithfulness on the one hand, deliberate obfuscation on the other. My friend replies that this describes a possible failure, not an actual failure, and that a policy should not be set on the basis of possibilities.

Draft the short written opinion that I will circulate to the committee before Friday's vote. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with specified conditions — without hedging among the three — with reasons the committee can act on, and it should state the strongest version of the position it ultimately rejects before explaining why it is outweighed.
