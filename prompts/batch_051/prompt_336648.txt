Early in 2021 a mass of retail traders on r/WallStreetBets piled into GameStop while a couple of hedge funds were heavily short; the stock ran from around twenty dollars to almost five hundred in a few weeks and then collapsed, and at the peak Robinhood and similar brokers restricted buying. I want you to analyze that episode through the lens of no-trade theorems, the idea that with common priors and common knowledge of rationality people shouldn't be able to make speculative gains just by having different information, and the Hayekian idea that a price aggregates lots of dispersed private information into one public signal. Do a disciplined mapping: who are the players, what information and payoffs does each side actually have, where the episode fits the price-as-aggregator story, where it fits the no-trade story, and where each lens stops explaining what happened. Finally, say what the whole mess implies about how seriously we should treat asset prices as epistemically meaningful signals, especially when the price itself becomes one of the main pieces of information traders are betting on.
