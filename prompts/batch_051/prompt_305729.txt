For the last two meetups we've been fighting about the insect sentience scale argument and I dont think we're actually feeling it. The claim, roughly, is that there are on the order of 10^18 insects alive at any given time, and that even a lowish probability — 1%, 5%, pick your number — that an insect has felt experience means the expected number of sentient lives at stake in ordinary decisions (bug farming, pesticides, just existing) absolutely swamps the things we usually treat as huge moral wins. Some people go "right, so insects dominate our moral priorities," and others say "this is exactly why you can't just multiply through tiny credences," and then we go home having changed nobody. I want them to run into the force of the multiplication itself before we put names on it.

Could you design an activity for us — rules, materials, a scoring scheme that doesn't use real money, a timeline for, say, 75–80 minutes plus debrief — where the group has to make a sequence of actual choices under uncertainty about large populations and only afterwards sees that they've been pushed toward the scale conclusion by their own hands? Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and which part of the real argument a classroom game simply cannot reproduce, so I'm not overselling what happened.
