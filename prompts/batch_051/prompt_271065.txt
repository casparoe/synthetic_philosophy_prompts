I have been arguing with a friend who defends a suffering-focused moral view — roughly, the family of positions that treat the reduction of suffering as weightier than the promotion of happiness, and at the limit treat only suffering as having any moral relevance — and our argument keeps stalling because we cannot agree on what "suffering" picks out. The word appears to be doing at least two different jobs: it names a certain kind of felt experience, and it is used as if it were the name of whatever it is that morality most demands we prevent. I would like a working definition that separates those jobs and then survives contact with a battery of hard cases.

Here are the cases that have defeated me so far:
- a person in the grip of an acute migraine, motionless in a dark room, who would accept almost any risk to end it;
- a person left in a bare room with no phone and nothing to do for twenty minutes, who reports feeling restless and faintly bored;
- a patient who reports a sharp, throbbing sensation in a leg they can no longer move, but who insists that the sensation does not trouble them and declines a treatment that would remove it;
- an anaesthetised patient whose body shows a measurable stress response during surgery but who has no experience of the operation whatsoever;
- a person in the first week of mourning a parent, who says the grief feels unbearable but also says they would refuse a pill that erased it because the grief is bound up with love;
- a person who spends an evening in anguished despair after being falsely told that a sibling has been badly injured, before the report is corrected the next morning;
- a person whose world has gone flat and colourless, with no acute pain and no tears, but who says that every hour is a burden and that nothing is worth doing;
- a bystander who takes keen pleasure in watching another person's humiliation while that person is in evident misery.

Please propose at least one definition of "suffering" — and a second, complementary definition if you think the term does both descriptive and evaluative work — then run the definition through each of the eight cases. Tell me which verdicts you are prepared to defend, which cases force you to revise the definition, and whether any case has no settled answer under the definition. I am not asking how suffering should be weighed against other values, or whose suffering matters; I am asking what the property is that these disputes are about. Please write the answer in British English.
