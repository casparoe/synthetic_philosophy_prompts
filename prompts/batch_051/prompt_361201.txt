Act as a professional philosopher of biology who works on the evolution of cooperation. The task is to infer someone's implicit classification rule from a small track record and to predict her next judgement. Think through all promising candidate rules before committing, and be explicit wherever the evidence underdetermines a choice.

I study cooperation, and a behavioural ecologist I know keeps a private library of simplified cases, each assigned to one canonical mechanism: kin selection (helping relatives), direct reciprocity (helping conditioned on past help from the same partner), indirect reciprocity (helping conditioned on reputation), spatial/network reciprocity (cooperation sustained by spatial assortment), and group selection (cooperation sustained because more-cooperative groups outproduce less-cooperative ones). She has a strong track record of assigning new cases to one mechanism, but she has never stated her decision rule.

Her past assignments:

1. Helpers aid close relatives in a well-mixed population with no memory and no repeated pairings: kin selection.
2. Individuals help non-kin who have previously helped them, in repeated pairs with reliable memory: direct reciprocity.
3. Individuals help only those with high public reputation scores, in one-off encounters with no memory of partners: indirect reciprocity.
4. Cooperators spread as a cluster on a static lattice with no memory and negligible relatedness: network reciprocity.
5. In a metapopulation, groups with many cooperators send out more dispersers than groups with few: group selection.

Next week she will be shown a new case: a cooperatively breeding fish population in which helpers are typically siblings (coefficient of relatedness about 0.5), the same individuals interact repeatedly over many months, they remember precisely who helped them, and help is contingent on past help.

What is she likely to name as the primary mechanism, and why? Lay out the plausible decision rules consistent with her track record (at least two should differ on this case), say what each rule predicts, identify the closest precedent for each choice, and then give your best guess with the strongest reasons for it. Finally, specify the single further case you would run next to settle which rule she is actually using, and explain why it would discriminate.
