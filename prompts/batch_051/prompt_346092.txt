I am a novelist, and I have long been puzzled by how thought experiments in science yield knowledge: the experimenter performs no new observation, yet something seems to be discovered. My own craft suggests an answer, and I want to know whether I should trust it. When I draft a scene, I posit a situation — a character under a specific constraint — and run it forward according to what I take to be the regularities of motive, pressure, and circumstance; when the outcome surprises me, I take the surprise as information about what the setup implies. A scientific thought experiment appears to do the same: an idealized scenario is imagined and then run by the rules of the relevant theory to extract a conclusion that outruns observation. Is this a genuine identity of epistemic structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? State the mapping precisely — on each side, what plays the role of the setup, the governing regularities, and the conclusion. Say what would have to be true for a novelist's scene-work and a scientist's thought experiment to be the same kind of cognitive instrument. Then take a committed position, and name at least one thing the parallel would lead me to expect that is false on one side or the other.
