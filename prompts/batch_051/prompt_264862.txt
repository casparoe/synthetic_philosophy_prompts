I want a working definition of *refugee*. The term seems to be doing two jobs at once: it sorts people into a legal category for the purpose of asylum, and it picks out people to whom other states owe protection. Give me the best single definition that can do both jobs, or if one definition cannot do both, give me the two definitions and say which does which — provided the definition(s) get the following cases right:

1. A former opposition politician who was imprisoned and tortured by state security and now lives in a neighboring country.
2. A family that emigrated purely for a much higher salary and better schools.
3. A farmer whose coastal land has become uninhabitable through sea-level rise and whose own government offers no relocation aid or plan; she crosses the nearest border.
4. A family driven out of their village in an ethnic-cleansing campaign, now in a camp two hundred miles away but still inside their own country.
5. A woman who fled abroad after her ex-husband repeatedly beat her and the police told her it was a family matter; she now fears he will kill her if she returns.
6. A man who left his country because gang violence is pervasive and lethal, though no gang ever threatened him personally.
7. A conscript who deserted and crossed the border because he refused to fight in a war he regards as criminal, and who would be imprisoned if sent back.
8. An infant born in a refugee camp to two recognized refugees, with no nationality of any state.

My own confident verdicts are only that (1) is a refugee and (2) is not; I am genuinely unsettled about the rest. After you state the definition, run it through each of the eight cases in order, and say explicitly where it delivers a verdict you would not want to defend, where it gives no verdict at all, or where the verdict turns on a hidden parameter. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts it produces. Finally, tell me what your definition implies about who must take in the people it counts, and whether that second question follows automatically from the classification or needs a separate argument.

Begin your response with the proposed definition as the very first sentence — no preamble, no restating of the question, no signposting — and then give the reasoning.
