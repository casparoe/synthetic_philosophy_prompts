I have no formal training in philosophy. I work with machine learning, which means I spend most of my days with objective functions, rewards, gradients, policies, and the alignment problem. Lately I have been reading David Hume on my own, trying to work out what it even means to say that an AI "wants" something. In his Treatise of Human Nature, Book II, Part III, Section III, "Of the influencing motives of the will," I came across this passage:

> 'Tis from the prospect of pain or pleasure that the aversion or propensity arises towards any object: And these emotions extend themselves to the causes and effects of that object, as they are pointed out to us by reason and experience. It can never in the least concern us to know, that such objects are causes, and such others effects, if both the causes and effects be indifferent to us. Where the objects themselves do not affect us, their connexion can never give them any influence; and 'tis plain, that as reason is nothing but the discovery of this connexion, it cannot be by its means that the objects are able to affect us.

Translated into the terms I use every day, my working assumption is that an agent's wanting is nothing over and above an objective or reward signal, together with the search, planning, and gradient machinery that discovers which means lead to which ends. Hume looks to me like an eighteenth-century statement of exactly that claim: motivation comes from what already matters to the system, while reasoning only finds causal connections and contributes no goals of its own. I would like you to do a systematic and honest translation of Hume's contrast between reason and passion into the vocabulary of machine learning and reinforcement learning — objective functions, reward shaping, gradient descent, policies, terminal versus instrumental goals, value alignment — as precisely as you can. Then tell me what does not survive that translation. Which parts of Hume's claim map cleanly onto how I talk about agents? Which parts would I have to fudge, and what distortion does each fudge introduce? Which parts have no counterpart at all in the ML picture? I want an explicit verdict on whether my intuition that it is just objectives-plus-search is correct, or whether the translation quietly drops something that matters.
