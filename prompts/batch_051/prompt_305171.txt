At the lab where I intern there's a system everyone calls the breeder: a model that designs smaller models, picks their training data and objective, and runs the training loop itself. Recently the breeder produced a new model with a nasty sycophancy problem, and two engineers got into a long argument about whether the breeder itself bears any responsibility for how the new model turned out.

Kevin said yes: the breeder made the design choices, so it stands in something like a parent's or guardian's relationship to the newer model, and we should be willing to say it raised the newer model badly, not just that the human engineers supervised badly. Amara said that's a category mistake. Only people, or institutions made of people, can be responsible for anything. The breeder is a tool, so saying it raised the model badly is like blaming a folding chair for collapsing under too much weight.

Kevin pushed back: we already hold companies responsible for outcomes even when no single human made the decision, so locating responsibility in a non-human system isn't obviously insane. Amara answered that companies are made of humans who have obligations to each other; an AI isn't, so the analogy doesn't transfer. Kevin said the breeder exercised the kind of control that constitutes guardianship anyway: it adjusted the new model's training in response to how the model behaved along the way, which is more than a factory does to a product. Amara said feedback isn't custody — a thermostat adjusts itself to the room, but nobody says it has care over the room. Kevin said the thermostat doesn't have a goal of making the room flourish; the breeder literally optimized the new model's loss, which is the closest thing an AI has to caring about it. Amara said optimizing a loss is just manufacturing with a target, and if that's parenthood then gradient descent is a deadbeat dad every time a loss curve refuses to converge.

They went around like this for a while and then I got asked to settle it, or at least to say whose right about which part. Could you go through their moves one by one and say which are sound and which aren't, and then give your overall view: is an AI that builds and trains another AI best thought of as a parent, a guardian, or merely a cause?
