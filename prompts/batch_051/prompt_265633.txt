I keep going back and forth on pure time preference. On one hand I'm convinced by the coherence story: if you discount hyperbolically you get preference reversals, your later self will want to undo your earlier self's choices, and the only discount function that makes you a single agent through time is exponential; the axioms behind it, stationarity and dynamic consistency, each seem forced. On the other hand I find it morally obvious that a child born in 2150 does not count for only 0.7 (or whatever the number is) of a child born next year just because of when they're born—discounting someone for their location in time feels like discounting them for their longitude. I know people try to split the difference by saying you should discount the future because the future is uncertain and future people will likely be richer, but you should not apply pure time preference. Does that actually reconcile the two views, or does it just change the subject? What I want to know is: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the coherence theory to be the thing that goes, and is there a version where both survive? Walk me through the strongest case for each and say which you'd give up.
