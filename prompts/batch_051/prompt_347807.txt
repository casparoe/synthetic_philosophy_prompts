I'm in a metaphysics course and we just hit the problem of universals, and honestly I'm tired of people jumping straight to "realism vs nominalism" without saying what a theory is even supposed to do. Before I pick a side, I want the full spec. Write me the desiderata that any adequate theory of universals has to meet: the phenomena it has to explain, the distinctions it can't collapse, the constraints from other parts of philosophy or science it can't just ignore. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it — e.g., why the fact that two electrons have "the same" charge puts pressure on a pure trope theory, or why "red is a color" is not the same kind of statement as "this apple is red". Flag which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of a particular camp (like naturalists who won't accept non-spatiotemporal entities, or logicians who want second-order quantification to be ontologically committing). Then say which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular views. If some pair of desiderata can't be jointly satisfied, say so — that's the part I want most. And don't score realism vs nominalism vs conceptualism; I'll do that in class. Start right in with the list, no preamble.
