Write a short story set in a small harbor town that gets flooded with silver after a treasure ship wrecks just offshore. One of the characters, an old merchant who has read Humes essay Of Money, keeps telling anyone who will listen that the town isnt actually richer: money is only a representation of labor and goods, so once prices catch up the silver will just have changed the numbers, not the real wealth. A younger shipwright, though, sees the result with his own eyes: new orders, more apprentices taken on, idle looms starting again. The story should dramatize Hume's distinction between the long-run neutrality of money and the short-run stimulus an influx of money gives to industry, and it should leave it genuinely open whether the old merchant's abstraction or the shipwright's experience better captures the moment. Avoid making either character a mere mouthpiece.
