I've been turning over a structural parallel between reflective equilibrium and the expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm, and I want to know whether it is a real isomorphism or just pattern-matching. On the EM side, one has observed data, parameters to estimate, and unobserved latent variables; one alternates between using the current parameters to compute expected values for the latent variables and using those expectations to choose parameters that maximize expected log-likelihood, iterating to a fixed point. On the reflective-equilibrium side, I read the considered judgments about particular cases as the observed data, the general moral principles as the parameters, and the unobserved right-making features of each case as the latent variables: hold the principles fixed and work out what each case would have to be like for the judgments to come out as they do, then hold those assignments fixed and revise the principles, and repeat until they stop pulling apart. Is this mapping sound, and is the structural identity deep enough that EM's properties would transfer? Specifically, what would have to be true of the moral side for it to inherit EM's guarantee that each iteration does not make things worse, its convergence to a local rather than global optimum, or its sensitivity to the starting point — and which of those, if any, do inherit? Finally, name at least one thing this parallel predicts that turns out false, and say whether the failure is a tolerable disanalogy or a sign that the parallel is doing no genuine work.
