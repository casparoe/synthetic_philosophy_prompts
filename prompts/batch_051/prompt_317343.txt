I manage operations at a shipping company whose safety manual, written in 1998, contains this clause: “The operator of a vehicle shall maintain full and effective control over the vehicle at all times and shall be held responsible for the safe operation of the vehicle and for any injury, loss, or damage arising from its use.” We have since introduced a small fleet of autonomous delivery vans. They select their own routes, accelerate, brake, and change lanes without human input, and they request human approval only when their own confidence falls below a fixed threshold. A mission supervisor watches up to eight vehicles at once from an office and can send a remote stop or reroute command, but cannot continuously steer, brake, or accelerate any individual vehicle.

Last month one van misjudged a corner in heavy rain, mounted a curb, and damaged a storefront and a parked car. The supervisor saw the alert and issued a stop command, but the impact occurred before the command took effect. No one disputes the facts; the question is whether our manual’s clause reaches the case. Since there was no driver in the van, some of my colleagues say no “operator” existed and the clause is silent. Others say the mission supervisor was the operator because she alone had any capacity to intervene. Still others says the company itself is the operator in the only sense the manual can now bear. I want a careful interpretation of the clause, not a prediction about liability law.

How should the word “operator” be understood in a rule written when vehicles had drivers? Consider what the text says, what the clause was for, what its drafters would most plausibly have said if asked about autonomous vehicles, and what a reasonable employee reading it today would take it to require. Which of these approaches should decide the matter, and why? More broadly, when a rule distributes responsibility to a human operator, can it survive the transition to a system in which no human continuously controls the vehicle, or does responsibility fall into a gap?
