I volunteer at a small wetland centre and I've been put in charge of rewriting the animal information boards. The old boards are full of general claims that nobody treats as false — ducks lay eggs, beavers build dams, that sort of thing — even though only female ducks lay eggs and plenty of beavers never build a dam. But our public health adviser wants us to take down a line saying mosquitoes carry malaria, on the grounds that almost no mosquito actually carries the parasite and its only the females of one genus that can transmit it. I cant see what rule we are meant to be applying, or why the first two are fine and the third is supposed to mislead people. What does this practice presuppose about how people should read a general claim about an animal kind? Is there a coherent view behind it, or are we just being inconsistent because one sign scares visitors and the others don't? And would we do better to replace all of them with statistical statements, or would that change what the boards are actually for?
