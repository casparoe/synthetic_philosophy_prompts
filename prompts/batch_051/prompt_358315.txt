In my county, an ordinance enacted in 1937 and never once enforced reads: "It shall be unlawful for any person to keep, maintain, or operate a place where the public may dance to mechanical music." Jukeboxes, record hops, wedding DJs, and nightclubs have operated openly for decades without a single citation. I have just opened a silent dance venue: up to a hundred people dance together in one room, but each hears the music through individual wireless headphones fed from a central stream, and the room itself is quiet. The county has cited me under the 1937 ordinance.

Does the ordinance reach my venue? I want the competing answers produced by (1) the plain text, (2) the policy the drafters most plausibly had, (3) what the drafters would have said about wireless headphones and silent dancing, and (4) the fact that the law has been a dead letter for nearly ninety years. Which of these should control, and what would it mean for the rule of law if a statute like this can, or cannot, be revived by selective enforcement once circumstances change?
