I'm a software engineer and I got interested in the species problem. My homegrown view is something I call species-as-tags. The idea is that a species name works like a git tag: it points at a commit in the tree of life, but the tag isn't itself part of the repo's history; it's a pointer we've chose to place at some spot and could have placed elsewhere. So "same species" just means "falls under the same tag we stuck onto this stretch of lineage." Ring species, asexual clones, bacteria that swap genes sideways — these aren't counterexamples, they're cases where the underlying history is a network rather than a clean branching tree, and we keep insisting the tag has to track a single clean split. The transition from non-bird dinosaur to bird is one long line of commits with no point where the tag has to go. I'm sure this exists under some name I don't know. Is this a real theory about what species are, or just a metaphor? Where does it break? Give me the three strongest objections and tell me whether any of them kills the view rather than just making it uncomfortable.
