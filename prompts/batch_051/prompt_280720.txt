I am a high school student in a philosophy of religion seminar, and we recently spent a session on the classical theist doctrine of divine simplicity. During the discussion, a classmate kept invoking the formula "God is simple" to settle a question I had raised about how a perfectly merciful and perfectly just God could never, even in principle, find those two attributes pointing in different directions. Her response was that my question was confused from the start: if God is simple, she said, then his mercy and his justice are not two distinct attributes at all but one and the same thing under two different human descriptions, so there is simply nothing to be in tension. I can tell she is appealing to something in the actual tradition, but the word "simple" seems to me to cover a wide spread of positions—from a fairly modest claim that God has no spatial or material parts, through the claim that God is not composed in any metaphysical sense, all the way to claims that there is no distinction in God between essence and existence, or even between God and his nature.

Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that "God is simple" could be expressing, and for each one say whether it is close to a platitude, a substantive but defensible thesis, or a thesis that probably should be rejected, and what it commits someone to about the divine attributes? In particular, I want to know which reading or readings, if accepted, would actually license my classmate's inference that questions about God's mercy versus his justice are confused, and which readings would stop short of that result while still fairly being called simplicity. A menu of readings with verdicts would be more useful to me than a single definition.
