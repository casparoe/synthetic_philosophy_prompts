In Isaac Asimov's story Runaround, there's a passage near the middle where Powell and Donovan try to diagnose why Speedy keeps circling the selenium pool instead of fetching the selenium. Asimov has Powell work through the diagnosis roughly as follows: the Second Law order was given casually, from a distance, so its potential was weak; meanwhile Speedy's Third Law self-preservation was strengthened by the danger from the environment; the two came into balance and Speedy was trapped in a equilibrium, circling the pool and reciting snatches of Gilbert and Sullivan instead of obeying.

Now vary one parameter. Suppose everything is the same, except Powell does not issue the order over the radio from inside the dome. He puts on the suit, walks out to the pool, stands right next to Speedy, and gives the same casual order in person: Speedy, fetch the selenium. The environmental danger to Speedy is the same, and the wording is just as casual. Does Speedy still get stuck? Work through what the Laws and the diagnosis passage imply for this variant. In particular, does the physical presence of the human issuing the order raise the Second Law potential in Asimov's model, or is proximity not among the factors he uses? If Speedy would not get stuck, what does that show about which feature of the original case was doing the work?
