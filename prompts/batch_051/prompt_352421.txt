I'm giving a forty-five-minute guest session in a master's-level public policy course. The students have encountered the word "satisficing" and think of it as picking the first option that clears a low bar because you're busy or lazy. I want to leave them with something more precise and more useful: satisficing as a principled response to search costs and ill-structured problems, and a clear sense of why it isn't simply maximizing with the cost of search folded into the utility function. Please plan the session's explanatory arc.

In particular:

- Work out the order in which the components must be introduced, and what depends on what. For example, what has to be in place before the distinction between evaluating options against an aspiration level and ranking options against each other can do any real work, and before the "just maximize the expected value of searching" objection can even be stated precisely.
- Choose the one running example that will carry the idea, and say what it must have and what it must lack so that it supports the point rather than quietly smuggling in a well-defined optimization problem. I was considering the example of a city council that receives proposals for reducing traffic deaths one at a time, doesn't know how many or how good the remaining proposals will be, and can adjust its bar as proposals arrive — but tell me if a different example would carry the idea better.
- Map the misconceptions the students will predictably bring or form, in the order they will arise, and say where in the sequence each one should be headed off. I expect at least these three: that satisficing just means low standards; that it is fine for trivial choices but irresponsible for consequential ones; and that once you add search costs to the utility function, satisficing collapses into ordinary optimization and there is nothing left to explain. Add any others you would expect.
- Specify a check for understanding by the end of the session that would show the students have grasped the distinct idea, rather than merely having learned to use the word for a familiar complaint about human laziness.
- Say what you would deliberately not explain at this level, and how you would mark it as omitted so that it doesn't quietly become a loose end.

Finally, please tell me how confident you are in the main claims of this plan, and single out the point you are least sure about.
