I've been going around in circles about what "knowing how" amounts to, so I wrote down my gut verdicts on a pile of cases and I want to know whether there's one principle underneath them.

Here's where I land:

- My aunt read three books on swimming and can stand on the pool deck pointing out exactly what I'm doing wrong with my catch and kick. She has never once stayed afloat. I don't think she knows how to swim.
- A college friend taught skiing for years and genuinely got half my family carving, but she says her own body never learned the move she teaches and she falls whenever she tries it. I still think she knows how to carve.
- A concert pianist loses both arms in an accident. Years later she can still coach a student through fingerings and pedaling from memory, better than a lot of teachers. I think she knows how to play.
- A club chess player can force the bishop-and-knight mate in almost every game, but he cant state the method and he gets it wrong whenever he tries to explain it. I still think he knows how to do the mate.
- Someone skis beautifully and consistently, but if you ask her what she does she says she turns by leaning back, which is false and not what her skis are actually doing. I think she knows how to ski anyway.

The pair that bugs me is the aunt and the ski instructor: both of them can say how it's done but can't do it, and I gave opposite answers. The pianist feels like a third version of the same thing. Is there a single account of knowing-how that gets all of these, or do I have to give some of them up?
