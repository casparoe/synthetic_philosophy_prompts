I keep getting stuck on the move near the end of the second part: after he’s built the whole idealist case that succession is an illusion and the self is a convenient fiction, he suddenly turns and says something like, and yet time is the material I’m made of, a river that carries me along, a tiger that tears at me, a fire that burns me—and then something like the world, unfortunately, is real, and I, unfortunately, am Borges. I know that’s a rough paraphrase, not the text, so don’t quote him. Could you write me a villanelle, with the two repeated lines being your own renderings of those two voices in that passage: the reflective voice that says time is unreal, and the lived voice that says the world is unfortunately real and we are made of time. The repetition should do structural work: the denial of time keeps returning, and so does the experience that won’t stay refuted. I’d like it to leave the tension open rather than resolve it. After the poem, give me a short note where you say what in your rendering is clearly in Borges and what is a contested or liberally interpretive move, and don’t present a contested reading as the consensus.
