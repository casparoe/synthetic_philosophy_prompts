Exam question: Explain Quine's thesis of the indeterminacy of translation and the role of the gavagai example in his argument. Be sure to say what the example is supposed to establish and what it is not supposed to establish.

Student answer: Quine's indeterminacy of translation is the claim that two linguists could each produce a translation manual for an unfamiliar language, and both manuals could be compatible with all possible observations of the speakers' verbal behavior, yet the manuals would not be equivalent. The gavagai example is meant to illustrate this. When a linguist sees a rabbit and hears a native speaker say gavagai, the linguist can tentatively translate it as rabbit. But Quine observes that the same prompting stimulations would also be consistent with translations such as undetached rabbit part, rabbit stage, or rabbithood. Because all of these hypotheses fit the same stimulus meaning, no behavioral evidence could ever settle which one is correct. This shows that translation is impossible in principle: there is no fact of the matter about what the speaker means, so any manual that fits the observed behavior is just as good as any other. Quine's conclusion is therefore a form of relativism about truth, since the truth of a translated sentence will depend entirely on which manual we happen to adopt.
