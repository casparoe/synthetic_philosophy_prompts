Take this passage from Thomas Aquinas, Summa Theologiae III, q. 75, a. 1, obj. 3:

"Further, no body can be in several places at the one time. For this does not even belong to an angel; since for the same reason it could be everywhere. But Christ's is a true body, and it is in heaven. Consequently, it seems that it is not in very truth in the sacrament of the altar, but only as in a sign."

Use it, together with Aquinas's reply to that objection, as the lens for an analysis of the Marburg Colloquy (1–4 October 1529), where Luther and Zwingli deadlocked over the real presence despite reaching agreement on fourteen of fifteen points. I want the analysis to run in both directions. First, map the positions actually taken at Marburg onto this dialectic: which figure presses the objection's argument, which figure reaches the reply's conclusion, and where the mapping fails — above all because both reformers rejected transubstantiation, so neither was simply an Aristotelian in the Summa's sense. Then say what the lens explains about why they deadlocked and what it cannot explain, such as the Christological dispute over the ubiquity of Christ's body and the semantic dispute over the literal force of the words of institution. Close by saying what Marburg, in turn, shows about the limits of this substance-and-place framework, and whether a semantic or Christological model of the dispute captures more of what actually divided them. Also tell me what would change your verdict and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
