I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about effort itself — when deliberate trying helps and when it only makes things worse — by recording my gut verdicts on a range of ordinary cases. Learning a new guitar-picking pattern with a metronome and slow repetition: works; the repetitions visibly build skill. Studying for a statistics exam by scheduling problem sets and working through them: works. Fixing a leaking faucet by following a step-by-step tutorial: works. But other cases point the other way. Trying to fall asleep before an early flight: the harder I will myself asleep and check whether I feel drowsy, the more awake I become, and sleep arrives only after I give up. Trying to recall an actor's name on the tip of my tongue: the more I strain, the further it recedes, and it surfaces later while I'm washing dishes. Trying to be funny at a dinner party by planning remarks in advance: the jokes land flat because they sound rehearsed, while the things that actually got a laugh were the ones I had not prepared. Trying to comfort a grieving friend by deliberately applying active-listening techniques I had read about: my friend felt handled, and I was better before I had a script. Trying to relax before a presentation by telling myself to relax and scanning my body for tension: the tension worsens; yet deliberately doing box-breathing exercises does relax me at least somewhat, even though that is also an effortful technique. Trying to suppress a worry by telling myself not to think about it: it intrudes more.

My first guess was that effort works for external, bodily tasks and fails for mental or emotional ones, but that does not survive the contrast between the breathing exercise and the failed sleep effort, or between the deliberate guitar practice and the scripted consolation. Is there a single principle that captures these verdicts, or close to it? What would that principle say about two cases I still go back and forth on: setting myself a deliberate program of becoming a more generous friend, and trying to fall back in love with my partner by scheduling date nights after the feeling has faded? And which, if any, of my current verdicts does the principle force me to revise?
