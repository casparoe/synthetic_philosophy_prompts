Last night a friend and I spent a long evening arguing about a single sentence: "Sherlock Holmes had a birthmark on his left shoulder." We agreed that nothing in Conan Doyle states or implies it, and we were evaluating the sentence within the world of the stories rather than as a claim about the actual world. We disagreed about what follows from the stories' silence. My friend held that the sentence is false in the fiction: a fiction fixes as true what it states or implies, and any well-formed sentence it fails to fix as true is thereby fixed as false. I held that the sentence is neither true nor false in the fiction: the stories are incomplete about his left shoulder, and a gap is not a no. I put my credence that the sentence is neither true nor false in the fiction, rather than determinately false, at about 0.7; he puts his at about 0.2.
