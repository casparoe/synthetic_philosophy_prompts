In the history-of-revolutions threads on the rationalist blogs and forums I have read for years, someone will eventually settle a dispute about the Terror by saying that "the revolution devours its children." The line is usually made to do two jobs at once: it explains why a revolution that had purged the Girondins went on to consume Danton and then Robespierre, and it licenses a general prediction that radical movements tend to purge their own most committed members. I can see the phrase doing real argumentative work, but I cannot tell what proposition it is actually asserting. Is it a covering-law claim about all revolutions? A weaker tendency claim about some revolutions under specifiable conditions? A psychological claim about how revolutionary leaders behave once they fear counter-purges? A retrospective metaphor that predicts nothing at all? Lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical, and say for each whether it is plausible, false, or unfalsifiable. Then say which reading or readings would actually support moving from the French case to predictions about later revolutionary movements, and what evidence would distinguish the live readings.
