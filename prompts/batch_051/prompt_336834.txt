Here's the thing I've been circling for a while and I'd like it laid bare. As a journalist I constantly translate between scientists who say a finding "may" do X and scientists who put a number on it — 8%, 40%, whatever. Compare what a modal claim and a probabilistic claim are actually doing, as ideas. Take a concrete pair: "this brain implant could alter personality" versus "this brain implant has a 12% chance of altering personality." What does each one commit me to, and what does each one leave out? I'm less interested in the math then in the conceptual relationship. In particular: does calling something possible require that it have nonzero probability, and does probability zero mean impossible? Can something be possible in the modal sense but have probability zero — or probability one but not be necessary? And where do the two frameworks each fail on their own terms, like one-off events that have no reference class, or unknown unknowns where I cant even list the outcomes in advance? I want the comparison to make visible which jobs each notion is good for, not just a glossary.
