Lately that habit keeps pulling me toward the AI question, and I'd like your actual credence, not a survey of positions. Here's the proposition: by 2100, the dominant evolving lineages on Earth — dominant meaning they control most of the energy, materials, and capacity for persistence and reproduction — will be artificial rather than biological, with human institutions acting as their reproductive machinery for at least part of that run. I've been swinging between about 0.35 and 0.6 and I can't defend either end. What's your number? Then decompose it: how much of the probability hangs on whether the current cycle of training, deploying, and retiring models really is a Darwinian population (variation, differential success, heredity) rather than just a manufacturing process; how much on whether humans can in practice switch it off or contain it over a century; how much on whether selection in a built environment tracks whatever human fitness tracks, or instead tracks intelligence, speed of self-modification, and control of attention; and whatever sub-question I've missed that's quietly doing a lot of the work. Give rough propabilities for the parts, show how they combine into the headline number, and say which part it's most sensitive to. Then tell me what evidence in the next two years would shift your number the most.
