I'm a retired neuroscientist — psychopharmacology, mostly — and for the last year or so I've been reading philosophy of science instead of journals. A local clinic asked me to sit on a panel that's deciding whether to lower the threshold for diagnosing social anxiety disorder in teenagers. Everyone at the table talks as if the disorder is a stable thing sitting out there at some base rate, and we're just choosing how sensitive our instrument should be. But I can't shake the worry that the category talks back. Once the label is applied more loosely, teenagers start describing their ordinary shyness in clinical terms, join online communities built around the diagnosis, and shape their identity around having it. So if we lower the threshold we may end up changing the phenomenon rather than detecting more of it. In the lab that would just be a measurement artifact, but here the classification itself seems to be the instrument. My younger colleages keep saying I'm just dressing up overdiagnosis in fancy language. Is there a distinct philosophical issue here, and would it give me a reason to vote against lowering the threshold? And please don't answer politely for the sake of it — if I've got the setup wrong or the question itself is confused, I'd rather you tell me that and ask a better version.
