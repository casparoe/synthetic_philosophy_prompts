I've been going back and forth on whether the hyperbolic discounting account of weakness of will is an explanation or just a relabeling, and I want you to force the issue instead of splitting the difference. The theory, as I find it compelling: people discount the future in a way that isn't consistent across time. From a distance I genuinely prefer writing my paper Friday night to watching tv, but as Friday night approaches the immediate reward gets over-weighted, the beta in the beta-delta model, and my preference flips. So the akratic person isn't acting against their better judgment at the time of action; there judgment has changed, and it'll flip back afterward with regret. That's appealing because it needs no mysterious will, explains why people precommit and why temptation depends on immediacy, and turns weakness of will into something you can model and maybe even call rational. But I have an intuition I can't shake. When I doomscroll instead of working, it does not feel like my preference flipped. I judge, at the same moment, that working is better and that I should do it, and I still don't. That's a synchronic conflict inside one moment, not a diachronic one between two time-slices. If the model responds that I simply preferred the phone in that moment, it's defining preference by choice, which is exactly the thing that needed explaining. So which should yield? Is my introspection just bad evidence about the time-course of my own valuations, and should I accept that weakness of will just is preference reversal? Or is hyperbolic discounting actually a model of temptation or impulsivity rather than akrasia, and should it be amended, maybe with two coexisting systems, a long-run self and a present-biased self, locked in simultaneous conflict? Or are theory and intuition compatible once you separate the causal mechanism from the phenomenology? Walk me through the strongest version of each side and say what would justify each move, whether it's a decisive experiment, a conceptual distinction, or a better formal result, and if the whole question is ill-posed, say why.
