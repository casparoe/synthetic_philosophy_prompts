So me and my friend were argueing about Carnap last night—well, less about Carnap himself than about the idea that you could make politics and planning rational by making them precise. He kept saying the problem with city planning decisions isn't value conflict, it's that we deliberate in mush, and if you forced everyone to state their proposals in a clean formal language with explicit observation conditions, most of the disagreement would collapse into engineering. I pushed back that choosing the language in the first place is itself a political act, and he said fine, then we just need a process for choosing languages. We went around like that for three hours.

Anyway I couldn't sleep and ended up with this half-serious scheme, which is a stretched version of his Carnap thing, and I want someone to actually play it out with me rather than just tell me if it's good or bad.

Proposal: a city adopts a rule that no planning or regulatory decision can be enacted unless it is filed as a self-contained "framework." Every substantive term in the decision has to be given an explicit definition in terms of publicly observable conditions, and the filing has to list pre-registered observation sentences that would count as the decision succeeding or failing after two years. The framework gets locked at enactment: later on, nobody can say "by affordable we meant something else." If the failure conditions are met, the decision automatically sunsets unless a new framework is filed and passes the same process. And only people who pass a short exam on the framework's definitions are allowed to propose amendments to it.

So how would this actually behave? What would it push officials and interest groups to do—with the definitions, the thresholds, the choice of what gets to count as observable? Who wins and who loses from the amendment exam? When a real dispute is about values rather than facts, does the scheme force that out into the open or bury it inside a technical fight over wording? What are the underhanded ways to make a framework that looks rigorous but is rigged, and can any of those failure modes be fixed without gutting the whole idea?
