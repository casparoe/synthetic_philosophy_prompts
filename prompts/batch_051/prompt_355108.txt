I have been discussing a proposal for reining in persuasive design with a small working group, and we have reached an impasse: we agree the mechanism is coherent on paper but cannot agree on how it would deform in practice. The proposal is as follows. Any online service that combines at least two of four engagement practices—autoplay, infinite scroll, variable-ratio rewards, and push-notification looping—must build in an escalating "attention brake." For each consecutive ten minutes of continuous use, the service has to interrupt the session with a pause screen of increasing duration: thirty seconds at the first trigger, then sixty, then ninety, and so on. If a user has explicitly chosen a personal daily usage ceiling below a board-published threshold, the brake is replaced by hard enforcement of that ceiling. Services may also buy exemption minutes by paying a per-user-minute fee into a common fund that finances deliberately low-pressure public alternatives, such as civic information services, long-form text, and tools for digital self-control. Certified educational and health services are exempt from the brake, with certification granted by a rotating panel under published criteria.

Walk through this as a mechanism rather than as a moral argument. What does it incentivize for platforms, designers, users, and the certification panel in the short run—for instance, redefining what counts as a "session," placing brakes at natural cliffhangers, steering users toward ceilings just below the threshold, or competing for certification in ways that defeat its purpose? What kinds of content and which users are likely to gain or lose, and where does the scheme get gamed first? How would a fee-setting board itself become a pressure point? Finally, which of these failure modes can be fixed by tuning the proposal's parameters—the escalation rate, the fee level, the number of practices that trigger the brake, the certification criteria—and which are structural, so that any version of the scheme with the same basic form would reproduce them?
