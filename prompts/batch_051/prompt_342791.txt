I'd like a deadpan satirical app-store listing, around 500 words, for Ballast, a subscription commitment-contract platform aimed at people with present bias. The copy should take the quasi-hyperbolic β-δ model literally: onboarding asks you to estimate your personal β (with a slider that gently nudges everyone toward 1), pricing distinguishes sophisticated from naive users because only the sophisticated ones really understand why the service is worth paying for, and the premium tier locks you into a contract your later self can escape only through a cooling-off period engineered to feel short when you sign and long when you try to quit. Include a glowing five-star review from a present self and an angry one-star rebuttal from his tomorrow self, plus a clause sending temporal-self disputes to binding arbitration at the time of signing. The humor should be built out of the real mechanics — preference reversal, precommitment as self-paternalism, whether the later self is still the same agent — and the target is the commitment-tech industry's assumption that earlier selves may rightfully bind later ones. Someone who works on akrasia or intertemporal choice should find the parody fair, and a general reader should still find it funny.
