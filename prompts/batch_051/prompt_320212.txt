I keep encountering the idea that an alignment technique is defective unless it provides worst-case rather than merely average-case guarantees, as though these were two points on a single scale of safety and worst-case were simply the more demanding end. That seems backwards to me: a deployed system is not facing a literal adversary, and it will encounter some actual distribution rather than arbitrary inputs, so average-case performance on that distribution looks like the standard that matters. Why is "only average-case" treated as a decisive objection rather than as the appropriate standard for a system no one is deliberately attacking?
