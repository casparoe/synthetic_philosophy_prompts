I am a PhD student in museum studies working on restitution disputes, and I keep finding that the phrase cultural property is used confidently by everyone in the field but defined in ways that fall apart under hard cases. I would like a working definition of cultural property, understood roughly as the kind of object whose removal from its source community and retention abroad can ground a claim for return, that gets the following cases right. The Benin Bronzes removed by the British in the 1897 punitive expedition are clearly in; a mass-produced replica sold in the museum gift shop is clearly out. The borderline cases are these: the Parthenon Marbles, removed under an Ottoman permit whose scope is contested; human remains held in a museum and claimed for reburial by a descendant community; a sacred object sold under a seemingly valid contract in the 1920s but now claimed back as inalienable; and a carved mask collected from a people with no surviving descendants or identifiable successor community. Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, say where it misfires or goes silent, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Lead with your bottom line—the proposed definition—in the first sentence, then give your reasoning; no preamble and no restating of the question.
