Write an essay on what Lewis's triviality results actually establish about indicative conditionals. Many philosophers take them to show that no account that assigns truth-conditional propositions to indicative conditionals can uphold Adams's Thesis — the claim that the rational credence in 'if A then C' equals P(C|A) — without collapsing the probability of the conditional into the probability of its consequent. Assess two replies: (i) the thesis concerns credence at a time, so it need not hold across the conditionalizations Lewis's proofs exploit; and (ii) a truth-conditional account can survive by restricting the thesis to a narrower class of probability functions or by denying that conditionals embed in the usual way. Which side of this controversy is more defensible, and why? Reach and justify a conclusion.
