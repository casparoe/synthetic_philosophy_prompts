In the debate over whether the quantum state is ontic or epistemic, defenders of the psi-epistemic camp often lean on an analogy: the wavefunction is like a weather forecast. A forecast is not a physical feature of the atmosphere; it is a summary of an agent's information and expectations, it gets updated abruptly when new data arrive, and nobody would ask whether the forecast itself exists out there in the sky. The analogy is then used to conclude that the wavefunction is likewise a bookkeeping device for an agent's information, and that asking whether it is real is a category mistake. Audit this analogy in detail. Give an explicit table of correspondences — the weather system, the probability of rain, the forecast's revision, and so on, mapped onto the quantum system, Born-rule probabilities, collapse, and the like. Identify where the mapping breaks. In particular, what does the analogy quietly import about the quantum side — for example, that there is a definite underlying state of the system about which the wavefunction merely records our ignorance, or that measurement is just Bayesian updating on newly acquired information — and are those imports licensed by the formalism? Distinguish the disanalogies that are harmless for the analogy's intended use from the ones that are not. Finally, state whether the psi-epistemic conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether a better analogy is available.
