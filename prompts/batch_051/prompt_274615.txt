Ibn Khaldun's whole theory was that dynasties rise and fall through a cycle tied to asabiyya — the group solidarity that lets a desert tribe seize power, but that then decays once the ruling group goes soft and luxury-loving. Turchin's cliodynamics project basically tries to make this quantitative: he models secular cycles with variables like elite overproduction, popular immiseration, state fiscal crisis, and internal violence, and claims the cycles are at least partly predictable. If Ibn Khaldun somehow got a look at Turchin's models, do you think he'd see them as a natural extension of his own project or as a wierd attempt to force history into equations? Where specifically do you think he'd be sympathetic, and where would he push back?
