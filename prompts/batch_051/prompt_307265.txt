I've been rereading the Four–Seven debate recently while preparing a lecture on emotion and moral motivation, and I keep wanting to see the disagreement play out at its most difficult point rather than in the standard summary. Could you write a dialogue between Toegye (Yi Hwang) and Gobong (Gi Dae-seung) in which Toegye opens with a version of his view that the Four Beginnings—commiseration, shame and dislike, modesty and deference, and the sense of right and wrong—issue from principle, while the Seven Emotions—joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, and desire—issue from material force? Have Gobong press his central objection: the Four Beginnings are already included among the Seven Emotions, so the same felt response seems to get counted twice or assigned to two incompatible sources. Then have Toegye respond not by stonewalling but by revising his position, perhaps along the lines that the Four Beginnings are pure principle with material force following, whereas the Seven Emotions are material force with principle riding on them. Then have Gobong ask whether this "pure" and "riding on" language marks a difference in kind or only a difference in degree of coherence or adequacy. Both figures should be competent and genuinely responsive to each other's actual points; neither should be a strawman. If this framing is historically misleading, or if the disagreement cannot be animated without more textual precision than the prompt provides, please say so plainly instead of proceeding.
