I've been trying to get my views on rule-following into reflective equilibrium, and it isn't working, so I want someone else to look at my case verdicts and tell me what principle, if any, is actually doing the work. I keep oscillating between two rationalist-flavored positions: rules are coordination technology and you should mostly follow them, vs. a lot of rules are legibility theater and the sane move is to quietly violate the dumb ones when nobody's harmed. My gut responses to cases are all over the place:

1. Jaywalking against a don't-walk signal on an empty street at 2am: fine, and I'd be annoyed if someone made a thing of it.
2. Standing on the left side of an escalator in a city where the convention is stand right / walk left: not fine, even though it's not a law and nobody's physically stuck; you're defecting on a coordination equilibrium.
3. A warehouse safety rule requiring a hard hat in an aisle with nothing overhead, enforced by cameras: I'd take the hat off when nobody's looking and not feel bad.
4. A "no outside food or drink" rule at a small indie cinema that makes most of its money on concessions: I follow it, and I sort of judge people who sneak snacks in.
5. The exact same rule at a giant chain theater charging $7 for bottled water: I sneak stuff in with zero guilt.
6. Driving ten over the speed limit on a dry empty highway: fine.
7. Driving five under in the left lane while holding up a line of cars: not fine, actually worse than 6, even though it doesn't break the letter of the law.
8. Wearing jeans to a funeral where the family asked for dark, formal clothes: not fine; I wouldn't round that rule off.
9. Calling in sick to work when I'm not sick but genuinely burnt out and need a day: fine within limits, even though it's a lie and it dumps work on coworkers.
10. Padding a business expense on my taxes by a couple hundred dollars: not fine, even though the probablity of being caught is tiny and the harm is impossible to point to.

The pair that's doing my head in is 3 and 4, because they're structurally the same — private property, owner sets the terms, enforcement is weak — but I treat the cinema's rule as more binding than the safety rule. Is the difference I'm groping at "the rule is doing real work for the owner" vs. "the rule is CYA theater"? Or am I just being inconsistent because one feels like a person with a business and the other feels like a faceless system?

The other pair I can't reconcile is 2 and 7: both are people following the letter of the law and being a problem for everyone else. I want to say the real rule in those cases is "don't obstruct," and the formal rule is just a crude proxy. But if formal rules are just crude proxies there, my respect for them in 4 and 10 starts to look unmotivated.

Tell me what principle, stated precisely, gets all ten of these, or close to it. And please be honest about which of my verdicts your principle can't accommodate. If the only principle that gets all ten is a ten-clause kludge, say so, and tell me which verdicts you'd overturn to get something simpler. Then tell me what your preferred principle says about a case I keep flip-flopping on: a family with young kids who live next door to a neighbor who keeps calling in a noise ordinance over ordinary daytime playing — should they obey the letter of the ordinance, violate it quietly, or violate it openly, and does the fact that the rule is being used as a weapon change the answer?
