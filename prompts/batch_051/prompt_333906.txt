Consider a thesis from Dharmakīrti's epistemology: perception is by nature free of conceptual construction (kalpanāpoḍha) and confined to unique particulars (svalakṣaṇa), whereas everything repeatable, general, or linguistically articulable belongs to conceptual thought rather than to perception. Suppose a philosopher of cognitive science proposes to treat this not as a stipulative classification of mental episodes but as a substantive, empirically testable claim about the architecture of the mind. What would be the strongest experimental design for testing it? Specify the manipulation, the behavioral or neural measurement, and the pattern of results that would favor this picture over the rival view that perception itself categorizes its objects. Then state exactly which part of the thesis, if any, no such test could settle, and whether that residue marks a defect in the operationalization or reveals that the thesis is partly non-empirical.
