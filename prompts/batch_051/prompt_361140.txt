I keep going back and forth with a friend about whether respect is ultimately an attitude and not just a set of actions. His line is: if the "inner respect" does no independent causal work, then it's a distinction without a difference — two people who behave identical toward me are equally respectful, full stop. I think that's too quick. I want to actually test the claim that genuine recognition involves an attitude — seeing the other person as a source of reasons, someone whose objections and claims can get a grip on you — over and above merely polite or strategic conduct. Design the strongest observational or experimental test you can. Be concrete about the situations where the attitude would leak out if it's real: how someone repairs a slight, whether they ever change their mind because of your reasons, how they react when you refuse a hint or push back. Say what pattern would favor the "attitude does real work" view, what would favor the skeptic, and then be honest about what even a positive result would leave unsettled.
