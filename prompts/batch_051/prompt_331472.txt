In my philosophy of technology class we've been on scales for how "advanced" a civilization is, and I keep getting pulled in two directions. The Kardashev approach is the only one I know that gives a single, non-arbitrary ladder: rank civilizations by the total energy they can command, and everything from planetary engineering to Dyson spheres falls out of that one axis. I find that systematic virtue genuinely compelling. But I also can't shake the intuition that a civilization that keeps it's energy footprint small while mastering control at smaller and smaller scales—molecular manufacturing, femtosecond chemistry, eventually Planck-scale manipulation—is more advanced in a way the energy scale completely misses. Barrow's counter-scale is supposed to capture that, but it feels like it's changing the subject rather than fixing the energy-based measure. So I want a negotiated verdict, not a side: what would it take for the small-scale intuition to be debunked and dropped, what would it take for the energy-based theory to be amended or abandoned, and is there a reading of "advancement" on which both survive?
