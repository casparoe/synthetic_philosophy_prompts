Ok so I'm on the leadership council of a small contemplative community (more like a lay order than a full monastery). Our founding rule from 1921 has this line: "Let no one speak of what is given in the prayer of quiet, for words defile the gift." The drafters were clearly thinking about forbidden conversations, spiritual testimonies, that kind of thing. He says they're not communication, they're "residue" of the experience, like sweat after a run. Another council member says that's obviously "speaking" in the sense the rule cares about: he's externalizing and showing others something about the content of his experience, even if he never says a word. We have to decide whether the rule has been violated and what, if anything, to do about it. I'm supposed to bring a short analysis to our next meeting. Can you write it? I need it to work through what "speak," "given," and "defile" mean here, using the usual approaches: plain words, purpose of the rule, what the 1921 drafters would probably say if asked, and what a reasonable member today would take it too mean. Also, the deeper thing bugging me: if the experience is really ineffable, can a painting even be about it enough to count as speech? Or does treating the painting as speech secretly assume the experience has some shareable, propositional content? Be concrete and not too long – this is for a council meeting, not a journal.
