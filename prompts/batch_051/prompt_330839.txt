I am a PhD student in the philosophy of technology, working on the ethics of artificial intelligence, and I would appreciate help thinking through a disagreement among colleagues. Several of us who work on advanced AI have drafted a short written compact stating what we will owe one another if one of us contributes to building a machine with radically superhuman general intelligence: once the technology is stable, whoever is in a position to do so will set aside a fixed share of the resulting resources and influence for the others, and will consult the rest of us before any irreversible deployment. One friend says the compact is empty. By the time performance falls due, he argues, the person who would carry it out may have been so altered cognitively and motivationally, possibly in ways we cannot now imagine, that there is no clear sense in which that person is the same individual who signed, and no determinate meaning to consulting us or sharing influence in a world changed that much. Another friend replies that this misses the point: the compact matters now, because it changes what each of us is willing to work on and disclose to one another, and because even a shaky future commitment can coordinate our present choices. I am torn between these views. Could you help me think through whether the first friend's objection undermines the compact, whether the second is right that its value could lie elsewhere, and what would make such an agreement more than a gesture? Please answer in American English, in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you would in an email.
