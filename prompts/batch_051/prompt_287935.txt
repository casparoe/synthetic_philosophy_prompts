I've been trying to get my head round this idea people call Moloch or a race to the bottom, where everyone makes the sensible move from their own point of view but the group ends up somewhere nobody wanted. I want a made-up example, but not the usual company undercutting wages or countries lowering environmental standards. I want one where the thing everyone is competing over is purely social: being seen as properly committed to a group. The trap should be that each person shows a bit more commitment than the last, and everybody can see where it's headed, but backing off looks like disloyalty or letting the side down.

Construct me a realistic case with these specs: one ordinary setting (a local club, neighbourhood, congregation, online community, parents' group, something like that); no bosses, no money changing hands, no laws or formal rules forcing anyone; nobody is villainous and nobody wants the end result; each step is reasonable given what others just did; and the end state is worse for every single person than where they started, even by their own lights. Then walk through it. For each step, explain the local reasoning that makes someone go further, and why not going further would cost them. Then say what the equilibrium is and why it's stable. Then show it's Pareto-inferior: everyone would rather have the starting state, but nobody can unilaterally get them there.

After that, tell me which structural feature of the case is actually doing the work — I'd guess something about the thing they're competing over having a fixed ranking, so one person's gain is someone else's loss, but you tell me. And what would happen if you removed that one feature: would the trap collapse into a normal coordination problem, or just a different trap? Finish with how somebody could argue this isnt really a race to the bottom at all — maybe they'd say the signalling is itself a thing the group wants, or that the supposedly worse end state reveals people's true preferences — and whether that objection can be answered. Please write your answer in British English.
