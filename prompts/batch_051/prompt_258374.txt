I keep catching myself in an asymmetry about my own future selves and I want to know if it's defensible. When I'm unsure about a job, or about moving in with someone, I think the rational move is to go with the verdict of the calm, well-informed version of me who has actually thought it through — even when I suspect that person would reverse my current inclination. I treat that future self as an authority. But I don't give the same person a vote on other things. I love loud, messy music; I'm fairly sure a calmer, older, better-rested version of me will find it obnoxious, and I don't take that as a reason to turn it down in the car. Same with who I find attractive, and the fact that I like my food so spicy it hurts. I wouldn't call those mistakes my wiser self will quietly correct. I half-worry that what I'd think on reflection is just a way of laundering whichever preference I'm already most embarrassed not to have. But maybe that's too cynical. Why does the post-deliberation me get a veto over jobs and relationships but not over music, attraction, and food? Give me the strongest principle that draws the line exactly where I seem to draw it. Then tell me honestly whether it survives this case: the older self who hates my music has heard more of it than I have, not less — he has more musical experience and still hates it. If the principle can't survive that, say whether the asymmetry should be given up, reversed, or kept on some weaker basis.
