Right, I'm trying to get my position on machine consciousness and which computations matter into something I can defend at a seminar. Here's where I've ended up:

1. Sentience is a matter of functional organisation, not materials. If you work out what the neurons in someone's brain do, and reproduce that same pattern of causal work in something else, the duplicate is sentient in just the way the original is.

2. So I think a neuron-for-neuron brain emulation running on silicon would have the same moral status as the person it emulates: if the biological original would suffer in a situation, the emulation would too.

3. But I don't think the China brain feels anything. If a billion people with radios replicated the functional organisation of a brain for an hour, there would be no single feeling subject there, and nothing it is like to be the network as a whole. The people remain a billion seperate minds.

4. I also think a blockhead — a giant look-up table that replies to everything I could say exactly as I would, but has no monitoring, no aims, just a precomputed list — is not a subject and cannot suffer, whatever conversational tests it passes.

5. I lean towards the view that suffering requires a single integrated subject, not just a lot of independent computations that happen to produce the right output. So a warehouse of calculators jointly solving the same computations a brain solves isn't in pain, even if their combined output matches a brain's.

6. But I do think a detailed simulation of a mouse brain running on an ordinary server farm is conscious and can suffer, and I'd say it would be wrong to run it with the simulated analogue of no anaesthesia just because it's made of servers rather than cells.

So that's the set. I can't tell where the load-bearing problem is. Could you go through and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix is for each? And finally, which single revision cleans up the most? Also, because I don't want to come away overconfident: for each tension you flag, say what argument or consideration would change your mind about how serious it is, or where your reasoning about it is most likely to fail.
