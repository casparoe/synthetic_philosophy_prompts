You're a professional political philosopher who works on collective action and the ethics of voting. I'm chairing an adversarial collaboration between two members of a reading group, and I'd like you to draft the joint statement they'll sign at the end. Please think carefully and do a bit of brainstorming before you write the final version.

Their dispute is about whether a left-leaning voter in a safe Conservative seat (think UK general election) should vote for Labour as the lesser evil or for a small socialist party that better expresses her actual views. One of them, the marginalist, thinks the causal effect of a single vote is the only thing that could ground a duty to vote one way or another, and in a safe seat that effect is near enough zero, so the expressive case wins by default. The other, the lesser-evilist, thinks the expected disvalue of letting the worse outcome stand is so enormous that even a miniscule chance of contributing to it outweighs the expressive considerations, and that voting for the small party is moral dandyism.

Draft the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. For the table: each row states a precise residual disagreement, phrased so each side would accept the wording of their own view; then a crux, meaning a sub-question which, if settled, would move at least one side; and where possible a concrete case or bit of evidence both would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The hardset part is seperating genuine disagreement from the two of them talking past each other about what counts as a causal effect of a vote in a safe seat. Don't let either side smuggle their conclusion into the agreed claims as something trivial.
