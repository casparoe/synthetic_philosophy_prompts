Two people in my building got into an argument at a dinner I was at, and I haven't been able to sort it out. It started over whether selling a kidney should be legal. One said it plainly should be: it's a consensual exchange between adults that saves a life, and banning it only pushes the trade underground, which is worse for everyone. The other said the real objection is not squeamishness but the fact that once a body part carries a price, a person's relation to their own body quietly becomes a relation of ownership and inventory; the price doesn't just move a thing, it teaches everyone that this sort of thing is moveable. The first replied that every job already rents out our bodies and time, so the other needed a line between working and selling organs that isn't just custom. The other said the line is that a worker's consent is ongoing and revocable, while losing a kidney is one-way and permanent; the first answered that people also sell houses they depend on. From there the argument moved to price gouging after a hurricane. The first said that letting bottled-water prices rise rations scarce water to those who need it most and draws in outside sellers, so it's better than empty shelves. The other said that's exactly a case where consent is empty because the buyer has no acceptable alternative, so calling it a free exchange just assumes the very thing in dispute. The first replied that the buyer still prefers paying the high price to having no water, which is the only comparison that matters. Finally they reached vote selling, where the first said the other's own standards should make it clearly permissible, since the seller faces no duress. The other said a vote is not a private possession at all but a share in collective self-government, so selling it wrongs the other citizens rather than only the seller. The first answered that the same collective-interest logic could then ban paid surrogacy or selling blood plasma, which also save lives. They never settled it. Can you go through the exchange point by point and say who is right about which specific claim, where each argument breaks down, and what both of them are missing about the differences among these three markets?
