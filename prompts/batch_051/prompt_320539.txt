I’m a journalist covering science and ideas, and I’m trying to understand a deep tension in hospital practice around death. Consider this: a pregnant woman suffers a catastrophic brain injury and is declared brain-dead under the statutory definition that treats irreversible cessation of all functions of the entire brain, including the brainstem, as death. The death certificate is filed, and legally she is dead. But if her fetus has a plausible chance of survival, the hospital may keep her body on a ventilator, provide intravenous nutrition, monitor hormones and fetal growth, and adjust fluids and electrolytes. The same institution will not treat her as a living patient for purposes of consent, but it will treat her body as something that must be maintained as an integrated physiological system. This is not a single case; it is an emerging practice sometimes called maternal somatic support after brain death.

The official rationale for whole-brain death, most influentially set out in the 1981 U.S. President’s Commission report, was that the brain is the master integrator of the body: once the whole brain stops working, the body loses its integration as an organism, and death follows. Yet the somatic-support practice seems to assume that a brain-dead body can remain sufficiently integrated to sustain pregnancy for weeks or months, sometimes with wound healing and other systemic adjustments.

What theory of death does this practice presuppose? Does it rationally require, or merely fit with, a higher-brain or personhood definition of death, in which what matters is permanent loss of consciousness and capacity for thought rather than loss of organismic integration? Or can a consistent whole-brain proponent accept it by distinguishing the life of the patient from the biological functioning of the body? Reconstruct the implicit theory, separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely tolerates, and tell me what would have to change in the policy if the presupposed theory were replaced by the circulatory-respiratory definition of death.

After the main analysis, give me a JSON object with these fields:

- implicit_theory: a one-paragraph reconstruction of the view of death the practice appears to presuppose
- strictly_presupposed: an array of claims the practice cannot easily operate without
- merely_compatible: an array of claims that fit the practice but are not required by it
- rival_views_ruled_out: an array of views of death that the practice, if described accurately, seems incompatible with
- changes_if_circulatory_definition_adopted: a one-paragraph explanation of how the policy would have to change if death were defined solely by irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory functions
