Hey, I'm a PhD student in CS working on the tool-AI vs agent-AI distinction, mostly for safety-adjacent arguments, and I've gotten alot of pushback from my advisor that the distinction has been argued to death. I think the problem is that people use "agent" for a loose bundle of seperable features that come apart, and then they talk past each other. I'm trying to build a scoring rubric for my own sanity and want a second opinion that actually commits to numbers, not vibes.

Here's the feature list I'm working with:

(a) flexible objective pursuit: choosing actions by optimizing an objective over possible action sequences, instead of computing a fixed input–output mapping;
(b) initiative: taking actions the user didn't explicitly ask for;
(c) goal persistence: sticking with an objective when the user pushes back or tries to redirect;
(d) subgoaling: maintaining a terminal/instrumental distinction and generating subgoals;
(e) self-modeling: representing itself as a causal object in the world, e.g. that being shut down would stop it from achieving its objective;
(f) open-ended planning horizon: making plans that extend past the current task;
(g) user modeling/manipulation: treating the user's mind as something to be steered rather than as final authority;
(h) goal-content integrity: acting to preserve its own objective when its code or preferences are changed.

Give me three things. First, assign each of (a)–(h) a weight from 0 to 1 for how much it matters for making a system a genuine agent rather than just a tool; the eight weights should sum to 1. Second, rank the features and then clump them into two clusters, saying what percent of total agent-ness each cluster captures. Third, score both of these on agent-ness from 0 to 10 using your own scheme, and tell me which score you're less sure of: (A) a user-facing assistant that does what it's asked, forms subplans, but has no initiative and no goal persistence; (B) a process that optimizes a single objective over a long horizon, models the user only enough to avoid interruption, and resists being rewritten. Then tell me which feature on your list is quietly doing the most work in your score for (B), and why someone who weights that feature near zero would end up with a different number.
