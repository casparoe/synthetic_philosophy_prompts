I am the recording secretary of my school's Student Government Constitutional Review Board. Our constitution is detailed in some places and entirely silent in others, and the Board keeps being asked to settle what happens when the rules run out; our advisor refers to these as interregnum cases. We treat our own prior decisions as binding, and five of them keep coming up whenever a new gap appears:

1. Two years ago, the vice-presidential election ended in an exact tie and the election code had no tie-breaker. The Board ruled that the sitting vice president would hold over past the end of her term as acting vice president, with only caretaker powers, until a rerun could be held within three weeks.

2. When the Assembly missed the constitutional deadline for passing the annual budget and no provision stated what would happen, the Board ruled that the previous year's budget would continue in force, adjusted only for inflation, until the Assembly passed a new one.

3. The secretary resigned while the deputy secretary position was already vacant. The Board ruled that the president could appoint an acting secretary, that the acting secretary could not alter election records or procedures, and that the Assembly had to confirm the appointment within two weeks.

4. The constitution allows recall of the president but does not say who governs while a recall election is pending. The Board ruled that the president remains in office with full powers until the recall is decided, on the ground that suspending the elected officer before the vote would let the gap in the rules itself decide who governs.

5. The constitution gives the Assembly the exclusive power to charter clubs and says nothing about charters that expire over the summer. The Board ruled that an expiring club may continue operating provisionally until the Assembly acts in the fall, but may not spend funds or reserve rooms.

Our new case: two days before inauguration, the Elections Commission disqualified the president-elect for an eligibility violation; the vice president-elect's own election is undisputed. The constitution contains a line of succession for vacancies in the presidency, but it says nothing about a president-elect being disqualified before taking office. The outgoing president's term ends Friday. The outgoing president argues that she should hold over as acting president until a new election can be held; the vice president-elect argues that she should take office, at least as acting president; and the Assembly argues that it should elect an interim president directly.

I do not want a bare vote. I want the reasoning. What should the Board decide if it wants to be consistent with its own prior decisions, and which prior decision is the closest analog? If those precedents underdetermine the case, say which general principle we would be adopting with each possible outcome. Also identify the candidate principles that could jointly explain all five prior decisions, say what each predicts about this case, and then describe one further case, concretely, that would most cleanly separate those principles before we decide. Finally, say how confident you are in the overall analysis and which point you are least sure of.
