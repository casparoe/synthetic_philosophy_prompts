René Girard's account of scapegoating holds that a community under internal pressure discharges its accumulated conflict onto a single victim, whose expulsion or killing restores unity. On this account the mechanism works only because the participants misrecognize what they are doing: they believe the victim is genuinely guilty and the violence is legitimate, so no one in the crowd sees the operation as sacrificial. Now consider a form of the same process that this picture does not anticipate: an online shaming campaign in which the "community" is an unbounded and shifting public, the violence is social rather than physical, and the entire episode is archived, narrated, and commented on in real time. Suppose, further, that many participants and onlookers openly describe what is happening as a witch hunt or scapegoating, and some even say they believe the target's punishment is disproportionate—yet the convergence still destroys the target's reputation by the end of the week. Does Girard's argument survive this development? Which premise—unanimity, misrecognition, the bounded community, the finality of the killing, or something else—is actually under pressure, and can the mechanism be repaired so that it still explains what happened? If it cannot be repaired, what exactly remains of the account?
