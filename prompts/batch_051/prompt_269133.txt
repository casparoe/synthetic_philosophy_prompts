I'm organizing a year-long reading group on the foundations of gauge theory — half physicists, half philosophers of physics — and we want to close by drafting a document that the two factions in the room can both sign. Please write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions about what is real in a gauge theory. The first position, which you should call the redundancy view, holds that gauge freedom is purely descriptive: the gauge-variant aspects of the gauge field are surplus structure, and all physical content is carried by gauge-invariant quantities such as field strengths and Wilson loops. The second, which you should call the gauge-structure view, holds that the connection (the vector potential, in electromagnetism) and its behavior under gauge transformations encode physically significant relational facts — how charges at separated locations are coupled, what happens to fields in a bounded region, how local descriptions are glued together — so that not everything gauge-variant can be dismissed as representational fluff.

The statement must have four parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims that are neither vacuous nor purely verbal. Second, a disagreements table in which each view is stated in wording its own advocates would accept as fair. Third, for each disagreement, the one or two cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. Fourth, for each crux, a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use the Aharonov–Bohm phase and the treatment of electromagnetic fields in a finite bounded region as at least two of the cases; keep the physics to electromagnetism except where a non-Abelian point is doing real work. The document should not decide who is right; its job is to make the dispute precise and productive. Keep the whole document to no more than 900 words.
