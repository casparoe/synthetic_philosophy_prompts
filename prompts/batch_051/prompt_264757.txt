I’ve been on a county work program called Bridges to Work for about a year, and I’m trying to figure out whether I’ve broken the rules or not. My participant handbook says, under Section 4.2: "Paid work means any activity for which the participant receives or expects to receive money, goods, or other valuable consideration. Participants must report all hours of paid work within five days, and the grant is reduced by $1.50 per reported hour."

The situation is this. For the last couple of months I’ve been playing one of those phone apps where you do little game tasks and it pays you real money to PayPal. It’s not much: last month I had the app open for about 26 hours and got $11.47. I didn’t report it, because to me a game is a hobby, not "work." My friend says I’m in trouble, that the clause says "any activity" and I should have reported all 26 hours, which would reduce my grant by $39 — more than I even earned, so I’d basically be paying to play. My sister says the people who wrote this in 2015 meant actual jobs (mowing lawns, bussing tables, that kind of thing), not a phone game that pays four cents an hour, and they’d never call it paid work. She also says even if it is work, I shouldn’t have to report 26 hours, only the hours the app actually logs me as playing, which was 9, or maybe only the $11.47 worth of hours, which is about an hour and a half at minimum wage. The handbook says you can loose the whole grant if you fail to report, so this isn’t just a technical question for me.

What I want to know is: under Section 4.2, is what I did paid work, and if so which hours do I report? Walk through what the plain words say, what the point of the rule is, what the people who wrote it in 2015 would probably say if they saw my PayPal reciepts, and what a reasonable person in my position would think the rule means. Then tell me which of those should actually decide it and why.
