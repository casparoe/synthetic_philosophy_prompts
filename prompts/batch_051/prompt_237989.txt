I have been working toward a view I call the Consent Criterion, and I would like an honest assessment of it. The claim is that the entire moral quality of a deed is settled at the moment a person inwardly consents to it, and the outward act adds nothing. By consent I mean a settled interior decision to do the thing, not a passing impulse. Thus someone who has fully resolved to betray a friend and is stopped only by a car accident is, on my view, exactly as blameworthy as the person who carries the betrayal out; and someone whose hand is physically forced into signing a harmful contract, or who causes harm while drugged and never decided to do so, is blameless because the interior consent was absent. I realize this seems to make the external world morally irrelevant and to excuse a great deal. What I want to know is whether the principle is coherent, whether it already exists under some name I have not encountered, and what the three strongest objections to it are — and whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
