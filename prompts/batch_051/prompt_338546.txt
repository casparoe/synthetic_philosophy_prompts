I'm in a philosophy of cognitive science course and we've spent the last three weeks on nativism vs. empiricism, and I've started to think the debate is stuck in a way my professor doesn't want to admit—its not that the arguments are bad, its that both sides seem to accept all the standard data and just interpret it differently. The nativist points to poverty of the stimulus: kids reliably end up with more than the input could possibly give them, so some structure has to be there from the start. The empiricist says the input is richer than the nativist admits, and connectionist models can soak up a huge amount of statistical structure without any explicit rules. Then the nativist says those models only work because somebody baked in the right architecture or training regime, which is just innateness smuggled back in under another name. The empiricist says that charge only works if "innate" is so broad it covers any domain-general learning capacity, at which point the nativist isn't saying anything distinctive.

Then you get the infant cognition stuff, and it's the same pattern: everyone looks at the same looking-time results about objects, number, and agency, and one side calls them early knowledge while the other calls them low-level perceptual biases, and both act like the data obviously favors them. My working theory is that "innate" is doing a lot of the damage because it slides between present-at-birth, genetically specified, not learned from experience, and domain-specific. But I suspect that's too tidy, because even when people carefully define their terms, neither side moves.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it verbal, a substantive empirical disagreement that's just underdetermined by the current evidence, a difference about what counts as an explanation or where the burden of proof sits, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And on your diagnosis, what would genuinely count as progress—is there an experiment, a formal result, or a distinction that could actually move it, or is the loop going to keep looping?
