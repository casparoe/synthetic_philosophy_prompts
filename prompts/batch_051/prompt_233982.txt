I sit on the rules committee of a large professional association. Our bylaws contain Article IX: "These bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds vote at any general meeting, provided that the text of the proposed amendment was circulated to the membership at least fourteen days in advance." At last month's meeting, a member moved from the floor to amend Article IX to read: "These bylaws may be amended by a majority vote at any general meeting; no advance circulation is required." The motion passed 78–22, but its text had not been circulated in advance. One member now challenges the result, claiming the amendment is void because its own passage failed the very requirement it purported to repeal. The sponsors answer that Article IX must be able to govern its own amendment, since otherwise the association could never revise its amendment procedure. Please draft the committee's decision memo. It should recommend a definite disposition and give the reasoning for it, including an explicit view on whether a rule can coherently state the conditions under which it is itself changed. It should also treat the strongest argument for the losing side honestly rather than as a mere procedural technicality. Please make clear whether the memo ultimately adopts the position that the amendment is valid, that it is invalid, or that it takes effect only for amendments proposed after the members have been given the notice the old rule required.
