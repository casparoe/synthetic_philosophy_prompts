Construct a pair of ordinary-life cases that pull akrasia apart from hyperbolic discounting. Use these definitions throughout. An exponential discounter discounts a reward delayed by t by δ^t, with δ constant, and so never reverses a ranking between two dated rewards; a hyperbolic discounter discounts by 1/(1 + kt) and may reverse a ranking between a smaller-sooner and a larger-later reward as the earlier reward draws nearer.

In the first case, the agent must be an exponential discounter who is nonetheless akratic: she acts freely and intentionally against her concurrent all-things-considered judgement, with no preference reversal and no change in her discount function. In the second, the agent must be a hyperbolic discounter who reverses a preference, acts on the reversed preference, and yet is not akratic in the strict sense, because she does not violate her own concurrent all-things-considered judgement. Both cases should concern mundane decisions — eating, spending, sleeping, or studying — and neither may involve addiction, compulsion, coercion, or mental illness.

Then provide the check. For each case: state the relevant preferences at each time, verify that the stated discount function actually holds, and say exactly which feature of the case makes the first agent akratic without hyperbolic discounting and the second time-inconsistent without akrasia. Finish with the strongest defence a theorist could give of the view that the first case is really hyperbolic discounting in disguise.

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence — the single feature that permits the two cases to come apart — and then give the cases and the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
