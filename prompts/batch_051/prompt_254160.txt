I work as a journalist covering the history and philosophy of science, and I recently read a synthesis whose argument, as I understand it, runs as follows. The Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution were not features of the past itself; each was imposed on the record after the fact, usually by people with something to promote. Petrarch and his successors invented the Middle Ages as a term of abuse for the centuries between antiquity and themselves. The Renaissance was a piece of self-fashioning by fifteenth- and sixteenth-century humanists that Burckhardt later turned into a scholarly period. And the Scientific Revolution was consolidated by mid-twentieth-century historians who wanted a single heroic rupture between a benighted past and a modern scientific present. The author draws the strong conclusion that these are empty categories: the primary sources show no clean discontinuities, only continuous piecemeal change, and working historians who still use the labels are retailing a myth. The conclusion appeals to me, but I cannot tell whether the author has earned it or has simply slid from a true point about labels to a questionable point about the past itself. How fair is this account of the historiography? Please separate what is accurate, what is compressed to the point of misleading, and what is genuinely contested, and say whether the inference from these categories being retrospective constructions to their being unreal or useless actually goes through.
