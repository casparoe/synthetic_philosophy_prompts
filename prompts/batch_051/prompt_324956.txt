I've been trying to make sense of the units-of-selection debate for something I'm writing, and a friend and I got stuck on a question I think is a bit deeper than the usual selfish-gene-versus-group-selection summary you see everywhere. On one side there's the gene's-eye view you find in G. C. Williams and especially in Dawkins: what persits across generations is the replicator (roughly, the gene), organisms are vehicles, and traits like worker sterility in ants or bees are explained by a gene increasing its copies in relatives rather than by any selection at the level of the colony. On the other side there's the multi-level selection view associated with David Sloan Wilson and Elliott Sober. That's not the old for-the-good-of-the-species story; it says you can get genuine group-level adaptations when between-group selection is strong enough to outrun within-group selection against altruists, and it often claims the gene's-eye view is just a bookkeeping trick that refuses to talk about the causally relevant level.

The case I keep coming back to is a sterile worker honeybee helping her mother rear reproductive siblings. The gene's-eye view says the genes underlying worker sterility are spreading because copies of them are being passed on through those siblings, while the worker itself is a dead end. The multi-level selectionist says no, look at the colony: colonies with more cooperative workers leave more offspring colonies, so the colony is a real unit of selection, and if you insist on counting everything at the gene level you miss that. So my actual question is whether these are genuine rival explanations, or just the same process described at different levels. Are there cases where the two frameworks would actually disagree about a prediction or about which experiment to run, or is this mostly a dispute about what counts as an explanation? And where the disagreement is substantive, which view should carry the day, or should we be pluralists?

I'd like you to compare the two frameworks directly and give me your honest verdict on whether they are rivals, compatible, or one just reduces to the other.
