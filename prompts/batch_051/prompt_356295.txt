I follow politics pretty closely, not an academic, and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for. A party wins an election and then starts changing the rules themselves: stacking courts, rewriting election law, capturing or defunding the public broadcaster, gerrymandering, tinkering with how votes are counted. Each move is legal — passed by parliament or by referendum — and each is sold as an ordinary reform or anti-corruption measure. Elections keep happening, and the same party keeps winning them, but each election is a little less of a real contest than the last. It's not a coup, and at no single step does the country plainly stop being a democracy. Commentators clearly have some category in mind when they group Hungary and Poland and Turkey together, but then they argue about whether the US belongs, which makes me suspect the category might just mean "governments I dislike." Does this pattern have an established name? If it does, what exactly counts as an instance and where are the edges — how would you tell a normal court reform from a move in this pattern? And if it doesn't have a name, what should it be called, with a definition that doesn't collapse into ordinary partisan disagreement?
