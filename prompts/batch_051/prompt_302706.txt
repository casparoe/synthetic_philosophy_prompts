I'm not in any field that talks about this stuff, but I've been watching my phone for a couple of years and there's a pattern I can't find a word for. When a subscription app wants me to pay, the path is always the same. Big colored button, same spot, my thumb already knows where it is. But when I try to cancel, the path is different almost every time I look. First it was settings, then account, then billing. A month later that exact menu is gone and its under "manage plan" but only if I scroll past a mostly blank page. Next time its a chat bot that makes me type out my reason before it shows me the cancel button. Meanwhile the buy button hasn't moved once and the color never changes.

I thought at first it was just bad design, but the contrast is too regular. The thing that costs me money stays so stable I could do it in the dark, and the thing that stops me paying gets re-shuffled like a deck. It feels like it's on purpose, not to make canceling impossible exactly, but to keep me from ever getting good at canceling, the way they make sure I get good at paying.

Is there a name for this specific setup? I know there's talk of "dark patterns" and "friction" or whatever, but those are broad. I guess what I'm asking is: has someone already isolated this thing where the exit route is deliberately kept unfamiliar while the paying route is kept familiar, and is it treated as its own move rather than just an example of the general category? How does it differ from a dark pattern like "hard to cancel" or from an app that's just badly organized across the board? And if it doesn't have a name, what would you call it, and what cases would be just outside the line, like an update that moves both the buy and stop buttons, or a company that buries everything equally? I'm not really satisfied with just being handed a label, I want the boundaries of the concept.
