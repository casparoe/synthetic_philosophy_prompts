I spent most of my career as a natural scientist, and I was comfortable saying that the reason a bird migrates earlier is that earlier migrants leave more surviving offspring. No bird formulates that reason, and no designer entertains it, yet the rationale seems to do real explanatory work. Since retiring into philosophy, I have discovered that this way of talking is more contested than I had assumed. Some hold that a reason, strictly speaking, is something that stands in relation to a mind; on that view, an evolutionary rationale is not a reason at all but a compressed causal story dressed in intentional language. Others defend free-floating rationales, reasons without reasoners, as real patterns realized in selection processes, functional organization, and other mindless systems. Write an essay on whether free-floating rationales exist. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, but do not end on a non-committal note; defend a verdict. Be precise about what you are affirming or denying: the question is not merely whether causes can operate unknown to anyone, but whether the rationale is there in the world as a reason before any mind represents it. Please write in continuous prose, as you would a letter or essay, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
