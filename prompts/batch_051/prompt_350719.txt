I run a nonprofit surplus-food kitchen that turns food that would otherwise be discarded into free meals. We get weekly offers of animal products: meat pulled from a supermarket just before disposal, leftover catered dishes containing meat or dairy, eggs from a small sanctuary for rescued hens that produces more than the birds' caretakers can use, and occasionally meat from an animal killed by a vehicle. Our shift supervisors, who are not ethicists, have to decide within a day whether to accept an offer, and our board will sign off on one written policy rather than on individual cases.

Please write a reusable decision procedure my supervisors can apply to any offer of animal-derived food: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Ground the procedure in the strongest version of the relevant ethical reasoning about using animal products that would otherwise be wasted — in particular, the argument that accepting such products creates no demand and therefore causes no additional harm, and the argument that serving them still expresses or normalizes something objectionable. Then run the procedure on three cases: supermarket meat rescued from a dumpster, eggs from the rescued-hen sanctuary, and meat from a deer struck by a vehicle. Make sure at least one case is one where the procedure tells us something we will not want to hear, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the procedure and in the reasoning behind it, and single out the point you are least sure of.
