Okay, so I've been reading Whitehead's essays on education for a class, and I've been arguing with my roommate about what he would make of ChatGPT-style tools. Whitehead spent a lot of energy attacking what he calls inert ideas — ideas students simply receive and hold without ever using, testing, or throwing into new combinations — and he also lays out a rhythm of education moving from romance (getting excited, wandering around in a topic), through precision (disciplined work on details and technique), to generalization (bringing it all back into active, synthetic use). My roommate thinks the verdict is obvious: an LLM hands you a ready-made answer, so it would manufacture inert ideas on an industrial scale. I'm not so sure. A tool that gives you exactly the explanation you need at the moment your stuck might actually defeat inertness by letting you move straight through a blockage into actually working with the idea, and it could serve as an endlessly patient precision-stage tutor, quizzing you and correcting your technique.

So if Whitehead were dropped into 2026, what would his position actually be? I don't just want "he'd be skeptical" — reason it out from the details of his philosophy. Where in the romance–precision–generalization cycle would he consider an LLM legitimate, and where would he think it's doing active harm? Would he object to the tool itself or to the use students would likely put it to? And is there any use of it he'd be unexpectedly enthusiastic about, from his own principles?
