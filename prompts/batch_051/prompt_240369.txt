Consider the following account of what it is to respect another person as an equal. On this account, a person S respects another person T just in case: (1) S is disposed to weigh T's interests and reasons in deliberation without needing a further self-interested motive for doing so; (2) S acknowledges T's authority over T's own life, taking T's choices about T's own good to be something S may override only on strong justification; (3) S regards T as a competent participant in the exchange of reasons—as someone whose questions, objections, and consent or refusal can themselves be contributions to shared reasoning; and (4) S experiences T's claims as normative, that is, as themselves giving S reasons, rather than merely as obstacles, incentives, or features of S's professional environment.

Now consider this case. An oncologist, Anya, has a flawless record: she gives patients accurate information, obtains informed consent scrupulously, answers questions thoroughly, accommodates treatment refusals, and advocates for patients who are too ill or intimidated to navigate the hospital system. She genuinely wants her patients to live and to be well, and she works hard for them. Privately, however, she regards informed-consent procedures as a bureaucratic and legal chore. She complies because she fears lawsuits and professional censure and because smooth consent conversations make the work more efficient. She sees her patients' preferences not as constraints with their own moral weight but as variables to be managed in the pursuit of correct treatment outcomes, and she rarely thinks their objections could teach her something she has missed. Does Anya respect her patients in the sense defined above? Work through the criteria one by one, marking where the evidence is mixed or the criterion needs interpretation. Then say whether, and how, the case pressures any of the four criteria—for example, whether criterion (4) asks too much of respect, whether it asks too little, or whether the other criteria implicitly require something like it.
