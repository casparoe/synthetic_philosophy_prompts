At my school’s spring carnival, I won the chance to draw one marble from a bag. The organizer showed me two identical-looking bags. Bag A, he said, contains exactly 50 red and 50 black marbles. Bag B contains 100 marbles, but he does not know the mix; he only knows that every marble is either red or black. If I draw red, I get $50; if black, nothing. I chose Bag A. My friend says this was irrational. He argues that since I have no reason to think red is more likely in either bag, I should treat the chance of drawing red as the same in both, so there is no rational basis for preferring A. He also says that if I prefer A when the payoff is on red, I should prefer A for the same reason when the payoff is on black; but if I then act as if both red and black are less likely in Bag B, I am committed to probabilities that cannot add up to one. This feels wrong to me, but I can’t say exactly where. Is my friend’s argument fair, and is there a defensible reason for choosing Bag A?
