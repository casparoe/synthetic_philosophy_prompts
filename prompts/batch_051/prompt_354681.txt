In a thread on one of the rationalist forums I have read for years, about whether a particular cryptocurrency rightly counts as money, a commenter met an objection ('the token has no intrinsic value and no issuer standing behind it, so it cannot be money') by replying, 'money is a social construct'. The reply was plainly meant to dissolve the objection, but I found I could not pin down what claim it was actually asserting, and the subsequent back-and-forth kept trading on the slogan without ever making its content precise. I would like you to disambiguate 'money is a social construct': set out the distinct precise theses it could be used to express – for instance, theses about how money comes to exist at all, about what its continued existence and value consist in, about what determines which particular objects or ledger entries count as money, about whether the form and particular token choices of existing money are inevitable or modifiable, and about whether the question of what is 'really' money has a fact of the matter apart from social practice. For each reading, give a verdict: near-trivial, substantive and defensible, or probably false. Then say which reading or readings would actually support the move the commenter was making – that an unbacked token can be money because all money is socially constructed – and which readings, though plausible and perhaps true, would not establish that conclusion.
