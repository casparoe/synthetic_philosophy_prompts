I'm drafting a short position statement on the nature of intelligence for a seminar that mixes philosophers and psychometricians, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I currently stand.

1. Intelligence is a real property of minds, but it is a family-resemblance concept with no sharp boundary between intelligent and non-intelligent systems.
2. The best evidence that a system is intelligent is sustained, flexible behavior across a wide range of novel tasks; there is no further fact about a hidden intelligence essence that we could be checking.
3. Whether a system is intelligent depends on its underlying capacities, not merely on its current output; a performance can look intelligent while the system is not.
4. The positive manifold — the finding that performance on diverse cognitive tests correlates — is one of the best-replicated results in psychology, and any account of intelligence must explain it.
5. There are incommensurable kinds of intelligence: it is a category mistake to place a crow's intelligence and a human's on a single quantitative scale.
6. Many animals are intelligent in ways standard psychometric tests systematically miss; our test batteries sample only a narrow band of possible intelligent behavior.
