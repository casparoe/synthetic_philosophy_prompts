I'm putting together a philosophy-and-literature unit on Borges and time, and I want to teach The Secret Miracle — the one where Hladík, a Jewish playwright in Nazi-occupied Prague, gets a whole year to finish his play in his head during the frozen instant before the firing squad's volley. I want to read it against the actual philosophical moves Borges makes about time, especially in A New Refutation of Time, where he runs a Berkeley-flavored argument that time is unreal because all we ever actually have is the present mental state and the past is just memory happening now — and then he more or less half-retracts it at the end, admitting the refutation is a consolation rather than a thing he can live inside. My worry is that my students will treat the story as a clean illustration of subjective vs objective time and stop there. So give me the more careful reading: where does the story genuinely dramatize the idea that time might be mental or unreal, and where does it quietly resist that idea — the historical framing, the scheduled execution, the fact that the extra year comes as a gift from a dream-God rather than as a discovery about the nature of time? Does the firing squad at the end punish the idealist reading or confirm it? And end by saying what the story gives us about time that the essay can't, if anything — the felt difference between time-as-succession-of-mental-states and time-as-a-scheduled-execution. Be willing to say the story and the essay dont fit neatly together.
