So I was sorting through seminar papers and overheard two of my students going at it in the hallway about Mill’s discussion of the composition of causes in A System of Logic, Book III, Chapter VI. Mill there contrasts cases where several causes jointly produce an effect that is just the sum of their separate effects, like two forces pulling on the same body, with cases where the combined result has properties absent from the components taken individually, his stock example being hydrogen and oxygen yielding water. Ben said Mill is basically an early emergentist: chemical laws can’t be reduced to mechanical laws because the properties of water are not just the properties of hydrogen plus the properties of oxygen. Rosa said no, Mill is still a thoroughgoing mechanist about explanation; he only thinks chemical laws are a different kind of law, not that they violate or float free from underlying physical principles, so calling him an emergentist is anachronistic. Ben came back saying that’s exactly what emergentists mean, and Mill explicitly denies you could deduce chemical regularities from the laws of the components in isolation. Rosa replied that Mill only denies we can do that deduction at present, and that Mill’s real target is how induction works when causes compose non-additively, not any ontological claim about distinct levels. Ben then said she was smuggling in contemporary physics to save Mill from his own text. At that point they asked me to referee, but I don’t want my own reading to steer them, so I’m asking you instead. Please go through the exchange point by point: who’s right about what, where each of them overreaches, and who, overall, is the more faithful and philosophically defensible reader of Mill on this issue.
