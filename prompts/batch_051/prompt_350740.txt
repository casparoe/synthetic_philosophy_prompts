In my ethics class we have been discussing the Golden Rule, the instruction to treat other people the way you would want them to treat you, and the more I try to apply it to particular cases, the less clear I am about what it actually requires. I would like you to propose a working definition of what it is to follow the Golden Rule, and then test that definition against the cases below. The first two feel easy: Amina finds a lost phone and returns it because she would want someone to return hers—I am confident this is following the rule. Ben spreads an embarrassing rumor about a classmate even though he would be mortified if a rumor were spread about him—I am confident this is a violation. The rest are harder. Cora genuinely enjoys being teased and insulted by friends, and she treats everyone that way because it is how she would want to be treated; my instinct says she is not following the rule as it was meant, but I cannot say why without changing the rule. Devi forces her seven-year-old to get a vaccination that the child hates, because Devi believes that if she were the child she would later be grateful for having been forced; I have no settled view about whether this is consistent with the rule. Eli, a judge, sentences a defendant to prison even though he would not want to be imprisoned in the defendant's position, on the grounds that the rule is about respecting others as persons rather than about sharing their exact preferences; I do not know whether that distinction works. Fatima passes a stranger struggling with heavy bags and does not offer help because she would prefer to be left alone in the stranger's situation; this feels like an unkind way to use the rule, but I cannot say whether she is actually violating it. Please run your definition through all six cases, tell me where it gives an answer that surprises you or no answer at all, which of your six verdicts is least secure, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.
