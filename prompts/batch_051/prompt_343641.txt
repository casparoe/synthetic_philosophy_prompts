George Orwell held that certain habits of political prose—stale metaphor, inflated diction, euphemism, and the stock phrase—both express and encourage muddled or evasive thought. He did not live to see large language models, which are trained on exactly the institutional and journalistic prose he criticized and now draft a growing share of political, bureaucratic, and corporate writing. Suppose Orwell were alive today with a clear technical understanding of how such systems generate text. What would he regard as their central political and linguistic danger: the automation of formulaic language, the diffusion of responsibility for word choice, the statistical flattening of style, or something else? Would he see them as merely accelerating old vices, or as a qualitative change in the relation between language and thought? Develop what you take to be his considered position, and indicate where his earlier views would need revision in light of how these systems are actually built and used.
