In my incident-review work I rely on a vocabulary drawn from aviation safety and safety management: root causes and contributing factors, active failures and latent conditions, the Swiss cheese model, just culture, blame-free postmortems, corrective actions, and the standing distinction between investigations that prevent recurrence and procedures that punish. I am fluent in that vocabulary; I have no legal training.

A lawyer colleague tells me that this material either maps onto or badly distorts an old debate in criminal law. Serious offenses, she says, normally require mens rea — purpose, knowledge, recklessness, or negligence — while a class of public-welfare offenses imposes strict liability, in which the prohibited act is enough and culpability need not be shown. She has challenged me to stop reaching for loose analogies and to do the translation properly.

Restate the strict liability and mens rea debate in my vocabulary as systematically as you can. Which legal distinctions map cleanly onto distinctions I already draw — for example, the mens rea grades against my categories of inadvertent error, at-risk behavior, and recklessness, or strict liability against the practice of holding an operator answerable for breaching a safety-critical rule regardless of their state of mind? Which mappings require fudging, and what exactly was bent in the process?

Then give me the residue report. In particular, the retributivist claim that punishing the blameless is a wrong in itself looks as though it may have no counterpart in a framework built around forward-looking prevention. Tell me whether it survives translation, whether it is distorted into a merely instrumental consideration, or whether it cannot be stated at all — and whether an untranslatable remainder is best read as a limitation of my vocabulary, a defect in the original philosophical debate, or both. Do not smuggle the assumptions of safety management into the translation and call that a neutral rendering; I want the failures of the translation kept visible.

If I have misdescribed the legal categories, or if the whole task presumes a false parallel between legal culpability and engineering responsibility, say so plainly and do the nearest defensible thing rather than forcing the translation.

Please write the answer in British English.
