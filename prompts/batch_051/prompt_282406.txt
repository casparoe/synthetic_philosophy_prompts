I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and over the last few months I've been reporting on why people seem to get locked into wildly different pictures of the world online. One thing keeps tripping me up. The same people I interview will describe a subreddit or a group chat as an echo chamber one minute and as an epistemic bubble the next, and then treat the two as if they're just different names for the same thing. But the two images are doing quite different work, aren't they? The echo chamber picture suggests the problem is acoustic: you're in a room where your own voice, or voices close enough to it, bounce back at you, so you become increasingly convinced partly because you never meet resistance and partly because the chamber teaches you to distrust any voice from outside before it can even be heard. The epistemic bubble picture suggests the problem is structural: you're inside a membrane or a sphere that filters what gets in, letting confirming information through and keeping the rest out, so your views end up as the product of a biased sample you never notice is biased. People reach for these images to support quite different remedies too — if it's a bubble, you pop it with a more diverse feed or more varied contacts; if it's an echo chamber, mere exposure won't do the job because the discrediting of outsiders has already happened, so you need something closer to deprogramming or rebuilding trust first.

What I'd like is not a verdict on who is actually stuck in what, and not a survey of the psychology. I want an audit of the two analogies themselves. What maps onto what in each picture? Where does each one break? What does the chamber metaphor quietly import that the bubble doesn't, and the other way round? Does either image actually support the remedy people attach to it, or do the disanalogies undermine the very advice that gets drawn from them? And if both are leaky, is there a better analogy out there, or is the real lesson that no single physical image can carry the social, epistemic and affective stuff going on at once? I'm especially interested in whether the "just burst the bubble" advice survives a proper look at how the two seperate, and in what each analogy silently assumes about whether the person inside is a passive victim or an active participant.
