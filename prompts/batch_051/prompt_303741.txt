But the people I'm reading seem to be claiming something stronger — that there's a fact about which priors are rationally permissible, not just which ones perform well.

Could you restate the objective Bayesian position, especially as it's defended by people who treat maximum entropy as a principle of rational inference, in the vocabulary a statistician like me actually uses: KL divergence, cross-entropy, model calibration, overfitting, penalised likelihood, reference priors, maybe information geometry if it helps. Which parts get distorted when I try to translate them?
