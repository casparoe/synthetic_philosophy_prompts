I direct the research unit of a national anti-corruption commission. We do upstream conceptual and institutional-design work rather than casework, and I have been given roughly six researcher-years over the next two years to spend on the question of what incorruptibility is and whether it is always good. Our practical products are integrity-training curricula, appointment and recusal rules, and guidelines for how regulatory agencies should be insulated from the industries they regulate. Most of those products currently presuppose an answer rather than argue for one.

Before I commit the budget, I want a defended agenda. Identify the main open conceptual questions about incorruptibility that are worth distinguishing from one another, rank them in the order you would fund them, and tell me which three you would prioritize for this unit. I am not asking for a literature survey or a bibliography. For each question, say: (i) why it matters downstream — which design or training decisions actually change depending on the answer; (ii) whether it is upstream of other questions or downstream of them; (iii) whether it is tractable with conceptual and philosophical tools alone, as opposed to needing empirical work we cannot do; and (iv) what a two-year result would look like — a specific conclusion or reframing that would either change our products or force us to rethink them. Also flag any question you think is discussed more than it is worth relative to how little turns on it.

As background, I want the agenda to respect the following distinction, which our internal debates handle poorly. Incorruptibility is sometimes treated as modal robustness: one would not be corrupted across a wide range of counterfactual temptations. Sometimes it is treated as insensibility: the corrupting incentive does not show up as a live reason at all. Sometimes it is treated as structural non-exposure: the office or environment makes the corrupt option unavailable, unaffordable, or absent from the agent's choice set. It is not obvious whether these are three species of one property, three competing explanations of the same behavior, or three points on a continuum, and it is not obvious that the question of whether incorruptibility is always good bites with equal force across all three. I want the agenda to take that ambiguity seriously rather than dissolving it by defining terms in whatever way makes the answer easy.
