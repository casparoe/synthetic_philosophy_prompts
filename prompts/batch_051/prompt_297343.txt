So I've spent the last couple months modding a mid-sized forum, and there's an argument pattern I keep having to referee that I don't have a word for. Two users will go at each other for days about whether something "really is" some other thing — like whether a scattered collection of remixes someone stitched into a thread "really counts as" a community archive, or whether a long-time member's behavior after a falling-out "really was" manipulative. The thing that gets me is that when I try to pin down what would settle the fight, they already agree on all the observable facts. Same events, same sequence, and each of them can predict exactly how the other will respond. And still they keep going. If I ask "what fact are you actually disagreeing about?", each side just restates their original wording in new words, and we loop.

I've started to suspect some of these fights aren't about the world at all. But it can't be that simple, because the fights do have real stakes — whichever word ends up in the rules changes how we moderate and how people describe themselves afterward. So: does this pattern have a name in philosophy, and, more importantly, is there a decent test for telling this kind of dispute apart from a regular one where one side is just mistaken? Because in some threads I'd swear it's only a fight over a label, and in others there's a real disagreement hiding inside the label. I don't just want a name — I want a way of carving the cases apart. And please actually commit to a diagnosis and defend it, rather than handing me a list of candidate views.
