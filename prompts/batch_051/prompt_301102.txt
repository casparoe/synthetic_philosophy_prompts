I'm on the collections committee of a university museum, and we're hashing out the text for a new gallery on early household life. The case in front of us is one excavated mudbrick house: under the floor of its largest room there's a small pit packed with around forty clay figurines, most broken at the neck, none with a flat base, plus three miniature bowls and some charred grain. The curator's draft label calls it a domestic ancestor cult and says the figurines were ritually killed. One member says you can't get belief from objects alone: the figurines could be toys, the breakage is just what happens to cheap clay, the pit a sealed trash hole. We're split between approving the strong label, weakening it to 'possibly ritual, possibly ordinary household debris,' or dropping talk of belief entirely and just describing the objects. I'm writing the short memo for Tuesday. Could you draft it? Give a clear recommendation with actual label wording, engage the strongest version of the side you don't take, and say what further evidence would flip your call. Also, some of us think 'ritual' has become our field's label for anything we just can't otherwise explain, and others think refusing to infer belief at all is a cop-out; that debate should be in there too.
