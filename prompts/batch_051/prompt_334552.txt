Two coworkers of mine got into it at lunch over a shared report that kept using the phrase "the client" in places where it wasnt clear which client the writer meant. Priya said the problem isn't just missing reference; the phrase presents the client under a particular mode of presentation, so even substituting "Client 482" won't fix the ambiguity unless everyone already shares the same sense. She said Frege uses "proper name" broadly enough to cover definite descriptions like "the client," and she pulled up Frege's On Sense and Reference and read this passage aloud: "The sense of a proper name is grasped by everybody who is sufficiently familiar with the language or totality of designations to which it belongs; but this serves to illuminate only a single aspect of the reference, supposing it to have one. Comprehensive knowledge of the reference would require us to say immediately whether any given sense belongs to it. To such knowledge we never attain."

Tom said that's overkill. A canonical client ID fixes the reference, he said, and that's all a shared document needs; no sense theorizing required. Priya replied that the ID only fixes reference because we already know how to interpret the register. Two people could see "Client 482" and attach different background assumptions about which legal entity or account is meant, and Frege's line about never attaining comprehensive knowledge of the reference says exactly that. Tom said she was conflating not knowing everything about a referent with not knowing which referent is meant; those are different problems. Priya asked whether there's a sharp line between them, because if senses can diverge while the reference stays put, then "same reference" underdetermines meaning, which is the original problem. Tom admitted the line was blurrier than he thought but said an explicit definitional schema would still get them further than appealing to Frege. Neither would budge. I honestly can't referee.
