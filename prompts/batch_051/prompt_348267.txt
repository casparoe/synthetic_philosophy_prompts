I've been trying to get clearer on a worry I keep half-seeing in discussions of iterated amplification and distillation (IDA), and I think I understand the setup but not the conceptual crux. As I understand it, the idea is roughly: you take a human overseer and amplify them by letting them break a hard task into subtasks, answer or outsource the subtasks, and combine the results; then you distill that slow amplified process into a fast model by training it to imitate the process's input–output behavior; then you repeat, using the distilled model to make the next round of amplification stronger. The appeal is that the overall system's behavior traces back to a human overseer at each step, so alignment is supposed to be preserved almost by construction.

What I keep getting stuck on is this. The amplification stage has the human in the loop, but the distillation stage is just supervised learning against whatever traces the amplified process left behind, and I don't see why alignment or even competence should survive that step in anything more than a thin, average-case sense. I've heard people gesture at a difference between a model learning the amplified process itself and it learning to produce outputs that merely look like that process's outputs on the training distribution, but I can't turn that gesture into something precise. In particular, I can't tell whether the problem is only the usual one of distribution shift and shortcuts in imitation, or whether there is something deeper in the fact that the things the overseer attends to while decomposing a task are not necessarily the things that make the final answer good, so the distilled model could get optimized for features of the process rather than for the underlying goal. Could you give an intuitive explanation of what is supposed to happen to alignment and competence when you distill an amplified process, and say which parts of the concern are specific to IDA as opposed to any kind of imitation learning? And if you think the standard way of putting the worry rests on a confusion about what the distillation step does, I'd rather you say so directly than give me a cleaned-up version of my framing. I'd also like a rough sense of how confident you are about the part of your answer where you think the real disagreement lies.
