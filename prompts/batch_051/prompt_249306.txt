So last night I got into a long arguement with my friend about the unilateralist's curse — the idea that when a bunch of people each have the power to take some big irreversible action on behalf of the whole group, and their private judgments about whether it's a good idea are scattered, the person whose threshold for acting is lowest ends up deciding for everyone, because nobody has to persuade the others, they just have to be the first one who can't be stopped. The example we kept circling back to was a small independent observatory collective with time on a powerful radio dish: any member can point the dish at a nearby star with a possibly Earth-like planet and send a high-power "hello." Most of the collective thinks that's wildly premature and wants to wait for some kind of international consensus; a couple of them think staying silent is cowardice, almost a kind of self-censorship. The group's actual rule is basically "any member can transmit." My friend thinks the curse dissolves if they all vote before anyone touches the dish; I think the vote mostly tells the enthusiasts exactly who's objections they'll have to ignore.

Anyway, it made me want a short story. Write one about that collective and the decision whether to send the message. Set it wherever you like — the night before the vote, the moment after the vote comes out 11 to 4 against, the walk home, the empty control room at 3 a.m. The important thing is that the story actually encodes the unilateralist's curse: the person who ends up sending shouldn't be the median member, and shouldn't be evil, just the one whose private estimate says "worth it" before anyone else's does, while everyone else's restraint turns out to be irrelevant because they don't need to be convinced, only unable to stop it. Don't make it a generic "group makes a risky call" story; the structure of who-gets-to-act should do the work.

For the format: please write it as exactly five numbered scenes, each scene just one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion around them.
