Last year I released a small toolkit under a copyleft licence. A company has since built a commercial product around it: in one version their proprietary program links against my library in the same binary, and in another the identical product ships as two processes, my library in one and their program in the other, talking over a local socket. Nearly every developer I know treats the first as a derivative work that must be released under the same licence, and the second as a mere aggregate of two independent programs whose boundary leaves their half proprietary. The code, the screen, and the function are unchanged; only the boundary between processes has moved. Is there a principle that justifies letting that packaging detail decide where one work ends and another begins, or is the distinction a tolerated loophole? I want the strongest justification for the asymmetry you can construct, tested against at least one nearby arrangement, and an honest verdict on whether the line should be kept, moved, or abandoned.
