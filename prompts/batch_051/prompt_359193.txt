I want a satirical sidebar op-ed, around 600 words, in the voice of the director of a made-up Center for Responsible Concept Design, who is announcing that the concept of addiction is being deprecated and replaced with a redesigned concept "addiction-star" (version 2.0). Keep it deadpan, no winking, and make the humor come from the actual machinery of ameliorative analysis, not from the idea that trying to fix concepts is silly. So the director should be someone a real conceptual engineer could almost be: start from the question of what work we need the concept to do, then build the definition to do that work — the ameliorative move, as against just describing the concept we already have. He should talk about how different concepts are apt for different purposes, and worry openly about backwards compatability: whether old sentences using ordinary "addiction" still say something true or false once the concept has been swapped out, and about who gets to redesign a concept everyone else has to live with. At some point let him slip, exactly once, into talking as if the new stipulation is what addiction "really was all along" — the slide his critics warn about — and then catch himself, sort of, without fully seeing why it matters. The attentive reader should feel the actual fault line between describing a concept and fixing it, plus the legitimacy question underneath. American English, please. And keep it in the weeds enough that someone who knows conceptual ethics recognizes the moves and laughs, but don't name actual people, papers, or schools.
