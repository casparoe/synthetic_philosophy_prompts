I'm trying to work out what I actually think about choices that affect who gets born, and my gut reactions across cases don't line up. Here they are, roughly as I feel them. A government has to choose between two energy policies: under conservation, people two centuries from now live decent lives; under depletion, there's a boom for a generation but the environment is badly damaged, and two centuries from now people live materially much worse lives that are still worth living. The two policies would also scramble who meets whom, so the particular people who exist under depletion would not have existed under conservation. I think choosing depletion is wrong, and not just because of the near-term benefits. A fourteen-year-old has a baby now, when she could have waited and given a different child a much better start; the child she has has a hard childhood but a life worth living overall. I think she did something wrong. A man with a serious genetic condition conceives naturally with his partner, knowing any child of his will very likely inherit it; the child they have does inherit it, and her life is harder than average but clearly worth living. I don't think this is wrong — or if it is, it's a much smaller wrong than the first two. A couple doing IVF deliberately selects an embryo that will be deaf, because they are deaf and want a child who shares their language and culture; the child will have a rich, worthwile life. I'm torn, but I lean not wrong. A government stores nuclear waste unsafely because it's cheaper; decades later there's a leak and children born nearby have a chronic illness, but the policy also changed the local economy and migration patterns, so those particular children would not have existed under the safe policy. I think that's clearly wrong. And for contrast: a couple has a child at all, knowing the child will suffer in ordinary ways. Obviously not wrong, and if the same logic that condemns the depletion policy also condemns ordinary procreation, something has gone off the rails.

So here's the thing. In every case where I feel a wrong, the person who exists is not made worse off by the choice, because the alternative wasn't a better life for them — it was never existing at all. Yet I want to call some of these choices wrong and others not, and I can't see the line. Is there a principle that gets all or most of my verdicts? State it precisely (or give me the smallest set of candidate principles and what speaks for and against each), say which of my verdicts it can't accommodate, and tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: a couple who both carry a gene for a condition that causes moderate chronic pain and who conceive naturally rather than using screening that would select an embryo without it — the child's life would be worth living but much harder than it needed to be.
