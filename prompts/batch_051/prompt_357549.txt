Criminal law says that sexual contact obtained by deception is not consensual, but the way it applies that principle draws a line whose rationale is far from obvious. When a doctor persuades a patient that a sexual act is a medical procedure, or when someone impersonates a person's partner, the contact is treated as non-consensual. Yet when someone lies about being single, about being in love, or about intending a relationship in order to obtain consent, the usual legal position has been that the consent remains valid, even if the lie was decisive for the other person's decision. What view of consent does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of what exactly deception has to undermine before consent is invalidated, and why deception about inducements is placed outside that. Say whether that theory is coherent, and explain what would have to change in the law if it instead treated any deliberately induced false belief that was material to the decision as vitiating consent.
