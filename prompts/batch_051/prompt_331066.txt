A friend of mine has been arguing lately, with a kind of cheerful fatalism, that conscientious consumption is mostly performance. His case rests on an analogy. Choosing not to buy a factory-farmed chicken, or deciding not to fly to a conference, stands to the operations of the food and aviation industries, he says, in exactly the relation that a single ballot stands to the outcome of a national election. No national election is decided by one vote; no producer kills one fewer chicken and no airline burns one fewer ton of jet fuel because one person withheld one purchase. Therefore, he concludes, individual restraint is at best self-expression, and the serious work has to be done through political movements and regulation rather than through what consumers choose at the margin.

I find the argument suspicious, but I want to know exactly where, if anywhere, the voting analogy stops carrying the conclusion. Please act as a moral philosopher who works on causal responsibility and collective action, and think this through slowly and carefully. Begin with a loose brainstorm of the correspondence before you write the final audit. Then lay out the analogy explicitly: what in the consumption case is supposed to play the role of the ballot, the electorate, the secrecy of the voting booth, the tie that a single vote could break, and the winning margin. Separate the disanalogies that matter for my friend's conclusion from merely superficial ones. In particular, address whether an individual purchase can have an expected marginal effect even when it is almost never the but-for cause of one particular animal's life or one particular ton of emissions; whether consumer choice feeds back through continuous aggregation, demand forecasting, and social signaling in ways that a secret ballot cannot; and whether saying that one's action makes no difference licenses the same inference in a winner-take-all binary contest as it does in a continuous, threshold-structured supply chain. Finally, say whether the conclusion that personal restraint is futile survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and propose a better analogy if you think one exists — or explain why no simple analogy is reliable here.
