I chair the credentialing comittee at a hospital. A senior resident is up for review because a billing-audit firm alleges she billed for procedures she never performed. The evidence is purely statistical: the vendor's model says that among clinicians whose billing pattern matches hers, about 93% turn out to have submitted false claims, and she matches the pattern. That's all there is—no chart anomaly, no witness, no complaint from a patient or nurse. Our standard is preponderance of the evidence. The committee is split: some think 93% is more likely than not, end of story; others think base rates about a class can't establish facts about this particular person. I need a short memo with an up-or-down recommendation, any conditions you'd attach, and the strongest argument on the losing side taken seriously rather than brushed aside. Most of all I want your view on whether evidence of this kind can ever carry a disciplinary decision on its own, and if not, what sort of further evidence would tip it.
