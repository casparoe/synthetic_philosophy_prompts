Construct a minimal, fully specified case for a lesson on the underdetermination of an optimizer's objective and search space: behavior can be predicted equally well by a simple objective paired with a strong search procedure and by a complex objective paired with a weak search procedure, and the objective one reads back depends on which assumption one starts from.

Set up a single-agent, deterministic, fully observable grid-world with at most a dozen states and exactly two actions available in every state. A reward is a function assigning a number to each state or state–action pair; make explicit which you use. The agent's possible search procedures are exhaustive optimal planning, which maximizes total reward over the whole remaining episode, and greedy planning, which always selects the action with the highest immediate reward and breaks ties by a fixed deterministic order.

Define two complete hypotheses about the agent. H_opt pairs a reward R_opt with at most two nonzero entries with optimal planning. H_true pairs a reward R_true with at least three nonzero entries with greedy planning. The agent is in fact implemented as H_true.

The case must satisfy these constraints:

1. From the initial state, H_opt and H_true generate exactly the same trajectory, and at every state on that trajectory the two hypotheses prescribe the same action. All in-environment behavior is therefore identical under both hypotheses.
2. There is at least one probe state, reachable in principle by a single off-policy action from a state on the induced trajectory, on which the two hypotheses prescribe different actions. Specify the probe, the competing actions, and why each hypothesis selects its own.
3. A behavior-only inverse learner that assumes optimal planning and restricts itself to rewards with at most two nonzero entries settles on H_opt, while a behavior-only inverse learner that assumes greedy search settles on H_true. Both hypotheses rationalize every observed action exactly, and on the observed trajectory alone neither can be eliminated.
4. The case must work entirely through the objective–search-space trade-off: no stochasticity, no hidden context, no partial observability, no unstated internal states, and no rewards beyond those listed in the two hypothesis tables.

Cheating means simply asserting that the two policies coincide on the reachable states; the coincidence must be derived from the two reward tables and the two planning rules. It is also cheating to create the divergence through noise, through a change in the environment's dynamics, or by making the probe state reachable only under a different transition function.

Then do the check. Give the transition graph and both reward tables, derive both policies, verify conditions 1 through 3 state by state, and explain in one paragraph why no amount of additional on-trajectory behavior could separate H_opt from H_true. Finish with an honest note: identify the feature of the case that does the real work, say what happens if that feature is removed, and give the strongest response a defender of the optimality-first, simplest-objective rule could make to stop the case from counting.
