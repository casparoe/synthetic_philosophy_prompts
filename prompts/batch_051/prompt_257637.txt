Write me a satirical public consultation document from a government office (call it the Office for Aggregate Welfare) announcing a two-year pilot program for "cross-personal offsetting" in policy appraisal. The premise is that once a policy's total utility is positive, losses to identifiable individuals no longer need any compensation, because the department treats the gains of others as arithmetically equivalent to compensation. The humor has to come from treating this as an unremarkable bookeeping reform. Deadpan bureaucratese, around 700 words, with at least one worked example where one man's chronic pain is offset by small conveniences to thirty thousand commuters. Do not name the philosophical objection, any philosopher, or the terms "separateness of persons," "utility monster," or "utilitarian." The target is the way cost-benefit analysis can treat people as interchangeable containers of welfare; a specialist should feel the objection with growing discomfort while a lay reader just thinks the policy is a bit off. End with a request for comments on an optional "minimum individual floor" safeguard, treated as a minor technicality. Make it read like a genuine document, not a parody of one, and answer in British English please.
