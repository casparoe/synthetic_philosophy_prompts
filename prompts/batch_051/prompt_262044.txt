I am drafting a novel set in a hospital during the early weeks of a respiratory pandemic, and there is a scene I have been unable to write honestly. A physician and a health economist are standing near the beds of two patients who need the single remaining ventilator. The economist is defending, as a matter of policy, a rule that would assign the ventilator to the patient whose treatment is expected to produce the greater gain in quality-adjusted life years; the physician replies with a sentence I keep writing and striking out, in one form or another: you cannot put a price on a human life. The scene worries me because there is no exit from the trade-off — someone is placed on the ventilator and someone is not — so the sentence cannot simply mean that the choice should not be made, and I do not want it to function as a sentiment the economist can knock down in one paragraph, or as a strawman. Before I write the exchange, I need to know what precisely that sentence could be claiming in this setting. Lay out the candidate readings as a menu, from the nearly trivial to the radical: a descriptive claim about the absence of a market price for a life; a claim about what QALYs can and cannot measure; a claim about whether individual patients may be compared on a single scale; a claim about the proper procedure for triage — and any further reading that should be kept distinct from these. For each reading, phrase it precisely enough to be spoken by the character, and say whether it is true, defensible, or false under the conditions of the scene. Then tell me which readings would actually block or seriously modify the economist's rule, and which ones only appear to block it; and state what the economist can honestly concede. I am aware of the standard countermove, that public policy already trades lives against cost in road safety and drug approval, but I do not know whether that answers the physician's deepest point or only the flattest reading of it. My working guess is that the strongest readings turn on a distinction between valuing a life and pricing a life, but I want the menu to test that guess rather than assume it. The chapter should turn on the strongest defensible version of each side, so also tell me where the line collapses and what the physician ought to have said instead.
