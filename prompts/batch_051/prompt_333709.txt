Avicenna's flying-man argument asks us to imagine a person created all at once as an adult, suspended in the air with limbs spread apart and eyes covered, unable to perceive any part of his body, who nonetheless affirms his own existence. Here are four candidate conclusions one might draw from this: (i) that the soul exists; (ii) that the self is not identical to the body or to any of its organs; (iii) that the soul is an immaterial substance capable of existing apart from the body; and (iv) that self-awareness is direct and non-sensory rather than inferred from experience. Rank these candidates from most to least defensible as conclusions the thought experiment itself can establish on Avicenna's own terms. For each candidate, give one sentence explaining why it lands where it does; for any candidate the passage alone cannot support, state the specific additional premise from Avicenna's psychology that would be needed to reach it.
