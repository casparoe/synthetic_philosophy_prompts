Ok, here's one for the seminar on positive, negative and republican liberty. I've been collecting the standard cases where a person is free on both of Berlin's readings — nobody actually interferes with them, and they're self-governing, acting on values they've made their own — but republicans still say the person is unfree. So far I've got: (1) the slave with a kindly master who never stops him doing anything; (2) the wife in a traditional marriage where the law gives the husband arbitrary authority and he happens to never excercise it; (3) the at-will employee whose boss could fire her for no reason but never does; and (4) the citizen under a merciful dictator who leaves everyone alone. I want one more case with a genuinely different structure from these, and in particular one where the domination runs through informal social relations rather than law, contract, or employment. Don't just re-skin one of mine (no servant with a nice lady, no prisoner with a lax warden, etc.). Then tell me: what exactly does the republican say is unfree about the case, which feature is doing the real work, and is there a serious negative-liberty reply that no actual interference means no unfreedom, or does that reply just miss the point? I'd like your honest read.
