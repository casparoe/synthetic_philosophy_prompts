My sister and I have been stuck on the same argument about frugality for years. She grew up poor and I didn't; maybe that's doing some work but I'm not sure. Her line is that the only point of being frugal is what it does: less stuff bought, less waste, fewer resources used, lower emissions. So if my personal thrift doesn't actually move those numbers—because whatever I don't buy just gets bought by someone else, or because my share is too small to register—then my frugality is basically a private hobby with good vibes. On her view the real obligation, if there is one, is to promote frugality: build norms, support policies, make waste socially embarrassing, get a lot of people to consume less. I think she's got it exactly backwards. To me frugality matters mostly as something you are: not always grabbing more than you need, keeping your own wants reined in, being the kind of person who can say no to themselves. Whether my specific thrift changes global emissions seems beside the point—what matters is that I'm not being greedy or wasteful here and now. Whenever she shows me that my one-person thrift doesn't scale beyond me, I say a virtue isn't supposed to scale. Whenever I point out that she spends years of her life promoting a norm she barely manages to live by, she says that just proves the consequences are what matter, not the performance. Then we loop. We agree waste is bad and that consumption is out of hand. We agree on the facts as far as I can tell. And yet neither of us has budged. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a hidden factual question in there—like whether one person being frugal really does reduce total consumption—or are we disagreeing about what even counts as a moral reason, or just using the word 'frugality' for two different things? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would actually move one of us?
