I've been going back and forth with my labmate and I can't quite get her position out of my head. She says "alive" is basically a scorekeeping word: you can lay out criteria like metabolism, growth, responsiveness, reproduction, and so on, but whether any particular weird thing counts as living is a choice about where to cut the score, not a discovery about the world. I want to resist that, but the usual examples (viruses, spores, prions) just make me more confused, so let me give you a specific case instead.

Suppose someone builds a cell-like vesicle that imports precursor molecules, keeps its interior far from equilibrium, grows, maintains its own membrane, and then splits into two daughter vesicles. The catch: its copying is error-free, so there is no heritable variation and nothing for natural selection to act on. How could one test whether this thing is alive, and equally whether "is it alive?" has a fact of the matter beyond the definition we happen to choose? I'd like a concrete experimental design (or the closest feasible thing), the result that would push toward each of our views, and some honest account of what even a clean result would leave unsettled about the concept of life itself.
