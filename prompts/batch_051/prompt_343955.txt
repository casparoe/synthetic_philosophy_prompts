ok so i've been going back and forth on the whole simultaneous discovery thing — Newton and Leibniz, Darwin and Wallace — and everyone i read just grabs one of these cases and uses it to prove their point about whether discoveries are inevitable or whether it matters who got there first. i want to stop doing that and get the full spec first.

before i read another take on what "really" happened with Darwin and Wallace, give me the desiderata: what does a theory of simultaneous discovery (or scientific priority, or discovery in general — you tell me what the object of the theory actually should be) have to get right? i want the list of requirements — the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the historical facts it can't contradict — with the reason each one is a requirement. then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just some camp's prejudice, and tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out the popular views. if some pair of requirements cant be satisfied together, say so — that's the part i most want, not a scorecard.

the passage i keep coming back to is from Darwin's letter to Charles Lyell of 18 June 1858, right after Wallace's paper landed and he's just said his originality will be smashed. then he writes:

"Though my Book, if it will ever have any value, will not be deteriorated; as all the labour consists in the application of the theory."

i think that sentence is doing a lot of work. build the desiderata around it: the theory has to say what you can and can't conclude from the fact that Darwin could feel his originality was gone while insisting the book's value was untouched. but dont let that one letter dominate — fold in the fact that Newton vs Leibniz is a different animal from Darwin vs Wallace (the theory has to register the difference, whatever it is), and that near-simultaneous discovery happens often enough that the theory needs some story about why it happens at all and whether the "who" carries any load. the purpose here is mostly: how much of our credit-and-priority practices in science are anything more than a lottery, and what would have to be true for them to be more than that. skip the scorecard of actual theories; i want the criteria and the tensions between them.
