I've been trying to formalize the nonidentity problem for an intro ethics unit and I can't find the hole in this version. (1) If a policy wrongs any particular future person, it must harm that person. (2) Harming a person requires making them worse off than they would have been had the policy never been enacted. (3) Suppose a climate policy will reshape the economy, migration, and social life enough that the people alive in 2100 under the policy are not the people who would of been alive in 2100 without it; the two populations are disjoint. (4) So for any given person alive in 2100 under the policy, they would never have existed without the policy. (5) A life that is, by stipulation, worth living is not worse for the person living it than never existing. (6) Therefore the policy makes no particular future person worse off. (7) Therefore the policy harms no particular future person. (8) Therefore the policy wrongs no particular future person, even if it leaves everyone in 2100 much worse off on average. I half-suspect step (3) is doing the sneaky work, but maybe the real problem is earlier. Is the argument valid as written? If not, exactly which step fails? If it is valid, which premise would you reject, and what does giving it up cost?
