For a novel I'm drafting, I have a household AI (a domestic agent, say) that it's manufacturer advertises as low-impact: it avoids side effects. I want the engineering in the book to be technicially honest, so I need to pin down what "side effect" could even mean for an agent like that. The obvious dodge is to tell the thing everything you value, but the whole point of low-impact measures is to avoid needing a full specification of value, and I'd rather not handwave that.

Give me a working definition of "side effect" for a bounded AI agent, and run it through these cases:

1. It's asked to clean the kitchen. To wipe the counters it moves the spice jars, and when it puts them back it alphabetizes them.
2. It's asked to book a flight. To do this it opens an account with the airline and leaves database records behind (email, card token, preferences).
3. While vacuuming, it consumes electricity and warms the floor slightly.
4. A delivery robot from the same household cuts across the neighbor's lawn and kills a strip of grass.
5. It's asked to help a kid with algebra. The kid's grade improves, and a classmate's class rank drops one slot as a result.
6. It's asked to cancel a coffee with a friend. It sends the cancellation, and the friend turns out to be relieved. That emotional change is an effect on another mind.
7. It declines to tighten a leaky pipe while recharging, because that task isn't in its queue. Is the water damage a side effect of its inaction?

I'm sure 1 is a side effect and 3 isn't, or at least isn't one worth caring about, but I suspect "unintended consequence" won't cleanly do the work because the agent doesn't intend things like a person, and I keep going back and forth on whether omissions count. If it helps, split "side effect" into a bare causal sense and a loaded, worth-caring-about sense, and say which cases shift under each. Then give the single best revisionary definition for a "low-impact" setting, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or bite the bullet.

At the end, tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you're least sure of.
