ok so i've been stuck on this for a few weeks and want someone to push back properly. I read some Jaspers on German guilt after the war, especially the way he seperates criminal guilt from political guilt from moral and metaphysical guilt, and it got me trying to build my own little distinction. I call it guilt as residue versus guilt as standing. The idea is that when we say a nation or a generation is guilty, we arent saying the people alive today caused the wrongdoing, and we arent even saying theyd do it again if given the chance. We're saying the wrong act left a residue in the institutions, habits, silences, and inherited positions of the group, and if you now occupy one of those positions—say as a citizen of the country that did the thing, or as a beneficiary of the stolen land—you inherit a kind of standing. You become the person to whom the unpaid debt is owed, or the person who owes it. Not because your a bad person, not because theres a national soul, but because the debt has to live somewhere and you are where it lives. I think this solves the usual problem where collective guilt either collapses into just the actual perpetrators or inflates into everyone with the same passport being tainted forever. It makes guilt a property of a position, not a person.

But I'm not sure it actually works. It seems to imply weird things, like if a wrong is old enough and the institutions have changed enough, the residue might simply be gone, and then the descendants are off the hook—not because time passed but because the standing got destroyed. It also seems to imply that a complete outsider who moved to the country last week and now pays taxes has inherited the debt, which feels both right (they joined the position) and wrong (they had nothing to do with the history). So what I want is an honest assessment: reconstruct the strongest version of this residue/standing view, tell me whether its already a thing under another name I'd probably know if I'd done the reading, give me the sharpest three or four objections, and say whether any of them kills it or just forces a rewrite.
