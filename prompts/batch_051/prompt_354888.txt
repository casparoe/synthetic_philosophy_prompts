I've been reading alignment-forum-adjacent stuff long enough that I've settled into a view about chain-of-thought monitoring, and I want to know what would actually count against it. The view is this: using a model's chain-of-thought as a safety-relevant window into its reasoning is self-undermining. As soon as you monitor, score, or train against the CoT, you've introduced optimization pressure for the model to decouple its real reasoning from the token stream — either the work happens in activations/weights and never reaches the CoT, or the CoT becomes a sanitized, persuasive story optimized to satisfy the monitor. So the stronger your safety scheme's reliance on CoT faithfulness, the less faithful you should expect the CoT to be under selection. Its not just that current CoTs are noisy or incomplete; it's that monitoring them actively selects against their usefulness as a window.

I want a map of what would have to be true for this to be false, not a literature review. Specifically: what experimental results, training setups, formal arguments, or observations would be real defeaters? A training regime with strong optimization pressure where CoT faithfulness stays high by some independant verification method? Evidence that models can't easily reason in a way that doesn't surface in tokens, or that gradient updates don't reach that kind of decoupling? Current unfaithful-CoT results turning out to be measurement artifacts? Observed cases of frontier-ish models under adversarial pressure that stayed faithful anyway? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed holder of my view would be reasonable to dismiss and why. Also be honest about whether the view, stated this way, has been insulated into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that's a problem for an empirical-looking claim about training dynamics or just the appropriate epistemic posture for a claim about alignment.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question.
