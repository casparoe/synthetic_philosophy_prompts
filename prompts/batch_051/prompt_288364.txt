I'm putting together a micro-seminar on Darwin's and Turing's strange inversions of reasoning, and I want it to pressure the view that genuine competence has to be built on prior understanding. Below are seven systems people often cite as competence without comphrehension. Rank them from least to most damaging to the understanding-first picture, as tiers if you want:

- a termite mound holding its inside temperature nearly constant
- the vertebrate adaptive immune system preparing for pathogens it has never seen
- natural selection producing the vertebrate eye
- a grain market clearing prices across a country without anyone planning it
- an ant colony finding a shortest path to food
- a trained deep network making Go moves stronger than any human player's
- a large language model writing an explanation of why bread rises that would pass a first-year chemistry exam

I also want the boundary, if you see one, between Darwin-style cases (no representations anywhere in the loop) and Turing-style cases (uncomprehending work on representations) to be visible in where you place things. If you need a sharper criterion for the ranking, state it in one sentence, but don't treat the question as ill-posed. For each item, give one sentence that says what earns it that spot and which side of the Darwin/Turing boundary you'd put it on. Lead with the full ranking in the first sentence, then the reasoning; no preamble, no restating the question.
