I'm a high school student, and I keep noticing an asymmetry in how people judge online games. If someone breaks into a classmate's account and deletes a rare in-game item that took hundreds of hours to earn, people condemn it but hesitate to call it theft or to say that anything real was destroyed. Yet if the same person steals the real money that such an item sells for, nobody hesitates to call it theft. At the same time, if someone hurls a stream of cruel insults at another player inside a game, we treat it almost as seriously as insults said in the school hallway. In effect, we treat virtual objects as less real than physical objects but treat virtual harms to people as fully real. Is there a principled justification for this asymmetry, or is it just an inconsistency? Give the strongest justification you can for treating virtual objects and virtual interpersonal harms differently, test it against a nearby case (for example, deleted digital artwork versus a humiliating message posted in a group chat), and say whether the asymmetry should be kept, narrowed, reversed, or abandoned.
