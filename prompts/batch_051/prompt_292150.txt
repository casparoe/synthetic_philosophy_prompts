For the past two years, four families on my street — including mine — have shared the task of driving our children to a school twenty minutes away. On each school day, one family's adult drives all of the children, and the turn rotates week by week. The arrangement began informally, and although we eventually pinned a roster to a kitchen noticeboard, no one ever made an explicit promise or signed an agreement; each of us simply took our turn. Recently one family announced that they have bought a second car and will now drive only their own children. They say they owe us nothing: they never promised, they never accepted a rule, and every turn they drove was a free choice. The rest of us feel that by taking part in the rotation for two years — by driving other people's children and letting us drive theirs — they entered into something that cannot be exited unilaterally without our concurrence, or at least that requires more of them than a week's notice. I understand that a promise or a contract would bind them, but we had neither. Is there nonetheless a distinct kind of obligation that arises from the fact that we did this together rather than as a sequence of separate favors? If such an obligation exists, is it owed to each family individually or to the four families as a group? And who is right here: are we justified in feeling wronged, or are we merely dressing up our disappointment as a moral claim?
