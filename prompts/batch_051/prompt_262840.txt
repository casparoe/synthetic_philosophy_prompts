Here's the setup. A freind gives me a black-box agent: a trained policy I can run in its environment and probe however I like, but I can't see inside it — no weights, no code, no reward function. He's convinced that if you poke the environment in the right ways (add obstacles, change payoffs, plant decoy goals, vary the action costs), you can recover not just what the thing is roughly trying to do, but the actual objective it's optimising and the space of options it's searching over — and that any two objective/search-space pairs consistent with the behaviour are trivially equivalent reparameterizations. I'm not so sure. I keep bringing up potential-based reward shaping, and he says that's just the trivial part. My worry goes deeper: behaviour plus interventions might underdetermine both the objective and the search space in a really stubborn way (the agent could be satisficing, or optimising over a different state representation, or discounting differently), and his claim might only be testable in tiny toy worlds.

So: design the best test you can of his claim. What would you actually do to bring evidence to bear on whether an optimizer's objective and search space can be recovered from its behavior under interventions? Say what pattern of results would push you toward 'yes, recoverable' and what pattern would push you toward 'no, fundamentally underdetermined.' And then be explicit about the residue — what even a clean positive result would still not show.
