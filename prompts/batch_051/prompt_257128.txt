I'm a novelist and I keep circling an idea I half-remember from modal logic. There are two formulas named after Barcan that, taken together, seem to say every possible world has exactly the same inventory of things as this one: nothing genuinely new can come into existence, and nothing that exists can ever fail to. So everything that actually exists, exists necessarily, and everything that could have existed already does. Write a short story that treats that as literally true of the world the characters live in—the actual physics, not a metaphor or a debate. Push the idea into places where it should be emotionally strange or brutal: a wanted pregnancy, a scientific discovery, grief, someone trying to become a genuinely different person. After the story, add a short note that seperates which parts of this are settled logic versus what's genuinely contested among people who work on the stuff, and that flags where the story leans on one contested reading rather than another.
