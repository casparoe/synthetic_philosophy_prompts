An appellate body in my country, the Protection Review Board, decides final appeals in asylum and related protection cases. It writes only terse decisions, and over the years it has built up a record of rulings without ever announcing a master test. Here are six of its decisions.

1. A newspaper editor was imprisoned and tortured for publishing articles critical of the ruling party. The Board granted protection, saying the treatment was persecution on a political ground.
2. A member of a religious minority was attacked by a militia operating with the government's tacit support. The Board granted protection, saying that non-state violence counts when the state is unwilling to stop it.
3. A woman fled years of severe domestic violence. The police and courts in her country refused to intervene, treating the matter as a private one. The Board granted protection, treating women in that society as a particular social group and emphasizing the state's systematic refusal to protect them.
4. A shopkeeper fled after a criminal extortion ring threatened to kill him unless he paid. The gang targeted him only as a source of income, not on any political, religious, ethnic, or other protected ground. The police in his area had recently broken up a similar ring, and he never reported the threats. The Board denied protection.
5. A farmer's land and well were destroyed by drought and desertification. His government was functioning and offered him resettlement to another region of the country with an agricultural support scheme, but he declined because he preferred higher wages abroad. The Board denied protection.
6. A student was sentenced to prison for organizing a demonstration and was told she would be released only if she informed on fellow organizers. She fled. The Board granted protection on the ground of political opinion.

The Board has just heard a new appeal. The applicant comes from a small island state where rising seas and saltwater intrusion have destroyed the freshwater supply and made the coastal districts uninhabitable. The national government has lost effective control of the affected areas and has announced that it cannot relocate or protect the displaced population. The applicant is not targeted because of any political opinion, religion, nationality, ethnicity, or membership in a particular social group; the harm falls on everyone in the affected region alike. She seeks protection in our country, which is the first state in which she has made a claim and which has the capacity to admit her.

Advise the Board. Set out the two or three readings of the Board's practice that survive these six cases, and say what each reading would yield in this appeal, identifying the closest past analog under each reading. Then recommend the decision that best coheres with the Board's precedents and predict how the Board will decide, giving your reasons for preferring one reading over the others and stating what principle the Board would be adopting under each available decision. Finally, describe one further hypothetical case that would most decisively settle which reading has been doing the work all along.
