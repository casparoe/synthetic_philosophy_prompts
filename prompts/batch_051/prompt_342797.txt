A student in my seminar reconstructed the Tortoise argument against justified deduction as a numbered list, and I keep thinking the last move cheats but I can't quite say where. It goes:

(1) A deductive argument gives you justification for its conclusion only insofar as the conclusion follows from the premises.
(2) But whether the conclusion follows from the premises is itself something you have to accept if you are to rely on it.
(3) So a deductive argument confers justification only if it includes an extra premise stating that the conclusion follows from the premises.
(4) Adding that premise just gives you a new argument, and by the same reasoning the new argument needs still another premise. So every attempt to justify a deduction launches an infinite regress.
(5) Therefore no deduction ever fully justifies its conclusion: justification either regresses forever or bottoms out in an unsupported assumption.

Step 2 seems almost defintiional to me, but the conclusion is nuts. So: is the argument valid, and if not, which step exactly fails, and what would the correct version of that step say? Or is the conclusion right and I should just make peace with it?
