im taking a philosophy of maths course and we've spent the last while arguing about wether the consistency of the standard axioms (PA, or ZFC if you want to go bigger) is something we actually have reason to believe, or just something we can never prove and sort of have to assume. One side is roughly Hilbertian: they think consistency is about as well-established as anything could be—daily mathematics, the usual relative consistency proofs, no contradiction after more than a century. The other side keeps pointing out that by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem any consistency proof has to use machinery at least as questionable as the system itself, so our confidence ends up resting on a bit of circularity plus the fact that nobody has found a contradiction yet, which they say is pretty weak evidence.

The task: write the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between these two camps would actually produce. It should include (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign; (2) the precise remaining disagreements, worded so each side accepts the wording of its own view; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'd expect at least one crux to be about whether relative consistency proofs genuinely transmit assurance, and at least one to be about how much evidential weight the absence of a contradiction so far can carry. Don't try to pick a winner; the aim is a text both camps could put their names to. Please write in British English.
