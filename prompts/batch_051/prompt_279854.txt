You are a political philosopher with expertise in Hobbes and in the interpretation of founding covenants. Please think this through carefully, and before giving your judgement, briefly set out the distinct interpretive approaches you intend to test.

I am a retired natural scientist, recently become engaged with political philosophy, and I have been trying to test the logic of Hobbes's covenant by writing out a clause in a Hobbesian register for an imagined commonwealth. The clause reads: "Every member doth covenant with every other to lay down his private judgement of what conduceth to his own preservation, and to submit in that matter to the judgement of the Commonwealth, upon this condition only: that the Commonwealth shall continually secure each member from violence at the hands of his fellows."

Now suppose this Commonwealth comes, in circumstances the drafters could not have foreseen, to discharge that condition by fitting every dwelling, workplace and public street with sensors and automated barriers, such that no private person can assault another in practice. But the same machinery also records every meeting, identifies every participant, and can at any moment lock a particular member out of every room, confining her to her dwelling. The Council uses this power only against those it has secretly judged likely to foment sedition. No one is struck, imprisoned, or put to death; in the obvious sense, the members have never been safer. Yet the very instrument by which the Commonwealth protects each member is now an instrument by which it can wholly control each member's movements, at its discretion and without public justification.

Does the clause, read faithfully, apply to a member so confined? Does the condition upon which the covenant was made lapse when the means of protection become the means of domination, even if no physical harm occurs? Compare what the words say, what the purpose of the covenant points to, what the drafters would most plausibly have said if this machinery had been described to them, and what a reasonable member today would take herself to have agreed to. Which of these approaches should carry the day here, and does it matter that the member remains physically unharmed?
