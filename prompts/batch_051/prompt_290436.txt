I'm not a linguist or anything, but I've been thinking about writing systems a lot since I started learning Spanish and poking at how English spelling works. I'll just give you my gut calls on a bunch of cases, and I want to know if there's actually one principle behind them, or if I'm just defending whatever I grew up with.

- English spelling is a mess, but I don't want a spelling reform where every letter stands for exactly one sound and every sound gets one letter. I like that 'sign' and 'signature' look related even though the g is silent in one. That feels like the spelling is telling me something real about the word's past.
- The old joke that English could spell 'fish' as 'ghoti' has never worked on me. It uses 'gh' the way it works in 'enough' and 'ti' the way it works in 'nation', but those only do that in certain spots. So I don't think English really could spell fish that way, and I don't think the joke shows English spelling is hopeless.
- Spanish feels almost transparent: you look at a word and you basically know how it sounds. If you're learning to read, that's got to be better than English. I don't see how that's just my preference.
- But then there's the plural s in English. 'cats' ends in an s sound and 'dogs' ends in a z sound, and we write them both with the same s. I like that. It's writing the plural marker, not the exact noise my mouth made. If someone changed it to 'dogz', I'd hate it.
- Chinese characters: I used to think they were just little pictures, but most of them aren't. Alot have a piece that hints at the sound and a piece that hints at the meaning. I don't think that's worse than an alphabet; it's a different bet about what's worth putting on paper.
- Japanese kana are one symbol per syllable, and that feels right for Japanese because it only has a smallish set of syllable shapes. English as a syllabary would be a nightmare, because we have too many possible syllables.
- I read somewhere that Korean letters are drawn to sort of match the shape your mouth makes for the sound. I can't tell if that actually helps anyone read or if its just a nice story people tell about the script.
- And I can't explain why I find 'thru' ugly. If transparency is good, 'thru' should be a small improvement over 'through'. But I'd never write it.

The rule I keep circling is something like: a good writing system writes down whatever the language needs you to notice, and different languages need you to notice different things. But that might just be a way of letting every system off the hook.

So: is there a principle, or a small set of principles, that actually gets these verdicts? What does it say about a case I didn't list, like a language that writes tone marks or one that mostly leaves vowels out? And which of my gut calls would it force me to give up?
