I'm giving a paper next month to a public-history workshop — half academic historians, half people who run museums and memorial sites — and I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a redraft of the talk. My thesis: when a community's memory conflicts with the archival record, an institution (a museum, a school curriculum, a truth commission) shouldn't treat the conflict as a factual tie to break; it should exhibit the conflict as the object. My argument so far: memory and history have different success conditions — memory succeeds when it sustains a usable identity, history when it tracks what actually happened — so 'which side is true?' is the wrong question, and letting either side win outright destroys the thing the conflict itself discloses. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll hit and by how much trouble each one is, phrased the way each of them would phrase them, with what to concede, what to hold, and where each reply is softest — plus the question I'm hoping nobody asks. At the end, tell me which part of my position you'd be most nervous about and what an objection would have to look like before you'd change the prep advice you're giving me.
