Give a working definition of "moral status" that classifies the following cases correctly: a healthy adult human; a dog; a human infant born without cerebral hemispheres; a sperm whale, whose brain is several times the mass of a human brain; an elephant, whose brain is roughly three times the mass of a human brain but, on the best available estimates, has only about a third as many neurons in its cerebral cortex; an octopus; a honeybee; a large language model completing a single forward pass; and a hypothetical neuron-for-neuron electronic replica of an adult human brain. Run the definition through each case, noting where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Identify the single classification you are least confident in. Finally, state what your settled definition implies about whether brain size itself is morally relevant, as opposed to being a rough proxy for something else.
