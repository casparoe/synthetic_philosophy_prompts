Chomsky's poverty of the stimulus argument says kids pick up grammar from input that is too sparse, noisy, and full of errors for any general-purpose learner to get the rules, so there has to be a built-in language faculty (UG or whatever). That seemed pretty compelling back when the only learners anyone thought about were children and the only data stream was child-directed speech. But now we've got LLMs that, with no hardcoded grammer, get trained on a giant text corpus and end up producing grammatical sentences—having apparently acquired a lot of syntax. Does the poverty of the stimulus argument survive LLMs, or does the LLM case show the original argument was secretly about the size and quality of the training data rather than any special innate grammar? If you think it survives, pin down exactly which difference between an LLM's pretraining data and a childs input is doing all the work. If you think it doesn't survive, say what the innateness claim becomes now: is it just 'children get way less data,' or is there a salvageable version where 'innate' still earns its keep?
