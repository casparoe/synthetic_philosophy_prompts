Next month I'm giving a lunch talk at an interpretability group, mostly researchers who build automated tools for explaining what language models are doing internally — using one LLM to summarize the features or circuits of another, or to generate hypotheses a human then checks. My claim, in one sentence: recursive interpretability (using AIs to interpret AIs) doesn't close the gap between us and the target system, it just relocates it, and it may actually make things worse because the interpreter's output is itself a black-box artifact, so the explanation gets more fluent and more convincing exactly when it has the least verifiable connection to what the target system computes.

The quick version of the argument I'll give: (1) whatever an interpreter model says about a target model is a generated text, and our grip on why it said that is no better than our grip on the target; (2) every way we have of checking the interpreter's claim either goes back into the target's internals (the original unsolved problem) or stays at the level of behavior (the thing interpretability was supposed to improve on); (3) so what "a good explanation" now means in practice is just "an explanation that a competent-seeming model produced and we find satisfying" — which is a standard an unfaithful explanation can game.

The audience matters here: two of them are big on LLM-as-judge and automated circuit discovery and basically think this is the only scalable route to supervising systems smarter than us; one is an ML theory person who thinks "understanding" was always a fuzzy concept and we should stick to behavioral evaluation; the rest are sympathetic but undecided. Prepare me for the question period. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those actual people would phrase them, with what I should concede on the spot, what I should hold, and a short reply for each (two or three sentences). Also flag the weakest point in my position that I should preempt rather than hope they miss, and tell me the one objection I should be praying nobody brings up.
