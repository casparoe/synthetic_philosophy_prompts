But a guy in my philosophy of language study group has started using the same line for a much bigger claim: that whenever you add a modifier to a noun you get a new kind, not just a narrowing, so 'fake news' isn't news and 'decaf coffee' isn't coffee. That feels like it overreaches horribly, but I can't cleanly seperate out the intermediate readings. Could you lay out the different precise claims 'a white horse is not a horse' might be making — about words, meanings, concepts, extensions, kinds, individuals, identity, whatever — and for each say whether it's trivial, defensible, or just false, and which of them would actually license my study-group guy's inference?
