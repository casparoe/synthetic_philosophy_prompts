In my AI governance seminar we've spent three weeks on whether the profit motive—or capitalism more broadly—is the root cause of AI risk, and the same two camps keep repackaging there arguments. One side says: competitive markets make labs race, so safety gets cut whenever it doesn't help the benchmark or the next funding round, and a lab that slows down gets outcompeted or defunded; so the real problem is the incentive structure, and technical fixes won't stick until you change who owns and funds the labs. The other side says: even a fully public lab run by a benevolent planner would still be optimizing a proxy that doesn't match what we actually want, and a misaligned system kills you whether or not its owner is chasing profit; so profit and competition are amplifiers or surface features, not the root. And the weird thing is both sides mostly accept the other side's facts—race dynamics exist, RLHF doesn't make models robustly value-aligned, the current labs are corporations—and nobody moves an inch. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this a substantive empirical disagreement about an untested counterfactual (what a non-capitalist AI project would actually do if you could run the experiment honestly)? Is it a verbal fight about what "root cause" means—necessary condition versus aggravating factor, structural versus proximal, precipitating versus sustaining cause? Are the two sides holding different counterfactuals fixed—same institutions but different motives, versus same motives but different institutions? Or is "root cause" doing political work that isn't really causal explanation at all? My own hunch is that "root cause" is quietly trading on two or three different senses and that once you pull them apart the two sides largely agree—but I can't tell if that's a real diagnosis or just me wanting everyone to get along. Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here—a distinction, a model or natural experiment, a different question—or should I expect the impasse to persist because the argument is really about something else?
