I'm drafting a short statement of my views on the epistemology of intuition for a workshop, and before circulating it I'd like an independent consistency check. The commitments I want to state are these:

(i) In normative ethics, firm and widely shared considered judgments about cases are strong constraints on theory. The judgments that you may not push the one person off the footbridge, and may not frame an innocent person to stop a deadly riot, are data that a moral theory must accommodate, not noise for it to explain away.

(ii) In fundamental physics, theory may override intuition wholesale. I accept, on something like an Everettian reading, that the quantum state is all there is, and I do not treat the felt absurdity of branching as a cost to the theory.

(iii) I regard the relativity of simultaneity as a paradigm case in which empirical confirmation licensed a theory to overturn an extremely strong pre-theoretic conviction.

(iv) I accept the empirical picture from experimental philosophy and dual-process moral psychology that many case intuitions, especially deontological ones, are substantially shaped by affect and framing, and that this makes them unreliable guides to the moral facts.

(v) I reject global intuition skepticism as self-undermining: the considerations that would support distrusting intuition must themselves be intuitive judgments about what counts as evidence, so distrusting all intuitions at once would undercut the grounds for the distrust.

(vi) I doubt there is any domain-general rule for when a compelling theory should override a firm intuition; I think such conflicts are settled case by case within reflective equilibrium.

I want to know whether this package hangs together. Which commitments, or pairs of commitments, are actually in tension, and how severe is each tension? Which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the least costly revision would be.
