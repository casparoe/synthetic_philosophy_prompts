I am a PhD student in the philosophy of mathematics, and next term I have been asked to give a single two-hour seminar to second-year undergraduates who have taken one course in discrete mathematics and one introductory logic course. They can work with finite sets comfortably, but the infinite is mostly a rumour to them: they know the slogan that 'some infinities are bigger than others' without knowing what 'bigger' is doing there. I want the session to make one structural point land — that one-to-one correspondence, rather than the part-whole relation, is the standard way to compare infinite collections, and that this is not an arbitrary stipulation but the extension of what counting already is. I am not asking for the explanation itself; I need a plan for how to build it.

Please give me the order in which the components must be introduced and what depends on what: in particular, where the finite-via-bijection account of counting has to be in place before Galileo's paradox can be felt as a real fork in the road rather than a puzzle. Then identify the single carrying example to build around — I am inclined towards Hilbert's Hotel, but I want to be told exactly what it must be engineered to show, what it must not be asked to show, and why it should or should not be that example. I also need the misconceptions this audience will bring or form, in the order they will arise, with the point in the sequence where each should be headed off. I am especially concerned about four: that infinity is just a very large number; that the hotel only works because an infinite hotel is physically impossible and therefore proves nothing about mathematics; that if the evens can be paired off with the naturals, then bijection has stopped measuring anything worth calling size; and the leap from 'these results are strange' to 'actual infinities are incoherent, so finitism wins'.

End with a diagnostic for the final ten minutes that would distinguish someone who has understood the concept from someone who has merely accepted that the hotel is odd — something that forces them to say which notion of 'more' or 'same number' is doing the work. Finally, tell me what to leave out at this level, which I take to be transfinite arithmetic, natural density treated as a rival rather than a different question, and the Continuum Hypothesis, and how to decline those topics without licensing the reaction that cardinality is therefore just a trick. Please write your answer in American English.
