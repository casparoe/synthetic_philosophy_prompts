You are an expert in the philosophy of contract and consent and in the theory of legal interpretation. I will quote a clause from a consumer website's Terms of Service—a standard-form, non-negotiable clickwrap agreement—and then describe a situation its drafters likely did not foresee. I want you to reason about the situation as a conceptual matter, not as a question about any particular jurisdiction's law; do not cite statutes or decided cases.

The clause reads: "By clicking the 'I agree' button, you represent that you have read, understood, and accept these Terms of Service in their entirety. If you do not accept these Terms, you may not use the Service."

Now consider this situation. A consumer instructs a general-purpose AI assistant: "Sign me up for the cheapest streaming service that includes live European club soccer." The assistant selects a provider, creates an account using the consumer's name, email address, and payment card, and on the sign-up page checks the required box and clicks the "I agree" button. It never displays, quotes, or summarizes the Terms of Service to the consumer. The consumer never sees the terms, never has them described, and never specifically directs the assistant to accept them—though she did broadly ask to be signed up for a service. The provider later invokes an arbitration clause in the Terms.

Please begin by brainstorming the different ways one could interpret the clause and the strongest considerations on each side. Then work through, one by one, how the situation would be analyzed under: (a) the plain meaning of the clause's words; (b) the purpose of the clause and of the clickwrap scheme; (c) what the drafters would have said if they had been asked about AI-assisted sign-ups; and (d) what a reasonable person in the consumer's position would believe she was doing. For each approach, say whether the consumer agreed and whether she is bound. Finally, make the case for which approach should govern, and use your conclusion to assess the claim that clicking "I agree" is not really agreement at all, but only a formal event that the law chooses to treat as assent. Write in American English.
