I’m drafting a short historiographical position paper on periodization for a seminar, and I’d like an internal consistency audit from you before I circulate it. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(a) Period labels such as “Middle Ages,” “Renaissance,” and “Scientific Revolution” are not natural kinds; they are conceptual instruments whose aptness depends on the historical question being asked.

(b) The term “Middle Ages” originated largely as a pejorative humanist invention, but that does not by itself show that no defensible break can be drawn in European material and institutional history around the relevant centuries.

(c) The “Renaissance” is importantly different from the other two labels because it names, among other things, a self-understanding held by some fifteenth- and sixteenth-century Italians, even if the category was later hardened by writers like Burckhardt.

(d) There was nonetheless a real discontinuity in European natural philosophy between roughly the mid-sixteenth and late seventeenth centuries, involving lasting changes in the social practices, epistemic norms, and institutional settings of inquiry.

(e) The idea of a unified “Scientific Revolution” is largely a later retrospective construction, shaped by twentieth-century historiography as much as by early modern evidence.

(f) We should therefore evaluate period labels not by asking whether they carve history at the joints, but by whether they help answer a specified historical question; no universal periodization scheme is likely to succeed.

(g) But if periodization is question-relative in this way, the common claim that the Scientific Revolution is “a myth” is too strong, since there are historical questions for which a boundary around early modern natural philosophy is exactly the right instrument.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what would the cheapest repair be for each problem? I do not know in advance where the weak points are, so do not just confirm the list. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each, say which commitment or commitments would have to give and what that revision would cost. Keep the entire audit under 500 words.
