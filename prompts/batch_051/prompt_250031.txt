I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about manipulation versus legitimate persuasion, and my verdicts on cases don't seperate cleanly, so I want to see if there's a principle behind them.

Here are the cases and my gut reactions:

- A friend who knows I just got dumped and haven't slept waits until I'm crying to ask me to cosign a loan, and says "this is what friends do." Manipulative; not okay.
- A car salesman answers my questions straight, lets me test drive, and says "I can tell you know cars." I see the flattery for what it is, and it feels fine—oily but not manipulation.
- A doctor tells me "95 out of 100 people come through this surgery fine" instead of "5 out of 100 die," because I've been avoiding a surgery I need out of fear. Fine, maybe even good.
- A political campaign buys my browsing data and shows me an ad about how the other candidate's plan will close the plant in my town, with a photo of my street, while my neighbor gets a totally different ad aimed at their own anxieties. Not fine.
- A dating app sends "your match is waiting" at the time of day I'm usually loneliest. Not fine, though it's not the worst thing.
- A partner says "if you really loved me you'd move for my job." Not fine.
- A professor tells me "you're exactly the kind of student who'd thrive on the honors track" mostly because the department needs numbers, and they know I crave approval from authority figures. Not fine.
- A friend lays out a case for lending them money: shows me their budget, walks through the risks, and tells me to take my time and that there's no hard feelings either way. Fine—textbook persuasion.
- A gym trainer yells "don't be weak!" to get me through the last reps. Fine, somehow; harsh but not manipulation.
- A charity mailer shows a starving child with the line "you can save her." I keep going back and forth on this one.

I'm struck that the doctor case and the campaign case both exploit my known psychology, but I feel completely different about them. And the trainer yelling "don't be weak" and my partner saying "if you loved me" both lean on identity and emotion, but I only object to one of them.

My rough principle is something like: manipulation is influence that works by bypassing or shutting down my capacity to weigh reasons, while legitimate persuasion engages that capacity. But that doesn't fit the doctor case well, since it seems to bypass my fear-based reasoning, and it might make the charity mailer come out fine when I'm not sure it is.

Is there one principle that gets all or most of these verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn, and why? What does the principle you end up with say about a case I haven't listed: an AI companion that has read months of my messages and waits until I've confided that I'm lonely late at night to push an expensive subscription, using a voice I've told it is comforting? Please write your answer in American English.
