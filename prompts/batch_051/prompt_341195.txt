At my place the other night two friends got into it over Nietzsche's Ecce Homo, the section headed Why I Am So Clever where he goes on about diet and health. Jon said the whole thing is basically nineteenth-century wellness content: Nietzsche blames German cooking and beer for making people heavy and dull, claims what you eat shapes how you think, and treats digestion like it belongs to philosophical method. Sarah said no, he's making a serious naturalistic point—thinking is something a whole organism does, so diet, climate, and how you spend your body's energy really do condition the quality of your judgments. Jon replied that even if some diet made Nietzsche feel sharper, that wouldn't make his conclusions true; if anything it makes you suspicious that his philosophy is just a symptom of his gut. Sarah said that's exactly what Nietzsche is willing to turn on everyone else: a badly nourished body produces sick valuations, so you can't judge higher values from a depleted physiology. Jon said that move is circular—you define health by whether it produces the philosophy you like, then dismiss the opposing philosophy as unhealthy. I couldn't tell who was winning. Can you adjudicate their exchange step by step, say who is right about what, and say which reading of Nietzsche is better overall?
