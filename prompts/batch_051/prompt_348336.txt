A friend and I recently spent an evening arguing about whether mental images are real pictures in the head or only a façon de parler, and I came away convinced that we were never actually disagreeing about the same thing. Her position was that what we call mental imagery is really a set of descriptive, symbol-like representations doing all the explanatory work, and that the vivid feeling of seeing a picture in the mind's eye is a side effect rather than the thing itself. I resisted that, but I also could not say clearly what a mental image is once the metaphor of a little internal screen is dropped.

So I want a working definition of mental image, precise enough to be tested rather than merely to match the metaphor. Run your definition through these cases and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer: (a) someone who deliberately calls to mind the layout and colors of their childhood bedroom and insists they can see it; (b) someone who understands the sentence "the square is inside the circle" and reports that nothing pictorial or quasi-visual occurs; (c) someone who mentally rotates an asymmetric letter to judge whether it is the same as its mirror image, with effort that grows with the angle; (d) a chess master who says they see a forced mate after a glance at a position but denies that there is any literal picture before their mind; (e) a person blind from birth who solves an orientation judgment by reasoning about spatial relations and reports no visual qualities; (f) an afterimage left by staring at a bright window, which floats in the visual field and cannot be voluntarily altered. I feel confident that (a) must come out as a mental image and (b) must not; my views on (c) through (f) are unsettled.

I suspect the term does two jobs: one names a quasi-sensory experience, something it is like for the subject, while the other names a functionally spatial or depictive format of representation as against a descriptive symbol string. Give me either one definition that keeps those two jobs straight, or two definitions with an account of which cases switch between them, and then tell me which of your classifications you are least willing to defend.
