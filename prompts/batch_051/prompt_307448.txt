I manage a small team at a mid-sized company, and I have a recurring experience I need help thinking through. Every so often — during a one-on-one, in a crowded meeting room, or while out with friends — I am struck by the vivid impression that I am the only person whose experience is somehow lit from within. Everyone else appears to me, in those moments, the way supporting characters in a story appear in relation to the protagonist: they say the right things, react plausibly, even display what looks like frustration or warmth, but the whole performance feels, just below the surface, as if there is no one on the other side. The odd thing is that I do not actually believe this when I stop to reflect. In a calmer moment I would insist that my colleagues and friends have inner lives as rich as my own. But the impression keeps returning, and I have begun to notice its effects. I interrupt more often in meetings. I find myself treating other people's complaints as data about their behavior rather than reports from an inside I cannot see. Once or twice I have caught myself making a self-serving decision and feeling quietly justified by the thought that the people affected would not feel the loss the way I would. This disturbs me, because I do not want my conduct to be governed by an intuition I can neither defend nor name. What is this intuition, exactly? Is there any reason to think it tracks a real difference between my experience and everyone else's, or are there good grounds for setting it aside? And whatever the metaphysics says, does anything about the intuition actually support treating other people's experiences as less real or less weighty than my own?
