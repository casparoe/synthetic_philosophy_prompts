A common gloss on chaos theory treats sensitive dependence on initial conditions as showing that certain deterministic systems are unpredictable in a strong sense: any finite specification of the present state, however precise, is compatible with trajectories that later diverge without bound, so that beyond some horizon the future state cannot be fixed by any measurement we could in fact make. I accept that strong claim, and I want it tested. What would count against it? Lay out the observations, formal results, and conceptual clarifications that would show the claim is false, that it trades on a loose use of the term unpredictable, or that it collapses into the much weaker claim that our instruments are limited. For each, say whether it is decisive or merely awkward, whether a defender of the strong claim could coherently absorb it, and what would have to be given up. Conclude by assessing whether the claim as stated has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that matters for a thesis of this kind.
