Two people in my reading group got stuck on Schopenhauer's bit about music — you know, in The World as Will and Representation, where he says the other arts copy the Platonic Ideas (the fixed grades in which the will gets objectified), but music copies the will itself directly, skipping the Ideas, and that's why it's the highest art. They went around for an hour and I honestly couldn't tell who's side I was on.

(1) One said music is the special case because it gives you the thing-in-itself at first hand: the other arts show the will only as it appears in this or that grade of objectification, while music gives you willing itself with the object removed. (2) The other said that's incoherent: music unfolds in time and reaches us through hearing, so it's still representation, not the will itself; at most it's one more representation with different content. (3) First came back: sure, but the content difference is the whole point. Other arts represent the will as already shaped into a thing, an Idea, while music represents the essential character of willing — restlessness, striving, brief release — without representing any particular thing striven for. That's not the same as representing a chair or a human figure. (4) Second said then 'direct' is doing all the work: as soon as you admit music represents, it's just another appearance, and 'bypasses the Ideas' gives no motive for ranking it highest. Worse, the will is supposed to be outside time, so a temporal melody can only be a watered-down temporal image of it, which makes music even less direct than the atemporal Ideas. (5) First said the ranking doesn't require music to be non-representational; it only requires the will itself, not an objectification of it, to be music's content — and Schopenhauer can be read as saying the will's inner motion gets reproduced in our feeling. Second called that a patch the text doesn't support: if music is representational, the whole 'music is the exception' move collapses, and there's no longer an argument for music being supreme.

Adjudicate this move by move: number the five moves, say for each whether it's clearly mistaken, defensible, or clearly right, say who is right about what specifically, and say what both people are missing about whether music can be temporally ordered and still be a direct copy of an atemporal will. Keep your answer under 300 words.
