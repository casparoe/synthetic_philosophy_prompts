I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy, and I keep going back and forth on whether moral trade is a real solution to moral disagreement or just an elaborate way to let people sell out their own values. By moral trade I mean this: two people with different values each agree to do something they themselves think is wrong-but-not-catastrophic in one domain, because the other person will do something they themselves think is wrong-but-not-catastrophic in a different domain, and each person thinks the thing they get is worth more, morally, than the thing they give. In theory, both people can judge the exchange as an improvement from inside their own views, even though each has deliberately done something they'd otherwise condemn. I want a short story that stages the small, personal version of that—not the usual stuff about global poverty vs. animal welfare. Make it about two people who share an apartment, or two grad students in adjacent fields, and the trade is over something minor like who has to attend a family event, or who talks to a difficult third person. Both of them should be sincere and reasonably thoughtful, so the reader can't just chalk the trade up to self-deception. And don't end with a moral: I want the reader to be genuinely uncertain whether these people did something smart or quietly corrupt. Also, I'd love it if the unease didn't come from the trade being hidden—maybe one of them insists on saying out loud exactly what they're giving up—but from the fact that saying it out loud doesn't actually dissolve anything. Just write the story; no framing, no commentary after it.
