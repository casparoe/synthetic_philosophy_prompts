I need someone to actually think this through with me, because I've been going around in circles and the people I ask either give me the standard "holistic admissions" speech or shrug. I do alumni interviews for my old college, and this year I got put on a small working group that reviews the admissions office's procedures after some bad press. The office has a Faculty Admissions Handbook, and I'll quote the exact clause I keep coming back to. It says:

"4.2(a): Except as provided in this section, the Committee shall not consider an applicant's race, color, or national origin. The Committee may take an applicant's membership in a group historically underrepresented at the College into account, but only as one factor among several in a holistic comparison of applicants who are otherwise comparably qualified. No applicant shall be admitted or denied admission solely on account of such membership, and the record of each decision shall set out the grounds on which the applicant was admitted or denied in sufficient detail to show that the decision did not rest solely on that factor."

The new thing is that we are piloting a screening tool bought from an outside vendor. The vendor feeds it the whole file, essays, recommendations, activities, the high school's zip code, all of it, and the tool spits out a single score from 0 to 100 predicting how likely the Committee is to admit the kid. The office uses it as a pre-screen: files under 55 never get read by a human at all, and above that the Committee reads and votes the way it always has. The vendor is very clear that race is not one of the fields it is given.

Here is what is bothering me. A woman on the working group, who is the only one of us comfortable with spreadsheets, took a few thousand scored files and showed that you can tell the applicant's racial group from the score alone far better than chance, like better than 80 percent for the two largest groups, and the pre-screen is now letting in white and Asian applicants at a much higher rate than Black and Hispanic applicants, a bigger gap than before we bought the tool. The tool was trained on this Committee's own decisions from the late 2010s, so I guess it learned whatever the Committee was silently doing then. The chair of the Committee says there is nothing to see becuase the clause only governs what the members actually consider when they vote, and no member ever sees a race box; the model is just triage, and anyway the record-keeping sentence only applies to final votes, not to a vendor's black box.

I think he is wrong but I cannot say exactly why without sounding like I am accusing everyone of racism, which I am not. I want you to act as a professional philosopher, the sort of person who thinks for a living about what rules mean and how they reach cases their authors never pictured. Before you settle on an answer, take your time and walk me through the strongest version of the chair's position, then the strongest version of the position that the policy already reaches the screening tool. Along the way, work through what the words themselves say, what the clause was for, what the people who drafted it in 2005 would probably have said if you had shown them the tool, and what a reasonable reader would take the clause to require. Then tell me which reading should win, and what the Committee would actually have to do to comply.

And please write it like an email back to me: continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
