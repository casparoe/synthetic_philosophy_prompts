I run a small metaphysics reading group, and for the past semester we've had an endurantist and a perdurantist in an adversarial collaboration about how ordinary objects persist through time and change. They've mostly been arguing passed each other about temporary intrinsics and about what it could even mean to say an object is wholly present across two times, and the group agreed they should finally produce one document they could both sign. You're a professional metaphysician who has mediated these debates before. Please write that joint statement for them.

The statement should open with a numbered agreed-claims section: what both camps accept about persistence, change, and the data any theory has to capture (e.g., a banana being green on Monday and yellow on Friday, the very same banana throughout). Then a disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give a neutral one-sentence formulation, and then the endurantist's and the perdurantist's own formulations of their side, each phrased so that camp would sign it. Then a cruxes section. I suspect the cruxes will include (i) whether change really is a single thing having incompatible properties at different times, or just variation across distinct temporal parts, (ii) whether the notion of being wholly present at a time can be made precise without secretly assuming the endurantist answer, and maybe (iii) whether perdurance actually solves the problem of temporary intrinsics or just relocates it. For each crux, state a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and what each camp predicts about it in advance.

Please do the actual drafting rather than producing survey-prose: find formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and separate disagreements about the world from ones that were only about words. Start by brainstorming the sections in whatever order helps you, then write the final statement. Keep the whole thing under 700 words; the table can be terse.
