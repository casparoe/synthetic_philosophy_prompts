Adorno and Horkheimer's culture industry argument says that once culture is produced with the same industrial logic as cars or soap, it stops being a place where things could be otherwise: it becomes standardized, pseudo-individualized (a million tiny variations on the same formula), and it trains audiences to be passive consumers who mistake being entertained for being free. The whole picture, though, depends on there being a small number of big producers pumping this stuff out to a mass audience.

Now suppose that part is gone. The production and distribution of culture has splintered: anyone with a phone can make and publish music, essays, video, whatever, and platforms distribute it through algorithms that micro-target billions of distinct niches. Much of what people watch is made by other users rather than by studios or labels, and the audience is constantly commenting, remixing, stitching, duetting. So the old image of a passive mass being fed standardized products from above looks wrong, or at least out of date.

Does the culture industry argument survive this? Is the new situation just a different way of manufacturing pseudo-individualized commodities, except now the audience does the standardizing for free?
