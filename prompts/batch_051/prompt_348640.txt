The practical difficulty is that these labels are embedded in disciplinary convention: they shape what counts as a coherent research topic, which sources are grouped together, and how a narrative is expected to run. Please describe the most important implications of this constructivist view of periodisation for the ordinary decisions involved in doing history – formulating a research question, selecting and grouping sources, structuring a thesis or monograph, and designing an undergraduate survey module. Present your answer as a table with the following columns: 'Implication', a concise statement of one consequence of the view; 'Site of practice', the aspect of historical work affected (research design, source selection, narrative structure, teaching, or appraisal of earlier scholarship); 'Why it follows', the reasoning connecting the constructivist view to that consequence; and 'Practical precaution', a concrete rule, question, or habit a historian might adopt in response. Please give between five and seven rows, ordered from most to least fundamental.
