I run a once-a-month ideas night in the back room of my local, and next month's topic is the claim that conscious experience is a kind of user interface: the inner voice, the feeling of being the decider, the single stream of experience is a heavily simplified, after-the-fact summary of what a lot of seperate systems in your brain have already done, not the place where the work happens. I don't want to give a talk on it — I want to run a 45-minute activity for about a dozen regulars (no philosophy background, a few engineers, a nurse, a retired teacher, some students). Design the activity: the roles or rules, what I need to prepare, a rough timeline, and the debrief. It needs to be built around a concrete decision in the game whose actual causes get recorded somewhere, so there's a real record to compare the first-person story with. The structure should get them to catch themselves giving a clean, confident first-person explanation of a decision when they never actually saw what produced it, and then have that story come apart when its compared with what the different 'systems' did. I want to know what will predictably go wrong when its run, and I want you to be honest about which feature of the user-illusion view a role-play can't reproduce — I'm guessing the point that in ordinary life none of us notices the smoothing, whereas here everyone knows they're playing a part. Also, if the only way to make it work is for me to explain the view beforehand, scrap it and design something else; they should hit the idea before anyone names it.
