I am currently taking a philosophy of mathematics course, and I keep being pulled between two positions about the continuum hypothesis. I would like help working through what it would take for either side to give way, rather than simply being told which side wins.

On one side, I find a pluralist or multiverse conception of set theory compelling for its systematic virtues. The standard first-order axioms of set theory seem to capture the iterative conception of set well enough for almost all of mathematics, yet they are consistent both with the continuum hypothesis and with its negation. If those axioms are our best account of what sets are, then the continuum hypothesis appears to be underdetermined: there are equally legitimate structures satisfying the axioms, some in which the continuum hypothesis holds and some in which it fails, and there is no further fact selecting one of them as the intended universe. This view explains why the question has resisted settlement without appealing to a faculty for perceiving a realm of sets, and it fits the way mathematicians routinely work with different models without needing the hypothesis decided.

On the other side, I cannot shake the intuition that there is a fact of the matter about the subsets of the natural numbers. The power set operation is supposed to yield all subsets, not merely the subsets available in some model, so the claim that every infinite set of reals is either countable or equinumerous with the reals should be determinately true or determinately false. When the pluralist says that some models satisfy the continuum hypothesis and others do not, I hear an answer to a question about provability or about model-relative truth, not an answer to the question about how many subsets there really are. The move of comparing an independent sentence to an underdetermined property of a fictional character seems to change the subject rather than to answer the intuition, because sets are not artifacts of a story and the phrase all subsets appears already to intend a maximal, definite totality.

I am not asking you to pick a side. I want a negotiated verdict. State each side at its strongest, and then tell me: what would it take for the determinacy intuition to be the thing I should revise or debunk, and what evidence would show that it is an artifact of finite or visual thinking? What would it take for the pluralist theory to be the thing I should amend or abandon, and is there a plausible route by which new axioms could decide the continuum hypothesis in a way that respects the original intuition rather than merely stipulating an answer? Finally, say whether there is a reading on which both survive, and what exactly that reading would leave of each side.
