I've been stuck on this idea that a state is basically a corporation that won. The old chartered companies already had their own armies, collected taxes, signed treaties and governed whole territories, so the line between public and private starts to look like a story told by whoever ended up on top. What I like about the theory is how much it explains with so little: no special moral magic, just a firm that got big enough to call it's rules law and it's debts state bonds. But I also cant shake the intuition that a citizen is not just a customer. If my country is a company, why is it fine for the state to lock me up or draft me, when a private security firm doing the same thing to its members would be obviously monstrous? Is the citizen-customer line something that survives the charter-company history, or is it just the winner's branding? Give me the strongest version of both sides and tell me which should yield — whether I should debunk the intuition or give up the theory — or whether there's a reading where they fit together. I want to know what each move would cost, not just which side to pick.
