I've been in a reading group that spent four months going through Mencius and Xunzi on whether human nature is good, and we've hit one of those loops where nobody moves. The Mencius side keeps coming back to the example where someone sees a child about to fall into a well and feels that jolt of alarm and compassion before any thought of reputation or the parents' gratitude; the Xunzi side says fine, but the stuff you're actually born with is just hunger, warmth, desire, aversion—if you let that run itself you get contention and disorder, and whatever goodness there is has to be built through deliberate effort, the way crooked wood gets straightened with steam and presses. After four months it's no longer obvious to me that they're describing two rival claims about the same thing rather than two different senses of the word "nature." Mencius seems to mean something like the direction a thing grows when it grows well, which is why his favorite image is a sprout you water and protect; Xunzi seems to mean whatever is given at birth before anything is learned, which is why for him the raw material can be bad without the finished person being bad. So one camp in our group says the whole Mencius–Xunzi fight is a terminological squabble about what should count as xing, and the other camp says that's too easy, because they also disagree about the shape of moral development: whether it's more like watering an internal tendency or straightening a thing from outside with presses and steam, and that isn't a defintional point, it's a point about what we owe to our inborn impulses. I'm starting to suspect the real disagreement is about whether "good" should be attached to the starting point or to the direction of growth, and that mencius and xunzi only look like they contradict each other because we keep using one word, "nature," for both the inborn material and the tendency it might grow into. Is that an actual diagnosis, or am I just dissolving the debate by quietly redefining it? And if my diagnosis is right, is there anything genuinely left to argue about, or would progress just be the two sides agreeing to use two different words for the raw material and the ripening?
