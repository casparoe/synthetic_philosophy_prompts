I keep coming back to a weird double standard about information about you, and I can't tell if there's a real principle underneath it or if we've just gotten used to one side of it.

Take two situations. In the first, a neighbor sits at his window almost every day and keeps a notebook: what times you leave, what times you come back, what you're carrying, who visits you. He's not planning to rob you or anything, he just likes keeping track, and maybe he sells the notebook to local businesses. Everyone agrees this is creepy, maybe even stalking. If you found out youd feel violated, you'd tell him to stop, you might call the police.

In the second, an app on your phone does basically the same thing. It logs where you are at all times, when you leave the house, which stores you stop at, how long you stay, what you order ahead. It sells that to advertisers and data brokers. Most people don't seem to mind. They click agree without reading and go about their day.

So in one case the guy is a creep and possibly a criminal, and in the other its just the phone being a phone. The information collected is nearly the same, and the part of it that's about you is exactly the same. What I can't figure out is whether there's a real difference between the two, or whether the app is just as creepy and we've all silently decided not to look at it.

Maybe the difference is consent, because there are terms of service. Maybe it's that the app gives you something back, like directions or the weather. Maybe it's that the company doesn't know you as a person, just as an advertising ID. But each of these seems to fall down when you move it to the other case: the neighbor could also say you were told and that he'll shovel your walk in exchange, and a company can know you as an individual far better than a neighbor with a notebook ever could.
