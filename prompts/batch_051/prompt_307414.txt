I am a teaching assistant for an intermediate course in the history and philosophy of science, and I am grading an examination answer from the unit on the Scientific Revolution. The unit spent a session on Galileo's attempt to reconcile Copernican astronomy with Scripture in his Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, and the instructors stressed that students should not treat later ideas about non-overlapping domains as if Galileo already held them. The exam question was:

"Explain Galileo's position on the relationship between Scripture and natural science as set out in his Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina. Address (a) why apparent conflicts between Scripture and Copernican astronomy arise on his account, (b) what the principle of accommodation commits him to in the interpretation of biblical passages about nature, and (c) whether his view is fairly summarized as the claim that science and religion occupy wholly separate domains."

One student answered as follows:

"Galileo argues that Scripture and nature are both expressions of God's word, and therefore they cannot genuinely conflict. When they appear to conflict, he says the fault lies in interpretation: either we have misread the Bible, or we have mistaken an uncertain astronomical conjecture for a demonstrated truth. To handle the apparent conflict over the motion of the Earth, Galileo invokes the principle of accommodation. Scripture speaks about natural things in a manner adapted to the understanding of common people, because its purpose is to teach salvation rather than the structure of the heavens. Passages that appear to say the Sun moves or the Earth is stationary should therefore be read figuratively, while Copernicanism is accepted on the basis of observation and mathematical demonstration. This makes Galileo an early version of Stephen Jay Gould's non-overlapping magisteria: physical facts belong to the astronomers, salvation and morals belong to Scripture, and the Bible simply has no authority on matters of physical fact."

Grade the answer out of 10 and provide the point-by-point comments that justify the grade. The marking scheme rewards accuracy about the historical position and penalizes, specifically, conflation with later positions and overstatements about the scope of Scripture's authority. For any claim that should be corrected, state precisely how the student should amend it.
