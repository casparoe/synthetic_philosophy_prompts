I keep going back and forth on what omnipotence even means, so I wrote down my verdicts on a bunch of cases and want to know what principle, if any, is behind them.

- God makes a stone too heavy for him to lift. I think the question is broken, not that God lacks power: the task is defined as 'something God can't do,' so failing it cant count against him.
- God makes 2+2=5, or a square circle. Same: there's no state of affairs there to bring about; not a limitation.
- God makes a being more powerful than himself. Here I get uneasy. If omnipotence just means maximally powerful, this should be like the square circle and I should shrug. But I don't. 'A being more powerful than God' feels describable, and a being who can't make one is failing at 'can do anything.' So maybe omnipotence is just an incoherent ideal.
- God makes a past event not have happened. I think it's impossible, but I don't want to call it a pseudo-task like the square circle — it feels like a real limitation, just not one that makes God less than perfect. Which feels like special pleading.
- God makes himself cease to exist. I lean that this is possible and a real power, and if he can't, that's a genuine limit. But that sits badly with my 2+2=5 verdict, since God's necessary existence is supposed to be just as necessary as arithmetic.

Is there one principle that sorts all of these, or close? Which verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the case I keep leaving off: can God create a free creature and guarantee it never sins?
