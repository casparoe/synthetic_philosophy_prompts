I keep chewing on a parallel and I can't tell if its real or just me pattern-matching.

One side is the MIU puzzle. You start with the string MI and four rules: you can append U to any string ending in I, you can double everything after an M, you can replace III with U, and you can delete UU. The task is to get to MU. The proof that you can't get to MU doesn't happen inside the string game. You jump out and count I's: the number of I's in any reachable string is never a multiple of 3, while MU has zero I's, and zero is a multiple of 3. That counting is arithmetic, which the MIU system itself can't even express. I've seen this move called JOOTSing, jumping out of the system.

The other side is law. It looks like a legal system's own rules can never settle which rules actually count as legal rules: at some point there has to be a practice among judges and officials about how they recognize law, and that practice isn't itself one of the system's rules. So law also needs a step outside the system to say what belongs to the system.

Is this the same structure, or is the analogy just loose? I can already see one disanalogy: in the MIU case the outside is a stronger formal system, arithmetic, while in the legal case the outside is a social practice, not a formal system at all. Does that kill it? If you had to spell out the mapping precisely, what maps to what? What would need to be true on both sides for anything to actually transfer? And what's something the parallel would predict about legal systems that turns out false?
