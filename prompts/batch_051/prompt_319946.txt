I've been working out a view I've started calling the priority account of sexual exclusivity, after an argument with two friends in which one insisted monogamy is only a convention and the other insisted that anything short of sexual exclusivity is a betrayal. I came away thinking both were conflating two things. Let me distinguish contact exclusivity from priority exclusivity. Contact exclusivity is the familiar rule: partners do not have sex with third parties. Priority exclusivity is a structural fact about how each partner deliberates and acts. Across the range of decisions where a romantic life is actually built — time, attention, care, disclosure, life planning — one person's interests and the relationship's continuance are given lexical priority (they simply come first, regardless of how small the competing consideration is) over the claims of anyone who might compete for that role, and over one's own passing preferences. My central claim is that contact exclusivity is not what monogamy is really about; it is a cheap, legible, enforceable proxy for priority exclusivity. I think this makes sense of three otherwise awkward cases. A couple can be contact-exclusive without priority-exclusivity: faithful out of inertia, fear, or convenience, while keeping the relationship peripheral to their actual lives. A couple can be priority-exclusive without contact-exclusivity, which is why some transparently negotiated open relationships strike me as more committed than some formally closed ones. And the wrongness of infidelity seems to me better located in a quiet transfer of priority — the moment a third party's claims begin to be weighed equally with or above the partner's — than in the sexual contact itself.

I'm aware this may be a distinction the literature already draws under a better name, or just a way of redefining the term until it matches my intuitions. I want an honest assessment rather than encouragement. Is the distinction coherent, does it carve commitment at its joints, and where does it break? Please give me the three or four strongest objections and say which are fatal and which only force revision. I would also like you to say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, and where your assessment is most likely to fail.
