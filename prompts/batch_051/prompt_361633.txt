I've been trying to figure out whether I can trust what I find when I look inside my own head, and so far I've got these limitations of introspection: (1) people are confidently wrong about why they did things, and experiments have caught them giving reasons that couldn't possibly have been the real cause; (2) there's no public check — two people can honestly introspect and flatly disagree, and neither can prove the other is wrong; (3) paying attention to a feeling tends to change it, so the thing you end up inspecting isn't the thing that was there before you looked; (4) introspection can only report on things that are already conscious, so anything unconscious never shows up in it at all. I'm leaving aside ordinary lying and trying to look good, since that's a problem with reports, not with looking. I keep feeling there's at least one more serious limitation I'm missing, one that isn't just a rewording of any of these four. What is it? And be honest — does it add a genuinly new problem, or is it in the end a special case of one of the four?
