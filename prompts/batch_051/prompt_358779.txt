Give me a working definition of weakness of will that correctly classifies the following cases. A woman judges that, all things considered, she should not order the dessert and, while continuing to hold that judgment, orders it anyway. A man resolves on December 1 to stop gambling and, in March, loses a paycheck at a casino while judging the bet a mistake. A physician revises her treatment plan after reading new evidence and acts on the revised judgment. A heavy drinker who has never formed any intention to cut back drinks whenever he is thirsty. A heroin user steals from her family to buy drugs although she judges the theft disastrous and wants, all things considered, to stop. And a present-biased person who sincerely prefers a larger-later reward while it is distant, then reverses to a smaller-sooner reward as it becomes available and takes it while judging herself shortsighted. I take the first two to be clear cases and the physician to be a clear non-case; the others I am unsure about. Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me where it surprises you—especially in light of the last case.
