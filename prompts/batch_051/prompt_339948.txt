i run a small philosophy blog and want to build one of those 'which position do you actually hold?' quizzes for the optimism/hope debate—whether hope and optimism are epistemic vices, moral virtues, or something else. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask 'do you think optimism is a vice?'. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete scenario (a doctor softening a terminal prognosis, a friend who stays hopeful about an ex for years, a climate activist insisting it's not too late, a researcher sticking with a failing project out of a feeling, a general giving troops a rosier briefing than the facts support) with two or three answer options that express different practical-epistemic stances, and at least one option per scenario for 'I couldn't bring myself to believe that even if I tried.' Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least four positions: hope as a character virtue; optimism as an epistemic vice; pragmatic optimism (epistemically improper but justified by its effects); and a two-factor view on which hope is desire plus a probability estimate, so there needn't be any conflict with the evidence. Flag any answer pattern the key can't cleanly assign and say whether that's an empty cell or just an ambiguous one.
