In a discussion about whether pandemic border closures and lockdowns were legitimate even where they bypassed or stretched ordinary legal procedures, one participant kept settling the argument with the slogan: the Constitution is not a suicide pact. His move was that scrupulous legality during an emergency is self-defeating, and that measures which bend or exceed existing legal authority can be legitimate for that reason. I suspect that sentence compresses several different claims, only some of which actually support his conclusion. Lay out the distinct precise principles the one-liner could be expressing — what counts as the pact (the written constitution, the principle of legality, particular rights, the social contract), what counts as suicide (collapse of the state, irreversible loss of public trust or legitimacy, failure to protect life and health, abandonment of the values the legal order exists to serve), whose judgment settles that the threat is truly existential, and what deviation from ordinary legality, if any, the principle actually licenses. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false, and then identify which readings genuinely get him to border closures or lockdowns rather than merely warning against legalistic rigidity.
