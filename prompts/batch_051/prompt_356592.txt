I've been turning over a version of the Born rule problem in many-worlds that I haven't seen put quite this way, and the conclusion seems way too strong, but I can't find the bad step. Setup: spin measurement with up amplitude √(1/3) and down amplitude √(2/3), so standard Born probabilities are 1/3 and 2/3. The Everettian says measurement doesn't collapse anything; the world branches and both outcomes happen, each with a copy of me seeing it. Now here's the argument I keep getting stuck on:

(1) Before measuring, I should be uncertain about which of the two future copies I'll turn out to be, since from the inside I'll only experience one.

(2) If I'm uncertain between exactly two possibilities and nothing in my evidence favors one over the other, rationality requires equal credence: 1/2 each.

(3) The wavefunction gives me no evidence favoring either copy. Both branches are equally real, both are "me" in the future of this same pre-measurement person, and the labels "up" and "down" don't mark any difference I can point to ahead of time.

(4) So my credence in seeing up should be 1/2, not 1/3—which flatly contradicts the Born rule.

(5) The only way to recover 1/3 and 2/3 is to count branches weighted by squared amplitude. But that can't be gotten out of "there are two real branches"; the weights are an extra posit about what I should expect.

(6) If the weights are an extra posit, many-worlds hasn't explained the Born rule, it's just reimported it in new vocabulary.

(7) So many-worlds either gives the wrong probabilities (1/2) or gives the right ones by smuggling in the thing it was supposed to derive.

That conclusion feels like it proves too much, since this is supposed to be a live, defensible position. I'd rather keep the Born rule than the indifference move in (2), but I don't want to just special-plead. Which step should I reject, why did it look fine, and what does each of the standard moves—branch-counting/indifference, decision-theoretic, self-locating uncertainty—have to pay to make the probabilities come out right?
