I am drafting a short statement of my views on the demarcation problem for a philosophy of science seminar, and before I circulate it I would like an audit of whether the position I find myself assembling actually hangs together. The commitments I am inclined to endorse are these: (i) falsifiability, or something close to Popper's idea that genuinely scientific claims must be open to refutation by observation, is the best criterion we have for separating science from non-science; (ii) astrology and homeopathy are not sciences, and their failure to expose themselves to genuine observational risk is a large part of what makes them pseudoscientific; (iii) contemporary cosmology's talk of a multiverse, and fundamental physics' talk of extra dimensions or a string landscape, can count as science even though we cannot presently test these ideas observationally; (iv) the rational response to an apparent falsification is very often to keep working within the theory and treat the anomaly as a puzzle to be solved rather than a refutation to be accepted, so a scientist who retains a theory in the face of anomalies need not be behaving unscientifically; (v) adding an auxiliary hypothesis to protect a theory from a falsifying observation is sometimes legitimate and not merely defensive—as when an unseen planet was proposed to account for anomalies in the orbit of Uranus—provided the hypothesis is independently testable; and (vi) Popper's picture of science as progressing mainly through bold conjecture followed by attempted refutation is descriptively wrong, since most day-to-day science consists in puzzle-solving within an accepted framework.

Could you work through these commitments at the pairwise or cluster level and tell me which are genuinely in tension, which tensions are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and how the various tensions differ in severity? I would also like to know, for each genuine tension, what would have to give to resolve it, and which single revision of my position would do the most to make the whole set coherent.
