I am taking a philosophy of mind course this semester, and we recently spent a session on whether a mind has to live in a single brain. We read Hofstadter's dialogue about an ant colony called Aunt Hillary, in which the colony carries on a conversation, appears to hold beliefs and intentions, and seems to know things about itself, even though no individual ant grasps any of it. The dialogue left me convinced that the colony is a mind in some genuine sense, but I have not been able to find a principle that yields that verdict without also pulling in a lot of other cases I am reluctant to accept.

Here are my honest reactions to a list of cases. An ant colony that exchanges chemical signals and organizes raids, nests, and brood care: yes, a mind in a real sense, not merely a metaphor. A honeybee hive that performs waggle dances and collectively renegotiates a new nest site: yes, though I feel it less strongly. A slime mold that aggregates from separate cells and solves mazes or makes foraging trade-offs: no, or at most a very deflated sense of mind; it is doing something clever, but I would not call it a mind. A starling murmuration with its sweeping, coordinated shapes: no. A corporation that deliberates through committees, memos, and voting and then acts in ways no single employee intended: I keep flipping; my inclination is no, but then I worry I am being inconsistent with the ant colony. A deep neural network that recognizes objects in photographs: no, despite the word neural. A crowd of humans in a panic or a riot: no. An octopus, whose nervous system is spread through its arms: yes, but that strikes me as a different kind of case because it is still one organism.

I would like to know whether there is a single principle about where minds can reside—something about information integration, unified agency, self-representation, goal-directedness, or something else entirely—that captures all or most of these verdicts. If no one principle gets them all, which verdicts should I revise, and does the ant colony survive the revision? Please also tell me what the principle you propose would say about cases I did not list, such as a decentralized autonomous organization, a city government, or a forest ecosystem.
