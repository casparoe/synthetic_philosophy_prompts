Here's a set of criteria people gesture at when they talk about "transformative AI," though they rarely spell them out. A technology counts as transformative AI if, within a generation of being deployed at scale, it does all or most of the following:

- changes the occupational structure of the whole economy, not just one industry;
- raises the long-run growth rate rather than producing a one-time level bump;
- brings new institutions, laws, or physical infrastructure into existence just to manage or exploit it;
- becomes practically irreversible — switching it off would be treated as a disaster, not a change of vendor;
- shifts default authority: for a growing class of decisions, "the model said so" starts beating "the expert said so."

Now consider a country that has built what people call the Dispatcher. It's a family of narrow machine-learning systems that, in real time, sets wholesale electricity prices, highway tolls, ambulance routing, teacher rotations between schools, and reserve prices at public land auctions. It doesn't converse, can't explain itself, and each of its individual tasks is a boring bit of prediction-plus-optimization. But because the tasks all touch prices and schedules, the thing has become load-bearing.

Fifteen years in, commuting has shifted so much that the city has re-sorted itself around who can live near the cheap low-toll corridors. The grid was rebuilt around the Dispatcher's dispatch curves. A minister who overrode a Dispatcher recommendation during a heat wave was fired, and the inquiry concluded the model's output "was the only defensible basis for acting." Families argue about whether visiting grandma is worth the toll and end it with "that's just what the Dispatcher says." Nobody can say exactly what it's optimizing, since its objectives were written seperately by five agencies and then merged.

Question: does the Dispatcher count as transformative AI? Go through the criteria one by one. Where it seems to qualify, say whether that's the system itself or the social adaptation around it. And if you end up at "yes, mostly," tell me whether the question "has transformative AI already happened?" is then the wrong one to be asking.
