Ok so in bio we learned about the handicap principle, like how a peacock's tail is this huge waste of energy but peahens trust it because only a really fit peacock could survive with it. And I've been noticing humans do the exact same thing with stuff like engagement rings and elaborate promposals. We treat the expensive version of the same message as more sincere: a guy who just says he's serious about you is nice, but a guy who drops two months of his paychecks on a ring is serious. Here's the asymmetry that bugs me. We wouldn't say the first guy is lying, but we actually beleive the second one more, just because it cost him something. Is there a real principle that justifies trusting costly signals over cheap ones when the message is identical? And doesn't the whole thing fall apart once cost is relative — for a rich kid the ring costs nothing, and for a poor kid it's ruinous, so what is the expensive signal even signaling? I want the strongest version of the idea that cost makes a signal honest, then whether you'd actually keep trusting expensive signals once you see where it breaks.
