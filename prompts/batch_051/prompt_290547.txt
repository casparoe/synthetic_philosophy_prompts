I keep coming back to a simple argument about printed money, and it feels airtight. New money does not create more goods, more machines, or more capable people; it only lets more claims chase the same real things, so what printing money creates is higher prices and liquid purchasing power, not wealth. If creating money created real wealth, every government could print its way to prosperity, and none can. Yet I cannot shake a firm intuition from ordinary experience: when newly created money is actually spent in a slump — on wages, on keeping a factory open, on building a road — it seems to pull otherwise idle people and machines into productive use, and the output that follows would not have been produced without that spending. That looks like the creation of real wealth, not merely the relabeling of existing value. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuitive side to be debunked as a confusion about money versus output, what it would take for the simple argument to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a precise reading of "create wealth" on which both claims can be true.
