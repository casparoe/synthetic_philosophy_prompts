Been trying to get my head round Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and I'm a bit stuck on what it's actually meant to show. As far as I understand it, the argument runs: what you do follows from the way you are, so to be truly morally responsible for your actions you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are. But you can't be responsible for the way you are, because that comes from your genes and your upbringing, neither of which you chose; and even if you deliberately set about changing yourself, the decision to do that would come from the way you already were. So the buck never stops anywhere, and nobody is ultimately morally responsible for anything.

What I don't get is this: doesn't that make blame and punishment completely baseless? If nobody is ultimately responsible, how can we justify sending someone to prison, or even just being angry with them? I've seen people say Strawson isn't out to abolish punishment, but honestly that sounds like a fudge — surely if the argument works, we should stop treating people as responsible altogether. Or have I misunderstood what the word 'ultimately' is doing here?

Also, where do you think the argument is weakest, and what would change your mind about the overall verdict?
