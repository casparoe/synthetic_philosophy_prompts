Article II of the 1967 Outer Space Treaty says, in full: "Outer space, including the moon and other celestial bodies, is not subject to national appropriation by claim of sovereignty, by means of use or occupation, or by any other means." The same treaty also says "there shall be free access to all areas of celestial bodies." Now take a case its drafters plainly did not have in mind. A private company, Pallas Extraction, wants to land an autonomous harvester on a metal-rich asteroid, scrape up a few thousand tonnes of platinum-group metals, and sling the payload back toward Earth. The company's home state authorizes the operation and publishes a "safety zone" around the landing site: no other vessel may come within two kilometres for the twenty-five years the licence runs, enforced by the state's own penalties. The state insists it is not claiming soverignty and is not stopping anyone from using the rest of the asteroid. My question: does Article II reach this? Argue it from the actual words, from the purpose of the clause, and from what the 1967 drafters would have said if they'd been asked; say which approach should govern and why. Does it matter that the clause says "national appropriation" but the actor is a company? And is there any coherent reading under which the answer doesn't just collapse into how long, how big, or how exclusionary the zone is?
