I'm applying for a small summer grant in our linguistics/cogsci group and need a proposal of about 1,500 words. My half-formed idea is this. Everyone knows the king − man + woman ≈ queen result, and it gets read as evidence that embeddings encode semantic relations linearly. But I've come to suspect the single-vector representation of polysemous words is doing a lot of quiet work in that reading. For a word like bank, the one vector has to serve both the river sense and the financial sense at once. The standard line is that the vector is roughly a centroid of the two sense-vectors; but the superposition story coming out of interpretability makes me wonder whether it's better described as two nearly orthogonal sense-directions sharing the same vector, so that analogy arithmetic recovers whichever sense the cue words reinforce and the other sense acts as interference. Those two accounts make different predicitons about how analogies should degrade when the senses pull in opposite directions—e.g., whether bank − river + money lands somewhere financial, whether putting a homonym among the cue words breaks the parallelogram systematically, and whether sense-frequency alone is enough to explain the errors.
