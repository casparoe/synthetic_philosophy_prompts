I am a retired natural scientist — I spent most of my career in isotope geochemistry, though I have lately been reading formal epistemology and Bayesian statistics for pleasure — and I have become interested in whether the convergence theorems about the washing out of priors actually describe how a real scientific controversy settles. The episode I keep coming back to is the decades-long dispute over the Alvarez impact hypothesis for the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction, from the discovery of the iridium anomaly at the boundary, through the later evidence of shocked quartz and the Chicxulub crater, to the gradual if uneven acceptance of a large bolide impact as the trigger, with the Deccan Traps volcanism as the main rival throughout. I want you to analyze that episode through the lens of the Bayesian convergence results, and then to let the episode press back on the theory.

Be disciplined about the mapping. Identify who or what plays the role of the agents whose priors are supposed to wash out — is it individual scientists, research groups, or the community as a whole, and what exactly were the competing priors distributed over? Specify what counts as the shared evidence stream and where it fell short of the theorem's assumptions: dogmatic priors that put zero mass on impact, disagreement about likelihoods due to auxiliary hypotheses, asymmetric access to samples and data, and the tendency of the gradualist side to move to a different hypothesis rather than update. Then say which conditions of the convergence theorems seem to have held in this case, which failed, and whether the eventual consensus is best read as a genuine washing out of priors, as the retirement of a generation of holdouts, or as something the theorems are silent about. I am less interested in a history lesson than in an honest two-directional assessment: what the convergence framework genuinely explains about how the debate ended, what it misdescribes or leaves out, and what the episode reveals about the idealizations built into the theorems themselves.
