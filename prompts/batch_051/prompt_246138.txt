In Turchin's secular-cycle theory, big pre-industrial agrarian states have an internal boom-bust rhythm: population growth produces more elites than there are elite slots, the surplus elites compete for a shrinking share, state finances go into crisis, the state fragments or collapses, and then the cycle resets. The strong reading says this is a genuine causal regularity, one you could in principle have used ahead of time to pick out which states would destabilize and roughly when — not a flexible story fitted onto cherry-picked collapses after the fact.
