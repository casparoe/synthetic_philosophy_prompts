I have spent years reading rationalist blogs and LessWrong-style forum discussions, and I keep running into a tension in how people use the inside view / outside view distinction from the forecasting literature. The empirical track record usually cited for the outside view looks overwhelming: base rates beat detailed expert narratives, the planning fallacy appears across domains, and superforecasters seem to get much of their edge by anchoring in reference classes. I find all of that convincing.

Here is the part I can't resolve. Why isn't the lesson simply: always take the outside view when one is available, and suppress the inside view as a bias? Why doesn't epistemic modesty require me to throw away my detailed story about why my project is different and just use the base rate? The standard advice is to balance the two views, but that looks like wanting it both ways: the inside view is the thing that drags people away from well-calibrated base rates, so integrating it sounds like mixing clean data with noise. Isn't "integrate" just a polite way of saying "keep the bias but call it Bayesian"?

What exactly is the inside view doing epistemically once an outside view is on the table? Does epistemic modesty commit me to ignoring my own detailed model whenever a base rate exists, or is there a principled Bayesian role for the case-specific story?
