I write about science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, and I'm working on a piece arguing that a writing system is a kind of theory of its language: a script decides which features of the speech stream are worth marking—tones? vowels? morphemes?—and which ones the reader already knows or can recover, and those decisions add up to a claim about how the language hangs together. Before I publish I want the full specification of what a theory in this area has to get right, because I keep catching myself judging every script by how close to an ideal alphabet it gets.

So: what would an adequate theory of writing systems have to account for? Give me the requirements list. Include the range of units scripts are known to encode—morpheme, mora or syllable, consonant, segment, feature, and any others—and the distinctions a theory can't collapse (grapheme, orthography, and script; phonemic versus phonetic; unit of encoding versus visual form). It has to handle Chinese, Japanese mixed kanji and kana, Hebrew or Arabic abjads, Devanagari-style abugidas, Hangul's featural letters, and deep versus shallow alphabets like English vs. Finnish or Turkish, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Mark clearly which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particular camps. Also tell me which single requirement does the most to kill the lazy popular view that a script is just speech made visible, and whether any pair of requirements pulls against another—learnability against morphological transparency, say, or phonetic accuracy against dialect coverage. I don't expect a formal impossibility theorem here, but I do want the tensions flagged.

There's one thing I dont want: a taxonomy of scripts. I already have one.

Oh, also: format the answer in sections, in this order. "The point of the theory" (2–3 sentences). "Non-negotiable requirements" (600–750 words, each requirement with its motivating case and why it's a requirement). "Camp-specific preferences" (250–350 words). "The requirement that excludes the most" (150–250 words). "Tensions" (150–250 words). American English, please.
