I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been thinking about weakness of will: the familiar case in which a person judges that one action is better all things considered, and yet deliberately does the other. I have collected four things people say about such cases and do not need any of them restated. (1) The preference-reversal account: the person discounts future rewards hyperbolically rather than exponentially, so a smaller, sooner good overtakes a larger, later good as it becomes imminent, and falls behind again once it has passed. (2) The divided-self account: the person really is on both sides at once, and weakness of will is the short-sighted part of the mind winning. (3) The change-of-judgement account: no one truly acts against their standing better judgement, because at the moment of action the judgement itself has shifted, and the later story of a lapse is retrospective. (4) The hot-cold account: the better judgement is formed in a cool state that cannot fully imagine how strong a hot desire will feel, and the action follows the hot desire once it arrives. I want an account of weakness of will that is not on this list and is not a variant, special case, or combination of these four. Then tell me honestly whether the account you give is any good. When you give that verdict, also say what would change your mind about it, which consideration or counterexample would lead you to revise it, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Please write in British English.
