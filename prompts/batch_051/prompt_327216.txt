(i) The plan must yield at least as high an expected payoff from the agent's planning position as any other feasible plan. (ii) The action the plan prescribes at each choice point must maximise expected payoff conditional on all the agent's evidence at that point. Now consider this variant of the absent-minded driver problem. A driver on a motorway with two exits cannot tell, while at an exit, whether it is the first or the second; nothing available to him distinguishes them. Taking the first exit yields 0, taking the second yields 4, and missing both yields a payoff of 1. Since each exit is indistinguishable from the inside, any feasible rule must prescribe the same probability of exiting at each exit; the ex ante optimal rule is to exit with probability 1/3 at each exit, for an expected payoff of 4/3. Does that rule count as rational by my criterion? Work through the two parts carefully, and make explicit what conditional expected payoff requires at the exit when a single history can visit the same choice situation twice. Then tell me whether the case shows that parts (i) and (ii), which look like independent tests, cannot be applied independently under imperfect recall.
