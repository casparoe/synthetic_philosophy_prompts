You are a senior researcher who works across algorithmic information theory, formal epistemology, and the foundations of machine learning. I am drafting a three-year research agenda for a small group (one PI at half time, two postdocs, and one PhD student) whose remit is to work on Solomonoff induction, Kolmogorov complexity, and the formalization of Occam's razor. I want your considered judgment about which open questions or lines of work in this area deserve the group's limited effort most, and why. This should be a defended agenda, not a survey of the field and not a list of references.

Before you settle on a ranking, spend some time brainstorming candidate questions and the criteria by which they should be judged; make your criteria explicit and show the reasoning that leads from the criteria to the ranking. Think carefully about what a small group can actually make progress on in three years, and about which questions, if resolved, would force changes in the largest number of other debates.

In particular, weigh candidates drawn from at least these clusters, and add any others you think belong on the list: the choice of reference universal machine and the fact that the invariance theorem only guarantees agreement up to an additive constant even though the constant can matter enormously for finite data; the relationship between ideal Solomonoff prediction and computable approximations such as two-part minimum description length or resource-bounded priors; whether any of this genuinely justifies Occam's razor as an epistemic principle, as distinct from an instrumental rule that says what tends to work; and what the formal apparatus does or does not explain about generalization, overfitting, or implicit bias in modern machine learning. You are not limited to these, and I expect you to consider trade-offs among importance, tractability for a group of this size, and how much other work depends on the question.

Structure your response in exactly the following sections:

3. Ranked agenda (about 500 words). Present your top three candidates, ranked, and for each defend its position using your stated criteria. Be explicit about how you resolved trade-offs between importance and tractability.

4. What you would not fund (about 250 words). Identify one or two plausible-sounding candidates that you are deliberately ranking below the top three, and say what would have to change for them to move up.
