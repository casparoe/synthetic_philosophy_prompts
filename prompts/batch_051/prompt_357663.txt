My coworker jokes that its suffering. I laughed, then realized I don't actually have a principled reason to say it isn't.

So give me your actual credence on this precise claim: by 2040, at least one artificial reinforcement learning system will exist whose negative reward signal constitutes genuine suffering — where 'genuine suffering' means a state with moral weight at least comparable to acute pain in a rat, not merely a training signal steering a policy away from bad states.

Decompose it into numbers. At minimum I want these terms, each with a probability and a one-line justification:
- P(a broadly functional/computational theory of valence is right — pain and pleasure don't require biological neurons in particular)
- P(conditional on that, RL reward has the right kind of functional role to be valence, rather than something like homeostatic error or predictive-processing signals)
- P(conditional on both, some system by 2040 will have the right structure for the signal to actually count — e.g., reward integrated with a self-model or global workspace, not just a scalar fed to a policy update)
- and whatever other term(s) you think belong (e.g., that moral weight doesn't require an evolutionary history).

Then show how you combine them into a final number, and tell me which single term the final credence is most fragile with respect to — the one where new evidence would move you most, and what that evidence would look like.
