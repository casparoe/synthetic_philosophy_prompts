I'm a PhD student in early modern philosophy and I've been going in circles about a tension in Spinoza, so I want someone to tell me exactly where my argument goes wrong (if it does). Here it is:

(1) Spinoza is a necessitarian: everything that happens follows necessarily from the divine nature. There are no genuinely contingent events, and no alternate possibilities for any finite mode. A person's ideas, affects, and strivings are as necessitated as anything else.

(2) The intellectual love of God (amor Dei intellectualis) is, for Spinoza, our highest blessedness. It is an affect that arises in the mind from the third kind of knowledge, which is knowledge of things as following necessarily from God.

(3) Now, whether a mind ever acquires adequate ideas of the third kind is itself one of those necessitated facts, because it is a state of a finite mode. So for any given person, wether they reach the intellectual love of God is already fixed by the causal order.

(4) But in Part 5 of the Ethics Spinoza writes as though reaching this state is something we can be led toward or fail at. The whole therapeutic side of the book — reordering our ideas, weakening passive affects, moving from bondage toward freedom — seems addressed to people who might not get there.

(5) If (3) is right, a person who never reaches adequate ideas could say: it was necessary that I never got them, so the practical path of the Ethics was never really available to me. And a person who does reach it didn't achieve anything either, since they couldn't have failed.

(6) So Spinoza's necessitarianism looks like it undercuts the practical aim of the Ethics: the intellectual love of God is both the goal of the work and a state that no one could have been helped into, because the outcome was fixed either way.

That conclusion can't be right, since Spinoza plainly thought the Ethics had a practical payoff. So tell me: is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and how would you restate that step so a Spinozist could accept it? If it is valid, how should a Spinozist live with the conclusion?
