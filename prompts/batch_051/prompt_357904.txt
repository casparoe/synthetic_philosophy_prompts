Explain the philosophical zombies argument against physicalism, as developed via the conceivability argument. Does the argument establish that physicalism is false, and if it does, does it go further and establish dualism? Assess the main objection to the conceivability step along the way.

Student answer:
The zombie argument, in roughly the form Chalmers gives it, runs as follows. A zombie is a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a conscious person with all the same physical and functional properties, but with no phenomenal consciousness: there is no subjective experience, nothing the zombie feels. The argument is:

(1) Zombies are conceivable. The description of a zombie world includes all the physical facts of our world while denying that any phenomenal facts obtain, and no contradiction can be found in that description.
(2) If zombies are conceivable, then zombies are metaphysically possible. Conceivability is our best guide to possibility, and there is no reason to make an exception of the zombie case, because the relevant concepts are individually coherent and their combination is not contradictory.
(3) If zombies are metaphysically possible, then physicalism is false, since physicalism says the physical facts determine all the facts, and a zombie world is one where the physical facts are the same but the phenomenal facts are missing.
Therefore physicalism is false.

The standard objection to premise (2) is that conceivability does not always entail possibility. Kripke's water/H2O case shows that we can find it 'conceivable' that water is not H2O even though water is necessarily H2O, so there is a real gap between conceivability and possibility. But Chalmers replies that cases like water are ones where the apparent possibility can be explained away as an illusion: when we conceive of water not being H2O, we are really conceiving of some other watery-looking stuff. No analogous debunking is available in the zombie case, because with phenomenal consciousness there is no gap between the state and how it appears to the subject—a state that feels like pain just is pain. So the debunking strategy fails, and the conceivability of zombies does establish their possibility. This shows that some form of dualism is true.
