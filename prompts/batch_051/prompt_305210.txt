I've spent a while in competitive debate (school, then online and courtroom-style stuff), and I keep noticing the same thing. The best debaters get extremely good at arguing either side of an issue, often just as fluidly one way as the other, but they don't get any better at figuring out which side is actually true. Training rewards whoever can generate more moves on both sides under time pressure, so the skill being trained is speed and coverage, not accuracy. It isn't exactly lying, since they usually believe whatever they've been assigned for the round; and it isn't exactly motivated reasoning, because they'll drop the position the moment the format tells them to. Is there an established name for this specific gap between argumentative skill and being right? I don't just want a bare label, though — I want to know what the phenomenon includes, where its edges are, and how it differs from the nearest neighbors. 'Sophistry' seems too broad, since it connotes deliberate deception and these people aren't trying to deceive, and 'bias' doesn't seem right because the issue is skill improving without accuracy, not a fixed slant. If there's no standard name, what would you call it? And is this gap a reason to think that turning argumentation into a spectator sport would make public reasoning worse rather than better?
