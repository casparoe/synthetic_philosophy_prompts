I have been asked to state my philosophical profile on time for a seminar on Confessions XI, and I want a consistency check before I present. The passage that anchors me is from Augustine, Confessions, Book XI, chapter XXVI: "But neither thus is any certain measure of time obtained; since it is possible that a shorter verse, if it be pronounced more fully, may take up more time than a longer one, if pronounced more hurriedly." My commitments, as they now stand, are these:

(1) Time is not simply the motion of bodies, because measuring a body's motion already presupposes time.

(2) Temporal measurement is fundamentally something the mind does: the mind holds past, present, and future together in a single present attention.

(3) Only the present exists; the past and the future have no mind-independent reality.

(4) Physics treats time as a real, objective dimension of the natural world, and its parameterization by clocks is not merely a human convention.

(5) The experience of duration over an extended interval is best explained as a judgment or comparison made by memory, not as a direct perceptual awareness of the interval itself.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and say what the least costly revision would be. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent.
