Write a deadpan FAQ page for a small high-street optician called something like Veridical Vision, run by a card-carrying disjunctivist about perception. Answer five or six ordinary customer queries — e.g. why the walking stick looks bent in the water feature even though it's straight, whether that 'looking bent' is the same kind of experience as seeing a genuinely bent stick, what's going on when someone dreams they're at the optician's, and whether two subjectively indistinguishable episodes are the same on the inside. Each answer should get disjunctivism right: the veridical case and the hallucinatory (or illusory) case are not two occurrences of one fundamental kind of mental state, however alike they feel, and in the good case you are directly presented with the mind-independent stick itself — so there is no common factor to generalise over. The humour has to come from the view's actual commitments — the denial of the common kind assumption, the odd consequence that two indistinguishable episodes have nothing essential in common, the careful refusal to let 'seeing' name a single kind across both cases — not from a straw man where the optician just insists the stick really is bent. Keep it readable to someone who has never heard of disjunctivism or the argument from illusion, but make a philosopher of perception nod rather than wince. About 500 words, laid out as questions and answers, with a deadpan practice slogan at the end.
