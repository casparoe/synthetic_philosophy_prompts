I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about delusions, and by the end I had talked myself into a set of commitments that I am no longer sure I can hold all at once. I would like a consistency check before I write any of this up. My commitments are these. (a) The two-factor theory is on the right track for monothematic delusions such as Capgras and Cotard: such delusions require both an anomalous experience (the first factor) and a second, reasoning-level deficit or bias in hypothesis evaluation, with neither factor sufficient on its own. (b) Delusions are beliefs, not imaginings, non-doxastic avowals, or some other sort of state; when a Capgras patient reports that his wife has been replaced by an impostor, the report expresses a belief. (c) Beliefs are functional states: to count as a belief, a state must, among other things, be inferentially integrated with the person's other attitudes and be apt to guide action outside the setting in which the topic is raised. (d) I also accept the familiar clinical observation that patients with monothematic delusions often fail to act on or reason with their delusions in the way (c) would lead one to expect: a Capgras patient may repeatedly assert the impostor claim and yet keep living with the person, not contact the police, and not use the claim as a premise in ordinary practical reasoning. (e) I hold a further, more interpersonal commitment: treating the patient's delusional report as expressing a belief is part of taking the patient seriously as an epistemic agent, and I worry that anti-doxastic accounts are a little too quick to write off the patient's testimony. (f) Finally, I have a nagging sense that the two-factor theory may be trying to have it both ways, since the first factor makes the delusion look like a reasonable response to a strange experience while the second factor makes it look like the output of a broken reasoning system; but I suspect this last worry is superficial.

What I need is an audit of this whole set: which commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, which are only apparently in tension, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency, and at what cost? Please order the tensions from most to least serious rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each tension specify which commitment or commitments would have to give.
