I've ended up as the reluctant rules person for our housing co-op after a blowup about whether the board could just make a one-off exception, and I've gone down a rabbit hole on what makes a bunch of rules law-like rather than just whoever's in charge deciding things as they go. The list I keep coming back to is Fuller's eight: there have to be rules at all, they have to be publicized, they can't be retroactive, they have to be understandable, they can't contradict each other, they can't ask the impossible, they can't change so often that nobody can orient themselves by them, and the officials applying them have to actually follow the rules as announced.

I want a ninth feature, one that isn't just a rewording, special case, or combination of those eight. So "don't write the rules in a dead language" doesn't count—that's just publicity and understandability. And I already know about the Raz-style add-ons (independent courts, natural justice, access to courts), so don't just feed me those or a variant of them either. If you give me a candidate, tell me honestly whether it earns it's place as a real ninth desideratum and how it relates to the eight—or whether the honest answer is that the list is already complete and my sense of something missing is about a different problem entirely.
