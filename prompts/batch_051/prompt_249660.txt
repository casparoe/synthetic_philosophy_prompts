i'm putting together a list of objections to Aumann's agreement theorem, and i already have the standard ones:

- the common prior objection: if the two people start from different priors the theorem just doesn't apply, and common priors are exactly the thing most real disagreements are about, so the theorem assumes away the hard part.
- the common knowledge objection: for the result to bite, the two posterior probabilities have to be common knowledge, which means infinitely many levels of 'I know that she knows that I know...', and that essentially never obtains.
- the ideal rationality objection: the theorem assumes agents are perfect Bayesians who condition flawlessly and have sharp posteriors, but real people are computationally bounded and often don't have precise degrees of belief at all.
- the silence about convergence objection: the theorem rules out a certain kind of stable disagreement, but it says nothing about whether any actual dialogue would bring the two posteriors together, or how long that would take.
- the fixed state space objection: the theorem presupposes a common set of possible worlds for both agents, so it has nothing to say about the (probably more common) case where people disagree because they're carving up the possibilities differently in the first place.

I want one more objection that is not on this list and not a variant of anything on it — so not just the common prior objection reworded, not a special case of bounded rationality, and not a mashup of two of the above. It should have a different structure from these. Give it a short name, explain it, and then tell me honestly whether it's any good or whether on inspection it collapses into one of the ones I already have.
