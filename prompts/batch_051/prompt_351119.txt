I have spent years practicing what I think of as epistemic kata: solitary, repeated drills on cases whose outcomes are already known. I write pre-mortems for decisions whose outcomes are on the record, then compare my imagined failure modes with the failures that actually occurred. I reconstruct historical arguments and identify which premise would have had to fail. I practice converting an inchoate dispute into a testable forecast with a resolution date. And I drill rewriting an opposing view until its actual proponents would accept the wording as fair. I have gotten much better at these drills.

In live cases, though, I impose two rules on myself. The No-Untrained-Move Rule says that I may not deploy a form of reasoning in a real argument or decision unless I have previously drilled that form in isolation. The Screening Rule says that if an argument presented to me cannot be translated, with reasonable effort, into one of my drilled forms, I treat it as not yet made and withhold updating. Part of the motivation is that live argument is disorienting and time-bound; the forms let me slow a dispute down and process it in a setting where I trust myself to be careful. The predictable result is that I rarely engage positions that resist being rewritten as forecasts, premise decompositions, or steelmanned statements of the other side.

Is this policy defensible? It has undeniably spared me some bad updates, but I can see that it might be a bias dressed as method: a test that arguments must pass in my own drilled idiom may admit only arguments that were already congenial to me, and it may quietly protect whatever priors I held before the training ever began. Since the whole point of the drilling was transfer to live cases, I need a way to tell whether this policy is transfer working as intended or a ritual that replaces updating with performing a kata. Under what conditions is a no-updating-without-a-completed-drill rule rational, and can the rule be restated so that it keeps its screening benefits without making the dojo the only place I can change my mind?
