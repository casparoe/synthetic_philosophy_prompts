I've been working out a principle I call "proxy decay," and I want an honest check rather than encouragement. The idea is this: whenever an institution uses a measurable proxy for a fuzzy target — teaching quality, customer satisfaction, departmental health — the proxy has a half-life. Once the people being measured become aware that the proxy is what gets rewarded, its correlation with the underlying target falls by half every fixed interval, say every two years, simply because everyone begins to optimize the proxy itself, even without any outright dishonesty. On my telling, Goodhart's law is usually presented as a binary switch: a measure that becomes a target ceases to be a good measure. I'm claiming something stronger and more dynamic — that proxy corruption is a continuous, law-like decay, and that it happens even among well-intentioned people, since effort drifts toward what is counted and away from what isn't.

I started thinking about this after a student-evaluation reform in my own department worked wonderfully for two years and then curdled. By year five most instructors had learned to run their classes in ways that raised the scores — crisper slides, more frequent low-stakes quizzes, explicit learning objectives — without obviously changing what students still knew a year later. My principle predicts exactly that trajectory: a metric that isn't being manipulated becomes steadily less informative, and after about three or four half-lives it is close to useless. It also predicts that the only way to keep a proxy honest is to rotate or recalibrate it on a schedule, the way security teams force periodic password changes.

What I want is a candid assessment. Is this just Goodhart's law with a clock bolted on, or is the half-life doing explanatory work the usual formulation lacks? Where does the model break — for example, a proxy that has stayed informative for decades despite everyone optimizing it, or a proxy that failed in a sudden step, not a smooth decay, when attention shifted? And where does the physics metaphor start lying to me by treating a social system as if it had a decay constant? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Also tell me which argument or finding would most change your verdict about whether "proxy decay" is genuinely distinct from Goodhart's law, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
