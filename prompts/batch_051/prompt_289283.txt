I am a high school junior in an economics elective, and a recurring family argument has made me want a sharper analysis of something we are studying in class. During the pandemic, the U.S. government made several rounds of direct payments to households, expanded unemployment benefits, and offered forgivable loans to small businesses, while the Federal Reserve bought trillions of dollars of Treasury and mortgage-backed securities. People around me tend to describe all of this simply as printing money. My teacher, by contrast, insists that creating money cannot by itself create real wealth: it can create liquidity, it can create new claims on wealth that already exists, and it can redistribute purchasing power, but it cannot summon goods, workers, or technology into existence.

I would like you to analyze the pandemic-era monetary and fiscal expansion, and the inflation that arrived in 2021 and 2022, through the lens of that distinction between money, liquidity, and wealth. I want a disciplined mapping rather than the usual vague argument. What exactly was the policy, who were the relevant actors, and what would the distinction have led them to expect in 2020, before the inflation appeared, about what they were and were not creating — and would it have told them to do anything differently? Which features of the episode does the distinction explain cleanly — for example, the difference between the Federal Reserve creating reserves and banks actually creating credit, the behavior of asset prices, or the role of inflation expectations — and which features does it misdescribe or leave unexplained, such as disrupted supply chains, the shift in spending toward goods, or the possibility that the intervention prevented a collapse that would itself have destroyed wealth? Finally, I want an honest verdict on what the episode shows about the slogan that printing money cannot create real wealth: does it survive as a reliable guide, or does it need to be qualified or replaced once money, liquidity, and wealth are carefully kept distinct?
