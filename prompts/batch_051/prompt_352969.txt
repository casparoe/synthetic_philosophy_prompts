So I'm an associate pastor at a confessionalish church and I'm the guy who ends up vetting worship ideas—everything from candles and liturgical dance to video testimonies and whether we can put a drum kit on stage. Last night I got into a three-hour phone argument with a friend from seminary about the regulative principle. He took the line that what Scripture doesn't command in worship is forbidden, full stop; I tried to say it really means anything not warranted by Scripture, where warrant includes not just explicit commands but stuff that follows by good and necessary consequence, plus the circumstances any sane congregation has to settle on its own (electric lights, order of service, what time to start). We went in circles about whether a projector for lyrics is more like lighting or more like introducing pictures into worship. He said I was being inconsistent because we already do announcements and church picnics; I said maybe that's a problem with our practice, not the principle. Anyway, he basically told me to stop doing theology in the abstract and turn it into something usable, and honestly he's right.

So here's what I need. Write me a decision procedure grounded in the regulative principle that I can actually run on a proposal before it goes to the session for sign-off. I get maybe three or four proposals a month; I have at most half an hour for a first pass; I'm clergy but not a biblical-studies expert on every item. I want an ordered list of questions, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it hands the decision to the elders because it's a values call rather than a fact, and where the harder and softer readings of the principle force a fork in the procedure. Then apply it to three cases: a congregational candle-lighting added during Advent, a four-minute pre-service video testimony, and replacing our piano with a six-piece praise band. Make sure at least one case comes out as a no I'm going to hate, and be explicit about what the procedure cant settle, because I know some of this is just judgment dressed up as method.
