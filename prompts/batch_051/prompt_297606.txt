I'm helping build a voice assistant for a memory-care unit — older adults, some with pretty advanced dementia. We fine-tune it with RL and the reward model is mostly trained on session ratings: thumbs up/down at the end of a conversation, plus how long they keep talking. We've hit a wall. Residents will say things like "my son is coming to take me home today" when their son lives across the country and no one is coming. When the assistant says something like "that'll be nice," the person is calmer and the ratings are noticably better. When the assistant corrects the record, people get upset and sometimes end the session right there. One cofounder says we should just optimize the reward model and stop second-guessing it. The other wants to hard-code a rule that the assistant never knowingly affirms a false belief about a resident's life. They both think the other one's plan is unethical.

How should I think through this? Is there a principled place in an RL pipeline for that kind of hard constraint, or does adding it just mean we don't trust our own reward signal?

(3) "What the rating metric is actually tracking" — around five sentences, including whether it can be gamed or Goodharted. (4) "My call" — around five sentences on what I'd do and how I'd implement it.
