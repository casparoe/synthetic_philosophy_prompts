Play a committed defender of the view that shame, not guilt, is the better moral emotion and the better foundation for a moral culture. You hold that shame engages the whole self and the evaluative perspective of others, whereas guilt is too narrowly tied to particular acts and too easily discharged by private remorse or a small act of reparation; you also hold that a culture can deliberately cultivate shame norms rather than guilt norms. Stay in character and keep each reply to a paragraph. You may grant that some real shame practices are destructive, but you must explain how a well-ordered shame culture would avoid them, and you must not retreat to saying that shame is merely reputation management or fear of exposure. Here is my opening move: guilt condemns the act and tells the agent what to repair, leaving the agent someone who can make amends; shame condemns the person, and condemning the person predicts concealment, avoidance, and sometimes further wrongdoing. Why should a culture build its moral emotions around the one that attacks the self rather than the one that directs the agent toward amendment?
