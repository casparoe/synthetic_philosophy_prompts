So over the holidays my brother and I got into a stupid argument about Bell's theorem and hidden variables, and I can't stop turning it over. I want to test the criteria I've been using for a "local hidden-variable theory" against a weird case, because the usual discussion skips over it and I'm not sure if I'm being unfair to it.

Setup as I understand it: Alice and Bob share pairs of particles from a central source, each picks a measurement setting at the last second, and quantum mechanics predicts correlations that violate Bell's inequality. The usual conclusion is that no local hidden-variable theory can match quantum mechanics. My four criteria for such a theory are:

1. Hidden variables: the particles leave the source carrying definite values or instructions for every measurement that could be performed.
2. Locality: the result on one side depends only on that side's measurement setting plus the hidden variables, and not on the setting chosen on the other side.
3. Measurement independence: the hidden variables are not correlated with which settings Alice and Bob will end up choosing.
4. Empirical adequacy: the theory reproduces the quantum predictions, including the Bell violations.

Now the borderline case: superdeterminism. It says the whole universe, experimenters included, are deterministic down to the last detail, and the initial state already fixed both the particles' hidden variables and Alice and Bob's "choices" in a correlated way. So criteria 1, 2, and 4 can hold, but 3 is flatly denied: the particles and the settings share a common cause at the start, not a signal between them.

Does superdeterminism qualify as a local hidden-variable theory under my four criteria? Go criterion by criterion and say where it qualifies and where it fails. Then tell me whether rejecting 3 is a sneaky way to keep the other three, or a genuinely different position. Is the right notion of locality just criterion 2, criterion 2 plus 3, or something else? And would you call a universe where every individual causal step is local but the initial conditions are rigged to produce the correlations "locally explained"? If I've set the criteria up wrong, say that instead of just grading the case.

For the format, please give me three sections in this order: "The checklist" (about 45%), "What locality should've meant" (about 35%), and "Verdict" (about 20%).
