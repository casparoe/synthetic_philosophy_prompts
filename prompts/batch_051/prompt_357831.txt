So I keep having this argument with a friend about who you should rescue if you can only rescue some people, and I've finally got her to commit to a bunch of specific cases. What's weird is I can't figure out what single rule she's actually running on.

Here's what she says. One stranger vs five strangers, all in equal danger and otherwise basically identical: save the five, no hesitation. One stranger vs two strangers, same setup: save the two. If the one is her sister and the five are strangers: save the sister, and she thinks anybody decent would. If she's already promised the one she'd come back for him, and then she finds out she could save five other people instead: keep the promise and save the one. And if the one is a ten-year-old and the five are all in their nineties: save the kid, because he's got his life ahead of him and the others have already had theirs.

Now here's the case I want to test her on. No family, no promise, no age difference—one stranger vs five strangers, everyone middle-aged and healthy. But before she gets there, a fair coin toss is held among all six people to decide who gets priority if only one can be rescued, and the lone stranger wins. Does she honor the coin toss and save the one, or does she save the five?

I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit her earlier calls just as well, and what a better test case would be to tell those rules apart.
