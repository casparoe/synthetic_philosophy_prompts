Over the last decade, a distinctive practice has grown up inside machine-learning research: the behavior of a large neural network is explained by locating "features"—individual neurons, or directions in the space of the network's internal activations—that are said to correspond to concepts the network uses. A typical report will claim that a particular direction tracks a broad property such as the language of a passage or the tense of a verb, or that removing a particular component reliably degrades a circumscribed ability, with the degradation described in the same conceptual vocabulary: the model lost the ability to form the past tense, or the edit strengthened its use of some concept. The stated ambition of much of this work is not merely to summarize input–output behavior but to discover the concepts the network genuinely represents and uses in its computation. Researchers intervene on these features—amplifying them, suppressing them, editing their values—and they take the predictable consequences of those interventions as evidence that the features are causally real parts of how the network processes information. At the same time, practitioners are quick to dismiss features that are unstable across contexts, or that appear only under a particular method of probing, as artifacts of the analysis rather than properties of the network.

I would like you to reconstruct the philosophy of representation that this practice presupposes. Specifically: what has to be true of a neural network for the sentence "the network represents feature F, and its use of F partly explains its behavior" to be a literal truth, as opposed to a convenient way of describing statistical regularities? Which family of theory—a causal-role account, a structural-similarity account, a teleofunctional or indicator account, some hybrid, or something else—best captures the commitments practitioners actually incur when they move from predictive labels to causal and explanatory claims? Then say which parts of the practice could survive under a thoroughgoing instrumentalism, on which representations are useful fictions imposed by the interpreter for the purpose of prediction and editing. Finally, identify where the practice underdetermines the philosophy: what rival theories of representation would issue in the very same experimental routines while disagreeing about what those routines show, and what would an interpretability practice look like that did not presuppose that networks literally represent anything—what exactly would it have to stop saying?
