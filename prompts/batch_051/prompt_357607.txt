A city is planning to deploy a public-facing AI assistant that will allocate certain scarce municipal resources — community-space bookings, after-school program slots, small neighborhood grants — and moderate the city's online consultation forums. The designers say the assistant should serve the values of all residents without the city government endorsing any particular contested worldview about how to live. Rather than giving the assistant a single developer-written values list, they propose this mechanism:

1. Once a year, every resident submits a ranked value-profile: a list of up to ten value-statements drawn from a standing menu (terms such as autonomy, security, tradition, self-expression, environmental stewardship, and so on), ranked in order of importance.
2. When the assistant faces a contested decision, it does not poll residents on the decision itself. It runs a Borda count over all the value-profiles — higher-ranked positions earn more points, and the value-statements with the most points win — to determine which values rank highest across the resident population.
3. The assistant then selects, from the feasible options, the option that in its judgment best promotes those winning values.
4. Before the decision is implemented, a Neutrality Review Board of seven residents drawn by lot for staggered eighteen-month terms must certify that the public justification for the decision does not rest on any contested worldview — any comprehensive view of how one should live about which residents reasonably disagree. If the Board denies certification, the assistant deletes the offending value-statement from all submitted profiles and recomputes, repeating until some decision receives a certifiable public justification.

Walk through how this mechanism would actually behave if adopted. What incentives does it create for how residents fill in their value-profiles, for how the assistant writes its public justifications, and for how the Review Board exercises its veto? Where is it gamed first? Over the first several annual cycles, what does the system select for — which values tend to dominate, which residents tend to get their way, and what kind of public vocabulary develops around decisions? Which failure modes could be fixed by adjusting the rules, and which look structural?

Please also indicate how confident you are in the individual predictions, and single out the point in your analysis you are least sure of.
