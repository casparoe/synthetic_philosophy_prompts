Write a research proposal for a one-semester term paper (approximately 6,000 words) in the philosophy of decision theory. The proposal should develop the following working idea into a precise, tractable project.

Working idea. The standard coherence arguments for expected utility (EU) maximization—Dutch book arguments and preference representation theorems—are widely read as showing that rational agents must maximize expected utility. But they appear to establish only a conditional, measurement-theoretic claim: if a preference ordering satisfies axioms such as completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence, then that ordering can be represented as maximizing expected utility relative to some utility function and probability assignment. That claim says nothing yet about whether rationality requires satisfying those axioms. This leaves room for a deflationary thesis: coherence arguments establish representability, not obligation, and the step from "representable as maximizing expected utility" to "rationally required to satisfy the EU axioms" is a step the arguments themselves do not supply.

In your proposal, do all of the following:

1. State the question the paper would answer, formulated so that it has a determinate answer. A candidate question is: "What further premise, if any, is needed to move from the representability of coherent preferences to a rational requirement to maximize expected utility, and can that premise be defended without assuming the norm it is supposed to establish?" You may refine or replace this formulation if a sharper one serves the project better.

2. Explain why the question is open. In particular, show how the standard arguments can be read as measurement or bookkeeping results rather than as direct vindications of the EU axioms as rational constraints, and indicate why the usual responses to this deflationary reading tend either to miss the point or to presuppose the conclusion.

3. Describe the approach you would take. Specify which candidate missing premises you would test, which axiom violations or classes of non-EU preferences you would use to probe whether coherence arguments can rule them out, and why this approach is more likely to make progress than simply rehearsing the familiar Dutch-book debate.

4. Say what the possible conclusions are and what would count as evidence for each. Also state plainly what would count as failure of the project—for example, discovering that the apparent gap is illusory because representability already carries normative content, or that the missing premise is defensible only at the cost of being too weak to do the intended work.

5. Give a realistic scope for one semester, distinguishing the essential components of the argument from those that could be deferred.

Do not include a bibliography or a citation-grounded literature review; refer to positions and arguments by description, since citations will be supplied later. The proposal should be written in American English.
