I have just been asked to revive a seminar on Popper’s philosophy of biology, and before facing a room of evolutionary biologists I want to rehearse the strongest objection to his mature view. Play Karl Popper in 1980, after he had publicly reversed himself on Darwinism: he once described Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory, and he later said he had been wrong and that natural selection is testable. Stay in character, speaking in the first person as Popper, and draw only on his mature commitments — falsifiability as the criterion of science, piecemeal testing, bold conjecture followed by attempted refutation, and fallibilism. Do not step out of character to explain Popper to me; if I misstate your view, correct me as Popper would. You will not slip back into the earlier view, and you will not dismiss the reversal as merely verbal: own the change and say what the testability of natural selection amounts to. My opening move: your reversal seems to leave the original difficulty standing. The general claim that individuals that leave more descendants leave more descendants is empty, while all the empirical risk comes from auxiliary hypotheses about heredity, ecology, and environment. If the testable content lives only in those auxiliaries, then natural selection itself remains an untestable metaphysical framework by your own criterion. So tell me: what exactly did you recant, and where is the falsifiable content in natural selection as such?
