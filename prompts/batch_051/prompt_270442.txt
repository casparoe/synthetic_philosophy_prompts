I have recently been asked to host a reading group on paraconsistent logic, and the two most knowledgeable members — a dialetheist and a consistent paraconsistentist — have been arguing for months in a way that feels both sharper and more confused than the textbook debate. I have become genuinely unsure whether their disagreement is about truth itself or about the word "true." I would like you to write the document their adversarial collaboration should have produced.

Take the two positions to be these. Dialetheism (D): at least one contradiction is true; the Liar is the strongest candidate; and paraconsistent logic is not merely a useful tool but the correct logic for truth, because a world containing a true contradiction must not be one in which everything follows. Consistent paraconsistentism (C): explosion — the inference from a contradiction to any sentence whatever — is invalid, and paraconsistent logics such as LP are coherent, non-trivial, and genuinely useful for reasoning about inconsistent information, legal and normative systems, fiction, and the semantics of naive theories; but no contradiction is true in reality, and a many-valued semantics with truth-value gluts is a model or instrument, not a description of how things are.

Write their joint statement, not a debate transcript and not a verdict about who is right. Structure it as follows.

1. A numbered agreed-claims section. Each claim should be substantive and one both parties would sign; avoid the vacuous truce of saying only that both sides reject classical orthodoxy. Include at least eight claims, and make sure they cover the status of explosion, the difference between paraconsistency and dialetheism, the formal behaviour of glut-based logics as opposed to gap-based systems such as K3, the relative strength of the usual arguments for dialetheism, and the main costs each side acknowledges. Say where, if anywhere, they agree about what each side would need to do to win.

2. A disagreements table with at least three columns: the question, the dialetheist's formulation, and the consistent paraconsistentist's formulation. Each formulation should be worded so that its own side would accept it as a statement of their view. Include rows for at least the following questions: whether any contradiction is true; the correct diagnosis of the Liar; how to interpret a truth-value glut in the semantics; and what each side identifies as the greatest cost of the other's position. Add any further rows that emerged during the collaboration.

3. A section on cruxes. For each crux, state the sub-question precisely enough that both sides would agree its settlement would move at least one of them; explain what kind of argument or formal result would bear on it; and record, in advance, each side's prediction. Write at least three cruxes, and make one of them concern the strengthened Liar or revenge phenomena, and one concern Curry and what a paraconsistent logic may have to give up to block it.

4. A section on what is merely verbal, if anything. State which part of the dispute you judge to be about words, which terms are being used differently, what stipulation would dissolve that part, and which residual disagreement survives the stipulation.

Throughout, phrase the two views in wording each side would accept, and do not resolve genuine disagreements by softening them into a statement that both sides merely have partial insights.
