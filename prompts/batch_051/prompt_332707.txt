I'm a backend engineer, so I'm going to reach for a systems metaphor. I've been kicking around a distinction I want checked by someone who actually knows philosophy. I call them axle beliefs and wheel beliefs. Wheel beliefs are the ordinary kind: they can be true or false, you can gather evidence for or against them, you can be wrong about them. Axle beliefs are the ones that don't turn — they're what the wheel beliefs spin on. Examples: the world didn't pop into existence five minutes ago, other people have inner lives, my memory is broadly working right now. I don't have evidence for these, and not because I haven't looked. If I try to defend 'the world didn't pop into existence five minutes ago,' I have to already rely on the clocks, the history books, the lab equipment — the very stuff that only exists on the assumption I'm trying to defend. So axle beliefs aren't just very-well-supported beliefs; they're closer to the environment my believing runs in, almost like an OS rather than an app. My friend who took a few philosophy classes says this sounds like hinge epistemology and something in Wittgenstein's On Certainty, but she couldn't tell me wether my version adds anything or just renames it. So I want an honest assessment: is this a real distinction or just vibes; does it already exist under a better name; what does it get right; and where exactly does it break? I'd especially like the strongest objection to it and whether that objection is fatal or just means I need to tighten the analogy.
