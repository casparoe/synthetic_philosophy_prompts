I am a novelist, and in the book I am writing a character defends concern for insect welfare by arguing that insects exist in numbers so vast that even a small chance that any given insect is sentient yields an enormous expected number of sentient lives, and therefore an enormous expected moral stake. I want the scene to be intellectually honest, but I cannot tell how much of this argument is testable. Could one in principle design an empirical test that would distinguish sentience in an insect from mere nociception, reflexes, or learning? Lay out the best such test: the organisms, measures, and design; which result would favor the sentience reading and which the deflationary reading; and why the two may remain hard to separate. Then say what a positive result would and would not settle, and whether the scale argument does any real work once the test is inconclusive or negative. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this area, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
