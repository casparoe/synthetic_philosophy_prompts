A book I am reading summarises the algebraic idea of well-definedness roughly as follows. The roots of a polynomial such as x³ − 2 are completely interchangeable: no purely algebraic condition singles out one of the three roots as the root, and anything said about a particular root is therefore meaningless unless it is invariant under all permutations of the roots. Most of algebra, the book says, is really the study of which choices are harmless in this sense. It then concludes that complex conjugation is not a genuine operation, because it depends on an arbitrary choice of which square root of −1 is called i.

I suspect this summary is broadly right but overstates something. How fair is it as an account of well-definedness and canonical choice? Go through it claim by claim: which parts would a careful algebraist or philosopher of mathematics accept, which are subtly misrepresented, and does the conclusion about complex conjugation actually follow from the accurate parts?
