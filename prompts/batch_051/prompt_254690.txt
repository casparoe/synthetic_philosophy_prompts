I am a senior and have been asked to give the five-minute student address at our high school's closing ceremony, to an audience of classmates, parents, and teachers. Over the past two years I have arrived, with some reluctance, at two convictions. First, I no longer believe that human life has a meaning or purpose built into it, independently of the purposes we choose to invest in it. Second, I no longer believe that anything is right or wrong apart from human attitudes, practices, and commitments; I do not think there is a moral order out there for a life to answer to. I also find I cannot treat groups such as our class, our school, or our generation as real things over and above the individual people who make them up, useful as that way of talking is. I do not plan to argue for any of this from the podium, but I refuse to say anything I now take to be false, and that rules out most graduation-speech material about each of us having a calling, owing something to the world, or being part of a larger whole. Please write the speech. It should leave the audience encouraged and moved, and it should show by example that a person can hold these beliefs and still take a graduation seriously, without pretending they are false.
