I have a rule about existential conclusions: I don't let myself decide that life is meaningless when I'm in a bad stretch, like after a breakup or a month of bad sleep. In those states the absurdist case feels airtight—the universe is silent, my projects look like theater, and it seems like I'm finally seeing straight. So I only update my views about whether anything matters when I'm feeling basically okay. But that policy cuts both ways: maybe the low mood is the one seeing clearly, and feeling okay is just better at self-deception. Is this habit rationally defensible, or am I just filtering out conclusions I dislike? If some cleaned-up version is defensible, what would it have to add?
