So I'm a journalist who covers the physics-and-philosophy beat, and next month I've agreed to give a talk to a philosophy of physics reading group, and I'm already dreading the Q&A. My thesis is that effective field theory gives a worked-out example of what I want to call the "autonomy of scales": the idea that higher-energy physics is not just practically seperated from low-energy physics but is systematically screened off from it. The spine of the talk will be the Wilsonian picture — irrelevant couplings suppressed by powers of the scale ratio, many distinct UV completions flowing to the same infrared fixed point — and I'll use the universality of critical exponents (magnets, water near its critical point, that sort of thing) to argue that details near the Planck scale can't show up in the low-energy theory even in principle, not merely in practice. I want to pitch this as a middle position between hard reductionism and mushy "strong emergence" talk, and I'll end with the mildly provocative line that in a real sense the low-energy theory is more robust than whatever UV completion sits underneath it.

The audience is the scary part. Two regulars are ex-high-energy physicists who now do phil phys and treat the word "emergence" like a tax audit; they'll likely say the autonomy I'm pointing at is just epistemic coarseness, or that the Wilsonian story already assumes a reductionist hierarchy because you write down the full theory and integrate out the high modes. Another is a philosopher of chemistry who thinks phil phys fetishizes QFT and will probably say chemists knew about quasi-autonomous levels long before Wilson. The rest are sharp phil sci grad students with no physics training who will pounce on any undefined use of "explain" or "fundamental." Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I should expect, in roughly the order they'll actually come, with a sense of how damaging each one is and which of these people it comes from, phrased the way they'd put it. For each, give me a two-minute reply, a note on where that reply is softest, and a flag for what I should concede up front versus hold my ground on. And include the question I should be praying nobody asks.
