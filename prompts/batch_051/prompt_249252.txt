I have been thinking about Hofstadter’s notion of a tangled hierarchy, exemplified by Escher’s Drawing Hands, and about his idea that such a loop needs an inviolate level—some underlying medium, hardware, or set of rules that the loop can use, refer to, or depend on, but cannot rewrite from within. I want to think through what happens when one tries to build an institution around this structure deliberately, with a normative inviolate level rather than a physical one.

Consider the following governance proposal for a new interdisciplinary research institute. The Director appoints the members of a Research Council. The Research Council sets the institute’s research agenda and writes the annual evaluation criteria for research fellows. The fellows’ continued funding depends on satisfying those criteria. The research fellows are initially selected by the Director, and they in turn vote each year on whether to reappoint the Director. The loop, then, is meant to run: Director appoints Council, Council constrains and evaluates fellows, fellows decide Director’s fate. The proposed inviolate level is a Charter, ratified by an outside foundation, that fixes the institute’s mission, the fields eligible for funding, and the basic decision procedure. No internal body may amend the Charter, and the Charter is supposed to supply the stable surface on which the rest of the loop is drawn.

Walk through how this institution would actually behave once real strategic actors are inside it. Where does effective power settle? What are the first ways an ambitious Director, an ambitious Council, and an ambitious bloc of fellows would each try to game the loop, and which rule is likely to be tested first? In particular, which parts of the inviolate level are genuinely inviolate, and which parts would turn out to be effectively revisable because someone inside the loop controls interpretation of the Charter? Finally, say whether the institution’s likely failure modes could be repaired by adding a second inviolate level, or whether that would simply push the tangle upward and leave the original problem intact.
