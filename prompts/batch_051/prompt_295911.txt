Physics teaching does a weird thing: you learn the wavefunction as this smooth, deterministic object that obeys the Schrödinger equation and seems to be spread out across space like a real field, and then as soon as a measurement comes up your told to drop all that and just use the squared amplitudes as probabilities—as if the wavefunction was only a ledger of your ignorance about what you'll see. What does standard textbook quantum mechanics actually presuppose about whether the wavefunction is a real thing out there or just a tool for predicting measurement outcomes? Reconstruct the implicit view, and say whether the practice really needs it or is compatible with several positions (psi-ontic, psi-epistemic, or something more deflationary). Also: is there a quiet tension between the deterministic, object-y feel of the dynamics part and the epistemic, ignorance-tracking feel of the measurement rule, and would a textbook written by a hardcore psi-ontic person versus a hardcore psi-epistemic person be forced to change anything?
