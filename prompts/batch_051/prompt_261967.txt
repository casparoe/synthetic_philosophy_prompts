I've been reading some stuff on the old Enlightenment idea that commerce softens manners, and I find the systematic version of it almost too good. The mechanism seems clean: trade rewards you for understanding what the other side wants and for being someone they will deal with again, so even if your motives stay selfish, your character gets sanded down into something more sociable. Repeat dealing and reputation do most of the moral work without anyone having to be good. The theory also explains a lot at scale: stranger-heavy commercial societies somehow pull off large-scale low-trust cooperation, and the historical story that commerce crowded out honor culture and violence feels about right.

But I have a stuck intuition that points the other way for a certain class of things. Not that markets are generally corrupting, more like the civilizing effect has a boundary where it flips sign. Paying people for votes or kidneys or queue-jumping doesn't just leave the practice unchanged but paid; it changes what the act is, what it expresses, and what kind of person is produced. And the subtler version of the same worry: adding cash for blood donation or for students reading books seems to replace an internal doing it because it's good with an external what am I getting for this, and that can degrade the exact motivation you were trying to harness. That looks like a real corruption of character, not just a rude price tag.

I don't want a reading list or a referee that picks a side. I want a negotiated verdict. Present the strongest form of the civilizing-commerce story and the strongest form of the corruption intuitions, and then tell me: what would have to be true for the corruption cases to be debunked and folded into the broader theory, say as expressive or framing effects, without gerrymanderd excuses? What would have to be added to the theory for it to predict, in a principled way, where commercialization corrupts and where it civilizes? And is there a stable reading on which both the civilizing mechanism and the corruption boundary are true at once?
