Section 5(c) of the Sandpiper Bay Condominium Association's parking rules reads: "Only red motor vehicles may be parked in the Glenview visitor lot." The rule was adopted decades ago to keep the lot visually uniform; the minutes say nothing about borderline shades. An owner has begun parking a car whose paint is a shade most residents cannot place — in morning light most call it a dark orange, in evening light most call it a light red, and at the board hearing the residents split roughly evenly, while the manufacturer's paint code lists it as a red-orange. Analyze whether the owner is in breach of the rule. Work through what the bare words of the rule, the purpose of the color restriction, what the drafters would plausibly have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would take "red" to cover each suggest. Then set those interpretive approaches against the semantics: explain how an epistemicist and a supervaluationist about vagueness would each assess whether there is a determinate fact of the matter whether this car is red, and what follows for whether the association has a determinate obligation to ticket the car. Which approach, if any, should govern the board's decision?
