Our film club is doing The Truman Show next week and I've been down a rabbit hole about the main character thing — the idea that everyone else might be an NPC, just sort of a backdrop, and maybe they don't have an inner life the way I do. Truman feels like the obvious case because he's literally the only one not following a script, and everyone around him is performing. But the more I think about it the movie seems to push the other way: the actors clearly have their own off-screen lives and real feelings (Sylvia gets pulled off the show because she actually cares about him, for one), and the audience outside is rooting for Truman, so the whole world is treating him as the one real center too — which is kind of the NPC fantasy, just coming from outside instead of inside. Thats the thing I want to work out.

Give me a real reading of the film through that lens. What does it actually show about the intuition that other people aren't really there the way I am, and where does the solipsism-ish reading just not fit the film — where would an honest critic have to say the movie isnt actually about that at all. Also, the hidden-camera thing bothers me: the movie hands us a god's-eye view of everyone's heads, which no actual solipsist could ever get, so is the film stacking the deck, or is that exactly its point? And if the movie is making a philosophical claim by doing that, say what it adds that just arguing about other minds in the abstract doesn't get you.
