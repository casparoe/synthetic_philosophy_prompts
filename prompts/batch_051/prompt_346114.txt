My friend keeps saying something I can't shake. Flip a fair coin ten times and get HHHHHHHHHH, and nobody calls the result random; get a scattered string like HTTHHTHTTH, and everybody does. But the two sequences are exactly as probable as each other—they're just particular outputs of the same fair process. We'd also be much quicker to accuse someone of faking the first than the second. Is there a real distinction there that survives the equal probabilities, maybe that the first is generated by a much shorter rule then the second? Or is "this finite sequence is random" confused talk, so that only the flipping process can be random? If you defend the distinction, tell me what a fraud detector could be flagging other than bare probability, and where you'd draw the line.
