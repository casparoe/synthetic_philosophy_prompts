Write a dialogue between two philosophers of biology who disagree about whether DNA is literally a code — or, more precisely, about whether genes carry information in a semantic, non-metaphorical sense. One of them, the semanticist, holds that a genome encodes instructions for building an organism: the sequence is arbitrary relative to what it specifies, is read by the cell, and can in principle be read incorrectly. The other, the causalist, holds that "genetic code" and "genetic information" are metaphors, and that everything the genome does is exhausted by ordinary causal and biochemical relations; on this view a cell has no sender, receiver, or shared convention, and what looks like a "misreading" is just a causal failure with no semantic content.

The dialogue should be a genuine exchange, not alternating monologues. Each speaker should respond to the other's strongest points, and neither should be a strawman: the semanticist should concede the force of the point that transcription and translation are chemical processes, and the causalist should concede that the information idiom does real organizational work.

The two should work through at least the following considerations:

- the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary in something like the way a symbol's shape is arbitrary: the same amino acid could have been assigned to another codon, and in some organisms and organelles it has been;
- a DNA sequence can be parsed in different reading frames and from either strand, yielding entirely different products, and in eukaryotes a single transcript can be spliced in multiple ways, so that what a sequence "says" depends on machinery external to the sequence;
- whether a genome can misrepresent or be in error, and whether the possibility of error is required for something to count as semantic information;
- the difference between a code in the sense of a cipher or language, which presupposes encoding and decoding conventions, and a code in the sense of a systematic causal mapping;
- whether the cell is usefully, or literally, described as an interpreter of the genome, and what would have to be true for that description to be more than a figure of speech.

Let the characters themselves raise the question whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Have them try to state each other's position in terms the other would accept, and let that attempt move at least one of them, at least slightly.
