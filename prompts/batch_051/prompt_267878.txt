I work in maintenance at a water treatment plant, and the alarm system is always throwing flags. What's been bugging me is that two alarms can look near identical — same pump, same trend shape, same time after a shift change — but one turns out to be a blocked filter and the other is a worn impeller, or even something else in the building vibrating through the floor. Then other alarms look completly different but the fix is the same. The senior techs say "same root cause" and "same failure mode" like those are one idea, but I'm convinced they're seperate things.

So here's what I want to know. How would you actually test whether two of these recurring alarms are "the same mechanism" rather than two different mechanisms that just make the sensors twitch the same way? Pretend I can do real things on site: swap a part, force a condition, run machines one at a time, pull old logs. Give me a concrete test setup, the result that would push toward "same mechanism," the result that would push toward "different mechanisms that happen to look alike," and then be honest about what part of the question no test on the plant floor could settle.

Before you design it, I think the tricky bit is that nobody here knows what "same mechanism" should mean. Is it the same physical part failing, the same chain of events, the same fix that stops it, or the same upstream thing you'd have to change to prevent it? Those give different answers. And if two anomalies always come and go together when we poke the system, is that enough to call them one mechanism, or is that just a shared cause and not really a mechanism at all? I keep feeling like whether we count two failures as one mechanism changes depending on how lump-y we decide to be — one thing for the maintenance crew, two things for the pump engineers — and I can't tell if that means the question is vague or just that the plant data can't decide it. So tell me which of those it is, too.
