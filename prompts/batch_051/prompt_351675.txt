I'm on the grants commitee of the Orben Foundation, a small funder that supports legal aid groups, independent media, and civic organizations. We keep two funding tracks. The normal track has standard reporting and annual renewals. The backsliding track kicks in when we classify a country as undergoing democratic backsliding: it unlocks fast, flexible money for litigation, security, and emergency relocation, but it also puts the country on a public list. Governments have retaliated against that list before, including expelling grantees.

The country currently under review is Velsonia, where we've funded normal-track grantees for years. Over the last twenty months the governing party, which won a free election and holds a narrow majority, has passed a series of changes. It moved the election commission into the Interior Ministry and let the minister appoint five of nine commissioners, where previously appointments required cross-party agreement. It shifted district-boundary redrawing from an independent commission to parliament, effective before the next election. It replaced the public broadcaster's board with appointees of the culture minister. It created a Media Ombudsman under the justice ministry who can fine outlets up to two percent of annual revenue for what the ombudsman decides is false or misleading reporting, with appeal only after the fine. And it passed a judicial efficiency law letting the justice minister open disciplinary cases against judges for a vague standard about undermining public confidence in the judiciary, with a new disciplinary chamber appointed by parliament.

The government's defense is that it won a free election, the constitutional court is still independant and has already struck down part of the election-commission change, no journalist or opposition politician has been jailed, and the media fines haven't actually been used yet. They say this is ordinary winner-take-all politics after years of cartel politics, and that our backsliding category is just a partisan label foreign donors use against governments they don't like. Some members of our committee agree and want to scrap the classification entirely, saying the term has become a partisan label—just look at how it's thrown around in US politics. Others point to Hungary and Poland and say administrative capture of exactly this kind is how the erosion starts, and we'd be naive to wait until the jailing begins.

I need you to draft the memo for our next board meeting. It should do three things: propose criteria for what counts as democratic backsliding rather than ordinary constitutional hardball or policies we simply oppose; apply those criteria to Velsonia and recommend whether to move it to the backsliding track, scrap the label altogether, or do something in between; and deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side. Make it a document the board can actually vote on. Please write the memo in American English.
