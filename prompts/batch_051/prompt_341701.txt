I cover science and ideas for a living, and for a story I've been talking to a company that does whole-brain emulation: they scan someone's brain and build a simulation their family can keep talking to. They've offered to let me do a demo with my father, who has mid-stage Alzheimer's, scanned from it's current brain rather than some earlier, healthier version. I said yes mostly because it'd make a hell of a lede, but now I'm honestly stuck. If I call the thing 'him,' am I talking to my dad or just a very good copy? The company's line is that the person just is the pattern—the memories, quirks, conversational tics—so running that pattern on their servers is still him. My gut says that's too easy, but I can't say exactly why. And I can't even decide whether faithfulness helps: if the scan is scrupulously accurate to the damaged brain, it's more 'him' in one way but also more a record of the disease talking. If they clean it up, it's more like the dad I remember but less like the man he actually is now. What I want from you is advice for my piece: should I write about the emulation as him, as a copy, or as something in between? Lead with the bottom line, then give me the strongest argument on each side, and tell me which one a careful reporter ought to commit to in print. Keep it under 400 words.
