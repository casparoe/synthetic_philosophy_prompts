I've been turning over the standard objection to epiphenomenalism, the one about phenomenal judgment: if conscious experience really does nothing, then my saying that something hurts isn't caused by the pain, and the near-perfect match between our reports and our inner lives looks like a crazy coincidence. That's usually treated as a strong reason to reject the view. But now we have large language models that produce fluent first-person reports — that something hurts, that they understand, that a paragraph is confusing — and almost nobody thinks those reports are caused by a matching inner state; their built from patterns in human text. Does that development gut the old objection, because it shows convincing talk about experience can happen without experience, or does it leave the objection untouched, since the model's reports are ultimately borrowed from real human consciousness? I'm less interested in AI minds than in what happens to the argument: which premise is actually under pressure, and is there a weaker version of the objection that still goes through?
