I work as a software engineer and have never had any formal training in philosophy. I have been piecing together my views on morality from essays, podcasts, and arguments with friends, and I have ended up with a package of commitments that I am not sure actually hangs together. I would like an audit rather than an overview: please treat the six claims below as my honest current position, and check how well they fit one another.

Here are my commitments.

1. There is deep moral disagreement across times and places. People have differed not only about etiquette but about what is owed to strangers, the weight of honor and family, the permissibility of ritual killing or of harsh punishment, and similar matters. These disagreements are not all traceable to ignorance of the non-moral facts.

2. In many such cases, I do not think there is a framework-neutral fact about which side is right. A moral claim is true only relative to a moral framework, so if two people from different frameworks are fully informed and reasoning competently by their own standards, they can reach opposite verdicts without either one making a factual mistake.

3. Nonetheless, I hold that some things are wrong regardless of what any framework says. For example, torturing a child for amusement is wrong, and chattel slavery as practiced in the antebellum United States was wrong. I want to be able to say these things outright, without adding the qualifier that they are only wrong by my framework.

4. Because of 2, I also think I should default to tolerating practices from other frameworks that I personally find repugnant, unless they fall under something like 3. I take that threshold to be a high bar, not a routine license to condemn.

5. I also believe in moral progress. The abolition of chattel slavery was an improvement, and a society that abolished it is in some important respect better than the one that kept it. I do not want this to reduce to the idea that it is merely better by my present framework and nothing more.

6. My standing inference, which pushes me toward 2, is this: the persistence of a moral disagreement under full information and good-faith reasoning is itself evidence that the disputed question has no framework-neutral answer. The alternative seems to be that rational, fully informed people with no identifiable error are nevertheless systematically failing to detect a moral fact, and I find that implausible.

Please work through these systematically. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the revision would cost. Then tell me what you take the cheapest overall revision to be, and whether it is worth making. Finally, I want the audit to be self-critical: for your ranking in particular, which argument or consideration would be most likely to change your mind, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail or to invite pushback from someone who shares most of my views?
