i've got a standing rule for skeptical challenges. when someone says i can't be sure i'm not a brain in a vat, or that the dog i see in the yard could be a perfect robot, i don't take that as a reason to stop saying i know the dog is there. the rule is: a doubt only counts against my knowledge if it's a possibility my belief had to be sensitive to in my actual circumstances, and far-off alternatives that change nothing about what i expect or do don't get a vote. so i'll happily say i know the dog's in the yard while also denying that i know i'm not a brain in a vat—which means i've given up closure under known entailment. is this a principled line on skepticism, or just a way of making sure no skeptical argument can ever land? and if it could be either, is there any way from inside my own skull to tell which one i'm doing?
