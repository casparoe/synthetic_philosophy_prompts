Write a villanelle from the point of view of Maxwell's demon, the little doorkeeper who lets fast molecules pass one way and slow ones the other, so a temperature difference opens up between the chambers, apparently without any work being done. Make the two refrains carry the two halves of the physics: the demon's boast that it is getting order out of randomness for nothing, and the hidden cost that every measurement it writtes down must eventually be erased, at which point the entropy gets paid back. The repetition has to do structural work, not just be decorative: the refrains should keep coming back the way a full cycle of the demon keeps coming back to where it started, the entropy decrease from sorting always bought back by the entropy increase from erasing the memory (Landauer's principle, Bennett's resolution). Don't just name-drop. A physicist who knows the thermodynamics of information should recognize why the second law survives and why information is physical; a reader who doesn't should still get a good poem about a tiny functionary who can't make the accounts come out ahead.
