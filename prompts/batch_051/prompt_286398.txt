I'm a novelist putting together a collection of philosophical short stories, and my current one is meant to be about the paradox of hedonism. You're a professional philosopher who also reads fiction with a critical eye—so think carefully and dont just hand me the standard cautionary tale. Start by brainstorming two or three ways the underlying mechanism could play out in a single person's life, then pick the subtlest one and write a short story around 1,500 words that embodies it.

A few constraints: avoid the usual rich-debauchee-burns-out arc, and no character should ever name the paradox or explain it in dialogue. The protagonist should be someone who earnestly and competently organizes their life around maximizing pleasure—maybe they quantify it, schedule it, optimize their routines—and the paradox should emerge from the plot itself: the direct pursuit of pleasure as a goal quietly drains the felt pleasure away, while the moments that genuinely feel good arrive as side effects of being absorbed in something else. Keep the tone restrained rather than preachy, and end somewhere honest, not with a tidy moral. After the story, add a brief note on which specific choice in the story is doing the philosophical work and what a committed hedonist might say in their own defense.
