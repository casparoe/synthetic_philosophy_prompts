Construct a thought experiment — a small toy possible world — that isolates the modal difference between a Humean best-system account of laws of nature and an anti-Humean governing account of laws (either a nomic-necessitation view or a dispositional-essentialist view, whichever you choose), at exactly one point: an interaction-type that never actually occurs anywhere in the history of the world.

The case is for a metaphysics-course handout whose lesson is that the real dispute between these two views is not about what the actual world is like, but about how far lawfulness reaches beyond the actual. That purpose fixes what counts as cheating: the two verdicts must fall out of the two accounts as applied to the fully described world, not be written into the description as conclusions.

Specifications, all of which the case must satisfy:

1. The world is closed and finite, with a small stock of fundamental entities — point particles, fields, charges, or something similarly austere — and their actual distribution across all of spacetime is fully specified. There are no observers, no epistemic limitations, and no gaps in the actual mosaic (the totality of non-modal particular facts). Do not rely on actual-world physics.

2. The two accounts agree on the complete actual history, and agree on the content of every law governing an actually instantiated interaction-type. The disagreement must be invisible if one looks only at what actually happens.

3. The world contains exactly one kind of interaction that never occurs in the actual history, for a structural reason visible in the mosaic — for example, the two relevant kinds are permanently confined to different sectors of the world. The non-occurrence should be robust rather than a statistical shortfall, yet consistent with the interaction's being possible in principle.

4. For that uninstantiated interaction, the two accounts must deliver opposite verdicts. The anti-Humean says there is a determinate fact about what would happen if the interaction occurred — state the outcome — and traces that fact to the governing structure of the world (for example, the relevant universals stand in a necessitation relation, or the natures of the properties fix the interaction). The Humean says there is no fact of the matter, because the best system over the actual mosaic contains no theorem for this interaction-type, and adding one would add complexity without adding strength.

5. The anti-Humean's determinate outcome must be generated by the anti-Humean metaphysics as applied to the world, and the Humean's silence must be a principled consequence of best-system reasoning over a complete mosaic — not a product of stipulation or underdescription. Make the best system unique so that tie-breaking by convention cannot be invoked.

Then deliver the following. State the actual history and the one uninstantiated interaction-type. State the laws both sides accept and briefly show why each side accepts them. Walk through, step by step, how the anti-Humean derives the determinate outcome while the Humean's best system remains silent, making explicit the standards each theory uses. Identify the single structural feature of the case that produces the divergence; say what happens if that feature is removed (for instance, if the interaction occurs even once, or the separation is weakened) and whether the two views collapse back into agreement. Finally, give the strongest reply each side can make against the other's use of the case, without declaring a winner.
