Write a decision tree for a reading group on anthropic reasoning that sorts a participant into one of the positions in the self-sampling/self-indication debate without ever asking them to name the view they hold. The tree should be usable by someone who has met the Doomsday argument and the Presumptuous Philosopher informally but has never worked through the formal statements of the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from the list of position names. First fix the forcing questions and the cells of yes/no answers they define; only then locate the named positions in that space. Make every branch a concrete case the participant can answer without knowing which view it leads to. Use at least these three cases:

- A doomsday urn: an agent knows their absolute birth rank (roughly the 60-billionth human) and knows a fair coin determined whether the total human population will be one hundred billion or one hundred trillion; the branch question is whether that birth-rank information should move the agent's credence toward the smaller total.
- The Presumptuous Philosopher: two theories make identical predictions about everything observable and are both consistent with the agent's existence, but one predicts a billion observers and the other a trillion; the branch question is whether the agent's own existence licenses concluding that the trillion-observer theory is far more likely.
- God's coin toss: a fair coin creates one observer on heads and two observers, in separate rooms ignorant of each other, on tails; the agent wakes up as one of them; the branch question is what credence they assign to heads.

The key should map each combination of answers to a diagnostic position. The leaves should include at least: one position that accepts the Doomsday-style update and rejects the Presumptuous Philosopher-style update; one that rejects the first and accepts the second; one that accepts both; and, handled as a family rather than a single leaf, the positions that reject both. Inside the reject-both family, try to separate a self-locating-probability skepticism (the indices have no determinate value until a reference class is stipulated) from an indexical-non-updating view (there is a fact of the matter, but indexical information should not shift an agent), and say explicitly whether the three cases can do that or whether that separation must be left unresolved.

Also test one specific near-equivalence: can the tree actually separate the self-indication assumption from a variant of the self-sampling assumption whose reference class is broadened to include merely possible observers? If the three cases cannot separate them, say so rather than inventing a leaf that does no work, and state what additional case would help, if any.

Attend to the empty or unstable cells of the answer space. For each, say whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, coherent but unattractive to its potential defenders, or merely unexplored, and identify what additional question would place someone there. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and either supply a further case that could separate them or admit that you cannot.

Cap the instrument at four branch questions and eight leaves, and do not ask for or mention any bibliography or citation.

In your answer, state how confident you are in the branch-to-position assignments and in the claimed inseparabilities, and single out the point you are least sure about.
