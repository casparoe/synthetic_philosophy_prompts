I have been trying to decide whether there is a structural parallel between Augustine's account of inherited guilt and the way software engineers talk about inheriting legacy code. In On Merit and the Forgiveness of Sins, and the Baptism of Infants, Augustine writes: "Concupiscence, therefore, as the law of sin which remains in the members of this body of death, is born with infants." The theological idea is that a morally relevant defect is present from birth, not acquired through one's own acts, yet it counts against the person and requires a remedy. In software, developers who take over a legacy codebase likewise inherit defects and hidden constraints from decisions they never made; the system's faults are there from the project's beginning, but the current team is the one that must answer for them in maintenance and reliability. Is this parallel deep or superficial? In particular, does the engineering case contain anything analogous to guilt, or only inherited causal burden? And does Augustine's account require a voluntary source for inherited guilt that the codebase case lacks?
