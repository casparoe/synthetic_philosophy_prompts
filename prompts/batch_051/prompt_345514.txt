I have been developing a position I call cohort realism. My claim is that generational categories such as Baby Boomers, Millennials, and Generation Z are not merely arbitrary labels or marketing inventions; they pick out real social kinds. Members of the same cohort pass through the same formative events—an economic crash, a pandemic, the spread of a new technology—at roughly the same stage of life, and this shared timing produces real though loose commonalities in outlook and values. I also want to say that the boundaries between cohorts are real but fuzzy, more like the boundaries between biological populations than like the lines between states on a map. I know many people argue that generations are entirely constructed and that the labels mostly serve to sell things or to excuse stereotyping. Is my view coherent? Has someone defended something like it? And where does it break? In particular, does it wrongly treat a wealthy and a poor member of the same generation as more alike than two people of similar class born a few decades apart? Are the fuzzy boundaries a fatal problem or an acceptable cost?
