You are grading a midterm in an early modern philosophy course. The exam question is:

“Here is a passage from John Locke, An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Book II, Chapter XXVII: ‘In the state of living creatures, their identity depends not on a mass of the same particles, but on something else.’ In your answer, (i) explain what the ‘something else’ is in the case of persons, and why Locke thinks personal identity cannot consist in sameness of soul or body; (ii) formulate Reid’s brave officer objection and identify the premise of Locke’s view it threatens; (iii) evaluate one possible Lockean response without abandoning the consciousness criterion.”

Student answer:
“Locke is making the point that identity for an organism is not about having the same material particles; an oak or horse can remain the same oak or horse while its matter changes. For persons the ‘something else’ is consciousness. On Locke’s view, a person at one time is the same person as a being at an earlier time just in case their present consciousness can reach back and appropriate that earlier being’s actions or thoughts as their own. This is why sameness of substance is neither necessary nor sufficient: a soul with no consciousness of its earlier states would not be the same person, and two substances sharing one consciousness would be. Reid’s brave officer objection is that this criterion makes personal identity non-transitive. The boy steals apples; the officer remembers the boy’s theft; the general remembers the officer’s campaigns but not the boy’s theft. Locke then has to say that the general is the same person as the officer, and the officer is the same person as the boy, but the general is not the same person as the boy, because the general’s consciousness cannot reach the boy. That is a contradiction. Locke could escape by saying that personal identity is not strict numerical identity but only a practical, forensic relation for attributing responsibility, and that a chain of consciousness through the officer is enough for the general to inherit the boy’s act.”

Grade this answer. Assign a letter grade and provide point-by-point comments tied to the three parts of the question. Identify any subtle errors or conflations, and explain what would need to be corrected for the answer to receive full credit.
