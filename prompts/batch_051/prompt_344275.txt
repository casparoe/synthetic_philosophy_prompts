I'm a high school student, and I recently got into a long argument with my friends about Santa Claus and the tooth fairy — specifically about whether parents are doing something dishonest when they keep those stories going, and why almost everyone treats it as completely fine while also telling children that lying is wrong. The more we talked, the more it seemed like there must be a whole underlying picture of what children are, what parents owe them, and what trust requires sitting underneath a practice that nobody ever states out loud.

I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher working on childhood, trust, and parental authority, and to think very carefully before answering. First, brainstorm the candidate theories that could rationalize this practice, then narrow them down and argue for the most defensible reconstruction.

Here is the practice I want you to analyze. In many families, adults deliberately build an elaborate fiction around Santa Claus or the tooth fairy: they write return letters, leave half-eaten cookies, label gifts as coming from someone who does not exist, and sometimes use the figure's surveillance as a behavior-management tool, with presents or payment made conditional on the child being good. They also typically teach children, in those same years, that lying and deceiving are wrong. At some point the fiction is dismantled, often gradually, and the child is expected to accept the revelation without concluding that the parents are untrustworthy — and many children do. My question is: what philosophical commitments does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of childhood, parental authority, epistemic respect, and trust that would make it coherent. Then distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is underdetermined, meaning that several different theories would license exactly the same behavior. In particular, can the practice be consistent with also punishing children for lying, and if so, under what distinction between the parent's lie and the child's? Finally, describe what child-rearing practices would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced with one that treats children as full epistemic equals from the start.
