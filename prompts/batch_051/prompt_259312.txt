For the Liberal Neutrality and Its Critics seminar, I have been asked to draft the document for an adversarial collaboration between two of my classmates: a Rawlsian liberal and a communitarian in the Sandel–Taylor tradition. They have spent the term arguing about the priority of the right over the good and have mostly talked past one another. Please write the joint statement they could both sign, as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be non-vacuous and should include what both sides accept about the social preconditions of autonomy and about the state's duty to justify coercion to citizens. In the disagreements table, phrase each side's view in wording its own defenders would accept, and mark any disagreements that are merely verbal rather than substantive. Then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether unchosen memberships such as family, language, or nation can ground obligations not traceable to consent or to universal moral duty; another is whether the state may promote a substantive conception of the good when doing so also shapes the very selves who will later evaluate that promotion. For each crux, give a concrete case or test both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If the two positions cannot be rendered into a jointly signable statement without distortion, say so plainly rather than forcing a consensus.
