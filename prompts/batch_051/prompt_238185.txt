Give the most important implications of Gödel's rotating universe solution for how we should think about time's passage. In that solution, matter as a whole is rotating and the spacetime contains closed timelike curves, so a traveler could in principle loop back into their own past. There is no single cosmic time slicing the whole thing into a shared past, present, and future. Some philosophers read this as refuting the A-theory of time, and Gödel himself took it to support the view that time is ideal. I'm less interested in whether the actual universe is like this, and more in what the mere possibility of such a world does to people who thinks there's a real, universe-wide now. Please give the most important implications, and be precise about which ones actually hit the A-theorist and which only hit someone committed to a global present.
