Origin essentialism is the view that a person could not have originated from a different sperm or egg than the ones from which she actually developed. The standard argument for it runs roughly as follows: had a different sperm from your father fertilized the egg from which you came, the resulting zygote would have carried a different complement of paternal chromosomes, and the person who grew from it would have been your sibling rather than you. The same goes, mutatis mutandis, for a different egg from your mother. On this picture, the intuitive force of the view comes from the thought that the alternative gametes would have produced a genetically distinct human being.

But the standard argument runs together two features that can in principle come apart: the numerical identity of the gamete cells and the DNA they carry. Consider two cases designed to separate them. In Case 1, the "alternative" sperm is a numerically distinct cell from the actual one but, by stipulation, carries exactly the same nuclear DNA sequence as the actual sperm; since the egg is held fixed, the resulting child has the same nuclear genome as you. In Case 2, the "alternative" egg is numerically distinct but contributes the same nuclear DNA as the actual egg and differs only in its mitochondrial DNA, so the resulting child is again nearly indistinguishable from you — same nuclear genome, different mitochondria, which the embryo inherits only from the egg. Set aside the biological improbability of these cases; they are stipulated to isolate the conceptual question.

Work through what origin essentialism should say about each case. Does the standard analysis survive these variations intact, or does it need to be revised? Specifically, does the essentialist still classify these as worlds in which you would not have existed? If so, identify the principle that yields that result, and say whether that principle still has the intuitive support that the sibling argument originally supplied. If instead the essentialist should concede that you could have come from the different gamete in one or both cases, explain exactly where and why origin essentialism draws the line, and assess whether the resulting position is independently defensible once it is separated from the sibling intuition.
