I have recently joined a pandemic-preparedness review body in my county, and a recurring debate among members has stalled in a way I find disturbing: everyone on the panel accepts that the lockdowns and border restrictions introduced during the last emergency were lawful under the emergency statutes; the dispute is over whether that lawfulness settles their legitimacy. One side treats legality as almost dispositive: if the measures passed through the constitutionally prescribed mechanism and survived review, objections to them are political, not principled. The other side keeps invoking a Lockean picture: in the Second Treatise, Chapter XIV, sections 159 to 160, Locke describes prerogative as a discretionary power to act for the public good outside the letter of the law, and sometimes against it; on this view, legality and legitimacy can come apart precisely because the executive may act without the law and yet be justified, or act within the law and yet betray its trust. The same people then divide again on whether my recent reading of Locke actually helps: one faction says it reframes the issue as a question of whether the executive is answerable to the ends of government; the other says it is irrelevant because Locke is describing a pre-modern monarchical power and we are dealing with administrative law. The argument has not moved in months, and my working hypothesis is that the two sides are not really having one dispute: one is arguing about the grounds of political obligation under emergency, while the other is arguing about the proper scope of judicial review. I want a diagnosis, not a survey.

Please organise your answer in the following sections, with approximate lengths:

1. The positions restated (about 250 words): restate the two positions at their strongest, as each side would recognise itself.
2. Diagnosis (about 500 words): say what kind of disagreement this is—a substantive normative disagreement, a verbal disagreement, a hidden empirical or institutional disagreement, or two different questions under one heading—and argue for your diagnosis using what each side actually says.
3. What would count as progress (about 250 words): given your diagnosis, specify what would actually move the debate, whether a distinction, a legal decision, an empirical finding, or a change of question.
4. Bottom line (about 100 words): tell me honestly whether your diagnosis makes the impasse tractable or whether I should stop expecting resolution.

Please write in British English.
