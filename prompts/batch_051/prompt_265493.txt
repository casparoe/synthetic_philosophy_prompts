I'm writing a short essay for a philosophy of physics seminar on effective field theories and the autonomy of scales, and I wanted some comments before I polish it. The project is basically to split the autonomy claim in two and argue that one half is doing a lot more work than people admit.

Here's the rough shape. In an EFT you can describe physics at some low energy scale without knowing the full theory at higher energies, because as you flow to low energies the irrelevant operators get suppressed by powers of the ratio of scales and the heavy stuff just shows up as shifts in a finite list of couplings. Call that 'decoupling autonomy.' The low-energy theory still has to take those couplings from measurement, though — the numeric values are partly set by whatever is going on up above. So the form of the low-energy laws is stable, but I wouldn't call the low-energy facts ontically independent of the high-energy theory; you've just packaged the dependence into a handful of parameters you haven't explained.

Then there's the thing that happens near a second-order phase transition: different microscopic theories with completely different building blocks flow to the same fixed point and you get the same critical exponents. Call that 'universality autonomy.' Here it's not just that the details are hidden in parameters — a whole family of microtheories gives literally the same quantitative behavior, fixed by the fixed point itself.

My claim is that most loose talk about the autonomy of scales smuggles the force of the second case into the first. If all you have is decoupling autonomy, then autonomy is a genuinely epistemic, practical matter; its about what you can ignore and what you have to import. Only universality autonomy supports the stronger idea that the low-energy facts are independent of the high-energy physics in some non-trivial sense. And I want to end by saying that the stronger idea is actually pretty rare outside of critical phenomena, so if that's the kind of autonomy someone wants, they should be honest about how special the situation is.
