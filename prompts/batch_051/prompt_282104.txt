Imagine that in 2030 the constitutional court of a stable, medium-sized democracy rules that ‘human dignity’ can no longer be cited as an independent ground for any constitutional right. The majority isn't hostile to rights; it argues that whenever dignity seems to be doing work, it's really just autonomy or bodily integrity doing the work, and when it's not doing that, it's too vague to adjudicate. Other courts and human-rights bodies soon copy the rule. Predict, with your reasoning made explicit, how this actually plays out over the next twenty years. Which concrete disputes come out differently — say, conditions of confinement, assisted dying, a ban on dwarf-tossing, selling a kidney, consensual sex work, degrading forms of punishment — and why? Does the old dignity reasoning simply migrate into words like ‘autonomy’ and ‘inhuman treatment’, or do some results genuinely change? Who looses most from the shift? And does ‘dignity’ sneak back in under another name once the hard cases arrive? I want the chain of mechanisms, not a yes/no. For each prediction, say whether it's robust or whether it depends on the assumption that dignity was doing real normative work rather then just decorating arguments.
