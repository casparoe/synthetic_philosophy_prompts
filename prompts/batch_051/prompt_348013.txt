I've been reading the fourth chapter of the Zhuangzi—the one where Confucius tells Yan Hui about fasting of the mind—with a few people from a philosophy club. I keep comming back to the passage. Roughly, Confucius tells Yan Hui not to listen with his ears or even with the mind, but with qi: the ears stop at sounds, the mind stops at what it can recognize or match, but qi is empty and attends to what comes. The Way gathers only in emptiness, and that emptiness is what he calls the fasting of the mind. Then he links this to Yan Hui ceasing to act from a fixed sense of himself.

One of the people in the club summarized it to us like this: Confucius is giving Yan Hui a practical method for wu-wei. Clear out your thoughts, drop your preferences about the outcome, stop caring so much whether you persuade the duke, and you will become more effective in the world—this is inner non-attachment as a kind of power. But that summary has always felt too results-oriented to me. The passage seems to be saying that as soon as you aim at being effective, you have not actually emptied anything; the self with plans and preferences is still there. I don't need another survey of the passage, but could you audit that summary? Specifically, which parts are a fair reading of what the text says, which parts are him importing a later or pop version of wu-wei, and whether the passage actually supports the claim that the fasting of the mind makes you better at practical affairs.
