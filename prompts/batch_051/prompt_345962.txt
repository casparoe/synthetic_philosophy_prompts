At the same time I've been playing a ton of roguelikes and time-loop games—the kind with permadeath, save states, and a chronology screen that list every run you abandoned. And I can't stop superimposing the two things.

Here's the question I'd want answered. Suppose Borges could sit down with one of these games, something interactive rather than a story, where the player keeps undoing their own bad futures. What would he say about the ontology of the abandoned timelines? Would he treat a reloaded timeline as a branch that genuinely happened in the fiction—so the player is just wandering a forking garden and deciding which branch to keep living—or as a moment that gets annihilated and never existed once you reload, which would put the player closer to his idealist denial of time?
