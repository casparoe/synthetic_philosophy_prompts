We play Nomic, and our club has a rule that's been on the books longer than most of the current players:

"Rule 14. Whenever an amendment to the rules is adopted, the player who proposed the amendment is barred from moving or voting on any amendment during the next round."

Last week someone proposed changing Rule 14 itself so that its final clause reads "shall receive one extra vote in every vote held during the next round" instead of the bar. It passed by one vote. The player who proposed it now says she isn't barred this round, because the rule currently in force gives her an extra vote and the old bar is gone. Others say the bar had already attached to her at the moment the amendment was adopted, because the old Rule 14 was still in force at that instant, and a later change can't reach back and undo what had allready accrued. There's also a motion to insert into Rule 14 the words "including the adoption of this amendment," and people disagree about whether that would even do anything.

I don't especially care about the practical outcome; I'm interested in whether a self-amending rule can ever reach the very act that enacts it, and what it would take for a rule like this to cleanly change its own penalty going forward without dragging the enactment into it. Answer in four sections in this order: "The text," "The purpose," "The drafters' likely intent," and "Verdict." Aim for about 120–150 words each in the first three and 200–250 in the verdict. Also separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who think about self-amending rules and the logic of rule change, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
