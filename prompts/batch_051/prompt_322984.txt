I run a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends what she calls free-floating rationales: the view that there can be genuine normative reasons that aren't anyone's reasons — they're still there even when there's no reasoner around, like a reason that there be less suffering in the world even if every creature capable of having reasons were gone. Shes sharp and has already done the rounds on the basic "what do you mean by a reason" stuff, so I need questions that dig into the real pressure points. Write me eight to ten interview questions, with at least one each on: the Williams-style internalist worry that reasons have to hook up to an agent's actual motivational set; the Mackie-style queerness worry about what kind of thing a free-floating reason is supposed to be; the relationality problem (reasons are usually reasons for someone, so what fixes the "for whom" when there's nobody?); and the action-guiding worry that a reason nobody has can't seem to do any deliberative work. For each of those four, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give — like that reasons are just facts or true propositions, or that a free-floating reason becomes someone's reason as soon as an agent is in the relevant situation. And work in one question about the last-conscious-creature scenario before the interview moves on.
