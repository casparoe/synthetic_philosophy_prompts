Next month I'm giving a twenty-minute talk at a small games-and-art symposium, filling in because my freind runs it and someone cancelled. I'm a game designer at a mid-size studio, not an academic, and this is the first real argument I've tried to make in public. My thesis is that games are art, but not for the reasons people usually give: not the visual splendor, not the soundtrack, not a movie-like story layered on top. The actual aesthetic medium of a game, the thing games can manipulate expressively, is agency — the structure and felt weight of the choices the game offers. Paint is to painting what agency is to games, roughly. A text-based management sim can be a better work of art than a gorgeous AAA game if the texture of its choices says more, and a visually stunning narrative game can be artistically hollow if its decisions are just traffic lights between cutscenes. My compressed argument is: (1) agency has a compositional structure — what you're allowed to act on, what you're made to undo, what consequences stick, how the game feeds your choices back to you; (2) designers can shape that structure expressively, so two games with identical mechanics can feel entirely different as choices; (3) expressive shaping of a medium is the core of art-making. So games don't need to borrow their art status from film or painting.

The audience is about forty people and honestly pretty mixed. There's a block of systems-first game developers who'll think I'm being pretentious or selling out mechanics. There are art-school and media-studies folks who already believe games are art but think it's because they have imagery and narrative. And I know for a fact a few curators are coming who think interactivity disqualifies games, because an audience that co-creates the work breaks the author's control over the shape of the thing. I don't want a polished defense; I want a prep sheet. Give me the objections I'll actually get, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those groups would actually phrase them, with what I should concede, what I should firmly hold, and a short reply for each. Tell me where my own position is softest, and include the question I should be praying no one asks.
