I am a PhD student working on the ethics of deception, and one section of my dissertation tries to get clear about whether the common-sense distinction between lying and merely misleading marks a moral difference. I have been collecting arguments against the usual view that misleading is, other things equal, the less serious wrong — arguments, that is, that there is no clean moral asymmetry between the two, or that misleading is sometimes the worse of the pair. The arguments I have so far are these. First, the parity of deceptive intention: in both cases the speaker acts with the same end in view, namely that the hearer acquire a false belief, and if the wrong of deception is located in that intentional project rather than in the syntactic or semantic vehicle, then misdirection is no cleaner than a direct lie. Second, the manipulation argument: the misleader deliberately engineers the conversational context so that the hearer draws the false conclusion by her own powers of inference, thereby using the hearer's rational capacities as an instrument of her own deception — which many regard as more disrespectful of agency, not less, than simply asserting a falsehood. Third, the deniability argument turned on its head: the fact that a misleader can later protest that her words were literally true is best read as evidence that she planned from the outset to deceive while protecting herself, and that premeditated exit strategy is an aggravating rather than a mitigating feature. Fourth, the consequential parity argument: a false implicature can leave the hearer with exactly the same false representation, and therefore the same downstream choices and harms, as a false assertion, so on any broadly consequentialist criterion the two come out on a par. Fifth, the shared-norm argument: both practices live off the same background trust that a cooperative conversational partner will not lead one into false belief, so misleading free-rides on communicative norms as much as lying does and cannot claim the moral high ground merely by not asserting.

I have reached the point where most suggestions I receive are restatements, special cases, or hybrids of these five, and I am trying to decide whether any genuinely distinct argument remains to be made. Give me an argument against the common-sense asymmetry — or an argument that misleading is often the worse wrong — that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on it. Then tell me honestly whether you think the argument carries real weight, and how it sits in relation to the five I already have.
