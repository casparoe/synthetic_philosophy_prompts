Write a dialogue between two math grad students in the department lounge at 1 a.m., one a set-theorist-ish defender of actual infinity, the other a finitist who thinks only potential infinities are legitimate and that talking about "the completed set of all naturals" is where things go wrong. Center the argument on Hilbert's Hotel: the finitist should not make the lazy move of calling it a contradiction in ZFC, but should argue that the thought experiment reveals something conceptually fishy about treating an infinite collection as a finished object you can shuffle room by room; the set-theory defender should push back by demanding exactly what "fishy" means, and by arguing that the hotel's weirdness is just the price of accepting perfectly precise definitions of cardinality, bijection, and set existence. They should actually respond to one another's strongest points rather than talking past each other, and neither should win by fiat or by just asserting their view louder. I'd like the dialogue to force both of them to clarify what "existence" of an infinite set could amount to, and it should end with the disagreement sharper and better understood rather than resolved.
