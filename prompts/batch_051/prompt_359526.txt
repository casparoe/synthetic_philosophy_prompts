Before I read any more theories of presupposition I want the requirements list first, because half the papers I see nail the king of France and then quietly punt on the hard cases. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of presupposition projection and accomodation: the data it has to predict, the distinctions it can't blur, and the things it has to actually explain rather than stipulate. For each requirement, give the motivating case (the definite description under negation, factives like know vs. regret, stop/again, the conditional where his twins seems to get presupposed unconditionally, the wait-a-minute response when someone presupposes something not yet in the common ground). Also say what the theory has to say about the conversational scoreboard: how presuppositions get in, when a hearer can block them, and whether the update rules are semantic or just pragmatics. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are one camp's taste (bivalence, compositionality through every connective, full dynamicness, whatever). Then flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement filters out the most candidate theories. If you score the main families -- a Stalnaker-style common-ground account, a Heim-style dynamic semantics, an anaphoric or satisfaction account -- keep it a sentence or two each; the list is the deliverable.
