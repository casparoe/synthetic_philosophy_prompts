Consider Wikipedia's stated neutrality policy, and especially its handling of fringe topics such as Atlantis or the ancient-astronaut hypothesis. The practice is roughly this: an article presents the consensus of mainstream scholarship as the main account, while the fringe view is identified as a fringe view, supported only in proportion to its presence in reliable sources, and is not offered to readers as a live alternative on equal footing. Reconstruct the epistemology this editorial practice presupposes. In particular, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with: does it presuppose a workable criterion for demarcating genuine history or science from pseudohistory or pseudoscience, or does it only presuppose a criterion of source reliability that permits editors to defer to accredited communities without themselves adjudicating the first-order evidence? Evaluate whether the practice is coherent or circular at any crucial point, and explain what would have to change in it if its underlying epistemology were replaced by the view that all hypotheses deserve equal initial treatment.
