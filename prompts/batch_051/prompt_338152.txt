I keep going back and forth on this reconstruction of the Nozick-style line that knowledge isn't closed under known entailment. It's my own version, so maybe I've built a trick into it, but it feels too smooth:

(1) You know you have hands. (I'm taking ordinary "hands" knowledge for granted here; a tracking/sensitivity view is supposed to deliver exactly that.)

(2) You know that having hands entails you're not an envatted brain being fed a full sensory simulation of having hands.

(3) If knowledge is closed under known entailment — the strong version where knowing p and knowing p entails q gives you knowledge of q — then (1) and (2) give you knowledge that you're not envatted.

(4) But you don't know you're not envatted. If you were envatted, you'd still believe you weren't, so the belief doesn't track the truth across the relevant possibility.

(5) So knowledge isn't closed under known entailment, at least in that strong "you do know" form.

I honestly can't tell wether this is a clean little modus tollens or whether the sneaky bit is in (3) (using the strong closure reading so the conclusion only kills the strong reading) or in (4) (just asserting that tracking/sensitivity is a requirement on knowledge).
