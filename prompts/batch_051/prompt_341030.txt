I've noticed this assymetry in my own judgments: when a celebrity donates a kidney to a stranger, I immediately think "they're doing it for the attention or the self-story," but when I do something generous that same week I think of myself as genuinely moved by the other person's need. And it's not just me—my friends all explain public philanthopists as secretly egoistic while explaining their own private kindness as real altruism. We're basically applying psychological egoism to other people and a much more charitable theory to ourselves. Is there any principled reason for this asymmetric attribution, or is it just self-serving bias? If there's no justification, does that tell us something about how seriously we should take everyday psychological-egoist debunking—and about whether ethical egoism becomes more plausible once we stop giving ourselves special treatment?
