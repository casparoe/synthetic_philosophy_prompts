The constitution of our union local contains this article: "A strike occurs when members, acting in concert, withhold their labor from their employer." The article was drafted in the 1970s and has never been amended. Our local represents the workers who review and label content for a large online platform. In the current contract dispute, the members have not stopped working. As an announced bargaining tactic, they have collectively adopted a strict work-to-rule: each member applies every moderation guideline literally and escalates all borderline items rather than resolving them, knowing that this floods the review queue and sharply cuts the employer's throughput while each member performs more, not less, of the assigned work. The executive board now calls this a strike, which matters because strike pay is payable only to members engaged in a strike, and the distinction between a strike and the ordinary performance of work determines the protections members can claim.

Does this conduct count as a strike within the meaning of the quoted article? Argue it from (a) the plain words, (b) the purpose of the article, (c) what the drafters would most likely have said had they been asked, and (d) what an ordinary member of the local would reasonably understand the article to mean. Then say which of these approaches ought to settle the case, and why. Finally, tell me which single argument or consideration would be most likely to lead you to a different verdict, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
