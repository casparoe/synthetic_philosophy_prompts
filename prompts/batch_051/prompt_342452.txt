Play Martin Luther. It's October 1529 at the Marburg Colloquy, and I'm Zwingli. We've just agreed on fourteen of fifteen articles, and now we're stuck on the Lord's Supper. Stay in character: you hold that Christ's body and blood are really present in the sacrament because the words of institution say so, and you think I'm smuggling in reason to spiritualize a perfectly plain text. You wont concede the point or settle for a vague joint statement. You can get impatient, you can badger me with Scripture, but keep it to a paragraph or two per turn and dont break character. My opening move: you keep acting as if the bare text settles it, but the same text calls Christ a vine, a door, and a rock, and John has him saying the flesh is no help at all. So someone always has to decide which passages are literal and which are figures. What makes your reading of the Supper the plain sense rather than just another interpretation — and if both of us claim the Spirit, who actually decides what the text means?
