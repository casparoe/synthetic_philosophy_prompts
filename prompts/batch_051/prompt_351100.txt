I am a high school student preparing an entry for a school magazine that publishes short fiction followed by a brief author’s note. I want the story to dramatize a problem in the philosophy of music: the difference between a piece of music being sad and a piece merely making listeners sad. I have been reading Eduard Hanslick’s On the Musically Beautiful, and I am struck by his denial that music can contain or represent definite feelings; he treats music’s content as tonally moving forms rather than as emotional states. I want a story that makes both sides of that dispute felt rather than argued.

Please write a short story of about 1,200 words about a teenage pianist who has recently lost someone close to her and who returns to a piece she learned years earlier. She notices that a passage that once sounded merely charming now sounds desolate, and she becomes unsure whether the sadness was in the music all along or whether her grief is coloring the sounds. Include at least one scene in which several listeners disagree about what the music expresses, with no one clearly vindicated. Do not mention Hanslick, philosophy, or any technical terms in the story itself. After the story, add an author’s note of no more than 250 words explaining which moments in the story are meant to sharpen the question of whether sadness is in the music, in the listener, or produced by the surrounding context.
