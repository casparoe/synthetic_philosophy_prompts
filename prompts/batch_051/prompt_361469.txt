I have been trying to think through a proposal that came up at a workshop on how the Anthropocene might be formalized within the geological time scale, and I keep going back and forth on whether it would actually help. The background is that geological time units are normally defined by a single physical reference site, informally called a golden spike, at which a committee picks one marker — a particular fossil's first appearance, a chemical excursion in a rock layer, or the like — as the boundary. The Anthropocene fits this scheme badly. Its signatures include plutonium from nuclear testing, plastic particles, rising carbon dioxide, shifted pollen and bone assemblages, and novel minerals, and they neither begin at a single moment nor appear everywhere at once. The proposal was to create a new kind of boundary, a diffuse boundary event, for the Anthropocene alone. To ratify a diffuse boundary event, the proposing working group would have to nominate at least three marker signals from three different Earth-system domains — one geochemical, one biological, and one lithological — each showing a permanent departure from a pre-industrial baseline, with all three contained inside a single 400-year window chosen by the working group. The formal decision would then be taken by the commission that ratifies time units, under three procedural rules. First, any voting member who has authored or co-authored a paper arguing for a particular start date for the Anthropocene, or against naming it at all, is recused. Second, abstentions do not count toward the denominator; ratification requires a two-thirds majority of those who actually cast votes. Third, the working group that proposes the markers must be at least half non-geologists, drawn by lot from history, archaeology, ecology, and sociology.

Treat this as a mechanism to be analyzed rather than as a position to defend or attack. I want to know how it would actually behave. What would the recusal rule do to the composition and expertise of the voting body, and how would it change what geologists choose to publish? What would the three-marker, single-window requirement do to which candidate dates get proposed, and what happens to a candidate date whose evidence is strong in only two of the three domains? How would the abstention rule and the lot-drawn non-geologists interact in practice, and where would governments, advocacy groups, journal editors, and rival working groups first try to game the system? Over ten or twenty years, would this procedure yield a boundary that is scientifically sharper, politically tractable, or stuck in permanent impasse? Which failure modes could be patched by adjusting the rules without abandoning the diffuse-boundary idea, and which are baked into trying to settle a diffuse event with a single formal vote?
