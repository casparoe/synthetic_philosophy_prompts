I sit on my city's commemorations commission, and we have to decide whether to rename a street currently named after a nineteenth-century governor who ordered the forced removal of an Indigenous nation from the valley where the city now stands. The historical facts are not in dispute. The street runs through a neighborhood where many enrolled members of that nation live today, and the name appears on their leases, licenses, and ballots. A tribal council has asked for the rename on the grounds that living under the name is a continuing indignity; a neighborhood association opposes it, arguing that the name has simply become geography and that the cost to residents and businesses is unfair. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, offer the commission a workable test for telling when a public name is commemorating someone rather than merely locating an address, address whether renaming erases history or merely stops a daily act of honor, and take seriously the strongest consideration on the side you ultimately reject.
