I'm a software engineer who helps maintain the financial reporting system for a mid-sized city, and I have no formal background in philosophy, so I would like help thinking through a situation in plain terms. A close friend of mine is the mayor. The city is in a budget crisis, and the only hospital serving a low-income part of town will close unless the state releases emergency funds. The state will release those funds only if the city's finances meet certain statutory criteria, and on the honest numbers they fall just short. She is considering classifying a one-time land sale as recurring revenue in the city's reports. Nothing would be outright fabricated, but the presentation would be deliberately misleading, and she knows the state's decision could turn on it. She also campaigned on a promise of total transparency about city finances. If she does this, the funds will almost certainly come through and the hospital will stay open, sparing a great many people serious harm. If she refuses, the closure will proceed.

I keep being pulled in two directions. Deliberately misleading a state agency is wrong, and she would be breaking both a rule and a campaign promise to her constituents. But refusing also feels like a self-indulgent concern with keeping her own record clean while other people pay the price. Could both of those thoughts be right at the same time? What should she actually do, and how should she regard what she has done either way afterward? Please write your answer in American English.
