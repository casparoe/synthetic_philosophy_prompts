You are a historian of economic thought with a specialty in Adam Smith's theory of empire, and you are being asked to produce a piece of careful counterfactual political economy.

I am going to give you a reconstruction of Smith's actual views on colonies, as developed chiefly in the Wealth of Nations. Treat this reconstruction as authoritative for this exercise. It is supplied so that you do not need to search for sources, and I am not expecting any citations or bibliography in your answer.

Reconstruction of Smith's position:

- Smith analyzes modern European colonization in the Americas as quite unlike the older Greek practice. Greek colonies, on his telling, were a release valve for population pressure, whereas the modern colonies were acquired for gold and silver and, above all, for a legally captive market.
- The decisive policy instrument was the exclusive trade: law reserved the commerce of the colonies to the mother country. Smith treats this monopoly as a textbook symptom of the mercantile system. By raising the rate of profit in the protected colonial trade, it pulled capital away from other employments, including more natural trades with nearer neighbors, while the home consumer paid higher prices.
- On Smith's account, the system enriched a narrow merchant interest and disguised the real social costs. The expense of defending and administering the colonies was borne by the whole society, while the concentrated gains went to those with a stake in the protected trade. Under the arrangements he observed, he judged the colonies to be a net drain on the wealth of the mother country rather than a source of it, and he thought the appearance of prosperity was partly an artifact of capital being pushed into a protected channel.
- He was not hostile to colonies nor to commerce. His objection was to monopoly and to the marriage of empire with mercantile protection. He believed an open commercial relationship between colony and metropole would benefit both, and that the extension of the market could be a cause of real development.
- His preferred remedies were correspondingly political. He favored gradually dismantling the colonial monopoly. On the question of governance, he thought Britain should either grant the Americans representation in Parliament, with taxation proportional to that representation, in a full constitutional union, or, if that proved impossible, accept an amicable separation and negotiate the most liberal commercial treaty available.
- He regarded colonial slavery as both morally indefensible and economically inferior to free labor, and he explained its persistence as the taste for domination rather than as sober economic calculation.
- Finally, he saw the arrangements as unstable in the long run: the settlements were growing, the monopoly was distorting the metropolitan economy, and he believed power would eventually follow that growth rather than the other way around.

Your task is to bring this actual Smith to bear on two developments that postdate his death by well over a century:

1. The post-1945 decolonization of Asia, Africa, and the Caribbean, and the very mixed economic records of newly independent states since then.
2. The modern architecture of trade and investment governance, including preferential trade agreements, special economic zones, and investor-state dispute settlement.

Suppose Smith were given a competent briefing on these developments in 2025 and asked to write a policy memo. What would he predict about which post-independence arrangements would best promote economic development in former colonies, and what would he identify as the main rent-seeking dangers in current trade and investment politics? What would he advise the former metropolitan powers to do about preferential trade schemes, development assistance, and the terms under which foreign capital is admitted?

Please answer in two parts. In the first part, brainstorm in compact scratch form: list eight to ten Smithian commitments from the reconstruction that bear on these questions, and note where they point in different directions or leave the matter underdetermined. In the second part, write the polished memo from Smith to a 2025 policy audience. In the memo, make clear which conclusion follows from which of his commitments, and explicitly flag the places where a 2025 Smith would need to add empirical or normative assumptions his own texts do not supply.

Do not frame this as grading Smith against later history, and do not simply say that counterfactual questions are unknowable; the point is to extract the predictive and advisory content of his actual position. Avoid quoting Smith verbatim; phrase his views in your own words. Write in American English, and keep the whole response under about 1,200 words.
