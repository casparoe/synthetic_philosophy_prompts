The go-to move against coincidence stories is that they fall apart once you count up how many chances there were. Like the birthday paradox: any particular two people sharing a birthday is meh, but that some pair in a room of 30 shares one is close to guaranteed, because the number of comparisons is huge. Apply that to anecdotes and you get the law-of-truly-large-numbers line: with enough people and enough events, someone was going have this exact "what are the odds" moment, so the anecdote doesn't license much of a conclusion. Fine. But the setup of a lot of those anecdotes has changed. These days the two old friends who bump into each other in a foreign city weren't drawing from the same reference class as the general population—their feeds, maps, and review sites have been quietly optimizing for the overlap. The whole debunking leaned on the matching process being close to random, or at least not tilted toward the exact rare thing that happened. If the matching is now partly done by recommenders that exist to surface low-probability overlaps, does the old debunking still go through, does it need a patch, or is it just dead? If it needs a patch, what's the new denominator, and what exactly is the anecdote evidence of once you've factored out the algorithm?
