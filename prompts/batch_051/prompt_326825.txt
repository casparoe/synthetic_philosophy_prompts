Hey — I'm in a reading group on spirituality without religion, and I got assigned to write up my own philosophical profile so the others can poke at it. I keep coming back to this line from William James's The Varieties of Religious Experience: "In my belief that a large acquaintance with particulars often makes us wiser than the possession of abstract formulas, however deep, I have loaded the lectures with concrete examples, and I have chosen these among the extremer expressions of the religious temperament." That's basically my method rule for the whole topic: dont start with a definition of "spiritual," start with the extreme cases and work in.

With that, here's my commitment set: (1) I'm a naturalist — no supernatural agents, no afterlife, no non-physical stuff; (2) there are nonetheless real spiritual states — awe, flow, gratitude, the sense of the sacred, ego-dissolution — and they form a roughly unified family; (3) what unifies them is a felt relation to something larger than the self, but that something need not be real in the metaphysical sense for the state to do its work; (4) religious traditions are mostly useful as repositories of techniques and practices, and their theological beliefs are dispensable frames; (5) the point of spirituality without religion is transformation of experience, not propositional truth; (6) I'm skeptical that "spiritual but not religious" is a stable social identity, but I think the practices can transfer across individuals.

Audit this. Format: four sections in this order. "Tensions" — go pair by pair, label each high/medium/low severity, one paragraph per real tension. "Apparent tensions that dissolve" — same detail, short paragraph each. "Cheapest fixes" — what has to give for each real tension and what it costs. "My verdict" — bottom line, which single revision would clean up the most, and what argument would change your mind about that diagnosis.
