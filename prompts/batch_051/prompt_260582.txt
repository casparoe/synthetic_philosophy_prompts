For the past year I have been leading a migration at work from a Java service to TypeScript, and it has forced me to think carefully about a distinction I used to take for granted. Two classes with identical fields and method signatures are different types in Java unless one explicitly declares that it extends or implements the other; the TypeScript compiler, by contrast, treats them as compatible as soon as their structures match. My teammates have split into two camps: one argues that the Java view treats an arbitrary label as a real boundary, while the other argues that the TypeScript view mistakes a transient similarity of structure for a stable identity. We still have not resolved the argument.

Around the same time, I was reading and listening to discussions about social construction, and I began to hear the same argument in a different register. With race, gender, and money, one side seems to hold that the kind is fixed by an underlying structure, such as genes, chromosomes, or physical composition, while the other side holds that the kind is conferred by how a community classifies, declares, and treats things, the way fiat currency is money because of legal and shared practice rather than because of the paper. The structural-typing view seems to line up with the underlying-structure view, and the nominal-typing view with the social-declaration view. That is the parallel I cannot get out of my head.

I would like to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. If it is a genuine parallel, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and which insights from programming-language theory could carry over, for example the way structural typing recognizes compatibility between types written with no common ancestor, or the way nominal typing preserves identity across changes of implementation? If the parallel is superficial, where exactly does it fail, and what is the social side doing that a type system is not? I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so please unpack any philosophical vocabulary rather than assuming I already have it.
