For my course on Plato's early dialogues, our last seminar was on the Euthyphro, and we ended with the dilemma unresolved: whether the holy is loved by the gods because it is holy, or holy because it is loved by the gods. I would like a poem to read aloud at the start of the next session, as a way of restating exactly where the discussion stalled. Please write exactly five numbered stanzas, each a three-line tercet with an ABA rhyme scheme and each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion outside the poem. Stanza 1 should voice the horn that the holy is loved by the gods because it is already holy. Stanza 2 should voice the horn that the holy is holy because the gods love it. Stanza 3 should draw out the cost of the first horn: the gods would then be responding to a standard of holiness that exists independently of them and above them. Stanza 4 should draw out the cost of the second horn: the gods' love would make the holy holy merely by loving it, so the claim that the gods love the holy would collapse into the empty claim that the gods love what they love. Stanza 5 should let both horns return in a single voice without resolving the dilemma. The result should be faithful enough that someone who has read the dialogue would recognize it as a fair rendering of the argument, not just a vague allusion to it.
