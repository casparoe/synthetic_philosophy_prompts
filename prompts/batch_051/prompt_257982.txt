I've been reading on and off about the recognition debate in international relations and I can't shake the feeling that the whole thing has the shape of a LessWrong-style definitions fight, but I'm not sure that's giving the participants enough credit. On one side you've got the declaratory camp saying statehood is just a fact: an entity is a state if it has a territory, a permanent population, a government, and actually exercises control over them. Recognition by other states is just a way of making that fact public. On the other side, the constitutive camp says recognition isn't a comment on statehood, it's part of what constitutes it: an unrecognized entity is a territory with a government, sure, but a state is something you are only inside a system of mutual acknowledgment, sort of like how being a "celebrity" isn't just a property of the person but of everyone else's attention.

What bugs me is that both sides seem to agree about nearly all the underlying facts in any given case. Take a Kosovo-shaped or Somaliland-shaped situation: both camps will usually sign off on the same description of who controls the roads, whose courts function, which passports get stamped at the border. And then they still disagree about whether to apply the sentence "X is a state." This argument has been running forever and neither side moves, which is exactly the profile of a hidden verbal dispute.

My working theory is that the word "state" is doing two jobs at once: it's used as a descriptive category for a kind of political organization, and as a status term that unlocks international legal entitlements, and people keep grabbing whichever job matches their interest. But I can also tell a more charitable story on which theres a genuine question underneath. Maybe one side is actually making a claim about whether unrecognized entities have the same rights and responsibilities as recognized ones, or about whether sovereignty is more like club membership than a natural kind. And maybe there's a normative disagreement hiding inside the descriptive grammar: an argument about whether recognition *should* be constitutive, disguised as an argument about whether it *is*.

So I'd like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this—verbal, normative-vs-descriptive, a real metaphysical one, or some mix? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a taboo on the word "state," a specific hypothetical case, or should we just expect no movement? Also tell me if the premise is wrong—maybe one side *is* making an empirical prediction about how unrecognized entities behave that the other side keeps denying, and I've missed it.
