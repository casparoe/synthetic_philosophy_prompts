I'm a journalist on the science and ideas beat, and I'm working on a piece about a long-running standoff in philosophy of biology. One side (the etiological or selected-effects camp) thinks the function of a biological trait is basically whatever it was selected for. The other side (the causal-role camp) thinks the function of a trait is whatever it actually does now, or contributes to, in the organism or some larger system, and that the selection history is a seperate question. The two camps have agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I'd like you to draft the joint statement they would issue. The test is whether each camp would actually co-sign it.

Please structure it like this:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign. I don't want bullet points saying "biological function is a real concept" or other mush. I want claims that actually constrain the disagreement but don't quietly favor one camp.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should give: the neutral question that divides them; the etiological camp's answer, phrased the way that camp would accept; and the causal-role camp's answer, phrased the way that camp would accept. If a disagreement turns out to be mostly about wording once it's sharpened, say that instead of pretending it's substantive.

3. A cruxes section. Pick three or four sub-questions where a settled answer would probably move at least one side. For each one, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both camps would agree bears on the question, and record what each camp predicts or expects the case to show. I'd want cases along these lines, though you can swap in sharper ones: a newly arisen mutation in a single organism that boosts survival but has no selection history; a trait whose current contribution is different from what it was originally selected for; a vestigial structure that used to do something and now appears inert; and maybe a duplicated gene where one copy is under selection for a new role while the other keeps the old one.

One thing about confidence: please flag what's genuinely settled among philosophers of biology versus what's still contested, and don't write as if your preferred account is the consensus where it isn't. Also, no citations or reading lists needed — I'll fact-check and chase down the names later.
