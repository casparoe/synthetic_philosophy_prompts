Give me an example of a debate where someone uses a strategy-stealing move and it goes wrong. By strategy-stealing I mean the trick from combinatorial game theory: to show the first player can't lose in Hex or tic-tac-toe, you suppose the second player had a winning strategy, then have the first player "steal" it — make an arbitrary first move, then play as if she were the second player running that strategy, treating the extra move as harmless. The move only works because the game is symmetric and an extra move can't hurt you. I want a case outside those tidy games where someone makes that kind of move — basically, "if my opponent had a winning strategy I could just run it myself, so they don't have one" — and the symmetry its relying on isn't there. Commit to one example, spell out the would-be steal and the exact symmetry it needs, then show which asymmetry (burden of proof, first-mover advantage, costs of being wrong, whatever) breaks the steal. Better to defend one sharp example than to list five half-hearted ones.
