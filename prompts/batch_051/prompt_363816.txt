Spent most of last night arguing with a mate about the no-Hitler-no-Nazi-Germany question. Im genuinely torn. I'm attracted to the structural line: Versailles, the Depression, Weimar's weakness and the deep background of antisemitism make some kind of revanchist authoritarian regime look close to overdetermined, and the Great Man view just looks like narrative bias towards whoever's at the top. But I also can't shake the intuition that this undersells it. I can picture a generic far-right strongman rearming and maybe starting a war at some point. What I struggle to picture is the actual sequence: the Rhineland gamble in 1936 against the generals' advice, Munich, war in 1939 on that timetable, and above all the move from persecution to industrial extermination. That last step feels like one person's obsessions, not something the structure would have coughed up on its own. My mate says I'm smuggling the Great Man back in. I dont want a 'both factors interact' answer. I want to know which side should give, or if both can survive, what the reading is that lets both survive and what it costs. Is my intuition the sort of thing the structural explanation should debunk as hindsight and individual-level tunnel vision, or does the structural story have to be amended so much—timing, style and the extreme tail of outcomes—that it ends up retrodicting rather than explaining? Give the strongest version of each move, then commit and defend it.
