ok so for my philosophy of biology class we've been doing the species problem, and I've been keeping a running list of how my professor rules on cases. she's pretty opinionated, so I want to stress-test the list before the exam. here's what she's held:

- dogs and wolves are two species, even though they can interbreed and produce fertile offspring, on the grounds that they don't normally do it in the wild and have been on separate evolutionary trajectories for a long time.
- in a ring species, where each adjacent pair of populations around the ring interbreeds but the two end populations don't where their ranges meet, she calls the ends two species, even though there's no non-arbitrary place to draw the line along the ring.
- a lineage of animals that reproduces only asexually, so there's no interbreeding with anything at all, is still a genuine species, not just a convenient taxonomic rank.
- two bird populations that look identical and will mate and produce fertile young in the lab, but in the wild they don't because their songs are different, count as separate species.
- neanderthals and modern humans are separate species, even though there was some interbreeding.
- bacteria: she says we have to name species for practical reasons, but the species category is a poor fit for them.

next week's new case is a lineage of crows that split off from a mainland species about five thousand years ago on an island, are clearly diagnosable genetically and morphologically, and occupy a different niche. but when mainland crows occasionally blow over, they interbreed freely, the hybrids are fertile and do fine, and there's ongoing low-level gene flow into the island population. does she call that one species or two? which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and what rule is she actually running? I'm less interested in the label she'd pick than in whether her earlier verdicts force an answer here, or whether a couple different rules both fit her and predict different things on the crow case, and what further case would break the tie.
