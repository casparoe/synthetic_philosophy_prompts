I want a requirements list before I assess any proposed solution to the problem raised by Zhuangzi's butterfly dream. Formulate the problem this way: Zhuangzi dreamt he was a butterfly and, while dreaming, had no indication that he was dreaming; on waking, he found he could not rule out that his waking state was itself a butterfly's dream of being Zhuangzi. An adequate answer must therefore supply, or justify doing without, a way of telling waking from dreaming. Write the desiderata that any criterion for distinguishing the two states must satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. In particular, address the following. The criterion must be usable from inside the state being assessed, rather than relying on an outside observer who already knows which state the person is in. It cannot treat coherence, vividness, or felt continuity as decisive unless it can rule out dreams that present themselves as coherent, vivid, and continuous. It must get the classifications right for lucid dreams, false awakenings, and dreams in which someone passes a reality check without thereby being awake. And it should say whether the fact that people routinely take themselves to know they are awake without employing any explicit criterion is a constraint on the theory or a sign that the question is misframed. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely parochial demands of one anti-skeptical stance, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the familiar candidate criteria, such as a coherence test, a clarity-and-distinctness test, or an external confirmation check. A table is fine, but keep any scoring of particular candidates brief.
