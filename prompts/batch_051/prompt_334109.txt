I spent my career in the experimental sciences, where one kept two kinds of failed result strictly apart: the error, produced by an honest procedure that happened to go wrong, and the fabrication, produced by someone who knew the truth and chose to report otherwise. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy, and I have become interested in a third category, the one Harry Frankfurt brought into focus: bullshit, in which the speaker is simply unconcerned with whether what is said matches how things are. I have come to a view I would like you to stress-test.

I am inclined to hold that large language model hallucination is best classified as bullshit in a functional extension of Frankfurt's sense, rather than as lying or as mere error. It is plainly not lying: the system has no beliefs, so there is no known truth it is hiding. I also do not think it is error in the usual scientific sense, because error presupposes a truth-directed procedure that misfired, whereas a language model is trained to produce text that is fluent and plausible under a predictive objective, not to track the truth. The clearest statement of my position is a counterfactual one: for a wide range of prompts, replacing the true answer with a false one of equal plausibility changes the system's behavior only trivially. That modal indifference to truth, I think, is what makes the output bullshit even though no mental state of indifference need be occurring anywhere in the machine.

Tell me what would count against this. I am less interested in a restatement of the view than in a map of its exposure: which considerations, cases, and results should move me, ordered from fatal to merely awkward, and which of them a defender of the view should themselves count as a genuine concession. In particular, I would like you to press on the following, while adding anything you think I am missing. First, does extending an intentional vocabulary to a system with no mental states amount to a category mistake, or can "bullshit" be defined functionally without loss? Second, is the right object of analysis the bare model, or the deployed system consisting of developers, training data, guardrails, and interface—and does relocating the bullshitter to that larger unit strengthen or dissolve the view? Third, if targeted training for truthfulness substantially reduces hallucination, does that count against the indifference claim, or only show that indifference can be engineered away? Fourth, can "error" be salvaged by treating the model as a truth-seeking instrument whose calibration is failing, rather than as a process indifferent to truth? Fifth, are our reactive attitudes evidence here: we tend not to feel the same irritation toward a confabulating chatbot that we feel toward a bullshitter, and if that asymmetry is robust, does it tell against the classification or merely against a naive version of it? Finally, give an honest verdict on whether the position, as I have stated it, is so qualified that it risks nothing—and if it is, whether that is a defect in the view or simply what happens when one applies intentional language to an artifact.
