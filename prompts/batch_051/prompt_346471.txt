I got appointed to our town's ethics advisory board this winter, after a bruising fight over a condo conversion, and we've got a complaint I can't stop chewing on. Our code says: "No member shall solicit or accept, directly or indirectly, any gift, favor, service, or thing of value from any person who has a direct financial interest in any matter pending before the body on which the member serves." That clause was writtten in the 80s.

One of our planning commissioners, Dana, runs a tiny local-history podcast by herself. A builder whose wetland variance is pending before the planning board just bought six weeks of ads on it, at exactly the rate Dana charges everyone, with the ad read as "sponsored by." Dana says its just ad revenue, a sale at market price, not a gift. The old-timers on the board say the plain words "or thing of value" catch it, and the fact that the rate was normal doesn't matter. Our chair has gone the other way entirely: she refuses to sell so much as a used book to anyone who might one day come before the board, and says that's what integrity requires.

Work through what the clause requires or permits in Dana's case under (1) the plain words, (2) its purpose, (3) what its 1980s authors would have said if asked, and (4) what a reasonable reader of the code takes it to mean. Then (5) say which approach should win and whether the chair's scorched-earth refusal is really incorruptibility in the good sense or something less attractive. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
