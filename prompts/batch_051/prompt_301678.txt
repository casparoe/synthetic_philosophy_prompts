My local arts-council arguments all seem to end up in Shelley. Someone reads out the bit from A Defence of Poetry where he says, "A man, to be greatly good, must imagine intensely and comprehensively; he must put himself in the place of another and of many others; the pains and pleasures of his species must become his own. The great instrument of moral good is the imagination; and poetry administers to the effect by acting upon the cause." Then they say: the state already funds leisure centres and sports pitches because fit bodies are a public good, so it should fund novels and poetry because a fit moral imagination is a public good too. Audit that gym analogy for me. Lay out the correspondence table — what's the muscle, the workout, the trainer, the overuse injury? Then show where it breaks. Do the disanalogies actually matter for the funding conclusion, or does Shelley's point survive even if the gym picture doesn't? And if the analogy is doing the real work, is there a better one lying about?
