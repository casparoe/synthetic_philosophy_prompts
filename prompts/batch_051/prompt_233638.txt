(1) Van Fraassen's Reflection principle: a rational agent's current credence in a proposition P, conditional on her future self assigning r to P, should itself be r. (2) Right now my credence in P is .7. (3) Tonight I'm taking a perfectly reliable pill; I'm certain it will make tomorrow-me assign .9 to P, without bringing me any new evidence. (4) So I'm certain that tomorrow I'll assign .9 to P. (5) Reflection then says my current credence in P, conditional on tomorrow-me assigning .9, must be .9. (6) Since I'm certain that condition holds, my current credence in P must already be .9. (7) But that's absurd: a pill that just messes with my neurons isn't evidence, and the same chain would let a down-pill force .3 instead. (8) So Reflection can't be a genuine requirement of rationality. I'd like to keep something like (1), though, becuase it seems to capture something real about taking your future self seriously. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
