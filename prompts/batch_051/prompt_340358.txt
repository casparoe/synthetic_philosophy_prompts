Write a dialogue between a defender and a critic of van Fraassens Reflection principle—roughly, the idea that your current degree of belief in a claim should match the degree of belief you expect yourself to have later. Have them actually get into the weeds: the critic should push something like the amnesia case, where you know your future self will have lost evidence, and the defender should point out that Reflection was never meant to apply to a future self in a worse evidential position. Then have the critic turn that back around and argue the principle is either false if left unqualified or just a consequence of conditionalization once you qualify it. Both characters should be sharp and respond to each others actual arguments, not talk past each other. Neither should be a strawman; its fine if they end in genuine disagreement.
