(1) i should reason as if my birth rank among all humans who will ever live is uniformly random: if there will be N humans total, im equally likely to have any position from 1 to N.
(2) roughly 100 billion humans have lived so far, so my rank is about 100 billion.
(3) take two hypotheses. Soon: there will be about 200 billion humans total. Late: there will be about 200 trillion humans total. before i look at my rank, i give them 50/50.
(4) under Soon, about half of all humans would have a rank at or below mine, so my position is unremarkable. under Late, the first 100 billion humans make up only 0.05% of the total, so im in a wildly early sliver. the observation 'im somewhere in the first 100 billion' is about 1000 times likelier under Soon than under Late.
(5) so a plain bayesian update shifts me strongly toward Soon, unless i had a strong independent reason to favor Late.
(6) therefore i should now think total human population will probably be in the low hundreds of billions, so the human story is already about half over and our future is much shorter than it otherwise looks.

i half-suspect the problem is (1): treating my rank as uniform over people who dont exist yet seems to assume i could have been one of them. but i also suspect (5) is too quick, since i only compared two point hypotheses and never set out a prior over all possible N values. which step, if any, fails? if (1) can be saved, give the strongest precise version of it and say whether the argument still goes through on that version. if the argument is actually valid, tell me exactly what the conclusion does and doesnt commit me to. keep the answer under 300 words.
