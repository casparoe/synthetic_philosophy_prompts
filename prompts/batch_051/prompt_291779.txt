I am working through a view in the philosophy of physics, and I keep being pulled in two directions. I want a careful treatment of which side should give way.

The theory I am attracted to holds that gauge symmetry is descriptive redundancy. When two electromagnetic potentials are related by a gauge transformation, they are two representations of one and the same physical situation, and anything that differs between them — the point-by-point vector potential, for example — is surplus structure rather than part of the world. The view is systematic and principled: no experiment can distinguish two gauge-related potentials, and the gauge-invariant content is exactly what survives all such transformations.

The intuition I cannot shake is that the vector potential is doing real physical work in the Aharonov-Bohm effect. There, an electron's interference pattern shifts when the magnetic flux inside an impenetrable solenoid changes, even though the magnetic field is zero everywhere the electron can travel. It seems as though something must be present along each path, acting locally on the electron, and the only candidate is the potential — yet the potential at each point is precisely the sort of gauge-variant quantity the theory tells me to treat as surplus.

I know the standard reply: what matters in the Aharonov-Bohm effect is not the point-by-point potential but the gauge-invariant loop integral of the potential — the holonomy — which encodes the enclosed flux, and the holonomy is not surplus structure on the redundancy view. That makes the theory consistent with the experiment, but it does not by itself tell me whether my intuition was tracking something real or was simply misreading what the experiment shows.

I am not asking for a verdict that simply picks one side. I want a negotiated treatment. Give me the strongest version of the redundancy view and the strongest version of the intuition, and then address these questions: what exactly would justify debunking the intuition and dropping it; what exactly would justify amending or abandoning the claim that gauge symmetry is mere redundancy; and whether there is a reading of 'real' — for instance, one on which only gauge-invariant but non-local structures such as holonomies count as physically real — on which both the theory and the intuition survive in modified form. Say which resolution you would adopt and why, and be explicit about where that resolution is weakest.

Please write your answer in British English.
