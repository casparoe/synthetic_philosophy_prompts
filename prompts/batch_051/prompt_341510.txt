I am a retired research scientist—my working life was spent in the natural sciences rather than philosophy—and I have recently been reading my way through the philosophy of probability. A difficulty keeps recurring, and I do not have a name for it.

The difficulty is this. I want to say how likely it is that a particular event occurs: that a given patient survives a certain operation, say, or that a particular house suffers serious earthquake damage in the next decade. The only way I know to attach a number is to ask how often such events happen within some class of similar cases. But the answer differs from class to class. The patient is a man of sixty, a diabetic, a resident of this city, a patient of this surgeon; the survival rate is different in each of these groupings, and none presents itself as obviously the right one. The same holds for the house. I can group houses by region, by soil type, by construction method, by age, and obtain very different rates. I can always narrow the class until it contains only the case at hand, or broaden it until the number is stable but too coarse to be useful, and neither move is forced by the facts of the case. This is not just the practical problem of missing data. Even if I knew the frequency in every possible grouping exactly, I would still face the question of which grouping to use.

I can see that this is connected to long-standing debates about how probability claims about individual events should be interpreted, but I am not asking for a general survey of those debates. I am asking specifically about the phenomenon of the unprivileged grouping: does it have an established name, and if so, is that name used more or less uniformly? What should it be distinguished from, and where do its boundaries fall—does it affect all interpretations of probability equally, or only some? Has any school of thought claimed to dissolve it rather than merely live with it, and how persuasive is that claim? If you do not think there is a settled name, I would be interested in what you would call it and how you would delimit it from adjacent problems. Please also say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
