ok hi—so me and my friend Priya run the philosophy club zine at our school and this month we're doing a thing on whether you should actually try to be the best possible person. We read this article (sorry, I forget the author) about how a "moral saint" would be kind of grim to be around: every choice would get captured by whatever does the most good, so you could never have a dumb hobby or an inside joke, because the funniest jokes are usually a little mean and a perfect person couldn't risk that. Priya thinks this is overblown and says were just imagining a boring saint, not an actually excellent person.

So for the zine I want you to write, like, a 600-ish word deadpan FAQ for a fictional retreat called "Total Moral Optimization: A Summer Intensive for Aspiring Saints" (or something similar). The joke should be that the FAQ is written by the people running the program and they're totally on board with maximizing moral goodness, but the more earnestly they answer the questions the more obvious it gets that the program would sand down your whole personality into a utility-maximizing husk. It should be funny to someone who already knows the debate—like it should get the actual arguments right about why a moral saint can't have non-moral projects, hobbies, or pretty much any close relationship without treating people as opportunities—but also readable to someone who hasn't read any of it. Please keep the irony straight-faced, no winking, and maybe include one or two fake testimonials from past attendees who used to have personalities. Target's fair though, don't invent weaknesses that the position doesn't actually have.
