At a party last week a friend of mine who trades for a living held forth about "geometric rationality," and I've been turning over how fair his little lecture actually was. He's smart but very confident, and I couldnt tell where the math ended and the sales pitch began. Here's the gist as I remember it.

He said that when you face a risky bet you'll see repeatedly — he was thinking of sizing a trade you can scale up or down each week — the thing to maximize isn't expected value, it's expected log wealth. That, he said, is geometric rationality. The reason, he claimed, is basically the law of large numbers: over many repetitions your wealth doesn't track the arithmetic mean of the possible outcomes, it tracks the geometric mean, so maximizing expected log wealth just is maximizing your long-run growth rate. Kelly's formula gives the bet size that does this. Then he piled on: (i) full Kelly is the objectively correct fraction of your bankroll to stake, full stop; (ii) any fixed fraction smaller than Kelly is irrationally timid, just leaving growth on the table; (iii) any fixed fraction bigger than Kelly eventually drives your wealth to zero with probability one. He then said this explains why people look irrationally risk-averse in one-shot lab experiments but are actually being sensible: real life is repeated and multiplicative, so ordinary people are implicitly geometric rather than arithmetic in how they size gambles. He finished by claiming the St. Petersburg paradox just evaporates once you switch from expected value to the geometric mean — the fair price comes out finite, so there was never really a paradox.

I'd like an accuracy audit of this. Go through it claim by claim and tell me what someone who actually works on Kelly betting, growth-optimal portfolios, or ergodicity economics would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong. In particular: are (ii) and (iii) right? Is "maximizes your long-run growth rate" the same thing as "maximizes your expected wealth in the long run"? And does the St. Petersburg bit survive the corrections, or is it resting on one of the earlier conflations? I'm inclinded to think the St. Petersburg move is too neat, but I honestly can't say why, and I don't quite trust my own sense of whether (iii) is too strong.
