I have no formal background in philosophy or mathematics, but a late-night argument with a friend has left me unable to stop thinking about a small question that has grown rather large. I was taught that the number four is the set {0, 1, 2, 3}, so of course 2 is an element of 4. But I have since discovered that one could construct the natural numbers differently: for instance, each number can be built as a kind of nested singleton, so that four is the fourth iterate built up from the empty set, and in that construction 2 is not an element of 4 at all. Both constructions seem to deliver the whole of arithmetic without a hitch, yet they give opposite answers to a question that feels as though it should be about the numbers themselves. The conclusion I keep being pulled towards is that 'Is 2 an element of 4?' is not really about the number two or the number four; it is about whichever arbitrary coding we happen to have chosen, and the numbers themselves are best thought of as positions in a structure rather than as any particular sets.

Here is my opening move. You say that numbers just are particular sets. But once you admit that different adequate constructions use different sets to stand for the same number — and that arithmetic comes out identically on all of them — what is left of the thought that one of those sets is really the number 2? The only thing common to every adequate construction seems to be structure, not set-membership; so why should the question 'Is 2 an element of 4?' be any more genuine than asking whether the number 2 contains the number 1?
