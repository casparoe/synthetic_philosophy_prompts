I chair the governance committee of a 40-member food cooperative. Each quarter we choose among three to seven competing proposals (capital projects, rule changes, compensation policies). We currently debate and then use a single plurality vote, and we keep seeing outcomes that appear to depend on which two similar proposals happened to appear on the ballot. I know Arrow’s impossibility theorem shows that several minimal-looking conditions—unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, non-dictatorship, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and transitivity of the social order—cannot all be satisfied by any aggregation rule, but I cannot convert that into practice. Give me a reusable one-page pre-vote protocol for a non-expert chair deciding how to structure each agenda: when a single ranked ballot is adequate, when to use pairwise head-to-head votes or a two-stage runoff, and when the procedure should stop and send the proposals back for redrafting rather than hold a vote. The protocol should flag which condition is being violated at each choice point, how to detect agenda-sensitivity or a likely Condorcet cycle during the meeting, and where the chair must exercise judgment despite the protocol. Separate what is settled in social choice theory from what is genuinely contested about applying Arrow’s theorem to small democratic groups, and do not present one contested interpretation as the consensus. Include two or three worked examples, including one in which the procedure gives me an answer I will not like, and say explicitly what the protocol cannot settle.
