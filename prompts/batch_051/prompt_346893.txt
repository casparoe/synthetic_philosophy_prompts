I'm applying for a one-year philosophy grant and need a two-page proposal. I've been going around in circles on the unilateralist's curse, so I want you to help me turn my half-baked idea into something fundable.

Quick version of the idea I'm working with: you have a group where any member can unilaterally do something that affects everyone—leaking a dataset, publishing a risky preprint, launching a project under the group's name, that sort of thing. If each member just follows their own judgment, the probability that *someone* acts goes up with group size, so the group can end up doing things it never collectively decided to do. The standard takeaway is that unilateral action needs a much higher bar than individual action: wait for consensus, imagine defending the move to everyone, raise your personal threshold, etc.

But I think that's only half the story. The usual treatments are all about false positives—someone acting when the group would rather not. I suspect there's a mirror problem of false negatives, where everyone is so scared of being the person who unilaterally messes up that nobody does the cheap, reversible, exploratory thing, and the group stalls. That's also a collective outcome nobody chose. The existing advice doesn't say when a single member is *allowed* to just try something. My half-formed thought: split the curse by reversibility and option value. For actions whose main downside is mostly just the cost of undoing them, maybe there's a unilateralist's blessing instead of a curse.
