Since I retired from physics I've been poking around in the philosophy of history, and there's a fight that keeps surfacing about how to talk about causes when a whole pile of things had to go a certain way together. In my old line of work we had perturbation theory and initial conditions, and nobody much pretended that the 'size' of a cause was a fact of nature; now I keep meeting historians and philosophers who do. I'll call the two sides the weighting camp and the causal-field camp. The weighting camp says that even in a massively overdetermined historical event—take the outbreak of the First World War—you can legitimately say some causes mattered more than others, because the actual causal process itself weighted them that way. The causal-field camp says that once you list all the background conditions that were also necessary (the alliance system, railway timetables, newspaper culture, the fact that every general staff had planned for a short war), any ranking of causes is just something we project onto the world for narrative convenience. I'd like you to draft the joint statement these two sides could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on overdetermination and multi-causal explanation in history.

Format it as follows: a numbered agreed-claims section; then a disagreements table, where each disagreement is phrased so the side itself would accept its own wording; then the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side—and for each crux, a case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recoreded in advance. Keep the agreed claims from being vacuous on the one hand or slanted on the other, and say if you think any chunk of the dispute is purely verbal. One more thing I'm curious about: whether the old INUS idea, where a cause is an insufficient but necessary ingredient in a condition that's unnecessary overall but sufficient for the outcome, is neutral between the two camps or secretly smuggling in a weighting. You don't have to settle it, but I'd like it handled somewhere in the document.
