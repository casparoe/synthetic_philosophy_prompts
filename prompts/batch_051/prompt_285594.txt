Had a long row with a mate last night about whether pain is 'really' in the brain. He ended up backing a rule a hospital trust is supposedly considering: a patient only qualifies for strong painkillers if a brain scan shows the standard neural pain signature; otherwise it's physio, talking therapy and paracetamol, since on this view the pain isn't real enough. I don't want a moral verdict, I want to know how the thing would actually behave. What does it incentivise in patients, doctors, and the trust's finance people? How would it be gamed, and what second-order effects show up in who gets treated and who gets dismissed? And what does the rule quietly assume about the multiple realizability of pain—chronic pain, phantom limb pain, CRPS, just people with unusual brains? Can any of the failures be fixed without binning the scan idea altogether? Answer in British English.
