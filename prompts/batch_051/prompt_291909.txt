I've been kicking around a view about impossible experiences and I want it stress-tested. The position is this: some experiences aren't just beyond our current sensory hardware; they're ruled out in principal by the structure of phenomenal space. The boring examples are the forbidden colours, like a field that is red and green all over at once, in the same place and the same sense—not red in one patch and green in another, not flickering, not a brownish mix. The view says reddish-green or yellow-blue are not merely hard to produce, they're structurally impossible, like a square circle, and that tells us something about the space of possible experience rather than just about opponent-process wiring in the retina. So play it straight: what would have to be true for this view to be wrong? I want a map of its exposure. Which findings would count decisively against it—say, robust psychophysical reports of reddish-green, cross-cultural colour systems that treat red and green as non-exclusive, or a brain interface that produces an experience a subject cannot classify except as genuinely both? Which findings would merely be awkward? What would an honest defender have to concede, and at what point does the view get qualified so heavily it stops saying anything interesting? I find myself leaning on the lack of stable, uncontroversial reports of the forbidden colours, but I want to know if that's load-bearing or just absence of evidence.
