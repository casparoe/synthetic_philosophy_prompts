Analyse the Oostvaardersplassen reserve in the Netherlands as a case study in the debate over whether habitat destruction reduces wild animal suffering and how the net welfare of an ecosystem should be assessed. The reserve was a fenced, predator-free area created on reclaimed land and stocked with large herbivores; during hard winters many of the animals starved, which managers initially treated as a natural process, until public pressure led to culling and feeding. Treat the episode as a test of a sentiocentric framework, on which the value of an ecosystem is the summed welfare of the sentient animals living in it rather than its ecological integrity. Map out what that framework would count as the relevant welfare effects, contrast it with an ecological-restoration framing, and then say where each framework goes silent or has to strain against the facts. Finally, state whether the episode strengthens or weakens the claim that degrading or replacing a rich ecosystem can be a welfare improvement. Please write in British English.
