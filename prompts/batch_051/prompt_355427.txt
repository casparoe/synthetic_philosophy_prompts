Before I read further about how intensive-care resources should be allocated in a severe pandemic, I want to fix the standard any proposal must meet. Lay out the desiderata for an adequate triage protocol: the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must and must not draw (age, disability, baseline life expectancy, expected QALYs, number of dependents, and the difference between withholding and withdrawing care), and the consistency constraints it must satisfy with respect to the value of a statistical life and antidiscrimination norms. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration. Then flag which requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and state which are non-negotiable conditions of adequacy and which are merely local commitments of utilitarians, prioritarians, egalitarians, or disability-rights critics. Skip the scorecard of existing protocols; the set of requirements is what I most want examined.
