I've got a friend with extremely crisp views about when you can assign probabilities to your own choices, and a few of us have been feeding her cases to reverse-engineer the rule. Her verdicts so far:

- While I was standing in front of two envelopes, one with cash and one empty, trying to decide which to grab, she said I couldn't coherently put 60% on "I'll grab the left one." Not that the probability was unknown or hard to estimate. She said it doesn't exist while I'm still in the middle of choosing.
- When I said I was 80% sure I'd hit snooze tomorrow morning, because I've hit it 15 of the last 20 workdays, she said that's fine.
- When my sister had already signed a binding contract to perform her set the next night, and said she was 95% sure she'd perform, she said that's fine too.

Those old cases leave open at least two rules. One is that you can't assign probabilities to your own choices only while you're currently deliberating, but predicting your own future choices from the outside is fair game. Another is that you can predict automatic, habitual, or externally constrained behavior, but never a genuinely free future choice, whether or not you're deciding right now.

Next week we're asking her about this: it's a lazy Sunday, I'm nowhere near making the decision, and I say I'm 65% sure I'll confront my neighbor about his constantly barking dog before the end of the month, even though I genuinely haven't decided and it would be a free choice I make later.

Predict how she'll rule, spell out the rules that fit her old cases (there should be at least two), say which one you'd bet on and why, and then give me a further case that would cleanly settle which rule shes actually running.
