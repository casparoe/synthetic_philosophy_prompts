I am a novelist. In the manuscript I am revising, two characters return repeatedly to an argument about photographs: a critic who maintains that photographs are transparent — that looking at a good photograph is a way of seeing the photographed object itself, in the extended sense in which one sees through a telescope or a mirror — and a painter who maintains that photographs are opaque images, made and read like other depictions. I want their quarrel rendered precisely rather than as caricature, so I am asking for the document they would produce after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Please draft that joint statement. It should open with a numbered list of claims about photographs, perception, depiction, and the role of the photographer's choices that both parties could sign, worded so that the claims are substantive rather than empty or slanted. It should then present a table of residual disagreements, each formulated so its own side would accept the wording, followed by a list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, propose one concrete case both would accept as bearing on it — for instance, a live surveillance feed, a portrait of someone now dead, a print made through a distorting lens, or a heavily retouched photograph — and record in advance what each side predicts the case shows. Finally, identify any part of the dispute you judge to be about words rather than substance, and say why.
