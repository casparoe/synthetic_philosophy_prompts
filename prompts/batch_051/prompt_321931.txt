I write a weekly ideas column for a science magazine, mostly on health and behavioural science, and I need a reusable procedure, not a one-off judgement. A lot of my job is assessing "net benefit" claims: an implant or app or workplace policy shows small-to-moderate gains in mood or productivity across many people, but carries a small risk of very severe pain (or a severe mental health crisis) for a few. Schopenhauer's On the Sufferings of the World, the opening essay in his Studies in Pessimism, contains this bit, which I can't shake: "Evil is just what is positive; it makes its own existence felt. It is the good which is negative; in other words, happiness and satisfaction always imply some desire fulfilled, some state of pain brought to an end." Taken literally, that would mean the pleasure side of my usual "costs and benefits on one scale" framing isn't a seperate positive at all, and the two aren't commensurable. I don't want to go all the way to his pessimism, but I do want a checklist for something like a lexical-threshold negative-utilitarian rule: above some level of badness, no quantity of expected mild-to-moderate pleasure should be allowed to balance it in a story. You're an expert moral philosopher used to giving practitioners usable tools, so could you turn this into a procedure I can actually run in about 90 minutes before filing? Fix it to my situation: weekly deadline, I'm not a philosopher, my editor signs off, and the steps that are just judgement calls must be flagged as such. Apply it to two or three plausible cases, including at least one where the procedure tells me not to write a story I badly want to write and one where it tells me to write a story that makes me uneasy. Before you settle on a procedure, brainstorm at least three candidate ways of doing it and say briefly why you rejected or kept each.
