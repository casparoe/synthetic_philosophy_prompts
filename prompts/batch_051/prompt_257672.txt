I run a small reading group of regulars from the rationalist blogs and forums, and I'd like a sorting quiz for a session on pre-AGI bargains: agreements made now among labs, states, or coalitions that are supposed to shape how power, compute, or the gains from a future AGI are distributed or constrained after that AGI exists. Write a "Which view of pre-AGI bargains do you actually hold?" quiz with seven or eight questions, each a concrete case with three answer options, and a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. The questions must sort people by their case judgments rather than by labels, so no question should ask directly whether an AGI would honor a contract or whether these bargains are worth making. Use at least these cases: two labs signing a no-exclusive-grab agreement whose only enforcement is a neutral third party's code escrow; a government buying an option on a fraction of the output of whichever AGI project first crosses a capability threshold in exchange for compute subsidies now; a coalition publishing a post-AGI constitution with no technical enforcement but public endorsements from every founder; and a lab giving its rival a kill switch over its datacenter in exchange for a promised share of post-AGI control. Build the instrument from the logical space rather than from a list of positions: begin from two or three load-bearing distinctions on which the positions come apart — I expect them to be something like where the bargain's binding force is supposed to live (present institutions, the future system's values, or neither, as only present-day coordination), whether the bargain must survive defection before AGI exists, and whether the bargain gives the later system any reason to comply — then locate the named positions in the answer space. The scoring key should include at least the technical lock-in view, the coordination-among-present-actors view, the values-handshake view, and the deflationary view, plus any further positions the space supports. For each unoccupied combination of answers, add one line saying whether it is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate, together with the single additional question that would separate them.
