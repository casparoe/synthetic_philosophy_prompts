I'm putting together a two-page handout for a continuing-ed night class on the philosophy of money, and I want it to finish with a flow chart that sorts people into a position on what money fundamentally is — sorted by how they answer about cases, not by which label they like. The spine of the handout is this passage from George Berkeley's The Querist (1735–37), which I want the tree to interpret and not just decorate with:

Query 23: "Whether money is to be considered as having an intrinsic value, or as being a commodity, a standard, a measure, or a pledge, as is variously suggested by writers? And whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?" Query 25: "Whether the terms crown, livre, pound sterling, etc., are not to be considered as exponents or denominations of such proportion? And whether gold, silver, and paper are not tickets or counters for reckoning, recording, and transferring thereof?" Query 26: "Whether the denominations being retained, although the bullion were gone, things might not nevertheless be rated, bought, and sold, industry promoted, and a circulation of commerce maintained?"

I'd like the tree built from the logical space first: each branch labeled by the concrete question that splits it, and the positions placed into whatever combination of answers they actually occupy, with empty combinations flagged and briefly diagnosed (incoherant, just unattractive, or plausibly real but unexplored). The leaves I need at minimum: the commodity/metallist view (the money stuff is the value), Berkeley's ticket-or-counter view, the chartalist/state view (money is what the state takes in taxes), and the credit view (money is transferable debt). There may be one or two extra leaves if the cases force them. The diagnostic questions should use these cases, at minimum: a gold sovereign, a twenty-pound Bank of England note, a Yap rai stone that changes ownership without moving, a bank deposit, and a bitcoin. Please say explicitly where Berkeley lands in the tree and which of the other leaves he is most easily confused with, since the handout's payoff is getting people not to read him as just another chartalist. And since the class is mostly adults who dont care about labels, each leaf should carry one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
