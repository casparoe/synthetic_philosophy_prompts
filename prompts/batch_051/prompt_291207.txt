ok so I've been reading pop-sciencey stuff about Gödel's incompleteness theorems — not a math person, just someone who likes thinking this stuff through on my own — and I keep constructing an argument that seems to prove to much. It would mean humans can't be machines, and then it seems like it would also rule out us being physical at all, which definately can't be right. But I can't find the mistake. Here it is:

1. Gödel showed that for any formal system F that's consistent and strong enough to do basic arithmetic, there's a sentence that says of itself that it cannot be proved in F.

2. If F proved that sentence, then F could check its own proof and would prove the sentence is provable. But the sentence is equivalent to the claim that it isn't provable. So F would prove both, contradicting its consistency. So F can't prove the sentence.

3. But the sentence just says it can't be proved in F. We just saw it can't be proved in F. So the sentence is true.

4. So for every system like that, there's a true sentence that F can't prove but that we, standing outside F, can see is true.

5. Now suppose the human mind is itself a system like that: a consistent formal system strong enough for arithmetic. Call it M.

6. Apply 1–4 to M. There's a sentence about M that M can't prove, but that the human mind can see is true.

7. If the mind just is M, then whatever the mind can see to be true is something M proves. So M proves the sentence from 6, and by 6 M can't prove it. Contradiction.

8. So the mind isn't any consistent formal system that can do arithmetic. Any computer is one of those. So no computer could think the way we do.

Step 8 is where I get nervous, because now I seem to have proved the mind isn't a physical thing (or at least, not a computer), and I don't see how to stop the argument there without just making an exception for brains being magical. I really want to keep step 3 — the feeling that we genuinely see the Gödel sentence is true. So where does this go wrong? And what does the answer show about what Gödel's theorems can and can't tell us about minds and machines?
