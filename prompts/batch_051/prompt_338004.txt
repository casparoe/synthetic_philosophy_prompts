I'm writing a section on whether material abundance can be a final value rather than merely an instrumental one, and I want a case built to specification. Construct a two-community comparison over a fixed interval such that a strict welfarist about the value of states of affairs must judge the communities equally good, while a proponent of abundance as a basic value must judge one better. Constraints: the communities are identical in every welfare-relevant respect you would put in the welfare base (hedonic states, preference satisfaction, capabilities, health, longevity, autonomy, relationships, meaning, felt security), and differ only in objective material resources. The surplus in the richer community must never enter anyone's welfare: it is not consumed, traded, given away, displayed, burned, used as insurance or collateral, bequeathed, or made a source of meaning or pride, and no one's sense of security or future options are improved by it. Do not achieve that by magic, mind-alteration, or by making the resource physically useless; make it come from the structure of the case (for example, a deposit or unused working capacity nobody knows about or would touch), in an ordinary setting, and in a form it is not absurd to care about. After the scenario, step through the reasoning that gets each theory to its required verdict, identify the exact feature of the case that makes them come apart, say what happens if that feature is removed or softened, and give the welfarist's strongest reply to the abundance-pluralist's ranking. No citations, and don't settle the question of whether abundance is actually a final value.
