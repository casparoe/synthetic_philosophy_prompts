Here's a weird asymmetry. We'll say the fern died because I forgot to water it, and that the swimmer drowned because the lifeguard didn't put up the rip-current flag. But we won't say a child died because some guy threw the bed-net charity's letter in the trash and gave nothing, even when the charity was short exactly the money he could have sent. In both cases an outcome depended on something not happening. So is there a real causal difference doing the work, or are we just smuggling duty and salience into the word 'cause'? I want to know whether anything actually justifies the asymmetry. Try to give a principle that puts the lifeguard's inaction and my dead fern on one side and the non-donors on the other, then test it on the cases that feel unsteady: a strong swimmer who watches a toddler drown in a shallow pond, an off-duty doctor who walks past a crash, a homeowner who doesn't clear ice from the sidewalk. Tell me which side you'd put each of those on, and whether we should keep the asymmetry or drop it.
