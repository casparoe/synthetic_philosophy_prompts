I've got a policy for what I'll count as knowledge: if the evidence makes a claim at least 99% likely, I'll accept it flat-out and act on it. But I won't accept the conjunction of a big pile of these claims, even when I accept every member of the pile. So with a 100-ticket lottery that has a single guaranteed winner I'll say of each ticket that it loses, but I won't say all of them lose; and I'll say each sentence of a long report I wrote is true, but I won't say the report contains no mistakes. My friend says this is just the lottery paradox and the preface paradox, and that its incoherent—if I accept P and accept Q I'm committed to accepting P-and-Q. Is he right? Is there a way to keep the policy without either lowering my threshold or dropping conjunction altogether, maybe by treating acceptance as something weaker than full belief?
