I'm a science journalist, and while reporting a piece on octopus cognition I keep running into Morgan's canon — the old rule of thumb in comparative psychology that says, roughly, don't explain an animal's behavior by a higher psychological faculty when a lower one would do. A marine biologist I interviewed used it to dismiss a colleague's claim that an octopus planned a multi-step escape route, and I realized nobody in my draft is being precise about what the canon is actually asserting. It gets used as if it were one claim, but I suspect it's five or six different claims sharing a coat.

So: what are the distinct, precise theses Morgan's canon could be expressing? Lay them out as a menu, from the almost trivial (a mere burden-of-proof rule, a stylistic preference) to the substantive and risky (an empirical bet about what evolution actually builds, a claim about which explanations are better by default). For each reading, say whether it's defensible, false, or defensible only under conditions. Then tell me which of these readings would actually support what my marine biologist was doing when he invoked the canon to rule out planning in the octopus, and which readings wouldn't.

Don't bother with a history of Lloyd Morgan or a bibliography; I care about the canon as a living principle people actually reach for.

Structure the response with these sections, in this order and with these rough lengths:

1. "The menu" — each reading stated in one or two sentences and labeled (about 400 words)
2. "Trivial, substantive, or false" — your verdicts on each reading and why (about 300 words)
3. "What the canon can't do for him" — which readings fail to support the marine biologist's dismissal and why (about 200 words)
4. "What a fair version of his move would be" — the strongest claim he could actually be making, restated carefully (about 150 words)
5. "Bottom line" — three or four sentences, including whether the canon is doing real work or just functioning as a conversation-stopper (about 80 words)

Write in American English.
