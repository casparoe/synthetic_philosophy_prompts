I've always run my scientific life on a rule: when two or three people independantly observe the same thing, I treat the observation as a fixed point and force the theory to move around it, unless I can identify an actual common cause of error. Now I've been reading about cognitive penetration of perception, and it seems to me that if expectations can literally alter what we see, then four people who share the same theoretical commitments aren't really independent witnesses—the shared expectations are a common cause. Does that mean my old rule was just bias dressed up as rigor? Is there a version of the rule that survives, and what would it need to add, or should I go back and be less certain about a whole career's worth of observations I treated as solid?
