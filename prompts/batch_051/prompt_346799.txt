I am a PhD student in machine learning, and my work is in reward modeling for AI systems. I am fluent in the technical apparatus—scalar reward functions, preference models, multi-objective reinforcement learning, Pareto optimality, reward aggregation operators, lexicographic constraints, KL penalties, reward hacking—but I have only an informal background in moral philosophy. I keep encountering a philosophical dispute about whether human values are ultimately one kind of thing or many irreducibly distinct kinds of thing. On one side are positions that treat all value as deriving from a single currency, such as well-being, preference satisfaction, or positive affect. On the other are positions that treat values as genuinely plural, not fully arrayable on a single scale, and sometimes resisting comparison with one another.

I would like you to restate this debate in the vocabulary I work in, as faithfully as you can. For the main monist positions and the main pluralist positions, show how their core claims would be expressed in terms of reward functions, preference models, Pareto dominance, aggregation rules, lexicographic orderings, and related formal tools; flag where the translation is honest and where it distorts or imports assumptions from the ML framework. Then give me what I think of as the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with fudging, and which have no counterpart in reward-modeling vocabulary at all. Conclude with your judgment about the residue—does the untranslatable part point to features of human value that my framework cannot yet represent, or does it point to places where the philosophical debate is less precise than it looks? I want a systematic translation whose interest lies equally in what it fails to carry, not an analogy that papers over the failures.
