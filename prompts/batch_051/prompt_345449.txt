I'm the listing coordinator at a state fish and wildlife agency, which means I'm the one who writes the biological justifications when someone petitions to list something under our state endangered species act. The statute says "species," but in practice we're listing populations, subspecies, and things we can't even agree are one thing or two. Every petition turns into the same fight: two salamander lineages are diagnosable by DNA but they interbreed where they meet, so is that one species or two for listing? A parthenogenetic whiptail lizard has no interbreeding at all, so does it count as a species by the definition our lawyers keep quoting? A ring species is one thing at one end and a different thing at the other. We have no consistent framework, decisions drag on for months, and we get sued from both directions — one side says we listed something that isn't real, the other says we refused to list something that obviously is.

I've been reading around in philosophy of biology about the species problem — species as individuals rather than classes, general lineage concepts, operational criteria, that stuff — and I want you to turn it into a decision procedure my team can actually run on a petition. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a human because the question is about values or statue rather than biology.

Lean on a lineage-based view of species rather than giving me a survey of twelve concepts, but be explicit about where that choice is doing the work and where it'll get us sued. Then run the procedure on two petitions: (1) a cryptic lineage of freshwater mussel that's diagnosable only by a couple of fixed DNA sites and is ecologically indistinguishable from a common wide-ranging species; and (2) a peripheral population of a wide-ranging plant, adapted to serpentine soils and reproductively isolated by distance, that the petitioner calls an incipient species.

Keep the whole thing to one page: hard cap of 900 words, and if the worked examples have to get shorter to fit, shorten the examples rather than the procedure.
