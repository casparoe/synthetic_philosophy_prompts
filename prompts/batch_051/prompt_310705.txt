I've been reading about the fall of Rome lately, and the more I read the more the debate feels like its been running in place for a very long time. Gibbon blamed Christianity and internal decadence; other people point to barbarian pressure, lead in the pipes, soil exhaustion, plague, or climate; and then there's a group of historians who say "fall" is the wrong word because it was more of a slow transformation, and anyway if you were in Constantinople around 600 you'd probably have thought the Roman state was doing fine. Everyone is looking at more or less the same archaeology and the same texts, but nobody seems to change their mind, and each new book mostly adds another cause to the pile.

My working theory is that this isn't actually one debate but several stacked under one name: an empirical question about what happened and when, a definitional question about what counts as falling rather than just changing, and a difference about what a good historical explanation has to do—whether you even need a cause for a centuries-long process, or whether hunting for one is the mistake.
