I am a PhD student in historical theology working on patristic and modern Protestant hermeneutics. For a seminar I have to set down my own commitments on literal and allegorical reading of Scripture, and I would like a consistency audit before I present. Here are the commitments I am trying to hold together:

(1) The literal sense of a scriptural passage is the sense intended by its human author in his historical context.

(2) Origen was substantially right that some passages cannot have a fitting or true literal sense—for example, the days of creation in Genesis before the sun exists—and that the Spirit’s deeper sense is sometimes the main point of the text.

(3) Nevertheless, the events constitutive of salvation—above all, the resurrection of Jesus—must be historical; a purely spiritual or allegorical reading of these events is not a faithful Christian reading.

(4) Inerrancy is best formulated as the claim that Scripture, in the original autographs, teaches nothing false; it does not require every report of ordinary historical detail to be exact in the modern sense.

(5) The fundamentalist demand for a plain-literal reading of all narrative texts is a modern innovation, not the reading practice of the Fathers.

(6) Historical-critical scholarship is necessary for recovering the human author’s intention; without it, appeals to the literal sense are just projections of the interpreter’s own cultural habits.

(7) The canon itself is a hermeneutical rule: individual passages are to be read in light of the whole of Scripture, so that no text may be interpreted in a way that contradicts the rule of faith.

I am not sure these fit together, but I cannot tell exactly where the worst strain is. Please audit the set: identify which pairs are in real tension, which pairs are only apparently in tension, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the revision would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set stable. If you think one of my commitments is too badly formulated to audit, or that the whole request presupposes something false, say so directly rather than producing a diplomatic list.
