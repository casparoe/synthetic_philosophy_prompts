I cover the science-and-ideas beat for a magazine, and I'm writing about a scheme people keep floating to bring wild animal suffering into food policy. Here's the mechanism: every packaged food gets a 'sentient-harm score' per 1,000 calories, equal to the farmed animals killed to produce it plus an estimate of the wild animals killed as collateral during crop cultivation (field mice, voles, insects), each weighted by expected remaining life-years and a rough welfare multiplier. Anything scoring above a threshold pays a per-calorie tax. The numbers get strange fast: chicken has a huge per-calorie score because birds are small, grass-fed beef has a tiny one, and a loaf of bread gets taxed mostly for the field animals rather than the wheat. Walk me through how this plays out as an incentive system, not a moral statement. What do farmers, processors, and shoppers end up doing differently? Where does the accounting get gamed first, and can the failure modes be patched without gutting the policy or turning it into a parody of itself?
