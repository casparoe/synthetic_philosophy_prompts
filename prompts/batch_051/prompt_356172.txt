I'm taking a philosophy of mind course and we've been going over the colour phi illusion, and I keep going back and forth on it. The setup, in case you don't know it: two dots flash in quick succession at slightly different positions, one red and one green. With the right timing you don't see two separate dots at all — you see a single dot glide across, and it seems to change from red to green roughly halfway along its path. But that's odd, because the green flash hasn't happened yet at the moment the halfway point is reached, so the information about the second colour can't have been available when the dot seemed, from the inside, to be turning green mid-flight.

There are two ways people explain this. The "Orwellian" story says you genuinely experienced a red dot moving, and then the later green flash arrived and your memory of the trajectory was rewritten afterwards, so it now seems to you that you saw the colour change mid-way. The "Stalinesque" story says nothing was rewritten in memory; your brain held the early signal back and stitched a cleaned-up version of the film before it ever reached consciousness, so the experience itself was doctored, not the recollection. From the inside they're indistinguishable, because either way you end up with the same report.

I'm attracted to the deflationary theory: there just is no fact of the matter about which of these happened. The question "did I see it change colour, or only misremember seeing it change?" is a question about a boundary — between experience and memory, or between conscious perception and preconscious processing — that the brain doesn't actually draw. The two stories are the same story told at different stages, and insisting on one is like asking which frame is the film's "real" cut. That view has systematic virtues: it tidies up a whole mess of illusions, change blindness and backward referral effects without positing a little observer in a theatre who gets shown either the edited or the unedited version.

But when I actually run the demo and watch it, I can't shake the intuition that my experience has a real temporal structure, and that there is a fact about which bits were fabricated before I became conscious of them and which bits my memory messed with afterwards. I know that's exactly the intuition the theory tells me to distrust, but it doesn't loosen its grip.

I don't want you to simply pick a side. Give me the strongest version of both positions (the deflationary no-fact-of-the-matter view, and the intuition that the felt order of experience guarantees a determinate answer), and then a negotiated verdict: should the intuition be debunked as an artefact of how introspection works, should the theory be amended or abandoned because it can't account for the felt temporality of consciousness, or is there a reading on which they're compatible? What would it take for each of these moves to be the right one? Oh, and please write the whole thing in British English.
