I've got three kids (7, 5, and 2) and over the holidays me and my wife got into a long argument with her brother about whether we should keep doing Santa. He thinks it's basically training kids to distrust adults; I think that's way overblown, but the conversation made me realize I've never actually checked whether the things I believe about this stuff fit together. I don't have any background in this, I've just thought about it a lot while answering questions from a seven-year-old. Below is the list I wrote down afterwards. I'm not sure where the problem is, if there is one, and I genuinely don't know yet which of these I'd give up if one has to go.

Here's what I believe:

1. Lying is wrong when it's for the liar's benefit, but lying for the person being lied to isn't automatically wrong, and Santa and the tooth fairy are clearly for the kid.
2. Most kids aren't harmed or even upset when they find out. If anything they're a little proud they figured it out, and then they enjoy being on the other side of it with younger kids.
3. But a parent's word is different from other people's. If a kid catches you in one lie, they start asking what else you made up, and that's not paranoia, it's just sensible.
4. The child's age changes the act. Santa for a three-year-old is one thing; Santa for a nine-year-old who's clearly testing it is another. The same words can be fine at one age and a betrayal at another.
5. Santa and the tooth fairy are closer to a game the whole culture is playing, like saying the floor is lava. Calling it lying at all sort of misses the point.
6. Even so, I would never look a kid in the eye and insist flat-out that Santa is real if they asked me directly whether he is. Going along with the story is one thing; denying the truth to a straight question is another.
7. The line that actually matters to me is fantasy versus real-world fact. The tooth fairy leaving money is fine because the tooth fairy is fantasy; saying the shot won't hurt is not fine because that's a claim about the real world.
8. And I'll admit the magic only works because the kid is left out of the game. If you told them from the start that Santa is pretend but we all act like he's real, you'd lose the whole point.

Go through these and tell me whether the set hangs together. Which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are, ranked by how severe the tension is? For each real tension, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? And if I can only change one, which revision cleans up the most?
