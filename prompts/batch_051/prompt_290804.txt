ok here's a weird one. Suppose you have to choose among three policies A, B, C and there are three mutually exclusive ways the world could be, S1, S2, S3. You honestly have no probabilities for them — one-shot choice under radical uncertainty, not a repeatable game. Payoffs are utilities, higher is better:

        S1  S2  S3
A       40  40  40
B        0 100  50
C       30  70  40

The rules split in the usual way: maximin says A (worst case 40), minimax regret says C (max regret 30), Laplace's principle of insufficient reason says B (average 50), which is also what Harsanyi-style equal-probability expected utility would pick if the states were treated as equally probable persons behind a veil of ignorance; Rawls-ish maximin would also pick A but for its own reasons.

I'd like you to rank five candidates by how much you'd trust them as the right way to chose here: (1) plain maximin, (2) minimax regret, (3) Laplace's principle of insufficient reason, (4) Rawlsian maximin-style reasoning behind a veil of ignorance, (5) Harsanyi-style equal-probability expected utility. Tiers are fine, a straight ordering is fine too. For each, a sentence or two on why it lands where it does. I want a committed ranking — your actual ranking, not a menu of possible rankings — and spend the most time defending the calls where two rules pick the same act but you put one above or below the other. Also say something about whether the Rawls–Harsanyi split here is really a disagreement about decision rules or just about what the states represent.
