I am a novelist, and a recurring problem in my work has turned out to be conceptual. I want to write moral disagreement in which my characters hold genuine preferences rather than mere biases, but I also do not want to launder biases by calling them preferences. The distinction matters to me because a bias is something a narrative can treat as a judgement to be outgrown, whereas a preference can be left standing as a value. Would you propose a working distinction between a moral preference and a moral bias — ideally a definition of each — and test it against the following cases from my drafts? Lead with your proposed distinction in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.

My cases, with my own verdicts, are these:

1. A character will not eat rabbit because she kept rabbits as a child, though she will eat lamb. I call this a preference.
2. A character opposes assisted dying because he holds that suffering can be redemptive. He would not prevent others from choosing it, but he would not help them. I want this to count as a preference, not a bias.
3. A character feels immediate revulsion at the thought of two men having sex, and reports that revulsion as moral wrongness, though he can name no harm. I call this a bias.
4. A character raised in a teetotal household judges drinkers as weak-willed, even though she can see no harm in a glass of wine with dinner. I call this a bias, but I fear the test that makes it a bias also makes case 2 a bias.
5. A character donates to a mediocre local museum rather than to a more effective overseas charity, because the museum is where he proposed to his wife. I call this a preference.
6. A character opposes factory farming and will eat only meat from small farms, but does not judge others who eat factory-farmed meat. I call this a moral preference rather than a bias, though I admit the case makes me uneasy.

Please run your distinction through all six cases, tell me where it disagrees with my verdicts, tell me which of my verdicts would have to go if all six cannot be saved, and identify the verdict you think is least secure.
