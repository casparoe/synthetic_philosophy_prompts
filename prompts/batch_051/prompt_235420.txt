The objection says suicide is a sin against God because it steps into God's job of deciding life and death and messes up the order God set up for the world. Here's my reconstruction of Hume's comeback:

1. If suicide is a sin against God, it can only be because it disrupts God's order in the world.
2. God's order is just the whole course of nature running under the general laws God made.
3. But no action by a creature can disrupt that order, since the action is itself an event inside the course of nature — you can't step outside God's order by doing something, because whatever you do is part of the same sequence.
4. So suicide doesn't disrupt God's order.
5. So suicide isn't a sin against God, at least not on this line of objection.
