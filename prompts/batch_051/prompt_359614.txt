Consider the following eight political entities and the question of whether each is sovereign: France as it exists today; the municipal government of Paris; Ireland as a member state of the European Union; Turkey, which is in a customs union with the EU but has no vote in EU institutions; Scotland as a devolved nation within the United Kingdom; Puerto Rico as an unincorporated territory of the United States; the Cherokee Nation as a federally recognized tribal government within the United States; and Virginia under the Articles of Confederation, a union usually described as a league of sovereign states rather than a national government. I take it as fixed that France is sovereign and that the municipal government of Paris is not; for the remaining six I am genuinely unsure. Offer a definition of sovereignty, run it through all eight cases, and tell me which of the definition's verdicts clash with my two fixed points or otherwise surprise you. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I especially want to know whether your final definition treats sovereignty as a binary status or as a bundle of powers that can be partly pooled, as in a customs union, or partly delegated, as in a devolved government.
