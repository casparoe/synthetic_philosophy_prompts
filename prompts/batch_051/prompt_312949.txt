I'm putting together a little lunch-and-learn thing for my team about when to kill a project, and I want it centered on a quiz that tells people what their actual stance on sunk costs is. We argue about this all the time: the standard nerd answer is that the money and time already spent is simply irrelevant, but half my team has a different intuition, and they're definitely not all saying the same thing. I want a decision tree (or a short quiz with a scoring key) that sorts people by the reasons they actually accept, not by whether they know the term 'sunk cost.'

The branch questions should be concrete enough that an engineer can answer them without any background — like 'does it matter that we already spent six months if the only thing continuing does is avoid admitting the project failed?' or 'does it matter that we'd lose credibility with the client on the next bid?' — and each leaf should be a named position with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Make sure the leaves include: the pure forward-looking view (sunk costs are never a reason), the view that past costs only matter as evidence about the future, something about reputation or commitment, something about avoiding regret or preserving a self-narrative, and a backward-looking view that says the past spending itself gives a reason. Shoot for six or seven leaves. Also mark the empty boxes in the logical space — combinations of answers nobody defends — and say whether each is empty because it's incoherent, just unappealing, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves the tree can't cleanly separate.
