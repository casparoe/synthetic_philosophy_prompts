I run a small roleplay MMO, and our conduct rules are about twelve years old. The clause everyone cites for griefing in player housing is 7.2:

"A member may not destroy, deface, or impair the function of property belonging to another member, including structures, vehicles, tools, and stored items."

We now have a case the rule plainly wasnt written for. A griefer found a way to attach a "phantom lag" effect to a specific player's avatar—not to any item. The effect follows her across servers, persists through logout, and adds about two seconds of delay to every input. She's an in-world performing musician; the delay means she effectively can't play anymore. No property was touched: her inventory, instruments, and build are all fine. But she says it's still vandalism of *her*, and other players who've been hit report that it feels like their own body has stopped listening to them.

Does 7.2 reach this? Walk through what the plain words give, what the clause was for, what someone who drafted it back then would probably have said if you'd described phantom lag to them, and what a reasonable player reading 7.2 today would expect it to cover. Then say which approach should win for us and why. And if you think I'm framing this wrong—say, that the interesting problem isnt property at all but what counts as virtual bodily integrity—tell me that instead of forcing an answer out of the clause.
