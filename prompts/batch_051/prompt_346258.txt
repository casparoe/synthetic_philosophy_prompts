I keep noticing a manuever in debates that I don't have a name for. Someone wants to convince me of something, and every individual thing they say is true and every individual step in their argument is valid. Still, I can tell they are picking which true things to mention, which order to put them in, and how to frame them, entirely to steer me toward their conclusion — they would have mentioned different true things if those moved me the other way. It isn't lying, it isn't even misleading in the sense of implying anything false, but it still feels like being worked over rather than reasoned with. Is there an established name for exactly this: using true information and valid inference as raw material for steering someone's belief, where the speaker's selection of material is guided by its expected effect rather than by what they regard as epistemically relevant? And how do you draw the line between this and ordinary good presentation of an argument, where you lead with your strongest point and leave out irrelevant truths? I don't want just a label; I need the boundary. Whenever I try to state it, either everything counts as manipulation or nothing does, and if there's no exact name for the maneuver, I'd want to know how to define it so it doesn't just collapse into lying, rhetoric, or ordinary persuasion.
