I've got a double standard about infinity and I can't tell if it's defensible.

As a programmer I'm completely comfortable with a `while true` loop that never terminates, a generator that yields 0, 1, 2, ... forever, a lazy list of the Fibonacci numbers, and defining a recursive type like a linked list that can be arbitrarily long. None of that ever feels like I'm smuggling anything in; its all just rules for producing the next item.

But Hilbert's hotel gives me the creeps in a way `[0..]` never does. You've got a hotel with infinitely many rooms, all occupied, and one new guest shows up, so you move every guest from room n to room n+1 and room 1 opens up. Then infinitely many guests show up and you move everyone to room 2n, freeing all the odd rooms. I can follow each step, but my instinct is that the trick works only because the whole infinity is somehow already there at once, and "everyone moves at once" is doing the magic. Same with Cantors diagonal argument: I accept it as a proof, but the moment it's dressed up as rearranging an actual completed collection, it feels like a con.

The clearest version of the asymmetry: I have no problem writing `evens = map (*2) [0..]` and saying there's a one-to-one correspondence between the stream of naturals and the stream of evens. But if you tell me an infinitely-full hotel can free up infinitely many rooms by shifting guests to double their room number, my brain says you've violated conservation of something. I don't get that feeling about the lazy list. I'm not even a finitist — I use infinite data structures daily and don't want them banned — so this isnt really about whether completed infinities are allowed at all, just about why some of them feel like cheating.

Am I picking up on a real distinction here — something like potential infinity versus actual infinity — or is Hilbert's hotel just `map succ` on a lazy list and the discomfort is just because it's unfamiliar and set in a physical-sounding hotel? If there is a real distinction, give me the sharpest version you can and tell me which side each of my code examples lands on. If there isn't, tell me why the hotel still feels like cheating and what that says about how I should treat infinities in the code I write.
