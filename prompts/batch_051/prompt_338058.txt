I recently went back to school part-time and enrolled in a course on historical linguistics, partly to make sense of something that had bothered me for years: my grandparents, and a few older people from their town, use word forms that sound to me as though a sound change that swept through the standard language never quite reached them. When I brought one example to class, the instructor stopped me with the old Neogrammarian saying that sound laws admit no exceptions. If a form looks as if it escaped an otherwise regular change, he said, the form must have been borrowed or reshaped by analogy; my task was to work out which, not to treat it as a counterexample to the law.

I came away unsure what exactly I was being asked to accept, because the saying seems to mean several different things at once. On some days I read it as a tautology: a process is called a sound law only if it has no exceptions, so an alleged exception is simply evidence that the law was misstated or its conditions left unspecified. On other days I read it as a strong empirical claim: sound change is mechanical and phonetically blind, so any genuine irregularity must be traceable to a different mechanism such as analogy or contact. And on still other days it sounds like a purely methodological rule: do not take an apparent exception as a refutation; take it as an unsolved puzzle and keep looking for the interfering factor. I also notice that the same course teaches go/went as a grammatical rule despite its suppletion, which makes me wonder whether the slogan is meant to apply to grammatical rules too, or only to sound change.

Lay out the distinct precise claims that 'sound laws admit no exceptions' could be making, and for each one say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or too strong. In particular, I want to know which reading actually supports the instructor's conclusion that my dialect form must be borrowing or analogy, and what would have to be true for that move to go through. If a true reading of the slogan still does not license that conclusion, point that out explicitly.
