I keep going back and forth on a parallel I noticed, and I want someone to tell me whether it's real or just pattern-matching. In programming there's a reasonably clean distinction between understanding existing code—figuring out what it actually does, quirks and all, maybe inferring what the original author intended—and refactoring it: changing the code because it's messy, confusing, or no longer fits what we need. Refactoring is supposed to preserve observable behavior; that's the whole point of tests and regression suites, and moving fast without tests is seen as risky precisely because you might silently change behavior.

In philosophy there's a vaguely adjacent distinction between analysing a concept—finding out what our existing concept actually tracks or how 'we' use it—and conceptual engineering / ameliorative analysis, where you propose replacing or revising a concept because it does bad work or bakes in bad assumptions. The slogan version is something like fixing concepts rather than analysing them.

The structural resemblance I can't let go of: both fields have a descriptive project and a revisionary project, and in both the revisionary project is supposed to be disciplined by what the thing is for. But the disanalogy also jumps out at me: refactoring is supposed to preserve behavior, whereas a lot of conceptual engineering seems to deliberately change a concept's extension—it wants the concept to carve the world up differently. Maybe that makes the analogy shallow. Or maybe the analogue of 'behavior' isn't a concept's extension but the job the concept does for us, and then conceptual engineering is refactoring with the same job but better internals—except it's unclear what the 'tests' would even be.

So: is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If anything does transfer from software to the concept-fixing debates—regression tests, backwards compatibility, semantic versioning, leaky abstractions—what transfers? And what's something the analogy would predict that turns out false? When you answer, please seperate what's fairly settled from what people in the debate genuinely contest, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
