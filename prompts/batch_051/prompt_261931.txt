I write on philosophy and the sciences for a national ideas magazine, and I also teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Augustine. I need a final examination question on Confessions XI, together with a model answer and a marking scheme out of 20. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes and should be built around a specific move in the text: after granting that the past and the future do not exist and that the present has no extension, Augustine locates the three times as a kind of mental stretching—memory, attention, and expectation—and argues that time is measured in the mind rather than in celestial motion. The exam question should force students to do more than summarize this. It should require them to say whether the mental stretching solves the puzzle or just reframes it, and to test the proposal that time is measured only by the mind against the fact that external, intersubjectively synchronized clocks seem to measure something independent of any single mind's attention. The model answer should reach a defensible verdict. The marking scheme should identify five must-hit moves, the marks each is worth, and a one-line note on the typical error each move corrects.
