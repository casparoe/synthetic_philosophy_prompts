I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, trying to understand a live dispute in the philosophy of biology about whether DNA actually is a code or whether the word code is only a metaphor. The dispute seems to trace back to the final section of James Watson and Francis Crick's 1953 Nature paper, Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid. In the penultimate paragraph, after saying that the specific base pairing they propose suggests a possible copying mechanism for genetic material, they add one sentence to the effect that the precise sequence of the bases appears to be the code that carries the genetic information. That sentence is left undeveloped in the paper.

I'd like you to explain the conceptual issue at stake when people ask whether that remark is a metaphor or something more literal, in a way that engages my engineering background rather than asking me to defer to philosophers. Concretely, my natural toolkit says a code is a mapping between two finite alphabets, and a communication system decomposes into source, encoder, channel, decoder, and destination. When biologists talk about transcription, translation, the genetic code, and gene expression, where does this engineering picture hold up, and where does it begin to strain? What is doing the encoding, what is doing the decoding, and what is the channel?

I'm not looking for a survey of authors or a bibliography. I want the strongest case for each side: on one hand, that the 1953 sentence identified a real code-like structure in nature; on the other, that importing code and information from communication engineering has systematically misled biology. Then give me your own verdict on which parts of my engineering picture a careful defender of the literal-code view should keep and which parts they should drop.
