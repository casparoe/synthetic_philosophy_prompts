The question I set was: "Explain the leveling-down objection to egalitarianism, and explain how Parfit's priority view is supposed to avoid it."

The student wrote: "The leveling-down objection is that if equality is valuable in itself, then a change that makes the better-off worse off so they become equal with the worse-off would be good in at least one respect, even though it benefits nobody. Since that seems absurd, the objection shows that equality cannot be intrinsically valuable. Parfit's priority view is meant to avoid this. It says that benefits matter more the worse off the person is — in fact, the worst off get absolute priority, so we should always help them first no matter how much larger the benefits to the better off would be. Because leveling down doesn't benefit the worst off, the priority view says it isn't an improvement, so prioritarianism keeps the concern for the worse off without endorsing leveling down."
