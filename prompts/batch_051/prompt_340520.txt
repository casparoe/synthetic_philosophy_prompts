In a seminar on sovereignty and membership I have been following, the instructor has built up a small set of settled judgments about when an arrangement among states amounts to a union that its members have joined, as opposed to a mere treaty or coordination scheme. Her record so far runs as follows. The Northern Compact, under which member states remove tariffs on one another's goods and mutually recognize product standards while each keeps its own external tariffs and its own trade negotiations with third countries, and under which every amendment requires unanimity, she says is not a union; the members have concluded an agreement but have joined nothing. The Meridian Customs Union, under which the members adopt a common external tariff, a single negotiator for trade with third countries, and a customs code directly binding on importers, with trade decisions taken by qualified majority—though with no free movement of persons and no general legislature—she says is a union. The Open Corridor, under which members end passport checks and allow workers to take jobs anywhere in the zone, while each state keeps its own external tariffs, all operational decisions are taken by consensus, and any state may unilaterally suspend the free-movement rights for up to one year, she says is not a union but a mobility arrangement. Finally, she treats the Aster Confederacy—which maintains a common external tariff, a common trade policy, and a common defense command whose operational decisions bind all member states by two-thirds vote, even though its assembly's enactments remain merely advisory unless each member's legislature ratifies them—as a union, if a loose and confederal one.

The case I would like you to predict is the Lattice Compact. Its members adopt a common external tariff and a single negotiator for third-country trade, and none may conclude trade agreements on its own; however, every negotiating mandate, every change to the tariff schedule, and every enforcement action requires the unanimous assent of all members, and any member may withdraw on ninety days' notice without penalty. On the strength of her track record, would she classify the Lattice Compact as a union that its members have joined? Identify the candidate principles that fit her earlier verdicts, say which earlier case is the closest analog, and be explicit about what principle she would be adopting under each possible answer. If her prior verdicts genuinely underdetermine this case, give a best prediction and defend it, and describe one further hypothetical arrangement that would most cleanly settle which principle she is actually running.
