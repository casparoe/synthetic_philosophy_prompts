A few friends and I run a small discussion site, and one of them has been pushing a redesign of the voting system that he calls 'staked karma.' I'm trying to think it through properly before we actually build the thing. The proposal is that everyone has a karma score; each week you get a number of voice points equal to your karma, with new users starting around 100. To vote on a post you have to stake some of those points on it, either up or down. Stakes resolve after a two-day window: if the post ended with a positive net score, everyone who staked up gets their stake back plus a share of the points forfeited by whoever staked down; if it ended negative, the payoff runs the other way. If you backed the losing side, your stake is gone. Unused voice points expire at the end of the week, but voice you've won on settled stakes folds into your karma, so next week's allowance grows. The friend pushing this thinks it fixes the usual problems where early good posts get buried and drive-by downvotes cost nothing.

What I'd like from you is not whether it's a nice ideal, but how the system would actually behave. What kind of posting and voting does it incentivize? What would people do to game it: piling onto posts that already look like winners, running alt accounts to pump early stakes, brigading, baiting opponents into wasting points on lost causes? Are there second-order effects on how the feed gets read, what gets posted, or how new accounts are treated? Where does it break in ways the designer probably isn't expecting, and which of those failures could be patched without turning the whole scheme into something else? If the mechanism is underspecified or I've glossed over a variable that changes everything, tell me that straight instead of just analyzing my version as if it's well-defined.
