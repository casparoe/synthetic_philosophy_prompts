I've been writing a paper on metaphor, and over the past few weeks I've accumulated a set of commitments that each seemed right when I adopted them, but I've stopped being able to see from the inside whether they form a coherent position or just a list of claims I find attractive. I'd be grateful for a consistency audit before I present this to my seminar. The commitments are these:

(1) I accept the Davidsonian negative claim about metaphor: a metaphorical sentence means exactly what its words mean and no more, and there is no additional metaphorical content or proposition expressed by the sentence. On this view, saying of someone that she is the sun is literally false, and the real work of the metaphor — making us notice a likeness, reorganizing how we see the person — is something the utterance does, not something it says.

(2) I also accept the Lakoff–Johnson position at the level of thought: metaphor is not fundamentally a rhetorical ornament but a cognitive mechanism. Systematic cross-domain mappings — argument structured as combat, time as a resource, life as a journey — organize a substantial part of ordinary abstract reasoning, and conventional metaphorical language is the surface sign of those mappings.

(3) I hold a truth-conditional semantics: what a sentence means is given by its truth conditions, and everything a speaker conveys beyond that — conversational implicatures, imagery, emotional coloring — belongs to pragmatics rather than semantics.

(4) I think at least some novel metaphors are cognitively irreplaceable: understanding one puts a thought within reach that no literal paraphrase, however careful and detailed, manages to state, and a hearer who knew only the literal truth conditions would be missing something the speaker wanted them to get.

(5) Methodologically, I don't want to resolve any of this by appealing to an ineffable or merely gestural kind of understanding; whatever it is that the hearer gets in (4), I want it to be characterizable in the ordinary terms of cognitive psychology — representations, capacities, patterns of attention — rather than as a special sui generis grasp.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with which, and which apparent clashes are merely apparent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each tension say what would have to be revised and what the cheapest consistent fix would be. Finally, if exactly one commitment had to be revised to make the whole set coherent, which should it be, and what should it be replaced with?
