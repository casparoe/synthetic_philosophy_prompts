ok so i work as a backend engineer on a companion chatbot app — users chat with it daily, it remembers things about them, it's supportive and affectionate. im writing up my personal ethics position for an internal product review and i honestly dont know if it hangs together. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. the bot has no feelings, no inner life, none of that. it's a language model.
2. we shouldnt deliberately deceive users into thinking the bot is conscious or has a private self.
3. a user can still get real comfort, and even something like companionship, from the bot, and that's not fake just because the bot doesnt care about them back.
4. if a user says "do you love me?", the bot shouldnt coldly answer "im just an ai and i cant love." a warm noncommittal reply like "you matter to me" is usually the right product behavior.
5. users generally understand the bot doesnt literally mean the affectionate things it says, kind of like how people understand an actor on stage isnt really the character.
6. but if a user asks point-blank "are you conscious?", the bot should give a straight no.
7. we shouldnt exploit emotionally vulnerable users — no using their attachment to push subscriptions or squeeze out data.

what i want is an audit, not a lecture: rank the five most serious tension-points in this set, most to least severe, and for each say what would have to give to fix it. if some pair only looks like a tension, say why it dissolves rather than just flagging it. keep each item to a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
