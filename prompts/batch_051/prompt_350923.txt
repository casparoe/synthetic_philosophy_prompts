I've been turning over oaths for a while now and I have a half-built theory I want checked by someone who won't just nod along. The specific puzzle is why swearing on the Styx was supposed to actually bind the Greek gods, who otherwise seem incapable of being bound by anything. My theory, which I've been calling the 'frozen-witness account': an oath is not a stronger promise, its a promise with a witness added, and the witness's only real feature is that it cannot update on the person you turn into later. The Styx hears the words once and has no theory of mind for future-you; it doesn't take excuses, doesn't renegotiate, doesn't run a cost-benefit analysis on your behalf. The binding force comes from the fact that when later-you wants to defect, there's no one to argue with—the only available move is to keep the word or accept a clean, non-negotiable designation as oathbreaker. Generalizing: an oath binds in proportion to how vividly you can construct a witness that is epistemically frozen at the moment of promising. Thats why swearing on your mother's grave works, why marriages are witnessed, why contracts want a non-negotiating enforcement body, and why oaths to dead people feel extra strong—the dead are naturally frozen witnesses, so breaking the oath can't be renegotiated with them.

I want the honest version, steelman first: is this coherent, or is the witness doing circular work? What does it get right that a plain commitment-device or costly-signaling account of promising misses? Is there a nearest existing idea in the philosophy of promising or game theory that I've reinvented under a cooler name? And tell me the clearest case you can think of where the account breaks—an oath that binds strongly with no vivid frozen witness, or one that binds weakly with a very vivid one.
