I recently lost my sense of smell and most of my sense of taste for two weeks after a viral infection, and coffee was the first thing to return, but it returned strangely: it seemed both exactly the same and somehow displaced. One friend said the taste itself could not have changed because the beans and my receptors are returning to normal, so only my estimate of it changed. Another friend said the experience itself changed, because what it is like now is not what it was like before, and I am just rationalizing. This has made me want a careful statement of what is actually at stake in Dennett's Chase and Sanborn argument about whether the idea of an intrinsic qualitative property of experience does any work.

I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed qualia realist and a Dennett-style eliminativist about the Chase and Sanborn case and its moral. The document should include: a numbered section of claims both parties can sign about the case, about what subjects can and cannot directly report, and about the observational behavior at issue; a table of precise residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; and a list of cruxes—sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party—each paired with a concrete thought experiment, imagined neuropsychological result, or behavioral test that both parties agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Do not let the qualia realist retreat to a private, incommunicable property no test can touch, and do not let the eliminativist collapse the disagreement into a flat denial that people have experiences. I especially want to know whether the dispute reduces to a verbal dispute about the word "same"—same quale, same judgment, or same functional role—and if it does not, which crux is the load-bearing one. Where possible, draw on Dennett's own Chase and Sanborn intuition pump without quoting it, and show the parties working through the details rather than gesturing at them.
