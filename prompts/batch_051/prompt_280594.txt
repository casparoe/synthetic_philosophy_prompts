I'm a retired physical chemist — fourty years on the bench, now reading metaphysics for fun — and I keep tripping over a pair of cases I can describe but not name. I learned the textbook picture that a disposition like solubility or fragility is just a conditional: if you were to add water the salt would dissolve, if you were to drop the glass it would break. But real cases keep failing that picture in two different-looking ways.

First case. A wire is live: if a person touched it they'd get a shock. But it's wired through a safety relay, so the instant your hand closes the circuit the relay cuts the current. Touch it and nothing happens. The thing it was disposed to do, it doesn't do — and it doesn't do it because the very event that should have triggered the shock switches the disposition off. Is the wire live or not? Call this failure one.

Second case. A poison is lethal — it shuts down a key enzyme. But you give the animal an antagonist that occupies the enzyme's active site first. Now the poison is right there in the tissue doing nothing, not because it got any weaker but because something else is in the way. It's still lethal, still the same stuff; add a fresh enzyme and it kills fine. Call this failure two.

And a third case from my own trade, which may or may not be a third kind: salt dropped into water already saturated with that salt doesn't dissolve, but nobody would say the salt has lost its solubility — maybe here I've just mis-stated the test conditions rather than found a counterexample.

What I want to know is whether philosophers have seperate names for failure one and failure two, whether they're genuinely two kinds of thing or one kind described at different levels, and how exactly to draw the line between losing the disposition when it's triggered versus keeping it while something blocks the manifestation. I want the carving, not just labels: which of my cases goes in which box, whether the saturated-salt case belongs to either box or is something else, what a borderline case between one and two would look like, and whether the simple conditional analysis can be patched to handle any of this or has to be given up.
