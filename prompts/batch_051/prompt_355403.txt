Until about a year ago I assumed the debate over intent and effect in racist speech was mostly academic; now I sit on the moderation team of a large forum, and the same case keeps arriving. A post uses language whose literal meaning is unremarkable but which members of a particular group on the forum consistently read as a dog whistle, while the author denies any such intent and uninvolved readers see nothing at all. We are considering the following rule, and I want you to treat it as a mechanism to analyze, not as a moral position to approve or condemn.

When a post is reported under the racism policy, the reporter names the group the post targets. A panel of six randomly selected users who identify as members of that group then reads the post with the author's identity, account history, and all other metadata removed. If at least four members vote that a reasonable member of that group would take the post to express contempt or hostility in virtue of race, the post is removed regardless of the author's stated intent. The author may appeal by documenting an established non-racist meaning of the contested phrase in a community to which they can show belonging; on appeal, a panel of six members of that claimed community votes, and a four-to-two vote that the phrase is used there without racial coding overturns the removal. Separately, the moderation team maintains a public list of contested terms, updated quarterly, with each entry naming the group it concerns; a post containing a listed term triggers this panel procedure even if no one reports it.

Please work out how this mechanism would actually behave. What does it incentivize among ordinary users, among bad actors, among panelists, and among the staff who maintain the term list? How would it be gamed, and what happens to posts, phrases, and channels the panels never see? Which failure modes can be fixed by adjusting the mechanism without giving up its core commitment to audience uptake over intent, and which are baked in? Finally, on the conceptual question: if the forum runs the rule for several years, would its experience settle, sharpen, or merely relocate the intent-versus-effect dispute? I want a definite verdict on what this mechanism would most likely select for in practice — the audience-uptake standard it was designed to implement, mere evasion, or something else — and on what its trajectory would show about whether intent can be kept out of the definition of racist speech. Give me that verdict with reasons, not a menu of possibilities.
