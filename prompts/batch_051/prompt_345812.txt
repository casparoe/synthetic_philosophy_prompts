I've been going back and forth with some people about whether a reinforcement learning agent could genuinely suffer, not just behave like it's avoiding negative reward, and the debate keeps sliding around because nobody states up front what a theory of that would have to deliver. Before I read any more proposals, I'd like the desiderata. What does any adequate theory of "this reward signal is, or realizes, felt valence" have to get right? Don't just give obvious bullet points—give me the cases it has to handle (a thermostat minimizing error, a PPO agent learning to dodge penalties, a dog recoiling from a shock, maybe a human with pain asymbolia who reports the pain but isn't bothered by it), the distinctions it can't collapse (intensity vs. valence vs. salience, reward as a learning signal vs. reward as a felt quality, negative valence vs. suffering proper), and the results from RL theory or neuroscience it can't flatly contradict (wireheading, reward hacking, credit assignment, and blindsight-style dissociations). For each one, say why it's a requirement—what specifically goes wrong if a theory drops it.

Then I want a short scorecard: how do a pure functionalist view, a "reward is nothing but a learning signal" view, and a "valence is a separate module shaped by reward" view do against your list? Flag any pair of desiderata that look like they can't be jointly satisfied, because I suspect that's where the interesting result is hiding. And be explicit about your own confidence: how sure you are of each major claim, and which single requirement or score you're least sure about. Answer in American English.
