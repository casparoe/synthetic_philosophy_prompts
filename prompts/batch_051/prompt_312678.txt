I am the secretary of my sixth form's debating society, and I am drafting a proposal for next term that would turn our internal competitions into something closer to a scored sport. The committee's complaint is that debates are currently won on delivery and confidence rather than on whether anyone actually gives ground or updates in response to the other side. I would like you to check whether the rule I have in mind would actually behave that way before I put it to the vote.

The proposed format runs as follows. Each speaker begins every debate with ten credibility points. During their own speech, a speaker may formally concede a point that an opposing speaker has already made; each accepted concession transfers two points from the conceding side to the other. For a concession to count, the chair must be satisfied that the opponent really did assert that point in their own terms rather than as a caricature. An attempted concession that the chair rejects loses the would-be conceder one point and transfers nothing. A speaker may attempt as many concessions as they like, and a speaker's total may go negative. The side holding more points after the final speech wins the round; if the totals are level, the chair decides on the quality of the argument as before. The audience's indicative vote is still taken but never affects the result. Individual transfers are recorded on a term-long league table, and the top three speakers at the end of term are seeded for the regional competition. Chairs are elected members of the society, and their rulings are final.

I am not asking whether competitive debating is a good thing in the abstract. Treat this as a mechanism: walk through what it would actually do in a school where the same forty or so students debate each other every week. Which speaking habits would it reward and which would it punish, and what would the word 'concession' come to mean in practice by the second month? Who would climb the league table, and who would stop attending? What happens to the audience and to the motions that get proposed? Where does the system fail first, and can any of those failures be fixed without gutting the point of the scheme?
