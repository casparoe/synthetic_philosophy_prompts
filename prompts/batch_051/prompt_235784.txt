In What Is Art? Tolstoy argued that something is real art only if it transmits to the audience an emotion the artist actually lived through, and he assessed works mainly by whether that emotion is sincere, clear, morally serious, and accesible to ordinary people rather than to a refined elite. Now imagine Tolstoy being shown a modern text-to-image model: a person types a short prompt and gets, instantly and almost for free, a polished, emotionally affecting picture that could reach anyone with a phone. His own criteria seem to point in opposite directions — the accessibility part is satisfied better than by almost any painter, but there's no feeling in the maker apart from a rough intention, and the model itself experienced nothing. So what would Tolstoy have concluded about generative AI: are its outputs art, counterfeit art, or something else, and who is the artist — the person typing the prompt, the people who trained the model, or nobody?
