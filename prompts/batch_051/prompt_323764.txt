Suppose a government committed to a maximizing form of impartial consequentialism decides to blunt the demandingness objection by turning its demands into a draft with a buyout. The institution works as follows. Every adult citizen is entered into an annual lottery; those selected are conscripted into a twelve-month Impact Corps and assigned to whatever role a central agency judges, on the evidence available to it, will produce the most impartial good, regardless of the draftee's career, relationships, or local attachments. A draftee, however, may decline the posting at any time before reporting for service by paying an exit fee, fixed in advance by the same agency, equal to its estimate of the cost of recruiting, training, and paying a replacement of comparable skill from a national register of willing substitutes. Substitutes register voluntarily and are paid from exit fees; once listed, they may not refuse an offered placement, though they may in turn buy their way out by paying the same fee plus a small non-refundable premium. The agency's impact model, fee schedule, and placement algorithm are audited by an independent body, but the formulas themselves are kept secret to prevent citizens from gaming them.

Treat this as a mechanism to be analyzed, not as a policy to endorse or condemn. Walk through what it incentivizes, and for whom: citizens deciding which skills to acquire and how much income to hold; the agency deciding how to rate placements, set fees, and choose assignments; substitutes deciding whether to register; and the state itself. Where is the scheme gamed first, and what does gaming do to the quality of the good supposedly produced? Does the buyout in effect allow the rich to purchase exemption while the poor perform the sacrifice, and if so, does the institution still answer the demandingness objection, or does it merely turn an objection about overdemandingness into an objection about commodification? What second-order effects would you expect on wages, on the social meaning of the draft, on the agency's incentive to inflate cost and impact estimates, and on the moral psychology of citizens? Finally, distinguish the failure modes that could be repaired by redesign, and say how, from the failure modes that are intrinsic to any attempt to run maximizing consequentialism's demands through a price system.
