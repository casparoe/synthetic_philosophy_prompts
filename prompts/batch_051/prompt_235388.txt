Been going back and forth on Kant's Refutation of Idealism in the B-edition of the Critique of Pure Reason. His claim there is roughly that the empirically determined consciousness of my own existence in time proves the existence of objects in space outside me, because time-determination needs something permanent, and inner sense gives nothing permanent. I tried to lay it out cleanly:

1. I am conscious of my own existence as determined in time (memories, earlier/later, and so on).
2. All determination of time presupposes something permanent in perception.
3. Inner sense contains only successive representations, so no permanent can be given there.
4. Therefore the required permanent must be given in outer sense.
5. So outer objects exist.

This looks like a transcendental argument, but it feels like it might be smuggling in the conclusion at step 4. I can't tell whether step 2 is too strong, or whether step 3 is doing the work but also quietly assuming that the permanent can't just be the representing self rather than an outer object. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails and under what reading? If it is valid, what does existence of objects outside me amount to here?
