Last night I had a long argument with a friend about Richard Rorty, and it left me with a question I cannot settle from what I know of his work. She insisted that his antirepresentationalism comes down to the claim that there is no truth, only what a community finds useful to accept, and that this is an irresponsible thing to believe at a moment when machine-generated text is flooding public discourse and shared factual baselines are eroding. I defended what I take to be the fairer reading: Rorty was not denying that some beliefs are better than others, and he was not denying that the world can push back against us. He was denying that we need a correspondence relation between language and a nonlinguistic given to explain why some beliefs are better. On that reading, vocabularies are tools for coping with the world rather than attempts to mirror it, and justification is local—roughly, a claim earns its keep by surviving the challenges of the people one is obliged to answer to.

What my friend and I could not resolve was how this view would handle a case Rorty never lived to see. Large language models produce fluent, apparently knowledgeable text without having been answerable to any community of inquirers in the way he thought mattered. They are, in effect, machines for producing vocabularies largely detached from the practices of justification he emphasized. It is now common to say that such systems hallucinate, that they sever language from reality, and that democratic societies urgently need information technology that represents the world accurately. If Rorty were alive today, how would he most plausibly assess this alarm? Would he treat the demand for language that represents the world accurately as a relapse into the representationalism he spent his career criticizing, or would he grant that something genuinely new and dangerous is going on? Reconstruct his likely position as carefully as possible, grounding it in his actual commitments rather than a generic pragmatist caricature, and mark the points where his earlier views leave the question underdetermined.
