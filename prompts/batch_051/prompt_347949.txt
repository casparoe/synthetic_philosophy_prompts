hey, for my philosophy of mind seminar i want to put together a "which theory of pain's badness do you actually hold?" quiz. the audience is upper-level undergrads who've read about pain asymbolia and the sensory/affective split but don't know all the position labels yet. eight to ten questions, each a concrete vignette (asymbolia, phantom limb pain, morphine where the pain is still there but "doesn't bother me," flinching before you register the burn, someone with congenital insensitivity to pain who has the tissue damage but no pain, etc.) with two or three answer options. the scoring key should map answer patterns onto the positions in the literature: pain is intrinsically bad because of its felt quality, pain is bad because of its unpleasantness/aversion, pain is bad because of what it represents or commands (evaluativist/imperativist), pain is bad only in virtue of con-attitudes toward it, or pain is bad because of what it does to agency. the central fork should be the asymbolia case: if someone says "it hurts but i don't mind it," is that still pain, and is it still bad? don't just ask people which label they prefer; sort by how they actually answer the cases. flag any two leaves your questions can't really seperate, and note if some answer combinations come out empty and whether that's because a view is incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored.
