I'm a hobbyist reader on evolution, not an academic, and I've been turning over the old charge that natural selection is circular. The charge runs: "survival of the fittest" is a tautology because if "the fittest" just means "the ones that actually survive and reproduce the most," then "the fittest reproduce the most" is true by definition and selection explains nothing. The standard reply is that fitness is not realized success; it is a propensity — a probabilistic tendency of an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment, something like an expected tally of offspring. That reply satisfied me for a while, but when I tried to spell out what the propensity adds, I found myself arguing toward a conclusion I don't believe.

Here is the argument. (1) If natural selection is to explain why a trait spreads, the claim "fitter organisms leave more descendants" must be empirical, not true by definition. (2) So fitness cannot be equated with actual reproductive output; it must be a propensity to survive and reproduce. (3) But a propensity only earns its explanatory keep if it can be identified independently of the outcomes it is meant to explain; otherwise "it succeeded because it had a tendency to succeed" is the old tautology in new dress. (4) There seem to be only two ways to identify such a propensity: through its manifestation, as frequencies of survival and reproduction in a suitable reference class, or through its physical basis, the concrete traits and mechanisms that produce those outcomes. (5) If the propensity is identified through its manifestation, then fitness is still fixed by the very pattern of outcomes it was supposed to explain, and the circle has merely been moved back a step. (6) If it is identified through its physical basis, then fitness becomes dispensable: we could run the explanation on the realizer traits directly, and "fitness" would be only a label for whatever those traits produce. (7) So fitness is either vacuous or idle, and (8) natural selection, insofar as it explains through fitness, explains nothing. I take the conclusion to be false, and I would rather keep (1) — that selection genuinely explains — than almost any other link in the chain. Which step should I reject, and what do I give up by rejecting it? Please also say how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure about.
