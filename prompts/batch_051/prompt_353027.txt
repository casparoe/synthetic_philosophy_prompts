I work for a state governor's office, and I'm trying to work through something that happened a few months ago. At the height of a public health emergency, the governor used emergency powers to close the border and impose a lockdown. Some of the specific orders went beyond what the emergency statutes actually authorized, and a court later struck down the border closure as unlawful while letting the rest stand. At the time, the public strongly supported it, and polls still show most people think it was the right call. Our defenders keep saying it was legitimate even if it wasn't strictly legal, because it was for the public good. My legal training says that's a slope towards just ignoring law. I've been reading John Locke's Second Treatise, the chapter on prerogative, and it's been bothering me. As I understand it, Locke argues that the executive may act according to discretion, outside the law and sometimes against the letter of the law, when the public good requires it, and that the people rarely scrutinize such uses too strictly when they think the public good is being served. That basically describes what happened here. But it also sounds like a permission slip for authoritarianism. How do I make sense of it? Is there a principled way to distinguish legitimate emergency action from mere lawless rule, or is "legitimate but illegal" just a phrase for doing what you can get away with? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
