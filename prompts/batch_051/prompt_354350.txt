I keep running into the word 'meme' used for things that seem pretty different, and I'd like a working definition of it in the Darwinian-culture sense—not the narrow internet-image sense, though I do want it to say something sensible about internet memes. My vague sense is that a meme should be a unit of cultural copying with some kind of differential survival, but I dont trust that yet when I push on cases.

Please give me a definition and then run it against each of these, including the ones where you'd want to bite a bullet:
- a chain letter
- a recipe passed between cooks
- a single word like 'ok' or a new slang term
- a tune stuck in someones head that they never pass on
- a yawn rippling through a room
- a knot-tying trick someone worked out alone and never copied from anyone
- a vowel shift spreading through a city
- a law passed by a legislature and enforced by courts (clearly imitated sometimes, but top-down)
- the theory of natural selection itself (adopted because its persuasive and well-evidenced, not because we copy it)
- a soccer team's set-piece routine taught by a coach
- a religious ritual done by rote
- an internet meme (is the image the meme, or the joke, or the version-with-variations?)
- a computer virus that copies itself between machines

I suspect the word does two jobs: one descriptive (a unit that gets copied) and one that's doing selection talk (it replicates, varies, and survives at different rates). If you agree, give me both and say which cases move between them.

Where your definition gives the wrong answer or no answer, either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Tell me which two or three of your classifications you're least secure about, and why.

Make the very last line a one-sentence summary of your definition, on its own.
