Some friends and I run a small philosophy discussion group, and two of our regulars have spent months going in circles about punishment. One is a Dennett-style compatibilist: she thinks you can deserve blame and punishment even in a deterministic world, because desert tracks how well you actually respond to reasons, not whether your act was ultimately undetermined. The other is a Caruso-style hard incompatibilist: he thinks basic desert is impossible whether determinism or indeterminism turns out to be true (he says the luck argument lands either way), so retributive punishment is never justified, although he is fine with forward-looking incapacitation and a public-health quarantine model of criminal justice. They keep talking past each other, and we finally got them to agree to an adverserial collaboration. I'm the one who has to draft the document they would both sign.

Please write that document. It should have a numbered list of claims both can actually sign — not truisms like "punishment exists," but substantive claims about what determinism and luck do and do not show, what makes a response to wrongdoing justified, and where forward-looking and backward-looking considerations part company. Then a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated so precisely that the compatibilist and the skeptic would each accept the wording of their own side, with no strawmanning. Then the cruxes: the few sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. For each crux, include at least one concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show. One exchange that keeps returning is the manipulation cases — someone secretly engineered or conditioned to commit a crime — which both cite and draw opposite morals from. If you think part of their dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.
