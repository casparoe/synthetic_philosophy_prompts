Write an essay on whether Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is best read as a real proof of utilitarianism or as a conditional, almost tautological result. Lay out the setup briefly: each person has a von Neumann-Morgenstern utility function over lotteries, the social planner's preferences over lotteries also satisfy the vNM axioms, and the planner's ranking of a lottery is a function of the individual utilities it yields, so it respects ex ante Pareto. Then weigh the main arguments. The pro-Harsanyi side says the theorem forces social welfare to be a weighted sum of individual utilities, ruling out concave transforms or extra weight for the worse off. The skeptical side says vNM utility is just an index of risk preference, unique only up to positive affine transformations, and the theorem never tells you which scaling of each person's utility is the morally relevant one — so the additive form is compatible with almost any distributive pattern once people get re-scaled. Defend a verdict rather than ending neutral, and near the end say which argument or consideration would most likely make you change your mind, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
