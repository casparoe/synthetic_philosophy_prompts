Listening to public radio for years without ever donating: fine. Working under a union-negotiated pay deal but opting out of the dues: not fine. A healthy adult who skips vaccination and quietly relies on herd immunity: not fine. Using permissively licensed open-source code in a commercial product and contributing nothing back: fine. A friend who comes to every potluck and never brings anything: not fine. Using my sister's streaming password: fine. A small business that pays staff cash in hand to dodge tax while its vans use the public roads every day: not fine. Not voting in a safe seat but still counting on the democratic order to hold: fine. An academic who never agrees to referee journal articles but happily submits there own: not fine.

I keep feeling there's one idea behind all of this, something about when you're entitled to take without contributing, but the public radio case and the union dues case sit very uneasily together, and so do the open-source case and the potluck case. Is there a principle that gets all or nearly all of these verdicts, and how much complication does it need to save all of them? Is that person in the wrong in the same way as the fare dodger, or are they more like the public radio listener? And be honest about which of my verdicts your principle cannot accommodate, if any.
