A friend and I recently spent an evening arguing about the color phi phenomenon — the way two differently colored dots flashed in sequence are seen as a single moving dot that changes color mid-trajectory, as though the second color had been known before it appeared. My friend kept reaching for the standard political analogy: the choice between an Orwellian and a Stalinesque account of how consciousness handles the timing. On his telling, the Orwellian story is one of retroactive editing, like a Ministry of Truth quietly rewriting the archived past so that no trace of the original remains; the Stalinesque story is one of prior staging, like a show trial in which the evidence the court will inspect has already been fabricated. He used this to argue that the two models are empirically equivalent in principle and that there may be no fact of the matter about which one is correct.

I suspect the analogy is carrying more weight than he grants. I'd like an audit of it. Lay out the correspondence table (what plays the role of the original record, the censor, the court, the victim, and the moment of revision), identify where the mapping breaks, and say what the political picture quietly imports that a nervous system lacks or is not like. Then say whether his conclusion — that the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction cannot be drawn on empirical grounds — survives the disanalogies, or whether it depended on the analogy all along. If a better analogy would do the same work more honestly, give it.
