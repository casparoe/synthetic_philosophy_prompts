I'm in a course on the philosophy of law, and a classmate passed me a study sheet she wrote for the week on legal fictions. It centers on Sir Henry Sumner Maine's Ancient Law and begins by quoting the first sentences of Chapter II:

"When primitive law has once been embodied in a Code, there is an end to what may be called its spontaneous development. Henceforward the changes effected in it, if effected at all, are effected deliberately and from without."

Her study sheet then summarizes Maine as follows: legal fictions are, for Maine, deliberate falsehoods that judges use to change the law while pretending not to; the judge says that some event occurred or that some fact is true when everyone knows it isn't, and the only honest alternatives would be legislation or equity. She adds that Maine thought fictions were the primary engine of legal progress in every society, including modern ones, and that his theory implies a well-functioning legal system should eventually replace fictions with explicit statute law because fictions are a form of institutionalized lying. She particularly leans on the phrase "deliberately and from without" to support the idea that Maine saw fictional reasoning as an intentional and external manipulation of existing rules.

I suspect her summary overstates the dishonesty point and maybe collapses Maine's three distinct agencies of legal change—legal fictions, equity, and legislation—into one picture in which fictions are just inferior legislation. I'd like an accuracy audit: go through her paraphrase claim by claim, using the quoted passage and Maine's actual distinction between legal fictions, equity, and legislation, and say what a careful reader of Maine would accept, what is a common simplification, and what Maine explicitly denies or would resist. Please also say whether her conclusion that fictions are institutionalized lying and should be abandoned follows from the quoted passage or from Maine's broader position, and where the quoted sentence is being made to do work it cannot actually do.
