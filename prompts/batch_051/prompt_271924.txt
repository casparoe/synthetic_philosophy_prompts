So this is my situation: I host a small philosophy podcast aimed at the rationalist-blog crowd, and next week I've got on a philosopher (I'll keep him anonymous) who defends a pure pragmatic account of slurs. His line is that a slur's literal truth-conditional content is just 'member of Group G,' and everything derogatory—contempt, exclusion, dehumanizing stance, all of it—is a conventionalized use-condition: a norm against out-group use plus a register that marks in-group solidarity. He explicitly rejects hybrid/expressivist semantics and thinks people who put contempt in the semantic content are confusing a word's force with its content. He also likes to say that if the taboo evaporated, the word would be no more offensive than 'cop' vs 'police officer,' just a sociolinguistically marked synonym.

I need 8–10 interview questions that don't let him restate that. They should push on the cases that actually stress the view: projective behavior under negation, conditionals, and attitude reports; in-group reclamation and what happens when an out-group speaker quotes it; why different slurs carry different degrees and different stereotype-ish payloads if the semantics is only group membership; the 'reporting a bigot's perspective without endorsing it' cases; and what evidence would actually make him give the view up. For each question include a follow-up aimed at his most likely deflection—he'll definately say that whatever looks semantic is really just another layer of use-condition—and make sure at least one follow-up pushes on the point where 'it's all in the use-condition' stops being an explanation and starts being a restatement of the data. No bibliographies or citations, and no 'what drew you to philosophy' filler.
