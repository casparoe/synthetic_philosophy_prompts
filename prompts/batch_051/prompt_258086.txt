I am a teaching assistant for a course called Problems in the Study of Religion, and I have been trying to infer how my professor classifies borderline cases, because next week she will be asked to rule on a new one in class. She has never stated a general definition, but over the term she has made a series of case-by-case rulings. She has said that Theravada Buddhism is a religion, even though it has no creator god; that Wicca is a religion; and that Confucianism is a religion. She has also said that secular humanism is not a religion, that an ordinary political party is not a religion, that a gardening club with strong social bonds and annual traditions is not a religion, and that a professional sports team's fan culture, despite its chants, pilgrimages, and shrines, is not a religion.

Next week's case is a recently formed organization called the Fellowship of Common Purpose. It is explicitly naturalistic: it teaches that there are no gods, spirits, souls, afterlife, or supernatural forces, and that only the natural world exists. At the same time, it is organized in a strikingly church-like way. It has a founding Compact that members treat as beyond ordinary revision, a weekly assembly with candles, silence, singing, and a reading from the Compact, trained ministers who conduct rites for birth, coming of age, marriage, and death, a moral code with a monthly community-service commitment, and members who describe it as their all-encompassing identity rather than one activity among others.

I can see at least two rules that would both fit her past rulings. One is a substantive rule: nothing is a religion unless it is oriented toward something supernatural or transcendent. The other is a functional rule: a religion is a community that organizes its members' lives around shared sacred symbols and ultimate concerns, whether or not its beliefs are supernatural. I am not sure these are the only candidates, and I am not sure which she is actually using. Please predict whether she will call the Fellowship of Common Purpose a religion, state which rule she is implicitly running and why you favor that reading over the alternative, say what each candidate rule predicts about the Fellowship, and name one further case, not already on her record, that would most cleanly settle which rule she is actually using. Lead with your bottom line, your predicted verdict, in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
