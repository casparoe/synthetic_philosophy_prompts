In the Switzerland that actually exists, a 2013 initiative to abolish conscription was defeated by roughly three to one, leaving the militia army in place alongside the country's armed neutrality. Suppose instead the initiative had passed narrowly. With effect from 1 January 2017, conscription is abolished, and Switzerland fields an all-volunteer professional force of around 60,000 full-time troops plus 30,000 reservists on short-notice contracts, while the constitutional commitment to armed neutrality remains untouched. Predict, with reasoning, how the Swiss defence system and the army's place in Swiss political life look by 2035.

Address at least the following. (1) Recruitment and composition: can a volunteer force of that size be recruited in Switzerland's tight private labour market, and which regions, languages, and educational strata would it draw from? (2) Military effectiveness: what happens to mobilisation speed, reserve depth, and the capacity to impose costs on an invader that the mass militia relied on? (3) Civic effects: what becomes of the militia ideal, of the army's role as a multilingual school of the nation, of the civilian service that had been tied to conscription, and of the salience of defence in referendums and party politics? (4) Armed neutrality: does a smaller professional force make Swiss neutrality more or less credible to neighbours and to NATO, and how does it shift the internal debate about collective security? (5) Stability: under what pressures, in the manner of Sweden's 2010 suspension and 2017 reinstatement of conscription, would conscription return?

Give the reasoning explicitly rather than narrating a timeline: identify each mechanism and the incentives or constraints driving it, and attach a rough confidence to each major link, separating effects you regard as robust from those that are guesses. End by stating which single consideration or piece of evidence would most change your forecast, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; do not preface it or restate the question.
