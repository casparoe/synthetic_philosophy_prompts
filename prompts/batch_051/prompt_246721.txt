I keep noticing a structural parallel between clinical equipoise and the veil of ignorance, and I cannot tell whether it is a deep identity of structure or only a shared image of judgment made from behind a barrier. Clinical equipoise holds that a randomized trial is ethically acceptable only while the relevant community of experts is genuinely uncertain which arm is better. The veil of ignorance holds that principles chosen for a society are fair only if the choosers do not know which social position they will occupy. In both cases, a certain kind of ignorance appears to be what legitimizes a decision procedure whose outcomes real people must live with. Yet I suspect the ignorance is doing different work in the two cases.

I would like the parallel checked rather than merely named. Can the mapping be made precise: trial arms as social positions, expert uncertainty as the ignorance of one's own position? What would have to be true of both sides for an argument valid in one domain to transfer to the other — for instance, for a Rawlsian argument to establish the fairness of a particular trial design, or for a breach of equipoise to count as an injustice in Rawls's sense? In particular, name one thing the parallel predicts about clinical trials that turns out false, and say what that failure shows about whether the two ideas share a common root or only a superficial resemblance.
