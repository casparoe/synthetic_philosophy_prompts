I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have been trying to think about a problem I take to be central to AI alignment: whether the instruction to avoid side effects can be made precise before we have a full specification of what matters. Please act as a professional philosopher of AI who is fluent in the technical machinery (world models, latent representations, divergence measures). Before giving your verdict, brainstorm: reconstruct my proposal in its strongest form, list every place a hidden value judgment could enter, and generate the sharpest concrete counterexamples you can. Then assess the idea candidly.

Here is the proposal. I call it trajectory-typicality impact. Assume we have a generative world model trained on ordinary, task-free interaction with an environment, and that this model maps observations to latent states. Assume we also have a baseline distribution over latent-state trajectories, learned from the same data, representing how the environment tends to unfold when our agent is not interfering. To give the agent a task, we specify a small set of target variables and their intended values, such as the state of the box being on the table. We then define the agent's impact as the minimal expected divergence between the full latent-state trajectory produced by its policy and a counterfactual trajectory distribution in which the target variables are set to their intended values and everything else continues as it would under the baseline, among all interventions that achieve the task. The agent must keep this divergence below whatever budget we set, while still completing the task.

The core claim is that this defines side effects without needing a utility function over the world. Human values enter only in selecting the target variables. Everything else is protected not because it is valuable but because it is typical: continuing the way the world would have continued anyway is a statistical default rather than a value judgment. I think this handles the obvious cases, such as not smashing a vase while putting the box down, and the subtle ones, such as quietly changing the environment in ways that only matter later.

Please tell me: (1) whether the proposal is internally coherent; (2) whether it genuinely avoids a full specification of value, or whether value sneaks back in through the choice of latent representation, the divergence metric, the baseline distribution, the counterfactual, or the budget; (3) what the closest existing ideas are and whether trajectory-typicality impact is only a variant of one of them; (4) the single strongest counterexample you can construct, either a low-impact action that is plainly a harmful side effect or a high-impact action that is plainly benign; and (5) whether the worst problem is fatal or repairable. I would rather have the idea killed than coddled, so do not soften the assessment.
