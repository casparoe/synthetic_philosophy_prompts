I am drafting the "position" section of a research statement on embedded agency — the study of agents that are physical processes inside the environments they act on, rather than Cartesian agents separated from the world by a clean interface — and I would like a consistency check on a cluster of views I keep endorsing before I show the draft to anyone. I have lost the ability to tell which of my hesitations are substantive and which are merely terminological, so I am looking for an audit rather than a survey of the field. The six claims I keep wanting to defend are these:

(a) The agent–environment boundary is an abstraction, not a natural joint. An embedded agent is made of the same parts as its environment, and its model of the world is itself a physical object inside the territory it models.

(b) Subjective expected utility theory remains the normative core even for embedded agents. If an embeddedness consideration seems to recommend acting against expected utility, I take that to show that the acts, states, or utility function have been misspecified, not that expected utility should be revised.

(c) The right decision theory for an embedded agent is functional rather than causal: in Newcomb-like cases, the act with the best causal consequences is often the wrong act. The agent should choose as though it is selecting the output of a decision procedure, because the environment can contain predictions or simulations of that procedure, and conditioning on the token physical action does not screen off those correlations.

(d) Logical uncertainty should be represented with probabilities rather than handled by a separate non-probabilistic logic. An ideal embedded reasoner ought to hold a coherent distribution over unproved mathematical sentences, including sentences of a formal language that can refer to its own source code and its own credences, and should update by conditionalizing on proofs as they arrive.

(e) Ideal rationality is situated rather than omniscient. Because a map drawn from inside a territory cannot fully represent the territory including the map itself, the correct normative ideal is a bounded, self-referential reasoner that is honest about its representational limits, not an infinite, logically omniscient Cartesian agent.

(f) Corrigibility can be handled inside the expected-utility picture. We can make an agent appropriately open to shutdown or modification by writing the relevant preferences into its utility function, as when histories in which a legitimate shutdown signal is overridden are assigned low utility; this does not force us to abandon expected utility or consequentialism.

Please tell me which of these commitments are actually in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension once the relevant terms are read carefully, and where the stress disappears or reappears depending on how strongly one states a given claim. For any genuine tension, I would like it ranked by severity, with a note on what each side would have to give up and what the least costly revision would be. If you think the set is already consistent, say which reading of the disputed claims does the work. I am not signaling which commitment I would surrender, because I genuinely cannot tell where the problem is.
