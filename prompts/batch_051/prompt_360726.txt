I keep tripping over the phrase "radical self-transformation" in transhumanist arguments, and I realize I can't say what the "self" part is doing. Give me a working definition of what it is for a later person to still be me—or, if you prefer, for a transformation to preserve the self rather than replace it. I'd like a definition that counts ordinary aging from 20 to 70 as survival, counts a religious conversion that flips your core values as survival too, and comes down as a clear "no" on the case where your brain is destructively scanned and uploaded to a computer. Then run the definition through these: (a) a slow neuron-by-neuron replacement of your brain with functionally identical silicon over ten years, where you stay awake and conversational the whole time; (b) a drug that erases all episodic memories but leaves temperament, skills, and relationships intact; (c) a decade of therapy that changes you enough that old friends say "he's a different person"; (d) a procedure that merges two minds into one; (e) cryonic suspension and revival after two hundred years. Tell me where the definition gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdict.
