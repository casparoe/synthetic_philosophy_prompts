You are a philosopher of science who designs classroom simulations. Design a runnable 75-minute activity for a 14-student philosophy of science seminar on Kuhn vs. Popper and the question whether scientific revolutions can be rational. The activity should make participants feel the Kuhnian problem before it is named: stage it as a game with two rival "paradigms," each with its own scoring manual for a shared stream of puzzle cards, so that students live through normal science, anomaly accumulation, crisis, and the individual choice of whether to convert or stay loyal. Fix the incentives so that conversion is locally attractive but socially costly, and so that what counts as a solved puzzle differs between the two manuals. Then specify: materials and setup; exact rules and the payoff structure (use points or candy tokens, not real money); a minute-by-minute timeline; how to close the simulation and run the debrief; two or three specific debrief moments that map game events to claims by Kuhn or Popper; what will predictably go wrong the first time and how to fix it; and which feature of incommensurability the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Before presenting the final design, briefly brainstorm two or three alternative mechanics and explain why the chosen one produces the experience rather than merely illustrating it.
