I'm a software engineer, and I keep running into the same knot in postmortems. Two engineers independently make the same change with the same latent bug, on the same information and under the same pressure. One copy of the change sits in a code path that almost no one hits, and the bug never fires. The other takes down a payment service on launch day. I'm drawn to the idea that retrospective judgment should track only what was under the engineer's control at the time of the change — same decisions, same conduct, same blame — and that any extra weight the outcome carries is precisely the kind of luck a good review process should filter out. But I also can't make myself believe the two engineers are on equal footing afterward: the one whose bug fired seems blameworthy, or at least answerable, in a way the other isn't, and the incident record rightly treats the second case as more serious. Which should give way — the control principle, the intuition that the outcome matters, or do both survive only once I draw some distinction I'm missing? Give me the strongest version of each side, don't just map the options, and commit: which would you give up, and what would have to be true for you to switch?
