I keep coming back to the standard contrast between Peirce and James on truth. Peirce's idea is usually summarized as truth being the opinion that inquiry would settle on in the long run, James's as truth being what it is useful or good in the way of belief to hold. But re-reading How to Make Our Ideas Clear, Peirce makes a big deal of the fact that Descartes didn't see the difference between merely feeling clear and being clear: "The distinction between an idea seeming clear and really being so, never occurred to him." What I can't shake is that Peirce's long-run community consensus sounds like Descartes' subjective clarity moved up a level—a feeling of clarity spread across enough people and enough time. If truth is just the belief where we'd end up, why isn't that a psychological prediction about researchers, and why does Peirce get to criticize James for making truth depend on us? Is there a real difference between Peirce's "would be agreed upon" and James's "is good to believe," or is the Peirce/James split mostly about rhetorical self-discipline?
