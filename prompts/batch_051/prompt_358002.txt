Here's a position I keep finding myself defending, and I want to know how exposed it actually is. The accuracy-first program in formal epistemology is not just a tidy axiomatization: the one fundamental epistemic good is having credences that score well on a strictly proper scoring rule, and norms like probabilism, conditionalization, and the Principal Principle are binding because violators are accuracy-dominated or expected-accuracy-dominated. What would have to be true for that to be false? Skip the cheap line that scoring rules are artificial; I want the precise points where the view actually risks something. Which formal results would a defender have to treat as a decisive refutation rather than a fixable wrinkle, and what kind of case would be worse than merely awkward? Also: is the deeper threat external (a non-accuracy epistemic value like understanding or explanatory coherence doing irreducible work) or internal (the dominance arguments quietly leaning on assumptions about partitions or objective chances that are carrying the real normative weight)? And if the only way to save the view is to hedge it until it survives everything, tell me wether the resulting claim is still the one worth defending.
