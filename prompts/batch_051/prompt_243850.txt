Take this statement of the quantum immortality claim, QI. An agent runs a 50/50 lethal quantum trigger repeatedly — a measurement with equal-amplitude 'survives' and 'dies' outcomes, where the 'dies' outcome is instantaneously fatal. On a no-collapse many-worlds account, every trial branches, and for any finite sequence of trials there is a lineage in which the agent survives all of them, though the Born weight of that all-survivor lineage halves at each trial. QI says that, from the inside, the agent should expect to keep finding themselves a survivor — the only future for them to experience is one in which they are alive — the ever-shrinking Born weight of the all-survivor branch notwithstanding. What is your actual credence that QI, so stated, is correct? I want one number between 0 and 1, not a survey of positions. Decompose it into at least these terms, giving each a number and a sentence or two of justification: (1) the probability that no-collapse branching is the right account of measurement in the first place; (2) conditional on branching, the probability that a future survivor counts as 'me' in whatever sense first-person expectation requires — that branching does not dissolve identity so thoroughly that the inference to my own survival is blocked; (3) conditional on both, the probability that first-person credence should track the existence of at least one survivor-branch rather than the branch's Born weight; and (4) any other factor substantial enough to shift the answer. Show how the terms combine, then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most. End with the final credence as a decimal in the form \answer{0.xx}.
