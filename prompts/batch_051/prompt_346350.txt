I am a PhD student in game theory preparing a handout for an undergraduate seminar on combinatorial games. I want one worked example in which the standard strategy-stealing argument for the claim that the first player wins appears to go through, yet the first player in fact loses from the initial position. Build me such a game under these constraints: it must be finite, deterministic, and perfect-information, with no chance, no hidden information, no draws, and identical legal moves and winning conditions for the two players; it must be describable in one short paragraph and playable with pencil and paper. The failure must come from the ordinary structure of the game—for example, from the fact that making a move can be bad for the player who makes it—not from a rule that forbids the stealing move or gives the second player a pass. After stating the game, (1) run the standard strategy-stealing proof on it, stating the proof as it would normally go; (2) identify the first step that fails for your game and explain why; (3) show concretely that the first player loses from the start; and (4) say whether the usual repair—adding the assumption that an extra move can never be a disadvantage—is sufficient, or whether your example exposes a further hidden assumption. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence: the game you built and the verdict. Then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the request. Write in American English.
