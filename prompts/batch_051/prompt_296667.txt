After the death of her younger sister, a woman finds herself caught between two very different kinds of consolation. Her pastor tells her that God is not watching her grief from a distance; He is suffering with her. Her father, a retired philosophy teacher, responds that this cannot be right: if God is perfect, complete, and the source of all things, then nothing outside Him can make Him suffer, because being made to suffer would mean that something in creation has power over Him and that God is lacking something He needs. She finds the pastor's words comforting and her father's reasoning difficult to answer. She also suspects that the two men might not mean exactly the same thing by "suffering" or by "perfect." Without appealing to religious authority, help her think through the issue: must a perfect being be incapable of being affected by creaturely pain, or can such a being genuinely suffer with someone? Are the pastor and her father truly contradicting each other, or might the disagreement rest partly on ambiguity in words like "suffer with" and "perfect"? Lay out the strongest considerations on each side and explain what is at stake practically in settling the question. Please answer in American English.
