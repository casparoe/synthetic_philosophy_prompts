I've been trying to port Fearon's rationalist explanations for war over to AI development races, and I came up with a distinction I keep using in my notes. I want you to stress-test it before I embarrass myself.

I call the two kinds 'information races' and 'commitment races.' An information race is one where the main driver of wasteful competition is that each side doesn't know how close the others are, so everyone overinvests because they think they're neck and neck. A commitment race is one where everyone roughly knows where everyone else stands and that knowledge doesn't help, because nobody can credibly commit to slow down or to share the payoff later. The leader can't credibly promise to wait; the laggards can't credibly promise not to defect the moment they pull ahead. My claim is that AI races are now mostly commitment races, not information races. And if that's right, the standard transparency-and-disclosure governance agenda — model cards, compute reporting, verificaiton, info sharing — is aimed at the wrong mechanism. In a commitment race, transparency can make things worse, because it removes whatever uncertainty was keeping a slightly-behind actor from panicking and doing something desperate. I said this to a friend and he told me I'm just relabeling the security dilemma.

So: is the distinction already a thing in the literature under a better name, or is it actually my own coinage? Does it carve the world the way I think it does? And is the substantive claim about AI races true, or just nice-sounding?
