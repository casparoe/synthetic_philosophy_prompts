I'm an undergrad in a class on AI and global catastrophic risk, and for our final project we have to script a podcast interview with a guest who defends the fast-takeoff intelligence explosion view. The guest is invented, but the position is the real one. The assigned reading was I.J. Good's 1965 paper "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine." There's a passage early in it where Good defines an ultraintelligent machine as one that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of any man, however clever, and then says that since designing machines is itself one of those intellectual activities, the machine could design an even better machine. He ends up with the idea that the first ultraintelligent machine would be the last invention man need ever make, provided the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control. My prof fixated on the word "provided" because Good just asserts it and moves on.

I want eight to ten interview questions that push the guest on the specific gap between machine-designed improvements and a fast takeoff. The guest will probably keep falling back on the claim that human intelligence is the bottleneck. For each question, write the follow-up I should give when he says that. Make sure at least one question hits each of these: hardware/compute limits, the time cost of experiments, tacit knowledge that you can't just reason out a priori and have to learn by doing, diminishing returns to recursive self-improvement, and what current large-model scaling would have to look like for him to change his mind. No generic questions like "why do you study this."
