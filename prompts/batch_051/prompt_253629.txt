I keep noticing a pattern in how people talk about beliefs over time, and I don’t know if it has a name. Suppose I’m 80% confident now that the train will be delayed, but I also think that in an hour I’ll be 40% confident, because I’ll have forgotten the announcement. People say my current state is already defective, not just my future state. Or someone says: my probability for a recession this year is 0.3 right now, and I expect that by July it will be 0.6, and neither of those numbers is a mistake. Everyone treats that as incoherent, even though the person isn’t expecting to learn any specific new evidence that pushes in one direction. It’s obviously related to updating on evidence, but not the same: the weird part is the attitude toward your own future opinion. Is there a standard principle that your present opinion must equal what you expect your future opinion to be? If so, what is it called, and how do people handle cases like forgetting, drugs, brain damage, or being manipulated? Or is the principle contested enough that the real phenomenon is something more limited?
