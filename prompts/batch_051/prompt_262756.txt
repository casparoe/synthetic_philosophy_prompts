I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and for the last couple years most of my reporting has been on large language models. Lately I've noticed that my own on-the-record judgments about whether a system "understands" what it's doing are all over the place, and I'd like you to help me figure out whether they add up to anything coherent.

Here are my verdicts, roughly as I had them at the time. (1) When a translation model turned a pun that only works in the source language into a different pun that works in the target language — one it couldn't of seen in training — I wrote that it had understood something about how the joke works. (2) When a frontier model flunked a simple "which way will this stack of blocks fall" task that my kindergartner gets right, I wrote with total confidence that the model doesn't understand the physical world at all. (3) When a model walked me through a long, correct chain of reasoning but then botched a trivial paraphrase of the same problem, I told my editor it was just pattern-matching, no understanding. (4) When a medical chatbot answered my questions accurately and flagged when it was out of its depth, I caught myself thinking there was genuine domain competence there, maybe even understanding. (5) And when another model wrote a grammatical, on-topic essay but contradicted itself two paragraphs in and then confidently defended the contradiction, I thought: nobody's home behind the words. The pair that bothers me most is (1) and (2) — if the same kind of architecture underlies both, why does language get the benefit of the doubt while physics doesn't?

I don't really want to give any of these up, which is where you come in. Act as a philosopher of cognitive science or AI, think carefully, and start by brainstorming candidate criteria — robustness, systematicity, causal grip, self-knowledge, whatever else — before committing. Is there a single account of what it is for a system to understand a domain that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts do I have to overturn, and why? Then apply the account you settle on to a sixth case I keep going back and forth about: a model that scores at ceiling on Sally-Anne-style false-belief tests, but the moment the story wording changes a little — a negation added, an idiomatic paraphrase, a slightly odd tense — it collapses to chance. Under your principle, does that system understand other minds?
