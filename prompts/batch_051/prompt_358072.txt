I am a quantitative social scientist by training, not a philosopher: I am comfortable with causal inference, structural models, Bayesian decision theory, counterfactuals, risk versus ambiguity, and the language of exogenous shocks and policy interventions, but I have only a lay sense of Machiavelli and Renaissance political thought. I have been reading Machiavelli’s The Prince, and I want to know whether my own way of carving up the world distorts what he is claiming.

What I have in mind is the general statement in chapter 25 about fortune and virtù. As I read it, he says that fortune governs perhaps half of human actions but leaves the other half to us. He illustrates the point with a river that floods not because weather is beyond control, but because people did not build and maintain the dikes while the water was low. He then ends the chapter by saying that fortune is a woman and yields more readily to the impetuous than to the prudent or calculating.

Restate Machiavelli’s position in that passage in the vocabulary I actually use, as systematically and honestly as you can. That means treating the claim as if it were a substantive empirical or decision-theoretic thesis: outcome variance, exogenous shocks, residual randomness, counterfactual worlds, agent skill, control, preparation, expected value, risk, model uncertainty, rates of change across time. Which parts of the claim translate cleanly? For example, is his roughly half a point estimate, a bound, a prior, or a rhetorical gesture? Does virtù map onto a policy, an ability, a treatment effect, or something else? Does fortune map onto a shock distribution, a residual, a constraint set, or a confounder?

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the passage had to be fudged or compressed to fit my vocabulary, and which parts cannot be expressed in it at all? Is the difference between Machiavelli’s view and a modern decomposition of outcomes into skill and exogenous variance merely terminological, or is there a residue that my vocabulary cannot represent? And when you are done, tell me whether the residue comes from a limitation in my framework, or from Machiavelli’s view being less precise than its admirers sometimes imply, or from some third possibility. I want the translation to be faithful, but I want the residue to do the real work.
