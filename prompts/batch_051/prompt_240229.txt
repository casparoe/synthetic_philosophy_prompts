Me and a friend have been going back and forth for weeks about Max Black's two-sphere universe, and Ive been volunteered to lead the next session of my local metaphysics meetup on it, so I want to turn the argument into a sorting quiz for the group. Could you write a which-position-do-you-actually-hold quiz, nine questions, each a concrete case with three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions? It should sort people by what they will say about pairs of objects, not by whether they recognize a label, so no question should just ask whether someone accepts the principle of identity of indiscernibles.

The group is a mix of grad students and hobbyists, so keep the cases vivid and skip the jargon. Use at least these cases: Black's two iron spheres in an otherwise empty universe, exactly alike and some distance apart; a world containing only two indistinguishable peas; left and right gloves that are perfect mirror images; and a possible world exactly like this one except that two indistinguishable particles have been swapped. I want the questions to sort people on three splits: whether Black's universe is genuinely possible, whether being at a particular place is a real property of a thing or only a relation, and whether numerical distinctness needs a qualitative difference to ground it or can just be brute.

The leaves should include a bundle-theory position that treats objects as bundles of qualities and so says the two-sphere description is underdescribed, a triviality position that says the identity of indiscernibles only works if you allow identity-involving properties like being identical to sphere a and then becomes boring, a spatial-individuation position on which the spheres are distinct because their places are properties, and a bare-particular or primitive-thisness position on which the spheres are two despite sharing every qualitative feature. You can add a fifth leaf if the logic forces it, but if you do, name it and say why it earns one. Build from the logical space first: lay out the questions and the combinations of answers, then locate the actual positions in that space, and mark the empty combinations with a sentence saying why nobody is there, like a bundle theorist who also accepts brute numerical distinctness. Also flag any two leaves the quiz cant really seperate and why.

Dont format the thing: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, not even for the scoring key. Write it all as continuous prose the way you would send an email, with the questions running together as paragraphs. American English please.
