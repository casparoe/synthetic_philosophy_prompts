I help run a philosophy of mind reading group, and our session on eliminative materialism ended in a stalemate that has now become my problem, since I am leading next week's follow-up and everyone expects a resolution. One member, a neuroscientist, kept reaching for an analogy to settle the matter: beliefs and desires, he said, are like phlogiston — the substance eighteenth-century chemists posited to explain burning before oxidation was understood. On his telling, phlogiston was a theoretical posit of a false chemistry, and once oxygen made combustion intelligible, phlogiston was not reduced or smoothly carried over; it was simply eliminated. Likewise, "belief" and "desire" are posits of folk psychology, and a mature neuroscience should be expected to eliminate them rather than preserve them under some other description. The analogy was doing real work in the room: it was meant to blunt the intuition that it is absurd to say nobody really has beliefs, by making eliminativism look like ordinary scientific progress. Several people found this persuasive; others said the comparison was quietly importing something but could not say what; we ran out of time before anyone actually audited the comparison.

Since I am the one who has to present it, I would like a rigorous audit rather than a one-sided brief. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of phlogiston, combustion, oxygen, the chemists, and the theory change in the folk-psychology-to-neuroscience case — and then a list of the places where the mapping breaks. For each disanalogy, say whether it is merely cosmetic or whether it matters for the specific conclusion the analogy was being used to support: that belief-desire talk should be eliminated rather than reduced, revised, or left in place as a useful but non-literal idiom. End with your verdict on whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether some different scientific analogy would do the eliminativist's work better, or whether the phlogiston analogy was carrying the argument the whole time.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
