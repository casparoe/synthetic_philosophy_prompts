Last night a friend and I spent several hours arguing about Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities, and by the end neither of us had moved an inch. My friend defended the principle: an agent is morally responsible for what he has done only if he could have done otherwise. I kept pressing a Frankfurt-style case: a counterfactual intervener is prepared to make Jones decide to kill Smith if Jones does not come to that decision on his own, but Jones does come to it on his own, the intervener never acts, and we still feel justified in blaming Jones even though, had Jones been about to decide otherwise, the intervener would have stopped him. My friend took the familiar flicker-of-freedom line: some alternative always remains, even in such cases — a prior neural sign, a hesitation, a distinct intention — so the agent is never entirely without alternatives, even if none of those alternatives could have issued in a different final action. I replied that these leftover alternatives are too flimsy to carry the weight of moral responsibility. He replied that without some alternative, however thin, the compatibilist is just changing the subject. And so we went in a loop.

I am not asking you to settle the case. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. Is this a substantive dispute about what alternative possibilities must be like in order to matter — their robustness, their causal proximity to the action, their role as evidence of control — or is it a verbal dispute about the phrase could have done otherwise? Is it a disagreement about which considered cases get to count as data, or about a hidden standard concerning how much control is enough? The diagnosis should engage the actual moves I have described rather than survey every position in the literature. Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a sharper taxonomy of alternatives, an agreed threshold of robustness, a formal result, a more discriminating thought experiment, or something else? Be honest about whether the stalemate is a sign that the question is ill-posed rather than merely hard. Please answer in no more than 600 words.
