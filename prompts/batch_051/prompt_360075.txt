Write a villanelle about the liar paradox where the two recurring refrains carry the philosophy instead of just decorating it. Make the first refrain a version of the liar sentence itself, so the poem keeps saying the very thing its about, and make the second refrain the standard too-quick fix, like denying bivalence or restricting the T-schema. The middle stanzas should walk through the standard moves in order: state the sentence, derive the contradiction from the T-schema plus classical logic, try to defuse it by calling the sentence neither true nor false, then let the strengthened liar (this sentence is not true) sneak back in and wreck that fix. Save the closing couplet for what actually has to be given up, and pick one thing to blame: unrestricted self-reference, bivalence, or the unrestricted T-schema. It should be a real villanelle (ABA rhyme scheme, refrains in the right places, final couplet bringing both refrains together) and accurate enough that a logician reading it wouldn't wince.
