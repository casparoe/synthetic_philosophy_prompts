I have been working through Descartes' claim that nonhuman animals are unthinking automata, and I find myself caught between a theory I increasingly respect and an intuition I cannot shake.

The theory, as I understand it, has real systematic virtues. The language argument does genuine work: a parrot can be taught to produce words, but it never uses them to answer a new question or to say what it thinks, whereas even a child — or a person whose reasoning is otherwise impaired — does; so language looks like a reliable outward sign of thought that no animal exhibits. Likewise, the skilled but inflexible behavior of animals looks explainable by the arrangement of bodily machinery alone, without positing an inner life behind it, so parsimony seems to favor stopping at mechanism. I find this cleaner than I expected: it does not merely insist that we cannot observe animal minds, it offers a criterion and a reason not to multiply unobservable things beyond what the behavior requires.

The intuition is equally firm: when a dog whimpers after an injury, it is in pain. It is not merely emitting pain-behavior while nothing is felt; something is there, and the difference between the dog and a toy built to reproduce the same sound seems to consist in exactly that something. I would feel I was deceiving myself if I concluded otherwise. And I suspect the language criterion is doing more work than it can bear, because it appears to test for the wrong thing: it tests for discursive thought rather than for the bare capacity to feel.

I am not asking which side is right. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives way, what it would take for the Cartesian theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a defensible reading of Descartes on which both survive — for instance, a reading that narrows his target to thought and reason while leaving bare sensation untouched. How would I tell whether such a reconciliation is exegetically and philosophically genuine rather than a merely verbal retreat?
