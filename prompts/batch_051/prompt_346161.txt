Standard stuff. But while we were going through it I noticed something weird about my own judgments: every time I said "yeah, that's still a heap," I felt the bar for "heap" drop a little for the next step. By the time we got near the end I was calling things heaps that I would've laughed at an hour earlier. So now I have a little theory I want checked.

I've been calling it "boundary drag." The idea is that a classification in a sorites doesn't just report your threshold; it drags the threshold a tiny bit in the direction of the classification. Each yes makes you slightly more permissive, each no slightly more strict, because you're under local pressure to stay consistent with the verdict you just gave. If that's right, the induction premise — if n grains make a heap then n-1 do too — is not a single premise being applied to a single predicate; it's being evaluated against a slightly different predicate at every step, so the paradox is an artifact of running the series in time. Side prediction: someone who answers the cases in random order, or with long gaps, should have a more stable cutoff than someone doing the forced march.
