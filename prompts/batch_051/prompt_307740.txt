So I've been trying to figure out whether programming is a language of thought — like, when someone writes code, are they doing the same kind of thing the mind does when it thinks, or is that just a metaphor. I've been reading Ada Lovelace's notes to her translation of Menabrea's Sketch of the Analytical Engine, the part where she makes the point that the Engine doesn't have to be about numbers. She distinguishes the operations themselves from whatever the operations are applied to, and says if things like the relations of pitched sounds, or harmony, could be expressed in the notation of the Engine, the Engine could compose music. She also says the Engine has no pretensions to originate anything: it can only do whatever we know how to order it to perform.

The readings of this passage I've collected so far are:

1. The software reading: the real breakthru is separating the operations from the data, because that's what a program is — a set of operations that can be applied to different kinds of content.
2. The computational-representation reading: if musical relations can be encoded, then maybe mental content could be encoded too, and running the program would be doing a bit of thinking.
3. The anti-AI reading: her line about the Engine originating nothing is the original version of 'machines only do what they're told,' so nothing about programming shows thought is computational.

I feel like there's another reading of that same passage that cuts across these three, not a mix of them, and that would matter for whether programming is a language of thought or just a notation. Can you give me one? It should be genuinely different from the ones on my list, not a rewording or special case of them. Then tell me honestly whether the new reading is any good, and which of the four readings does the most work for settling the language-of-thought question.
