I am a PhD candidate in philosophy of technology, working on the value-theoretic commitments implicit in platform design. Please act as a professional philosopher with expertise in both well-being and the ethics of technology. I would like your analysis of the following practice. Large consumer platforms—short-form video, streaming, social feeds—evaluate their recommender systems against engagement metrics such as predicted watch time, sessions, likes, or retention, and routinely A/B test candidate models to move those numbers. The same companies describe their product as giving users what they want and, in some cases, ship digital-wellbeing features alongside it.

What theory of user welfare does this practice presuppose? More specifically: does optimizing engagement metrics treat engagement as welfare itself, as a reliable proxy for welfare, or merely as revealed preference? Does the practice strictly presuppose that more engagement is better for users, or only that engagement is instrumentally valuable to the company? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can, distinguish what is strictly presupposed from what is merely compatible, and flag the points where the practice is philosophically underdetermined. Then consider the limit case: when a metric is optimized without constraints, recommender systems can drift toward content that keeps people watching without satisfying any prior desire—a phenomenon related to wireheading and reward hacking. Can the practice's implicit theory distinguish giving users what they want from reward hacking? Finally, suppose the implicit theory were replaced by a view on which well-being requires, among other things, variety or meaningful activity rather than repeated engagement. What concrete design changes in how platforms select and evaluate recommendations would follow?

Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate reconstructions of the implicit theory before committing to the one you find most defensible. After your prose analysis, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: implicit_theory (a one-sentence reconstruction), strict_presuppositions (array of strings), merely_compatible (array of strings), underdetermined_choices (array of strings), reward_hacking_distinction (a one-sentence assessment of whether the implicit theory can draw this line), and design_changes_under_alternative_theory (array of strings).
