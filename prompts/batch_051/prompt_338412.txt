I'm a novelist, and my current manuscript has two characters who keep colliding over this: Mira, a retired copyeditor and old-school prescriptivist who will mark a sentence "wrong" without losing any sleep, and Ben, a descriptive linguist who insists there's no such thing as bad grammar, only different grammars plus social stigma. In the book they end up co-teaching a night class at a community college (long story), and for the course website they have to publish a short joint statement about what it means to call a sentence bad grammar. I need the actual document they'd both sign after a genuine good-faith adversarial collaboration; I dont want a transcript of them arguing, I want the text they'd publish together.

The statement should include the specific claims they can both agree on, and I'd love it to get concrete about cases like ain't, double negatives, singular they, between you and I, hypercorrection, and literally as an intensifier, since those are the examples they actually fight over. Then I want the precise residual disagreement, with each side's view worded so that side would accept the phrasing; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. For each crux, include the kind of case or evidence both would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. It's fine if the agreement section dissolves part of the original fight, but the part they still genuinely differ on shouldnt be watered down or reduced to a definitional quibble.
