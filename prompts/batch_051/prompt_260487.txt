I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been trying to think through whether agreements made before AGI exists have any real weight. I have a loose set of commitments in this area, and I want to know whether they form a jointly tenable package or whether I'm carrying around a contradiction. Here is where I've landed:

1. If two parties make an explicit agreement before AGI exists, under full information and without coercion, a later shift in the balance of power does not by itself dissolve either party's obligation. If a lab signs a benefit-sharing agreement and then builds the first AGI, it is still obligated to honor the original terms, even though no one could then force it to.

2. An agreement of this kind should be judged by what was known at signing, not in hindsight. If one party's contribution turns out ex post to have been worthless, that fact does not retroactively make the deal unfair or void.

3. It does not make sense to say that a person can be bound by an agreement made before they existed. The people who come into existence after the deal are not parties to it and never consented to it.

4. But if the deal determined which people came to exist, and those people have lives worth living, then no specific person has a legitimate complaint against the deal. Nobody is worse off than they would have been, because under a different deal they would not have existed at all.

5. A commitment with no enforcement mechanism is empty in practice. If one party can renege at zero cost, a rational party will renege, and calling the arrangement a contract changes nothing.

6. Nevertheless, I think it can be rational for several AI labs to enter a voluntary coordination agreement — for example, to slow a race dynamic or pool a fraction of compute — before any credible enforcement mechanism exists. I mean this as a claim about what each lab should do, not just about what would be nice.

I can't tell whether these six positions form a coherent package. I suspect some of them come apart, but I don't know where. I'd like an audit of the set: identify the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, state what would have to be dropped or modified to resolve each, say which repair is cheapest and what it costs, and flag any apparent conflicts that dissolve once the relevant terms are made precise.
