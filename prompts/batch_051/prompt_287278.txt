Okay, I need an actual working definition, not just vibes. When do several things compose a further thing—when is a bunch of stuff one object instead of just a bunch of stuff? I know this is basically the special composition question, and I've bounced between "always" (universalism), "only living things," and a pile of ad-hoc middle positions. I want you to give a definition of composition (or "being a single object") that correctly classifies the following cases, run each one through the definition, tell me where the definition gives a weird answer, and then either revise it or defend the weird answer. No citations, no history of who said what, and please lead with the definition itself in your first sentence, with no preamble and no restating of the question, then walk through the cases.

Cases:

1. The atoms in my left hand compose my left hand. (I'm confident this is yes.)
2. My left hand and my desk, at the exact moment they're touching, do not compose a new hand-desk object. If your definition rules this out by saying "no contact" or "no spatial contiguity," that can't be right, because they are touching—so what rules it out?
3. The grains of sand in a heap on the beach compose a heap. (Yes, right?)
4. The same grains, after I kick them across the boardwalk, don't compose anything. Same stuff, different arrangement—what changed?
5. The two halves of a wooden chair sitting loose on my floor do not compose a chair; the same halves glued back together do. What exactly did the glue change, and why is glue different from the touching in case 2?
6. A starling murmuration: one object, many objects, or is that a false dichotomy?
7. The Moon, the Eiffel Tower, and my left shoe compose something. (I want this to be no, but I can't name a principle that rules it out without killing cases I want to keep.)
8. A fresh corpse: the cells are still there, arranged more or less human-wise. If my live body composes one thing, what does the corpse compose, and when does the switch happen?
9. Bonus if it helps: over ten years I replace every part of my car and keep the originals in a pile. Do the piled-up originals still compose my car, or is that a different question (identity over time) from the composition one?

Also say which of your verdicts you're least sure about, and whether you'd actually bite that bullet outside a blog comment.
