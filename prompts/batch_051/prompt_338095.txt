Take the post-Dobbs fight over the Comstock Act and analyze it through the lens of desuetude — the idea that laws that are never enforced raise special rule-of-law problems and maybe lose some of their legitimacy. Heres the setup as I have it, so we dont burn time re-litigating basics: the Comstock Act has been on the books since 1873, and one provision makes it a federal crime to mail things meant or designed to produce an abortion. For essentially the whole post-Roe period, nobody enforced that provision against abortion-related mailings. After Dobbs in 2022, abortion opponents started arguing that the dormant statute actually bans mailing mifepristone or misoprostol, which would amount to a kind of nationwide abortion-pill ban just from an old law. The Biden administration's Office of Legal Counsel issued an opinion in 2022 reading the statute narrowly, so it only covers senders who intend the drugs to be used unlawfully. The issue got pulled into Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine v. FDA, and the Supreme Court threw that case out on standing in 2024, which means the Comstock question is still open and a future administration could enforce it broadly.
