You are a game theorist who specialises in extensive-form games and the debate over backward induction. Please think carefully, and begin by laying out the candidate principles you are entertaining before you commit to a prediction.

Over the course of a term, my game theory professor has given the following judgments about how rational players will play various finite games. Payoffs are written as (Player 1, Player 2).

1. Hi-Lo game (simultaneous). Each player chooses High or Low. If both choose High, the payoffs are (10,10); if both choose Low, (5,5); if one chooses High and the other Low, (0,0). Her judgment: rational players choose High; playing Low is not rational.

2. Entry game. Player 1 chooses Out, which ends the game with payoffs (3,3), or In. If Player 1 chooses In, Player 2 chooses Accommodate, giving (5,5), or Fight, giving (1,1). Her judgment: rational play is In followed by Accommodate.

3. Three-stage passing game. Player 1 chooses Stop, giving (2,2), or Pass. If Player 1 passes, Player 2 chooses Take, giving (1,2), or Pass. If Player 2 passes, Player 1 chooses Left, giving (0,0), or Right, giving (5,4). Her judgment: rational play is Pass by Player 1, Pass by Player 2, then Right by Player 1.

Next week she is assigning this case:

4. Four-node centipede game. Player 1 moves first, and at each node the player whose turn it is can choose Take or Pass. Taking ends the game immediately; passing moves play to the next node. The terminal payoffs are:

- Player 1 passes, then Player 2 takes: (1,3)
- Player 1 passes, Player 2 passes, then Player 1 takes: (4,2)
- Player 1 passes, Player 2 passes, Player 1 passes, then Player 2 takes: (3,5)
- Player 1 passes, Player 2 passes, Player 1 passes, Player 2 passes: (6,4)

I want to predict her verdict on the centipede game, and to know what that verdict would commit her to. Please do the following:

(1) Identify at least two candidate principles that are each consistent with all three of her recorded judgments.

(2) State exactly what each candidate principle prescribes in the four-node centipede game.

(3) Say which of the earlier cases is the closest analog to the centipede game, and whether that analogy actually favours one principle over another.

(4) Give your best prediction of her verdict, with the reasons for preferring it, and state which principle she would be committing herself to with that verdict.

(5) Propose one further game — not merely a relabelling or extension of the centipede game — that would settle any remaining ambiguity about which principle she is using, and say what each possible answer would show.
