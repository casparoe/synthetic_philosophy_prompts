My hospital's death-determination committee has a new case, and I want a prediction of what we'll do if we're staying consistent. Our recent calls: a head-trauma patient on a ventilator with no brainstem reflexes, we declared dead. A woman in a deep barbiturate coma with no brainstem reflexes but expected to recover, we didn't declare dead. A man whose heart stopped after a heart attack and could not be restarted, we declared dead without checking his brain, since his circulation was gone for good. A fully conscious ALS patient on a ventilator, we didn't declare dead. This month's patient had her cerebral cortex destroyed by a cardiac arrest but her brainstem is intact: she breathes on her own, has sleep-wake cycles, sometimes grimaces, but shows no sign of awareness and no realistic chance of getting any back. Should we declare her dead or not? I mainly want the reasoning laid out: which past case is the closest analog, which rule or rules fit everything we've done so far, and if they point different ways here, what principle are we committing to by going one way rather than the other? And what future case would settle which rule we've actually been running?
