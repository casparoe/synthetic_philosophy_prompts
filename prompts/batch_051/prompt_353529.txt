I'm giving a colloquium next month and I need a Q&A prep sheet more than another round of argument revision. My talk is on whether LLMs are agents, simulators, or something else, and I'm going to defend the view that the question is misframed: both camps want to locate the agency in the model, whereas I want to locate it in the prompt-conditioned policy plus the decoding process, which is neither an agent nor exactly a simulator. My rough line runs like this. (1) A frozen model is a mapping from token histories to next-token distributions, and it realizes different policies under different prompts and sampling parameters. (2) The states we'd ordinarily call beliefs, goals, or intentions show up only relative to a particular prompt-plus-decoding setup. (3) There is no stable prompt-independent state of the system that has those states. So (4) calling the base model an agent is a category mistake, and calling it a simulator is only slightly better, because that still treats the model as running one coherent simulation rather than as a space of policies that merely looks agential in particular runs.

The room is a philosophy department but with an unusual mix. There's a functionalist philosopher of mind who will want to hear why this isn't just old instrumentalism with new jargon; an enactivist who thinks cognitive ontology is all process and will say I haven't gone far enough; a Bayesian decision theorist who wants everything in terms of policies and priors and will press on the difference between a policy and an agent; and an ML postdoc who works on RLHF, thinks "just next-token prediction" is naive, and has no time for the word "simulator" outside a stats context. A few grad students will be there too, repeating arguments from blogs. If I have to loose someone, I'd rather it be the enactivist than the postdoc, and I need the functionalist to leave thinking the view is distinct from behaviorism.

Prepare me for the questions: give me the objections in roughly the order I'll get them, weighted by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way this exact audience would phrase them, with what I should concede and what I should hold, and a note on where each reply is softest. Include the question I'm praying nobody asks. Hard limit: the whole prep sheet has to be 700 words or fewer.
