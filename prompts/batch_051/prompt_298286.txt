i’m in a metaphysics of music class right now and we read this bit from C. S. Peirce, “Prolegomena to an Apology for Pragmaticism” (1906), as quoted in the SEP entry on types and tokens:

“A common mode of estimating the amount of matter in a MS. or printed book is to count the number of words. There will ordinarily be about twenty thes on a page, and of course they count as twenty words. In another sense of the word “word,” however, there is but one word “the” in the English language; and it is impossible that this word should lie visibly on a page or be heard in any voice, for the reason that it is not a Single thing or Single event. It does not exist; it only determines things that do exist. Such a definitely significant Form, I propose to term a Type.”

I’ve built a whole position out of it and I want to know what would count against it. Here’s the view. A musical work is a Type in Peirce’s sense, and a live performance is a Token of that Type. A recording is not directly a token of the work; it’s a token of a performance, which is itself a token of the work. So a recording is a token of a token, or an instance of an instance. The work itself doesn’t exist; it only determines the performances. I don’t think the score is the work either; it’s just another sign of the Type. I know that’s a stronger claim about recordings than people usually allow, because it makes the relation between a recording and the work pass through one particular performance. Two recordings of the same performance are related one way, and a recording and a live performance are related another.

But I’m not sure the view risks anything. What observations, possible cases, arguments, or intuitions would have to be true for this to be false? Not just the textbook objections; I want the cases like works created as recordings, electronic music with no performance, field recordings, studio editing from multiple takes, live improvisation where there is no stable work, and recordings of performances of works that no longer exist. I want defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Which of them would a committed type-theorist about musical works concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss as category mistakes or changing the subject? Also tell me honestly whether I’ve qualified the view into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that’s a problem for this kind of view. I definately don’t want to be accused of just insulating a position.
