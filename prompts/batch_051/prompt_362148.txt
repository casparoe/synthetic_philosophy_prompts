I keep landing on a middle position about memes and I want you to stress-test it. I don't think 'meme' is only a metaphor: cultural variants genuinely spread, persist, compete, and get selected in something that looks like a population process. But I also don't think they're full Darwinian replicators in the strict sense, because the copying is low-fidelity, there's blending and recombining everywhere, and there's no obvious particulate unit whose parent-offspring chains stay intact. So my view is: keep the selection talk, drop the replicator strictness — relax the requirements for what counts as a replicator instead of demanding cultural transmission meet the original ones. Tell me what would count against this. What would have to be true about cultural transmission, or about how selection works under heavy mutation, to push me either all the way to 'yes, memes are real replicators' or all the way to 'no, the whole thing is a metaphor'? I'd like the defeaters sorted by how decisve they'd be, an honest answer about whether the middle position is substantive or just unfalsifiable hedging, and some sense of which parts of the view are actually doing work rather than just vibes.
