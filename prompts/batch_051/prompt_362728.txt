I'm an undergrad in a philosophy of mind course and we've been doing self-deception, and I'm going in circles trying to find a working definition. The ones people keep offering ("believing something false," "lying to yourself," "believing what you want to believe") either let in to much or leave out the clearest cases. I need a definition that actually sorts the following cases, not just a dictionary gloss.

Cases:

1. My uncle, who's a nurse, finds a lump and keeps saying it's "nothing." He's seen enough patients to know better, and he quietly cancels a checkup and stops reading anything about the symptoms. Months later he genuinely, sincerely believes it's nothing.
2. My flatmate failed an exam, then spent weeks telling everyone the marking was rigged and, anyway, she actually understands the material. She threw out the answer key without looking at it. By now I think she really believes both.
3. A relative hasn't been told yet that the biopsy came back bad. They confidently say everything's fine.
4. A guy I know tells his girlfriend he was working late when he wasn't, and he's fully aware he's lying.
5. After a rough breakup a friend started repeating "I never cared about them anyway" every morning. He admitted at the start it wasn't true, but now he says it and seems to mean it.
6. Another friend gets drunk specifically so he won't think about whether his partner is cheating on him, then treats the fact that he isn't thinking about it as evidence that there's nothing to worry about.
7. A friend remembers an argument we both saw as if his insulting remark had been a reasonable response to provocation, and he genuinely can't separate that memory from what happened.
8. A parent refuses to believe their kid did something violent, even after a confession and witnesses. They keep saying "there must be context" and seem utterly convinced, not just putting on a front.

I'm confident 1 and 2 are self-deception and 3 and 4 are not. For 5 through 8 I have intuitions but no definition that handles them all. Give me your working definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it returns a verdict you'd defend even if it's surprising, where it breaks down, and which of the borderline cases you'd be least confident classifying under it. I'm also aware of the basic worry that to deceive yourself you'd have to know the truth and hide it from yourself at once, which sounds impossible. So if your definition handles the cases only by denying that, say, case 1 is really one person doing both jobs, just say that flat out.
