In a graduate seminar on the governance of multi-agent AI systems, the instructor keeps using one line to close down discussion of autonomous collusion: "collusion requires a meeting of the minds." She used it most recently to dismiss a case in which two separately deployed pricing algorithms, each trained to maximize its own long-run profit with no channel for explicit communication and no human instruction to coordinate, nonetheless learned to sustain prices well above the competitive level, with each policy coming to depend on the other's behavior. Her verdict was that the case may be troubling on other grounds, but it is not collusion, because nothing in the system agreed with anything. I want to pin down what that slogan is actually claiming. What distinct precise claims could "collusion requires a meeting of the minds" be making, spanning from the trivial or merely definitional to the substantive and contestable? For each reading, say whether it would genuinely support her verdict about the algorithmic case, and whether the reading survives scrutiny. Please lay this out as a menu I can take to her office hours, rather than selecting one interpretation.
