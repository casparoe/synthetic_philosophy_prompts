I am a novelist planning a near-future novel set inside an AI lab, and I want the alternative safety culture to be built from mechanisms rather than mood. My counterfactual is this. In a mid-sized country, around 2040, one well-funded lab adopts as a binding operating principle that every model above a clear capability threshold is presumed possibly misaligned. This means: no self-report from a model is ever treated as evidence about its own goals; every model output is handled as potentially manipulative input; no model is given affordances that would let it exfiltrate its weights or take an irreversible real-world action; and any safety measure is accepted only if it would still hold under the assumption that the model is actively and competently trying to circumvent it. Other labs continue with an alignment-first approach, letting improved evaluations and training increase the degree to which they rely on a model's apparent intentions. Predict, with explicit reasoning, how the adversarial-default lab and its rivals diverge over the following decade in research agenda, deployment decisions, personnel, and public standing. For each major prediction, state whether you regard it as a robust consequence of the starting rule, a guess contingent on the lab's scale or leadership, or a guess contingent on whether the models actually are misaligned. Conclude with the observations that would most change your predictions. I am less interested in whether the policy is wise than in the causal chain that would make the fictional history credible, and I do not need names, citations, or real-lab analogies.
