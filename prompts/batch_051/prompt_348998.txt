Suppose that in 2000, a newly formed country with a codified constitution, an elected parliament, and an empowered constitutional court adopts a founding charter whose framers believe that economic efficiency and individual liberty can be jointly constitutionalized. They entrench two clauses. The efficiency clause says: whenever every citizen strictly prefers a proposed policy to the status quo, the state is constitutionally required to adopt that policy. The sovereignty clause says: each citizen has an enumerated personal sphere — what they read, consume, display, and make inside their own home, and with whom they privately associate — and for any pair of alternatives within that sphere, the citizen's own preference between the two options must be treated as the social ranking of those options; the state may not rank the dispreferred option above the preferred one, and may not act so as to bring the dispreferred option about. The clauses are the framers' attempt to constitutionalize at once the Pareto principle and something close to minimal liberalism in Amartya Sen's sense.

Fifty years later, you are asked to assess how this arrangement actually played out. Give me a reasoned prediction of the country's legal and political development, attending specifically to the point where the two clauses collide: when citizens hold nosy preferences about one another's personal-sphere choices, the efficiency clause can demand exactly the outcome that the sovereignty clause forbids. Trace the mechanisms by which this collision gets resolved — which clause is read down, carved out, or informally abandoned; how legislatures and courts exploit ambiguity in the terms unanimous, preference, and personal sphere; how political parties and interest groups adapt their strategies around the two clauses; and whether the efficiency clause remains a live constraint on ordinary policy or atrophies into rhetoric. For each stage of the prediction, state whether you think the effect is robust or a guess, and what factual assumption would have to fail for that link of the prediction to be wrong. Begin your answer with your overall prediction in a single sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the scenario.
