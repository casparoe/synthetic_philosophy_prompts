I'm a retired systems neuroscientist — forty years of EEG traces and single-unit recordings, and I only started reading philosophy a couple of years ago, so I'll own my bias: I've always thought the original Libet setup was too artificial to bear the weight people put on it. Spontaneous wrist flexions with no stakes, lab students, a readiness potential that keeps getting argued about. Lately, though, I can't stop turning over a nastier version of the experiment, and I want you to reason through it with me.

Here's the counterfactual. Suppose that by 2030 a consortium publishes a pre-registered, replicated series of experiments that generalizes Libet. Not "lift a finger whenever you feel like it." People play a VR game where the choices have real money and social costs attached — sending a mildly embarrassing message, rage-quitting a negotiation, choosing between two job contracts. The experimenters train a decoder on each person's own EEG, pupil, and subcortical activity, and the decoder picks the upcoming choice four seconds before the person reports having made up their mind. The crucial twist: it works as well for choices people call deliberate as for snap choices, and it also predicts the decision to override an already prepared action — the so-called free won't turns out to be as unconsciously initiated as the act it suppresses. When the researchers nudge the early signal with transcranial magnetic stimulation, the choice distribution shifts, and participants later give perfectly sincere reasons for the shifted choices, as if they had decided for those reasons all along.

Give me your best prediction of how this plays out over the next thirty years, with the chain of reasoning visible. I want you to separate the robust effects from the panic: does the finding itself survive adversarial replication, out-of-sample decoding, and messier real-world tasks? Do courts and ordinary folk psychology actually change, or do they mostly quarantine the result? Which parts of your prediction are guesses, and what observation would make you abandon the whole trajectory? Don't just give me a on-the-one-hand essay; commit to a path and flag the weak links.
