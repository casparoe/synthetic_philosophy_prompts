I've been going back and forth on whether van Fraassen's Reflection principle is a real norm of rationality or just conditionalization wearing a nicer jacket. As I understand it, the principle says your current credence in P, conditional on your future self assigning probability r to P, ought to be r. The obvious problem is that if your future self is simply going to conditionalize on whatever evidence comes in, Reflection already seems to follow for free; and if your future self isn't going to do that — say you know you'll be sleep-deprived, or will lose information, or will be under some biasing influence — the bare principle seems plainly wrong. So the only version worth caring about would be a qualified Reflection that does real normative work beyond 'conditionalize on your evidence' and still survives the cases where you know you'll later be in a worse epistemic position.

What's your actual credence that such a version exists? Give me one number, then decompose it. I want the sub-questions separated, with a rough probability on each: how much rides on whether the forgetting/impairment cases are counterexamples to Reflection itself rather than warnings that it only applies to ideal future selves; how much on whether conditionalization already swallows any qualified version worth having; how much on whether its even coherent to treat your future self as an expert without that being circular; and anything else you think carries real weight. Show roughly how you combine them, tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move that one the most.
