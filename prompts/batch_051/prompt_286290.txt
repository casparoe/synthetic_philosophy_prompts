Consider this counterfactual. In 2028, after several near-miss accidents, the governments hosting the major AI labs sign a binding pre-AGI settlement. The deal creates an international authority with verified access to all training runs above 5e25 FLOP and to advanced chip-fabrication bookings. It also places an escrow claim on any system that later crosses a capability threshold, such as being able to autonomously replicate or conduct open-ended research. In exchange, signatory labs get guaranteed shared compute and a fixed formula for sharing the profits of any transformative system. Defection is defined as training or deploying such a system without depositing its weights and a full description; the penalty is automatic exclusion from advanced chips and energy contracts. Verification is imperfect but good enough to detect a large covert training run with roughly 50% probability within a year. Assume no signatory has yet built AGI, but two labs believe they are within two years of doing so, and both are quietly uncertain whether the other will defect first.

Predict how this bargain plays out over the next decade. I am less interested in a bare yes-or-no than in the mechanism: which parties' incentives shift as capability comes closer, how the uncertain detection probability enters the defection calculus, how the first near-defection changes the authority's willingness to enforce, and whether the deal unravels even though all parties would have signed its terms ex ante. For each major link in your reasoning, mark whether it is robust or a guess, and say what would have to be true for the prediction to fail. Lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
