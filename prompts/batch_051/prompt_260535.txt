I am preparing a novel whose protagonist has spent a decade on a failing undertaking—a magazine, a marriage, a civil suit—and must decide whether to walk away or see it through. I do not want the novel simply to ratify the textbook verdict that past investment can never count as a reason to continue. Before I put the opposing arguments in characters' mouths, I want the specification of what an adequate theory of when honoring sunk costs is rational would have to deliver. Write the desiderata: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect (for instance, sunk cost as diagnostic evidence versus as an independent reason, and past outlay versus the cost of exit), and the results from decision theory or psychology it cannot contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement. Mark which are non-negotiable and which are only the preferences of particular camps, flag any subset that cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does most to exclude the position that sunk costs are simply irrelevant. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring rival theories.
