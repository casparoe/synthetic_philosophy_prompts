After a long argument with a friend about whether metaphysical grounding is a single relation or just a loose family of dependence relations, I kept returning to an analogy and now I cannot decide whether it is deep or a distraction. In a spreadsheet, a formula cell's value is defined in terms of other cells: C1 = A1 + B1. The dependence is synchronic rather than causal—recalculation does not make A1 and B1 produce C1 over time; it merely recomputes a value that was already defined that way. The graph is asymmetric as written, acyclic in any well-formed sheet, and chains of formula cells bottom out in typed-in constants. That sounds almost exactly like the standard package attributed to metaphysical grounding: an explanatory, non-causal dependence relation that is asymmetric, transitive, and terminates in the fundamental. But the disanalogies look sharp: the spreadsheet graph is stipulated by a user, can be redesigned to invert the dependencies, and treats a circular reference as a fixable design error rather than a metaphysical impossibility. So which is it—is the spreadsheet formula relation a genuine model of grounding, showing that grounding is an abstract structure of asymmetric explanatory dependence, or does the analogy fail precisely because grounding is supposed to be mind-independent and worldly? What would have to be true of grounding for the analogy to illuminate it rather than merely restate it, and what false prediction does the analogy make about one side or the other?
