I've been reading rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums for years — mostly the calibration, double-crux, steelmanning side of things — and I've gotten it into my head that the reason public argument is so bad isn't that people cant reason, it's that there's no institution that makes good reasoning legible or rewarding. So I'm sketching a pilot for a "reasoning league": a spectator-friendly format where two people are assigned positions (not necessarily ones they hold) and a panel of judges scores them on things like accurately restating the other side, visibly updating or conceding points mid-round, flagging their own weakest premise, and resisting cheap rhetoric. Basically debate as a sport, except the scoring is designed to reward epistemic moves rather than persuasion. The talk's thesis is that turning argumentation into a scored spectator sport would improve public reasoning more than the usual everyone-just-be-more-charitable advice, and that we should pilot a league to find out.

The problem is the audience. I'm presenting this at a small conference on argumentation theory, and the room will be mostly rhetoricians and informal-logic people — the kind who work on pragma-dialectics, argumentation schemes, virtue theories of argument, and who think of argument as a cooperative repair mechanism rather than a contest. A few of them coach competitive debate and have come away pretty sour about how speed and judge-adaptation crowd out actual reasoning. I don't expect a friendly room. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a full defense: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way they'd put it (using their jargon where it matters), each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

One more thing: I'd rather get an honest prep sheet than a reassuring one, so at the end tell me which objection you think is the strongest test of whether my proposal is actually a good idea, and what argument or evidence would change your mind about it. In other words, where do you expect your verdict on the league to flip, and what's the most likely failure point in my whole pitch?
