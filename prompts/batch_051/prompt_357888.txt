At a party last weekend two friends of mine got into it over something our mutual friend Elena said, and I've been turning it over ever since. Elena is applying to a fellowship that takes about 2% of applicants, and someone told her "it's possible you get it." Dana jumped in and said that's exactly right: "possible" just means not ruled out by what we know, and it doesn't commit anyone to any particular probability. Possible and probable are different kinds of judgment, she said, and people confuse them all the time. Mark disagreed. He said if you'd assign literally zero chance to something, you'd never call it possible — so calling something possible is a covert probability claim, saying the chance is above whatever threshold you treat as live. Otherwise the word carries no information and people use it to dodge the question.

Dana came back with the dartboard: throw a dart at a board and any exact point has probability zero, but each point is still possible, so "possible" can't be a threshold on probability. Mark said that's a limit artifact from continuous mathematics, not how the word works between people. In real deliberation, he said, "I think the probability is zero but it's possible" is just a refusal to commit, and if possibility doesn't constrain probabilities, two people could share all the same probabilities and still disagree about what's possible — at which point the word is doing no work. Dana said possibility does its work before probabilities get assigned: you can't start assigning probabilities until you've fixed the set of live possibilities, so possibility frames the decision and probability ranks within it. They aren't rivals. Neither would budge.

I couldn't referee it at the time, and I still can't. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, where each of them overreaches, and what a correct account of how possible and probable relate would say about each move? I care less about who wins overall then about the point-by-point verdict.
