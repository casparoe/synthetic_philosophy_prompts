I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, so I have the standard decision-theoretic background — VNM axioms, money-pump arguments, Dutch books — but I've only recently started reading the philosophical literature on whether rational preferences can fail transitivity. A popular article aimed at general readers summarizes Warren Quinn's self-torturer thought experiment as follows:

"Warren Quinn's self-torturer is a thought experiment that shows rational preferences can be intransitive. A man wears a device whose dial runs from 0 to 1,000; each one-notch increase brings a clearly noticeable payment while adding an increment of pain too small to detect on its own. For any adjacent pair of settings, the man therefore rationally prefers the higher one: he gets a real gain and no felt loss. Yet at setting 1,000 he is in agony, and he would pay a large sum to return to 0 rather than remain there. His pairwise preferences form the cycle 1 > 0, 2 > 1, ..., 1000 > 999, and yet 0 > 1000. Quinn's conclusion is that the man is not rationally criticizable at any step and that transitivity is therefore not a genuine requirement of rational preference. The example also shows that the relation 'better than' can be intransitive, since each higher setting is better for the man than the one below it while setting 0 is better for him than setting 1,000. And because every local trade is a genuine improvement, the usual money-pump objection to intransitive preferences misses its target here; the self-torturer cannot be exploited."

I suspect the initial setup and the intransitive cycle are right, but I have an uneasy feeling about the later gloss on 'better than' and the claim that money-pump objections fail in this case.
