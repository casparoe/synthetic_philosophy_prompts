Write the document a naturalist about disease (healthy = statistically typical biological functioning, disease = a failure or absence of that functioning) and a normativist (you can't define disease without value judgments sneaking in) would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the deliverable itself, not a description of how they'd write it. Open with numbered agreed claims, the kind that aren't vacuous: things both sides grant about obvious cases, about what a concept of disease is supposed to do, and about the usual cheap shots on each side. Then a disagreements table, with rows for each point of residual disagreement: the issue in neutral terms, then the naturalist's own wording of their position (such that a naturalist would say 'yes, that's exactly it') and the normativist's equivalent. Then the cruxes -- the subquestions where actually settling it would visibly move at least one side -- and for each crux a concrete case or condition both sides accept as relevant evidence. Use cases like asymptomatic hypertension, infertility in someone who doesn't want children, or chronic pain without an identifiable lesion, and record each sides prediction for each case in advance. End with a paragraph on where the document is most likely to be cheating: which agreed claim or wording one side probably shouldn't have signed, where a disagreement is partly verbal, and what finding would most likely make each side budge.
