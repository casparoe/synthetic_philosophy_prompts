My brother sent me this argument and I can't find the flaw. (1) If 'white horse' and 'horse' were interchangeable, then whatever would satisfy a request for a horse would also satisfy a request for a white horse. (2) They aren't: if I ask someone to bring me a horse, a bay horse does satisfy the request; if I ask someone to bring me a white horse, a bay horse won't. (3) So 'white horse' and 'horse' pick out different classes of things. (4) But then a white horse is not a horse. (5) And the same reasoning shows a red car is not a car, a tall man is not a man, and a three-sided figure is not a figure. (6) That can't be right. I'd like to keep (1) and (2), since they seem undeniable, but I don't know what I'm paying if I reject (4). Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
