I'm drafting a position statement for a workshop on diachronic rationality and the normative force of money-pump arguments, and I'd like an audit of my commitments before I circulate it. The workshop is organized around the contrast between naive, sophisticated, and resolute choice, so I've been trying to commit myself on each of the standard moves rather than just survey the options.

Here is roughly where I've landed:

1. Cyclic preferences (A preferred to B, B preferred to C, C preferred to A) are irrational, and the standard money-pump argument is basically sound as a demonstration of that irrationality.
2. When preferences can change over time, the right model of the rational agent is sophisticated choice: at each decision node the agent maximizes expected utility given a correct forecast of how they will choose later, rather than naively treating each choice as if it were the only one.
3. I reject resolute choice as a requirement of rationality. I don't think an agent is bound to carry out a plan simply because the plan was optimal from an earlier vantage point, and I'm inclined to say the later self's preferences are authoritative at the later time.
4. Nonetheless, I think precommitment can be rational — Ulysses lashing himself to the mast is the paradigm case — even when the later self would, at the time, prefer to undo the precommitment.
5. I also hold that dynamic consistency is a genuine rational requirement: absent new information, a plan judged optimal at one time should still be judged optimal at a later time.
6. Finally, I think present bias is a defect, but only when it produces preference reversals or exploitable inconsistency; the bare fact that someone discounts the future hyperbolically rather than exponentially is not by itself irrational.

I can't tell where the real fault lines are. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, and say for each what would have to give. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent, and what that revision costs.
