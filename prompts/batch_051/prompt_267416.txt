I write a small philosophy explainer site for people who have never opened an ethics textbook, and I want to build an interactive 'Which population ethics view do you actually hold?' quiz. The reason is that ordinary people have strong gut feelings about whether a bigger family, a bigger city, or a bigger human species matters morally, but their intuitions pull in opposite directions once you start changing the average quality of life at the same time. I've noticed my own friends will happily say 'more happy people is obviously better' and then ten minutes later insist that a crowded, mediocre world sounds worse than a sparse, flourishing one, without ever noticing the tension. I'd like the quiz to sort readers by what they say about cases rather than by which label they recognise, so no question should ask 'is total wellbeing what matters?' or put jargon in the answer options. The leaves I want it to sort into are at least: totalism (a population's value is the sum of everyone's wellbeing), averagism (its value is the average wellbeing), a critical-level view (adding a life is good only above some threshold, with one or two different thresholds distinguished in the scoring key), a person-affecting view on which only people who already exist or will exist regardless of our choice count, a variable-value/number-dampened view (each extra good life can add value, but at a diminishing rate), and the position that adding a life barely worth living is neutral rather than good. Please include eight to ten questions, each a concrete case about children, cities, or species-level choices, with two or three answer options each. Make sure the cases probe: the familiar idea that a vast population of lives just barely worth living could beat a small population of excellent lives, whether a merely possible happy life's absence is a loss, whether average drops can be compensated by more people, and whether miserable lives can be fully offset by happy ones. Build the quiz from the logical space rather than from the names: lay out the dimensions on which these views come apart (average, total, threshold, existence as a benefit versus a precondition, the procreative asymmetry), locate the positions in that space, and flag any unoccupied combinations of answers with a note on whether that combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot reliably seperate from one another, and say why.

Please return the whole thing as a JSON object with this schema: top-level fields "title" (string), "intro" (string, one or two sentences shown before the quiz), "questions" (array of objects with "id", "case", "options" where each option has "id" and "text", and "distinguishes" listing which of the target positions that question separates), "positions" (array with "name", "one_sentence_description", and "typical_answers" mapping question ids to option ids), "scoring_key" (array of objects with "pattern" and "position", where "pattern" maps each question id to the option id that scores one point towards that position, and ties should be reported as 'conflicted between X and Y' rather than silently assigned), "unoccupied_combinations" (array with "pattern" and "diagnosis"), and "inseparable_pairs" (array with "position1", "position2", and "reason").
