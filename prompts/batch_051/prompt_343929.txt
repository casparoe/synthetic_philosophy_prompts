I'm trying to determine whether David Hume's analysis of chance in A Treatise of Human Nature survives the later arrival of objective probability in physics. In Book I, Part III, Section XI, "Of the Probability of Chances," Hume writes:

"The idea of cause and effect is deriv'd from experience, which presenting us with certain objects constantly conjoin'd with each other, produces such a habit of surveying them in that relation, that we cannot without a sensible violence survey them in any other. On the other hand, as chance is nothing real in itself, and, properly speaking, is merely the negation of a cause, its influence on the mind is contrary to that of causation; and 'tis essential to it, to leave the imagination perfectly indifferent, either to consider the existence or non-existence of that object, which is regarded as contingent."

The development I have in mind is this. Since Hume, probability has been formalized as a measure over a space of alternative outcomes, and later physical theories — the Born rule in quantum mechanics is the standard case — seem to put probability values into the fundamental law itself rather than deriving them from our ignorance of hidden causes. On at least one serious interpretation, nature would then contain irreducible chances: the laws assign each alternative outcome a probability even after every causally relevant fact about the prior state has been specified. Put in modal terms, Hume ties chance to a kind of possibility that is epistemic — an object is called contingent when the imagination is left indifferent between its existence and non-existence — whereas the later development treats probability as a measure over real alternative possibilities, not over our ignorance.

Does Hume's argument survive this development, does it need to be repaired, or is it now dead? Say exactly which premise of Hume's argument the development bears on; whether the argument can be restored as an account of evidential or subjective probability while conceding that objective chance may be real; and whether anything in Hume's own text blocks that repair or quietly allows it. I care about the fate of the argument itself, not about settling quantum mechanics.
