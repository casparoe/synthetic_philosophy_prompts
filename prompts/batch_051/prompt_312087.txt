I'm fine with a hefty soda tax, though I can't say why I'm fine with it while the New York large-soda cap struck me as overreach, when if anything the tax is more coercive across the board and the cap only blocked one size of cup. I'm fine with mandatory calorie counts on menus. I'm fine with an opt-out organ donation default, and I even waver toward thinking a system with no opt-out at all would be fine — though that wavering scares me alittle. I'm not fine with a competent Jehovah's Witness being forced to take a blood transfusion even if she'll die without it. I'm mildly fine with temporarily holding someone in florid psychosis who refuses care and is a danger to themselves. I'm not fine with a friend hiding a smoker's cigarettes when the smoker has explicitly said they don't want that kind of help. I'm not fine with a law requiring a three-day waiting period before a divorce, though I can't tell whether that's because it's paternalistic or because it's moralistic.

My working theory is something like: interference is fine when it protects people from harms they'd disown from the inside, or when the choice is being made under conditions they'd not want to govern them — addiction, myopia, panic, ordinary salience effects. But that doesn't obviously handle the soda pair, and it may not explain why the cigarette hiding is wrong but the seatbelt is fine.
