I'm a novelist, and I run a small workshop for literary translators and bilingual writers — 15 people, one 90-minute session. Design a game or activity that makes them feel, not just understand, the move from "there's no word for it" to the stronger claim that even a good paraphrase alters the thing itself — the sense that some things are untranslatable rather than merely hard to translate. I want them to come away seeing translation as a series of forced choices where keeping one thing means losing another, and that the translator should own that loss rather than paper over it. The game should run in a shared language only, have rules or small stakes, and the loss should hit them before anyone names it. Then give me a debrief that maps actual moments in the game onto that idea, says what predictably goes wrong the first time and how to handle it, and is honest about which part of the idea the exercise cant reproduce. When you answer, lead with the bottom line in the first sentence — the one-sentence design hook the whole thing hangs on — then give the full plan, no preamble and no restating the question.
