For a reading group I've been asked to write down my settled views on voting so the others can poke at them, and I'm not sure the list coheres as well as I'd like. Here's what I'd say today:

1. Voting is a civic duty, though not absolute — it can be overriden in unusual circumstances.
2. At the level of individual consequences, a single ballot almost never decides an election, so any argument that turns on what my one vote will causally change is close to irrelevant.
3. Voting is nonetheless important as expression: a vote can say "solidarity with this movement" or "none of the above, but I still showed up."
4. So I don't blame people who vote third-party in a safe state; I actually think it can be a truthful and even praiseworthy thing to do.
5. But in a swing state, doing that when one major candidate is much worse is self-indulgent — you should hold your nose and vote for the lesser evil.
6. Still, I think voting for a candidate whose policies you find abhorrent leaves a moral remainder; saying "I held my nose" doesn't fully clean it off.
7. And I think people who skip elections entirely act wrongly — not just unwisely, but blameworthy — even in a safe state where their absence changed nothing.

I'd like an audit, not a lecture on your own theory of voting. Go through the list and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look that way once you separate the causal role of a vote from its expressive role (or some other distinction), what the cheapest fix for the worst tension would be, and which single revision would make the whole set cohere most cleanly — and what it would cost me.
