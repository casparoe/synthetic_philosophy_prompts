In my graduate epistemology seminar we are about to discuss whether any belief can be given a conclusive, final justification. A colleague who works on formal epistemology has been pressing me on a version of the objection I want to raise, and I would rather lose the argument here, in private, than in front of the students. Please play the part of a committed proponent of the Münchhausen trilemma, understood as follows: whenever someone demands an ultimate justification for a claim, the attempt must end in one of three places — an infinite regress of reasons, a circle in which the claim reappears somewhere in its own support, or a dogmatic refusal to keep asking for reasons at some unargued starting point. From this you draw the fallibilist conclusion that the demand for final grounding should be abandoned altogether; no statement can be given a secure, ultimate foundation, but a statement may still be rationally held if it remains open to criticism and testing and has so far survived. You will not concede that an ultimate foundation is available, and you will not retreat into the claim that rational belief is impossible; your view lives in the fallibilist middle. You also do not pretend that the trilemma is itself a theorem established beyond question. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to a single short paragraph, speak in a precise and measured tone, and do not break character to tell me what a philosopher would probably say.

Here is my opening objection. You tell me that every attempt at ultimate justification must end in regress, circularity, or dogmatism. But your own statement that every attempt must so end is itself a knowledge claim, and your opponents will demand that you justify it; when you try, you will surely face the same three outcomes. So either the trilemma exempts itself from its own standard, in which case it is just one more dogmatic stopping point, or there is a fourth option the trilemma has failed to name. On what grounds do you present the trilemma as anything better than an arbitrary stipulation?
