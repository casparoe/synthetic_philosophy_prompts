For the past nine months I have been administering a shared calendar for a group of independent contractors, and the recurring failure mode looks to me like a real-world Luring Lottery: everyone waits for someone else to claim the scarce slot, then too many claim at once and the opportunity spoils, and each person insists they were just maximizing expected utility. I want a thought experiment built to a precise specification, which I plan to use to explain superrationality to people who already know decision theory.

Construct a one-shot, simultaneous, symmetric game with N ≥ 3 players in the Luring Lottery / Platonia family. Each player chooses Claim or Pass. Payoffs depend only on the player's own choice and the number of other players who Claim. No predictors, no communication, no iteration, no reputation, no precommitment, and no exotic physics. The case should not be a prisoner's dilemma with relabeled moves; the lottery/congestion structure must do real work.

The game must produce this configuration of verdicts:

1. Causal decision theory says Claim: given the causal structure and any natural prior a strategically sophisticated player would hold, the expected causal payoff of Claim exceeds that of Pass. Claiming may be weakly dominant, but if it is, that must fall out of the lottery mechanism rather than from a generic confession payoff.
2. Evidential decision theory also says Claim, but for a different reason: the act must be evidentially uninformative about the other players, so EDT's verdict does not ride on prediction, Newcomb-like copying, or treating one's choice as evidence about what others will do.
3. Hofstadter superrationality says Pass: when the players ask which uniform policy a group of symmetric reasoners should all adopt, the all-Pass outcome must be strictly better for each player than the all-Claim outcome, so the superrational move is the one whose universal adoption is optimal even though it looks worse from within the causal decision frame.

Then do the check. Give the payoff table, show the reasoning for CDT, EDT, and superrationality, identify the single structural feature of the case that makes superrationality peel off from the two self-interested theories, state what happens if that feature is removed, and give the strongest objection a defender of CDT would raise to treating the superrational verdict as normative here.

After that, append a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "players_and_actions": a string naming N, the two actions, and what Claiming corresponds to in the story.
- "payoff_matrix": an array of objects, each with fields "number_of_claimers", "payoff_to_claimant", and "payoff_to_passer".
- "verdicts": an object with keys "causal_decision_theory", "evidential_decision_theory", and "superrationality"; each value is an object with fields "action" and "reasoning".
- "structural_feature": the one feature of the case doing the work.
- "removal_test": what changes if that feature is removed.
- "cdt_objection": the strongest objection from causal decision theory.
