I'm on the grants panel for a philosophy department's undergrad research fund, and we've got a proposal that's split us down the middle. The student wants to run a cross-linguistic replication of Knobe's chairman study, plus two new vignettes that flip the script: instead of a bad side-effect (the environment getting harmed), the chairman knowingly brings about a morally good side-effect (say, a policy that also saves a local charity money). The student's pitch is that if the famous asymmetry flips or vanishes when the side-effect is good rather than bad, that's evidence the original 'Knobe effect' is really about how moral valence leaks into people's judgments, not about the ordinary concept of intentional action itself. Some of us think this is exactly the sort of boundry work between philosophy and psychology the fund should reward; others think whatever the data show, they won't touch the philosophical question of what intentional action is — folk application is just noise, and semantics isn't done by exit poll. I've been asked to draft the decision memo: recommend fund, don't fund, or fund with conditions, give reasons the student could actually respond to, and steelman the loosing side properly before explaining why it loses. Aim for maybe 800–1000 words, and write it in British English.
