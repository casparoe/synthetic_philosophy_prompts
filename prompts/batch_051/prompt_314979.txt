I am a novelist, fluent in the craft vocabulary of fiction — point of view, free indirect discourse, reliable and unreliable narrators, authorial intention, diegetic truth, verisimilitude, story logic — but not fluent in the philosophical machinery. Take Frankfurt's distinction between the liar, who knows the truth and deliberately misrepresents it; the bullshitter, who is indifferent to how things are and speaks only to achieve an effect; and the person who makes an honest error. Restate that distinction systematically in my narrative vocabulary, term by term, treating narrators and authors rather than speakers in general. Then give me a residue report: what translates cleanly, what had to be distorted to fit, and what has no counterpart at all in the fiction-writer's framework. Finally, use the translation to work through whether an LLM's hallucinations are best described as lying, bullshit, or mere error, and say whether anything left behind in the translation shows that the fiction-writer's vocabulary or Frankfurt's original distinction is doing the real work.
