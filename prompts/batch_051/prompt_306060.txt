For a paper I am writing for my philosophy class, I want to stress-test Boethius’s solution to the problem of divine foreknowledge in the Consolation of Philosophy, Book V. Boethius argues that God does not know future human choices from a point earlier in time; instead, God knows all things from an eternal present that is simultaneous with every moment of time.

Construct a thought experiment to the following specification: a normal person faces an ordinary, foreseeable choice; one being knows the choice in advance through simple foreknowledge; and a second being knows the choice atemporally, in the way Boethius describes. The case must make the person’s freedom harder to preserve under the atemporal knowledge than under the simple foreknowledge—the reverse of the direction Boethius and many of his readers expect. Constraints: you may not appeal to any divine attributes beyond the kind of knowledge specified, the agent may not be in an exotic or science-fictional situation, and the scenario must be one paragraph.

After the scenario, state precisely what each model says about when the knowledge occurs relative to the choice, explain why the case makes the atemporal model the greater threat to freedom, and identify which feature of the scenario is doing the work. End by giving one reply a defender of Boethius could use to neutralize the case.
