I teach an undergraduate history seminar on the French Revolution — about 18 students, one 75-minute session, and we've just finished the Terror. Design an in-class simulation that makes them feel the internal logic of denunciation: how fear of being denounced, shifting standards of revolutionary purity, and the need to display your own reliability can push a committee-like body to purge its own members one after another, including people who were allies the week before. I want them to come away seeing that the Terror's habit of devouring its own children may be less about one leader's paranoia and more about a trap that locally rational choices set for the whole group — so the pressure has to come from the rules, roles, timing, or payoffs, not from me explaining it afterwards. Give me rules, materials (cards or slips are fine), a points or survival scheme that doesn't need real money, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto 1793–94. Also tell me which of the big interpretations — class struggle, ideology, contingency — the games engine quietly builds in, what it can't reproduce about the actual Terror that I'll have to supply myself, and what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it.
