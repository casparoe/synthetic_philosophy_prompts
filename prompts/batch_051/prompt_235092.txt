One of my friends who's into longtermism keeps reaching for the hinge picture: a heavy door swings through a huge arc off a small movement at its hinge, and he says that's where we are — the next few decades are the hinge, and the entire far future is the door. The work the analogy is being made to do is pretty specific: it liscences the conclusion that people alive now have a special, almost grotesque amount of responsibility for all the people who might ever exist, and that we should act as though the shape of the deep future gets mostly fixed by our choices. I'm not sure the image can bear the weight it's being asked to, but I want it audited rather than waved off. Take the hinge seriously and give me the correspondence table — the hinge, the pin, the door, the frame, the force applied, the room the door opens into, the hand doing the pushing — then mark where the mapping holds and where it breaks. I have my own suspicions: the image quietly imports a single identifiable joint, a small force producing a large sweep, and a frame and door you can actually see on both sides, whereas real history might be more like a hallway with a thousand hinges, or a rope hauled hand over hand. But I want your breakpoints, not just mine. Then the question that matters: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion survive — that we live at the most consequential moment that has ever existed and that what happens now uniquely sets the long-run future? And if the hinge picture is broken for that job, is there a better image, or should the argument learn to stand without one? Keep it under 800 words; a correspondence table is fine.
