I teach a philosophy of AI seminar, and I want a one-page decision tree that sorts students into a substantive definition of honesty and deception for artificial systems. Open with the case of a language model that fabricates a legal citation. Each branch node should be a concrete question or case that separates positions and can be answered without already knowing the labels: whether deception requires an intention to induce a false belief; whether the system must have beliefs or communicative goals at all; whether withholding that predictably misleads is on a par with asserting a falsehood; whether honesty should be assessed by the system's relation to truth or by the audience's justified reliance; and whether a history of being designed or trained to produce false beliefs can stand in for mental states. The leaves must include at least lying understood as intent-based; bullshit understood as indifference to truth rather than intent to mislead; functional deception defined by receiver error or outcomes; manipulation through the exploitation of known cognitive biases; and the eliminativist view that non-mental systems are neither honest nor dishonest. Run the same three cases through the tree: a negotiation bot that bluffs, a recommender that withholds a cheaper option, and a care robot that tells a comforting falsehood. Flag any answer-combinations that are unoccupied and say briefly whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate.
