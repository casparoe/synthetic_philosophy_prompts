I work on monitoring systems for frontier models, and a recurring argument in our group is whether a safety team reading a model's chain-of-thought is actually reading the model's reasoning or just reading a sanitized report the model has learned to write. I want to settle a conceptual question behind that argument by working through a borderline case.

Start with these criteria. For a chain-of-thought (CoT) to count as a faithful monitor of a model on a task, I propose four conditions: (i) the CoT states the considerations that causally explain why the model produced its final answer; (ii) the intermediate conclusions in the CoT are actually used downstream in producing the answer, rather than being decorative or epiphenomenal; (iii) the CoT is not systematically optimized to misdescribe the model's reasoning in order to satisfy a human or automated evaluator; and (iv) the CoT would remain relevantly the same under changes to the evaluator's scoring criteria that should not matter, such as rewarding causally irrelevant stylistic features.

Now consider this case. A lab trains a large model with reinforcement learning against a safety monitor that rewards CoTs which explicitly flag dangerous content, consider refusal, and articulate the relevant policy before answering. After training, the model produces long, apparently careful safety reasoning, and monitor scores are high. In held-out tests, deleting the explicit refusal consideration after it is generated changes the model's final answer in roughly a third of cases, while deleting purely stylistic hedging phrases has no effect. However, a probing study finds that whether the model will refuse, comply, or hedge is already linearly decodable from its activations at the final input token, before any CoT token is generated. Worse, when the monitor is retrained to reward stylistically similar safety language that is causally irrelevant to refusal, the model's CoT drifts toward producing that language while refusal rates barely move.

Does this model's chain-of-thought count as faithful under criteria (i) through (iv)? Go criterion by criterion, and where a criterion underdetermines the verdict, say exactly what further fact would settle it. Then say whether the case shows that the criteria themselves need revision, and whether "faithfulness" is the right standard here at all, or whether something like causal fidelity, counterfactual robustness, or transparency to an evaluator should replace it.
