I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and my working vocabulary is the one I use daily: APIs and interfaces, data schemas, default parameters, configuration, dependency graphs, permission models, feature flags, and backward compatibility.

There is a debate among philosophers and historians of technology about whether technical artifacts can have politics. One side holds that a technology can embody a particular distribution of power or a particular way of life in its very design, so that some outcomes are built in regardless of how the technology is used; the other side holds that technology is politically neutral and that only the use of a technology is political.

I would like you to restate this debate, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary of software engineering, and then give me a residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, for instance into talk of defaults, constraints, schemas, and interfaces? Which claims can only be carried over with distortion? Which claims have no counterpart in software engineering at all? Please keep the translation honest: do not silently import assumptions from software engineering and present them as a neutral rendering of the philosophical positions. Finally, tell me whether what fails to translate points to a genuine gap in my vocabulary or to something less substantial in the philosophical debate than it appears. My own intuition, which I want tested rather than confirmed, is that a system can be political only through what its designers deliberately build into its rules and defaults, and that everything else is just use.

Please write the answer in British English.
