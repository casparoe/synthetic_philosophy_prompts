I keep bouncing off the litterature on pleasure because every account seems to fix one version of the heterogeneity problem by quietly changing the subject—one minute it's a felt quality, the next it's liking, the next it's just whatever you'd rather be having. Before I read any more of it, I want the desiderata: what does any adequate account have to do if it's going to say what all pleasures have in common, where the account has to carry real weight inside a hedonist theory of well-being? I don't want a survey or a reading list; I want the requirements, with the case or consideration behind each, and for each one a flag on whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's taste. Make sure the hard cases show up somewhere: relief when pain stops, malicious pleasure, the pleasure of understanding something, background contentment that isn't felt as a distinct buzz, and pleasures we value but can't locate in any particular sensation. In particular I want to know whether the list is itself unstable—are there two or three requirements that each look compulsory but that no live view can satisfy at once? Then a short scorecard of the felt-quality view, the attitudinal view, and a functionalist view against the list; keep the weight on the criteria, not the scorecard. Bottom line first: give your overall verdict on the desiderata in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate the question. And separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this—please don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
