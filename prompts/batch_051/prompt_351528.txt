In the Nicomachean Ethics VI.5, Aristotle says:

“But no one deliberates about things that cannot vary, nor about things not within his power to do. Hence inasmuch as scientific knowledge involves demonstration, whereas things whose fundamental principles are variable are not capable of demonstration, because everything about them is variable, and inasmuch as one cannot deliberate about things that are of necessity, it follows that Prudence is not the same as Science. Nor can it be the same as Art. It is not Science, because matters of conduct admit of variation; and not Art, because doing and making are generically different, since making aims at an end distinct from the act of making, whereas in doing the end cannot be other than the act itself: doing well is in itself the end.”

I need someone to help me work through a case this passage obviously wasn't written for. My friend runs a small nonprofit that gives emergency cash grants to people who would otherwise be evicted. They are building a machine learning system that reads application files and makes the final yes or no decision, because they say caseworkers are inconsistent and biased. The model ranks people by a predicted “one-year flourishing score” and the staff mostly rubber-stamps whatever it outputs. The team's pitch is: this isn't art, because they aren't making anything, and it isn't science, because human welfare is variable and particular, so it must be prudence—just mechanized prudence.

But the passage says no one deliberates about things not within his power to do. The model can't spend the money, call the landlord, or change its mind a week later. Its “doing well” is just getting the classification right on held-out data. So it feels like the model can't be prudent. But if it's not prudence, not art, and not science, then what is happening when the humans defer to it? Does the passage imply that the human operator who rubber-stamps has stopped deliberating too, because the actual decision is no longer in her power? Or is the operators prudence just displaced, not eliminated?

Don't just say what Aristotle would think of computers. Argue it from the text, from the purpose of the distinction between prudence, art, and science, and from what Aristotle would have said if asked about a machine that ranks by predicted flourishing. Tell me which approach should win here and why.
