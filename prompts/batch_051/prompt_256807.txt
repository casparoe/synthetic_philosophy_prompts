I cant stop thinking about an asymmetry in how reproductive labor gets regulated, and I want to know if there's a real principle behind it or just a gut reaction. In much of Europe, egg donation is legal and the donor gets compensated — officially for her expenses and inconvenience, but it's money — while commercial surrogacy is banned (or at least the contract is unenforceable). The U.S. mostly allows both: a healthy twenty-two-year-old can get thousands of dollars for a cycle of hormones and a surgical retrieval, and a surrogate can get paid for carrying a pregnancy, and hardly anyone treats the first as the same kind of moral problem as the second. Even in the U.S., though, lots of people who are fine with paid egg donation are queasy about paid surrogacy. So the line isn't just some European quirk.

I'm trying to figure out whether anything actually justifies drawing the line between gametes and gestation. The obvious candidates seem to fail. Risk? Egg donation means weeks of hormone injections and a minor surgery; surrogacy means pregnancy and childbirth. If risk were doing the work, you'd either ban both or allow both — the asymmetry doesn't track it. Exploitation? Both can exploit poor women, and both can be perfectly fine. Commodification? Both put money into the reproductive system. The only real difference I can see is that the surrogate grows and hands over a whole child, while the egg donor hands over a cell. But is that a moral difference or just the "ick" of a baby being involved?

So give me the strongest principle you can find that lets someone keep compensated egg donation while banning commercial surrogacy, and then stress-test it. Run it against nearby cases: sperm donation, plasma donation, paying people for risky work like commercial fishing, paying a birth mother's medical expenses in an adoption, hiring a nanny to raise a kid she'll never see again. If the principle can't survive those, say so, and tell me which way the law should move: ban the egg payments, legalize the surrogacy payments, or keep the inconsistency because the line, even if unprincipled, is doing some social work worth keeping.
