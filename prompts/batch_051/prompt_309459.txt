my friend dana is our group's unofficial judge of flakiness and small-promise violations. she's weirdly consistent, so i started writing her verdicts down. here they are:

- alex was supposed to send a reference letter monday, sent it wednesday, and the application went in tuesday missing the strongest letter. dana: real wrong, not catastrophic but real, and "i was busy" doesnt excuse it.

- beth was watering carla's plants for a week, missed one day because of a migraine, told carla when she got back, everything was alive. dana: not a wrong at all, just life.

- dev promised emma coffee saturday, texted two hours before that a work thing came up; emma hadn't left the house yet. dana: not a wrong, these things happen.

- gina promised to drive hussein to the airport; her car wouldn't start, she called right away, he got a cab and still made the flight. dana: bad luck, not blameworthy.

- jordan is always twenty minutes late and says everyone knows that about him. dana: still wrong, people planned around the time he agreed to, and "this is just how i am" isnt the defense he thinks.

- leila backed out of helping maya move because leila's sister had a crisis; she told maya friday night and maya found someone else. dana: no wrong at all.

- marcus bailed on a dinner party an hour before for a last-minute concert ticket, after the host had cooked for six. dana: obviously wrong — not mainly because of the food, but because it treats the host's evening as a backup plan.

new case: priya promised her old roommate she'd spend next saturday helping him assemble ikea furniture. three days beforehand, she gets offered a free spot on a long-weekend cabin trip with her two closest friends, who are only in the country that week. she takes it, texts the roommate immediately, and he genuinely doesn't mind — he's already found someone else. what does dana say?

and which rule is she actually running: (a) you've only wronged someone if they actually got burned, (b) what matters is whether the breaking was chosen — a better offer is its own kind of wrong — or (c) what matters is whether you gave timely notice and a real chance to replan? give me your best prediction with the reason, and tell me what future case (or pair of cases) i should bring her next to settle which rule she's using.
