I have been running a research group on the safety of advanced AI systems, and for two years the same debate has stalled in the same place: should these systems be supervised on their process or on their outcomes? By process-based supervision I mean giving training feedback and evaluation on the intermediate steps a system takes — the chain of thought, the sub-decisions, the reasoning trace — so that credit and blame attach to how a result is reached. By outcome-based supervision I mean giving feedback only on the terminal result: the final answer, the completed task, the real-world consequence, whatever shows up only after the reasoning is over. The process side says outcomes are sparse and arrive too late; a system can reach the right answer for the wrong reason, and by the time that error shows up in behavior the harm has already been done. The outcome side says the process is not an independently observable fact but another output of the very system we are trying to supervise; it can be insincere, and rewarding it just selects for systems that produce convincing justifications rather than safer behavior. Everyone in the group accepts both of these points, and nobody changes sides.

My working theory is that the impasse is only superficially empirical. If a study showed that one approach predicted downstream reliability slightly better than the other, I believe each side would say the study measured the wrong thing. I suspect the real disagreement is either about what a process is when our only access to it is a text generated by the same system, or about whether transparency has value in itself rather than merely as an instrument for getting good outcomes. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: what kind of disagreement is this, exactly — empirical, conceptual, verbal, or a clash of bedrock values — and what does each side need to assume for its position to hold together? Given that diagnosis, please say what would count as progress, whether a distinction, a theorem, an experiment, a redefinition of supervision, or a change of question, or argue that the question is ill-posed and no progress should be expected. Please write your answer in British English.
