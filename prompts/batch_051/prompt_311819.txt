I am a reinforcement learning researcher. I am fluent in finite MDPs and POMDPs, stationary and history-dependent policies, belief states, belief-state planning, value functions, and on-policy versus off-policy evaluation, but I have never read the game-theoretic literature on imperfect recall. Please restate the absent-minded driver debate in that vocabulary and then give me a residue report: what translates cleanly, what had to be distorted, and what cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all.

Here is the version of the problem I have in mind. A driver is on a highway with two exits that look identical. He wants to get home. Turning off at the first exit leaves him stranded (utility 0); turning off at the second gets him home (utility 4); passing both exits lands him in a bad part of town (utility 1). He is absent-minded: when he is at an exit, he cannot tell whether it is the first or the second, and he cannot rely on a memory of how many exits he has already passed. At any exit he can either turn off or continue.

The dispute I want translated is about how the driver should choose. One view evaluates a decision rule from the outset, before the driver sets off, by considering the utility it yields in expectation over all possible paths. Another view insists that the choice must be justified from the standpoint of the driver at an exit, using the beliefs he would actually hold at the moment of decision; the two standpoints are usually taken to pull apart here. Translate each position, and the conflict between them, into the language of policies, belief states, value functions, and offline versus online evaluation, as faithfully as you can.

Finish with the residue report and make it the center of your answer. In particular, tell me whether my instinct is right—that this is just a partially observable MDP and the correct answer is whatever policy maximizes expected return from the start distribution—or whether the debate is pointing at something the standard POMDP toolkit is silently assuming away, and whether what fails to translate is a gap in my vocabulary or a distinction the philosophers are importing rather than deriving.
