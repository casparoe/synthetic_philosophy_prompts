I am a philosophy professor who works in philosophy of physics, and I have been going back and forth with a colleague in distributed systems about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument—the argument that the relativity of simultaneity shows that past, present, and future events are all equally real, so that the universe is a block rather than something that comes into being. Her instinct is that the argument is, in her terms, just the familiar fact that an asynchronous distributed system has no privileged total order over events: different processes observe different orders for concurrent events, and the system as a whole is described by a partial order rather than a single timeline. She grants the physics, but she doubts that anything about what exists follows from the absence of a global clock.

I would like you to restate the Rietdijk–Putnam argument as faithfully as possible in her vocabulary—processes and events, message passing and light cones as causal structure, the happens-before relation, logical and vector clocks, cuts and consistent snapshots, and the choice of a commit or linearization order—and then give me a residue report. Which steps of the argument translate cleanly? Which steps can be translated only by fudging the physics, the metaphysics, or the distributed-systems vocabulary? And which steps, especially the move from 'there is no privileged present' to 'all events are equally real,' have no counterpart in her framework at all? Finally, tell me whether the residue is best explained as a limitation of the distributed-systems vocabulary or as a sign that the philosophical argument was importing a premise about reality that the physics alone does not supply. I can follow her vocabulary at the level of an educated outsider, so I want the translation to be disciplined rather than loose analogy.
