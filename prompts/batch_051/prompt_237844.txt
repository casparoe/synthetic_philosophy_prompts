Every few months I run into the same stalled argument about judging historical figures like Jefferson, Churchill, and Columbus by present-day standards. One side says that's presentism: they were men of their time, shaped by their culture, and it's unreasonable to expect them to have seen past it. The other side says that's bad history, since plenty of their contemporaries did condemn slavery, colonial cruelty, or racial hierarchy, and anyway some things are bad enough that cultural context doesn't get you off the hook. The same moves get made every time and nobody shifts. I'm after a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a factual disagreement about what was actually contested at the time? A moral disagreement about whether context can excuse? A verbal disagreement about what "judging a historical figure" even means? Or is it several different questions—explaining why someone acted, apportioning blame, and deciding whether to commemorate them now—bundled under one heading? My hunch is that the slogan "man of his time" quietly slips between an explanatory claim, an epistemic claim about what he could have known, and a moral claim that it was less wrong for him, and that slippage keeps the argument going. But that's just a hypothesis. Tell me what kind of disagreement this really is, and what would count as progress given that diagnosis.
