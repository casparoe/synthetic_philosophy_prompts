I'm taking a philosophy of mind and animal ethics module this term and I keep getting stuck on a weird mismatch in UK animal law, and I'd like help working out what it actually presupposes about sentience and whether moral status comes in degrees. As I understand it, the Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 says that a protected animal is any living vertebrate plus cephalopods — so a zebrafish or a mouse triggers licensing and ethical review, and so does an octopus, while a crab, a lobster or a bee can be used in research with no licence and no ethics committee sign-off. But the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 then says ministers must have regard to the welfare of all vertebrates, cephalopods and decapod crustaceans as sentient beings — so crabs, lobsters and prawns are officially sentient for government policy but still not protected for research, and insects are on neither list. On top of that, even the protected animals aren't protected equally: mice and rats get used in enormous numbers under fairly standard protocols, whereas primates get special restrictions and have to be individually justified.

That whole pattern — a sharp threshold between vertebrates and cephalopods on one side and everything else on the other, a second threshold that lets decapod crustaceans in through the side door, graded protection above the threshold, and nothing at all for insects — seems to presuppose some picture of what sentience is. I want you to reconstruct the implicit theory. Does the law tacitly assume sentience is a threshold property (an animal is in or out, and the line is drawn at something like a centralised nervous system or nociceptive wiring), or does it assume sentience is graded, with stronger protection for stronger capacities? Is it coherent to treat decapods as sentient in one statute and unregulated in another, or can that be defended as a difference between acknowledging sentence and deciding what needs research oversight? And if you took seriously the idea that moral status really is a matter of degree all the way down — with insects getting a little and mammals a lot, and some principled reason why thermostats and plants still get zero — what would the current system have to change? Please say how confident you are in your reconstruction and single out the point you're least sure of. British English, please.
