I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep running into a one-liner in consciousness debates that seems to settle arguments without ever being unpacked. At a conference last month, a neuroscientist responded to a question about whether a subliminally processed word could still be conscious in some minimal sense by saying, "A mental state is conscious only if you're aware of being in it." A week later, a philosopher used the same idea to explain blindsight: the patient does not consciously see, he said, because the patient is not aware of being in the visual state. I can tell the slogan is doing real work in both arguments, but I cannot tell what it actually asserts. I'd like you to take that one-liner and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, as a menu. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and say which reading is actually needed to support the blindsight explanation. Along the way, please distinguish the possible answers to questions like: awareness of what, by what kind of higher-order state, whether that state must itself be conscious, and whether it can misrepresent the state it is about. Please write your answer in American English.
