I keep going back and forth on whether spirituality without religion is a real thing or just a rebranding of stuff we already have words for. Heres a claim I want to test: once you take away the explicit religious framework, what a spiritual-but-not-religious person is left with is nothing over and above (a) a set of emotion-regulation techniques and (b) an aesthetic preference for experiences like awe, calm, or feeling connected. The extra thing they gesture at — the sacred, the transcendent, something larger — does no descriptive or causal work; its just a label they apply after the fact. How would you test that? Design the best study, experiment, or observation you can, and be concrete about what you'd measure or compare. Try to avoid just asking people whether they feel spiritual, since that may only measure willingness to use the word. Then say what result would push you toward accepting the claim, what result would push you toward rejecting it, and what no test of this kind could settle even in principle.
