I've noticed something unstable in trying to assign probabilities to my own future choices, and I can't find the right word for it. Take a decision I have not yet made — whether to leave my job within the next year. If I try to step outside myself and estimate the probability that I will leave, the estimate feeds back into the choice itself. A high estimate makes leaving start to feel like the kind of person I already am, which seems to raise the estimate further; a low estimate makes staying feel settled, which pushes it lower still. The only way I can stop this feedback is to postpone the estimate until after I have decided, but at that point I am no longer predicting. Does this pattern have a name? More than a label, I want to know where its edges are: is it impossible in principle to hold an accurate probability about one's own not-yet-made choice, or only impossible while one is still treating the choice as open? And what exactly is breaking down — is the prediction altering the thing predicted, or is there a conflict between the stance of the chooser and the stance of the predictor?
