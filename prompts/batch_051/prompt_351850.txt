I'm putting together a short dialogue for my intro ethics class and I want it to be genuinely good, not one of those staged exchanges where one side is a punching bag. Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Maya and Dan, on the question whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible. Maya defends the Kantian line from the Metaphysics of Morals: since duty expresses an objective practical necessity, two conflicting duties cannot both be real duties – if one maxim really obligates, the contrary simultaneously cannot. So any apparent collision of obligations must be a sign of misdescription or of treating a merely subjective principle as if it were a duty. Dan pushes back in the spirit of Sartre's student: some cases involve competing claims that aren't commensurable by any single metric, and a theory that rules out real dilemmas ahead of time has simply redefined duty to avoid them. The dialogue needs real back-and-forth, with each responding to the other's actual points, and at least one moment were one of them is forced to grant something significant. Keep it about two pages. The tone should be collegial but pointed; these are people who respect each other and disagree hard.
