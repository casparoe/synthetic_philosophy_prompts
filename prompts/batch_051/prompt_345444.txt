Here's a parallel I keep turning over and can't tell if it's deep or just pattern-matching. The German tank problem — estimating total tank production from the serial numbers on a handful of captured tanks, assuming those tanks are sampled randomly from 1 to N — looks to me like the doomsday argument with the labels swapped. My birth rank, roughly the hundred-billionth human, plays the role of a serial number; the total number of humans who will ever live plays the role of N; and the self-sampling assumption plays the role of the captured tanks being a random draw. In both cases, seeing a number that isn't huge pulls the estimate of N down. If the structures really are the same, does the fact that the tank estimate actually works transfer any support to the doomsday argument, or is that just an illicit appeal by analogy? And if they're not the same, I want to know exactly which joint gives way: maybe that the tanks already exist while future humans don't, maybe that there was never an actual sampling procedure that picked me out of all possible humans, maybe the reference class about who counts as an observer. One thing specifically bugs me: with more captured tanks the estimate tightens, so the parallel seems to predict that asking more currently-alive people their birth rank would also tighten the doomsday estimate — but that feels wrong, since everyone alive now has nearly the same rank. Does that broken prediction show the analogy is superficial, or does it point to something stranger inside the doomsday argument itself?
