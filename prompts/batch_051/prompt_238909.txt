I've always found Alan Turing's 1950 essay on machine intelligence kind of vertiginous. He spends the first chunk setting up the imitation game as the only way to get past the hopeless question "Can machines think?" But then, in the last section, he pivots to extrasensory perception, saying the statistical evidence at least for telepathy is overwhelming and that if ESP is real the game could be disrupted unless you put the contestants in a telepathy-proof room. Am I wrong to read those two moves as in serious tension? On one hand he's saying observable behavior is the whole game; on the other he's entertaining a phenomenon that, if it works, would wreck the readibility of that behavior. Maybe he's just being a good sport about the objections, or maybe he sees the test itself as always embedded in a larger set of background assumptions about the world.
