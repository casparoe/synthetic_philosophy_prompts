I did a known egg donation in the US ten years ago, and the contract I signed had a clause: "Neither party shall seek or initiate contact with the other, and the Donor shall have no contact of any kind with any child resulting from the donation." The intended parents tracked me down last month because their nine-year-old daughter has a rare blood disorder and needs a stem cell transplant, and I'm the only likely half-match donor they can find. If I agree to get tested and donate, I'd meet the kid and the parents at the hospital, which is obviously "contact" in some sense. But the clause was written to stop me from prying into their family or claiming a parental role, not to stop me from donating cells when the kid's life is on the line. Where should the line be drawn: the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what we'd have said if someone had asked at signing, or what a reasonable person reading it now would think? And does it change your read of "contact" that in much of Europe this arrangement would have been anonymous or non-commercial, and the clause likely wouldnt be enforceable in the first place?
