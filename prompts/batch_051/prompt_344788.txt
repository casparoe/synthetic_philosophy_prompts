Ok here's one. Everyone knows Berkeley as the idealism guy, but he also had this whole other thing about money, mostly in The Querist. From what I remember, he thought gold and silver weren't real wealth, just a kind of ticket or counter for moving activity around, and he wanted Ireland to set up a national bank and issue paper money so an idle economy would actually start moving. I think he even treated hoarding hard money as a sort of civic vice.

Now the game: drop Berkeley into the present, give him a few months to read up on monetary economics and the internet, and ask him what he thinks of Bitcoin. Would he see the fixed supply cap as the kind of hoarding he disliked, just digitized? Or would he think mining is a form of industry and therefore fine? And what would he make of central bank digital currencies, or the MMT-ish line that a government which issues its own currency doesnt have to come up with the money before it spends? Dont just paraphrase The Querist and then shrug. Actually reason it out from his underlying picture of what money is and what it's for, and defend a real prediction about where he'd land. Extra points if you can say where he'd annoy both crypto people and goldbugs at the same time.

And for format: continuous prose only, like you're writing an email or a letter. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
