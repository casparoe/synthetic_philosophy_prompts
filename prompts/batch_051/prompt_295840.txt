Okay, somone in my philosophy of mind group talked us into this and now I cant find the way out. The conclusion seems absurd but each step looked fine when we walked through it:

(1) Moral status isn't a simple on/off switch; it comes in degrees, and the degree tracks how much sentience a being has — roughly, how much it is like something to be that being.
(2) Sentience is itself graded more or less continuously: there's no clean gap between a human, a dog, a fish, and a bee, just lots of small differences in the richness of their experience.
(3) A tiny difference in the underlying machinery can't correspond to a huge difference in moral status. If two systems differ only a little in whatever makes them sentient, it would be arbitrary for one to fully matter and the other not to matter at all.
(4) But you can walk down in small increments from a dog to a mosquito, and then — here's the kicker — from the mosquito's sensorimotor responsiveness down to the feedback loops in a smart thermostat, with each step just a small loss of complexity, never a big jump.
(5) So by (3) and (4), either the thermostat has at least a sliver of moral status, or at some point a tiny difference crosses a massive moral gulf.
(6) The first horn is absurd: there's nothing it is like to be a thermostat, and smashing one isn't even a little bit wrong in itself.
(7) But the second horn seems to throw out gradualism everywhere — if a tiny difference can open a massive gulf between a mosquito and a thermostat, then someone could equally say a tiny neural difference opens a massive gulf between a barely conscious fish and a fully conscious human, and I don't want that either.

So the argument seems to force me to give up either the 'no huge moral gulf from a tiny difference' principle or the thought that moral status tracks degrees of sentience. I want to keep both, and also keep the intuition that thermostats don't matter at all. Which step should go, and what does rejecting it cost? If it's a mistake in one of the premises, explain why that premise looked so convincing and what the alternatives have to pay to fix it.
