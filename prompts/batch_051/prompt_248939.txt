Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing for a year about the feeling that life is hollow or empty inside. One camp—call them the disclosure view—says the inner void is a real and even veridical sense that there is no solid self, and the right move is to stop trying to fill it and let it dissolve into a kind of freedom (think Buddhist no-self, but don't make it scholastic). The other camp—the deficit view—says the void is a depleted state: anhedonia, disconnection, lost meaning, often with bodily and biographical causes. The right move is repair, not surrender. Write the single document they could both actually sign. It should have: (1) agreed claims about the phenomenology and where it overlaps with depression; (2) a disagreements table where each row is phrased so each side would accept it's own wording; (3) the cruxes, including whether the void can ever be pried apart from anhedonia and whether "seeing through the self" is insight or dissociation; and (4) for each crux a concrete case or intervention both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Finish by saying which bits of their fight were only verbal.
