Write a parody terms of service, numbered clauses, around 650 words, for a made-up self-improvement app called Prokopton that sells "daily Stoic practice": morning Marcus Aurelius meditations, journaling prompts called premeditatio malorum, a rejection-therapy streak, that sort of thing. Keep the legalese straight-faced the whole time, no winking. The joke should be that the app quietly uses the dichotomy of control as a liability shield and a retention tool: anything that could go wrong on the user's side—server outages, data loss, features not shipping, panic attacks triggered by the memento mori push notifications—gets classified as an external that's not up to us, while anything that benefits the company (autopay, arbitration clauses, the user's obligation to keep journaling so the streak survives) gets classified as up to you or as a matter of judgment. The humor should come from the company doing the exact thing Epictetus warns against: using the distinction to offload responsibility rather than locate it. Someone who actually knows the Enchiridion should wince at a couple of precise misuses, like treating "whether you keep paying" as up to you in the same sense assent is, or offering a "preferred indifferents" feature tier that can be removed at any time with no refund. End with a clause where the user pre-authorizes any inability to unsubscribe as "amor fati." Its fine if the clauses get a little absurd by the end, I just want it deadpan and honestly funny rather than a list of obvious jokes.
