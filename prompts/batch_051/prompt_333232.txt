I keep seeing people claim that charter cities and seasteads let you 'opt out' of the state, and I've realized I don't actually have a sharp idea of what a state is in the first place. Can you give me a working definition of a state — or, if 'state' is the wrong unit, whatever concept does the work — and then run it through these cases?

- France.
- A cruise ship flagged in Panama that spends years in international waters.
- A seastead in international waters that runs it's own security force, courts, and arbitration, and sells residency to whoever pays.
- A charter city on land leased from a host country, where a private company writes the civil laws, runs the courts, and contracts for policing.
- A company town that owns the land, the houses, and the only court that hears disputes between residents, and also employs the police.
- An online platform that takes a cut of every transaction on its marketplace, polices speech, and bans accounts without appeal.
- A monastery where novices consent to an abbot, a shared rule, and internal discipline, and can leave anytime.
- A stateless person who renounced their only citizenship and now moves between hostels and boats.

My gut says France is a state, the platform and the monastery aren't, and I genuinely don't know about the seastead, the charter city, and the company town. Part of the problem is that 'state' seems to mark both a descriptive fact (who actually controls force in a place) and a legal status (who gets recognized as a peer by other states). If that split is right, give me both definitions and say which cases change their verdicts. If it's not, or if the whole framing is confused and 'opting out' doesn't actually turn on defining 'state,' tell me plainly instead of humoring the exercise.
