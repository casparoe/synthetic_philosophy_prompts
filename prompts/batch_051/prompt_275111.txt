I am a high school student writing a term paper on origin essentialism, by which I mean the view that a particular person could not have originated from a sperm and egg pair other than the one from which they actually originated. For the paper I need to survey objections to this view, and so far I have gathered what I believe are the standard objections:

1. The tolerance-chain objection. If a different sperm from the same father during the same act of conception would still have made you, because the difference seems too small to make a different person, then by a chain of similarly small substitutions — a different sperm, then a different egg, then a different act, eventually a different pair of parents — we reach a person with a wholly different origin. The essentialist must then either deny some small step, drawing a cutoff that looks arbitrary, or accept that identity is not transitive across the chain.

2. The twinning-and-chimerism objection. The correspondence between a person and a particular fertilized egg is not always one-to-one: a single zygote can split into two people (identical twins), and two zygotes can fuse into one person (a chimera). This undercuts the idea that each person's identity is anchored to a unique originating gamete pair or zygote.

3. The downstream-equivalence objection. In a possible case in which a different sperm fertilizes the same egg but everything downstream — the body, the brain, and the whole life history — is exactly as it actually is for you, origin essentialism says the resulting person is not you, yet leaves it mysterious how so large a difference as being a different person could have no downstream difference at all.

4. The counterpart-theoretic objection. De re modal claims, including claims of the form that I could have had different parents, are evaluated relative to a chosen counterpart relation; on some reasonable counterpart relations the person from a different sperm and egg counts as your counterpart. If so, the dispute is not about a single non-negotiable fact about the world but about which respects of similarity are being selected, and origin essentialism is a decision about description rather than a discovery about essence.

I suspect this list, though it covers the standard objections, is incomplete, and that there is at least one further objection with a different structure from all four — not a rewording, special case, or combination of them. Please give me one, explain how it differs from the four I have listed, and tell me honestly whether you think it is any good. Please write your answer in American English.
