I'm a retired chemist, and I've been reading philosophy of science for a year or so now; the natural kinds debate has really snagged me. The realist case strikes me as basically sound: our inductions and explanations work because they track real, mind-independent groupings — electrons, gold, water are out there waiting to be discovered, not conjured up by our classifications. I find the projectability argument genuinely compelling. But I also have a stubborn intuition from decades at the bench that classification is always relative to our purposes and tolerances. Whether two samples count as "the same substance" depends on what purity, isotope profile, phase, or level of analysis we care about, and different questions seem to call for different carve-ups. So I keep fliping between "kinds are discovered" and "kinds are partly our tools." Does these two thoughts actually conflict, or does the realist view only conflict with a philosophical gloss on my intuition? If they do conflict, which should give way — and what would make you change your mind about the verdict? Please write it in continuous prose, like a letter, no headings or bullet points.
