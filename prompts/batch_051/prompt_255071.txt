I've been kicking around a view I call "causal-backdrop monism" and want someone to tell me how broken it is before I get too attached. The core is that there's exactly one substance, but unlike Spinoza I don't want to commit to God or infinite attributes. My whole argument leans on a line from Spinoza's Ethics, in the Elwes translation: "There cannot exist in the universe two or more substances having the same nature or attribute." That's basically the engine. My twist is this: the only identity conditions we actually have for substances are their causal histories. Two substances with the same causal history would be indistinguishable, so they'd be one. Two substances with different causal histories can't be independently existing, because to have different histories they'd already have to be embedded in some common causal medium that individuates them as separate. So plurality collapses. I call the common medium the "backdrop." I suspect I'm smuggling in a principle Spinoza got from his attribute doctrine but I'm just asserting: every real difference requires a shared medium of comparison. Is my principle new, or is this just Spinoza with the attributes quietly removed? Where exactly does it fall apart, and is the damage fatal, or can the view be saved by cashing out what a backdrop is?
