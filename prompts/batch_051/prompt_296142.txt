In my philosophy club we have spent several meetings stuck on whether people can cause things by doing nothing, and I have started to think the real problem is that we lack a clear working definition of causation. What is it for one thing to cause another? I am looking for a definition that counts a struck match causing a pile of dry leaves to catch fire as a case of causation, and a rooster's crowing causing the sun to rise as not a case of causation. The definition should also give a definite answer either way for each of these cases: a bystander who watches a child drown in a shallow pond and does not jump in; a doctor who does not prescribe a cheap antibiotic that would have cured the patient's infection; a neighbor who forgets to water a plant while the owner is away, and the plant dies; a city that never repairs a cracked levee, which then breaks in a flood; and a factory owner who does not install a fire alarm, so a small fire spreads unchecked. I am confident about the two clear cases but not about the omission cases. If the definition says that some or all of the omissions are not causes, I want you to explain why we still often hold people responsible for them; if it says they are causes, I want you to explain what it is about an absence or a non-event that does the causal work. Bonus if the definition explains why the omission cases feel less straightforwardly causal even when we decide that they are.
