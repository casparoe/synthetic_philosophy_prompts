People still cite Hofstadter's old argument about machine translation like it was the final word: Google Translate doesn't understand anything, it just does statistical matching of phrase patterns, with no model of the scene behind a sentence. But the systems he was complaining about were phrase-level pattern matchers; the current crop of LLMs is a different beast. Trained on enough human text they plausibly build something like a world model, and they handle ambiguity, pronoun resolution, and style in ways the old systems couldn't. So does Hofstadter's argument survive that shift, or does it lean on an architectural premise that no longer holds? If it dies, is there a weaker version that's still true and important (something like 'fluent output is not evidence of understanding,' or 'the training objective doesn't guarantee concepts')? Or does it go the other way: an LLM is still just next-token prediction at a bigger scale, so Hofstadter was right all along and the improved translations are fooling us? I keep going back and forth, and I want to know which premise is actually doing the work.
