I'm writing a one-pager on where I stand about rights for a reading group on Bentham and contemporary human rights, and before I circulate it I want an outside check on whether the whole thing hangs together. Heres where I've landed, roughly. (1) The only thing that ultimately matters is how well people's lives go, and institutions, laws, and legal rights are good or bad purely in virtue of their consequences. (2) I think Bentham is basically right that the idea of natural rights—rights that exist before any legal system, just because we're human—is pernicious nonsense. (3) At the same time, I think the postwar international human rights system is one of the most valuable things modern politics has produced, and I use rights language in my work constantly. (4) I think judges should treat entrenched constitutional rights (free expression, due process, equal protection) as strong side-constraints, enforcing them even when doing so seems to make things worse in the case before them. (5) I think the main reason for (4) is that a legal culture whose officials treat rights as near-absolute produces better results over the long run than one where judges openly weigh welfare case by case. (6) I also think rule-utilitarianism as a moral theory has a familiar problem I can't get around: it seems to collapse back into act-utilitarianism at the moment of decision, because whenever breaking the rule really would be better, the theory tells you to break it. (7) Every attempt I've seen to ground human rights in human nature, rationality, dignity, or autonomy strikes me as a failure, and I doubt that project will ever succeed. (8) In my own life I treat ordinary moral rules like honesty and promise-keeping quite seriously, but in a clear emergency where lying or breaking a promise would spare someone serious harm, I think the best thing to say is just that I should do it; I'd find it strange if a theory made the lie wrong. (9) I also suspect that part of why rights-respecting legal cultures work is that the people inside them really do take rights to be binding constraints, and that if most judges and citizens came to share my own view of rights, the system would be worse off.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly. I'd like the genuine tensions ranked by severity rather than a flat list of everything-against-everything. For each tension, say what the cheapest repair would be and what it costs me; and also flag any pairs that might look inconsistent but aren't, and say why. Finally, if I were only willing to change one of the nine, which change would do the most to clean the set up?
