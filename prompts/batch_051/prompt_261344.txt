I'm currently taking a philosophy of mind course, and we've spent the last two weeks on the dispute between type identity theory and functionalism. In the standard multiple realizability argument, the two theories come apart in only one direction: a human and an octopus can share the same pain because they share the same functional role, while the type identity theorist must classify them as different states because the physical realizations differ. It struck me that this is only half of the logical space. There should also be cases where the identity theorist is forced to say that two episodes are the same mental state because they are instances of the same physical state type, while the functionalist says they are different mental states because the functional roles differ. Every case I've tried to build in that direction falls back on something exotic — aliens, silicon replacement, a rewired brain — so I can't tell whether the point is real or an artifact of the setup.

I want you to construct a thought experiment that forces the two theories apart in this reverse direction and to show that it satisfies each part of that specification. Build a scenario in which a single physical state type — described finely enough that it plausibly counts as one natural type across the two episodes — realizes two different functional roles, and in which those two roles correspond to two different mental state types an ordinary person could be in. Constraints: the case must be psychologically realistic, with no exotic biology, no prosthetics, no partial brain transplants, and no science-fictional interventions. The physical state should be specified concretely enough that the identity theorist cannot simply dismiss it as not really being a single type; the interesting failure should come from the theories' criteria of type-individuation, not from an unrealistic scene.

After you present the scenario, walk through what each theory says about the two episodes and why it says it. Then identify the feature of the case that is doing the work — what exactly allows a fixed physical state type to occupy two distinct functional roles — and say whether removing that feature would collapse the case back into the familiar multiple realizability direction or into something else. Finally, offer the strongest resistance a type identity theorist could give to the case and assess whether that resistance is principled or just a way of protecting the theory.
