I keep hearing that autonomous systems will create responsibility gaps, but I've never seen anyone state what a theory of those gaps is actually on the hook for, and half the time the phrase just means that lots of people share the blame and that feels unsatisfying. Before I evaluate any account of the topic, I want the complete set of requirements. What does an adequate theory of responsibility gaps in autonomous systems have to deliver? For each requirement, give the case or pressure that forces it. Make sure the list pins down at least: when a gap is genuine rather than merely distributed, layered, or temporally removed responsibility; whether a system that keeps learning after deployment raises a different problem than one whose behavior was fixed before release; whether the theory has to take talk of the system itself being responsible literally, or can explain the gap while denying that an algorithm is the kind of thing that can be answerable; and whether it owes an account of moral blame and answerability, or whether legal liability and compensation can carry all the weight. Finally, take the requirement you're least sure deserves its place and argue both sides: genuine desideratum, or just an artifact of thinking about human agents. I want the criteria, not a report on who passes them.
