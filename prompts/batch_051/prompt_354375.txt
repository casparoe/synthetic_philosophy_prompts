Here is a step-by-step argument that quantum indeterminism, even on the assumption that it operates inside the brain, cannot make a decision free.

(1) A decision is free only if the agent's reasons at the time explain why she made that decision rather than an alternative.

(2) If a decision is produced by a process whose final step is a genuinely chancy quantum event, then even holding fixed the agent's reasons and prior neural states, there remains a nonzero probability that the decision comes out otherwise.

(3) If, holding fixed the agent's reasons and prior neural states, the decision might nonetheless come out otherwise, then those reasons did not settle which decision occurred.

(4) A factor explains an outcome only if it settles that outcome rather than an alternative.

(5) Therefore, when a decision ends in a genuinely chancy quantum event, the agent's reasons do not explain the decision.

(6) Therefore such a decision is not free.

Is the argument valid? Are the premises true? If the argument fails, I want to know exactly where. I half-suspect that "settles" is sliding between two readings—roughly, "causally determines" and "is the explanatory factor that makes the difference"—and that the argument's plausibility trades on the slide. If that is right, identify which step or steps fail, give the strongest reading of the relevant premise on which it is true, and check whether the argument remains sound on that reading. If you think the argument is sound, say how a libertarian who accepts quantum indeterminism in the brain should answer it.
