After fourty years of reading off priors and error bars, I've recently fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole, and the thing bugging me now is moral uncertainty: what you should actually do when you aren't sure which moral theory is right. I don't mean first-order ethics, I mean the decision problem you face when, say, a mostly utilitarian picture and a leftover chunk of deontology are both live options for you and they rank the available acts differently. It seems like there should be something systematic to say, but every proposal I've seen either fudges the fact that the two theories don't share a common scale or quietly picks a scale that makes its own favorite theory win. Before I read any more, I want the spec: what does an adequate theory of decision-making under moral uncertainty have to get right? I care about action guidance here, not the metaphysics of value. Give me the requirements list, each with the case or consideration that forces it, and for each say whether it is non-negotiable or just one camp's preference. I'm especially interested in the in-the-weeds ones: how it handles rival theories without a common unit, a deontic ranking against a cardinal value function; what it does about choice-worthiness being fixed only up to affine transformations, so different normalizations can reverse the verdict; how it avoids laundering a first-order view through the choice of normalization; how it handles uncertainty about the theory of moral uncertainty itself without an infinite regress; and what it says when one of the candidate first-order theories is self-effacing or explicitly tells you what to do when you are unsure of it. If some subset of those can't be jointly satisfied, that's the part I most want to see, and tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the standard moves. Then give a short scorecard for the main families — maximizing expected choice-worthiness, going with your favored theory, whatever else is on offer — but keep that brief, the criteria are the point. And take a definite side: don't lay out the options and leave me to pick. No headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables; just continuous prose like an email.
