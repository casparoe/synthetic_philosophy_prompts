I'm running the one-evening session at my local rationalist/EA meetup on the unilateralist's curse. Fifteen or so people, mostly programmers who can all read a little probability notation but I don't want the hour to turn into a seminar on order statistics. Everyone has seen the term thrown around on LessWrong, but I'd guess fewer then half could reconstruct the actual argument, and at least one person will nod along and then explain something that is really groupthink. I have roughly an hour and a whiteboard, no slides.

Don't explain the curse to me; plan how I should teach it. I want the arc to make the curse feel like it falls out of two or three boring assumptions about how a group aggregates individual judgments once any single member can pull the trigger on behalf of everyone — not like yet another bias with a scary name. There should be one concrete running case that carries the whole session. Pick it, and say what it has to have and what it has to avoid; I suspect a whistleblowing case would derail the room, but maybe its secretly the right one, so tell me if I'm wrong.

The real work I need from you is the dependency structure and the misconception map. What has to be in place before the curse is even visible, what each later step leans on from earlier steps, and what breaks if I teach things out of order. Which wrong ideas will form at which points: I especially expect people to collapse the curse into information cascades, or groupthink, or 'wisdom of crowds fails,' or to conclude the fix is just 'give the most careful person a veto.' For each one tell me the exact point in the sequence where it would naturally form and how to head it off there rather than waiting for Q&A.

Send me the plan as JSON with these fields:
- carrying_example: { scenario, why_it_works, what_it_must_have, what_it_must_avoid }
- steps: an array, one object per step ordered as I should teach it, with { order, move, depends_on, example_thread, misreading, kill_sentence, check }:
  - order: integer position in the session
  - move: what I actually say, ask, or draw in this step
  - depends_on: which earlier steps this one needs and what breaks if it comes too early
  - example_thread: how the running case is used or updated in this step
  - misreading: the specific wrong idea most likely to form at this point
  - kill_sentence: the one sentence that heads it off immediately
  - check: something I can observe at the end of the step that tells me it landed
- leave_out: { what, refusal_sentence } — what I should explicitly refuse to explain at this level, and a sentence that makes the refusal feel deliberate rather than like a gap
- final_check: one closing question that would separate someone who actually got the curse from someone who can only repeat the name.
