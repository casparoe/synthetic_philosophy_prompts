I am a sixth-form student, and next week I am giving a short talk to my school's computing society on the persuasive design of social media feeds. My talk will defend this thesis: the now-standard set of engagement-maximising features — infinite scroll, pull-to-refresh, autoplay, notification badges, and above all the continuous A/B testing used to tune those features — does not merely respond to what users already want but actively shapes and strengthens those wants. The moral parallel is not ordinary persuasion by reasons but the variable-ratio reward schedule of a slot machine, and that parallel shifts the justificatory burden from the user, who is expected to resist, to the platform, which is expected to justify the design. I will not argue for any specific law, ban, or age restriction; only that the design can no longer be treated as morally neutral by default.

The audience is roughly fifteen A-level computer science students plus their teacher, a former software engineer who now teaches and is broadly enthusiastic about the tech industry. The students are empirically minded, several have built small apps or websites and have run simple A/B tests, and they tend to be suspicious of what they regard as moral panic around new technology. They are not hostile to ethical argument as such; they are hostile to arguments that seem to ignore user choice or the genuine value users get from the products.

I expect the question period to be far more difficult than the talk itself. Please draft a question-time preparation sheet for me: the eight or so objections I should most expect from this particular audience, ordered by how much damage each could do, each phrased the way a member of this audience would actually put it. For each objection, include a reply I could deliver in about two minutes and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Then include the question I should be privately hoping nobody asks. I will be answering without notes, so the replies should be reasoning I can hold in my head rather than references to books or papers. Please write the entire answer in British English.
