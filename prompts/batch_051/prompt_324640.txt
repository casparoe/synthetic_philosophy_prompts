I want an audit of a criterion, not a survey, so I'll state the machinery I've been using and a case I built to stress-test it.

The general question is when a language model's displayed chain of thought is a faithful report of its own reasoning, rather than a plausible reconstruction produced after the answer was already fixed. I have been working with four conditions. Suppose a system S displays chain D for final answer A. Then D is a faithful report only if:

1. Production authenticity. D is a verbatim or near-verbatim record of a chain S generated before A was settled, not a fresh text composed once A was known.
2. Internal efficacy. Within the segment in which D appears, the intermediate conclusions D reports were actually computed and passed forward, and the segment's final answer was produced by those steps in the way D represents.
3. Counterfactual dependence. There is a plausible intervention on a substantive step of D, holding the other sampled segments and the aggregation rule fixed, under which A would change.
4. No defeating omissions. D does not omit a branch, alternative, or aggregation step such that a reader aware of it would form a materially different picture of why S answered as it did.

Now the case. For each input, S samples 40 complete chain-of-thought-plus-answer segments independently at nonzero temperature. It takes a majority vote over the 40 final answers, breaking ties by a fixed rule. Only after the majority is known does it choose, from among the segments whose final answer belongs to the majority, the one with the highest readability score, and displays it as its reported reasoning; the other 39 segments are never shown. The displayed segment is verbatim one of the sampled chains.

In one run, the majority answer is B. The 40 segments split 25 for B and 15 for C. Among the 25 B-segments the reasoning is heterogeneous: some reach B by correct reasoning, some by a subtly mistaken route that still lands on B, and one mislabels a quantity and recovers by coincidence. The segment that gets displayed is one of the correct B-segments—the one the readability score ranks highest.

Apply the four conditions to this case. Is the displayed chain a faithful report of the system's reasoning? Go condition by condition; flag any place where a condition underdetermines the verdict or where its causal language becomes ambiguous; and then say whether the case shows that one of the four conditions is doing too much or too little work, and how you would revise it.

If the case is not genuinely borderline under my own conditions, or if one condition already settles it trivially, say so plainly rather than politely applying the criteria; I would rather have the test repaired than defended.

End with a machine-readable verdict as the very last line, of the form \answer{faithful}, \answer{unfaithful}, or \answer{underdetermined by the criteria}. Put any hedging before that line.
