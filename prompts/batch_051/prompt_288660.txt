I want to mess with counterfactual mugging in a way I haven't seen: instead of a binary pay/don't pay, make the payment continuous. In the standard setup, Omega flips a coin; on tails she asks you for $100, on heads she pays you $10,000 iff she predicted you would pay on tails. Now on tails, Omega asks you to choose a number r between 0 and 1 and you pay 100r dollars. On heads, she pays you 10,000 times the exact r she predicted you would choose on tails. She is still a perfect predictor of your policy. What does an updateless decision theory say you should choose for r? What does a causal decision theory say? Does evidential decision theory even get a purchase here, since your actual choice of r on tails is the thing being predicted in the other branch? Then add a wrinkle: before the game, Omega announces a threshold θ, and on heads she only pays if the r she predicted for you on tails is at least θ. If you actually choose r=0.4 with θ=0.5, you still lose your 40 bucks on tails but get nothing on heads. For θ=0.5, walk through the calculation for each theory and say whether the optimal r is exactly θ, above θ, or 1. Does the announced threshold change the story, or does it just create a new blackmail-like wedge where you'd like to have the disposition but not the act?
