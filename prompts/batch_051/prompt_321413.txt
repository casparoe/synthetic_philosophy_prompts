Here is the account I was taught for speaker meaning, drawn from Grice's distinction between natural and non-natural meaning. An utterance counts as a case of non-natural meaning when the speaker produced it with a three-part intention: (i) to produce some response in an audience; (ii) that the audience recognize intention (i); and (iii) that the audience's recognition of intention (i) function as at least part of the reason for producing that response. A conversational implicature adds a further layer: the speaker means something more than, or different from, the literal content, and expects the audience to infer it partly by relying on shared cooperative expectations — the maxims of quantity, quality, relation, and manner.

Now consider a case. A user asks a fine-tuned language model, "Is Professor Owusu in today?" The model outputs, "There is a light on in his office." A human being who uttered that sentence in response to that question would normally be taken to conversationally implicate that Professor Owusu is likely in. The user takes the model's output that way. Now add the background: the model has no beliefs, no goals, and no awareness; it was fine-tuned by developers who intended, at a general level, that it produce the kind of helpful responses a cooperative human assistant would produce, but no developer anticipated this particular question or this particular output. The model itself intended nothing, and there is no individual human who intended this particular content for this particular occasion.

Work through the criteria and argue whether the model's output carries a conversational implicature, in Grice's sense, on this occasion. Check the three intention conditions one by one. Where a condition fails, consider whether the right conclusion is that the output has only timeless sentence-meaning and no speaker meaning, or whether the criteria themselves should be revised — for example, by attributing standing or delegated intentions to the developers, by treating the user-model-system as a distributed speaker, or by relaxing the psychological requirement entirely. Finally, say what the case shows about whether Grice's analysis is too psychological, too narrow, or substantially right.
