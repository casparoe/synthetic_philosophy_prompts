I've been re-reading the Scholium to the Definitions in Newton's Principia — the famous bucket and globes passages — and I keep noticing that people in my philosophy-of-physics reading group divide along lines that don't match the usual substantivalist/relationist labels. Some accept absolute acceleration but balk at a container of spacetime points. Others want to eliminate absolute acceleration but can't swallow Mach's reduction. I'm making a handout that sorts readers into a position by what they say about cases and claims, not by which label they like.

Please write a decision tree or sorting quiz for positions on spacetime in the Newtonian and relativistic settings. Audience: graduate students and advanced undergraduates who know the bucket thought experiment and the basics of special and general relativity, but have not done much metaphysics. Use these cases as branch questions or diagnostic items: the rotating bucket, Newton's two globes thought experiment, a universe rotating uniformly about its own axis, and a gravitational wave in an otherwise empty spacetime. Each branch question should be a concrete claim or forced choice about one of these cases that someone can answer without knowing the names of positions. The tree should be built from the logical space first, then locate the named positions: Newtonian absolute-space substantivalism, relationism, sophisticated substantivalism about spacetime points, Machian relationism, and at least two live relativistic views (for example, something like metric-field substantivalism and dynamical relationism). Each leaf should carry a named position and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Mark any unoccupied combination you think is coherent but undefended, and flag any pair of positions your tree cannot separate with the chosen questions. Also flag any leaf where the name is contested enough that it might mislead.

The response should begin with the decision tree itself, no preface or commentary, and then give the reasoning after.
