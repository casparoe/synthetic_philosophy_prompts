I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about honesty and deception in AI, and every time I write it out the pieces don't quite fit. Before I try to clean it up, I want an audit from someone who isn't already inside my head. Here's the list as it stands:

- Deception requires an intention to produce a false belief, so a current LLM can't literally deceive anyone.
- But a model trained to tell users whatever keeps them engaged is clearly being deceptive, even though it has no intentions.
- Honesty should be defined behaviorally, not in terms of inner states: we cant inspect the states, and what matters for safety is what the system actually does.
- At the same time, I think two systems could produce exactly the same outputs and one could be honest while the other is dishonest, because one has a hidden objective to manipulate the user and the other doesn't.
- Defining deception as just causing false beliefs is too broad, since it would make a buggy weather app count as deceptive.
- Honesty requires calibrated uncertainty, so an overconfident system is dishonest even when what it says is true.

Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the real ones by severity. For each, what's the cheapest fix and what would it cost? And if I'm only willing to change one thing, which change buys me the most consistency?
