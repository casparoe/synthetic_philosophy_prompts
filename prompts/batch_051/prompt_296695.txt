I work in the legal and compliance team of a publishing group that produces and syndicates investment research: credit ratings, buy/sell/hold-style ratings, and forward-looking forecasts such as price targets, default probabilities, and revenue projections. We publish dozens of these each week, and perhaps a dozen times a year a company writes to us claiming that a rating or a forecast has defamed it — that what we dressed up as opinion actually asserts a false fact about the company. I need a reusable pre-publication triage procedure, not a one-off decision, that a non-lawyer editor can run in about twenty minutes. The procedure should ask its questions in a fixed order, say what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, say where it stops with publish or escalate to counsel, and mark clearly the points where no procedure can eliminate judgement because the fact/opinion line depends on context, framing, and what a reasonable reader would infer.

Please make the procedure engage with the particular difficulty of ratings and forecasts rather than restating the slogan that opinion is protected. The hard cases are these: a rating presents itself as subjective, but a two-star review of a professional firm may imply checkable facts about how the firm actually behaved; a forecast is about the future, but the sentence 'we expect revenue to fall because their largest customer has already cancelled' embeds a present-tense factual assertion. I want the procedure to make use of distinctions such as provable falsity, statements that imply undisclosed facts, rhetorical hyperbole that no reasonable reader would take literally, and predictions that are protected as predictions but cease to be protected when they smuggle in assertions about the present or past. Then run the procedure on three hypothetical cases: a credit downgrade saying the company is 'already in distress and likely to miss its next debt payment', with a sell rating; an analyst note giving a price target of a 40 per cent fall over twelve months partly because management is 'incompetent and has repeatedly misled the market'; and a professional directory that rates a law firm two stars with the line 'in our experience, they padded their hours'. Include at least one case where the procedure gives me an answer I would rather not receive, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Please write the whole response as continuous prose, the way you would write a memo to a colleague: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
