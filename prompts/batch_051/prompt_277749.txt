I've been calling this idea "compounding QALYs" and I want someone to tell me whether it's real, obvious, or broken.

Epistemic status: half-baked, probably reinventing a wheel, but I can't find the wheel.

Standard QALY accounting is additive. Each year in a health state just adds its quality weight, and you sum over the remaining lifetime. So a year of full health is one QALY whether it happens at 25 or 85.

I think that's incomplete because health is an investment good, not just a consumption good. A year of full health at 25 isn't consumed and gone: it's reinvested. It lets you hold a job, learn things, keep up your routines and social ties, and avoid the spiral where sickness causes poverty causes more sickness. Those in turn produce more future health. So a QALY should compound, like money in an account: a QALY at 25 is really 1 point something, where the decimal is the discounted stream of downstream QALYs it causes.

If that's right, a few things fall out. Age-weighting in triage should be even steeper than normal QALY math implies. Standard models already favor the young because they have more remaining years to gain; my version says an individual QALY gained while young is worth more than the same QALY gained while old, because only the former throws off interest. Cost-effectiveness analysis should also credit an intervention for health effects it doesn't directly cause, if the direct effect compounds into them. Which feels like double counting, but also like the entire point. And the value of a statistical life for a young person would be even higher relative to an old person than the usual numbers suggest, because the killed 25-year-old loses not just their remaining QALYs but all the compounding those QALYs would have done.

I have three questions. Is this just the health-as-investment idea from health econ under another name, or does that literature cover the reverse direction only? Where does it actually break: does it end up pricing people by their productivity, or double counting future QALYs that were already in the lifetime sum, or assuming health can grow without bound when quality weight is capped at 1? And what's the strongest version of it: a literal multiplier on individual QALYs, or just a reminder that health-state utilities over a lifetime shouldn't be treated as independent of each other?
