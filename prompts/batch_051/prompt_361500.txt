Here is a mismatch I keep noticing in the way effort enters our judgments, depending on the domain. Consider a difficult passage of music. We tend to regard the performer who produces it with apparent ease as the greater musician, while the performer who produces the identical passage with visible strain is rated lower, even though the audible result is indistinguishable. Yet in moral contexts the same cue seems to point the other way. We tend to admire the person who does the right thing after a hard inward struggle — someone who would rather have done otherwise and overcomes that — more than someone for whom the same right act is easy and costless. So the amount of effort behind an outcome appears to count against the agent in skilled performance and for the agent in moral action, with the outcome itself held fixed on both sides. There is a related peculiarity: some states are spoiled by direct pursuit. Trying to be spontaneous, trying to fall asleep, trying to seem natural, or trying to perform with effortless grace tends to produce something worse than what was aimed at, while trying to be punctual or trying to learn a musical scale is not spoiled in that way. Daoist discussions of wu-wei circle this same knot, since wu-wei is supposed to be a kind of cultivated ease that cannot be acquired by the very forcing it is meant to replace.

I want to know whether these are really three separate quirks or one principle wearing different costumes. Is there a defensible account of what trying contributes in each case that simultaneously explains why effortless performance beats strained performance when the output is fixed, why strained virtue beats effortless virtue when the deed is fixed, and why some targets are destroyed by direct pursuit while others are advanced by it? If such a principle exists, does it ratify both of our original judgments — the downgrading of visible effort in performance and the upgrading of visible effort in morality — or does it show that one of them should be revised? Be as specific as you can about the shape such a principle would take, and say where it would predict cases we have not yet considered.
