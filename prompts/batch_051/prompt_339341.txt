I'm a student in an undergraduate seminar on Rousseau's politics and the arts, and we've just finished the Lettre à d'Alembert sur les spectacles. One passage from the preface has been bothering me. Rousseau writes:

"Justice & vérité, voilà les premiers devoirs de l'homme. Humanité, patrie, voilà ses premieres affections. Toutes les fois que des ménagemens particuliers lui font changer cet ordre, il est coupable."

Roughly: truth and justice are our first duties, country and humanity our first attachments, and he frames his attack on the theater as something it would be wrong not to write. That framing matters to me because the letter's arguments against the theater are often read as aesthetic snobbery, when Rousseau explicitly casts them as civic duty.

The target of Rousseau's critique is a specific institution: a live, public playhouse. He worries that a theater company would settle in Geneva, that citizens would gather indoors in a single space, that the actors would form a socially degraded class, and that the audience would become passive spectators of pleasures that replace active civic life. His worries about dressing up, social comparison, and idleness all seem to presuppose that attending a spectacle is a public, repeated, collective ritual.

Now suppose we change that assumption. Most dramatic fiction is consumed at home or on a phone, alone or in very small groups, through streaming services; there is no resident company, no shared civic venue, no public gathering. The "spectacle" has become private, on-demand, and algorithmically personalized. Does Rousseau's argument survive this development? Which premise does it most directly weaken: his moral psychology of spectatorship, his sociology of the actor, or his claim that the theater replaces public with private pleasures? Is there a repaired version of the argument that still has force against the streaming economy, or does the argument become inapplicable once the public dimension is gone? I'm asking about the fate of the argument itself, not about whether streaming is good or bad.
