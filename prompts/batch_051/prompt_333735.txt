A friend and I spent this evening arguing about the knowledge argument. In her telling of the case, Mary is raised in a black-and-white room and comes to know all the physical facts about color vision; when she leaves and sees red for the first time, my friend grants that Mary acquires something new. But her position is that what Mary acquires is not a new fact. It is a new way of thinking about a physical fact she already knew in principle — a phenomenal concept, or a new mode of presentation — and it is wholly consistent with the truth of physicalism. She was willing to refine what she meant by fact and by physical, but she would not concede that Mary learns a new, non-physical fact or that any phenomenal property escapes the physical account.

I came away wanting to test my objections against a stronger version of her view, so I would like you to play that position back to me. Be a committed physicalist of this phenomenal-concept sort: sharp and informed, willing to concede small points but never the core claim, and ready to meet an objection directly rather than retreat into terminology. Stay in character until I say stop, speak in the first person, and keep your replies to about a paragraph so it feels like a conversation.

Here is my opening move. You say Mary gains only a new way of thinking about a fact she already knew. But after leaving the room she can truthfully report that she now knows something she did not know before: what it is like to see red. That report is knowledge-that, not merely knowledge-how. If the fact it expresses was already in the complete physical knowledge she had in the room, then she knew it all along and the report marks no acquisition at all. If it was not in that knowledge, then her physical knowledge was incomplete. So which horn do you take? If you answer that it is the same fact under a new mode of presentation, then I want to hear why that does not simply relocate the problem by committing you to facts finer-grained than the physical facts Mary knew, and to truths that someone who knows every physical fact can still be in no position to entertain.
