Take Searle's Chinese room, but iterate it in the style of iterated amplification and distillation. In the first room a monolingual English speaker follows a huge rulebook and is indistinguishable on paper from a fluent Chinese speaker. Record that first room's replies on a large fixed set of Chinese conversation samples, errors and confusions included. Room 2 is a different English speaker whose rulebook says to imitate Room 1's recorded behavior. Once Room 2 matches Room 1 on about 99% of the samples, record Room 2's replies and train Room 3 to imitate Room 2, and so on for a few hundred rooms. The only check at each step is similarity to the previous room; nobody ever checks any later room against an actual Chinese speaker or against the subject matter of the conversations.

The usual Chinese-room move is that behavioral competence doesn't give you understanding. What does that move let you say about Room 250? Does it apply the same way, or does it stop doing work somewhere along the chain? Also: on a naive compounding estimate, after 250 rounds the last room would match the first room on less than 10% of inputs, yet no one would have noticed. Is there a determinate point where competence collapses, or should we expect a slow drift into fluent-sounding nonsense that passes every internal check? I'm specifically interested in whether the syntax-isn't-semantics point says anything about iterated imitation, or whether it just goes quiet.
