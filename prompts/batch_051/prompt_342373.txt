I am giving a departmental seminar next month in an ecology and evolutionary biology department, and I would like you to draft my Q&A preparation sheet. The audience is mostly behavioral ecologists and population geneticists. Several of them learned evolution from the inclusive-fitness tradition and are inclined to treat group selection as either a confused redescription of kin selection or a career mistake; a couple work on social insects and are open to multilevel selection; and one philosopher of biology in the department works on the difference between causal explanation and statistical bookkeeping and will come to the talk for exactly that reason.

My thesis is this: in at least some evolutionary episodes, the group is the right level at which to locate a causal explanation of a trait's spread, and the multilevel partition of the Price equation does not by itself settle that question in favor of the gene's-eye view, because the partition is causally neutral until it is given an interpretation. I will defend this with a microbial case: a structured population in which costly production of a shared extracellular good varies more between groups than within them, and in which group persistence and group extinction do explanatory work that a purely individual-level, neighbor-modulated fitness account leaves opaque.

Please give me around eight objections, ordered roughly by how likely they are and how much damage they would do, each phrased the way this particular audience would phrase it. For each objection, give: the strongest one-sentence version of my reply; what I should concede immediately; and where the reply is soft. Separately, tell me the question I should hope nobody asks, and explain why it is the dangerous one.
