So I keep seeing takes on whether Reddit karma, eBay feedback, or Stack Overflow reputation "works," and everyone just points at their favorite failure case and calls it a day. Before I read any more of these, I want the full spec. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of a unified karma/reputation score: what job or jobs a single number is supposed to do, the cases any account has to handle—the brand-new user, the account that behaves for years and then walks off with one big scam, the high-score user who moves into a community where the score means something different, sockpuppets and upvote rings, the pile-on—and the distinctions it cant collapse, like score-as-information-for-readers vs. score-as-incentive-for-posters. Give the consideration behind each requirement and what breaks when you drop it. Then flag any pairs or triples of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied by one scalar metric, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out the naive "just average everyone's ratings" designs. Keep the scorecard of existing systems short; I mostly care about the criteria, and I especially want to know which requirement is doing the real work. A table is fine.
