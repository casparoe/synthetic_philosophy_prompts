Design a runnable classroom activity for a twelve-student upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophical theology, in a single seventy-five-minute session, on whether God can suffer. The session must open with no lecture and no mention of the terms "impassibility" or "passibility"; those terms should appear only in the debrief. The activity itself should be a structured deliberation or role-play in which students are forced to make constrained moves in a staged case, so that they encounter the central trade-off through what happens rather than through open debate. Specifically, the exercise should make them feel that the denial that God can suffer is not simply a cold or distant-theist move, and the affirmation that God can suffer is not simply an obviously humane move: each side is protecting something the other cannot easily retain, and the real pressure is over what it would mean for a perfect being to be acted on from outside as opposed to freely sharing in creaturely pain. Give me a full activity specification: the scenario or case, roles or rules, a timeline for the seventy-five minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the underlying concepts (impassibility, passibility, being acted upon versus suffering with, and the distinction between ordinary passions and something like affectivity within an unchanging nature). The debrief should also say what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run and how to handle it, and it should be candid about which feature of the disagreement the activity cannot reproduce and that I will need to supply myself. Do not assume or cite primary texts, and do not assign any reading. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items: (1) setup and materials; (2) the scenario and rules; (3) sequence of constrained moves and likely sticking points; (4) debrief mapping to the concepts; (5) predictable failures and the feature the activity cannot reproduce. Each item should be one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
