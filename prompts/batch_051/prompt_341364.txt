I teach philosophy and serve on my university's academic initiatives committee, which must vote next week on a proposal to establish a new Center for Digital Culture and Policy. The proposal makes an inside-view case: a director with a strong record, letters of support from three professional schools, and gifts covering roughly half the first five years of operating costs; the remainder is projected to come from grants and executive education by year six. Our staff analyst reviewed twelve broadly comparable centers launched at peer institutions since 2005: four were self-sustaining by year six, five required repeated bridge funding, and three were closed or absorbed. The proposers reply that the reference class is gerrymandered—it mixes teaching, research, and advocacy centers—and that no two centers share enough history for the base rate to be probative. Committee members are divided between those who say the outside view should discipline decisions like this and those who say that stance would rule out every genuinely novel initiative. I have been asked to circulate a short decision memo before we vote. Please draft that memo. It should reach a definite recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—give reasons the proposers could respond to, and take seriously the strongest consideration on the losing side.
