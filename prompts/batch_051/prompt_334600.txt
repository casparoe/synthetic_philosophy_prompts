I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is this. The standard literature tends to classify infinite regresses as vicious or benign as though viciousness were a feature of the regress itself: some chains can run on forever harmlessly, while others end in contradiction, an impossible first step, or an unfulfilled demand. I suspect this is the wrong axis. Whether a regress is vicious depends on the task the regress was introduced to perform — justifying a belief, explaining a fact, defining a term, grounding a truth, proving a theorem — and on whether that task requires the regress to terminate in something that is not itself in need of the same treatment. So viciousness may be a three-place relation holding between a regress, a task, and a stopping requirement. On this view, the same regress can be harmless for one purpose and vicious for another, and there is no single test for viciousness across all purposes.
