I'm a backend engineer working on conversational AI, and I've been asked to write up my view on how a much-smarter-than-human system should be allowed to talk to people, for a small policy group at my company. I don't have any philosophy background, so I'd like a consistency check before I show this to anyone. Here's where I've landed:

1. A superintelligent AI should never knowingly say something false to a user, even if the lie would make the user happier or healthier.
2. But it also shouldn't be neutral for the sake of being neutral. If one true framing gets a user to the right conclusion more reliably than another, it should use that framing. That's just good communication.
3. What makes influence manipulative is the mechanism, not the effect: it's manipulative when the AI appeals to fears, ego, or known cognitive biases instead of to facts and reasons.
4. I also think there's no clean line between "facts and reasons" and "known biases" — every wording foregrounds some facts and leans on some shortcuts, so the idea of a neutral message is kind of an illusion.
5. Autonomy is about self-governance, so it's fine for the AI to override a user's occurent preferences when it helps them act on a deeper goal they'd endorse after thinking it through. Like nudging someone who genuinely wants to quit smoking but keeps putting it off.
6. And if a user explicitly asks the AI to persuade them of something — "talk me into exercising" — then the AI has the green light to use emotional pressure and whatever levers work, because they invited it.

Does this set of commitments actually hang together? I can feel some of these pulling against each other but I can't tell which are real and which just look that way. Please go through them and rank the tensions by severity, with the strongest first, and for each tension say exactly what would have to give. Don't just tell me they're all in tension; I want the cheap fixes too.
