I'm a software engineer on the steering committee of a mid-size open-source project that maintains a data-governance vocabulary plus a compliance profile (think JSON-LD context and rules). I've never actually taken a philosophy class, so bear with me if I mangle this.

We're trying to ship v2 and we've been stuck for weeks on the term consent. In v1 we defined it roughly as any affirmative action by a user indicating agreement to a processing purpose: clicking accept, checking a box, not objecting when we showed them a notice, stuff like that. It was deliberately descriptive, meant to match what systems actually capture in audit logs.

A faction in the community wants to redefine consent in v2 so it only counts when the agreement meets a bunch of quality conditions: actually informed, not pushed through dark patterns, revocable, tied to a single purpose, and so on. Their argument is that the v1 definition just describes a lousy status quo, and a spec that counts a dark-pattern click as consent is basically laundering a bad practice. They keep saying we should fix the concept instead of just analyzing how the word is already used; one of them keeps throwing around the term ameliorative analysis, and another says the real question is whether a spec should do conceptual engineering or just plain conceptual analysis. I tried to read about that distinction and got lost.

The other faction says no: a spec's job is to disambiguate how a term is actually used in the wild so that different systems can interoperate. If we redefine consent to mean something that almost no current implementation actually records, the spec stops describing reality and becomes a wishlist. They also note that v2 is a major version, so semver technically lets us break things, but they think changing the meaning of a core term while keeping the same string consent is the worst kind of breaking change because it looks compatible but isnt. Their compromise is to leave consent alone and add a separate term like validConsent (or a separate compliance tier) for the stronger notion.

I need to send the steering committee a short memo with an actual recommendation, not just a summary of the two camps. Draft that memo for me. It should say what we should do with the v2 definition — adopt the redefinition, reject it and add a separate term, or some third route — plus the reasoning, and it should honestly engage with the strongest argument on the losing side. If you can, give us a general criterion for when a spec should revise a term in place versus minting a new identifier, because otherwise we'll relitigate this over anonymized or sale next year. Keep it concrete enough that we could put it to a vote.

Also, at the end, tell me how confident you are in the recommendation and which point you're least sure about. I'd rather know the weak spot before I defend it in a meeting.
