I run a casual Sunday discussion group for about twelve adults — we meet in a community center, people bring snacks, nobody has any training in linguistics or philosophy, but we like getting into how language actually works. Lately ive been stuck on the way a conversation carries around a pile of unstated assumptions. Like if someone asks "have you stopped leaving your dishes in the sink?" the question already assumes you were leaving dishes in the sink, and if you just answer "no I havent" you've kind of accepted that part. Or when a friend says "even my cousin knows how to use the remote now" and nobody asks why my cousin is supposed to be the person least likely to know it — the thing just slips through and everyone's nodding. It feels like a game where you can add stuff to the shared table just by talking as if it were already there, and if nobody objects in the moment, the table gets quietly updated.

I want an activity for the group that makes this felt rather than explained, so that by the end people are a bit spooked by how easily they went along with it. We have around 75 minutes, a whiteboard, and can use paper and pens if needed. No lecture up front — I want folks to stumble into the problem themselves, and only hear the vocabulary for what happened during the debrief. Could you design a game or role-play with actual rules and small stakes where people end up either sneaking in unstated assumptions, letting other people's assumptions pass, or paying a real price when they try to object to every single one? The incentives should be set up so that both moves — challenging everything and challenging nothing — go wrong in interesting ways, and the real tension is in the middle. Then give me a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the underlying idea, says what predictably goes wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it, and is honest about which part of the phenomenon a game like this just can't reproduce, so I'll have to supply that myself afterwards.
