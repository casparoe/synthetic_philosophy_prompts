I work as a software engineer, and when a piece of reasoning looks suspicious I have a habit of trying to isolate the exact step where it fails, the way I would isolate a bad line in a diff. A dispute with a friend has left me unable to do that, so I would like a careful diagnosis. I have been vegan for ethical reasons. A colleague of mine keeps rescue hens and told me that the hens lay unfertilized eggs that just rot if nobody takes them; she offered me some. I was inclined to accept, but a friend said this was inconsistent with my veganism, and he then reconstructed my inclination as an argument:

(1) If an action neither causes suffering nor increases the demand for animal exploitation, then the action is morally permissible.
(2) Taking eggs laid by a rescued hen that no one would otherwise use causes no suffering and increases no demand for animal exploitation: the hen already exists, is already being cared for, and the eggs would simply be discarded.
(3) Therefore, taking and eating such eggs is morally permissible.
(4) The same reasoning extends to other animal products that would otherwise go to waste, such as dumpster-dived food containing dairy or meat, roadkill, and wool sheared from sheep kept as companion animals.
(5) Therefore, a vegan who consumes animal products only in cases where they would otherwise be wasted is doing nothing morally wrong.

He said this is roughly the position that online communities call freeganism. I half-suspect that there is a bug here, perhaps in step (1) or in the way the word demand is being used, but I cannot articulate it. Please work through the argument step by step. Is it valid? If it is not valid, identify the exact step at which the inference fails. If it is valid, identify which premise, or hidden assumption, is most reasonably rejected, and explain what rejecting it would imply for whether consuming such byproducts is permissible.
