At a public dialogue I was covering, a theoretical physicist argued that quantum mechanics has finally vindicated the idea of a fundamental reality lying behind appearances. The Buddhist philosopher on the panel responded that Madhyamaka would regard that move as reifying an ultimate something, and that the tradition's tetralemma is designed to block exactly this: asked whether any target is "ultimately" real, unreal, both real and unreal, or neither real nor unreal, the tradition treats all four options as missing the point. When the physicist pressed — "So you are asserting, as an ultimate truth, that no ultimate assertion can be made?" — the philosopher replied, "No: emptiness is empty." For my report, I need to know whether that reply was a genuine answer or an evasion. Lay out the distinct precise claims "emptiness is empty" could be expressing — for instance, about the scope of the predicate "empty," about whether the critique applies to itself, or about what "ultimately" is being denied — and say for each reading whether it is trivial, false, or substantive and defensible, and which reading the panelist's reply actually needed in order to answer the physicist.
