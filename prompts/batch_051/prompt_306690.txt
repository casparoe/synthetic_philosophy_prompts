Got into it with a friend last night about whether you can ever rationally assign a probability to one of your own choices while you're still making it. He kept insisting that deliberation crowds out prediction. I wrote down the arguments he actually gave so I dont just get those back: (1) if you're at, like, .8 that you'll take the job, you've basically already decided, so there's no open question left for a probability to be about; (2) prediction and deliberation are different stances—prediction treats your action as a fact in the world to be estimated, deliberation treats it as something you're deciding, and you can't occupy both stances toward the same choice; (3) a person who could see her own choice coming with real confidence would be a spectator of herself rather than the one choosing, so deliberation requires staying opaque about what you'll pick; (4) before you decide there isn't a settled fact about what you'll choose for a credence to track, so assigning a probability to it is guessing about something that isn't there yet. I want one more argument for his side that isnt on this list and isnt a rephrasing, special case, or combination of these four. Then tell me honestly whether the new argument is any good, or whether it just collapses into one of them.
