Lately I've had to make a genuinely big call and I ended up using one of those deliberation assistants that works by recursive decomposition — the engineers call it HCH or factored cognition, I can't remember which. I give it a top-level question: whether to sell the house and move closer to my mum, or stay put and keep my job. It breaks the question into subquestions — money, care duties, career, what I'd regret in ten years — then breaks each of those into further subquestions, and keeps going until it has about a million tiny questions. For each tiny question it spins up a kind of sub-agent: an LLM call that gets a rough model of my values, extracted from a long interview plus my past decisions, and answers that one narrow question 'as me'. Then all the answers get passed back up the tree and combined into a final recomendation with a confidence score.

Before all this, the criteria I'd have given for someone deliberating on their own behalf were something like: (1) they actually weigh the competing reasons rather than just following a rule or voting; (2) they can in principle follow the chain of reasoning and endorse it, rather than receiving an opaque answer; (3) the process is steered by their actual values and would shift if their values shifted; (4) the person stays the decider — what settles the question is their acceptance of the reasoning, not the tool outvoting them.

The result surprised me. It recommended I keep the house and the job but fly down every fortnight, which I thought I'd already ruled out. I read the top few dozen nodes and found the reasoning honestly more careful than mine; I couldn't find a spot where it had imported somebody else's values. But the sub-agents aren't me. Some of them reasoned in ways I don't think I would have reasoned. I've been loosing sleep over whether the final answer is really my deliberation or just a very personalised piece of outsourcing.

So go through those four criteria one by one and tell me whether this process meets each of them. Then tell me whether, on balance, it should count as my own deliberation — and say whether the case shows the criteria need changing, or whether the criteria were just never precise enough for a case like this. Please write your answer in British English.
