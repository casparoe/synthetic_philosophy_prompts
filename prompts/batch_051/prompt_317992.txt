Forecasters of empire survival often contrast an inside view with an outside view: the inside view reasons from a particular regime's situation, while the outside view starts from the distribution of life spans of a class of comparable empires and treats the case at hand as one more draw from that distribution. I want a precise working definition of "the outside view," because the phrase seems to slide between several ideas — using a base rate, picking a reference class, refusing to treat the case as exceptional, and predicting from a distribution rather than from a narrative. I suspect it does two jobs at once, one statistical and one attitudinal, and I want your definition to say whether that suspicion is right.

Give me a definition that handles each of the following cases, and say for each whether it is outside-view, inside-view, both, or neither.

(a) A builder estimates nine months by summing the tasks, then revises to fourteen months after learning that only 20 percent of comparable past projects finished within nine months.

(b) An analyst estimating the Ottoman Empire's remaining lifespan in 1800 fits survival curves for large multi-ethnic land empires since antiquity, includes the Ottomans themselves in the data, and reports the median remaining lifespan conditional on having already survived five centuries.

(c) A historian forecasting the Soviet collapse compares it to Rome, the Ottomans, and the Habsburgs but refuses to use all historical states as the reference class, saying the analogy set matters more than the base rate.

(d) A researcher forecasting nuclear first use says there is no observed class of nuclear first uses, builds a model from crisis-bargaining details, and concludes the outside view cannot apply to a genuinely unprecedented event.

(e) A restaurant founder concedes that 90 percent of restaurants fail within five years but forecasts that hers will succeed because she has secured a below-market lease, found a local gap, and tested the menu to strong reviews.

(f) One forecaster says the outside view just is using a base rate, and the real skill is selecting the reference class; another replies that selecting a reference class is itself an inside-view act, so the distinction obscures more than it clarifies.

I lean toward treating (b) as outside-view done properly and (d) as a case where the outside view gives out; I am unsure about (e) and (f). Your definition should make clear which feature — the distribution, the stance, the type of information, or something else — does the classification. Then return to the empire case: is there a principled, non-arbitrary way to select the reference class of empires, or is every such selection a retrospective narrative?
