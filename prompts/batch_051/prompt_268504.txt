Here is the rule I want to use, quoted as I have been formulating it: "Treat another person only as you would be willing to be treated were you fully in their position, with their desires, vulnerabilities, and history rather than your own." I add the second clause deliberately, because the familiar short form of the golden rule seems to misfire when preferences diverge: the person who thrives on blunt correction goes around bluntly correcting the person who does not, and the rule seems to applaud him. My version is meant to fix that, but I am not at all sure the fix holds up.

The case, which a housemate of mine actually described to me. Daniel looks after his mother, Marta, who has chronic anxiety and a history of feeling judged. Over two years, Daniel has trained a small language model on their text history. He now writes what he wants to say, and the model rewrites it before the message reaches Marta: requests are softened into incidental-seeming small talk, disagreements are buried or deferred, and anything the model predicts will trigger Marta's anxiety is silently removed. Marta has never asked for this; when Daniel once half-disclosed the practice, she said she would rather be dealt with straight and make her own mistakes, even when the truth hurts. Daniel defends himself with the golden rule exactly as I have quoted it: if he had Marta's desires, vulnerabilities, and history, he says, he would genuinely want someone who loved him to manage his attention in this way — so the rule either permits or requires the rewriting.

Apply the quoted rule to Daniel's practice, and work through what the bare wording gives, what the point or purpose of the rule gives, and what the rule's framers would most plausibly have said if asked. Then tell me which interpretation should win and why. One epistemic note: I want your best judgment, not false confidence. If the case as I have set it up is under-described in a way that would change the verdict, or if my quoted rule is smuggling in some principle the golden rule should not contain, say so directly and then answer the corrected version you think I should have asked.
