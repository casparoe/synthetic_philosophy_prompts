Condorcet died in 1794, long before the discursive dilemma was formulated in the theory of judgment aggregation. In the dilemma, a panel votes separately on two conditions and on a conclusion the two conditions jointly entail. Each member's individual judgments are internally consistent, yet majority voting on each proposition can produce a collectively inconsistent set: three judges might divide so that one accepts both conditions and the conclusion, a second accepts the first condition but not the second and rejects the conclusion, and a third accepts the second but not the first and rejects the conclusion—yielding a majority for each condition and a majority against the conclusion. Condorcet never encountered this problem, but he had thought more deeply than almost anyone about majority rule, cyclical majorities, and the epistemic value of collective judgment. Reconstruct how Condorcet would most likely have diagnosed the discursive dilemma and what remedy he would have favored. Would he have classified it with his voting paradox, treated it as a distinct threat to the jury theorem's claim that majority rule tracks the truth, or drawn some other line? Be explicit about which parts of your reconstruction rest on Condorcet's known commitments and which parts are extrapolation beyond them.
