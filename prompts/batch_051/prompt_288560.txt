After a long argument with a friend about whether pain is bad in itself, I am trying to isolate the conceptual issue underneath our disagreement. In pain asymbolia, people report having pain — they can locate it and judge its intensity — but they say it does not bother them, and they show no urge to withdraw from it. This is usually treated as a dissociation between pain's sensory-discriminative component and its affective-motivational component, with only the latter carrying the unpleasantness. Does this show that the badness of pain is not intrinsic to the pain itself, but belongs to a component that merely accompanies it in ordinary cases? Or should we instead say that asymbolics do not have pain in the ordinary sense, because unpleasantness is part of the concept of pain?
