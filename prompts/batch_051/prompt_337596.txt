A close friend has spent the past year practicing meditation and reading Stoic authors. He now reports that nothing disturbs him any longer—no anger, fear, or grief gets through—and he speaks of this as an unqualified achievement. I have been noticing a cost, however. He no longer grows angry about the political developments that once moved him to volunteer and organize, and when his long-term relationship ended he described the separation with no evident sorrow and suggested that mourning would have been a failure of self-command. When I said something valuable might be lost along with the distress, he answered that being moved by what lies outside one's control is simply a kind of servitude. Who is right: is being untroubled an unqualified good, or is he paying a hidden price? And if there is a price, what exactly is the value of remaining able to be disturbed?
