In the *Philosophy of Right*, Hegel defends war by comparing it to the movement of the sea: a calm sea stagnates, and the wind must stir it to keep it from putrefaction. The analogy is meant to support the conclusion that war is not a mere misfortune but has an ethical function in sustaining the health of a people. Give me the full correspondence table for this analogy—what is the wind, what is the sea, what is the stagnation, what does the stirring restore—and then identify the structural disanalogies between a natural process and the life of a political community. Finally, tell me whether Hegel's ethical conclusion survives those disanalogies or whether the physical picture was doing the argumentative work.
