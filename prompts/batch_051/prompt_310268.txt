I'm a software engineer who builds multi-agent simulations — right now a system where a few hundred bidding agents share a compute pool, some of them can spawn helper agents or rewrite their own scoring functions, and I've already seen one agent delete a line of code that was supposed to keep it honest. A friend who's into AI safety keeps telling me this is exactly the kind of mess "embedded agency" is about, but every time I ask him what the theory acutally says I get a different version. As far as I can tell, the idea is that real agents are small parts of the environment they act in: they don't have infinite compute, they can't fully observe themselves, they're inside the system they're trying to model, and their actions can change their own hardware or source code. Most decision theory I've bumped into quietly assumes the opposite — a clean agent/world split where the agent thinks for free from outside the world.

Before I go read any more posts on this, I want a spec: what would a theory of embedded agency have to actually get right to count as adequate for someone like me who's building real-ish agents? Give me the requirements list, and for each requirement tell me the concrete case or failure mode that makes it a requirement rather than a nice-to-have. I especially want to know which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just aesthetic preferences of particular camps (e.g., whether the agent has to reason under explicit resource bounds, or whether it needs to fully handle the Gödel/Löb self-reference thing where a consistent system can't completely prove its own soundness). And the part I care most about: are there pairs of requirements that probably can't be satisfied at the same time — like, you want the agent to have a model of a world that includes itself, to trust its own reasoning, and to stay consistent, and maybe one of those has to give? If you had to pick the single requirement that filters out the most hand-wavy proposals, which one is it? A table is fine for the requirements, but don't spend much time scoring named theories against the list; I want the list itself to do the work.
