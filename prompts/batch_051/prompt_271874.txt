I'm a retired physical chemist, so I've spent a career doing back-of-the-envelope arguments where you patch together three or four approximate lines of reasoning and somehow the result comes out believable. I've started reading philosophy of science and keep running into the phrase the presumption of independence — roughly, the licence to treat two pieces of evidence or two arguments as independent unless you have a specific reason to think they are connected. It's doing a lot of work, and I don't think I could say precisely when it's warranted. I'm after the justificatory question, not a description of when people happen to make the assumption.

So here's what I want. Give me a working definition of the presumption of independence, precise enough to decide the following eight cases. Run the definition through each case and give a verdict. If your definition gives what you think is the wrong answer, or no answer, say so, then either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. I'm not bothered about what the literature says; I want your definition and how it performs.

1. Two successive flips of the same coin after a good shake.
2. Me re-checking my own arithmetic on the same bit of paper twenty minutes later.
3. Two labs measuring the same physical constant with the same apparatus and the same protocol, with no contact between them.
4. Two rather different methods for measuring the speed of light, say time-of-flight and cavity interferometry.
5. Two witnesses to a road accident who both saw it but then discussed it before giving statements.
6. A Monte Carlo estimate and a completely different analytic approximation for the same quantity, both derived from the same underlying model.
7. Two purported proofs of a conjecture that each depend on the same unproved lemma.
8. Two opinion polls run by different firms using the same sampling frame and the same weighting proceedure.

For what it's worth: I'd place 1 as clearly independent and 2 and 7 as clearly not, and I genuinely don't know about 3, 4, 6 and 8. But I'd sooner you tell me where my instincts are wrong than adjust the definition to fit them.
