I'm a PhD student in decision theory, working through imprecise probability stuff, and I keep running into something I don't have a clean word for. Suppose two people both end up with the same interval of credence, say [0.4, 0.6], for some claim. One of them got there from a single small pilot study with messy controls; the other got there from a whole meta-analysis where the studies conflicted and pulled the estimate around, so the interval wound up the same width. They'd agree on the range, maybe even on the midpoint, but it feels wrong to say they're in the same epistemic situation. The second person's [0.4, 0.6] is held up by more — more evidence, more checks, more opportunities for the estimate to move and not moving, whereas the first one's is basically a guess between two numbers with a bit of data. The interval width captures some of it, but not this.

I've been reading Keynes's A Treatise on Probability, and in Chapter VI, which he calls something like The Weight of Arguments, he draws a distinction between the magnitude of a probability and the amount of evidence the probability rests on. His point, if I'm reading him right, is that two probabilities can be equal in value while one is based on much less evidence than the other, so the weaker one is less supported even though the number is the same. That's basically my pilot study versus meta-analysis case. So what Im after is this: is there an established name in the imprecise probability literature for exactly this — not the spread of the interval, but the amount of evidence behind the interval itself? I know about second-order probabilities, but those are just another numerical layer on top, and I know Ellsberg distinguishes risk from ambiguity, but that's about where the interval comes from, not how supported it is. Is Keynes's "weight" the same thing, and has it been formalized anywhere under a different name? If there's no standard term, I'd want one that separates it cleanly from both second-order probability and the width of the interval, and also from Knightian uncertainty. If there is a term, I'd want to know where it lives in the current literature and whether people think Keynes was pointing at the same phenomenon. I care less about the label itself than about getting the carving right.
