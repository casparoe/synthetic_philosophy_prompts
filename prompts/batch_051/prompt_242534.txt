I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy, and I keep noticing something in ordinary judgments about small promises that I don't have a word for. If I promise to meet a friend at 3 and show up at 3:15, people treat the lateness as a serious breach if she was waiting outside in the cold, but as barely worth mentioning if she ran into someone she liked and had a nice chat. Same with a book returned a week late: it's a big deal if the owner wanted to reread it that week, hardly anything if it sat untouched on her shelf. The promise and the breach are identical; the verdict tracks what the promisee actually lost, not what I commited to. I know that's adjacent to moral luck, but it seems like more than outcome-dependent blame — it's as if the obligation is being retroactively rewritten as "be on time, but only insofar as you'd suffer." Is there an established name for this specific thing, how exactly is it different from plain moral luck, and is the ordinary judgment defensible or does it run together what I owed with what happened to be lost? If there's no name, coin one and give me the boundary cases — I'm not just after a label.
