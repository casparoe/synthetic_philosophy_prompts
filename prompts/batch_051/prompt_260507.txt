Analyze the Luddite machine-breaking in the English textile districts (roughly 1811–1816) through the lens of Marx's theory of technology and automation. Take the following facts as given: skilled artisans — framework knitters, croppers, and handloom weavers — smashed the new wide stocking frames, shearing frames, and, somewhat later, power looms that were displacing their labor, driving down piece rates, and devaluing years of apprenticeship; Parliament made frame-breaking a capital offense; and a combination of trials, executions, and military force had largely suppressed the movement by 1816 or so. Also take as given these elements of Marx's theory: (a) under capitalism, machinery is introduced less to lighten toil than to cheapen labor power, break strikes, and discipline workers; (b) machinery under capitalist relations expropriates skill, turns the worker into an appendage of the machine, and swells a relative surplus population of the unemployed; (c) the workers' mistake was to attack the material instrument rather than the social form in which it is employed, though Marx found this confusion historically understandable; and (d) in the Grundrisse discussion often called the Fragment on Machines, Marx suggests that as production comes to depend on accumulated social knowledge and automation, labor time could cease to be the measure of value, thereby undermining the capitalist mode of production on its own terms.

Run the analysis in both directions. First, give Marx's account of what the Luddites were up against: on his theory, was their resistance an irrational rejection of technology from which workers stood to gain, or a rational, if misdirected, response to the actual distribution of costs and benefits under capitalist automation? Say what Marx would have expected the workers to do, and why. Second, test the fit between theory and episode: did the Luddites really fail to distinguish the machine from its capitalist use, or did many of them selectively target particular owners, particular products, and particular wage arrangements in ways that complicate the story of pure technophobia? Where does Marx's distinction illuminate the episode, and where does it misdescribe or flatten it? Third, use the episode to probe the theory: does Luddism confirm the machinery-as-a-weapon-of-class-rule reading of Capital, the machinery-as-a-material-condition-for-overcoming-capitalism reading of the Grundrisse, or does it cut against both? Conclude by defending a definite verdict on whether Marx's framework explains the Luddite episode better than it distorts it.
