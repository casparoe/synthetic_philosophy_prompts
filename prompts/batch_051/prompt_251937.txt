I'm a novelist, and I've said yes to giving the opening remarks at a small literary festival session called Borges and Time. The room will be half Borges obsessives who catch every borrowed move, and half people who've only vaguely heard of the branching garden and the Aleph. Five minutes, hard cap of 500 words — and I'm not kidding about the cap. I dont want a lecture that explains Borges's views on time, and please dont quote him (I can't have the speech borrowing his actual sentences). What I want is a speech that performs the view instead of explaining it: sequence breaking down, a future memory, the self forking, one ordinary moment containing the whole evening. The scholars should be able to tell exactly which Borges move is being stolen without me ever naming the story or essay; the first-timers should just feel the floor tilt a little and half-understand why afterwards. It has to be spoken, not read — so no bullets, no table, no section headers. Can you write it?
