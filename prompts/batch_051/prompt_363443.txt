Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two economists who've spent a year arguing about what revealed preference theory actually establishes. One side (the "ledger view") holds that utility is just a compact bookkeeping device for summarizing consistent choice, and that it is a category mistake to say choice data reveal how much welfare, satisfaction, or "true utility" somebody got, since the function that rationalizes a batch of choices is arbitrary up to any strictly increasing transformation. The other side (the "welfare view") holds that when someone's choices satisfy the standard consistency axioms, those choices reveal a genuine preference ranking, and that ranking is rich enough to ground welfare claims: you can say the person would be better off choosing A over B. The dispute is specifically about how much a finite batch of choice data can support; dont turn it into a debate about broader welfare policy.

You can assume both sides accept the formal facts: for a finite set of observed price–bundle choices, GARP (the generalized axiom of revealed preference) is the standard necessary-and-sufficient condition for the data to be consistent with utility maximization, and the utility function that does the rationalizing is not unique. The argument is about what that formal result means.

Now write the document this collaboration should have produced, in this exact structure:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign—make these substantive, including claims about what passing GARP does and does not establish.
2. A disagreements table: rows that give each side's wording of its own view (phrased so that side would accept it), with the residual disagreement stated neutrally.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side.
4. For each crux, at least one concrete choice experiment, hypothetical case, or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

I suspect one crux is whether a set of choices that passes GARP but was produced by an agent following a simple rule of thumb (with no internal utility representation) should count as utility maximization. Another is whether menu-dependence and framing effects can be absorbed into some more general utility representation, or whether they instead undermine the welfare reading. Add any other cruxes if you think there are better ones. If you conclude part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. No citations or reading list needed—just work out the positions.
