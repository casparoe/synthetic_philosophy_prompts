At a party last week I got stuck listening to two friends, Dan and Maya, argue about whether it means anything to say a catchphrase "evolves." Dan had described a dumb phrase that keeps spreading around his office as a meme in the evolutionary sense; Maya said that's just a loose analogy and it falls apart if you push it. They went back and forth for ages, and every time one of them landed a point the other came back with something decent, so I honestly couldn't keep score. Here's the exchange as best I can reconstruct it (I've numbered the moves):

1. Dan: It's fair to call the phrase a meme because it copies from person to person, it mutates a little with each retelling, and some versions catch on more than others. That's variation, inheritance, and selection right there.
2. Maya: That's not inheritance. A gene copies itself; a phrase doesn't copy anything. People choose to repeat it, and they usually adapt it to the situation. There's no high-fidelity copying, so calling it "replication" is already the metaphor.
3. Dan: Replication doesn't require perfect copying. Genes mutate too; if copying were exact, evolution would have nothing to work with. The phrase is repeated closely enough, and the punchier versions spread further. That's enough for selection to act on.
4. Maya: But genes are discrete units. You can point to one. With a phrase, what's the unit—the words, the rhythm, the idea behind it, the delivery? If you can't say what's being copied, you can't track lineages, and "selection" is just a story you tell afterward.
5. Dan: Genes are less discrete than you'd think—there's recombination, and whole complexes get selected together. And okay, the units are fuzzy at the edges, but there's still heritable variation in success. Dave's version of the phrase reliably gets bigger laughs, and people pass that version on. That's differential success.
6. Maya: "Gets bigger laughs" just means people like it more. That's ordinary preference, not natural selection. The phrase isn't competing for anything; minds are deciding it's funny. The whole point of Darwinism is to explain order without any chooser, and with culture there's a chooser at every step.
7. Dan: But in biology "fitness" is also just environmental success. If a variant spreads because human minds prefer it, the minds are just the environment. By your standard, prions and computer viruses wouldn't count as evolving, and that seems like setting the bar too high.
8. Maya: Computer viruses are also an analogy—they were designed and iterated by people. And with culture, the "environment" is the same minds doing the copying, so it's not an independent environment at all; it's just what people want. At some point "meme" stops being a discovery and just becomes a synonym for "thing that spreads."
