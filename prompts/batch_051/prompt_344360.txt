At a workshop, a speaker defended hedonism: the view that only pleasure is good and only pain is bad. An audience member objected that pleasures are too various to form one kind — the mild bodily comfort of lying down, the intellectual pleasure of grasping a proof, the satisfaction of winning a contest, the relief of a pain ceasing, the malicious delight in a rival's failure. I am unsure whether this objection is an empirical claim that could be tested or a conceptual point that observation could never settle. Please design the strongest test you can of the claim that all these states share a single underlying quality, a common hedonic tone, that distinguishes them from non-pleasures. Say what result would favor the single-quality view and what result would favor the view that pleasures are only a loose family unified by overlapping similarities, and how one could tell evidence for a genuinely shared quality apart from evidence for a shared label. Then state what part of the disagreement no test can reach — for example, whether the shared feature, if one were found, is what makes pleasures good — and why that residual question is not itself empirically testable.
