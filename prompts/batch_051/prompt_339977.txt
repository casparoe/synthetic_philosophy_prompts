I've been going back and forth about Searle's Chinese room, and I'm curious how far the argument survives when one particular feature of the setup is changed. In the original, a man who knows no Chinese sits inside a room and follows a rulebook that matches incoming Chinese symbols to outgoing Chinese symbols; Searle's conclusion is that neither the man nor the room-as-a-system understands Chinese, because the rulebook is only manipulating formal symbols. Now change the implementation: instead of a hand-written symbol-manipulation program, the rulebook is the trained weights of a modern neural machine-translation model, written out as an enormous finite table of real numbers. The man executes the model's forward pass by hand—one matrix multiplication, nonlinearity, and attention step at a time—using pencil and paper, with no idea what any of the numbers mean. The room's output is a fluent Chinese translation, exactly what the same network would produce if it were run on a GPU.

I want to know whether the analysis changes in two respects. First, does Searle's argument now also show that the model's internal activation vectors—what interpretability researchers describe as features or concepts encoded in the residual stream—do not really represent anything, or is there a coherent position on which the room contains genuine representational content even though nobody in it understands Chinese? Second, suppose someone is a realist about interpretability: they hold that the internal states of a trained network literally represent features of its input, and not merely in an as-if or instrumental sense. Should the fact that the very same vector arithmetic could be carried out by a Searlean clerk who is unaware of any semantics push them away from that realism? Or is the clerk irrelevant, because representational content is fixed by the training history and functional role of the weights rather than by who happens to execute the arithmetic? Work through where the original dialectic is genuinely strained by this variation, and which standard reply it puts the most pressure on.
