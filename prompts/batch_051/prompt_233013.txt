A colleague and I keep going around in circles about whether 'disease' is discovered or partly invented, and I'd rather see how far evidence can settle the thing. The naturalist line is that diseases are biological dysfunctions — a heart that doesn't pump, a virus ravaging the lungs — and that the concept picks out a real kind in nature, with the value judgements (this is bad, this needs treating) tacked on afterwards. The normativist line is that the boundary of the concept is itself value-laden: we decide which bodily states count as diseases partly by deciding which ones we find harmful or undesirable, which is why infertility, obesity and various psychiatric categories slide around as attitudes shift. I'm not asking you to just pick a side. Here's the actual question: how would one test the claim that disease is a value-free biological kind rather than a partly normative concept? Propose the strongest observations or experiments, say which result would push toward naturalism and which toward normativism, and then be honest about what even a strong result for either side would still fail to settle. Please write your answer in British English.
