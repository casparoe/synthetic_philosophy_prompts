Our state court's code of conduct for interpreters says: 'An interpreter shall render the source message accurately and completely, without addition, omission, or explanation, and shall preserve the tone and intent of the speaker.' I'm on the ethics committee, and a case has come up where a witness testified through an interpreter in a language whose word for 'debt' also means 'fault' (the interpreter confirmed both readings are equally live in the original). Asked whether he acknowledged any obligation, the witness answered with a sentence that means, at once, 'I owe him nothing' and 'I am at fault toward him in no way'—the ambiguity was deliberate and was the whole point of the answer. English forced the interpreter to pick one reading; any way of getting both across required a short gloss, which the code appears to call an explanation. The drafters clearly never imagined a message that needs glossing to be faithful. What does the code require here? Give me the textual reading, the purpose reading, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable judge expects, and say which should win and why.
