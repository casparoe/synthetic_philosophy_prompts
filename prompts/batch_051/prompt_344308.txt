I have been rereading the early chapters of Einstein's Relativity: The Special and General Theory, where he introduces the operational definition of simultaneity: for two clocks at rest in an inertial frame, one stipulates that a light signal takes the same time to travel from A to B as from B to A, and then uses this convention to synchronise distant clocks. He goes on to show that events which are simultaneous relative to the embankment are not simultaneous relative to the moving train. I cannot see how this sequence of moves supports the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. If Einstein's definition is explicitly a convention for assigning time coordinates, why do Rietdijk and Putnam treat the relativity of simultaneity as if it revealed something about what exists, rather than as a choice of labelling? Is the block-universe conclusion just a category mistake in which a conventional coordinatisation is mistaken for an ontological discovery about the future? Or does the argument require reading Einstein's operational definition as more than a convention, and what would justify that reading?
