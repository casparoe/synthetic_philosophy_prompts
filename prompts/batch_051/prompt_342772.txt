Two players each destroy a rendered humanoid that has no experiences and cannot feel pain, and both acts are streamed live. The first player, after forty hours of learning a companion character's history and making in-game promises to it, shoots it on a whim; viewers call the act cruel, and some say they find it hard to watch. The second player, in a strategy game, rounds up and deletes hundreds of procedurally generated, anonymous characters as a routine move; viewers treat it as unremarkable, and no one calls it cruelty. Construct the strongest principle you can that would justify treating these two cases differently, for example a principle on which the player's history with a character, rather than the character's own states, does the moral work, then test that principle against nearby cases and give an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept, revised, or abandoned.
