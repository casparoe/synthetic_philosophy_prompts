My aesthetics professor keeps saying that photographs are transparent—that when you look at a photo you literally see the object through it, not merely a depiction of it. Last week he used it to dismiss my point that photographs are constructed images (framing, lens choice, exposure, development choices), so a photo can't give you the same kind of access as actually being there. His comeback was something like: photographs are transparent, so you do see through them, and the selection involved is no different in kind from deciding where to point your eyes or which way to turn your head. I'm not sure if that's a deep point or a pun. Can you lay out the different precise claims that slogan might be expressing, as a list I could bring to office hours? I'd want at least: the boring causal claim that the image depends on the scene in a belief-independent way, the stronger claim that a photo is more like a mirror or telescope than like a painting, and the really strong claim that looking at the photo puts you in literal visual contact with the object itself even if it's long gone. For each reading, say whether its plausible and whether it actually gets him to the conclusion that my constructed-image worry fails. Also say something about whether the time delay between the scene and my seeing the print matters to any of these readings.
