I am deciding how to spend roughly a year of research on low-impact AI. I have a background in machine learning rather than moral philosophy, so tractability with current methods matters, but I am willing to spend some of the year on conceptual work. The question I want to make progress on is whether an agent's side effects can be identified and scored well enough to be discouraged without a full specification of human value. By an impact measure I mean a function that takes a state or a trajectory — typically relative to a baseline, such as what would have happened without the agent, or the set of options available before the agent acted — and returns a penalty for how much the agent has changed the world or closed off options. The ambition is that a suitably penalised agent would keep options open and avoid irreversible changes even though the measure is supposed to remain neutral about which options humans actually care about.

I could spend the year on any of several sub-questions: how to choose or construct the baseline; how to aggregate many distinct kinds of change into a single score; how to avoid penalising the agent's intended task so heavily that the agent becomes useless; how to handle side effects that run through other agents or delayed causal chains; whether the relevant quantity is the range of outcomes from which high reward could still be achieved (sometimes called attainable utility), or whether side effects should instead be defined directly over physical changes; and the upstream question whether any measure of this kind can stay value-neutral once a baseline, a set of dimensions, and an aggregation rule have been chosen.

I want a defended agenda, not a survey. Rank the sub-questions — correcting my list where it misdivides the territory — by how much of the year they deserve. For each, say whether it is upstream of the others or downstream from them, how tractable it is with current tools, how neglected it seems relative to the rest and why, and what would change downstream if I settled it one way rather than another. Then tell me which two or three I should actually pursue, in order, and defend that ordering, including what result or evidence would make you demote the current first choice and promote something else. Please write the answer in British English.
