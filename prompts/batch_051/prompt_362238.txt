Write the joint statement that a committed counterspeech advocate and a committed contagion-focused platform-safety advocate could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about the moderation practice of allowing propaganda—recruitment videos, edited atrocity footage, forged documents, coordinated meme clusters—to remain up when it is embedded in a clearly critical, educational, journalistic, or scholarly frame, while removing or downranking the same material when it is posted standalone. Treat the first camp as holding a context-first view: the frame under which audiences encounter propaganda does more work than the bare content, so contextualized reproduction is generally protective and should be permitted with clear labeling. Treat the second camp as holding an exposure-first view: propaganda operates substantially through attention, repetition, and emotional uptake that can outrun any framing, so even contextualized reproduction spreads the thing it claims to contain and should face heavy friction. Do not reduce the two camps to "free speech versus safety" slogans; each side has a considered view about both the value of expression and the reality of harm.

The statement should include:
(a) a working characterization of "propaganda" neutral enough for both sides to use for the rest of the document, followed by a numbered list of agreed claims—about what propaganda does, about what contextualized-reproduction policies actually select for, about the cases where the two camps agree, and about what the dispute is not about;
(b) a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement is stated twice, once in phrasing the context-first camp would sign and once in phrasing the exposure-first camp would sign, with a note on whether each row is substantive, partly verbal, or a difference in how harm is operationalized; and
(c) a cruxes section: for each crux, state a concrete case, observational design, or prospective study that both sides agree would bear on it; record each side's prediction in advance; and say what outcome would move each side.

Use three touchstone cases throughout: a de-radicalization researcher who posts an annotated recruitment video with interleaved rebuttals; a documentary that embeds a short propaganda clip without running commentary inside an otherwise denunciatory film; and an academic archive that hosts full propaganda specimens behind a plain "for research" notice. If you conclude that part of the dispute is verbal or rests on counting different populations' harms, say precisely which part and rewrite the contested claim so the non-verbal remainder is exposed. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this terrain, and do not present a contested empirical claim about media effects or a contested characterization of platform behavior as consensus. Write in American English.
