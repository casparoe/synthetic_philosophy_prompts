I spent my career as a natural scientist and have recently begun reading constitutional law. I keep wanting to treat a constitution as a system that specifies transitions between states of a political order, and to ask what happens when the system enters a state for which no transition is specified.

Suppose a hypothetical but otherwise stable parliamentary republic in the present day enacts, by amendment, the following rule: if the president (the head of state, with largely ceremonial powers but the formal authority to appoint the prime minister) and the prime minister (the head of government) are both unable to exercise their offices at the same time — through death, resignation, or incapacity — no one may exercise the powers of either office until a special election has been held and its result certified. The amendment says nothing about who may call that election, who may certify it, who acts in the interim, or who commands the armed forces; the existing succession rules cover only the case in which one office falls vacant, not both. On the day the double vacancy occurs, the country has no lawful interim government.

Predict, with reasoning, how the first such double vacancy plays out over the following weeks. I want the chain, not just the outcome: which actors step into the gap — the senior civil service, the courts, the military, subnational governments, the leaders of the major parties — under what implicit norms or justifications, and why those rather than others. For each, say whether it has the capacity and the perceived legitimacy to act, and how that balance shifts as the days pass.

Then tell me which links in your prediction are robust features of any constitutional order with a gap of this kind, and which depend on parochial features of the country: the prestige of its judges, the discipline of its civil service, public attitudes toward the military, the timing of the vacancy. What would have to be different — in the constitutional text, in the political culture, or in the circumstances of the vacancy — for a different actor to fill the gap, or for the episode to end in genuine crisis rather than quiet improvisation?
