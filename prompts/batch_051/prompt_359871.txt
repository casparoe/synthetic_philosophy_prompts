For a paper I am drafting in the philosophy of language, I have been assembling a position across several adjacent debates, and before I commit it to print I want a consistency audit rather than a defense of any one plank. The view, as I would currently state it, is this:

1. Confirmation holism. No individual sentence of an empirical theory carries its own testable content. Experience tests the theory as a whole, and every statement is revisable in principle, because enough compensating revisions elsewhere can always reconcile a recalcitrant observation with a retained sentence.

2. No analytic–synthetic boundary. There is no principled distinction between truths that hold in virtue of meaning and truths that hold in virtue of fact. The boundary between the two is something we project onto our current willingness to revise.

3. Necessary a posteriori truths. Certain identity claims — for example, that water is H2O — are true in every possible world even though they can only be known empirically. Their necessity is metaphysical, not a matter of meaning or of immunity from revision.

4. A robust a priori. Elementary logic and arithmetic are knowable independently of experience, and this independence marks an epistemological difference in kind between them and ordinary empirical truths, not merely a difference in degree of entrenchment or breadth of acceptance.

5. Lexical entailments. In compositional semantics I routinely treat examples like 'bachelor' entailing 'unmarried' or 'buy' entailing a transfer of possession as meaning relations fixed by the lexicon, not as ordinary facts about the world.

6. Possible-worlds semantics. I analyze sentence meaning and the truth conditions of modal and counterfactual claims in terms of functions from possible worlds to truth values, and I use possible worlds as a non-metaphorical piece of semantic machinery.

I cannot tell where this package strains. Which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each real tension? Rank them by how much the view would have to change to fix them, and for each say what minimally would have to be given up or weakened. If any apparent tension dissolves on a more careful reading, say what the needed distinction is. I would rather be told that the view is unstable as stated than have a conflict soft-pedaled.
