I've been re-reading Hofstadter and keep returning to the pair of ideas crystallized in Escher's Drawing Hands: the tangled hierarchy — a loop in which the higher level depends on the lower level while the lower level also depends on the higher one — and the inviolate level, the level of description (symbols, meanings, interpretations) that cannot be eliminated in favor of a lower level without changing the system into something that no longer does the thing you were trying to explain. A long argument on a rationalist forum about whether the meaning level is merely a convenient fiction convinced me that no amount of assertion will settle this; I want people to run into the structure themselves before anyone names it.

I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar called Minds, Machines, and Meaning; there are about sixteen students, and I have an eighty-minute block. Please design a structured activity or game for this group. The participants should first encounter the loop directly — the moment where an attempt to ground the higher level in the lower one forces them to smuggle the higher level back in — and only later have the phenomenon identified in the debrief. I would like the activity to involve real roles and real artifacts (paper, tokens, a restricted code, instructions that get hidden or destroyed, or something similar), not just discussion. Give me the complete runnable plan: a materials list, the rules or role instructions in full, a timed sequence for the eighty minutes, and a debrief script that maps exactly what happened in the exercise onto the notions of a tangled hierarchy and an inviolate level.

One constraint from experience: I have tried a naive message-passing-through-layers exercise before, and it only taught lossy communication — the group learned that information degrades, not why the higher level refuses to disappear. Please avoid that failure mode, or build the activity so that the degradation itself becomes diagnostic of the inviolate level. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how I should handle it, and be explicit about which feature of the inviolate level the activity cannot reproduce and what I will need to supply in the debrief to cover that gap.
