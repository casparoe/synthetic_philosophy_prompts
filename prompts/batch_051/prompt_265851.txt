I've spent enough years reading rationalist blogs and forums to feel fluent in Bayesian basics, but I only recently noticed that nearly everything I believe about objective Bayesianism traces back to a single summary I encountered long ago and never checked. That summary ran roughly as follows. When your information about a set of mutually exclusive possibilities consists only of coarse constraints — a die has six faces, or an ensemble's expected energy is fixed — the rational probability assignment is the one that maximizes Shannon entropy subject to those constraints. Shannon entropy is a measure of how much information a distribution contains, so maximizing it gives the distribution that builds in the least information beyond your actual constraints; it is the least opinionated, most conservative assignment consistent with what you know. Jaynes showed that this converts the old principle of indifference from an ad hoc rule into a theorem: with no constraints beyond exhaustiveness and mutual exclusivity, maximum entropy simply delivers the uniform distribution. And because the procedure satisfies the Shore–Johnson consistency axioms, the maximum entropy distribution is provably the unique rational prior, the same for every reasoner facing the same information — so the problem of objective priors is solved. The summary closed by saying that residual worries about maximum entropy updating conflicting with ordinary Bayesian conditionalization are mostly technical wrinkles that the Shore–Johnson result already cleaned up.

I find myself inclined to believe most of this, since it matches the way forum commenters talk about maximizing entropy as though it settles what a rational prior has to be. But I suspect the paragraph contains one or two quiet sleights of hand, and I would like an accuracy audit before I repeat or rely on any of it. Go through the summary claim by claim: which parts a defender of the maximum entropy approach would endorse, which parts they would wince at or insist on restating, and which parts are simply wrong. I would especially like to know whether the move from "the procedure is consistent" to "it yields the unique rational prior" genuinely goes through, and whether the conditionalization worry is actually answered by the axioms cited or is a separate, still-open problem. As a reader rather than a grader, I do not know where the errors are; I only want to know what to trust and which of the summary's conclusions would survive if it were corrected.
