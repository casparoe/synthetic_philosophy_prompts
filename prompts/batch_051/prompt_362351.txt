I’ve become pretty convinced by a view in the neighborhood of Hofstadter’s Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery. The view is this: in certain symmetric collective-decision problems, the rational thing to do is pick the action that would be best if everyone who is like you in the relevant ways picked the same action, even when there is no communication and your choice cannot causally influence theirs. The usual game-theoretic reasoning in a prisoner's dilemma says to defect because whatever the other person does, you do better by defecting. But I think that treats your own choice as independent of the choice of another equally rational mind facing the identical problem, and that this independence is exactly what should not be assumed. In the Platonia setup, a letter goes out to a huge number of people and each can send a postcard or not. If exactly one card arrives, that person gets an enormous prize. If zero arrive, or more than one arrive, nobody gets anything. The "rational" answer, if everyone reasons the standard way, is to send, but then everyone sends and everyone loses. The only way anyone wins is for the group to somehow select a single sender from among themselves, even though all the would-be senders are symmetric. The Luring Lottery variant, with its huge temptation to break the pact, seems to me to make the same point.

I want to know what would count against this position. Not the stock objection that it assumes your decision causes the other person's decision, because I don't think that lands. I want the actual defeaters: what empirical results, arguments, formal results, or edge cases would make a committed defender of this view concede its wrong, or at least restricted to a much narrower range of cases than they thought. Which things count as merely awkward, and which would be fatal? Are there asymmetric cases where the view cannot even be stated? What would experimental findings from people playing Platonia-like games actually show, and how would a defender distinguish "people are too irrational to find the right answer" from "the model is wrong about what the right answer is"? And has the position been insulated with qualifications to the point that it risks nothing, and if so, is that a problem for a view of this kind?
