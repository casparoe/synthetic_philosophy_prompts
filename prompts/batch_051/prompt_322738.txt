Following a long argument with a friend who kept insisting that any start date for the Anthropocene is a political choice dressed up as stratigraphy, I am preparing a small podcast interview with a researcher who defends the mid-twentieth-century start associated with the Anthropocene Working Group: the Anthropocene as a formal epoch beginning around the Great Acceleration, with a GSSP tied to a mid-century plutonium or radiocarbon signal. Write me eight or so questions that press the view rather than letting the guest restate it. I want at least one question, each with a follow-up keyed to the standard reply, on the following: (1) why the Anthropocene should be a dated epoch with a GSSP at all, rather than a diachronous geological event with no single boundary; (2) why the authority of the ICS/IUGS in deciding this does not just relocate the politics into a human institution; (3) how a mid-century boundary can be globally synchronous when industrialization and its direct markers were so regionally uneven; and (4) why the 1610 Orbis spike and the early-agriculture candidates fail by criteria strong enough to survive the same scrutiny the guest applies to the mid-century marker. End with a question about what kind of evidence or re-analysis, if it appeared, would genuinely lead the guest to abandon the mid-century date.
