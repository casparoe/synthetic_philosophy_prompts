In our Buddhist epistemology seminar we've been working with a loose translation of Dharmakīrti: 'Perception is a cognition that is non-conceptual and does not mislead; and since whatever is real is momentary, perception can only take a momentary particular as its object.' Once the rule was on the table, someone brought in a diagnostic AI that labels chest x-rays 'pneumonia.' Internally there's no concept of pneumonia and no reasoning from other findings: it's just a cascade of weighted sums over pixels, and it outpredicts expert radiologists. By Dharmakīrti's clauses, is the machine perceiving, inferring, or neither? Does it grasp a momentary particular when it labels a lesion, or does its output always name a universal? Run the case through the wording, the purpose of the perception/inference distiction, and what Dharmakīrti would probably have said if shown the machine. If those three readings come apart, tell me which one should win and why.
