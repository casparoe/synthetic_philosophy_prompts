I am a typeface designer and letterform cataloguer, and I am trying to make explicit the rule I use when I sort ambiguous marks into "an a" and "not an a." My verdicts are stable one by one, but I cannot recover the principle behind them. Each case below is a mark seen in isolation, so no surrounding word can settle it.

1. A single-storey "a" — a closed bowl with a vertical stem on its right — set in a geometric sans. Verdict: an "a."
2. A double-storey "a" — the two-storey form familiar from most serif book faces — which shares almost no contour with (1). Verdict: an "a."
3. The very same single-storey outline as (1), presented as the Greek letter alpha in a Greek alphabet chart. Verdict: not an "a."
4. A single-storey "a" wrapped in a complete loop so that the whole mark reads as the at-sign "@". Verdict: not an "a"; I do not judge the enclosed strokes separately, even though they still resemble a single-storey "a."
5. A handwritten "d" whose ascender has been erased down to nothing, leaving only the bowl and its right-hand stem — the same shape as (1). Verdict: an "a," not a defective "d."

Cases 1–3 seem to say that what matters is the character assigned by the writing system, not the outline: different outlines count as the same letter within one script, and one outline splits across scripts. But case 5 seems to say the reverse: a mark that was not produced as an "a" at all is recruited into "a"-hood by its shape alone. Case 4 sits between them, since the enclosing ring seems to dissolve an "a" shape into a different sign.

I would like a principle — or a small set of candidate principles — that gets these five verdicts, or close. Please state it precisely, with the case that motivates each clause, and say what it predicts for one or two cases I have not listed. Then apply it to the case I cannot settle: a single-storey "a" whose bowl is left open at the top, accepted without hesitation in fast handwriting but striking me as a mistake in a carefully drawn font. I keep going back and forth on whether that is still an "a." If no principle gets all five, tell me which of my verdicts I should overturn and why.
