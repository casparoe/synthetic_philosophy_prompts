My opening move: I'll grant that typogenetics is a neat toy, but the Gödelian part is exactly where it seems to collapse. In Gödel, diagonalization gives you a fixed point: a formula that provably refers to its own Gödel number, and you can't tell the story without it. In actual DNA there's no fixed point, no diagonal step, and nothing that "says" anything of itself. A polymerase just walks along a template and copies it; the strand never interprets anything — the cell's existing machinery does that. So where, concretely, is the analogue of Gödel numbering and the fixed-point lemma in the cell? And what does calling replication "self-reference" explain that "template copying by polymerase" doesn't already cover?
