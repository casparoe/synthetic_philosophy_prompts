I'm a PhD student in collective decision-making and I also help run a small not-for-profit that organises citizens' assemblies for local councils. We run maybe eight of these a year, and the councils really do take the votes seriously, so the way we frame the outcome changes what happens next. The thing I keep going back and forth on: when are we justified in presenting a majority vote as an epistemic signal (the assembly, on balance, thinks the proposition is true, and thats a reason for the council to think so too) rather than just as an aggregation of preferences. The Condorcet jury theorem gives the classic conditions—there's a correct answer, voters are more likely right than wrong, votes are sufficiently independent—but our organisers don't have a way to apply them in real time. Independence especially: our participants have usually all read the same briefing pack, so they are never literally independent, and we need a workable line between shared evidence (probably fine) and shared bias (not fine).

Could you turn this into a decision procedure we can actually use? Two organisers, one afternoon, only whatever they can collect before and during the assembly (calibration questions with known answers, questionnaires on information sources, that sort of thing), no statistical model. The output is a short framing note the council's scrutiny officer signs off on, so it has to be defensible without any theory. I need the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no (treat as preference only), and where it hands off to the council because it's a value judgement rather than something the procedure can settle. Then run it on two cases: (i) an assembly votes on whether a proposed bypass will deliver the journey-time savings the transport consultants claim, and (ii) an assembly votes on whether the borough should trial a four-day week for council staff. I suspect one of those comes out in a way we won't like; don't soften it. Also say, plainly, what the procedure can't settle even in principle about majority votes as truth-trackers.

Please write it in British English, and don't worry about citations—I'll fit it to the literature myself.
