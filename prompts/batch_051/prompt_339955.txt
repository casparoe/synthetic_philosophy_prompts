I keep reaching for a notion that I suspect philosophers of science must have made precise, but I cannot find the right name, and I am fairly sure a bare name would not settle what I want to settle. Consider two theories, both known to be false. One says the planets are carried round the Sun in perfect circles; the other says they are carried round the Earth in perfect circles with the Earth at the centre. Both are false, yet it seems absurd to call them equally false: the first gets the centre right and errs only about the shape of the paths, while the second errs about both. That difference, the one theory's being closer to the truth than the other, does real work: it is part of why we would teach the first over the second and would sink effort into amending the one rather than the other. The trouble is that I cannot make closeness to the truth precise. If I measure it by how much a theory gets right, a tautology, which gets nothing wrong, comes out perfect rather than empty; if I measure it by how much a theory says, a contradiction says everything and comes out best; and two false theories can be closer in different respects, so it is not obvious there is a single ranking. My working thought is that the notion must combine something like true content with the absence of false content, but I doubt that is the whole story. Does this have an established name, and do the established attempts capture exactly the feature I am describing, or something adjacent? If nothing established fits, propose a term and define it precisely enough to rank my example the intuitive way, with boundary cases. I am after the carving, not a label.
