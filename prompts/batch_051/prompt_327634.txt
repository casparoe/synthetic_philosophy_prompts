I keep going back and forth on whether planning is overated, so I wrote down my actual verdicts on a bunch of cases and I want to know if theres a principle behind them or if theyre just inconsistent.

- I planned a cross-country move down to the truck size, the route, and which day each box got packed. It went smooth and I'd do the exact same thing again.
- My friend planned her wedding hour by hour, with a binder, and the day felt stiff and rushed. She later said she wasn't even present for half of it because she was watching the schedule.
- The startup I worked at wrote a 60-page product spec before building anything, and we ended up throwing most of it out once real users got their hands on it. The teams that built rough throwaway versions learned what the product should be way faster.
- In cooking, I used to follow recipes to the letter, and that's how I learned. Now I mostly wing it with whatever's in the fridge and it usually turns out good. Except baking, where I still measure everything and don't mess around.
- When I had appendicitis, the hospital had a protocol for everything, and I was glad nobody was improvising on me. But a nurse friend says the best nurses know exactly when to bend the protocol.
- I tried outlining essays in detail before writing them and the writing came out worse, kind of dead, like I already decided what I thought before I had a chance to think it. But if I don't plan a week of meals, we end up eating takeout four nights.

My rough guess is that planning is for when you can't afford to redo something, and improvising is for when you learn by doing. But that doesn't explain why the wedding plan failed, since a wedding also can't be redone, and it doesn't explain baking versus cooking.

So: is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? What would it say about cases I still can't decide on, like a 20-year career plan, planning a first date, a city planning its downtown redevelopment, or a ten-day vacation with kids? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to give up?
