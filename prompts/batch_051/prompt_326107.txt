Over the past several months I have found myself repeatedly failing to follow through on plans I make with complete sincerity. On Sunday evening I decide that, all things considered, I should spend Monday morning writing a report I have long deferred, and that tomorrow-me will agree. On Monday morning I check messages instead and let the report slide, even as I tell myself that the report is, all things considered, what I should be doing right now. I am trying to work out whether I should understand this in the vocabulary of akrasia or in the vocabulary of hyperbolic discounting, and whether those vocabularies are even rival accounts of one phenomenon rather than accounts of two different phenomena.

Compare the akratic description with the hyperbolic-discounting description of this kind of case. On the akratic description, a single agent acts against her own simultaneous all-things-considered judgment, and the philosophical problem is how such action is possible. On the hyperbolic-discounting description, the value of a delayed reward such as a finished report is discounted along a curve that is steep for the near future and flatter for the far future, so the relative ranking of "finish the report" and "check messages" reverses as the choice approaches; the Monday-morning agent is then not acting against her own all-things-considered judgment but in accordance with the preference she has at that time, while the conflict is between two temporally separated preference-orderings. Is the second framework best read as an explanation of akrasia, as a rival diagnosis that replaces it, or as an account of a distinct phenomenon that merely looks like akrasia from the outside? What does each framework commit one to about whether the agent at different times is a single agent, and about whether acting against one's own all-things-considered judgment is a real possibility or a confused description? Which of the two better captures the phenomenon of weakness of will, and what, if anything, does the better framework still leave unexplained about the Sunday-evening-to-Monday-morning case?
