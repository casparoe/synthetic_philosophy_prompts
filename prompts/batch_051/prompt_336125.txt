What is a natural kind? I want a working definition: a criterion, or a small set of criteria, that tells me when a category used in describing the world is a natural kind and when it is merely conventional, pragmatic, or gerrymandered. A good definition should give a definite verdict, one way or the other, on each of the following cases, and should be able to say what it is about each case that makes it so.

Here are the cases, with my rough starting views:

1. Gold, understood as the element with atomic number 79. I take this to be as clear a case of a natural kind as anything is.
2. The class of things that are either an electron or a cello. I take this to be clearly not a natural kind; a definition that admitted it would, I think, have failed an important test.
3. Biological species, such as the gray wolf (Canis lupus). I have no settled view here; this is the standard hard case.
4. Planet, as defined by the International Astronomical Union, the definition under which Pluto does not count. This looks partly conventional to me, but I want a definition that gives a definite verdict.
5. Jade, in the gemstone sense, which covers two distinct minerals, jadeite and nephrite. One kind in ordinary and commercial classification, two in mineralogy; what does that make it?
6. Cancer, as a general category of disease rather than any particular cancer. Is this a natural kind or an umbrella term for a disjunction of processes?
7. Tree, as an ordinary biological category. It is not a clade: an oak and a pine are each more closely related to some non-tree plants than they are to each other. Is it still a natural kind?
8. Race, as the term functions in ordinary social classification. I lean toward thinking it is not a natural kind, or at least not a biological one, but I want the definition to explain why rather than simply stipulate it.

Please proceed as follows. Propose your definition. Then run it through all eight cases and state the verdict it yields in each. Where the definition gives no answer, gives what I would regard as the wrong answer, or forces you to bite a bullet, say so explicitly, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Finally, say which of the eight verdicts you think is least secure and why.

I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; citations are not needed. What I want is the definition, its verdicts, and your reasoning. Throughout, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on natural kinds, and do not present a contested view—essentialist, cluster-kind, or conventionalist, for instance—as the consensus.
