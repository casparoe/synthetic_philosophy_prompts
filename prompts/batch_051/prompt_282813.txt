I work at an AI lab that has begun using debate for scalable oversight: two models argue opposite sides of a disputed claim before a human judge, who may ask questions and then must decide. The proposal keeps being defended with a courtroom analogy — the debating models are like opposing counsel, the human judge is like a jury, and just as adversarial examination can bring out the truth in court even when the jury lacks direct expertise, debate should allow a non-expert human to adjudicate claims far beyond any human's ability to verify. Audit this analogy. Give an explicit correspondence table (advocates, jury, evidence, cross-examination, rules of procedure, standards of proof, verdict), then identify where the mapping breaks, and mark which of those disanalogies matter for the intended argument and which are only cosmetic. Then take a definite position rather than laying out options: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the courtroom analogy still license the conclusion that debate scales oversight to claims no human can check directly, or does that conclusion rest on a false analogy? If the analogy fails for this purpose, say what should replace it.
