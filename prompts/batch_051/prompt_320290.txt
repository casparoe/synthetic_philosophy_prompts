Rationalist discussions of population ethics have the same shape every time: someone proposes an axiology that avoids the Repugnant Conclusion, and the reply is always that it thereby implies the Sadistic Conclusion, violates transitivity, or quietly assumes the intuition it was supposed to earn. Before I read another such proposal, I want the full specification it is supposed to satisfy. Write the desiderata for an adequate population axiology: the particular population comparisons it must get right (mere addition, the Repugnant and Sadistic conclusions and their stronger variants, the asymmetry between creating happy and creating miserable lives), the formal conditions it must respect or else explicitly relax (transitivity, anonymity, completeness, separability), and any fixed points it may not move. For each requirement, give the case or argument that makes it a requirement rather than a preference, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just the commitments of a particular camp. Then say which subset of the desiderata you believe is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding the familiar theories. The criteria themselves are the point; if you score particular views, keep it brief and do not let it crowd out the requirements.
