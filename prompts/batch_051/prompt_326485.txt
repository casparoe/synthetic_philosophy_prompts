I write a small history-of-ideas blog and I want a sorting quiz for my readers: "which of Nietzsche's three ways of relating to history do you actually have?" The three ways are the monumental, the antiquarian, and the critical. Quick paraphrase so we're working from the same thing: monumental history looks to the past for peaks—great deeds and achievements—to inspire present action and show what's possible; its danger is airbrushing the past into a highlight reel. Antiquarian history preserves and cherishes what's been handed down for its own sake, out of loyalty; its danger is mummifying the past and resenting anything new. Critical history drags the past before a tribunal, condemns what deserves condemning, and tries to break free so present life can breathe; its danger is imagining a clean break from roots you can't actually escape.

The point is to sort people by what they say about concrete cases, not by which label they'd pick for themselves, so no question may mention the labels or ask anything like "do you think the past should inspire, preserve, or be escaped from?" Every question has to be a small scenario where the three attitudes genuinely come apart, with two or three response options that track the attitudes without naming them. The options should read as things a person would actually do or feel.

Deliverables: 8-10 questions plus a scoring key. The questions should be answerable by someone who's never read Nietzsche. Include at least these cases, though you can add others and should make each one your own rather than copying my wording: a city deciding whether to demolish a crumbling but beloved 1920s theater to build housing; a new, well-sourced biography showing that a national hero was privately cruel; a family holiday ritual whose original reason everybody's forgotten; a campaign to remove statues and rename streets honoring a figure now widely seen as vicious; and a high-school history curriculum choosing between a great-figures course, a local-history-and-genealogy course, and a history-of-oppression course.

The scoring key should say which pattern of answers yields each of the three positions, and it should handle mixed results honestly instead of forcing everyone into a box. If a pattern is naturally a blend, say so. If some combination looks incoherent (strongly preserving the past for its own sake on one question while strongly breaking with it over the same object on the next), flag it and say what it might mean. Also flag any position you can't seperate cleanly with your questions rather than pretending you separated it.

Keep the whole thing in American English.
