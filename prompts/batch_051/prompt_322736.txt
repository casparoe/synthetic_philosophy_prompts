It's what comes after: even if we build a system aligned with human values right now, the values themselves could drift over centuries, and nobody might notice until it's too late because each generation just accepts slightly different defaults. A friend of mine keeps telling me this is a non-problem, because future people should get to pick their own values, and if some of them end up with values I'd find horrifying, that's their call and maybe even for the best. But that feels too quick to me. There's a difference between a future person freely rethinking their values and a slow slide where every step is nudged along by their tools or their environment and no one ever sees it happening. I also don't know if we'd even be right to lock in today's values, since our values have plenty of flaws we can already see. And I can't tell whether "value drift" is one problem or several: changes in human values over generations, deliberate self-modification by an AI, and the ordinary nudging effects of technology all get lumped together, but they might need different answers.
