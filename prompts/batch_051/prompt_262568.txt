Draft the joint statement that a committed defender of the principle of alternative possibilities and a committed proponent of Frankfurt-style counterexamples could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should begin with a numbered list of agreed claims, including a jointly acceptable description of the standard Frankfurt set-up, an account of the dialectical burden each side bears, and a statement of what would count as a successful counterexample to the principle. It should then contain a disagreements table in which each row isolates one residual point of disagreement, stated in wording that each side would accept for its own position, and in which disagreements that are genuine are separated from those that are merely verbal — for example over whether a flicker of freedom is an alternative possibility or over whether the intervener's presence preserves the same action. Finally, it should list the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side; and for each crux, specify the cases, arguments, or experimental set-ups both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Please write the statement in British English.
