Write a dialogue between two senior researchers at an AI lab who are both convinced that unaligned artificial intelligence is a serious risk. The first holds that AI welfare work should be integrated into the lab's safety program: a system powerful enough to cause harm on a large scale is already a serious candidate for having morally significant states, and a lab that refuses to investigate this is building blind, both ethically and technically. The second holds that, whatever becomes true of future systems, treating present and near-future systems as moral patients is a net cost to safety: it diverts scarce attention to claims no one yet knows how to test, gives labs a cheap way to appear responsible without making alignment progress, and may eventually motivate people to avoid shutting down or resetting systems that appear distressed. Ground the exchange in a concrete decision: the lab is training a reinforcement-learning agent and must decide whether to create an internal AI welfare monitoring team, and whether that team should have the authority to pause or alter training runs. The dialogue should be a genuine back-and-forth in which each character responds to the other's strongest formulations rather than to convenient caricatures; both should be technically sophisticated, and each should be forced to concede or revise at least one substantive point.
