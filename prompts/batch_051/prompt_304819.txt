Heres a poem prompt. I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, so I spent my life trusting instruments to say when things happened) and I've lately been reading Dennett on the color phi effect. Quick version: a red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes a little to the right, and subjects swear they saw one red dot glide over and turn green roughly halfway. The kicker is that the brain must have done some editing: the Stalinesque story says the whole moving-and-changing percept is staged before consciousness shows it to you, and the Orwellian story says you consciously see the moving red dot and then your memory gets quietly rewritten when the green arrives. The subject's report can't tell them apart.

Write me a pantoum about that. The pantoum's recycled lines should do the structural work: the same images should return in different stanzas the way the same two flashes get stitched into either narrative depending on when you locate the editing. Start in the ordinary 'I saw it move' voice, and let the reader slowly notice that the Orwellian and Stalinesque readings both fit the final report. Dont resolve it — the closing return of the opening lines should leave the timing of experience genuinely open. Accurate enough that someone who knows Dennett wouldn't wince; someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem with the vertigo.
