I'm a fourth-year PhD student in monetary economics, and next month I'm giving a one-off ninety-minute class to first-year master's students in public policy. They've had a semester of micro and no macro, and most arrive believing one of two things: either that a government that prints its own currency can pay for what it wants, with inflation merely the danger of overdoing it, or that printing money is just inflationary and therefore always a mistake. I want the session to teach something more precise than either view: what, exactly, does creating money create? Not literally nothing, but a change in who holds claims on real resources, a reordering of private and public balance sheets, and an increase in liquidity that is easily mistaken for new wealth — with real effects only under specific conditions. I don't want the session to become a policy argument about stimulus or Modern Monetary Theory; the target is the underlying distinction between money and wealth.

Please do not write the explanation itself; plan the explanation. I need the order in which the pieces should go and what depends on what; the one running example or scenario that could carry all ninety minutes and what it needs and lacks — my instinct is a closed island that prints notes to pay for a public project alongside a fixed harvest, but I suspect that example may quietly assume all new money is spent and may make the redistributive mechanics too easy to miss; tell me whether to keep it, modify it, or replace it. I also need the misconceptions students will bring or form — I suspect the main one is treating 'liquidity' as a kind of wealth, with the class swinging between printing-creates-output and printing-only-causes-inflation without seeing the balance-sheet and redistributive mechanics between those poles — and the point in the sequence at which each should be headed off. Say what to leave out at this level and how to flag it, and close with a check-for-understanding question or short exercise that reveals whether a student actually has the money-versus-wealth distinction rather than just the slogan that printing causes inflation. Put a hard limit of 1,000 words on the plan.
