The standard move is to prefer the minimal Bayes net that fits the independence data, and to assume faithfulness: roughly, the only independences in the data are the ones the true causal graph actually implies, with no weird parameter cancelations. People talk like this is why the algorithms recover real causal structure instead of just some graph that happens to fit. Would that actually test the claim, or would it just tell me that I built my simulations to be faithful and minimal in the first place? If the minimal model won on interventions, what exactly would that show — and what would it still leave open, like the worry that we've just defined causation in a way that makes minimality true by construction?
