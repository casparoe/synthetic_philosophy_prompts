Here is a structural parallel I cannot settle on my own. In statistical learning, a model overfits when it fits the noise in its training set, improving fit to old data while worsening performance on new data; the standard remedies are regularization, early stopping, cross-validation, and a prior favoring simpler hypotheses. Rumination, the repetitive self-critical rehearsal of one's failures and shortcomings, looks to me like the same failure mode in a person revising a self-conception: one fits that self-conception too closely to a small, negatively biased sample of remembered episodes and thereby becomes worse at acting in the new situations the self-conception is supposed to guide. Is this a genuine identity of structure, or only a loose analogy carried by the word fitting? If it is genuine, state the mapping explicitly: what plays the role of training data, test data, loss function, hypothesis class, and regularizer. Then say which of the standard machine-learning remedies has a plausible introspective analogue and which does not, and identify at least one prediction the parallel makes that looks false or untestable.
