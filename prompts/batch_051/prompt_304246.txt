I keep a notebook where I work out the moral plumbing of my characters, and I've been using it on the old desert hitchhiker puzzle because my next protagonist is built around it. The set-up, in case you haven't met it: a person is stranded in the desert, a driver will only take her to town if she promises to pay him once they're there, and the driver is a sharp reader of people, so he'll only stop if she genuinely means it. She knows that once she's in town paying is a pure loss, and nobody would ever find out if she didn't. My writing group asked me to write down what I actually believe about this, and now I cant tell weather the pieces hang together or I've papered over a crack. Here they are.

1. A rational person does whatever she has most reason to do at the moment she acts. If keeping an old promise is, right then, a pure loss, the clear-eyed move is to break it.
2. A bare promise adds nothing by itself to that later calculation. It matters later only if the world has changed because she made it.
3. It can still be rational ahead of time to make yourself into the kind of person who keeps the promise anyway.
4. That's because other people can use that trait as a signal, and they'll only hand you things — a ride out of the desert, keys to a flat — if the signal is the real thing. Faking it doesn't survive a good reader of people.
5. But being the real thing can't just mean keeping promises when a fresh calculation happens to favor keeping them; that's prudence, not trustworthiness. What earns the trust is being someone who pays because she said she would, even when the later sum is against her interest.
6. Self-deception is the most quietly destructive thing a mind can do to itself, and deliberately turning yourself into a person of her word because it's useful feels like self-deception with better furniture.
7. Still, training yourself into a habit or a rule is not the same as believing something false.

Walk through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it. For each real tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would be; then tell me which single change would make the whole set hang together best, and what I'd be giving up by making it.
