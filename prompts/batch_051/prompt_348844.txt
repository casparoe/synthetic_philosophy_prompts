In my astronomy club we have been discussing the Fermi paradox, and one member keeps answering almost every proposed resolution of the silence with the same line: "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." He uses it to mean that our failure so far to detect extraterrestrial civilizations gives us no reason at all to lower our confidence that such civilizations exist. I want to know what that slogan could actually amount to. Please lay out the distinct precise claims it might be expressing, from the most trivial to the most radical, and evaluate each one: which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false or overstated once applied to a search with finite sensitivity rather than to complete ignorance. In particular, which reading or readings would actually support the use he is putting it to — dismissing the absence of confirmed detections as uninformative about the existence of intelligent life — and which readings would fail to support that? I would like a menu of readings with verdicts that I can bring to the next meeting, rather than a single interpretation.
