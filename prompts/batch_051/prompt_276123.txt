I keep running into a idea in Galois theory that feels like it belongs in a philosophy seminar. My instructor said the roots of a polynomial don't come with a canonical labeling: for x² − 2, there's a field automorphism of Q(√2) that swaps √2 and −√2 while fixing Q, so the polynomial plus the field structure can't tell them apart either. Calling one of them "the positive square root" uses the ordering of the reals, which is extra structure the algebraic object doesn't have by itself. Same for i and −i: complex conjugation swaps them and fixes the reals, and "i is the one in the upper half-plane" just presupposes the very choice of i that lets you draw the plane in the first place. So the theory side says the two roots form a symmetric pair: neither has a privileged role, and any labeling convention is as good as another.

And yet I can't get myself to believe that √2 and −√2 are on an equal footing. One is positive, the other negative; they sit on opposite sides of zero; every truncation of their decimal expansions distinguishes them. In the complex case, once the plane is drawn, i is plainly the one in the upper half-plane and −i the one below, and the fact that I had to draw the plane doesn't make that difference evaporate. It feels like the theory is sliding from "the equation doesn't single out a root" to "the roots aren't really distinct," which has to be a confusion.

I want someone to play both sides hard and then tell me which should give way. Would it be right to debunk the intuition — is it just a fossil of having learned one arbitrary convention so early that it feels like a fact? Or should the theory be amended — for instance, is the ordering of the reals a legitimate part of what fixes the roots, and why should the algebraic structure get to ignore it? Or is there a reading on which both survive, like "√2 ≠ −√2 is a fact about the embedded real numbers, while the absence of a canonical labeling is a fact about the abstract extension field"? What would actually decide this — is there any mathematical development that could make one side give, or is it ultimately a choice about what we count as the relevant structure? And when someone says the roots "have no canonical labeling," what are they committing themselves to beyond the truism that a set of size two has two bijections to {A,B}?
