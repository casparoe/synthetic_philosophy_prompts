I have been thinking about the debate in normative ethics over whether virtues and duties are derivative from the promotion of good consequences, merely instrumental heuristics that usually promote the good, or independently justified. It struck me that the formal machinery of reinforcement learning contains a striking structural parallel. There, a value function or reward model supplies the consequentialist standard: it scores states or actions by the expected good downstream. A learned policy, understood as a standing mapping from situations to actions, plays the role of a virtue or trait of character and is standardly justified by the return it secures rather than in its own right. And constraints in a constrained Markov decision process play the role of duties: they mark a region of policy space as admissible even when expected-reward maximization would leave that region, and they are often implemented through Lagrangian penalties that turn violations into prices rather than hard prohibitions. I would like to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. In answering, please make the mapping precise on both sides; say what would have to be true of each side for results about reward–constraint trade-offs to transfer from reinforcement learning to the debate about consequentialism, virtues, and duties; and identify at least one commitment the parallel seems to predict that is false on one of the two sides.
