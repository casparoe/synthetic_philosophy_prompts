Before I read another theory of why promises bind, I want the full spec first. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of promissory obligation: the cases it has to get right (a promise the promisee never learns about, a promise under duress, a promise to the dead, a promise to yourself, a promise that becomes impossible, a promise made by a group), the distinctions it can't collapse (promising vs merely intending or predicting, the wrong of breaking vs the wrong of never being bound), and the results from speech act theory or game theory or moral psychology it can't flatly contradict. For each requirement say why it's a requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just what one camp would like. Then say which single critera does the most work against the usual theories, and whether you suspect any pair of the requirements can't be jointly satisfied. I care about the criteria, not a scorecard.
