My friend and I got into a long argument last night about whether soldiers on both sides of a war are morally on a par, and I came away with a bunch of gut verdicts I can't make cohere. My friend kept pushing the line that if you fight on the unjust side, you're doing wrong point blank; I kept saying it depends on what you knew and what your choices actually were, but then he'd bring up the volunteer who knows the war is wrong, and I'd sort of cave. So now I'm trying to figure out what I actually think. Here are the cases. A teenager conscripted into an army fighting a plainly aggressive war, who's been lied to his whole life about the enemy: I can't bring myself to call him blameworthy, even when he kills enemy soldiers. A volunteer in that same army who knows the war is unjust but enlists because his little brother is serving and he wants to keep him alive: I think he's blameworthy, but way less than the generals, and I mostly feel sorry for him rather than angry at him. The generals and politicians who started it: fully blameworthy, for every death on both sides. A soldier on the just side who kills an enemy combatant in an ordinary firefight: no problem at all. That same soldier executing prisoners in a rage after his friend is killed: definitely blameworthy, even though his side is just. A soldier on the unjust side who only fights in clear self-defence, never harms civilians, and would stop if he thought he could: I think what he does is wrong but I don't think he is a bad person. And a mercenary who signs up with the unjust side purely for pay: blameworthy, maybe even more blameworthy than the brother-protective volunteer.

I dont know whether these are a coherent position or just sentimentality. Is there a principle that gets all of them, or almost all of them, and which of my verdicts would it force me to overturn? And where does that principle land on the moral equality of combatants question? It should also give a verdict on the case I kept going back and forth on with my friend: a soldier on the just side who supports the war for terrible reasons, not because it's just but because he wanted an excuse to kill.
