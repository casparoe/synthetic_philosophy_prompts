I keep encountering this analogy in discussions of perception: a hallucinated dagger and a seen dagger can be experientially indistinguishable; because no real dagger is involved in the hallucination, what is directly before the mind must be an inner item, like a picture on a private screen; and seeing a real dagger is then just having that same inner picture plus a causal connection to the world. The conclusion drawn is that we never directly see ordinary objects, only our own sensory representations. I want an audit of this analogy. Spell out what corresponds to what if we take the inner-screen picture literally, including the screen, the picture, the projector, and the viewer, then say where each correspondence breaks down and which features of a literal screen-and-picture setup are being quietly imported into perception without argument. After that, say whether the indistinguishability of the two cases genuinely forces a shared inner item, or whether it only shows that a hallucination can be mistaken for a seeing. Finally, consider the disjunctivist alternative that veridical perception and hallucination are fundamentally different kinds of episode even when indistinguishable to the subject, and say whether it survives the audit and whether any better comparison captures what the original analogy was reaching for. Answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
