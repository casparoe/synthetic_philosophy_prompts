here are the cases i want it to get right. clearly honoring: (1) a statue of a confederate general on horseback put in front of a courthouse in the early 1900s by a confederate heritage group, with no plaque except his name and dates. (2) the vietnam veterans memorial in dc — it lists tens of thousands of dead soldiers, and i'd say it honors those people rather than just recording that they died. clearly not honoring: (3) a roadside historical marker that says a massacre of native people happend near here and gives the facts. (4) a statue of a dictator displayed face-down in a museum exhibit about how the regime fell — if anything that's un-honoring. borderline ones: (5) say a city renames a street named after a kkk leader to "george floyd avenue" as a direct protest — is that honoring floyd, or is it more of a counter-monument? (8) an abstract statue of a woman labeled "Liberty" that was erected to commemorate a specific politician but doesn't look like or name him.

my first try was that honoring means publicly expressing that the person was good or admirable, but case 6 and 8 both wreck it in different ways, so i'm stuck. can you propose a definition, run it through all eight cases, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer at all, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts? and since i'm collecting answers for a project, please finish with your best one-sentence version of the definition as the very last line, starting with "Definition:".
