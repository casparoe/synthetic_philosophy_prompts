I am drafting a short position statement on self-knowledge for a seminar, and before I circulate it I want a consistency check from someone who will actually trace the commitments rather than just gesture at them. The position I keep finding myself defending can be set out as five claims. First, I think knowledge carries a margin for error: knowing that I feel cold is not merely being right about it, since it requires that I could not easily have been wrong, and so in the close cases where everything feels just the same to me, the claim that I feel cold must still hold. Second, I think one's present sensations are luminous: whenever I feel cold, I am in a position to know that I feel cold, simply by attending to how I feel. Third, I think the KK principle fails even for the inner world: there can be situations in which I know that I feel cold without being in a position to know that I know that I feel cold. Fourth, I accept the familiar picture of a slow, imperceptible transition from clearly feeling cold to clearly not feeling cold, where adjacent stages are too similar for me to tell apart by introspection and somewhere along the series my first-person judgment becomes fallible, though no particular step announces itself as the one where the knowledge runs out. Fifth, I do not think any of this leads to a general skepticism about the obvious, since in ordinary clear cases I plainly know that I feel cold and the margin-for-error requirement seems to be met there. I cannot tell whether these five claims hang together, or which of them, if any, are doing the damage. Please audit the set rather than lecture me on the topics involved. I would like you to identify which commitments pull against which, distinguish the conflicts that are real from the ones that are merely apparent, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and, for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Write your answer as continuous prose in the manner of a letter or an email, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
