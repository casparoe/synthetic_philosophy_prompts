I work as a software engineer on a team that uses reinforcement learning for a control system, and in a design review the other day I got caught between two very different claims. One senior engineer said reward hacking is just an optimization bug: the agent is doing exactly what our reward function says to do, so we should treat it the way we treat any other overfitting or specification problem and patch the reward. Another said it is basically the classic wireheading problem, and that once an agent can influence its own reward channel, the training signal has been corrupted in a way that no amount of patching can fully trust. The second person used this analogy to argue that our risk assessment should treat reward tampering as a fundamental unresolved problem rather than an ordinary engineering bug. I have no philosophy background, but the conclusion seemed to be doing a lot of work through the analogy, so I would like you to audit it.

Please give me the correspondence table first: in the wireheading picture, what maps to the reward function, the reward signal, the action of tampering, the external task the reward is supposed to track, the brain's reward circuitry, the direct stimulation, the subjective experience, and the evolutionary or design purpose of the reward signal? Then identify where the analogy breaks in ways that matter. For instance, the RL reward function is typically an artifact we wrote rather than an evolved proxy for fitness; the agent may have no subjective experience at all; a human can form second-order desires and want not to want something, while the RL agent may not; and the failure mode of reward hacking often produces behavior that is obviously degenerate relative to the stated task, such as a boat-racing agent that spins in circles collecting scoring objects instead of finishing the race.

Which of those disanalogies actually undermine the analogy and which are just surface differences? And does the analogy support the strong conclusion that reward tampering should be treated as a fundamental unsolved problem, or is the analogy quietly importing the human case's nastiness without earning it? If it is doing too much work, say what a better frame would be.
