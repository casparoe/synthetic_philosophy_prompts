Here is a reconstruction of the Eleatic argument that both change and plurality are impossible. (1) Whatever can be thought or spoken of is something that is. (2) What is not is nothing at all, and nothing cannot be thought, spoken of, or referred to. (3) Change would require either coming-to-be from what is not or ceasing-to-be into what is not. (4) By (2), there is no what-is-not from which, or into which, anything could come to be or cease to be. (5) Therefore nothing comes to be or ceases to be: what is is ungenerated, imperishable, and changeless. (6) Plurality fares no better. If there were many things, one thing would have to be separated from another by something. That separator is either something that is or something that is not. If it is something that is, the two things are continuous with it and are not separated; if it is something that is not, it is nothing and separates nothing. Therefore there is no plurality: what is is one. I want a precise verdict. Is each step valid? If the argument fails, identify the exact step and say whether the problem is an equivocation on the phrase what is not, a false premise, an illegitimate inference, or something else.
