Before I commit to any view on which arrows of time are fundamental and which are shadows of us, I want the full set of adequacy conditions on the table. I keep seeing people gesture at entropy increases like that settles what time's arrow is, but I suspect there are several arrows and they get flattened whenever things get hard. Give me every phenomenon a good theory of time's arrow(s) would have to fit into one story: asymmetric records and memories, the future-directedness of interventions and choices, the fork asymmetry (traces of an event sharing a common cause rather than a common effect), the thermodynamic gradient itself, and the time-reversal invariance of the relevant microdynamics — plus whatever else you'd insist on. For each condition, name the observation or case that makes it a requirement rather than a nicety, and say honestly whether a theory can get away with treating it as merely agent-relative or pragmatic, or whether that move just relocates the problem. Then give me the single condition you think filters out the most candidate theories (especially the popular entropy-explains-time ones) and explain why that one does more work than the others. Cap it at 800 words; a table is fine.
