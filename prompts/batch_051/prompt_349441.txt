Say someone claims that people value privacy for its own sake, not merely as a way of avoiding burglary, doxxing, targeted ads, or social embarassment. As a science journalist, my first instinct is to ask how you'd get that onto a lab bench. Design a concrete experiment — for instance, paying people to reveal something trivial but genuinely private, where no downstream consequence follows and no audience ever finds out — and say which pattern of choices would suggest they treat privacy as an end in itself, and which would suggest they treat it as a bundle of risks to be managed. Then be honest about what even a clean result would leave open: is the residue just a limitation of the setup, or is there something in the very idea of valuing privacy for its own sake that no test can pin down?
