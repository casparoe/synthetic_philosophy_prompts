I have been working through Galen Strawson's Basic Argument that no one is ultimately morally responsible for anything, and I want to test whether the standard replies exhaust the logical space. To fix a shared statement of the target, here is the argument as I understand it. (1) In any situation a person acts as she does because of the way she is — her character, values, desires, and so on. (2) For a person to be ultimately responsible for what she does, she must be ultimately responsible for the way she is, at least in the respects that matter for the action. (3) To be ultimately responsible for the way she is, she would have to have intentionally brought it about that she is that way. (4) But a person can intentionally bring something about only on the basis of a nature she already has — aims, principles, and dispositions in light of which she forms the intention — and the same demand then arises for that nature. Since the only way to stop the regress would be to be causa sui, which is impossible, no one can be ultimately responsible.

The replies I have already collected are these. I list them because I do not want restatements, special cases, or combinations of them.

1. The compatibilist-capacities reply. Ordinary responsibility requires only that the action issue from rational capacities — reflective self-control, responsiveness to reasons, and sensitivity to one's own values — not that one created those capacities. Premise (2) attacks a notion of responsibility distinct from the one at issue in practice.

2. The Aristotelian piecemeal reply. You do not need to be responsible for your original endowment. By repeatedly acting to cultivate or resist your inclinations you form your character over time through your own choices. The regress is blocked because responsibility for character is acquired piecemeal, not obtained all at once through an act of total self-creation.

3. The identification reply. Whether an action is yours to answer for depends on whether you wholeheartedly identify with the motive behind it, not on whether you created that motive. Since identification does not require self-creation, responsibility for action does not require it either.

4. The agent-causal reply. An agent can originate an action without being determined by prior states, and this strong origination can make her the source of the action even though she did not author her own nature. The Basic Argument, on this view, assumes that the action's being explicable by the way she is must undercut her control, whereas the agent-causalist denies that the way she is settles the action.

5. The self-forming-action reply. Undetermined choices made at moments of motivational conflict settle which direction the agent's character will take. Because such a conflict-resolving choice is not fully settled by the way she already is, she can become responsible for the resulting character without having created herself from nothing.

6. The attributionist reply. Responsibility for an action is a matter of which judgments and attitudes the action is properly attributed to, not of causal control over how those judgments and attitudes arose. The Basic Argument assumes a production relation between agent and self that attributionism rejects as the wrong model.

I believe these six capture the standard responses, though I am open to correction on that. What I want is a seventh objection to the Basic Argument that is genuinely distinct from all six — not a rewording, special case, or combination of items already listed, and not a bare attack on one premise unless the line of attack is different in kind from the above. Then tell me honestly how good the objection is. What is the strongest Strawsonian reply to it, and does it ultimately collapse back into one of the six?

One further request: if any part of my statement of the argument or any listed reply is a mischaracterization, start by correcting it. And if you conclude that the six really do exhaust the live options and any further objection would inevitably reduce to one of them, say that outright and explain why, rather than inventing a pseudo-objection to complete the list.
