I'm a software engineer, and much of my job involves estimating risk: failure probabilities, tail events, expected downtime. I'm comfortable with the quantitative side, but a friend recently posed a version of the trolley problem that has me stuck on the normative side. In the standard switch case, a runaway trolley is about to kill five people on the main track, and you can pull a lever to divert it onto a side track where one person is standing. In my friend's variant, the five people on the main track remain the same, but the side track is empty except for a rotating pool of N maintenance workers. Each worker independently has probability p of being on that side track right now; you know p and N, but not who is present, and whoever is present will be killed. Pulling the lever saves the five with certainty and imposes on each of the N workers a risk p of death. Set p = 1/N, so the expected number of deaths on the side track is exactly one — the same expected toll as in the classic case, though in the variant the actual number killed could be zero, one, or several. None of the N workers has consented to this risk, and there is no way to warn them in time.

I'd like you to treat this as a professional philosopher working in normative ethics and decision theory. Before answering, maybe brainstorm the different considerations that might distinguish the cases: expected lives lost, variance, how risks aggregate across non-consenting people, the difference between concentrating a harm and distributing it, and whether consent matters differently when the probability imposed on each person is tiny. Then work through the variation carefully. Do the standard verdicts and rationales for the classic one-certain-death switch case carry over unchanged to this variant for a straightforward expected-utility consequentialist? For a deontological or rights-based view that permits the original switch because the one death is a foreseen side effect rather than a means? At what values of N do these frameworks stay in step or come apart, and if they come apart, what feature — non-consent, the dispersion of risk, or something about the difference between probability and certainty — is doing the work?
