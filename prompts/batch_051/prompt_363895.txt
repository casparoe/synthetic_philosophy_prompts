Please argue that the memory objection to karma—the objection that one cannot deserve reward or punishment for deeds one cannot remember—presupposes rather than establishes that desert requires autobiographical recollection of the deed. Defend instead a dispositional account of karmic desert: the desert base is not the remembered act but the volitional tendency that the act both expresses and entrenches, and such tendencies can persist through the loss of particular memories and across rebirths. Your argument must explain why this remains a theory of moral desert rather than a mere causal account of how deeds shape character, and it should answer the strongest counterargument that, once recollection is abandoned as a condition, karma no longer assigns desert to persons at all and becomes a doctrine of impersonal cosmic hygiene.
