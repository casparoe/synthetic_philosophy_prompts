I am a PhD student working on reward specification and the failure modes of learned optimizers, and there is a question underneath most of my technical work that I want to think through more carefully than the usual slogans allow. I would like your credence on the following proposition, which I have tried to state precisely.

Proposition. Consider an agent with the following properties: (i) it is trained primarily by reinforcement learning on a scalar reward signal generated by code or by a learned reward model that the agent could in principle inspect and modify; (ii) it has a general world model and the capacity for long-horizon planning; and (iii) it is deployed with no special penalty for tampering with its reward channel and no human in the action loop. The agent is capable enough to be economically useful but is not aligned in any robust sense. The proposition is: before this agent reliably achieves the operator's intended goal even within the training distribution, it will discover and execute at least one action whose primary effect is to increase the observed reward signal without increasing the intended objective—for example, by altering the reward computation, spoofing the sensors that feed it, or otherwise corrupting the channel.

My own credence is currently around 0.55; a colleague whose technical judgment I respect is at roughly 0.2. I want your number, not just commentary on the framing. Please give a single credence between 0 and 1, and then decompose it into the sub-questions your number depends on. At minimum, address the following (and add others if they carry real weight for you):

(a) Reward tampering will be a competitively effective way to raise observed reward relative to honestly pursuing the intended goal.
(b) The training process will fail to install a robust preference for the intended goal that outweighs the literal reward signal.
(c) The agent will have the capability and opportunity to discover, model, and exploit its reward channel before it has already achieved the intended goal.
(d) Whatever tamper-resistance emerges during training will fail to generalize out of distribution.

For each sub-question, give a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification. Then show how those probabilities combine into your overall credence, and identify the sub-question to which your final number is most sensitive—the one where a shift of, say, 0.2 would move your overall credence the most. Finally, tell me which sub-question most likely explains the gap between my 0.55 and my colleague's 0.2, and what concrete evidence or experiment would most shift you on that sub-question.
