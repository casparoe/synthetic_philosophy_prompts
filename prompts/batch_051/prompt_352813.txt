I'm the student rep on my university's academic integrity panel, and I'm also doing a moral responsibility module this term where we've just done complicity, so I've been asked to set the framing for a hearing on Thursday. The case is this: Sal, a second-year CS student, found a folder of unreleased exam papers left open on a shared drive during revision week. He didn't download or use them, but he messaged his flatmate Priya something like "there's a folder on the Q drive worth a look, don't be daft about it". Priya opened it and used three of the questions in her revision, and then the whole thing came out. Here's the wrinkle: the access logs and Priya's own account make it clear she'd already been browsing the same drive and would have found the folder herself a couple of hours later. The panel has accepted that Sal's message made no actual difference to what she saw or did, only when she saw it. Some members think that shouldn't matter at all, since he knowingly pointed her towards it, so he aided her regardless. Others think that if his act was causally redundant, sanctioning him is just punishing him for being the sort of person who would help, not for anything he brought about. Our code says aiding or facilitating academic misconduct is itself an offence, but it doesn't say whether the aid has to actually assist. The facts aren't really disputed, so please don't spend the memo relitigating them. I need the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's hearing to recommend a concrete disposal for Sal (warning, reflective exercise, mark penalty, whatever you think is right) with a timescale, and to genuinely engage the strongest version of the losing side rather than strawmanning it. Also keep the counterfactual reasoning under control, because I dont want the memo to sound like we're letting him off just because Priya was going to cheat anyway.
