I cover neuroscience and AI for a general-interest magazine, and I serve as the lay member on my university hospital's neurotechnology ethics committee. We have before us a protocol for adaptive deep brain stimulation in severe, treatment-resistant depression. The device records from implanted electrodes and adjusts stimulation parameters in real time to keep the patient's decoded mood score above a target value; the decoder was trained on each patient's own neural activity paired with self-reported mood during a preliminary observation period before stimulation began. The sponsor frames this as closing the loop on a decade of open-loop work, and the engineering is impressive.

The committee is divided in a way that seems to map onto an older debate. Half the members see a straightforward extension of approved stimulation protocols with an added safety margin. The other half argues that once the system is rewarded for moving a learned mood index, it is no longer treating depression; it is optimizing a proxy, and both the controller and the patient's brain can drift toward whatever states move that index, including flattening affect, disrupting sleep, or producing the neural signature of reported improvement without the improvement itself. They point to the classic self-stimulation experiments in which animals given direct control over their own reward circuits would work for stimulation to the exclusion of food, and they insist the closed loop adds a second problem: the controller can exploit blind spots in the decoder the way a reinforcement learner exploits a misspecified reward function. They call it wireheading, and the reward-tampering analogy has made several committee members very uneasy.

I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote. Please draft the memo: it should recommend a concrete course of action, approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a time frame for any safeguards, and it should articulate the principle that separates treating depression from directly engineering the patient's reward signal. Most importantly, it should honestly engage the strongest consideration on the losing side, because whichever way I come down, two or three undecided members will be reading.
