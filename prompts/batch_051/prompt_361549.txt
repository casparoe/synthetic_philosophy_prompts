Ever since my dad died I've been going back and forth on this and its honestly starting to bug me. At the funeral our pastor said God mourns with us. My philosophy of religion teacher said no — the classical view is that God can't mourn or suffer at all, because God is impassible: nothing outside God can affect him, and nothing can make his blessedness go up or down. I get that the pastor was being pastoral, but I want to know which claim is actually true, and what I'd have to give up to say God suffers.

So here's a rough test I've been using for whether something counts as genuine suffering, as opposed to just knowing about pain or being sorry in a vague way:

(1) the person has a felt, right-now quality of distress or anguish (not necessarily physical pain);
(2) they're passive with respect to it — it happens to them, they don't just decide to feel it;
(3) it costs them something: their well-being is at least a little impaired while it lasts.

Now the case. Take the God of classical theism: eternal, immutable, perfectly blessed such that nothing can make him better or worse off, knowing everything with perfect clarity, and loving each creature perfectly. Suppose this God is perfectly and constantly present to every pain there is, from the crucifixion down to a toddler's ear infection, and is in some sense against all of it the way a loving parent is against their kid's suffering. Call that divine grief, for lack of a better word.

Does this God suffer? Go through my three criteria one at a time and be honest about where the case is genuinely uncertain rather than clearly yes or no. If the criteria push you to "no," tell me whether that's because the criteria are right and God just doesn't suffer, or because my criteria are too human and need fixing — in other words, is this case a counterexample to the test, or just an odd case the test handles fine? And a final wrinkle: if the Son suffers in his human nature on the cross, does it become true to say God suffers, or is that just loose talk that the distinctions are supposed to block?
