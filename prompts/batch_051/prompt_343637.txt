I'm grading a short-answer question for an intro practical reason course and id like a second opinion. The question was: "Your friend says that when two values are incommensurable, any choice between them is arbitrary. Is she right? Explain." The student wrote:

"She's basically right. Incommensurable means there's no common unit or scale in which the two values can be measured, like trying to measure beauty in lives saved. Since rational choice requires comparing how much of each value the options realize, lacking a shared scale means the options are incomparable. If they're incomparable, any ranking has to be imposed from outside rather than read off the values themselves, so the choice is arbitrary. That's what makes a hard choice hard: reason underdetermines an answer. So the upshot is that incommensurability makes rational choice impossible, though it also means the agent can just choose without their choice being rationally criticizable."

Could you give this a grade and, more importantly, point-by-point comments on where it goes wrong and what it gets right? I don't want to overpenalize the parts that are actually fine, or give credit for claims that arent established.
