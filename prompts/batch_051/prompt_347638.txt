Me and a friend just spent two hours arguing about this old ordinance in our town, which is still on the books and reads: "No person shall ride, drive, or lead any horse, mule, or ass upon any sidewalk within the city, under penalty of a fine not exceeding ten dollars." Now somebody got a ticket for riding a rental e-scooter on the sidewalk, and the city is actually trying to enforce it. My friend says the plain words obviously don't cover a scooter, and anyway a law nobody's enforced in living memory shouldn't suddenly be good. I say the point of the thing was keeping the sidewalk clear, and the non-enforcement just shows the city forgot it existed, not that it died. How should this come out? I don't so much care about the practical legal answer as about what the different ways of reading it (plain words, the original purpose, what the drafters would have said if you asked them back then) each give, and whether a law can be dead-but-still-valid and what that says about the rule of law.
