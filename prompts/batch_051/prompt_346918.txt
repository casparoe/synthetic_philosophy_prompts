When someone predicts that some action of mine will have far-flung effects — "if you buy that car you'll turn conservative," "if we automate this the whole neighborhood falls apart" — I have a rule: I don't accept it unless they can trace most of the causal chain. For my own predictions I'm even strickter: I trace two links out and then I stop, becuase you have to stop somewhere. My partner says that's a fine heuristic, but I've been reading about the frame problem in AI, and now I suspect the "somewhere" is doing all the work: I don't actually know that the links after two are irrelevant until after I've looked at them, and by then I've already decided not to look. Last year I used the rule before buying a car and never got past "it gets me to work and costs X" — I completely missed how it would rearrange my weekends, my friendships, and my sense of what counts as close by. So: is my two-link rule defensible, or is it laziness wearing a computational costume? Is there a version of it that says when to stop without already assuming that everything past the cutoff is irrelevant? And does the burden-of-proof part — they have to show the chain — actually help, or does it just move the same problem onto someone else?
