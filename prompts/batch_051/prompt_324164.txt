I'm an undergraduate in an upper-level seminar on intuitionistic logic and mathematical constructivism, and I've taken on writing a practice final for our study group. The course has worked through the BHK interpretation, the failure of excluded middle and double-negation elimination, the continuing validity of non-contradiction and ex falso, and the double-negation translation by which classical propositional logic reappears inside intuitionistic logic. I want the practice exam to move past the one-line summary that intuitionists merely "deny P or not-P"; it should force students to confront the disjunction and existence properties and to explain exactly why ¬¬(P ∨ ¬P) is a theorem of intuitionistic propositional logic while P ∨ ¬P is not — and why that fact does not collapse the system back into classical logic.

Please draft one essay question a student could answer in ninety minutes, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 marks. The model answer must do more than summarize: it should (a) explain why, on the BHK reading, a proof of A ∨ B requires a proof of one disjunct, so excluded middle is not generally available; (b) treat negation as implication of absurdity and use this to show why non-contradiction remains provable and why proof by contradiction is still legitimate for negative claims; (c) derive or at least motivate ¬¬(P ∨ ¬P) and distinguish it sharply from P ∨ ¬P; (d) spell out the consequence for existence, namely that a constructive proof of ∃x A(x) requires a witness, while an argument that assumes no witness exists and derives a contradiction yields only the double negation of the existential; and (e) say something precise about the double-negation translation as the reason classical mathematics is reinterpreted rather than simply rejected. In the marking scheme, assign four marks to each of those five points, and give a one-line note on the specific misconception each point is meant to catch.

Please begin the response with the exam question itself, with no preface or commentary. Then give the model answer, and have the model answer open with its bottom-line thesis in the first sentence and then develop the reasoning, without restating the question. Then give the marking scheme.
