i've spent the last year trying to actually practice stoicism rather than just read about it, and i've ended up with a set of commitments that i now suspect don't all fit together, but i can't tell which ones are the problem. here's the list: (1) the dichotomy of control is basically right — the only things up to me are my judgments, choices, desires, and aversions; my health, reputation, money, and what other people do are not up to me. (2) since only what's up to me can be genuinely good or bad for me, losing a job, getting sick, or being slandered can't really harm me, so i should never be distressed by them. (3) at the same time i think if your spouse or child dies, grief is the fitting response and it's right to feel it for as long as it has force; someone who just snaps back to normal in a week seems shallow to me. (4) i also think health, wealth, and reputation are preferred indifferents: not good or bad in themselves, but reasonable to pursue when doing so doesn't cost you your character. (5) i think a modern stoic should be politically engaged and work to make institutions fairer, not retreat into private equanimity. (6) and i'm convinced anger is always a mistake, even at injustice. what i'm after is a proper audit: which pairs are in real tension, which only seem to be, how bad each tension is, and what the single cheapest revision would be to make the whole thing cohere.
