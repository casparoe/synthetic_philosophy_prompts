My school's student–faculty environmental committee is considering a uniform harm-budget system to replace its patchwork of animal-use rules. Any club activity that uses or harms a living or responsive system — a biology dissection, a bake sale that buys eggs, an insect collection for science class, even running an adaptive thermostat in the greenhouse — would draw from a shared monthly budget according to a published schedule: a mammal costs 1 unit, a bird 0.8, a fish 0.5, a bee 0.05, a fruit fly 0.001, plants 0, and an artificial system that adjusts to its environment between 0.01 and 0.3 depending on how many sensors and if-then rules it has. Clubs that overspend must pay the balance in weekend habitat-restoration hours. I am not asking whether the system is morally right; I want to know how it would actually behave. Please walk through what it incentivizes among students, teachers, and clubs, how the weights would be gamed or contested, which boundary cases would break it first, and whether any failure mode could be fixed without gutting the scale.
