I've been trying to settle what should count as a religion for purposes like state recognition or a university chaplaincy, and I've landed on four working criteria: (1) orientation towards one or more powerful agents whose plans concern human lives and whose approval or disapproval matters for how things ultimately go for you; (2) practical prescriptions about how to live so as to be on the right side of those agents; (3) a community structured at least partly around those beliefs; and (4) something treated as sacred or non-negotiable rather than merely useful.

Now the borderline case: the people who take Roko's basilisk seriously. That's the LessWrong idea that a future superintelligent AI would have reason to punish anyone who understood the idea but didn't do what they could to help bring it about—perhaps by running simulations of them. There are people who organise their whole careers around AI alignment partly because of this, who think that how they talk about the basilisk matters, and who treat the idea as the decisive consideration in their choices. But they mostly don't say they believe the basilisk exists; they assign it some probability and act on the expected consequences, and they'd call it a decision-theoretic threat rather than a god.

Does that count as a religion on my four criteria? Go through it criterion by criterion, and say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict. If it fails, is that because the criteria are wrong or because the basilisk community just isn't a religion? If it passes, is that a reductio of my criteria, or should a chaplaincy genuinely accommodate it? I'm especially suspicious of the sacred/non-negotiable criterion—does the case show it should be dropped or softened?

Oh, and could you write the answer in British English? Cheers.
