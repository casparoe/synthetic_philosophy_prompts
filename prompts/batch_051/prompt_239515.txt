I've been drafting a principle for when the vegan obligation to avoid animal products actually applies, and I want to test it on a case I can't settle. My current criteria, roughly:

(1) there are plant-based alternatives that would meet the person's caloric and micronutrient needs;
(2) those alternatives are actually available where the person lives and are affordable in money, time, fuel, and storage;
(3) giving up the animal products wouldn't leave some other basic need unmet — income, food security, ability to stay put, whatever;
(4) the animal products come from a system that predictably causes nontrivial suffering or death.

The case bugging me: a fishing family on a small island with no refrigeration and an unreliable ferry. They eat small fish daily; it's their main source of protein, B12, and fat. They have rice, maize, beans, and cooking oil available year-round, and per calorie the plant food is cheaper than the fuel and gear that go into fishing — though beans get scarce late in the dry season and the kids' energy needs are hard to cover on the available amounts then. The island clinic stocks B12 tablets but only intermittantly, and when it's out the family would be taking a real risk on an all-plant diet. The family also sells most of the catch to pay school fees; the fish they eat are mostly the small or damaged ones that can't be sold, so the food and the income come from the same activity — you can't give up one without giving up the other. The fish are caught with gear that kills turtles and juvenile fish, and the reef is visibly declining.

On my own criteria, is this family inside the obligation or not? Go criterion by criterion and say where the case falls relative to each. I'm especially interested in whether (3) just restates (2), and whether the case shows my criteria draw the line in the wrong place. If the criteria underdetermine the verdict, say exactly where and what the cheapest repair would be.

At the very end, on its own line, put \answer{...} with one of: obligated, not obligated, or criteria underdetermine the case.
