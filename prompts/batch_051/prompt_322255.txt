I'm on the volunteer committee for a climbing gym, and the recurring fight is over the word "safety." Right now a safety incident means something that actually put someone in physical danger — a dropped rope, a loose hold, gear failure. A faction wants to expand "safety incident" to include cases where a member felt unsafe or unwelcome even though no one was physically at risk, because they think the current concept silences people. My habit is this: I refuse to judge a proposed concept on its merits until someone tells me what the concept is for — what work it does. If the job is "capture something the old one missed," I'll engage. If the job is "get people to take these complaints more seriously," I vote no, because that's using a definition to launder a conclusion rather than to improve a classification. But I worry that this rule of mine is just status quo bias wearing a methodological costume, and I honestly can't tell from the inside. Is the rule defensible? On what view of concepts would it be right, and on what view would it be wrong? And how should I restate it to keep the skepticism without the conservatism? Lead with your bottom line — defensible, mostly defensible, or not — and then give the reasoning. Max 300 words.
