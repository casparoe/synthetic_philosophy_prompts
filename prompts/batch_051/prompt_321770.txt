I'm writing a novel where a character loses her sight young and gets around with a long cane, and there's a storyline where she's issued an experimental ultrasound sensor shaped like a flashlight that vibrates in patterns on her palm. I went down a rabbit hole on Merleau-Ponty's bit about the blind man's cane and motor intentionality: the standard story is that once you're fluent the cane stops being an object you hold and becomes something you perceive through, the tip is where your touch is, the cane gets absorbed into your body schema, and you don't infer the curb from a vibration in your hand, you just feel the curb out there. So here's what I can't settle. Take the same case but change one thing: the device never touches anything. It's an ultrasonic rangefinder, and its only output is a grid of tiny motors on the palm that fire in distinct pulse patterns for different distances. At first the character has to learn a kind of code — two quick taps means a hard surface around arm's length, a long buzz means open space, and so on — and she reads the pulses like a text. After a year, she just walks through a crowded station and says she feels doors and stairs and someone's backpack out there; she doesn't translate anymore, and if you ask what patterns her palm just got she can't tell you. Does the body-schema analysis carry over to this version, or does the fact that the signal starts out as a learned, decodeable code mean she's still reading the room rather than perceiving it? Where exactly does the standard Merleau-Ponty line get strained, if it does — is the crucial thing the initial translation, the discreteness of the signal, the absence of contact at the far end, or something else? For contrast, run the principle over these tweaks and tell me which ones would count as folded into the body like the cane: the same sensor but a continuous vibration whose frequency tracks distance; the pulses delivered through a tiny speaker instead of the palm; the sensor mounted on the cane she still swings; the same palm sensor with a half-second delay. I'm less after a yes/no than after the principle that does the real work, and which tweak would force a Merleau-Pontian to say she's no longer perceiving through the tool but consulting an instrument.
