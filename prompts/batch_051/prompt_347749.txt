I keep running into this when reading about disinformation and content moderation, and I've realized I dont have a stable view about reproducing propaganda in order to criticize it. My gut calls are all over the place, so I'm trying to find the principle, if there is one.

Here are my verdicts. A historian including an unaltered 1930s antisemitic cartoon in an academic book about visual propaganda: fine. An op-ed quoting an antivax pamphlet's claim that vaccinated people shed the vaccine, in order to rebut it: fine. A professor screening a Nazi recruitment film in a small seminar with assigned reading and discussion: fine. A journalist embedding a clip from a terrorist propaganda video in an article about how such videos are edited, with a content warning and autoplay off: fine, but the conditions matter to me. A random account with a few hundred followers posting the same clip with just the caption "this is what they do": I want to say not fine, but I cant explain exactly why, since they'd say they're raising awareness. A YouTuber debunking a racist meme by displaying it large, monetizing the video, and laughing at it more than analyzing it: not fine. A private Discord of organizers sharing a neo-Nazi poster to mock it: fine, even though there's no real critique happening. The same poster surfaced by an algorithm as related content under a news article debunking it: not fine.

The pair that bugs me most is this: if the seminar professor puts the same unaltered cartoon in a recorded public lecture that gets auto-captioned and search-indexed, I flinch, even though the content and the intent are unchanged. My working rule was something like reproduction is okay when the audience came for analysis and the speaker is accountable for the framing, but the private Discord case breaks that, and the YouTuber half satisfies it. Is there a coherent principle that gets all or most of these, and what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on, a journalist putting the fabricated great replacement line in a headline even though the article immediately debunks it? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to drop?
