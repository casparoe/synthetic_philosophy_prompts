So I keep seeing the claim that the brain isn't a Cartesian theater — there's no single place where everything comes together and gets shown to a little guy in your head, and no exact moment when a thought becomes conscious. The version I've internalized has four criteria: (1) there's one canonical copy of whatever you're currently conscious of; (2) that copy lives at a definite place; (3) there's a definite moment when it hits the stage and becomes conscious; and (4) there's someone or something watching the stage.

I do game-engine programming, and this just sounds like my frame loop. Every 16.7ms, input, physics, AI, animation, and netcode each write their results into a single GameState object — the one canonical version of the world at tick N, sitting in a definite spot in memory at a definite tick. The renderer then reads GameState and draws the frame. So criteria 1–3 fit pretty cleanly. Criterion 4 is where it gets weird: the renderer isn't understanding anything, it just turns matrices into pixels, and the actual audience is the human player, outside the system.

Wich would make you think game engine = Cartesian theater minus the homunculus, except for the buffering. In practice the loop double-buffers: the sim thread is writing state N+1 while the render thread is still drawing state N, and with interpolation the rendered picture is often a blend of N and N+1 that never existed as any actual GameState. So the canonical copy is a thing nobody ever sees, and the thing that does get "presented" is a non-canonical draft.

So: is the per-frame GameState a Cartesian theater in every way that matters, or not? Work through the criteria honestly. Where it fails, say whether that's because the criteria are wrong (e.g., the real problem with the Cartesian theater was never the central copy, it was the inner observer, and the where/when stuff is a red herring) or because a game engine is just a bad analogy for a brain. And does the double-buffering detail show that even a system with a central copy can be multiple-drafts-y, or am I overreading my own code?
