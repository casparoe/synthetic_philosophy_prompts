A provision in our church's constitution reads: "In the public worship of God, no practice shall be admitted except such as is expressly appointed in holy Scripture; whatsoever is not so appointed is thereby forbidden." A deaf member has asked that a sign-language interpreter stand beside the pulpit for the entire service, signing the prayers, the hymns, and the sermon, so that she can take part along with everyone else. The elders divide into three camps. Some say the interpreter is a practice Scripture never appoints, and therefore the rule excludes her. Others say she is not a distinct practice at all: she only carries commanded words to a member in a receivable form, just as a Braille Bible or a translation of the sermon would. Still others say interpretation belongs to the circumstances of order and decency, like amplification or lighting, and so the rule was never meant to reach it. The people who drafted the constitution could not have anticipated this case. How should the bylaw apply, and which of the three readings is most faithful to what it actually says and to the principle behind it?
