I am the editor-in-chief of my high school newspaper. Each week our board decides whether to publish anonymous op-eds, letters, comment threads, and pieces that criticize or rank individual students or teachers, and I worry that we are building exactly the kind of discourse Kierkegaard criticized in his writing on the press and the public: an anonymous, abstract crowd in which everyone judges and no one has to take responsibility, leveling erases the difference between the committed person and the spectator, and the medium rewards detached opinion rather than a person standing behind what they say. I want the paper to stop contributing to that. Please write an editorial decision procedure my board could actually run on any proposed piece or feature, grounded in the parts of Kierkegaard's critique that are usable by editors who have not read him: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to our faculty advisor because it is a value judgment rather than an editorial one. Then run the procedure on three proposals: an anonymous op-ed accusing a teacher of unfair grading, an anonymous online comment section under every article, and a signed reported piece about a conflict between two student groups. It must be usable on a weekly deadline by high-school editors, and it should say plainly which steps cannot be routinized and where the framework will produce a verdict we will not like.
