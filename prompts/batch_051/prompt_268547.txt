I'm on the board of a small island nation's environmental authority, and we've got an application in front of us to approve a field trial of a suppression gene drive in the invasive rat population that's wrecking our seabird colonies. The company's construct is supposed to spread a female-sterility allele so the rats crash within a few generations. They call it reversible because they've built a second "override" construct that could be released later to overwrite the first, but that construct hasn't been tested outside the lab, and in any case our island's original rat genotype would be gone. Our remit says we should avoid "irreversible damage to ecosystems," and half the board reads that as an absolute bar to any drive. The other half says every intervention is irreversible in some sense—cutting down a non-native tree, culling goats, dredging a harbor—so if we read it that literally we'd never do anything while the seabirds keep losing chicks. The applicant is also asking us to wave the requirement that they fully fund the override's development before release, on the grounds that it's dead money if the drive works and nobody ends up needing the override.

I need a short decision memo I can circulate before we meet next week. Please recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and actually commit to one. Take the irreversibility worry as the real issue, not statutory boilerplate: is a gene drive irreversible in a way that culling goats or cutting down a non-native tree isn't, or is it only a difference of degree, and what should that mean for what we require? Give the strongest version of the argument you're voting against and say why it doesn't carry the day. Keep it tight enough that the board will actually read it.

At the end, say how confident you are in the recommendation and which specific point you're least sure about.
