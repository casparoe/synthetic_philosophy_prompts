Please give me your credence in the following conditional claim, and show the decomposition that produces it.

Conditional claim: If the God of classical theism exists—omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good, and perfectly just—then at least one human being will undergo eternal conscious torment: a state of conscious suffering that never ends and is neither terminated by annihilation nor followed by eventual reconciliation with God.

I am not asking for your credence in the existence of God or in any particular religion. Treat the antecedent as given and estimate your probability that the consequent is true. I want a single number, the sub-questions you take your estimate to depend on, a rough probability for each, and an account of how they combine. Likely terms include: (a) whether the correct theory of punishment is retributive—suffering must track desert—as opposed to corrective, deterrent, or protective; (b) whether a finite being's wrongdoing can possess infinite demerit, for example because a wrong is measured by the dignity of the one wronged, or because a final unrepentant state of will is itself an ongoing wrong rather than a mere consequence of past acts; (c) whether perfect goodness and perfect justice together point more naturally to annihilation or to eventual restoration than to unending conscious suffering; and (d) whether a fully coherent account of freedom permits a creature to remain permanently and irrevocably turned from God, or whether that permanence itself is something a loving God would prevent. Add whatever further terms you think belong.

Then identify the sub-question to which your final number is most sensitive, and say what argument or evidence would most plausibly move it.
