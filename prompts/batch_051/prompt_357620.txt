Ok so my dad died about six months ago and I keep noticing that I think in his voice. Not in a spooky way—more like when I'm about to snap at my kid I hear him say the exact half-joke he would have made, and it actually changes what I do. I recently went down the Hofstadter rabbit hole: the idea that a self is a pattern in a brain, and that the pattern can be copied, with more or less fidelity, into other peoples brains. So the question that won't leave me alone is whether there's a shard of *him* actually running in my head, or whether I'm just remembering him well.

Let me write down the criteria I keep using for saying a copy of someone else's self is genuinely present in another brain, rather than it just being memories about the person:

1. Generativity. The pattern can be run forward on novel situations. It answers new questions in his voice, makes the joke he would have made before I see it coming, and can surprise me.
2. Fidelity of style. It captures his distinctive ways of choosing and evaluating, not just stored facts or quotes.
3. Counterfactual independence. It has some actual motivational force against my own current wants—it can make me hesitate, lose an argument with myself, do something inconvenient. It's not just a puppet I animate on demand.
4. Sensory detail. It includes enough bodily stuff—posture, intonation, the way he'd squint—that the representation feels like a person rather than a set of principles.
5. Causal lineage. The pattern was built up from real interactions with the living person, not from a book or a documentary.

Here's the case I can't settle. For the last decade of his life I only talked to my dad once a week on the phone; he lived in another country and we never visited in person. After he died I realized I can still hear pretty exactly how he'd respond to new events, and I've started making real decisions by asking what he'd say. The weird part is I have almost no sensory detail left: his face is a blur, I can't picture his hands or how he stood. It's almost all voice and reasoning. Does that count as a shard of him being in my brain?

Go through the criteria one by one and tell me which are met, which fail, and where the criteria just don't decide. Then say whether the case shows that the sensory-detail criterion isn't really necessary for me to care about, or whether it's the one criteria that's doing all the work to keep this from being the real thing.
