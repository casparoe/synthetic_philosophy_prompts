Question (10 points). Let G be a finite two-player strategic-form game. In an epistemic model with a state space and a partition for each player, define (i) what it means for an event to be common knowledge at a state, and (ii) what it means for a player to be rational at a state. Then state the main characterization result connecting common knowledge of rationality to solution concepts, and explain whether common knowledge of rationality implies that the players' strategy profile is a Nash equilibrium.

Student's answer. An event E is common knowledge at a state ω if everyone knows E, everyone knows that everyone knows E, and so on; equivalently, E contains the cell of the meet of the players' partitions that contains ω. Player i is rational at ω if her chosen action maximizes her expected utility given her information and her beliefs about the other player's action. Common knowledge of rationality means that the event that both players are rational is common knowledge.

When rationality is common knowledge, no player can be playing a strategy that is never a best response, and each player knows that the other is not playing such a strategy either. Iterating this argument eliminates exactly the strategies that are not rationalizable, so the strategies consistent with common knowledge of rationality are the rationalizable strategies, which in two-player games are the strategies surviving iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies.

Because rational players form correct beliefs about what the other player will do, common knowledge of rationality implies that each player's strategy is a best response to the other player's actual strategy, so common knowledge of rationality implies that the profile played is a Nash equilibrium. Moreover, since iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies eliminates all but one strategy per player in most games, common knowledge of rationality typically gives a unique prediction.
