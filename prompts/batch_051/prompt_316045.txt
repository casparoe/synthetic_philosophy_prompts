I'm on the moderation team for a medium-sized Discord server that runs collaborative fiction events. We have a rule that any single moderator can lock a thread if they think a fight is escalating. Last week we had an incident where five different mods locked seven threads in one evening, and afterwards it turned out that each of them had seen the same two or three heated messages and thought something like "if I don't lock this now, it might get worse and nobody else will act in time." None of them was wrong exactly, but the outcome was a mess: people got confused about which rule violations were actually serious, and we had to walk back half the locks. I noticed the same shape in a lab I used to work in, where any lab member could order a replacement reagent if they saw it was low, and we ended up with four people each ordering the same $400 antibody in the same week because everyone assumed someone else might forget.

My question: is there an established name for this specific failure mode? A group gives many people the individual ability to do the same thing, each person reasons that acting is slightly better than not acting because they might be the only one to notice, and then several people act, and the result is worse than if exactly one had. I'm not looking for a bare label; I want to know how it relates to the bystander effect, diffusion of responsibility, and tragedy of the commons, and where its exact borders are. I have a hunch it's not quite the bystander effect, because nobody was assuming someone else would act; they were assuming others might not act. If there's no standard name, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from cases where the problem is just people free-riding on one another's inaction?
