After a long argument with a friend about whether buying both lottery tickets and insurance at the same time is irrational, I tried to write down everything I actually believe about decisions under risk, and now I'm worried the list doesn't cohere. I'd like you to audit it: go through the commitments, tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, how bad each tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be, and then say which single revision would do the most to straighten the whole thing out. I'd also appreciate it if you kept track of which apparent conflicts dissolve once you spell things out, because a couple of my friends have pointed at different pairs and I think some of those are false alarms.

Here's the list:
(1) I think the correct normative theory of decisions under risk is expected utility maximization: there's some utility function and some probability assignment such that the rational act just is the one with the highest expected utility.
(2) I think the independence axiom is the deepest part of that picture. If I prefer one gamble to another, tacking the same extra branch onto both shouldn't flip the ranking, because the common part cancels.
(3) But I also have the classic Allais preferences and don't take them back: I'd choose a guaranteed million over an 89% chance of a million, a 10% chance of five million, and a 1% chance of nothing; and I'd choose a 10% chance of five million over an 11% chance of a million. I've looked at the numbers and I'd do the same thing tomorrow, so I don't think either choice is just a mistake.
(4) I think what's going on in the first choice is a legitimate taste for certainty. A guaranteed outcome has value over and above it's expected value; a sure thing isn't just equivalent to a spread of probabilistic chances.
(5) I also think anticipated regret is part of it. If I turned down the sure million to gamble and then lost, I'd feel like an idiot, and avoiding that feeling is a real reason to take the sure thing.
(6) I'm pretty convinced by money-pump and dynamic consistency arguments: if a set of preferences lets a clever trader make a guaranteed profit off me, or makes my later self pay to undo what my earlier self chose, then those preferences are irrational.
(7) I think framing effects are just errors. Whether people flip their choices when the same gamble is described as a loss instead of a foregone gain tells us nothing about what it's rational to choose; that's a bug to be corrected, not a feature to respect.

So, which commitments are in tension with which? For each real tension, how severe is it, and what's the cheapest way to resolve it? And if I want to change as little as possible, which single commitment should I revise or drop?
