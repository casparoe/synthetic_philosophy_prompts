A friend of mine, who knows that I have been reading about consent and autonomy, recently summarised a magazine article for me in a way that I found suspicious but could not fully answer. As she told it, the article drew the distinction between manipulation and legitimate persuasion roughly as follows. Legitimate persuasion engages the other person's rational faculties: one offers reasons, evidence, and arguments, and lets the other person weigh them. Manipulation, by contrast, bypasses those faculties; instead of addressing the target as a deliberator, the manipulator works directly on the non-rational springs of action—fear, flattery, guilt, framing, repetition—to produce the desired conduct without the target's ever scrutinising the matter. Because the influence is non-rational, it is also necessarily hidden: if you notice that someone is appealing to your emotions, you can discount it, so manipulation only works when the target does not notice it. And because the target never gets to exercise rational control over the decision, manipulation always undermines autonomy; that is why it is always wrong, and why consent obtained through manipulation is never valid. The article ended by concluding that, since even the most honest persuasion has to choose some framing, some emphasis, and some emotional register, the line between manipulation and persuasion is ultimately just a matter of whose tactics one happens to like.

I am inclined to accept the initial contrast between being addressed as a reasoner and having one's emotions worked on directly, but the slide from that contrast to 'always hidden' and 'always wrong' feels too quick, and the closing cynicism seems to be doing a lot of work. I would be grateful for an accuracy audit. Which parts of this summary would a careful philosopher working on manipulation and autonomy accept as fair, which parts are controversial or subtly misstated, and why? In particular, is the 'bypassing reason' picture as clean as the summary suggests, is manipulation really always covert, and does the article's final relativist conclusion actually follow from the parts that are accurate? Please say what a defender of a bypassing account would defend and what they would disown.
