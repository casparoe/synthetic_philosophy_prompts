I run a small undergraduate seminar on formal systems and puzzles — 12 students, 75 minutes a week. I want to design one session around the MU-puzzle where students genuinely feel the difference between working inside a system and jumping out to reason about it, without me announcing that distinction up front. The rules: start with the string MI. You may add a U to any string ending in I; from Mx you may form Mxx; any occurrence of III may be replaced with U; and any occurrence of UU may be deleted. The goal is to reach MU. Design the session as a group activity: how to set it up, what to hand out, a light competitive scoring scheme (no money), timed phases, and a debrief. Don't tell them in advance wether MU is reachable — I want groups to get stuck and then discover for themselves that there's a property of the strings that every rule preserves and MU lacks. In the debrief, map what they did onto the idea of jumping out of the system (jootsing): derivation inside the rules versus proving something about the rules. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong — a group solving it too fast and spoiling it, a group getting bored or frustrated and sneaking a look online, or a group refusing to switch from pushing symbols to looking for an invariant — and how I should handle each. And be honest about what this puzzle can't teach about jootsing that I'd have to supply myself, maybe including why finding an invariant feels like a different kind of move than applying a rule.
