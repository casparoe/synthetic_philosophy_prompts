A friend summarized negative utilitarianism for me like this: "The basic idea is that preventing pain is morally more urgent than creating happiness, and the two aren't just symmetrical columns in a ledger—you can't weigh a new harm against a new pleasure like they're the same currency. A lot of negative utilitarians take that to the logical end: no amount of happiness justifies adding even a little suffering, roughly the way no number of mild headaches added to the population could make up for torturing one person. So push the view honestly and you get the world-destroyer conclusion: a true negative utilitarian has to say that pressing a button that painlessly ended all sentient life would be right, since nothing bad would ever happen again. Most of them dodge this, which shows they're smuggling in ordinary moral limits their principle can't actually pay for."
