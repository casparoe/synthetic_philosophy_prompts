In an AI alignment working group I'm part of, we have spent the past year stalled on a single question: how the multiple dimensions of value should be combined when specifying what a system should steer for — the sign and intensity of felt experience, its duration, the number of subjects, autonomy or consent, and knowledge or achievement, among others. Three positions keep reproducing themselves. The additive camp treats value as a weighted sum of separable dimensions, so a shortfall on any one dimension can in principle be offset by enough of another. The multiplicative camp holds that some dimensions multiply rather than add — the value of an experience is roughly the product of its degree of consciousness and its hedonic tone, possibly times duration — so that zero on one factor zeroes the product, a highly conscious being in pain counts as worse than the sum of the valenced parts would suggest, and an unconscious state gets no intrinsic credit for the other goods that are present. The lexical camp holds that some dimensions, such as the absence of extreme suffering or the presence of consent, outrank the others and cannot be traded away for any amount of what ranks below them. The frustrating part is that we agree on nearly all the descriptive facts and on the paradigm cases; the disagreements show up only in edge cases, but those edge cases are exactly where a deployed system's objective function would do most of its discriminating.

My working hypothesis is that this is not an empirical dispute and may not even be a first-order evaluative one. I suspect the three camps are using the aggregation function to do three different jobs — reporting an underlying quantity, legislating how tradeoffs are to be made, and expressing which values are incomparable in the strong sense — and that this is why more cases never move anyone. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this actually: verbal, substantive, a difference about choice theory, a difference about what a value function represents, or bedrock intuitions about when compensation is illegitimate? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a formal result such as an impossibility theorem, a new distinction, a decision experiment, a change of question, or a principled argument that no progress should be expected?
