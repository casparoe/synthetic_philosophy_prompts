I work as a software engineer, and a question about rules that are never enforced has been nagging at me. In my field, a rule that no one actually invokes is treated as effectively dead: a code path that has not executed in years gets deleted without much debate, and a company policy that management never enforces stops being part of how the place actually operates. Private legal arrangements seem to follow the same pattern. A contract term the parties have ignored for years can be treated as waived or abandoned, and a custom that no one has practiced in living memory carries no authority.

Yet the state's own statutes seem to work by a different logic. A law can go unenforced for decades — no prosecutions, no citations, no official action under it at all — and still be treated as valid law. A prosecutor can revive it against a single defendant tomorrow, and courts will often hold that the long silence has no bearing on whether the law binds. I have since learned that there is a name for the contrary idea, desuetude: the claim that a long-unenforced law should lose its force. Many legal systems reject it, or at least refuse to apply it to statutes.

That strikes me as a sharp asymmetry in how we treat rules generally. Continued non-use slowly dissolves private rules, contracts, customs, and workplace policies, but public statutes survive indefinite non-use intact. I want to know whether anything actually justifies this asymmetry. Please give me the strongest principle you can find that would explain and defend it — something about democratic authorship, separation of powers, who should have the power to repeal, fair notice, or whatever else you think does the work — and then test that principle hard. Try breaking it against the nearest cases you can construct, such as a private rule suddenly revived after decades against a person who relied on its death, compared with a criminal prohibition suddenly revived under the same conditions. Whatever principle survives, tell me plainly whether the asymmetry is one we should keep or one we should give up.
