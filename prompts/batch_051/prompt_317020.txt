I've been reading rationalist-blog and forum discussions of the paradox of voting for years, so I know the standard resolutions (expressive benefits, civic duty, altruism toward everyone affected by the result, minimax regret), and I'm comfortable with the basic expected-utility calculus. I want you to work through a modified version of the canonical setup, not to rehearse the standard answers.

You are a formal political theorist with a background in social choice and applied game theory. Take a large two-candidate winner-take-all election. In the standard rational-choice account, a purely instrumental voter votes when pB − C > 0, where p is her probability of being decisive, B is how much she values her preferred candidate winning, and C is her cost of voting. Now change the payoff structure. Suppose the voter also cares about the margin: her total utility is B·W + λM, where W ∈ {0,1} indicates whether her preferred candidate wins and M is that candidate's margin, counted as positive if she wins and negative if she loses. Because casting a vote increases M by exactly one regardless of how everyone else votes, the λM term yields a benefit that does not have to be multiplied by p.

Work through how this variation changes the paradox. What does the modified voting condition reduce to, and what happens as the electorate grows? Then assess whether the λM term is a genuinely instrumental resolution of the puzzle or merely a relabeling of expressive voting. Finally, consider the most defensible interpretation of λ—say, that larger margins produce stronger or more durable mandates—and examine whether the paradox reappears once λ is itself modeled as depending on M, or on whether M crosses salient thresholds, rather than being a fixed constant.

Please start by brainstorming two or three candidate formalizations of the mandate term, choose the one you think is most faithful to the intuitive idea, and then reason through the question with that formalization. At the end, tell me explicitly what would change your mind about whether this variant dissolves the paradox, and where in your reasoning a mistake is most likely to creep in.
