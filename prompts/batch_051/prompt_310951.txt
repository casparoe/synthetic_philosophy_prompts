Write an essay on a controversy about the DNA–Gödel isomorphism as presented through Douglas Hofstadter's Typogenetics, the toy formal system in Gödel, Escher, Bach. In Typogenetics, DNA-like strings encode enzymes, and an enzyme's structure determines how it cuts, copies, inserts, or deletes other strings; the system is set up so that some strings encode enzymes that act on the very strings that encode them, allowing a string to direct its own replication. Hofstadter treats this as the same kind of loop found in Gödel's incompleteness theorem, where arithmetization—coding symbols and formulas as numbers—lets a formal system talk about its own proof relation. Weigh the case for treating this as a genuine structural isomorphism against the objection that it is only a loose analogy: DNA and enzymes interact causally in physical time, with errors, energy, and no intrinsic notion of provability or truth, while Gödelian self-reference exists only relative to a chosen coding and an interpretation of formulas as arithmetical statements. Take the strongest version of each side seriously, but do not end on a non-committal note. Defend a verdict about whether the isomorphism claim earns its name, and justify it.
