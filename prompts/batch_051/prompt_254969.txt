For the past several months I have been living inside a decision: whether to take up a permanent post abroad or remain here to care for a parent whose health is failing. What unsettles me is not only the choice but the recurring sense that both futures are already complete and that deciding is less like making something than like discovering which of two finished rooms I was already in. That feeling has led me back to Borges, who seems, across different works, to have held both that every path is actual and that time, the medium of paths, is not real at all.

I would like a careful philosophical reading of "The Garden of Forking Paths" through the lens of the case Borges develops in "A New Refutation of Time." As I understand the essay, its central move is to treat temporal succession not as something we discover in the world but as something the subject contributes to experience; it then borrows Berkeley's and Hume's denials of enduring substance and of a continuous self to suggest that, once the self is dissolved into a bundle of present contents, the flow of time loses its apparent bearer. The story appears to move in the opposite direction: Ts'ui Pên's novel is a labyrinth in which every possible outcome of every event occurs, and the garden of forking paths becomes an image of time as a web of diverging, converging, and parallel futures, all equally real.

The reading I need should say whether these two texts are ultimately consistent or in tension. Does the forking garden preserve time by multiplying it, or does it dissolve serial time into an atemporal web, closer to the essay's conclusion than it first appears? Where does the story resist the essay's case — for instance, in its insistence that choices are consequential and even lethal, since Yu Tsun kills a man in order to transmit a name? And where does the essay expose something the story's spatial metaphor quietly smuggles back in, since even a web of forking paths is built out of lines, positions, and directions, and may presume the very succession it claims to replace? I want a reading that treats the fiction as thought in its own right rather than as an illustration of the essay, and that is willing to say where the two cannot be made to agree.
