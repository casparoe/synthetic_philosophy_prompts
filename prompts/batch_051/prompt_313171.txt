In my government class we learned that in the United States a corporation counts as a legal person: it can own property, enter contracts, be sued, and even be convicted of crimes. Yet a corporation is not a natural person; it seems to be just a collection of people and assets, and every one of those people and assets can be replaced while the corporation is said to stay the same. At the same time, when a few neighbors form a casual carpool, the law does not treat their group as a single entity at all. I am trying to figure out what this practice presupposes about when a collection of things makes up one thing, and about how such a thing can persist while its parts change. What theory of composition and identity over time does corporate law implicitly rely on? Does that theory really mark the difference between a corporation and a carpool, or does it quietly commit the law to a controversial position about social groups?
