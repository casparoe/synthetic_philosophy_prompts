I keep trying to steelman the way rich-country governments actually treat paid work, and I cannot get the pieces to fit into a single view. I want to know what philosophical commitments the whole pattern presupposes.

Here is the pattern as I observe it. Governments and politicians claim credit for the jobs created by a project, a subsidy, a trade deal, or a regulation; development agencies pay firms per job retained; automation, offshoring, and mergers are attacked in public debate primarily for destroying jobs. In this register, what the job produces is often treated as beside the point; that the job exists is itself treated as a benefit. Yet the same governments require unemployment-benefit recipients to search for work and accept suitable offers on pain of losing support, while treating retirement, disability, and sometimes full-time study or caregiving as legitimate ways to be outside the labor force. The same legal systems also regulate work as if it were a burden: they cap hours, mandate rest and leave, set a floor under wages, and compensate occupational injury. And when someone proposes simply paying people not to work, the resistance is moral, not merely fiscal.

Please reconstruct the implicit theory of the value of work that makes this pattern coherent, or show that no single theory can. In particular: distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, since a purely economic story about idle capacity, public budgets, and skill depreciation seems to cover part of the pattern but not the specifically moral tone; say whether the practice is underdetermined between rival views of why work matters, such as contribution, social recognition, desert, self-realization, or membership in a cooperative scheme; locate the fault line between the job-creation register and the work-as-burden register, including the make-work case where a job produces nothing anyone wants; and say what policy would have to change under each of the main rival theories, especially a pure-disutility view and a meaningful-work-only view. I am not asking for a history of these policies or for scholarly references; I want the commitments stated precisely enough that I can see whether the practice really requires them.
