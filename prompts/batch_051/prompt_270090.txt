For the past few years I have kept a running list of the verdicts my epistemology advisor gives on knowledge cases, because she is strikingly consistent. She says Dana does not know her lottery ticket will lose when she believes this only on the basis of the million-to-one odds before the draw, but does know it lost the next morning after reading the published numbers in the paper. She says, in what looks like a deliberate denial of closure, that a man who looks at his hands knows he has hands, but does not know he is not a handless brain in a vat being fed those very experiences, once that hypothesis has been raised. And she says that when Nadia asks a passing stranger where the library is and is confidently told it is two blocks east, with no reason to mistrust the stranger, Nadia knows the library is two blocks east.

Now I want to predict how she will rule on a new case. Nadia asks a stranger where the train station is and is confidently told it is two blocks left; the station really is two blocks left, and she believes it. This time, just after the stranger walks away, Nadia's companion remarks that some locals amuse themselves by sending visitors the wrong way, and Nadia cannot tell whether this passerby was one of them. At least two rules seem to fit the earlier verdicts—something like a tracking or sensitivity condition, and something like a relevant-alternatives condition—and they look as if they point in different directions here.

I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the rule behind the track record. Give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the candidate rules that best fit her earlier verdicts, stated precisely; (2) what each rule predicts about the station case; (3) your best guess about what she would say, with the strongest reason for preferring that rule as the one she is actually running; (4) which earlier case is the closest analog and how it cuts; (5) one further case, not among those already described, whose verdict would most cleanly decide between the remaining rules.
