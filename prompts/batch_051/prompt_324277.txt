I'm an intake counselor at a university counseling center, about fifteen half-hour intakes a week. A lot of students come in saying they want to "get control" over something — doomscrolling, porn, food, spending, gaming — and I have to decide on the spot whether to write that into their treatment plan as a legitimate self-control goal or flag it as something closer to internalized shame or self-punishment that we should explore instead. My coworkers are split: half treats any self-restraint goal as healthy discipline, half treats any strong self-denial as repression by default. I want a reusable intake procedure for making this call, grounded in the actual philosophical distinction between temperance as a virtue, prudence as ordinary rational self-management, and repression — not a summary of the theory, but the distinction turned into questions I can actually ask: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with "support the goal" and where it stops with "explore instead," and where it hands the call to me or my supervisor because it's a values judgment rather than a factual one. It has to fit in a 30-minute intake, can't require any formal diagnosis, and has to end with something I can justify to my supervisor, who signs of on every plan. Then run it on three cases: a student who wants to quit porn because it's wrecking his sleep and self-respect; a student who wants to stop all masturbation because she feels disgusting afterwards; and a student who wants to cut her calories drastically because she says her eating is out of control. Make sure at least one case comes out in a way I won't like, and say plainly what the procedure can't settle.
