Im a retired physical chemist — spent thirty years with very well-behaved instruments, so the messiness of human reasoning is a recent hobby — and Ive been reading around in the 'rationality can be trained like a martial art' scene. The pitch is that good thinking is a set of practiced moves, katas and sparring and graded belts of calibration, not just a pile of facts, and that the moves transfer out of the dojo into real life. I'm skeptical but also can't stop poking at it, which is probably itself the kind of thing they'd have a name for.

I'd like you to write me a parody in the form of a promotional FAQ for a Rationality Dojo: about 700 words, straight-faced, in the voice of a sincere but slightly culty martial-arts school website. Format it as a short 'about the dojo' blurb and then maybe six or seven questions and answers. The jokes should come from getting the actual content right rather than from cheap shots: belt ranks tied to calibration and base-rate fluency, a kata for updating on evidence against your pet theory, sparring as steelmanned disagreement, a premortem drill, the awkwardness of trying to drill slow deliberate moves into fast automatic responses, the sensei who answers every question with a koan about noticing your own confusion, and above all the transfer problem — students who perform beautiful moves on textbook problems and then go home and fall for the exact same biases with their uncle. Include a testimonial from a student who passed their black-belt calibration exam and then immediately lost an argument about vitamins. It should be funny to someone who knows this subculture and readable to someone who doesn't, and the satire should feel fair — exaggerate real features, don't invent flaws. The target is the practice itself, the promise that drilled technique transfers, not any particular person, so invent the dojo and don't name real people or organizations. Stay deadpan; no winking.
