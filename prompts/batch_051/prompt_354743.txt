Analyze the mid-century controversy over whether cigarette smoking causes lung cancer as a case study in the epistemology of causal discovery from observational data, using the framework of causal Bayes nets together with the conditions of faithfulness and minimality.

Treat the following background as given. From the 1950s onward, case-control and cohort studies reported a strong, dose-related association between cigarette smoking and lung cancer. R. A. Fisher and others argued that association alone does not establish causation: he pressed the hypothesis that a shared constitutional or genetic factor might dispose people both to smoke and to develop lung cancer, so that smoking would be at most a correlate rather than a cause. The 1964 report of the U.S. Surgeon General's Advisory Committee reached the opposite conclusion, judging smoking a cause of lung cancer; it leaned on the strength, consistency, specificity, and temporal ordering of the association, on experimental and biological evidence, and it explicitly addressed Fisher's alternative without finding it sufficient to overturn the causal conclusion. Subsequent laboratory and molecular work is generally taken to have closed the gap. I know the historical record; the task here is the mapping between theory and case, not further historical reconstruction.

For this analysis, adopt the following precisifications. A causal Bayes net is a directed acyclic graph over variables, paired with a probability distribution satisfying the Causal Markov Condition: every variable is independent of its non-descendants conditional on its parents. Faithfulness says that the distribution exhibits no independence relations beyond those entailed by d-separation in the graph. Minimality says that one should not retain any edge whose deletion leaves a graph that still satisfies the Causal Markov Condition for the same distribution. Latent common causes are permitted.

Write the case analysis. In particular: (a) reconstruct the inference problem as the two sides actually faced it — what candidate causal structures did Fisher and the committee countenance, and what dependence or independence patterns over the measured variables did each structure predict? (b) Say what a reasoner whose only input is the pattern of conditional dependence and independence among the measured variables, and whose only structural principles are faithfulness and minimality, should have concluded from the 1950s observational evidence, and exactly which alternatives remain open. Be explicit about where faithfulness and minimality pull apart. (c) Identify what the committee's appeal to non-statistical and experimental criteria accomplished that faithfulness and minimality could not accomplish on their own. (d) Finally, assess what the episode shows about the thesis that minimal or faithful Bayes nets track the true causal structure: does it confirm that thesis, refute it, or reveal it as incomplete, and in which respects does the theory fall silent? Do not force the theory to speak where its assumptions give out. Please answer in British English.
