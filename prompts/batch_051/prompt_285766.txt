I've been volunteered to lead a two-hour seminar on Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action (zhixing heyi) for a group of final-year philosophy undergraduates whose training is almost entirely analytic, and who have just finished a unit on weakness of will. Their reflex will be that akrasia straightforwardly refutes the doctrine, and I want the session to get them past that without turning Yangming into either a behaviourist for whom only the outward act counts or a Socratic intellectualist with Chinese vocabulary. Please don't explain the doctrine to me; plan how I, the seminar leader, should explain it.

What I need is a structured teaching plan: the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what at each step; the single carrying example that should do most of the work, together with a case for it. I keep reaching for the stock cases of the person who can recite what pain is like without ever having felt it and the person who knows everything about filial piety but does not act on it, but I suspect each sells the view short, so tell me clearly which example to build the session around and what it must have and what it must lack. I also need the misconception map: the misreadings this particular group will form, in the order they will form them, and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off. I am especially unsure how to stop them concluding either that deliberation becomes pointless or that weakness of will has merely been defined away, so address that directly. End with one question I can put to them whose answer would show that someone has grasped the doctrine rather than learned to mouth the phrase. And tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level and how to say so without leaving them with a caricature — I take it the later teaching on extending innate knowledge and the polemic against Zhu Xi should be set aside, but I would like that decision made cleanly.

In your reply, lead with the bottom line in the first sentence — the one teaching decision the whole session should be organised around — and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
