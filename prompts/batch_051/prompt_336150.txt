Our climate group has been going in circles about whether to keep pouring weekends into a campaign that most of us privately think has a couple percent chance of working. Every time it comes up, someone ends up saying "hope is not a strategy," and everyone nods like that settles it. Last night Priya actually spelled it out against Dev: she said his "we've got a real shot" was just base-rate neglect wearing a virtue costume, and that hope that outruns the evidence is an epistemic vice even when it keeps people moving. Dev said she was confusing two jobs a thought can do. Hope isn't a probability estimate, so it isn't the kind of thing that can be miscalibrated; it's a practical stance that keeps you acting when the odds are lousy, more like courage than a weather forecast. Priya replied that this is a motte-and-bailey: when he gets credit for being hopeful, the hope is propositional (there's a real chance), but when the belief is attacked, he retreats to "it's just a feeling." I'm less interested in who won than in the sentence they were fighting over. Unpack "hope is not a strategy" for me: what distinct claims could it be making, which readings are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are probably false, and which reading would actually have done the work Priya needed? And is Dev's distinction between hope-as-virtue and hope-as-estimate a genuine one that survives the motte-and-bailey charge, or a retreat?
