My buddy Dana has this personal system for sorting animals (and a few non-animals) into four piles based on how they get good at things. I've been trying to work out the actual rule she's using, because she never says it straight out, and I want to know how she'd sort some new cases.

Her four piles, the way she talks:

1. Creatures that dont improve during their own lifetime. They come prewired, and the species only changes over generations by blind selection. She puts ticks and bacteria here. You can't train a tick.

2. Creatures that learn by trial and error from their own wins and mistakes. No planning; they just do stuff and whatever works sticks. She puts pigeons, rats, and dogs here, though she admits dogs sometimes feel like more than that. Her first answer is always pile 2 for a dog.

3. Creatures that can try stuff out in their head before they commit. They can get it right on the first try, or close to it. She puts crows and chimps here. She also put a chess program here, the kind that searches through millions of moves before choosing, because it tries things internally instead of in the real world.

4. Creatures that get smart by borrowing tools from other members of their kind, like words, maps, counting, writing. She puts humans here, especially once they have language. She says a toddler is mostly pile 3 until she's talking, then she's moving into 4.

The thing I cant figure out is what she really means by pile 4. Sometimes she talks about it as if it's about words and symbols, like thinking with tools that sit outside your own skull. Other times she says it's about getting a trick from the group instead of figuring it out yourself. Those are not the same thing, right? But she doesn't seem to notice the difference, probably because her only pile 4 examples are humans, and humans do both.

So here's the new case I keep going back and forth on. There's a monkey (a macaque, say) that learns to wash the dirt off its food by watching another monkey do it, and the trick spreads through the troop over time. There's social learning, but no words or maps. Where would Dana put it? Pile 2 because it's still just learning by doing, pile 3 because the monkey picks up a new move and uses it sensibly, or pile 4 because the trick gets passed around the group? And what does her answer tell me about what pile 4 is actually tracking? If I could ask her about one more creature to force the issue, what should it be?
