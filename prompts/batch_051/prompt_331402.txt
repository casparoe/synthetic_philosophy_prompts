Thesis: Testimony (śabda) is an irreducible pramāṇa — a distinct source of knowledge. Its successful exercise can yield knowledge of the attested content, and its operation cannot be reduced to perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), or comparison (upamāna) without losing something essential. In particular, testimony is not a disguised form of inference.

My argument in brief: In genuine inference, the knower must cognize a pervasion relation (vyāpti) between an inferential mark and what it indicates. In ordinary testimonial uptake, a competent hearer comes to know that p on the speaker's say-so without computing any standing generalization about that speaker's reliability. The would-be reductive inference — from the fact that the speaker said that p, plus some general premise about the speaker's reliability, to the truth of p — is either unavailable to the hearer or already presupposes the very testimonial warrant it was meant to replace. So the epistemic route runs through the hearer's understanding of the assertion itself, and reducing it to inference misdescribes it.
