An argument that you should never press the button, no matter how many people it saves from a dust speck. The button tortures one random innocent person for fifty years, but it prevents an enormous number of people—every human who will ever live, times a billion—from each getting a single dust speck in the eye. Here's the argument:

(1) One dust speck in one person's eye is not worse than one person being tortured for fifty years.
(2) If n dust specks, each in a different person's eye, isn't worse than the torture, then n+1 dust specks isn't worse either. One extra speck—a harm so tiny most people barely register it—can't be the thing that flips the comparison from "not worse" to "worse."
(3) So, by induction, no number of dust specks is worse than the torture.
(4) So you shouldn't press the button, since what you'd prevent is not worse than what you'd do.

I cant see the hole, but I also don't want to accept (4). Is the argument valid? If it is, and I want to say the enormous pile of specks is worse after all, which premise do I have to give up—and does rejecting it force me to say there's an exact number of specks where it suddenly becomes worse? If the argument is invalid, point to the step and give the corrected version.
