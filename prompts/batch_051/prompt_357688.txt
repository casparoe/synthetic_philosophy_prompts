Her recent verdicts are these. She said a lecturer's use of a slur while reading from a historical document was not racist, because it was quoted in an instructional setting, flagged in advance, and aimed at no one. She called a search-committee chair's email describing a candidate as a poor "cultural fit" who did not understand "the community we actually serve" racist, but explicitly on the ground that the phrases were doing coded work rather than that they contained slurs. She called a colleague's repeated habit of telling their Black coworker that they were "so articulate" racist even after being told it was meant as praise. She called a news segment that paired footage of Black protesters with on-screen words such as "thugs" racist, saying the word had no explicit racial content but did not need any to do its work. And she said a statistician's remark that certain racial gaps were "exactly what one would expect under a purely socioeconomic model" was not racist, because she read it as a technical inference inside a scholarly dispute rather than as an evaluation of a group. What should I expect my colleague to say, and why? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the rule she seems to be running—intent, reasonable effect on the target group, dog-whistle or coded meaning, or some mix—the other rules that fit her past verdicts just as well, which of her earlier cases the new one is closest to, and the one further case you would most want before settling on the rule.
