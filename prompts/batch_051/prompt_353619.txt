ok so i just spent an hour arguing with a freind about subsidiarity and we realized we were working with totally different definitions of it. his version was basically: always let the smallest unit decide unless it absolutely cannot handle the thing. mine was: pick the lowest level that can actually achieve the goal, which in practice means the small level loses a lot more often. neither definition survived the examples we threw at it, so i'm handing them to you.

give me a working definition of subsidiarity that gets these cases right, or at least tells me clearly where the definition surprises me:

- a city raises its minimum wage above the state level, and the state preempts the local law
- a national ban on lead in house paint
- the EU's cookie-banner rules, which a small-business owner thinks is brussels meddling in something local
- a state bars a county from opting out of school vaccine mandates
- a school board votes to remove a book from the library while one parent wants it kept
- a town refuses to approve a group home on a residential street because the neighbors don't want it there
- a city sets emissions rules stricter than the federal minimum

i think the lead ban and the vaccine mandate belong at the higher level, and i think the city should be allowed to go stricter on emissions than the feds. i honestly don't know about the minimum wage one or the group home. propose the definition, run it through all seven, and tell me where it gives you a verdict you didn't expect. then be explicit about what would change your mind: which argument or which case is most likely to break the definition, and where your reasoning is least secure.
