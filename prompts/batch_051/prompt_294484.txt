A colleage and I have been going back and forth about Williams's criticism of utilitarianism, and I wrote down my own position on the integrity objection so it would stop shifting around while we argued. I'd like you to check whether the set actually hangs together. Here's what I've ended up committed to:

1. The wife/stranger example, where a man weighs whether to save his wife by appealing to general utility, is best read as a point about adopting act-utilitarianism as a decision procedure. It shows something off in the deliberation, but it's not by itself a refutation of act-utilitarianism as a criterion of rightness.

2. Even so, the alienation worry isn't fixed by indirect or sophisticated consequentialism. Being told to cultivate deep partial projects because having them tends to maximize expected utility, while holding them ready to be dropped when the calculation turns against them, is not compatible with genuinely having those projects.

3. Integrity, in the sense Williams is after, is just the having of projects that are not up for impartial trade—not because a rule forbids the trade, but because they are one's own.

4. So in the Jim case, where a visitor is asked to shoot one villager to stop a captain from killing the other nineteen, shooting the one would be wrong, and a person with integrity wouldn't do it. But I also wouldn't blame someone who shoots under that kind of duress; it's an excusable wrong.

5. The wrongness in the Jim case is best explained in broadly Kantian terms: shooting the one uses him merely as a means to the good end of saving the rest.

6. In the George case, where a chemist is deciding whether to take a job developing chemical weapons, the integrity theme has the opposite valence: refusing the job is permissible from integrity but not required, since George's qualm tracks his own commitments rather than a constraint he owes to anyone else.

What I'd like from you: go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and then order the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Be explicit about how confident you are in each of these verdicts and single out the point you're least sure of.
