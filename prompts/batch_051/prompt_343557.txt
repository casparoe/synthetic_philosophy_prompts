Write a satirical End-User Licence Agreement (EULA) of roughly 650 words for Closure, a belief-management platform whose stated service is to bring its users into full compliance with the idealisations of epistemic logic. The document should be deadpan corporate-legal boilerplate: numbered clauses, a definitions section, acknowledgement of terms, limitations of liability, and governing law. The target of the satire is the assumption of logical omniscience — the idealisation that a rational agent believes all the logical consequences of its beliefs. The joke must be carried by the clauses themselves: the platform should treat deductive closure as a contractual obligation rather than a theoretical fiction. It should land, without ever stating them, the standard objections to this idealisation: that even a single accepted proposition has infinitely many logical consequences; that real agents routinely fail to recognise consequences, sometimes trivial ones; that closure makes belief revision and retraction intractable; and that an inconsistency turns a belief set into the set of all propositions. Work the technical machinery into the clauses so a specialist can see the author understands the material: closure under conjunction introduction and disjunction introduction, modus ponens, the gap between explicit and implicit belief, the single-premise/multi-premise closure distinction, and the Preface-paradox case of an author who rationally accepts each claim in a manuscript while also accepting that at least one claim in it is false. Use the reflexivity of the genre where it is funniest — a user who accepts clause 1 should, by the agreement's own terms, already be deemed to accept every consequence of clause 1, including clauses they have not yet read. The document should never break character by mentioning epistemic logic, possible-worlds semantics, or logical omniscience; the satire should stay deadpan and yet remain legible, so a reader who has never heard of the idealisation still sees the absurdity, and a specialist recognises the criticisms are being landed fairly. Write the piece in British English.
