I'm an applied statistician who got pulled into a philosophy-of-science reading group, and there's a pattern in the Bayesian material that I can't get a clean grip on. On one side there's the "washing out" or "merging of opinions" story: if two rational people start with different priors but update by conditionalizing on the same stream of evidence, then after enough data their posterior distributions end up arbitrarily close, so the initial disagreement is eventually swamped. On the other side there's the objection that if your prior puts literally zero probability on the region that turns out to be true, Bayes' theorem keeps you at zero forever, and no amount of data can dig you out. These can't both be right as stated — or at least they have to be about subtly different things.

I'm not after a citation; I want the carving. My questions: (1) Is there a precise condition under which the first story holds — something like "the two priors have to agree about which events are possible," or "neither prior may put zero mass where the other puts positive mass" — and is that exactly the right condition, or is it a bit stronger or weaker than the real one? (2) Is "merging of opinions" (two agents ending up in agreement) the same thing as "consistency" (one agent ending up at the truth), or can you have one without the other? For instance, can two agents each converge to the true value and still fail to merge with each other; and can two agents merge even when one of them is settling on the wrong value? (3) Even where eventual merging is garuanteed, is that a result about the limit only, and does the speed of merging itself depend on the priors — so that in any finite, human-sized inquiry the priors still matter enormously?

Does this configuration have a name, or a family of names, and which name covers which part of it? I know there are nearby terms — "washing out of priors," "merging of opinions," "asymptotic agreement," maybe "grain of truth" — but I can't tell wether they're synonyms or genuinely different pieces of the picture. I'd be annoyed by a bare label; I need to know where the edges are: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and exactly what has to be true of the two priors for the washing-out slogan to be safe.
