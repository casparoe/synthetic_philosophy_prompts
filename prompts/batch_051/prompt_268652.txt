I am a patient and family representative on my hospital's clinical ethics committee; I have no clinical or philosophical training. We vote next week on a proposal to formalize time-limited trials of ventilation: a patient in respiratory failure is intubated under a written plan stating that, if there is no meaningful improvement after three days, ventilation will be withdrawn. The proposal is meant to offer patients a real chance at recovery without committing them to indefinite treatment. Several members object that this is planned killing: they say it is one thing never to start ventilation, and another to start it with the day of stopping already written into the plan, because then the death is part of the original design rather than a later decision. Others reply that the hospital already treats withholding and withdrawing treatment as morally equivalent, so a pre-scheduled withdrawal cannot be worse than never starting. Please draft the short memo I would circulate before the vote: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, and lay out the strongest version of the losing side before giving the recommendation. End with a line of the form \answer{approve}, \answer{reject}, or \answer{approve with conditions}.
