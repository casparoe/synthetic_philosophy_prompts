My kid got obsessed with our new saltwater tank and asked me last week whether the cleaner shrimp can be sad, and I realized my answers to this sort of question are all over the place. I want you to help me figure out whether there's a principle under my gut calls, or whether some of them have to go.

Here are my verdicts on cases:

- A dog with a broken leg: it feels pain, no question, I'd bet my house on it.
- A goldfish with a torn fin that's still eating and swimming around: yes, probably — I flinch at a fish on a hook even though I know some people write that off as reflex.
- An octopus with an injured arm that keeps curling the stump away from things: yes, and this one actually gets to me.
- A honeybee with a crushed abdomen that keeps foraging like nothing happened: I don't really think so, or if it does it's such a faint thing I can't bring myself to care.
- Our cleaner shrimp after it lost an antenna: no, or at least not in the same category as the fish — it just looks like a little robot to me.
- A clam: no.
- A frog with a cut leg: yes, though maybe less vividly than the dog.
- A starfish losing an arm: no, doesn't bother me.

The pair that pulls at me hardest is the fish and the bee. The bee's behavior is at least as flexible as the fish's — it learns, navigates, communicates — but I'm a lot more confident about the fish. I suspect I'm just giving the fish extra credit for being a vertebrate like me, and I'd rather not be doing that for no reason.

My working theory is something like: a creature counts if it has a centralized brain doing enough integration to make a unified picture, and behavior flexible enough that a pain signal could actually help it learn. That gives me dog-yes, fish-yes, octopus-yes, frog-yes, starfish-no, clam-no. But it makes the bee come out yes or maybe, and I keep wanting the bee to come out no. So either I need a cleaner criterion that keeps the bee out without just drawing the line right past it, or I overturn the bee verdict and bite the bullet on insects.

So: what principle (or small set of candidate principles) best captures my verdicts, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a new animal? For each candidate, tell me which of my cases motivate which clause. Then apply it to animals I haven't listed: a jumping spider that detours around obstacles to sneak up on prey, a crab with a torn-off claw, a sea slug with a tiny simple nervous system, a moth, and a lancelet — the little fish-shaped invertebrate with no true brain that's supposedly the closest invertebrate relative to vertebrates. Tell me honestly which of my gut calls the principle can't accomodate, and whether you think I should overturn those instead of complicating the principle to save them. Also tell me how confident you actually are in what you've said, and single out the point you're least sure about.
