I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether the no-free-lunch theorems in machine learning have anything interesting to say about Hume's problem of induction, and I want to see the two sides hashed out properly rather than just asserted at each other. By the no-free-lunch theorems I mean roughly the results that say that if you average over all possible target functions a learner might be trying to learn (or all possible objective functions an optimizer might be trying to optimize), any two algorithms have the same average performance: every advantage on one class of problems is exactly paid for by a disadvantage somewhere else. One reading is that this is basically a formal version of the old skeptical point. Nothing about the data themselves favors one generalization over another until you bring in some prior or inductive bias; the bias can't be justified from the data; so at bottom induction rests on a kind of leap. The other reading is that these theorems are philosophically over-sold. Averaging over all logically possible functions is a bizarre measure of expected reliability, because we are not trying to learn over a uniform space of all possible worlds. We are embedded in this world, where the future has so far tended to resemble the past, and a result about how an algorithm fares over every conceivable world may not touch the question of whether relying on induction is rational in the actual one.

Could you write a dialogue between two characters defending these two readings? Maybe make one a machine learning researcher and the other a philosopher of science, but the casting is up to you. I would like both to be equally sharp and neither a strawman, and they should actually respond to eachothers arguments, not just take turns monologuing. The best version would force them to get precise about where their disagreement really lives: is it about the math, about what the math shows, or about what Hume's problem even is? End the dialogue wherever you think the argument genuinely stands, even if that's at a standoff.
