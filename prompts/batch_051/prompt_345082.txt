I've been trying to figure out whether a historical claim is pseudohistory without getting stuck on credentials, and I keep reaching for a test I made up. I call it the reciprocity test. A position has good reciprocity when, before the argument even starts, its defender can say what kind of discovery would make them drop or seriously narrow the theory. Bad reciprocity — what I've been calling "confirmation sponging" — is when every apparent problem gets absorbed as more support: abscence of evidence means it was suppressed, contradictory evidence means it was faked, expert consensus against it means the experts are compromised. To me, the real difference between a live revision (say, the debate over when humans reached the Americas) and Atlantis, ancient aliens, or Fomenko's chronology isn't the amount of evidence; it's that the first passes my test and the others mostly don't. I realize this might just be Popper's falsifiability in a new hat. Is the reciprocity test actually picking out a real difference? And where does it go wrong — are there respectable historical positions that would fail it, or fringe views that would pass?
