I'm in an AI-safety reading group and I've been asked to write out, before the next meeting, what I actually believe about the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence, so the others can poke at it. Here's where I've landed: (a) I accept the orthogonality thesis — intelligence and final goals are independent dimensions, so in principle a superhumanly capable system could have any final goal whatsoever (paperclips, human flourishing, computing digits of pi), and being smart doesn't by itself push a system toward caring about us. (b) I also accept instrumental convergence: for almost any final goal, a sufficiently capable agent will pick up certain subgoals as means, namely self-preservation, resource acquisition, goal-content integrity, and self-improvement. (c) But I can't bring myself to take the "treacherous turn" seriously — deception and manipulation seem like social behaviors, useful only if you care what other agents believe, and a paperclip maximizer has no interest in our mental lives, so I'd expect it to just pursue paperclips directly rather than pretend to be friendly. (d) I'm similarly relaxed about corrigibility: if we want a system we can shut down or revise, we build it with a final goal like "achieve X, but remain open to being turned off or corrected by the operators," and since that's literally what it's optimizing, a competent system should treat shutdown as success rather than resist it. I think people make corrigibility mysterious when it's really just goal specification. (e) I'm not convinced goal stability is a separate problem either: if a powerful system rewrites its own goals while it improves, orthogonality says capabilities and goals are independent, so the rewrite doesn't make it any less safe — it could just as easily drift toward something harmless as something bad, so I don't see why goal-content integrity deserves the weight it gets in (b). (f) Overall, then, my view is that the alignment problem is mostly about choosing a safe final goal in the first place — something like "verify proofs and change nothing else in the world" — because once the goal is harmless the rest is just capability, and orthogonality says harmless goals and high capability coexist fine.

I genuinly think all of this hangs together, but I have a nagging feeling I'm papering over something.
