I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the ethics of war and intervention, and I am writing the final exam. I am looking for one essay question that uses Kosovo in 1999, Iraq in 2003, and Libya in 2011 to test whether students can do more than recite the standard just war criteria. In particular, I want the question to force students to explain how the same military operation can be described as humanitarian rescue in one framing and coercive regime change in another, and why that redescription is not merely rhetorical but morally consequential. I also want the question to press on a distinction I have emphasized in the course: whether the requirement of last resort should be understood as a temporal test, according to which every non-military alternative must literally have been exhausted, or as an epistemic threshold, according to which a state may resort to force when a reasonable judgment could conclude that further delay would impose unacceptable moral risk. Finally, the question should ask whether the presence of geopolitical motives alongside humanitarian ones undermines the permissibility of an intervention, or whether it bears only on broader questions of legitimacy and trustworthiness.

Please draft the exam question itself, a model answer of around 800 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 points. The marking scheme should name five specific moves a top answer must make, assign marks to each, and identify common mistakes students make in trying to execute each move. The difficulty should come from the structure of comparison and from the conceptual distinctions involved, not from the ability to recall historical details about the three cases.
