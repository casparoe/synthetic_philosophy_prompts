ok so im a junior in high school and i have to give a presentation for our "politics across the aisle" club, which is this thing where the young dems, young republicans, and a couple of socialists actually sit in a room and argue. my thesis is basically that we should stop trying to work out who's arguing in good faith, because from the inside you can almost never tell, and calling the other side bad faith is mostly a way of signaling to your own side and shutting the conversation down. i'm going to argue that even when your pretty sure someone is sea-lioning or concern-trolling, you should still answer the argument they actually made, because the alternative is that every disagreement just turns into a trial of the other person's motives.

my rough argument is: (1) good faith isnt observable from the outside -- the same behavior (asking a ton of questions, moving goalposts, repeating the same point) can be sincere confusion or deliberate trolling, and which one you see is mostly predicted by which side your on. (2) calling something bad faith does real rhetorical work: it lets you stop engaging and it rallies your own side, so we should expect people to over-ascribe it, which means treating it like a neutral diagnosis of the other side is a mistake. (3) the cost of a false positive (writing someone off as bad faith when theyre actually sincere but wrong or just annoying) is worse in this club than the cost of getting occasionally dragged into a sea-lioning, because the whole point of the club is to keep people talking across the line.

but the audience is not going to just nod along. the young republicans think the young dems constantly police tone instead of answering their actual arguments, and they still bring up this kid from last year who kept asking for sources and then refused to read them. the socialists think "good faith" is kind of a privilege thing and that some positions shouldnt get platformed at all. and theres one kid who thinks the whole club is a liberal trap and only joined to document it.

can you make me a q&a prep sheet? i want the ten objections i'll actually get from this group, not the polite textbook ones, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each one phrased the way those people would actually say it, with a two minute reply for each and a note on where my reply is weakest. and include the question im hoping nobody asks.
