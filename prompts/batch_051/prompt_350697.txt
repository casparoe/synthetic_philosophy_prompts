I serve on the board of a mid-sized food-recovery nonprofit that collects surplus food from grocers and restaurants and distributes it through a network of community pantries. We have long declined donations of meat, poultry, and dairy, partly because our facilities and volunteer drivers could not handle cold-chain storage and partly because several board members are committed vegans who believe an organization like ours should not treat animal products as ordinary food. We recently installed a walk-in freezer that solves the handling problem, which has forced the underlying values question into the open. A national fried-chicken chain has now offered to donate all of its unsold end-of-day chicken — food that would otherwise be discarded — on the condition that the chain may mention the partnership in its zero-waste and community-giving publicity. The executive director is genuinely undecided and wants a board decision before the end of the month.

I have been a vegetarian for years and find my sympathies genuinely split: I do not want our organization to become an instrument of industrial chicken farming, but I also find it hard to accept that edible, nutritionally valuable food should go to landfill while the pantries we serve run short of protein. The underlying issue, as I see it, is the one freegans raise — whether food that would otherwise be wasted can be used with a clean conscience even when it contains animal products — and I want the memo to take that issue seriously rather than treat it as settled. The animals are already dead, and accepting the donation sends no ordinary market signal that would increase future production; yet serving the chicken may still express and normalize the view that animals are a legitimate food resource, and the chain's publicity condition may make us complicit in burnishing its reputation.

Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate to the board. It should recommend one concrete course of action — accept the donations, refuse them, or accept with specific conditions — and give the reasoning for it, including an honest statement of the strongest consideration on the losing side and why it does not carry the day. Write the memo as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The first point should be the recommendation the board would vote on, and the remaining four points should be the reasons; at least one of the four should state the strongest argument for the position you are not recommending and your reply to it.
