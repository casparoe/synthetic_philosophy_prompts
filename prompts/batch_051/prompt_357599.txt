I am recording a philosophy podcast in a few weeks with a guest who defends a version of the afterlife conjecture: roughly, the claim, developed in Samuel Scheffler's work, that our confidence in the value of our activities and projects rests on an implicit assumption that human beings will continue to exist after we are gone, even though we ourselves will not. In the thought experiments she uses, you learn either that everyone will die shortly after you do, or that humanity will become infertile and quietly disappear once your generation is gone. The conjecture is that these scenarios would undermine the value of most of what we do, and that this reveals how much we depend on a collective afterlife rather than a personal one. The guest tends to insist that the reaction these scenarios provoke is a rational evaluative dependence rather than parochial sentiment, and to resist the idea that personal immortality or the posthumous endurance of one's own achievements could do the same work as the existence of future human lives.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press this view where it is hardest to defend. I want at least one question on each of the following, each with a follow-up that anticipates the reply she is most likely to give: (a) what exactly does the explanatory work in the thought experiments — the destruction of one's projects and artifacts, the absence of future valuers, or the ending of the human practice within which the activity had its point; (b) the antinatalist or pro-extinction challenge, from someone who judges human extinction good and orients their projects around hastening it; (c) whether the dependence she identifies is rational, psychological, or merely cultural, and what would distinguish those readings; and (d) how the conjecture relates to a personal afterlife, including whether sincere belief in one would dissolve the horror of the doomsday scenario. The follow-ups should not merely restate the first question. End the sequence with a question about what would most pressure her to revise the conjecture short of abandoning it.
