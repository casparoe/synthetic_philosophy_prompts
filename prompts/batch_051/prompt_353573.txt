I help run a debating club that archives every claim made in opening statements and tags each one as true, false, or neither. I came into this assuming that every statement is either true or false, with the third category reserved for a few odd edge cases, but my recent rulings have been less tidy than I expected. Looking back, I find I want to say all of the following. "The present king of France is bald" is neither true nor false, because there is no present king of France; but "There is no present king of France" is straightforwardly true. "The present king of France is not bald" is also neither true nor false, yet "It is not true that the present king of France is bald" is true, and I feel that the difference between the two negations matters. "There will be a sea battle tomorrow," supposing nothing in the present settles it either way, is neither true nor false; nevertheless "Either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there won't be" is true. "Jones is tall," said of a 5'10" man in a context where the threshold for "tall" is clearly higher than that, is neither true nor false, though I doubt this is the same kind of "neither" as the king of France case. "The number seven is green" is neither true nor false, not because it is vague and not because its subject fails to exist, but because the predicate seems not to apply to that kind of thing at all. And "Unicorns have four legs" is a case I keep flipping on: sometimes it feels vacuously true, sometimes neither, and I cannot explain why it differs from the king of France case.

I would like to know whether there is a single principled account behind these rulings, or whether I am actually running two or three distinct ideas of a truth-value gap at once. State the candidate principles precisely, say what each one predicts about the cases above, tell me which of my verdicts each principle forces me to give up, and then say what the most defensible account predicts about two cases I have not yet tagged: "The present king of France is either bald or not bald" and "It is neither true nor false that Jones is tall."
