I am taking a seminar on inverse reinforcement learning and its use in modelling human preferences, and an argument I built for a presentation has come to worry me. It seems to show that the entire enterprise is empty—which cannot be right if the field is taken as seriously as it is. I can no longer see where the chain breaks, so I want each step stress-tested.

(1) The task of inverse reinforcement learning is to infer an agent's reward function—roughly, its preferences—from observations of its behaviour.

(2) The inverse problem is underdetermined: for any finite set of observed behaviour, many different reward functions, plausibly infinitely many, fit the observations equally well, and if no constraints are placed on the form of the reward, a degenerate reward function can normally be found that rationalises almost any demonstration.

(3) It follows that no body of behavioural evidence can disconfirm, or even favour, one proposed reward function over another. Whatever the agent does, the data can be accommodated by some reward function, so every candidate is at best one among countless equally consistent alternatives.

(4) A method that cannot discriminate among hypotheses on any possible evidence is empty as an empirical method. It may encode prior assumptions, but it cannot be said to recover preferences from behaviour.

(5) Therefore inverse reinforcement learning cannot, even in principle, recover an agent's preferences, and its apparent successes are illusory.

(6) Yet inverse reinforcement learning is a serious research programme, used in robotics and in AI safety precisely to infer human values and preferences from demonstrations.

(7) So either one of these claims is false, or a substantial field rests on a confusion.

My loyalties are with (1) and (6), and I am reluctant to accept (5). Which step should be rejected, and why did it look so natural? If the argument is valid after all, which premise is least costly to give up, and what does a defender of inverse reinforcement learning have to assume in order to block the conclusion?
