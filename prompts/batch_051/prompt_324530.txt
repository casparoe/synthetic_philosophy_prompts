I keep going back and forth on a fight I've been having with a colleague about whether ordinary modal talk is secretly probabilistic. Her view is that when people say an outcome could happen, they're not making a categorical claim that there's a world where it does; they're saying the outcome clears some contextually-set probability threshold. My hunch is that could/might/possible are genuinely categorical — a kind of existential claim over whatever possibilities the context makes salient — and that probability is something we impose later, maybe as a rough gloss. She says this is testable: hold the stated probability of an outcome fixed across vignettes and vary only what's at stake, or vary the severity of the outcome, and see whether people are less willing to say 'it could happen' when the stakes go up. If willingness shifts with stakes at a fixed probability, that's evidence for a threshold; if it doesn't, the categorical picture wins. I'm not so sure a clean result would settle much.
