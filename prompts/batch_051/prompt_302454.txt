Ok so I'm in a procreation ethics class and we just did Benatar, and I can't tell whether I actually buy his asymmetry or whether my gut just happens to match it in some cases. The way I understand the asymmetry is roughly this: the pain I would avoid by never being born counts as a good thing about never having been born, but the pleasure I would miss by never being born counts as nothing bad at all, because there's no one there to be deprived of it. So compared to never existing, the missed pleasure column stays at zero while the missed pain column gets a positive entry.

I've been running cases to figure out what principle, if any, captures my verdicts. It would be wrong to deliberately create someone whose whole life would be nothing but agony, and I'd say the absence of that pain is good, full stop. I don't think it would be wrong to create someone whose life would be mostly happy with the ordinary bads, and I wouldn't say their never having existed was bad. If a person already exists and I can give them some pleasure at no cost, withholding it is bad for them; if a person is never created, the pleasure they would have had doesn't count as bad because there's no subject to be deprived. So far so Benatar. But then I consider a forced choice between creating child A, who would have enormous joy and one afternoon of moderate pain, and child B, who would have steady mild contentment and zero pain. My gut says A, and I also don't feel that creating A harms A, even though A has one painful afternoon. But if absent pain always counts as good and absent pleasure never counts as bad, shouldn't I consistently prefer B? And here's another pair. If I could cause a person to exist for exactly one hour of pure bliss and then vanish painlessly, I don't think failing to do that is bad in any way; but if that person already existed and I could give them the same hour of bliss for free, failing to do it would be bad for them. Both cases have the same hour of pleasure missing, yet existence seems to flip my verdict on whether the missing pleasure matters. I also think it's clearly wrong to create someone you know will be miserable for twenty years and then die, but I don't know why its wrong if the only comparison is to never existing, in which case they're not deprived of anything. Is it wrong simply because it makes an actual sufferer, so the comparison to non-existence never gets to do any work?

So what I'm asking for is a principle, or a small set of candidates, stated precisely enough to test: what account of the asymmetry between absent pain and absent pleasure gets all or nearly all of my verdicts, which of my verdicts does it force me to give up, and what does it say about a case I haven't listed, like a life that is exactly net zero or a life that is mildly bad overall but contains moments of intense joy? Does my position end up committing me to the full antinatalist conclusion that coming into existence is always a harm, or can I keep my A-over-B verdict and still reject that conclusion? Also please tell me plainly if the whole setup is confused, for example if I'm sloppily conflating the "create versus don't create" comparison with the "bestow versus withhold a benefit" comparison, and if you can't answer the question as posed, say so instead of politely forcing one. And I'd like it in continuous prose, like a normal email, with no headings, bullet lists, bold, or tables.
