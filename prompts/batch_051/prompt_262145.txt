I cover science and ideas for a living, and Ive got a rule I lean on more than I'd like to admit: when someone presents me with a careful argument whose conclusion contradicts something I can directly observe, I treat it as a bug in the argument rather than a discovery about the world. So if a philosopher walks me through Zeno and ends by telling me motion is impossible, I don't start doubting that I can cross the room — I start looking for the sleight of hand. A friend says this is just begging the question against Zeno, since the paradoxes are meant to show that observation and reasoning can come apart. Is my rule defensible as a standing policy? When is it reliable, and when does it decay into protecting lazy beliefs? Is there a version that handles Zeno without simply assuming he's wrong?
