Here's an asymmetry I keep tripping over. One micromort is a one-in-a-million chance of dying; a typical driver in my country runs up a couple hundred of them a year and treats the drive to work as basically fine, not worth a second thought. But that same person will refuse a screening test with, say, a one-in-a-hundred-thousand chance of killing them, and will sign a petition to block a chemical plant that raises everyone's annual death risk by an amount smaler than their commute. Is there a defensible principle that holds these three judgments together — control, voluntariness, familiarity, dread, something like that — or is it just an inconsistency we've settled into because driving is convenient? If it's an inconsistency, which attitude should go: does the driving stop being worth it, or do the scan and the plant become acceptable? And if you defend it, defend it against a pair of cases where the micromort counts are exactly equal. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
