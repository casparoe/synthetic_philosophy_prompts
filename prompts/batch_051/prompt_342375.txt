My friend and I argued for hours last night about whether the Trinity is logically coherent, and he kept saying that any way you slice it you end up with either tritheism or modalism. I said he was missing an option, but afterward I tried to write down my option and it came out looking like seven separate claims that might not all fit together. I'd like a consistency check before I bring it back to him. Here is where I landed: (1) The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are each fully God. (2) There is exactly one God, not three. (3) The three persons are numerically distinct; the Father is not the Son, and so on. (4) Identity is absolute — I don't buy relative identity or sortal-relative identity, it feels like changing the subject. (5) In a sentence like 'The Son is God,' the phrase 'is God' works as a kind-term or predicate, not as a strict identity claim, so you can't just run transitivity on it. (6) God has no parts, and the three persons don't together compose God the way boards compose a fence. (7) What distinguishes the persons is only their relations of origin — begetting, being begotten, proceeding — not anything in their essence. I want to know whether this package hangs together: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and what would have to give. Please order the genuine tensions by severity and, for each, say what the least costly revision would be. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
