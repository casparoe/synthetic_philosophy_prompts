I've been in a course on the philosophy of AI, and the debate that keeps coming up in my group is whether we should build systems as tools or as agents — and whether it even makes sense to draw the line. We keep bringing it down to cases we can actually run today: if you wire an LLM into a loop with a search tool, some memory, and the ability to send emails or spend money, is that a tool or an agent? One friend says its obviously just a tool, since it has no goals of its own and everything it does is downstream of the prompt. Another says once the thing is selecting subgoals, choosing among actions, and replanning over a long horizon, calling it a tool is just a refusal to look at what it does. Neither can say what would settle it, and I realize I can't either.

So: suppose someone makes the specific claim that such a system — say, a personal assistant AI that can book flights, draft and send messages, and make purchases — is a tool and not an agent. How would you test that? Design the strongest test or battery of observations you can: what you'd set up, what you'd measure, what results would favor each reading, and then be precise about what a positive result would and would not establish. Also say whether "agent" and "tool" can survive being operationalized in this way at all, or whether a clean test would force us to replace them with something narrower.
