A regional association of crane and scaffolding contractors is stuck in a familiar multipolar trap: every firm underbids the next by quietly thinning its inspection budget, and any firm that unilaterally spent more on safety would simply lose the contract. The association, which can expel members but has no legal enforcement power, proposes the following rule. Whenever a member wins a contract with a bid at least 20 percent below the next-lowest member's bid, it must pay the gap into escrow. The escrow is released to the winner only after an independent post-project safety audit comes back clean; if the audit finds inspection shortcuts, the entire escrow is paid to the second-lowest bidder as a bounty, with the auditor paid by the association. Walk through what this does to bidding behavior, to the strategic relationship between the two lowest bidders, and to who ultimately ends up holding the escrow money. Then identify the first way this gets gamed, and explain whether that failure mode can be patched without turning the rule into a dead letter.
