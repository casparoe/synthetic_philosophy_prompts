Read Steven Spielberg's Minority Report through the lens of futarchy: the proposal that a legislature should set a measurable welfare goal and prediction markets should then choose whichever policies are expected to meet it, so that society votes on values but bets on beliefs. In the film, PreCrime centralizes prediction in three precogs, treats their convergence as a basis for coercive punishment, and suppresses dissenting minority reports; the plot turns on whether the chief of PreCrime, once predicted to murder a stranger, is fated to do it or makes the prediction come true by acting on it. Is the film best read as a warning about prediction-based governance, or does it dramatize the opposite of futarchy: secretive, monopolized, unaudited prediction rather than an open market aggregating divergent bets? I would like a reading faithful in both directions, one that says precisely where the film's anxiety transfers to prediction markets and where a defender of futarchy would object that the analogy misdescribes them. Give particular attention to how each system treats disagreement among predictors, the reflexivity of acting on one's own prediction, and the difference between selecting a policy and convicting a person. End by saying what the film forces a futarchist to answer that the formal proposal alone might not.
