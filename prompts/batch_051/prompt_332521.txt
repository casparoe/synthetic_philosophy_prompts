I'm a data scientist at a health system, and I prep evidence reviews for our formulary committee about once a week. The committee is mostly clinicians, they sign off on whatever I put in front of them, and the calls decide whether thousands of patients can get a treatment without prior authorization. I keep running into trials where the overall result and the subgroup results point in opposite directions — Simpson's paradox territory — and right now each analyst handles it by feel. I want a written procedure I can actually run in about half a day, often with just the summary tables from a paper, no raw data. The procedure should lay out the questions to ask in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, where I stop with "the data can't settle this," and where I hand off to the committee because it's a values call rather than a statistical one. Flag the steps that are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. Then run it on three made-up cases: (1) a new drug that looks better overall but worse within every severity subgroup; (2) a subgroup effect that reverses the aggregate result but is from a tiny, post-hoc slice with no pre-registration; (3) a reversal that depends on whether you adjust for a variable that could be either a confounder or part of how the treatment works. I want at least one application to give an answer the committee won't like, and I want you to say plainly what the procedure cannot do.
