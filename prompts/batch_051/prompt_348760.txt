After a long argument with a friend about whether the Classic Maya "collapsed," I noticed our disagreement came down to an asymmetry neither of us could justify. We call it a collapse when palaces, inscriptions, and royal courts disappear, but we don't call it a collapse when a whole way of life ends and the people largely continue—like the end of the Edo period, which killed off an entire ruling class's world but gets called modernization. Even with Rome we argue about "fall" vs. transformation, but with the Classic Maya almost nobody hesitates to say collapse, even though there are still millions of Maya people. So our "collapse" judgments seem to track the fate of elites and monuments rather then the fate of people. Is there a principle that justifies that asymmetry, or is "collapse" just the word we use when the upper class loses its palaces? Test whatever you come up with on Easter Island and the Bronze Age, because those are the two cases my friend kept throwing at me.
