I have been trying to get clear about what it means for an individual to be complicit in a collective harm. People apply the word without hesitation—to the person who eats factory-farmed meat, to the frequent flyer, to the citizen who votes for a destructive policy—but I cannot tell what condition the word is actually tracking.

What I want is a working definition of "complicit in a collective harm": one that gets the clear cases right, forces a definite answer on the borderline cases, and explains why the label so often feels like a matter of degree.

Here are the cases I have been testing it against.

A factory-farm owner who removes animal-welfare protections purely to save money. I would call this clearly complicit.

A person who buys and eats factory-farmed meat every day, knowing how the animals are treated. My instinct is yes, but no single purchase keeps the system going.

A person who takes one vacation flight a year. The plane would fly without them, and their seat is a negligible part of the flight's emissions. I cannot settle this one.

A voter who supports a candidate in a landslide; the candidate wins and goes on to do serious harm. The vote changed nothing, yet it feels like signing on.

A non-voter in a safe district who stays home and watches that same candidate win. Is that complicity by omission?

A truck driver who transports crude oil for a living, hates the industry, but needs the wage and has no realistic alternative in the local economy.

A person who buys a cheap shirt, suspects the supply chain may involve exploitation, and deliberately does not check.

A person who uses coal-heavy grid electricity to run a medical ventilator. I would say clearly not complicit, but I need the definition to explain why.

Someone who spends years publicly persuading others that individual lifestyle changes are pointless, convincing thousands of people to give up trying. They act through other people and never touch the direct harm themselves.

Is "complicit" fundamentally a causal idea, a moral idea, or an unstable mixture of both? Is there a principled difference between the vacation flight and the ventilator, or only a difference in how willing I am to apply the word? And if complicity comes in degrees, what exactly is being graded—the size of a causal contribution, the availability of alternatives, the closeness to the harm, or something else?
