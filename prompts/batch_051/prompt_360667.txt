Take Descartes's evil demon and upgrade it a bit. The textbook demon can decieve you about the external world and maybe about mathematics, but it can't touch your awareness of your own thinking, wich is why the cogito is supposed to survive. Now suppose the demon can also fiddle with the occurrent stream itself: it can inject thoughts that feel exactly as much yours as any thought you've ever had, it can delete a memory here or there, or make a thought you're currently having feel as though it was planted from outside. It can even make the proposition 'I am now thinking' feel luminously certain while the thought is, in fact, one it dropped into you. Work through precisely what this does to the move from thinking to existing. Does 'there is thinking going on' survive even if 'I am the thinker' does not? Can the demon make you falsely believe you are thinking, given that a false belief is itself a thought? Once the demon can plausibly own some of the thoughts in the stream, is there any way to hold off the slide from Descartes's I to Lichtenberg's point that the data only license saying thinking is occurring? And what, if anything, would Descartes have to establish next to rebuild a self out of that? Be precise about what the cogito actually proves in the upgraded scenario, and whether it now proves only the existence of thought-events rather than a thinking substance.
