Take the China-brain thought experiment usually credited to Ned Block: the population of a large country is given two-way radios and each person is assigned the role of a single neuron, so that the whole population implements the functional organization of a human brain for an hour. Block's target is the intuition that such a system could be behaviorally identical to a conscious person without there being anything it is like to be the system as a whole.

Now vary it as follows. Replace the billion radio-linked agents with a single-threaded program running on one ordinary computer. The program visits the simulated units in a fixed, arbitrary order, applies exactly the same state-update rules, and preserves exactly the same dependency graph as the original functional description. The only change is that no signal travels directly from one simulated unit to another: each unit's output is written to the machine's memory, and whenever another unit's update depends on it, that value is read from memory at the scheduled step. The whole run proceeds much slower than real time. Feed this system the input patterns a human brain would receive from a body in severe pain. Over a sufficiently long run it emits the outputs such a person would emit, including fluent reports of pain, requests to stop, and later accurate descriptions of the episode.

Assess how the shift from radio-linked agents to a single serial scheduler affects the case. Specifically: (1) What should a computational functionalist say about whether there is anything it is like to be each version of the system, and is there any functional difference between the two versions that should matter? (2) What should a biological chauvinist say about the two versions, and does the serial version strengthen or weaken that position? (3) What does integrated information theory suggest about the two versions, given that on its usual reading the causal structure of the physical substrate matters and not only the abstract computation? (4) Suppose the system's reports of suffering are produced by a general capacity to report, reason about, and later remember the simulated state. Is there a determinate fact about whether shutting the simulation down harms anyone, and if so, what makes it determinate? (5) If your own reaction to the original China brain shifts when you consider the serial version, try to identify the feature doing the work: could it serve as a general criterion for drawing the boundary of sentience, or is it an artifact of the example?

Throughout, say how confident you are in your attributions of these views and in your main claims, and explicitly single out the point you are least sure of.
