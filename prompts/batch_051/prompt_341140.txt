In my high school philosophy club we have been working through some classical theism, and I have become stuck on an argument against divine simplicity that I cannot quite shake. As I understand the doctrine, it says that God has no parts, that God is identical with God's own existence and nature, and that God is identical with each of God's attributes. Here is the objection I keep going back to:

(1) If God is identical with each of God's attributes, and God is both just and merciful, then God is identical with justice and identical with mercy.
(2) Identity is transitive: if A is identical with B and A is identical with C, then B is identical with C.
(3) Therefore, justice is identical with mercy.
(4) But justice and mercy cannot be identical, because justice is giving someone what they are owed, whereas mercy involves not insisting on what is owed.
(5) Therefore, divine simplicity leads to an unacceptable consequence and should be rejected.

I suspect the problem is somewhere around the phrase "identical with an attribute," but I have not been able to say exactly where the argument goes wrong. Take a definite position on it: decide whether the argument is valid, identify precisely which step, if any, fails, and explain how a defender of divine simplicity should restate or qualify the doctrine to escape the problem. If you think the argument really is valid and the conclusion, however strange, must be accepted, say so plainly and defend it.
