I keep noticing a move in arguments that I don't have a clean name for. Someone says, more or less: if you keep holding that view, I'll be hurt — I won't sleep, I'll feel unsafe, I'll spiral. Then the forecast of harm gets treated as a reason for the other person to change their mind. It's not a threat, because the predicted harm lands on the speaker, not the listener. It also doesn't feel like plain guilt-tripping, because the person often seems sincere, as if they're just honestly reporting how the disagreement will affect them. But the effect is that continuing the argument starts to look like cruelty no matter how gently you argue. Is there an established term for this? And what separates it from legitimate persuasion — can an honest report that someone's view will harm you ever function as a reason for them to change their mind without being manipulative? If it is manipulative, is that because of intent, because the prediction is self-serving or unfalsifiable, because of a power imbalance, or because of something in the structure of the move itself? A bare label won't help me much; I'm after the boundaries.
