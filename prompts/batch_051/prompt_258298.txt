Write the joint statement that a committed defender of the conjugal view of marriage and a committed defender of the relationship view of marriage could both sign after a good-faith adverserial collaboration, where the deliverable is the document that comes out of the collaboration rather than a recap of the debate. By conjugal view I mean the position that marriage is a comprehensive union of mind and body, inherently ordered toward procreation and family life, which is why exclusivity, permanence, and sexual complementarity belong to marriage. By relationship view I mean the position that marriage is a chosen public commitment to share a life and care for one another, so gender is irrelevant, procreation is optional, and the spouses get to set the terms.

I want the agreed-claims section to include real, substantive claims, not "both sides agree to disagree respectfully." In particular, the two of them should manage to jointly sign claims about: what the dispute is not (it is not about whether same-sex couples can love each other or be good parents, and not about anyone's basic dignity); why the state has any interest in recognizing and regulating marriage rather than leaving it to private contract; the fact that each side faces a hard internal problem (the conjugal view has to explain why an infertile man-woman couple is married without sneaking in a per-couple fertility test; the relationship view has to explain why marriage is not just close friendship or a sibling caregiving arrangement with a license). Include a claim about whether the historical legal doctrine of consummation is a fact the two sides can state jointly even if they disagree about what it shows.

Then give a disagreements table. Make sure each side would accept the wording of its own formulation, and each side's view is phrased as a claim about what marriage is for or what its good consists in, not as an insult or a policy preference. The rows should cover at least these cruxes, with the case or argument both sides accept as bearing on the crux and each side's prediction recorded in advance:
- the "body" crux: whether the biological/reproductive-type act is internal to the good of marriage or an incidental backdrop to an otherwise personal union;
- the platonic co-parents case: two committed friends or siblings raising a child together who want marriage — does each view classify them as marriage, and does each view treat that conclusion as fine or as a problem;
- the polyamory pressure test: three or more adults in a shared, committed caregiving relationship — what does each view's logic say, and does each side bite that bullet or draw a line somewhere;
- the institution-change claim: whether changing the legal definition from the conjugal one to the relationship one would predictably loosen the norms of permanence and exclusivity, with each side stating their prediction and what they'd count as confirming evidence.
