i'm a novelist, three chapters into a book set in a small english town whose identity runs through one of its war memorials — a Dunkirk veteran — and i keep fumbling the gap between what the evacuation actually was and the story the town tells about it. i want a sonnet to use as the book's epigraph: fourteen lines, volta after the octave, closed rhyme scheme. the octave should be the thing itself — the encirclement, the perimeter shrinking, men waiting on the beach, abandoned equipment. the volta should be the moment the camera pulls back and you hear the story being told: the little boats, the Dunkirk spirit, a retreat turning into proof of national character. the closing couplet should say what the myth has to leave out to keep that version upright — the part the town never says out loud. i need it accurate enough that someone who knows the actual history of Dunkirk and the way it was remembered afterwards nods along, but it also has to land for a reader who just thinks theyre reading a novel. modern english is fine, i dont need period voice.
