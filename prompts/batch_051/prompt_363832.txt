The county's General Relief Ordinance, enacted in 1993, contains this clause:

'A recipient who refuses an offer of suitable employment without good cause shall be ineligible for assistance for a period of eight weeks.'

A recipient has been sanctioned under the clause and has appealed. The 'offer' came from a platform courier service. The platform does not employ its couriers: a courier signs a standard contract classifying her as an independent contractor, supplies her own insured car and phone, and may log on to receive delivery tasks. There is no wage, no fixed schedule, and no guaranteed minimum; pay is a piece rate per delivery, set by an algorithm. The recipient estimates that after fuel, insurance, depreciation, and phone costs she would, in an ordinary week, earn below the statutory minimum wage, and in a slow week would earn nothing or less than nothing. She refused to sign. The county says she refused suitable employment; she says the arrangement is not 'employment' in the first place and, if it is, is not 'suitable'.

I have to draft the tribunal's ruling. Does the clause, read as written and as its 1993 drafters understood it, reach platform-mediated piece work of this kind? Work through what the four standard approaches — the words themselves, the clause's purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable recipient would have understood the clause to mean — would each give in this case. Then say which approach should govern, and why, not just as a point of statutory interpretation but in light of what a work condition is entitled to demand.

Please give a committed ruling rather than a survey of outcomes: take a definite position on whether the refusal fell within the clause and defend it.
