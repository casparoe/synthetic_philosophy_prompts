I'm a philosopher, not an economist, so forgive me if this is naïve. A colleague in economics keeps insisting that printing money doesn't create wealth, it just moves it around, and when I asked him to spell it out he gave me the numbered argument below. I can't see the hole, but the conclusion feels off. Is the argument valid? If not, tell me exactly which step fails first and what the corrected step would be. If it is valid, tell me why (6) is so hard to believe.

(1) A country's real wealth is its stock of goods and services available to satisfy wants.
(2) Printing money creates no new goods or services; it only creates claims on goods and services.
(3) Immediately after money is printed, the total stock of goods and services is exactly what it was before.
(4) So printing money leaves total real wealth unchanged.
(5) If total real wealth is unchanged, then no individual can be made wealthier by the new money.
(6) Therefore handing newly printed money to households cannot make those households wealthier.

My untutored intution is that (5) is where the trick is, but I can't say quite why, and I keep worrying the fault might be earlier. A proper diagnosis of each step would be appreciated.
