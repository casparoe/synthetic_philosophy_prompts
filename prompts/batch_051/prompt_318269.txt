My friend and I spent a long night arguing about self-sufficiency, and I walked away stuck on an asymmetry neither of us could justify. Consider two cases. A neighbor, call her Dana, stops buying vegetables, fixes her own truck, won't accept a ride or a loan, and takes real pride in owing nobody; we think she's a bit rigid, maybe, but the ideal itself — self-reliance, independence, not burdening others — strikes us as admirable. Now consider a country that announces exactly the same policy at national scale: tariffs and subsidies so it produces everything at home, refuses to depend on foreign suppliers, treats dependence on other countries as a humiliation. Suddenly the same ideal gets a different name — autarky — and we hear it as an indictment: backward, paranoid, maybe even a little sinister. Same principle, adjacent scales, opposite verdicts.

My friend's whole answer was "scale changes everything, stop expecting everything to be principled," but I don't see why that should satisfy anyone. If depending on other people is a defect, then a whole society of non-dependents should be the purest expression of the virtue, not the most pathological case. The candidate justifications I can come up with all seem to collapse: it's coercion (the state imposes self-sufficiency on citizens who didn't choose it)? But we admire off-grid parents who raise kids that way too. It's feasibility (a country literally can't be autarkic)? Dana's self-sufficiency isn't total either — she didn't mine the metal in her truck. It's that the country harms outsiders by withdrawing from trade? Fine, but then we're just doing consequences, not valuing self-reliance at all, and we should say so.

So here's what I want. Find me the strongest principle that yields both verdicts — individual self-sufficiency is admirable, national autarky is not — and then test it against the hard cases. If there's no such principle, say that plainly, and tell me which judgment I should give up and what I should replace it with. I'd rather know wether the asymmetry is defensible than keep it just because it feels natural.
