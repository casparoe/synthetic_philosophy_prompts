Could you write me a parody, about 500 words, in the form of a deadpan university press release or one of those dry "strange findings" roundups from a skeptical science blog? The subject is a researcher who sets out to study why scientific discoveries keep getting named after the wrong person. You should lean on the real background that this pattern already has a name: Stigler's law is the idea that no scientific discovery gets named after its actual discoverer, and Stigler credited the idea to the sociologist Merton, so the law is its own best example of what its describing. The target of the parody is the practice of casually attaching people's names to discoveries without checking who actually did the work, plus the way even the diagnosis of that practice keeps getting miscredited.

Make the running joke recursive: every time the researcher thinks he has found a new law about eponymy — a second-order law saying laws about eponymy will themselves be misattributed, or a rule that credit migrates toward whoever is already famous, or some proposed fix for the whole mess — he finds out someone else already proposed it, usually under somebody else's name. Keep the tone deadpan the whole way through: the researcher should stay genuinely fascinated and a little proud of each new law, never winking at the reader or stopping to announce how ironic it all is. By the end a reader should have laughed but also felt the real point — that in a system where credit is systematically misassigned, even the diagnosis of the problem can't get credited correctly. A fictional researcher and department are fine; don't invent specific facts about real living scientists, and since it is a press release, skip footnotes or a reference list.
