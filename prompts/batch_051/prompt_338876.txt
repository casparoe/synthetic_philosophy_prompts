I am a PhD student in cognitive science working on representational format, and I keep returning to a parallel I cannot fully adjudicate. It concerns the mental imagery debate. On one side, Kosslyn argues that imagery relies on depictive representations: representations that exploit a spatial medium and preserve metric properties, so that features such as distance and orientation are directly encoded in the format itself rather than computed from something more language-like. On the other side, Pylyshyn argues that what looks like inspecting a picture is really the manipulation of propositional, symbolic descriptions, and that the apparent spatial character of imagery is explained by tacit knowledge and cognitive penetrability rather than by the intrinsic geometry of the underlying representation.

The parallel is with the distinction between analog and digital computation. An analog computer represents quantities by continuously varying physical magnitudes, so that operations on the representations transform those quantities in virtue of their continuous structure. A digital computer represents quantities as discrete symbols manipulated by rules, with no intrinsic resemblance between a symbol's physical form and what it denotes. The analogy seems to map depictive representations onto analog computation and propositional representations onto digital, symbol-manipulating computation. My computer science colleagues find this mapping obvious; my philosophy supervisors think it papers over the key semantic question, since analog and digital concern implementation levels while the imagery debate concerns functional level format, and since a digital system can implement a depictive representation, as with a bitmap, while an analog system can encode symbols purely by convention.

I would like to know whether this parallel is deep or superficial. If it is deep, what exactly follows: would results about the limitations of analog computation transfer to depictive imagery, and would Pylyshyn's arguments become a special case of arguments for symbol-based architectures? If it is superficial, where precisely does the mapping break, and is there a better, more illuminating analogy from computer science for what is really at stake between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn? In your answer, please state the strongest version of the mapping, identify what would have to be true of both sides for lessons to transfer from one domain to the other, and name at least one specific feature the analogy would predict that one of the two parties in the imagery debate would reject.
