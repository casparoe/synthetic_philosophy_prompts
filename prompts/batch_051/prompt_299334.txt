I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and after a couple years reading around the physicalism literature I've gotten stuck on a meta-question about the debate itself. The physicalist/anti-physicalist fight seems to hinge almost entirely on what 'physical' means, but the argument over that defintion has been running in place for decades. Hempel's dilemma is the usual way of stating the bind: if 'physical' means whatever current physics says, physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics is incomplete and is likely to be revised; if it means whatever a future completed physics says, then physicalism either becomes trivially true (whatever exists is whatever that physics describes) or becomes a placeholder claim nobody can evaluate until that physics arrives. People have tried to escape by defining the physical as what a future physics fundamentally posits, or negatively as the non-mental, or by retreating to causal closure of the physical rather than a definition, but none of these has won, and the debate just keeps cycling.

My working theory is that the real disagreement isn't about any of those proposals—it's about metasemantics, i.e., how the term 'physical' gets its reference in the first place. Some people talk as if it's a natural kind term whose reference is fixed by the actual stuff in the world; others talk as if it's a placeholder for whatever an ideal science converges on, like a description with a gap waiting to be filled. If that's right, the two sides aren't disagreeing about the world at all; they're running different theories of meaning under one word, and no amount of physics would settle it. But I'm not confident about that, because their might be a substantive dispute underneath: over whether future physics could in principle include phenomenal properties, or over whether being exhaustively characterizable in structural, mathematical terms is enough to count as physical.

What I want is a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is the physicalism debate a verbal dispute about the word 'physical,' a substantive dispute that's been misdescribed as verbal, or an ill-posed question that can't move until the metasemantics gets fixed? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like—a definition that sticks, a distinction the literature hasn't drawn, or an argument that we should stop expecting progress here and put the energy somewhere else?
