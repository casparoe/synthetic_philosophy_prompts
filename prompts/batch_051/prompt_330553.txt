Consider the following version of Quinn's self-torturer, stated so that no outside text is needed. A person is fitted with a device whose dial has 1,001 settings, numbered 0 through 1,000. Setting 0 delivers no electric shock, setting 1,000 delivers excruciating pain, and adjacent settings differ by an increment of shock too small for the person to introspectively distinguish setting n from setting n+1 even when attending carefully. The person begins at setting 0. Each week they are offered a one-time, irreversible choice: advance one step in exchange for $10,000, or stop permanently and keep whatever has accumulated. The full menu, including the final setting and the total payment for completing the sequence, is known in advance.

Now consider the following conjunction as the target claim: every adjacent advance is rationally permissible, and yet the completed sequence of advances is rationally impermissible. In other words, practical rationality permits each local step while condemning the course of action those steps jointly compose.

I want a number rather than a survey of possible views: your actual credence in that conjunction. Decompose the number into at least the following four terms, assigning a rough probability and a one- or two-sentence justification to each.

First, how much rides on whether the basic unit of rational evaluation is the local choice or the extended course of action — that is, on whether permissibility is closed under stepwise composition.

Second, how much rides on whether rational preference is required to be transitive, so that a pattern of preference assembled from local pairwise comparisons and a global comparison could not be coherently held at a single time.

Third, how much rides on whether phenomenal indiscriminability creates a genuine normative threshold, such that an agent cannot be rationally required to discriminate differences they cannot feel — as opposed to an objective difference between adjacent settings grounding a reason to stop even when it is imperceptible.

Fourth, how much rides on whether the later preference for setting 0 over setting 1,000, revealed by the willingness at the end to pay far more than the accumulated winnings to go back, has authority over the earlier local choices — rather than being a mere change of evaluative standpoint with no retroactive force.

Then explain how the terms combine into your single credence, state which term that credence is most sensitive to, and say what argument or evidence would move that term most. Finally, say whether your credence in the conjunction would change if the person could costlessly precommit at the outset to a stopping point, and what that difference, if any, reveals about the kind of rational requirement at stake.
