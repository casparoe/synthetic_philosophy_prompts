I'm giving a talk at a mixed workshop of political philosophers and AI alignment people. My thesis is that the alignment target should be a procedure, not a single value function: there is no neutral way to aggregate everyone's values or preferences, so any fixed reward target quietly privileges one comprehensive view of the good. Instead I argue the system should treat contested moral tradeoffs as outside its competence and defer to the outcome of a specified political or legal procedure, even when the designers think the outcome is morally wrong. The philosophers work on public reason and value pluralism, so they'll worry I'm dressing up majority or status-quo bias as neutrality. The alignment people mostly build reward models, so they'll say deference to a procedure isn't a training signal and that even the interface design is a moral choice. Give me a prep sheet: the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come, phrased as those two groups would put them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and the weakest part of my own position I need to defend rather than hope they miss.
