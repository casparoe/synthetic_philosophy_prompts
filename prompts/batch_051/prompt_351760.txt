I'm a software engineer who works on LLM evaluation, and I've been asked to write internal guidance on how we should classify model outputs that assert false things. Before I take my commitments to the team, I want a consistency check. I hold:

(1) Frankfurt's distinction seems right to me: lying requires an intention to deceive, whereas bullshit is a matter of not caring whether what one says is true.
(2) LLM hallucinations are not lies, because a language model has no beliefs or intentions and therefore cannot intend to deceive anyone.
(3) LLM hallucinations are not errors either, if we reserve the word error for a subject holding a false belief; a model does not hold beliefs.
(4) One can only literally bullshit if one is the kind of agent that could have cared about the truth; a model has no attitudes at all, so a model cannot literally be a bullshitter.
(5) At the level of the model, I think hallucination is best described as bullshit: the model is trained to maximize the plausibility of the next token rather than the truth of the completed answer, which amounts to a kind of structural indifference to whether its outputs are true.
(6) When a repeated misleading output traces to a decision to ship a model without adequate checking, I think the bullshit belongs to the organization that deployed the model rather than to the artifact.

Which of these are actually in tension, and which only seem to be? Please rank the tensions from most to least severe, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the cheapest fix would be. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set cohere.
