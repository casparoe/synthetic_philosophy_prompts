I've been teaching the ontology of music for years, and I've settled into roughly the view that a musical work is an abstract type—something like a sound-structure, or a bundle of normative requirements on performance—and that a live performance is a token of that type while a recording is a different kind of thing: a record of a particular token, or maybe a token of a distinct work (the recording as such). Lately I worry I've insulated the view with so many qualifications that its no longer clear what would count against it. So I'd like a map of its genuine exposure. Walk me through the cases and say which are merely awkward for the type/token picture and which would be fatal, and where a committed defender has a good reply as opposed to just straining. The cases I keep chewing on: purely electronic works that are record-first, with no prior performance to token; free improvisation where no two instances share much of a sound-structure; pop records where the work seems to be the production itself; works that are lost or never scored. For each, tell me whether I'm revising the view or abandoning it. And be straight with me: has the view left itself anything to be wrong about, and is that a bug or a feature?
