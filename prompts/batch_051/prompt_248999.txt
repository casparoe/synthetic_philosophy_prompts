Construct a matched pair of intuition pumps—thought experiments whose persuasive force comes substantially from narrative framing rather than from explicit argument—about a single agent's action. The two cases must stipulate exactly the same facts, yet be engineered through nothing but framing, the order in which information is disclosed, and the degree of concrete detail so that a careful reader judges the agent to have acted wrongly in the first telling and not wrongly in the second. Keep the scenario ordinary and realistic: no science fiction, demons, perfect predictors, or exotic technology, and do not stipulate either verdict. First state the facts common to both tellings; then give the two tellings; then show your work. For each telling, identify the specific pump features and the mechanism by which each one moves the judgment. Test the diagnosis by swapping a single pump feature between the two tellings and explaining how the verdicts drift. Close by saying what the pair forces a defender of the claim that such framing details are morally irrelevant to concede.
