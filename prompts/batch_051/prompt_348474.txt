I'm a second-year undergrad in an aesthetics class and we're on philosophy of music. We've been on the problem of how purely instrumental music can be sad, and I feel like every explanation I meet is basically one of these four, with different window dressing:

1. The resemblance view: the music shares acoustic features with sad human expressive behavior, like slow tempo, low register, and falling contours, so we read the emotion off the sound.
2. The persona or imagination view: we imagine a musical agent or virtual person behind the unfolding structure, and that agent is expressing sadness.
3. The contagion or infection view: we don't hear the music as sad itself; we catch sadness from it the way we catch a mood from another person's posture or voice.
4. Hanslick's dynamic analogy, which we read in his On the Musically Beautiful. He says roughly that music cannot depict the actual feeling of sadness, only its dynamic properties, like motion, acceleration, hesitation, and intensity, and our feeling follows that abstract pattern.

I'm not asking for a fifth popular theory if it is just one of those four in disguise. I want a genuinely different account of how, or how far, instrumental music can be sad, one that is not on that list and not a special case or combination of anything on it. Then give an honest assessment: is it actually a rival to these four, or does it just redescribe one of them? I'd also be happy if the right answer is that there is no fifth, and why the list is exhaustive, but only if you can say why it is exhaustive.
