I’m drafting a paper on whether Western anti-corruption norms can be applied across cultural contexts, and I keep stalling on the foundational question: what exactly is corruption? The working notion I inherited—something like the misuse of an entrusted position for private benefit—seems to break down precisely in the cases my paper cares about, so I want a definition that tracks the boundary between corruption and neighboring practices (gift exchange, clientelism, lobbying, ordinary favors among friends) rather than one that only matches intuition in easy cases.

Here are the cases I need the definition to sort. (a) A judge takes an envelope of cash to rule in favor of the payer. (b) A minister awards a no-bid contract to a company owned by her sister at an inflated price. (c) A ward organizer for a political machine helps a newly naturalized family get a city job and expedites a housing form, with the quiet understanding that they will turn out for the machine on election day. (d) In a society where it is customary, a businessperson gives an official an expensive holiday gift while a license application is pending, and would give the same gift regardless of the application. (e) A corporation donates heavily to a candidate who, once elected, votes for a tax break the corporation had lobbied for; no explicit exchange was ever discussed. (f) A traveler pays a small tip to a customs officer to avoid a queue that would otherwise take hours. (g) A voter accepts a pen printed with a candidate’s name at a rally. (h) A former head of a regulatory agency accepts a high-paying board seat at a firm she used to regulate. (i) A person uses family connections, sometimes called wasta, to have a less qualified cousin hired for a public-sector job. (j) A friend gives another friend a generous birthday present in a relationship with no official position on either side. I take (a) and (b) to be clear cases of corruption and (g) and (j) to be clearly not; the difficulty lies in between.

What I’m looking for is a working definition of corruption. Please propose one, run it against each of the ten cases, say explicitly where it forces a verdict that conflicts with my likely intuitions or where it gives no answer at all, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I’d also like a brief note on which two or three cases are hardest for any definition of corruption, and on whether the cross-cultural worry—that Western standards misread customary gift exchange as bribery—should be built into the definition itself or handled separately as a question about justification and excuse.
