A physics channel I watch was trying to make many-worlds feel intuitive, and I worry the analogy they used is doing all the work while pretending not to. They said to picture the universe before a measurement as a tree trunk, and each possible outcome as a branch it splits into. Then they claimed the Born rule is just the fact that some branches are thicker than others: if the wavefunction says 90% spin-up, the spin-up branch is nine times thicker than the spin-down branch because that's how much "reality" is flowing into it. Can you lay out what maps onto what between the tree picture and many-worlds, and tell me where the mapping actually breaks down — like, are the branches really countable, do the splits happen at one definate time, and is the thickness something the wavefunction already had or something the analogy quietly adds? I especially want to know whether the conclusion the channel was pushing, that probability in many-worlds is no more mysterious than fractions of a tree, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the tree is mostly a bad analogy, what would a better one be?
