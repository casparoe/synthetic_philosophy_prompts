I am arranging the public question-and-answer segment that follows a colloquium paper by a visiting metaphysician. In the paper she defends a reformed conditional analysis of dispositions: an object is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S just in case it would M if it underwent S while retaining the causal basis of the disposition. She argues that this single amendment handles both finkish cases, in which the stimulus would remove the disposition itself, such as a live wire connected to a safety cut-out that would disconnect the current if touched, and masking cases, in which the disposition persists but an accompanying factor prevents its manifestation, such as a fragile glass protected by packaging that would stop it breaking if struck. When pressed on circularity, she tends to reply that the causal basis can be described in independent physical terms and that she is not offering a reductive analysis. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the position. Include at least one question on whether the fink–mask distinction earns its keep once the analysis is amended; at least one on whether the requirement that the causal basis be retained overgenerates, undergenerates, or simply relocates the explanatory burden; and at least one on how the account treats dispositions whose plausible basis is extrinsic, such as a key's disposition to open one particular lock. For each of these, add a follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and structure the sequence so that her circularity deflection cannot simply be replayed later in the discussion. Please write the questions in British English.
