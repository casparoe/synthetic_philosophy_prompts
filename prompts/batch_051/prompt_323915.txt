For years I have gone back and forth on a question that seems simple but apparently is not: whether dreams are experiences I actually have while I am asleep. I am not an academic, and I have mostly thought this through on my own and in online discussions, so I will describe the positions in plain terms.

One side says that when I dream of running from something, I am genuinely undergoing an experience during sleep: something is happening in my consciousness at 3 a.m., with images, fear, and a sense of motion, however different from ordinary waking perception. On this view, the report I give in the morning is a later memory of an earlier conscious episode, and like any memory it can be incomplete or distorted. The other side says that during sleep itself there is no experience of the dream at all. The whole thing is assembled at or just before waking, when the brain produces a vivid narrative and stamps it as a memory. On this view, saying I experienced last night's dream is like backdating a letter: the letter is real now, but nothing corresponding to it happened at the earlier time. The morning report is not a memory record; it is the dream, or rather the waking experience of having dreamt, misleadingly projected backward into the night.

What frustrates me is that both sides seem to agree on the observable facts. They agree that people woken from REM sleep usually report long, vivid dreams; that large parts of the brain are active during REM; that the dreamer cannot report anything until awake; and that dream memories fade very quickly unless they are retold or written down immediately. And yet neither side moves. One side says the waking report is evidence that an experience happened during sleep. The other side says the report cannot be evidence for anything except the waking state itself, because waking is when the report exists. At that point the argument usually dissolves into accusations that the other side is redefining the word experience.

I want to know why this debate has stalled and what kind of disagreement it really is. My working suspicion is that there is a factual question about when the neural events underlying the dream occur buried under a verbal fight about the word experience, but I am not sure. Please give me a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a verbal disagreement, a factual disagreement that has not been settled because the decisive observation is hard to imagine, a difference about what counts as evidence for a conscious episode, or two people answering different questions under one heading? Set out what each side is actually committed to at bottom, and if the fight is partly verbal, restate each position clearly enough that it stops trading on an ambiguity. Then say which parts of the dispute an observation could settle and which parts no observation could settle, and tell me what progress would look like given your diagnosis, or whether I should stop expecting the argument to move at all.
