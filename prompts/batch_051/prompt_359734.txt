Last month our seed round fell apart in about 48 hours. One lead investor emailed that they were out, two more went quiet by Friday, and our CEO kept saying everyone was just reading the room instead of our actual deck. A friend of mine who did econ in undergrad called it an information cascade, and I've definately gone down a rabbit hole trying to figure out if he's right or just using a buzzword.

The book chapter I read summarized it roughly like this: imagine people deciding one at a time, each with their own private information, and each can see what earlier people chose but not why they chose it. It can be perfectly rational to copy the earlier choices even when your own private information points the other way. Once a couple of people have chosen the same option, everyone after them rationally ignores their own signal and follows, so the group can lock onto an answer that most members privately disbelieve. The chapter also said an information cascade is basically conformity with better PR—people don't want to look stupid in front of the people who already moved—so the social story and the informational story come to the same thing. And it claimed that once a cascade gets going, the only thing that can stop it is new private information from someone who enters late. It finished by saying this is why bubbles inflate far past what any individual trader actually believes.

Is that a fair summary? I'd like to know which parts a game theorist would sign off on, which parts are subtly off, and wether the bubble conclusion actually follows from the accurate parts. And whether "information cascade" is the right diagnosis for what happened to our round, or just a flattering story we're telling ourselves.
