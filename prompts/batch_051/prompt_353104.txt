A teammate reminds me I've been awake for 30 hours, and that over the last ten incidents where I was this sleep-deprived, I picked the culprit with total confidence and got it wrong eight times. That seems like it has to matter, so I wrote the argument out:

(1) I should believe what my total evidence supports.
(2) My total evidence includes evidence about my own reliability, like that sleep-deprivation track record, not just the logs.
(3) So I should move my 90% down to something like 55%.
(4) But the logs on their own, read carefully, support 90%, and I can still see it.
(5) If I go to 55% while, by my own reading, the logs say 90%, then my belief and my take on the evidence disagree — that's epistemic akrasia, the thing everyone treats as irrational.
(6) So rationality tells me to trust the logs and not trust the logs.

The conclusion can't be right, so a step has to go. I'd most like to keep (1), since total evidence feels like the whole game. Which step breaks, and what do I pay if I reject it?
