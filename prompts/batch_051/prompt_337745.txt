i run an after-school maths club at a community centre, fifteen teenagers, and 45 minutes is what i get. there's a thing i want them to feel rather than hear me explain. i once read (allais? i dont trust the spelling) about two choices where people flip: someone will take a guaranteed $5 over an 89% chance of $5, a 10% chance of $25, and a 1% chance of nothing. but then the same person will take a 10% chance of $25 over an 11% chance of $5. both pairs are the same bet once you ignore the 89 percent piece that the two options in each pair share, but only the first pair has a sure thing sitting there, and that seems to do all the work. i want the kids to catch themselves doing exactly this, in their own choices, before anyone names it, and to leave seeing that the way the same numbers are packaged changes what they pick.

design me a game for those 45 minutes. tokens or points that become snacks at the end, no real money. i need the rules, the exact choices and the order to present them, a payoff scheme that makes the inconsistency show up on its own, and a debrief that maps what they did back to the shared piece and the pull of the sure thing. also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it and how to steer around it, and be honest about which part of the real effect a fake-stakes room full of teenagers cannot reproduce.
