I have been trying to pin down what we mean when we call something fragile, and the obvious definition keeps failing me in ways that matter for a course I am preparing on dispositions. The obvious definition is conditional: an object is fragile just in case, if it were struck or dropped in ordinary circumstances, it would shatter. That handles the easy cases, but I suspect it collapses on some contrived ones. I understand that the troublesome cases are sometimes called masked and finkish dispositions; I mention the labels only to locate the problem, since what I actually want is a definition, not a taxonomy of the literature.

Here are the cases I want your definition to classify. (1) A wine glass dropped one meter onto concrete shatters: clearly fragile. (2) A solid steel bearing dropped from the same height does not shatter: clearly not fragile. (3) A wine glass exactly like the one in (1) is wrapped in thick bubble wrap and then dropped; it does not shatter, but it seems to me the glass itself is still fragile, while the wrapped package is not. (4) A wine glass exactly like the one in (1) is fitted with a device that, the instant an impact begins, alters its structure so that it turns pliant and does not shatter; if you strike it, it will not shatter. I am inclined to call it fragile just before the first impact, but the conditional analysis would not. (5) A solid steel bearing is fitted with a device that, the instant an impact begins, transforms it into ordinary glass, so it does shatter when struck. I am inclined to say it is not fragile just before the first impact, even though the conditional analysis says it is. (6) An ordinary wine glass sits undisturbed in orbit, where no impact is likely; if it were struck with ordinary force at room temperature, it would shatter. I genuinely do not know whether to call it fragile while it sits there.

Propose a working definition of "fragile" — or of a more precise term such as "disposed to shatter when struck," if you think the ordinary word needs replacing — that gets (1) and (2) right, matches or explains away my instincts in (3) through (5), and gives a definite verdict for (6). Then run the definition through all six cases, flag the case where it most surprises you, and either revise the definition or defend that verdict. I would also like you to say whether your definition makes fragility intrinsic to the object itself or partly dependent on its surroundings.
