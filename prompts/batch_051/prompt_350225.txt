I run a small repair shop, and the machine we rely on every day is controlled by a piece of software whose maker went out of business years ago. The company is dissolved, the website is gone, and I cannot find anyone who can give or refuse permission when we need to fix a fault or copy the program onto a replacement machine. I have no academic background, but I have been trying to work this out on my own.

The thought I keep returning to is that property may not be a thing that sits inside a file or a title, but a live relationship between an owner and everyone around them: someone you can ask, someone who can say no, someone who can be harmed or helped by what you do. On that view, a claim with nobody on the other end is not much like ownership at all. The rival thought is that a right can persist on its own, the way an unclaimed bank account still belongs to someone even when no one is around to withdraw the money.

Please describe the most important implications of the relationship view for how I should treat abandoned software like this. I especially want to know what it would imply about copying the program, modifying it, sharing fixes with other users, and whether I should keep searching for an owner. I am not looking for legal advice or references to specific laws or cases; I want the underlying reasoning. Keep your answer to no more than 600 words, and please write it in British English.
