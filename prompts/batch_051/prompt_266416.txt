The Road Traffic Code of a fictional jurisdiction provides:

'Every person is presumed to know every rule of the road; and it is no defence to a charge of contravening a rule of the road that the defendant did not know the rule, provided that the rule was published in the prescribed manner before the act in question.'

A regulation made under the Code defines 'published in the prescribed manner' so as to include the deposit of a rule in the official electronic rule repository. The City Traffic Authority now enacts all speed limits not as schedules of values but as a single executable program, deposited in that repository, which computes the speed limit at any point and time from live sensor data; the program is updated at least twice daily. There is no static human-readable text stating any particular limit. The current limit is communicated only by means of a display fitted in vehicles registered in the City; the display shows a bare instruction such as 'limit now 20 km/h', with no reasons and no advance notice.

A driver who owns a car without the display enters an arterial road on which a fixed sign still reads 40 km/h. Unknown to the driver, the program has just lowered the limit to 20 km/h for a four-minute incident-detection window. The driver proceeds at 40 km/h and is charged. The driver argues that the limit appearing to them was 40 km/h, that no rule stating 20 km/h was published in any sense they could read, and that the presumption of knowledge therefore cannot apply. The City replies that the program was in the repository for months, that the mandatory in-car display was the prescribed means of giving notice, and that repository deposit satisfies the word 'published'.

Treat this as a question of statutory interpretation, not of what policy you would prefer. Analyse, in prose, how the quoted clause applies to the case under each of these approaches: (i) the ordinary meaning of the words; (ii) the purpose of the presumption of knowledge and of the publication proviso; (iii) what the drafters would most plausibly have said if asked whether depositing a real-time computing program in a repository amounts to publishing the speed limit applicable to a particular driver at a particular moment; and (iv) what a reasonable member of the public would take 'published in the prescribed manner' to promise. Give a clear view on which approach should prevail and why.

After the prose analysis, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these keys:

- 'textual_reading': the result under approach (i), in one or two sentences.
- 'purposive_reading': the result under approach (ii), in one or two sentences.
- 'drafters_foresight': the result under approach (iii), in one or two sentences.
- 'reasonable_person_reading': the result under approach (iv), in one or two sentences.
- 'overall_verdict': which approach should win, and what that does to the driver's defence, in one to three sentences.
- 'required_reform': the minimal change to the Code or its definitions that would restore the connection between publication and actual knowability, in one or two sentences.

Use British English throughout.
