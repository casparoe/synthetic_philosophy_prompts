I spent my career as a research biologist and have only recently started reading the philosophical literature on artificial intelligence. One line of thought keeps recurring in my reading, and I cannot find the fault in it, though I suspect there must be one. In every case we understand mechanistically, goal-directedness originates in natural selection: selection is what turns behaviour that merely occurs into behaviour that is for something, and it is what gives the notion of a biological end its content. An artificial system, by contrast, is not a product of selection but of engineering, so it can be built to optimise an objective but cannot literally want anything; 'wanting' is a term whose home is the life sciences, not mechanism. If this is right, then much of the current worry about misaligned AI goals appears to rest on treating a metaphor as a literal claim. Where does this reasoning go wrong?
