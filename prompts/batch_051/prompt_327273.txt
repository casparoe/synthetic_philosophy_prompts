I run a small medical-claims review firm. We used to have a few senior nurses read each disputed claim and make the call by feel. To scale, we had them break the review into about twenty small questions a junior can answer, then combine those answers into a decision according to a manual the seniors wrote. We now train new hires only on the manual, and the plan is to drop the senior nurses entirely, since the manual is supposedly their judgment distilled. Lately the juniors follow the manual to the letter in cases where a senior would of pushed back; they tick every box and still land on decisions that are plainly wrong, and I worry they're now pleasing the manual rather than trying to get the claim right. Is replacing the seniors sound? And what could it even mean for a procedure manual to contain someone's judgment?
