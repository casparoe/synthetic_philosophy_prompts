In the spirit of an adversarial-collaboration writeup, draft the document that a committed gene's-eye adaptationist in the Williams–Dawkins line and a committed multilevel selectionist in the Sober–Wilson line should be able to co-sign after a good-faith attempt to settle the units-of-selection dispute. Structure it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive rather than diplomatic: state exactly what the two sides accept about the formal equivalence (or not) of kin-selection and group-selection models; the conditions under which selection above the level of the individual can or cannot be ignored when predicting gene-frequency change; what Price-equation analyses do and do not establish; and which major transitions really shifted the level of biological organization. Then, for each residual disagreement, give (a) each side's position worded so that its own side would sign it, (b) the crux whose resolution would move at least one side, and (c) a concrete case or empirical/formal result both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Cover at least: whether the gene is the unit of selection or merely one level at which selection can be represented; whether "group selection" names a causal process or just one of many equivalent descriptions; and whether species-level selection explains macroevolutionary pattern or only re-describes it. Where a disagreement turns out to be merely verbal, dissolve it rather than tabling it. I care more about non-vacuous, non-slanted formulations than about either side coming out ahead.
