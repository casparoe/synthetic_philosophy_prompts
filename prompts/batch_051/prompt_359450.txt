Question: Consider a person who, on Sunday night, firmly decides to get up at 7 on Monday morning to write, but at 7 on Monday stays in bed scrolling on their phone. (a) Explain why an exponential-discounting model cannot represent this behaviour as akratic. (b) Explain how a quasi-hyperbolic (β–δ) model would represent it. (c) Say whether, on Donald Davidson's account, the person would count as genuinely akratic. (d) Briefly say which of the two frameworks you find more illuminating, and why.

Student answer: "(a) With exponential discounting the discount rate is constant across delays, so when getting up to write and staying in bed are both evaluated for the same time — Monday morning — the same discount factor applies to both and drops out of the comparison. If the person preferred writing on Sunday night, time-consistency says the Monday self must still prefer it, because exponential discounting rules out preference reversal. So the model can't represent the behaviour as acting against the agent's own better judgement; it just predicts they write. (b) A quasi-hyperbolic model adds a present-bias parameter β < 1, which gives extra weight to whatever is immediate. On Sunday, both the writing and the scrolling are still in the future, so both are discounted by βδ and the comparison is driven by δ. On Monday, the discomfort of getting out of bed is immediate (weight 1) while the payoff to writing is delayed (weight βδ), so staying in bed can win even though the Sunday self endorsed writing. That is where the preference reversal comes from. (c) Davidson would count the person as akratic only if they act against an unconditional, all-things-considered judgement that writing is better. The person here does form that judgement and then acts against it, so at first glance Davidson has to count them as akratic. But Davidson's real view is that when the person stays in bed they are acting on a conditional judgement, such as 'given that I'm cosy, staying put is better', and simply don't pass to the unconditional conclusion. So the action isn't really against their best judgement after all; Davidson can explain apparent akrasia away as an incomplete piece of practical reasoning. (d) I find the β–δ model more illuminating, because it gives a mechanism for the reversal without having to convict the agent of any irrationality, whereas Davidson has to describe the person as making a logical mistake about which conclusion follows from which."
