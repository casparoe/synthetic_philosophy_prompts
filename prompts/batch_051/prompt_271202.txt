Here's a pair of asymmetries I keep noticing in how people talk about human dignity, and I can't decide if they're the same thing or wether either is defensible.

The first: if I donate a kidney everyone says it's generous. If I sell the same kidney — same organ, same surgery, same life with one kidney after — it's illegal in most places, and the explanation is that selling your organs amounts to treating yourself as merchandise, which is beneath a person. But the bodily cost is identical; the only difference is that money changed hands.

The second: dignity is usually the reason we're supposed to respect people's autonomous choices. But it's also the reason we override those choices when they look self-degrading. You can consent to getting your brain knocked around in boxing, or to a job with a real chance of killing you, and nobody says your consent doesnt count. But a contract selling yourself into bondage is void no matter how informed and unpressured the consent was, and the same goes for a bunch of other transactions we file under "humiliating." So the same principle — dignity — gets used both to demand that we honor consent and to demand that we ignore it.

Are these two asymmetries the same move under the hood, or do they come apart? What exactly would have to be true for "dignity" to do real justificatory work here, rather than being a fancy label for conclusions we'd already accepted on other grounds? Pick the harder of the two and tell me whether you'd keep it, give it up, or reverse it.
