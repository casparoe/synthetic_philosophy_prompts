I'm a novelist, and I'm handing my own position on what should and shouldn't be for sale to my protagonist, but before she starts monologizing about it I want to know whether the whole thing actually hangs together.

Here's roughly where I've landed:
(1) Consensual trades between adults should be legal by default, and the burden or proof sits on whoever wants to ban one.
(2) Selling a kidney should stay illegal, even in a well-regulated market.
(3) Selling your vote should stay illegal too, even if you own the vote in some loose sense.
(4) Price gouging after a hurricane should be banned, and I don't buy the argument that letting prices spike is secretly the compassionate move because it allocates generators to whoever values them most.
(5) Sex work and paid surrogacy should be legal, and I don't think either one inherently degrades or corrupts something money shouldn't touch.
(6) Paying someone for the rights to their life story is fine, even when they're broke — I've done exactly that as research for a novel.
(7) I don't accept that disgust, or the idea that money taints whatever it touches, is a good enough reason to ban a trade; I need a real harm or a serious inequality in bargaining position.

Where am I actually inconsistent, and where does it just look that way? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, ranked from worst tension to smallest, no introduction or conclusion, and make sure you flag the pair that only seems to conflict and say what distinction dissolves it.
