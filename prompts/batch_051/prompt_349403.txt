I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting pulled into a puzzle from a game my friends and I run. The game is in the style of Nomic: the rulebook is itself part of the game, and the rules can be changed from within, by procedures the rules themselves define. Our constitution is deliberately short. Rule 1.2 reads: "A rule change is adopted if it receives the votes of at least two-thirds of the members present at a regular meeting." Rule 1.3 reads: "A rule change takes effect immediately once the votes are counted and the result is announced." When we drafted these two sentences, we were picturing ordinary amendments — adding a scoring rule, renaming a role, changing the usual meeting time — and it never occurred to us that someone would try to amend the amendment rule itself during a vote on that very amendment.

At last week's meeting, with ten members present, Proposal 21 was introduced in this exact wording: "Rule 1.2 is amended to read: A rule change is adopted only if it receives the votes of every member present, and this new unanimity threshold also applies to the very vote by which Proposal 21 is adopted." Seven members voted in favor and three against. So the proposal passed under the old two-thirds threshold but could not have passed under the unanimity threshold it purports to install for its own adoption. We are now deadlocked. One camp says it was conclusively adopted, because the only rule in force when votes were counted was the two-thirds rule. A second camp says it defeated itself, because the text it enacts, applied to its own adoption, declares that adoption invalid. A third camp says the retroactive clause should simply be severed and the unanimity threshold kept for all future votes only. To me, as someone who works with systems that patch their own configuration, it feels like a patch that rewrites its own deployment condition while the deployment is still being evaluated.

Please take the role of a philosopher of legal rules or formal systems and work through the problem carefully. In particular: what did the rulebook actually require at the moment the votes were counted, and how should an interpreter treat a clause that sets conditions for its own adoption? Argue the case from three angles — the plain text of Rules 1.2 and 1.3, the apparent purpose of the two-thirds threshold, and what the people who drafted those rules would have said if they had been asked about a self-applying amendment — then say which approach should govern here and why. Begin by brainstorming the candidate interpretations before committing to one. End your answer with a line beginning exactly "Verdict:" followed by one of: "adopted", "not adopted", "adopted but retroactive clause void", or "inconsistent".
