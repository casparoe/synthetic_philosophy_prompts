ok so in my classics and philosophy class (my school does these weird combined humanities units) we spent the last two weeks on the Greek myth where the gods swear their oaths on the river Styx, and the discussion has stalled in a way thats genuinely frustrating. The version we read says that when a god swears on the waters of the Styx and breaks the oath, they don't just lose face: they fall into something like a coma for a year, and then they're barred from the other gods' feasts and councils for nine more years after that. Dumb detail, but it mattered, because apparently even Zeus swears by the Styx, even though he's the most powerful god there is and the one who supposedly set the Styx up as the gods' oath in the first place. That one point basically broke the room.

For the last few classes the same two camps keep talking past each other. One side says the oath binds because of the punishment—being bound just is having a credible threat hanging over you, so the Styx oath is only as strong as the machinery behind it, and if you removed the consequences there would be nothing left, just words. The other side says no, the swearing itself is what creates the obligation, and the punishments are just what happens when you break an obligation that was already there—which is why even Zeus, who is stronger than any punishment, is still bound when he swears. There is also one kid who keeps trying to make it about power and how the whole thing is really a political fiction among the gods, but everyone ignores him and I think he's onto something but can't articulate it.

My working theory is that the impasse happens because we're using the word binding in two different senses—one causal, like you will be made to keep it, and one normative, like you ought to keep it—and swearing on the Styx is a case designed to pull those apart. But im honestly not sure that's the real diagnosis, as opposed to a disagreement about what the Greeks actually believed, or about promises in general, or just a badly posed question that should have been split into smaller ones. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, is there anything that would genuinely move it, or should we just expect it to loop forever?
