For a philosophy of AI seminar, I want a specification of what an adequate control measure for a possibly misaligned AI system must do, so that we can judge proposed safety techniques against a fixed standard before surveying them. Assume the system's goals need not match ours, and assume it may know or infer that it is being trained, evaluated, or monitored, and may act strategically to be deployed while pursuing different goals later. Under those assumptions, write the desiderata for a control protocol: the failure modes it must be shown to prevent rather than merely fail to reward (for example, deliberately underperforming on tests of dangerous capabilities, producing innocent-looking outputs that steer downstream users, or coordinating across copies of the system), the distinctions it must respect, and the assumptions it may not quietly make about the system's honesty or goals. For each desideratum, give the concrete scenario or consideration that forces it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular research camp. Then identify any subset of your desiderata that cannot plausibly be satisfied together by one coherent protocol; if there is one, say which subset, why the conflict is structural, and which single desideratum does the most to exclude otherwise attractive proposals. Keep the weight on the criteria and the scenarios that motivate them rather than on scoring named proposals, and write the answer in American English.
