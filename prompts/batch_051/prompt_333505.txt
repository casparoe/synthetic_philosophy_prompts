I'm a novelist, and one of my characters is an animal cognition researcher who's strict about Morgan's canon — she'll never chalk a behavior up to a 'higher' psychological process when a lower one will do. I'm trying to keep her consistant across scenes now that the book's got enough of her rulings in it, and I'd rather not just announce the rule. So far she's said:

(a) a digger wasp that endlessly re-checks its burrow when an experimenter keeps moving its paralyzed cricket an inch is running a fixed action pattern, no representation of where the cricket is;

(b) a rat that learns a T-maze over dozens of trials is just forming an association, not reasoning;

(c) a scrub jay that re-hides a worm after caching it while a rival watched, and only once the rival is gone, does represent the rival having seen it, not mere association;

(d) an octopus that gradually, over many trials, learns to open a jar is just being reinforced, no insight;

(e) a crow that bends a wire into a hook with no prior training and fishes food out of a tube is doing means-end reasoning.

The next scene has her watching a jumping spider that sees prey disappear behind an opaque barrier and immediately takes a route it has never taken before to intercept it on the other side. What would she say about the spider? I want the candidate rules that fit her track record, what each one predicts she says about the spider, which reading is the best bet and why, and the single follow-up case that would settle which rule shes actually running. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
