I have been thinking about possibility and existence, and I keep returning to a difference I cannot quite state. Compare these two claims:

(1) There could have been a talking donkey.

(2) Some actually existing thing could have been a talking donkey.

I am confident these are not simply the same claim, because (2) points to a particular existing thing whereas (1) does not. But I cannot say exactly how much of a difference that is, or whether accepting (1) forces me to accept (2), or the other way round. The same kind of puzzle appears when I ask whether everything that exists exists necessarily. There seem to be two different claims: that some particular actual thing could have failed to exist, and that the world could have contained fewer things than it actually does; I cannot tell whether one is stronger than the other or whether they come to the same thing.

Could you give me a carefully worked example, or a small pair of examples, that separates the claims in each pair in a way I could defend to someone else? Use the example to explain whether either inference is forced, and to say what this tells us about whether possible things must already be counted among the things that exist, and whether actual things must exist necessarily.
