Before I read any further theories of truth, I want the specification of what a theory of truth is actually for. Write the desiderata: the cases any adequate theory must classify correctly, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the uses of the word true it must preserve, and the paradoxes it must explain rather than merely exclude — with the case or consideration behind each requirement. In particular, make sure the list covers at least: ordinary truth ascriptions and the T-sentences they seem to license; the use of truth in blind generalizations like everything she said is true; the distinction between truth and warranted assertibility, including the possibility of a justified false belief; the standard truth supplies for assertion and inquiry; and the full Liar family — the simple liar, the strengthened liar, Curry sentences, and Yablo-style paradoxes — together with the truth-teller and other ungrounded sentences. For each requirement, say whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable for your purposes, and flag any subset of the list that looks jointly unsatisfiable. End by naming the single requirement you think does the most to exclude the leading candidate theories, and briefly score deflationism and a fixed-point treatment of the paradoxes against that requirement. I want a committed answer: take a definite position on which desiderata are genuine and which are parochial, and on whether the full list can be jointly met, rather than surveying the options.
