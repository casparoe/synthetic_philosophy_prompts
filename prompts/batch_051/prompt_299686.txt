I am attracted to the hinge-epistemology reading of Wittgenstein's On Certainty: the idea that beneath all inquiry sit certainties—that the Earth has existed for many years, that hands are there to be looked at—which are not ordinary conclusions of evidence and, more importantly, are not items of knowledge in the way a well-supported empirical claim is. On this picture they are the fixed scaffolding or riverbed against which evidence counts as evidence, and it is a kind of grammatical mistake to ask for their justification or to call them known. The theory is elegant and explains how the regress of justification can come to an end. Yet I cannot shake the conviction that I simply do know that I have hands, and that Moore was onto something important when he held up his hands and claimed to know it. Does the hinge account rightly diagnose that conviction as a piece of ordinary language misleading us, so the conviction should be dropped, or does the conviction show that the account has conflated not needing justification here with not being known? Give the strongest version of each side and then say, with a definite verdict, which you would give up and why.
