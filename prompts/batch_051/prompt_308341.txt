A friend and I just spent a ridiculous amount of time arguing about what makes an infinite regress vicious. The thing that set it off: I said the what-justifies-your-justification regress is vicious because it never bottoms out. She said that regress is only vicious if following it through actually contradicts the original claim — otherwise its just long, and long chains are fine. We never agreed on a single test, and now I'm annoyed enough to actually catalogue the candidate tests people float.

So far I have these five:

1. The incompletability test: the regress is vicious when the original claim can only be true if every step in the series gets completed, and the series has no final step to complete.

2. The self-defeat test: the regress is vicious when, if you take it seriously, the original claim is never actually reached or true — the thing the claim was supposed to secure keeps getting deferred forever.

3. The failed-explanation test: vicious when each item was introduced to explain or ground the previous one, but the same structure repeats indefinitely, so the original item never actually receives the explanation it was owed.

4. The ungrounded-dependence test: vicious when the regress is one of metaphysical dependence — grounding, constitution, realization, priority — and there's no independent bottom, so every item's status is passed along to a further item that never arrives.

5. The actual-infinity test: vicious exactly when the regress would require an actual infinity of items, and actual infinities are impossible, or at least shouldn't be posited.

I'm trying to find one more candidate test that is genuinely different from all five — not a restatement of one of them, not a special case, not a mash-up of two of them. It can be niche or weird, I dont care. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good: would it actually settle the fight I had with my friend, or does it just move the fight to a different spot?
