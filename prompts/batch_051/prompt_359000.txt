I have been working through the problem of constitutional interregna — periods when a constitution's orderly rules about who governs simply run out — and I keep returning to a specific scenario that I would like reasoned through carefully rather than handled in generalities.

Consider the constitution of a small republic with a presidential system. It contains the following two provisions.

Article 47: "The President shall be elected for a term of five years by direct universal suffrage, and shall not remain in office after the expiry of the term for which he or she was elected. A presidential election shall be held on the first Saturday of April in the fifth year of the term."

Article 48: "If the office of President becomes vacant by death, resignation, or removal from office in accordance with this Constitution, the Vice President shall assume the office of President and shall serve for the remainder of the term."

Now suppose that in an election year, ten days before the first Saturday of April, a fire destroys the National Election Commission's only complete and certified electoral roll. The electoral statute requires a ninety-day reconstruction and re-certification process before a valid presidential election may lawfully be held. The incumbent President's term expires on the last day of April. On the first of May there is no president-elect; the incumbent has not died, resigned, or been removed; and Article 48 on its face does not apply. The incumbent claims that he must remain in office to preserve continuity. The Vice President claims that she must act because the country cannot be without an executive. A third camp in the assembly says the constitution places the country in a true interregnum, in which no constitutional officer may lawfully act.

Set out what each of the main approaches to interpretation — the plain text, the purpose and structure of the provisions, what the drafters would likely have said if they had been asked, and what a reasonable citizen reading the constitution would understand — implies about who should govern on the first of May. Then give a committed answer: who has lawful authority on that day, and which interpretive approach should control? Defend your choice by explaining why you reject the alternatives, not merely by listing them.
