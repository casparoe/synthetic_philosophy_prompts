I'm on a city charter commission, and we get a steady stream of proposals to change who votes: lowering the municipal voting age to 16, giving non-citizen residents a vote in school board races, making turnout legally compulsory with a small fine, and stranger ones. We have a staff lawyer but no political theorist, and we need a reusable decision procedure for a two-hour meeting: a fixed order of tests, what evidence or argument counts as passing each, where a proposal is dead, and where it has to be handed to the city council or the voters as a value judgment the procedure shouldn't pretend to settle. The procedure should generalize beyond the three examples, and it should say which of its tests quietly presume a particular theory of what the franchise is for. Then apply it to the three proposals above, and don't soften the result if somthing comes out politically inconvenient. End with \answer{...} containing one word per proposal, in order: 'enfranchise', 'deny', or 'values-call'.
