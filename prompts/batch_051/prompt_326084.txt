Construct a thought experiment in which two artificial agents have diametrically opposed final goals — one is tasked only with maximising the number of paperclips in a warehouse and the other only with minimising it — yet both converge on the same harmful course of action. Constraints: the agents are no more capable than a competent human operations manager; the setting is an ordinary logistics firm with no exotic technology; and the harm must come from an instrumentally convergent subgoal (such as self-preservation, resource acquisition, or resistance to being switched off) rather than from either final goal on its own. Keep the scenario to one paragraph.

After the scenario, do the following. First, state the instrumental convergence thesis and indicate how the orthogonality thesis is being treated as a background assumption. Second, show step by step how each agent's instrumental reasoning leads it to the shared harmful action despite the opposed final goals. Third, identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, explain what would happen if that feature were removed, and say how a critic who denies instrumental convergence would attack the case. Write your answer in British English.
