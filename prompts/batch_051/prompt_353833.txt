Hey — I'm putting together a short essay on AI-generated art and the nature of creativity, and I want someone to tear into it. The thesis is that the debate about "can AI be creative?" is posed as a property question (does the machine have the creative property?) when it should be a division-of-labor question. In a traditional painting or poem the same agent does two things: generate candidate material and select among what arises. When someone works with an image model, those two functions get pulled apart: the model generates, and the human mostly selects — writing the prompt, curating dozens of outputs, choosing the one that's actually good, deciding when to stop fiddling with the seed and parameters. My claim is that the real conceptual pressure isn't "is the machine imagining?" so much as "can selection, detached from generation, carry the weight we place on authorship, praise, and aesthetic responsibility?" And my hunch is that a lot of the unease people feel comes from the fact that our default picture of the artist is still basically Romantic — the maker who conjures something into being through craft — and we haven't figured out whether taste with a good eye but no studio practice is enough.

Where I need help:

(1) What's the strongest objection to the generation/selection split itself? E.g., an objection that says prompting is really a generative act, or that the model never truly "selects" anything and so the split is overdrawn — which one should I take most seriously, and why?

(2) Does the essay lean too hard on a Romantic strawman? If someone has a more austere account of art — say, art is just whatever functions as art in a practice — does my argument collapse?

(3) Is the bit about "deciding when to stop" pulling its weight, or is it just color? Be blunt, I'd rather here that a section is dead weight than keep it.
