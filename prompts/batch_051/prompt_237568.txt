I hold the view that a large share of the resistance to radical life extension is best explained not by genuine ethical objections but by a motivated, status-quo-biased attachment to aging and natural death — the phenomenon sometimes called deathism. I want to know what would count against this. Tell me what would have to be true for that resistance to be, by and large, an honest ethical position rather than a rationalization: which observations, arguments, intuitions, and experimental or survey findings should move me, sorted from decisive to merely awkward. Include the evidence that a defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against it, and say whether the view has been — or can be — qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be a problem for a psychological-explanatory claim of this sort.
