Give me a working definition of filiality (xiao) suitable for a Confucian role ethicist—someone who treats persons as constituted by their relationships and roles rather than as detached individuals who then decide which roles matter. I want the definition to do real work: it should distinguish filiality from blind obedience, from warm feeling, and from the mere performance of outward forms.

Run your definition through these six cases and say where it gives the wrong answer, no answer, or a surprising one.

1. A daughter lives with and physically cares for her demented mother, but she describes her to friends as a burden and has not had a conversation with her in three years that went beyond medication, meals, or household logistics. Is the daughter filial?

2. A son leaves the family farm to become a schoolteacher against his father’s wishes. He sends a third of his income home, arranges for a neighbor to help with the harvest, and returns for the New Year and his father’s birthday. Is this filial?

3. A woman reports her father to the police for a hit-and-run that killed a pedestrian. She later visits him in prison every week and manages his household affairs while he is inside. Is the visiting filial? Is the reporting a failure of filiality?

4. A son whose father has died trains an AI assistant on his father’s letters and voice messages and asks it, in his father’s voice, for advice about his own children. Is this a practice of filiality, a refusal to let the father’s role end, or neither?

5. A woman whose mother was abusive in childhood pays for her mother’s nursing-home care after a stroke but refuses to visit. She says she does not owe the role of daughter to someone who used that role as a weapon. Is this filial?

6. A man keeps his late father’s ancestral tablet and makes offerings before it, though he privately believes the rites are empty. He does so because his father asked him to preserve the rite and because his teenage children watch him do it. Is this filial?

I am fairly confident that case 2 is filial and case 5 is not, and I cannot settle the others. Propose the definition, test it against all six, and tell me where it surprises you or forces you to revise it.
