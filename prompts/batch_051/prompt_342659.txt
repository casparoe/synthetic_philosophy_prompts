Here's one I keep coming back to. A big city government — the kind that treats every budget overrun and data loss as a months-long hunt for someone to blame — decides to formalize the scapegoat. New rule: after any headline-level failure, one official from the relevant agency's top two grades is chosen at random, gives a public mea culpa in which they may overstate their actual causal role, and is then moved to a job of equal pay elsewhere and shielded from any further city blame over that episode. The agency they leave is officially "cleansed" and can't be cited for the failure in future fights. Treat it as a mechanism, not a proposal I'm endorsing. Who gets picked in practice? Where does this get gamed first — by agencies, by the officials themselves, by the press? Does the public accept the rite and move on, or does it keep hunting for a more satisfying villain now that the sacrifice is cheap and bloodless? And do you think any actual guilt can be transferred this way, or does the city just get better at laundering it? Also tell me which of these failure modes you could patch without gutting the whole point, and which ones are baked in.
