I'm in a philosophy of math course, and we've spent the last two weeks on the indispensability argument, and I keep swinging between finding it obvious and finding it too clever by half. The professor keeps running it as an analogy. We believe in electrons even though nobody has seen one, because positing them is indispensable to our best physical theories — they do explanatory work. Likewise, he says, we should believe in numbers, functions, and sets because they're indispensable to our best scientific explanations: its not just bookkeeping or notation, the mathematics itself explains. His two go-to cases are periodical cicadas and honeycomb. The cicada species with 13- and 17-year life cycles are supposedly explained by the fact that prime-numbered emergence minimizes overlap with predators' and competitors' life cycles, so the prime structure of the number is doing real explanatory work. And hexagonal honeycomb cells are explained by hexagons being the most efficient way to tile a plane with equal-area cells while minimizing wax, so again a mathematical fact — an optimality fact — explains a biological structure. His conclusion: if electrons earn their existence by being indispensable to explanation, numbers earn it the same way. Probably you can tell I don't trust arguments by analogy.

I want a full audit, not a literature summary. You're a professional philosopher of mathematics who works on mathematical explanation; start by steelmanning the analogy, then examine it. Then identify the disanalogies and separate the ones that matter from the cosmetic ones — in particular, is "numbers are causally inert" a devastating disanalogy, or does it beg the question by assuming that only causal things exist? Go through the cicada and honeycomb examples and say whether the primes or hexagons are genuinely doing the explaining, or whether they just describe a pattern whose real causal work is done by ecological or mechanical facts. Finally, say whether the conclusion — full-blown Platonism about mathematical objects — survives once the disanalogies are spelled out, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument the whole time.
