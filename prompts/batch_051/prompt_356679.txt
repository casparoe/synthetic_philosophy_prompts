Write a sestina in the voice of a god who has just been asked to swear by the Styx. The six end-words should be drawn from the terms of the oath (something like swear, bind, water, break, name, keep), and the sestina's obsessive reordering of those six words should do the argumentative work: once you swear by Styx, the obligation stops being in your control and keeps returning from angles you can't hold off. Get at the odd structure of an oath among gods — gods are the usual enforcers of human oaths, so when a god swears, the guarantor has to be something older or lower then the gods, a power even immortals are under. Don't just name-drop the myth; work out the mechanism of binding, and let the envoi be the moment the vow actually takes and the repetition becomes obligation rather than just worry.
