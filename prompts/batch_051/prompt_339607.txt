I am a professor of philosophy, and I have been trying to make my own concept of a transformative technology precise enough to lecture about, rather than relying on a term I use more confidently than I understand. I am not trying to match any particular author's usage; I want the principle that best rationalises my own judgements. I have collected my verdicts about the following cases, and I would like you to tell me what principle, if any, captures them.

The invention of writing seems to me transformative in the strongest sense: it changed what a human life could be and altered the long-run path of whole societies. The steam engine also seems transformative, though in a less obviously cognitive way. By contrast, I am unsure about the smartphone: it has rearranged daily habits, attention and social norms, yet I doubt it has changed the trajectory of the species in the way writing did. Recommendation algorithms that shape social-media feeds and video streams strike me as deeply consequential socially, but I want to say they are not transformative in the intended sense. A machine translation system that lets a speaker of one language read the news in fifty others feels important, but not transformative. A spreadsheet, which automated clerical calculation and quietly reshaped business administration, I classify as transformative for a particular domain of activity but not transformative tout court; yet a machine translation system seems to have done something analogous for the domain of communication, and I do not classify it as transformative even in that qualified way. I cannot state what the difference is. A hypothetical artificial intelligence that could perform all human intellectual labour at human quality or better seems unequivocally transformative. An artificial intelligence that could design successive generations of improved versions of itself with no human in the loop also seems unequivocally transformative, and perhaps in a different way. Finally, I think a technology that lifted trend economic growth from two per cent to three per cent for a century would count as transformative even if everyday life changed little, because the compounding would redraw what is attainable in the long run; but I do not think a technology that merely makes many existing tasks slightly cheaper is transformative, even when billions of people use it.

What I want is a definition of transformative artificial intelligence, or a small set of candidate definitions, that makes sense of these verdicts and tells me which of them I must revise. In particular, does an adequate principle need to distinguish changes in the kinds of activities that remain human from changes in the rate at which capabilities grow, and does it set a threshold on the size or duration of consequences? Once you have stated the principle, please apply it to large language models and related systems and say whether, on that account, transformative AI has already happened. Please also apply it to one further case of your own choosing that I have not listed, and say explicitly which of my verdicts, if any, the account cannot accommodate. Please write your answer in British English.
