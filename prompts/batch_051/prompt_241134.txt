I've been trying to work out for myself what makes a small promise different from a vague plan. The rough criteria I keep coming back to are these: first, the person says they'll do something at a particular time or in a clear window; second, the other person actually changes what they would have done because of it; third, the person knows the other person is counting on them; and fourth, if they don't do it, the other person loses something—time, money, or at least the chance to make other arrangements.

Now here's the case I can't settle. My friend agreed to come over at 9:00 a.m. to help me move a couch before my storage unit window closed at 9:45. He said, "I'll be there at 9." He showed up at 9:25, no text. I had to rush, I missed the window, and I got hit with a late fee. He says this wasn't a real promise because between friends a time like "9" just means "around 9-ish," and it's unfair to expect formal punctuality for something casual. I think he's dodging responsibility. But I also wonder: if I already knew he has a habit of being 20 minutes late, did I partly fail my own criterion about actually relying on him? Or does my knowledge only make me less surprised, not less wronged? Go through my criteria and tell me whether he made a promise and whether he broke it. I want a definite answer, not a menu of possible views. If my criteria get this case wrong, say which criterion needs changing and why.
