I have been trying to settle what I mean by social punishment, and I want a working definition rather than a dictionary paraphrase. The term comes up whenever people argue about whether a boycott, a firing, a deplatforming, or a collective demand for an apology is a form of punishment, and whether that matters. By a working definition I mean a criterion that separates social punishment from legal punishment, from contractual or administrative enforcement by a platform or employer, from the ordinary private choices of individuals, and from the simple fact of becoming unpopular.

Here are the cases I keep returning to. (1) A social media platform permanently removes a user's account after a documented pattern of harassment directed at other users; the company says it is only enforcing its terms of service. (2) A judge fines a person for a minor offense. (3) A company dismisses an employee, whose job is not public-facing, after campaigners unearth and circulate old posts of the employee's that many people find offensive. (4) After it is reported that a restaurant owner donated to a political campaign, several regular patrons quietly stop eating there; no one organizes the disappearance, and no one announces it. (5) A professional association passes a resolution formally censuring a member for conduct that violates its ethics code; the member keeps their license and their job. (6) A group of friends gradually stops inviting someone to their gatherings after the person refuses to apologize for an incident; no one states a policy, but the invitations stop. (7) An independent bookstore decides not to stock a new book after its own staff complain about the author's public statements. (8) A small crowd gathers outside a person's home every evening for a week, shouting for a public apology, and disperses only after the person posts one.

My starting views are these. The nightly crowd is social punishment if anything is. The judge's fine is punishment but not social punishment. The quiet disappearance of customers and the bookstore's decision do not feel like social punishment, though I cannot say exactly why. The platform's terms-of-service action seems to me not to be social punishment either, but I suspect the company's framing is doing some work there. The dismissal, the censure, and the fading friendship are the ones I cannot decide about.

Give me the best definition of social punishment you can construct, run it through all eight cases, and say where it forces me to revise my starting views or where you think it should overrule them. If the definition ends up splitting the concept, for example into organized condemnation, ostracism, and decentralized punishment, tell me which of the eight cases then fall under which heading.
