i'm in a philosophy of mind course and we just did a stretch on Hofstadter — the idea that the self isn't a substance or a place but a pattern, a strange loop, and that pieces of your self can genuinely exist in other people's heads if they're close enough to you, not as metaphor but as a lower-resolution version of the same loop. i find the underlying picture weirdly attractive: there's no soul-stuff, no central point where the 'I' sits, just a self-referential pattern of memories, dispositions, habits, self-descriptions, running on whatever neural hardware is available. so if my girlfriend has internalized a grainy version of me — she can predict how i'd react, she catches herself arguing with me in her head, she carries around some of the same loops — then shouldn't i say that part of my self is literally located in her skull, and that if i got hit by a bus tomorrow something of me keeps running there? the theory seems to force me to say yes, and it's not just that she remembers me; the whole point is that me-ness gets instanciated in more than one place at different resolutions.

but then i hit the wall. my experience feels like the one place in the universe where i am directly, privately, unavoidably present. no set of descriptions or predictions, however rich, seems like it could add up to the inside of being me. a model of me isn't me — it's a representation of me, the way a painting is. and if selves come in degrees and overlaps, then 'who is this consciousness for?' becomes vague, and that feels wrong because the for-me-ness of my experience seems all-or-nothing: either the waking-up is mine or it isn't.

i don't know which side should give. walk me through it like a forced choice but don't just pick. give me the strongest version of the theory that the self really can be distributed across brains, and the strongest version of the intuition that says no, that's just a model of me, and then tell me what would be required to (a) debunk the intuition, (b) amend the theory, or (c) show they were never actually in conflict. i'd also like to know whether this all-or-nothing for-me-ness is doing real philosophical work or whether i'm mistaking a feature of how a single brain runs its loops for a fact about what selves are. be honest about what each option commits me to, because right now both sides feel true and i need to know which one i can revise without lying to myself.
