I've been in and out of the rationality-training world for a while—meetups, blog posts, one weekend workshop that was basically a martial arts dojo with whiteboards—and the thing that keeps bothering me is how much the movement borrows the trappings of martial arts (drills, katas, belts, sparring) without ever quite facing the transfer question: does grinding on Bayesian puzzles and bias exercises actually make you less wrong about your landlord, your startup, or your politics? I've watched people crush the bias-of-the-week drills and then, an hour later, do the exact thing the drill was supposed to innoculate them against.

So I'd like a piece of sustained satire: a deadpan brochure or FAQ for an invented rationality dojo (name it whatever you want) that has responded to the transfer problem by doubling all the way down. The school now offers belt ranks, adversarial sparring sessions where a partner attacks your reasoning in real time, a kata of the month, and maybe a black-belt test where candidates have to ad-lib a Bayesian analysis of a relationship argument without visibly losing a friendship. Keep it around 700 words, in a calm, encouraging, slightly cultish wellness-adjacent voice, formatted like a brochure or FAQ with headers and fine print.

The jokes should come from real features rather than a strawman: the gap between practice-room performance and real-world behavior, the credentialism, the way training starts to substitute for actually knowing things about the domain you're reasoning about, and the possibility that the whole thing is optimizing for a feeling of clarity rather than accuracy. It should be legible as satire from early on, but a true convert should be able to nod along for most of it, and the FAQ should quietly imply that when transfer doesn't happen, its the student's fault. Land the standard objections without ever stating them, and don't let the piece wink at the reader.
