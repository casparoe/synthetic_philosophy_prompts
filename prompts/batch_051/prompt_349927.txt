Before I go any further with the what-is-life literature, I want someone to actually specify what an adequate definition of life has to deliver. The cases it should get right or deliberately leave open: viruses, a HeLa cell line, a dormant bacterial endospore, an obligate intracellular symbiont that can't replicate outside its host, a self-replicating RNA with no metabolism, and a hypothetical membrane-bound thing in a subsurface ocean that maintains an ion gradient but never reproduces or evolves. Tell me which demands on a definition are non-negotiable, which are just one research program's preferences, and wether any subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable. Give me exactly five numbered desiderata, each a sentence or two giving the requirement and the case or consideration behind it, with no introduction or conclusion, and use the fifth item to say which desideratum does the most to eliminate candidates.
