Ok so I keep getting into arguments online about whether something someone said was racist or not, and I've realized people are using the word to mean totally different things. One friend basically thinks a statement is racist only if the person meant it that way (like they actually have something against a racial group), and another friend says intent doesn't matter and what matters is the effect or harm, so even an innocent comment can be racist if it lands badly. Then there's the whole dog-whistle thing where politicians or pundits say stuff that sounds normal to most people but supposedly signals something to racists, like talking about "inner-city crime" or never mentioning race at all but still saying "law and order" in a way their audience clearly hears as being about race. I definately feel like the word is doing two jobs at once, describing something and also condemning someone, and I want a definition that doesn't just smuggle in one friend's side of the argument.

Define "a racist statement" in a way that correctly classifies: (1) someone yelling a racial slur at a stranger; (2) a journalist accurately quoting that slur while reporting the incident; (3) a teacher saying that average test scores differ between racial groups, stated as a neutral fact; (4) a comedian making fun of a racial group's accent or customs on stage; (5) a politician who keeps saying "we need law and order in our cities" and never mentions race, but whose base hears it as being about race; (6) a Black person making a joke about a stereotype of Black people; (7) a well-meaning person saying "I don't see color" during an argument about racism; (8) a 1950s textbook stating that a particular racial group is less intelligent, written back when that was mainstream science. Then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure, and whether the phrase "racist statement" is actually doing two jobs at once (a descriptive job and a condemnatory job), because that's what it feels like in these fights every time. The dog-whistle cases are the ones that break my brain the most, since the whole point is plausible deniability, so spend some time there.
