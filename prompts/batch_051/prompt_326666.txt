I serve on a departmental working group that is reconstructing a position on personal identity from the recorded rulings of an earlier commission. The commission took itself to be working within the Lockean memory tradition, refining the criterion that a later person is the same person as an earlier one just in case the later person genuinely remembers at least some of the earlier person's experiences; but it proceeded case by case, and we must now extend its rulings to a case it never settled.

The commission's recorded rulings are these. First, in an implanted-memory case, a subject was given a complete and detailed set of apparent memories of another person's life, with no causal connection to that person's actual experiences; the commission ruled that the subject is not the same person as the other person. Second, in a body-transfer case, a woman's complete consciousness — her memories, projects, and traits — came to reside in a body other than her original one, while her original body retained none of that consciousness; the commission ruled that the person in the new body is the woman, notwithstanding the change of body. Third, in a memory-loss case, an accident left a patient with exactly one genuine memory of a childhood episode and no other recollection of the child's life; the commission ruled that the patient is the same person as the child.

The new case is of the familiar brave-officer kind. An elderly general genuinely remembers being a young officer who captured an enemy standard, but he can no longer remember being flogged as a schoolboy; the young officer, at that earlier time, did genuinely remember being flogged as the schoolboy. We must decide, consistently with the commission's record, whether the elderly general is the same person as the schoolboy.

Lay out the candidate principles that fit the three recorded rulings, and say what each would require in the general-and-schoolboy case. Identify which recorded ruling is the closest analog and explain why. Then give your best judgment about the verdict. If the record genuinely underdetermines the choice, state exactly which principle the commission would be adopting under each possible verdict, and describe the further case that would most cleanly distinguish any principles that remain live afterward.
