The standard outside-view argument says: stop telling yourself a story about why this empire is different, and instead look at how long empires of its kind have actually lasted, then anchor on that base rate. I've been turning this over and I think there's a hole in it that nobody mentions. The empires in your reference class didn't know they were data points in someone's base rate, and even if a few leaders suspected decline, they couldn't run the numbers the way a modern state can. Once a country's leaders know the median lifespan of past great powers and know they're below it, they can act on that information — avoid the specific failures that preceded collapse historically, or panic and do something rash. So the forecast changes the thing being forecast. Does the outside view survive when the reference class contains agents who can read the base rate? Is this a real problem, or am I just rehasing something? And if it does survive, what exactly is the remaining argument — or should we just say the method is dead for self-aware systems?
