I have a policy about settling factual questions that I've been using for years, and I want it checked before I keep using it. My rule: I generate explanations in the order they come to mind, and I adopt the first one that fits every fact I currently have and survives three objections I can think of on the spot. Then I stop looking, unless new evidence shows up. I don't compare it against alternatives I didn't think of; I treat the fact that I couldn't think of anything better as weak evidence that nothing better exists. The practical upside is that I'm never stuck in research loops, and it honestly works okey for things like diagnosing car noises or figuring out why a friend is being distant. But I can't tell if 'survives three objections' is measuring how good the story is or just how uncreative I am at objecting. Is this a defensible implementation of bounded rationality, or is it just premature closure with a fancy name? What would the world have to be like for my rule to be the right tradeoff, and what's the cheapest fix that keeps the speed but loses the worst errors?
