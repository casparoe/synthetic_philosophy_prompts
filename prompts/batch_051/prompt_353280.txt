For a workshop on the philosophy of ritual I have been asked to write down, as plainly as I can, the set of commitments about scapegoat rites and the transfer of guilt that I actually hold, so that the other participants can press me on their coherence before I turn them into a paper. I would like the same audit from you first. The commitments are these:

(1) Rites in which a community's guilt is laid upon a scapegoat — a goat, a bird, or a human being — who is then killed or expelled recur across unrelated cultures, and the best explanation of that recurrence is functional: such rites really do reduce intragroup conflict and restore a fragile social order in the aftermath of collective wrongdoing.

(2) Guilt is not the kind of thing that can be literally transferred from one bearer to another through a ritual gesture, a declaration, or the death of a substitute. There is no metaphysical channel along which it travels.

(3) Nevertheless, when such a rite is performed under the right conditions, it genuinely discharges the community's guilt. This is not merely a matter of the participants feeling relieved, though feeling relieved is part of it: the guilt is extinguished, not relocated.

(4) The rite's capacity to discharge guilt depends on the participants' experiencing the victim as really bearing the transferred guilt. A performance in which everyone understands the transfer to be purely symbolic does not do the same work.

(5) The scapegoat is not guilty of the offences laid upon it, and it is wronged by being killed or driven out, even though the community typically does not see the rite in those terms.

(6) An adequate account of these rites should be naturalistic throughout. It should appeal only to ordinary social and psychological mechanisms, and should posit neither supernatural agents nor occult causal channels.

(7) The Christian doctrine of atonement is best understood as a theological extension of this same logic of substitution rather than as a categorically different kind of phenomenon.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating each as equally serious; for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be; and then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set cohere. I have deliberately not indicated which of these I would be willing to abandon, because I do not want the audit steered by that.
