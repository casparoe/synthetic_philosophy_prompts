I've been in an environmental-history reading group where the geologists and the historians keep talking past each other about the Anthropocene, and I want something constructive out of it. Write the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two positions could actually produce. Position A ("early Anthropocene") holds that the Anthropocene starts when human activities first left a durable, distinguishable mark in the planetary record — early deforestation and rice-farming signals, the anomalous CO2 and methane trends from the mid-Holocene onward, maybe a candidate boundary around 1610 tied to the Columbian exchange and a brief CO2 dip. On this view the unit is a long, cumulative, diachronous historical interval, and insisting on a single sharp "golden spike" mistakes a historical period for a geological event; the start date is a question for historians and Earth-system scientists, not something a stratigraphic commission owns. Position B ("Great Acceleration") holds that the Anthropocene starts in the mid-twentieth century, when radionuclide fallout, plastics, concrete, and the carbon spike became globally synchronous and took the Earth system clearly outside its Holocene envelope; on this view the mid-centry boundary is the only one that can anchor a formal, synchronous, stratigraphically usable unit, and deciding where the unit starts is properly the business of stratigraphers, not historians of diffuse millennial-scale human impacts.

Draft the document the collaboration would produce. Begin with a numbered "agreed claims" section: what both sides can sign about the empirical records, about what a start date has to do for a historical period as opposed to a formal geological unit, and about where the real technical disagreement sits, including the meta-question of who has standing to decide. Then a disagreements table. For each row, give the precise statement of the disagreement, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view, with no strawmanning. Then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and for each crux, give a test, argument, or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute looks verbal (e.g., about what "epoch" or "start" means), say which part and why. Don't give me a bibliography or citations; I want formulations precise enough that both camps would actually sign them, with the residual disagreement made exact.
