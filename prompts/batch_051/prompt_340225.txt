At my company, HR pushes two things at once. There's a talent mobility program that rotates people into new roles every few years, tells them their skills are portable, and warns them not to over-identify with any one job. And the same HR department runs workshops on bringing your whole self to work and being authentic. Together these seem to assume a strange picture of what a self is: a fixed core that moves unchanged from role to role, trying on jobs like costumes. I want you to pull that apart. What exactly does this practice presuppose about the relation between a person and their roles (manager, mentor, coworker, employee)? Be precise about what the practice strictly presupposes, which rival pictures of the self it quietly rules out, and what it merely fits with or leaves underdetermined between different theories. Also say whether the two programs actually presuppose consistent things. Then tell me what would have to change in the program's design and rhetoric if we swapped in a Confucian role-ethics picture, where a person is constituted by their concrete relationships and roles (son, teacher, friend, colleague) and there is no neutral core self behind them. Dont make it a generic essay on authenticity.
