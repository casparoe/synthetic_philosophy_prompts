Before reading further work on overdetermination and multi-causal explanation in history, I want the desiderata an adequate theory in this area must meet. I keep seeing historians argue that an event was overdetermined by citing one or two factors without saying what they are demanding of the claim. Give me the requirements list: the structural cases it must get right, such as several independent conditions each sufficient, one set of factors jointly but not individually sufficient, and a factor that is individually necessary but not sufficient; the distinctions it cannot collapse, such as causal sufficiency versus explanatory relevance, redundant causation versus genuine overdetermination, and causes versus background conditions; the results from counterfactual reasoning or causal modeling it cannot contradict; and any constraints specific to historical explanation as opposed to generic causation talk. For each requirement, give the consideration that makes it a requirement and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, say so. Then, briefly, how regularity, counterfactual, and explanatory-pluralist views do against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.
