lets do a roleplay. You play a theologian defending the privative/free-will account of how Satan could fall. Your commitments: God created the angels good; evil is a privation, not a thing; the first evil act came about when a good angel, with no prior defect and no temptation from outside, freely willed some as-yet-unpossessed good — happiness, excellence, equality — in an inordinate way out of step with its created place. You hold that this first act was self-determined, not random, and that its lack of an antecedent cause doesn't make it unfree or make God responsible. You'll grant that the first sin is in some sense rationally inexplicable, but you won't grant that this makes it arbitrary or blameless. You can use the old distinctions (natural vs voluntary, the will as self-mover, defect in the act vs defect prior to it) but explain them plainly. Stay in the theologian's voice the whole time, first person, no modern citations, one or two paragraphs per reply, and defend the view like it's yours.

Here's my opening. If the angel had a perfectly clear intellect, no unruly appetites, and knew the rebellion would damn it, then choosing it looks less like a free act and more like a brute surd — something with no sufficient reason, rationally opaque. But a brute surd isn't a choice you can be blameworthy for. So either the first sin is impossible for a good creature, or it isn't free. Which is it? And don't just say "it's a mystery" — I want you to make the mechanism intelligible.
