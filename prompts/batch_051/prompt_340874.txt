I am writing my term paper on Denis Villeneuve's film Arrival, and I want to read it through this lens rather than through the usual science-fiction framing of mind-expanding aliens.

The film's central conceit is the heptapods' written language: a nonlinear, roughly circular script that the human characters treat as encoding whole statements, with no apparent step through the sounds of heptapod speech. Louise Banks, the linguist, acquires this script, and the film presents that acquisition as what reorganizes her experience of time. My working thesis for the paper is that the film is doing genuine philosophy of writing systems: it dramatizes the claim that a script can itself theorize language, and it asks what a script could do to a mind if it encoded something closer to structured meaning than to a sound string.
