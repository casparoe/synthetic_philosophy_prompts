I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for a long time, and I've run into a recurring problem that I don't have a settled name for. When I learn a proposition for certain — say, I see that a die landed showing less than four — the order in which I process my evidence never matters; learning A and then B leaves me exactly where learning B and then A would have left me. But a great deal of my evidence isn't like that. A blurry glimpse of a die, a lab value that comes back "slightly elevated," a friend's tone on the phone: these things shift my credences without making any possibility impossible. I've found that when I update on two such uncertain pieces of evidence, the final probabilities depend on the order in which I process them, and also on how finely I slice the space of possibilities when I write down what each observation did to my credences. Does this phenomenon have a name? Is it a sign that my update rule is under-specified — that "lower this credence and raise that one, without learning anything for certain" doesn't determine a unique posterior — or is order-dependence just a fact of life once evidence is uncertain? I'm not mainly after the label; I want to know exactly what this phenomenon is, what it is not, when it goes away, and how a careful Bayesian should think about it.
