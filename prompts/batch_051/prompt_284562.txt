My friend teaches philosophy of biology and I've been collecting her offhand verdicts on which traits have "a function." So far she's said all of these are functions: the heart's pumping blood, a peacock's tail attracting mates, a moth's camouflage hiding it from predators, a bee's waggle dance telling hivemates where nectar is, and an orchid's colors attracting pollinators. She's also said the thumping noise a heart makes is not a function, and the whiteness of a bone is not a function. Next week's case is a mutation that arose this year in a single moose, helps it digest willow bark, and has no history of selection behind it, but is currently keeping that moose alive. Predict whether she'll say that digestive effect is a function, say which rule she seems to be running—something like etiological/selected-effects versus current causal contribution—and tell me what further case would best settle which rule it really is. Also say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you're least sure of.
