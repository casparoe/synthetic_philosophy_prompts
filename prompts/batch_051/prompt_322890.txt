Please rank the three main positions on the postmortem fate of the unrepentant—eternal conscious torment, annihilationism (also called conditional immortality), and universal salvation—by how successfully each answers the proportionality objection to hell (the objection that no wrongdoing committed during a finite life can deserve an unending penalty). Give each position a score out of 10 on proportionality alongside the ranking. Treat each view in its strongest form, and before scoring specify the axes of proportionality you are applying—for example, duration, intensity, and scope of the loss. In your scoring, say which conception of proportionality is doing the work for each position: retributive desert, remedial correction, or a natural consequence of a person's fixed postmortem will. Finally, address two finer-grained questions: whether annihilationism genuinely disposes of the objection or merely replaces unending suffering with permanent deprivation, and whether universalism resolves the objection only by ceasing to treat the postmortem outcome as a punishment at all.
