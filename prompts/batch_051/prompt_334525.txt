I have been trying to settle a question about quantum mechanics, and I keep getting stuck on a more basic word: real. On one side, people seem to treat the wavefunction — the mathematical object that specifies the state of an electron — as a genuine feature of the world. On the other side, people treat it as a bookkeeping device, more like a weather forecast than a storm: something that sums up an observer's knowledge and makes predictions, but is not itself out there. Before I can make up my mind about the wavefunction, I think I need a working definition of "physically real."

Here is my list of test cases. I am confident that a baseball is physically real. I am also confident that the center of mass of a spinning dumbbell is not: it is a useful abstraction, and in a U-shaped object it sits in empty air rather than in the object itself. I have no firm view about the rest, though I lean toward saying that the electromagnetic field around a magnet is real and a rainbow is not, and I cannot find a criterion that separates those two without doing damage elsewhere.

1. A baseball.
2. A rainbow.
3. A shadow on a wall.
4. The center of mass of a spinning dumbbell.
5. The electromagnetic field around a magnet.
6. A law of nature, such as the rule that gravitational attraction falls off with the square of the distance.
7. The statistical spread of molecular speeds in a hot gas — the bell-shaped distribution of how many molecules are moving at each speed.
8. The wavefunction of an electron.
