I have a rule for which sources and methods I'll trust: I dont accept one until I've checked it against something I already accept. That's how I moved from believing my dad about things to trusting a particular news outlet — enough of the people I already trusted lined up behind the outlet. But I recently realized the rule has no ground floor. Whatever I used to check my first method would itself have needed checking, and whatever I use to check things now has to be assumed before I can use it to check anything. So I've quietly changed the rule to: I keep asking for justification until I hit a method or source I just can't bring myself to doubt, and then I stop and treat it as solid. I think this is just honest — everyone has to stop somewhere — but I also suspect I've just renamed the point where I got tired of asking, or the point where my ambition ran out. Is the new rule a defensible epistemic policy, or is it a bias wearing the outfit of a principle? If there's a real difference between "I can't bring myself to doubt this" and "I'd rather not investigate this," what could that difference consist in, and how would I tell which one I'm doing from the inside? And where does that leave the difference between having a method and just having a temperament?
