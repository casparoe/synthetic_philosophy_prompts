I've been going back and forth on something since a conversation with my sister over the holidays. She told me she's mostly stopped driving in the city because she can't get comfortable with the fact that every time she gets behind the wheel she's imposing a small risk of death or serious injury on pedestrians and cyclists who never agreed to it. I pushed back at the time—everyone does it, the risks are tiny, and society would grind to a hault if we treated every tiny risk as a rights violation. But then I looked up the numbers and found that a regular driver who drives for decades has something on the order of a 1% lifetime chance of killing someone in a crash. That unsettled me, because I wouldn't accept a 1-in-100 chance of accidentally killing a stranger in almost any other context, and I'd be furious if someone else accepted that gamble with my life just too save fifteen minutes.

So I'm trying to work out whether routine private car use in a dense city is actually morally permissible, or whether my comfort with it is just socialized complacency. I'd like you to give me your credence that routine private car use is morally permissible in a city with decent public transit, so that driving is mostly convenience rather than necessity. Decompose the credence rather than just giving a number. The moving parts I can see at least include: whether imposing risk on someone without their consent is wrong in principle or only once the risk crosses some threshold; whether it matters that the risk is spread over many anonymous people and no particular victim is identifiable in advance; whether the reciprocity of the situation—most pedestrians also drive sometimes, most drivers also walk—changes the consent analysis; whether a distinction between ex ante risk and ex post harm should matter for how we judge the driver; and how much weight the availability of alternatives carries.

Give me a rough probability for each of those, then show how they combine into your overall credence. Then tell me which component you're least confident in, and what evidence or argument would shift your overall number the most. For calibration, my credence is around 0.75 that routine driving is permissible, and my sister is around 0.3; if you can also say where the disagreement between us most likely lives, that would help.
