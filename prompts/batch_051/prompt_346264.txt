Write the list of requirements an adequate theory of humor has to meet for explaining real laughter rather than just canned jokes: the cases it has to get right or explain away (puns, slapstick, nervous laughter at a funeral, tickling, jokes that get funnier on repetition, jokes that flop, cringe comedy, things that only become funny much later), the distinctions it cant collapse (finding something funny vs actually laughing, laughing with vs laughing at, the content of a joke vs the social work of telling it), with the consideration behind each requirement and a note on which are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's preferences. Then flag any two that probably can't be jointly satisfied, and give a quick scorecard of superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation against the list, and say which single requirement does the most to knock out the most popular views. I don't need citations, just the reasoning, and write it in American English.
