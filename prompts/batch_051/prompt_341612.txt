In my Chinese philosophy course we have been reading James Legge’s translation of the Zhuangzi, and I want to press on one episode in the chapter titled The Adjustment of Controversies. As Legge presents the episode, the narrator relates that he dreamed he was a butterfly, fluttering about contentedly and following its own inclinations; when he woke, he could not determine whether he had been a man dreaming he was a butterfly, or whether he was now a butterfly dreaming it was a man.

I would like you to propose a working definition of being awake. The term seems obvious in ordinary use, but this passage makes me suspect it is doing more than one job: perhaps a physiological state, an epistemic standpoint, and a normative one. Please give a definition precise enough to classify each of the following cases, and then run the definition through each case one by one:

- A student taking notes in a lecture, fully oriented and responsive.
- A sleeping person in the middle of an ordinary non-lucid dream.
- A person in a lucid dream who knows they are dreaming and can direct the dream's course.
- A person having a false awakening: they seem to have gotten out of bed and begun their day, but they are in fact still asleep.
- A person gradually surfacing from anesthesia who can hear nearby voices but cannot yet form thoughts or move.
- Zhuangzi's case from the passage: the man who wakes from the butterfly dream and cannot determine which state is the dream.
- A brain in a vat receiving sensory input indistinguishable from ordinary waking life, with no memory of having been envatted.

Finally, say where your definition is least secure, and whether the Zhuangzi case is best classified as waking, dreaming, or neither on your account. Please write your answer in American English.
