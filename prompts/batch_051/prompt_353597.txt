I spent my career in sensory neurophysiology and have come to philosophy only since retiring; I expected the philosophy of mind to be congenial, but I find myself genuinely unsure whether the whole notion of qualia is, as Dennett urged, an incoherent posit. The Chase-and-Sanborn contrast keeps bothering me: two coffee tasters who have both come to dislike a coffee they once enjoyed, one insisting that the taste has stayed the same while his standards have risen, the other insisting that his standards have stayed the same while the taste has altered. The case seems designed to show that introspection cannot settle which of two adjacent things really changed, and I keep changing my mind about whether that shows qualia are dubious or only that our judgments about them are. I belong to a discussion group of retired scientists with one professional philosopher, and I want to administer a sorting quiz that assigns each of us to a position by how we answer concrete cases rather than by which label we find sympathetic.

Please write such a quiz, eight to ten questions, each built around a compact scenario with two or three answer options that encode a theoretical commitment without using the jargon of the debate. Include at least the Chase-and-Sanborn coffee case, an inverted-spectrum case, and the case of Mary the color scientist, each glossed in a sentence or two so that respondents who have not read the literature can still answer; also invent one or two further cases in the same spirit, such as a wine taster whose descriptions become more refined over decades, or a patient whose color vision changes after surgery. Then provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the principal positions: qualia realism, representationalism about phenomenal character, Dennett-style eliminativism or illusionism, and the type-B materialist view that there is an explanatory gap but no metaphysical one. The key should state plainly which positions it cannot separate, and where a cell of the answer space contains no position it should say briefly whether that cell is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. The instrument must stand alone for readers with no formal background in philosophy of mind.
