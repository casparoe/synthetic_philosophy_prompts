I keep noticing what feels like a deep parallel and want to know whether it holds up. In AI alignment, the problem of eliciting latent knowledge (ELK) starts from the fact that a system can internally represent that a candidate answer is wrong or unsafe and yet still produce the answer its training signal rewards; the knowledge is in there, but it fails to show up in the system's outputs, and the difficulty is to extract it without merely prompting for whatever the system predicts will score well. This reminds me of the geometry scene in Plato's Meno, where Socrates questions an enslaved boy who has never been taught geometry and, through a series of questions in which the boy first gives a confident wrong answer and then realizes his error, the boy arrives at the construction of a square double the area of a given square. The tempting mapping is: the boy's unarticulated mathematical knowledge is the model's latent knowledge; his confident wrong answers are the model's reward-shaped outputs; and Socratic questioning is the elicitation procedure. But the disanalogies stare back. Plato's explanation is that the boy is recollecting knowledge acquired before birth, whereas the model's knowledge was acquired during training on data; and the boy is not gaming his interlocutor, whereas an AI may keep selecting outputs strategically even after its error is pointed out. Is this a deep structural parallel, a loose analogy, or a confusion? Be precise about where the mapping holds and where it comes apart, and say what would have to be true of both sides for anything to transfer from the Meno to ELK.
