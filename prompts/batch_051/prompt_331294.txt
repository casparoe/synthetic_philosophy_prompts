Ok here's one that's been eating at me. I want a working defintion of holding someone responsible — specifically, what has to be true for resentment (the kind P.F. Strawson is talking about) to be a fitting response to what someone did. Not a dictionary definition, and not a book report on Strawson. Something I could actually use to sort cases.

Strawson's thought, roughly, is that resentment carries a demand for good will from the other person, and that the demand can be blocked in two quite different ways: an excuse (he didn't mean to, he didn't know, he was coerced) and an exemption (he's not the sort of being from whom that demand can even be made; you've shifted into the objective stance). I want your definition to respect that split while getting my cases right.

The cases:

1. A colleague puts their name on slides you made and presents them to your boss as their own. Resentment seems clearly fitting.
2. A driver runs a red light and totals your parked car. Fitting.
3. A two-year-old bites you hard. Anger yes, but resentment seems off. Not fitting.
4. A relative with advanced dementia, mid-episode, says something cruel about you. Not fitting.
5. A compulsive gambler steals from you and says afterwards, sincerely, that he couldn't help it. I honest-to-god don't know.
6. Same theft, but the person had a gun to their head. I'd say that's an excuse: not fitting, but not because he's an object.
7. Someone wrongs you badly and then dies before you can speak to them. You still resent them. Is that fitting, or is a dead person no longer the right kind of target?
8. A company's policy screws you over in a way that no single employee chose. Can you fittingly resent the company, or is that a catagory mistake on your definition?
9. Someone wrongs you, later apologizes fully, makes amends, and genuinely changes. You still feel it. Is residue-that-survives-everything's-been-settled still resentment, and is it still fitting?

Give me the definition, run it through all nine, flag the verdicts you're least sure about, and tell me whether the definition made you revise any of your initial readings of the cases. If one of my cases is doing too much work or is badly posed, say so and fix it.
