I am a research engineer who works mostly in reinforcement learning: policies, value functions, reward shaping, model-based planning, learned world models, evolutionary strategies, imitation learning, meta-learning. I use this vocabulary fluently every day, and I am much less fluent in philosophy. Lately I have been reading Dennett's hierarchy of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — and I find myself compulsively translating it as an RL story. Darwinian creatures look like an evolutionary outer loop that searches over genotypes and lets the environment supply all the selection pressure. Skinnerian creatures look like model-free learners whose trial and error happens in the world, paid for in actual actions and reinforcements. Popperian creatures look like model-based agents that run rollouts in an internal simulator, so candidate actions die in the model rather than in the world. Gregorian creatures look like agents that inherit much of their competence from a shared cultural store of tools — above all language — instead of searching for it themselves. I am not asking for an introduction to the four grades; I want the translation and, above all, the account of where it breaks.

Restate each of the four grades in contemporary reinforcement learning vocabulary and, for each, say what translates cleanly, what translates only by stretching the RL concept, and what has no counterpart at all. Then give me the residue report. I especially want to know whether Dennett's Skinnerian–Popperian distinction is really the model-based/model-free distinction under another name, or whether that identification flattens something important; whether the idea that a system can perform competently while comprehending nothing — that representation can make a creature smarter without adding anything like understanding — has any expression in my vocabulary; and whether what fails to survive is a gap in the RL framework, a place where Dennett's ladder blurs once you try to build the things, or both.
