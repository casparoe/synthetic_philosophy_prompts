I have long held a broadly functionalist view of the mind, on which mental states are individuated by their causal roles — their typical causes, effects, and interactions with other states — rather than by the physical machinery that realizes them. From this I have drawn what I took to be the field's settled lesson: because a given mental state could in principle be realized in a mammalian brain, a silicon-based system, or an alien biochemistry, mental kinds are not identical to neural kinds, and psychology retains an explanatory autonomy that a type-identity reduction would deny it. I have also insisted that this is an empirical, defeasible claim about the relation between functional and physical taxonomies, not a terminological stipulation.

Lately I have begun to worry that the position is far harder to falsify than I claimed. My worries are these. If mental states are typed by their causal role, multiple realizability seems to follow from the typing, so the headline claim looks like a theorem of my choice of taxonomy rather than a discovery. If one instead fixes both the mental and the neural taxonomy at comparable grains, the supposed diversity of realizers may shrink, and psychological kinds may turn out to correspond one-to-one with neural kinds. And if one allows sufficiently coarse-grained realizers, nearly every macro-level kind — hearts, genes, currencies — turns out multiply realizable, which would drain the notion of any special significance for the mental. I want a map of what would actually count against the functionalist multiple-realizability position: which findings from neuroscience, comparative cognition, or artificial systems would be decisive as opposed to merely awkward, which objections its defenders would themselves concede as genuine strikes against it, and which they would dismiss as mismatched grains or as changing the subject. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to peripheral, and give me an honest verdict on whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — and whether such insulation is a defect for a position of this kind.
