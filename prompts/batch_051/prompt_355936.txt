I recently joined a citizens' assembly on a contested housing measure, and I keep hearing both sides claim that their position is "what the people actually want." That led me back to Jean-Jacques Rousseau, The Social Contract, Book II, Chapter 2 ("That Sovereignty is Indivisible"), where he writes:

"for will either is, or is not, general; it is the will either of the body of the people, or only of a part of it. In the first case, the will, when declared, is an act of Sovereignty and constitutes law: in the second, it is merely a particular will, or act of magistracy — at the most a decree."

But when I try to apply this, I get an argument that ends somewhere plainly false, and I cannot find the exit.

(1) By the quoted principle, a will is general only if it is the will of the body of the people as a whole; the will of only a part is particular and cannot constitute law.
(2) For the body of the people to will something as a body, the people must actually do the willing: the decision must issue from the collective itself, not from a part acting for it or claiming to speak for it.
(3) In any actual large state, no collective decision involves literally every member of the people: some are too young, some are disenfranchised, some are ill or abroad, and many simply abstain.
(4) So in any actual large state, the people never actually do the willing as a whole; at most some part of the people decides.
(5) Therefore, by (1), no decision in any actual large state is the will of the body of the people as a whole.
(6) Therefore no law in any actual large state is an act of sovereignty; every such law is at most a decree of a part, an act of magistracy.
(7) But then every modern state, democratic or not, is equally without law in Rousseau's sense, and no actual exercise of power is legitimate.
(8) That conclusion is too strong: it makes the distinction between legitimate democratic law-making and rule by a faction impossible to draw in practice.

I want to know which step goes wrong, or whether the argument is valid and the real cost is one of the premises. If I have to choose, I would rather keep the quoted Rousseau principle than accept the conclusion, but I do not want to pretend the cost is cheap. Please answer in no more than 700 words.
