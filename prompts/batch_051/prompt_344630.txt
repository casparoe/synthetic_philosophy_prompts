I want a working definition of "commodification" — the concept at stake when critics say an exchange treats something that should not be for sale as if it were a commodity. Please give me a definition precise enough to classify each of the following eight cases, and then run the definition through all eight in turn. The cases are: (1) a healthy adult sells one of their kidneys to a stranger for a substantial payment; (2) a homeowner pays a neighbor's teenager twenty dollars to mow the lawn; (3) in the days after a hurricane, a hardware store raises the price of portable generators from eight hundred to twenty-five hundred dollars; (4) an amusement park sells a pass that lets buyers go to the head of every line; (5) a couple pays a woman a large fee to gestate and deliver a baby under a commercial surrogacy contract; (6) a city council sells the naming rights to a public park to a corporation; (7) a political group pays registered voters a small sum to vote a particular way in a referendum; and (8) a school district pays students two dollars for each book they read.

I am confident that (1) is commodification and that (2) is not. I have no settled view about the others, and I particularly suspect that (3) is objectionable for reasons that may have nothing to do with commodification, though I cannot state the boundary precisely. The definition should therefore distinguish commodification from mere buying and selling on one side and from exploitation, injustice, or repugnance on the other, without collapsing all three into a single verdict.

As you run the definition, note any case where it yields a verdict you did not expect or where you had to revise the definition to accommodate the case. End by saying whether calling something commodification is a purely descriptive classification or already carries a critical charge — or whether the term does two jobs at once — and then say how confident you are in the definition you settled on and which of your eight verdicts, or the definitional choices behind it, is least secure.
