Write a deadpan, sustained-irony sales page, roughly 700–800 words, for Hindsight™, a service that retroactively explains any historical event or personal decision by reconstructing the past as an inevitable, tightly plotted story. The register should be polished Silicon Valley product-launch prose, credible enough that an inattentive reader might almost click the button. The satiric target is the practice of treating hindsight coherence as if it were foresight: the assumption that what happened was what had to happen, and that someone who can narrate the past compellingly was therefore in a good position to have predicted it. The humor must come from getting the psychology right rather than from mere absurdity, including at least the following: creeping determinism, the systematic overestimation of what could have been anticipated at the time, the narrative fallacy’s preference for a coherent story over an accurate record, retrospective revision of one’s earlier confidence to match the outcome, and the asymmetry between explaining a past event and predicting a future one. Include a features section, one invented testimonial from a historian who now explains every past outcome with total confidence, and a fine-print disclaimer stating that the product is validated only against events that have already occurred and is not certified for forecasting.
