ok so I've been trying to get a bunch of my views about agents to hang together, and I'd like an audit before I write this up. For background, I'm thinking about the kind of agent that's inside the world rather than looking at it from outside. My commitments, as near as I can make them:

(1) The boundary between agent and environment is a conveniance for toy models, not a real cut. The agent's model of the world is itself part of the world being modelled, and the world can push on the agent's model in ways the agent doesn't catch.

(2) Logical omniscience is only an idealization and a dangerous one. A real agent can be uncertain about what follows from what it already knows, and about what its own future computations will output.

(3) In Newcomb-style cases the rational policy is to act as if you had precommitted; you shouldn't treat the fact that the prediction is now fixed as a reason to throw away the correlation between your decision procedure and the predicted outcome.

(4) There's a determinate fact about what your own decision procedure would output on inputs it will never actually receive, and rational evaluation bottoms out in these counterfactual facts.

(5) A bounded agent can be fully rational without computing the ideal policy; what matters is how well it approximates that policy given its resources.

Which of these actually conflict, and which only look like they do? Please rank the real tensions by severity instead of a flat list, and for each say what has to give and what the repair costs. Also tell me how confident you are in each part of your answer and flag the single point you're least sure of.
