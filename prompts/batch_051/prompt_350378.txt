I keep running into papers arguing about whether a big neural net or a whole-brain emulation is a moral patient, and they all seem to be grading candidate theories against whichever cases the author happens to care about. Before I read any more of them, give me the desiderata: what does an adequate theory of which computations matter morally have to deliver, and where may it draw the boundary of sentience without cheating? I want the hard cases, not just "a person matters and a rock doesnt" — a lookup table that outputs the right conversation, a point-for-point simulation of a nematode's 302 neurons, a human upload running a million times slower, two copies of the same computation sharing one physical substrate, a system that computes the relevant states only in fragments or with a lesion. For each desideratum say which case forces it and why. Flag any pairs you suspect can't be jointly satisfied (e.g., a theory that counts every implementation of the right computation may struggle to exclude the lookup table). Then briefly score the main families of view against your list, but keep that part short; the criteria are the deliverable.
