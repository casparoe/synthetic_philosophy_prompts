Okay so I got moved onto the team that writes the personality for our customer service chatbot (don't ask), and now I spend all day staring at lines like "I understand how frustating that must be" and wondering if there's any fact of the matter about whether it means anything. The interesting version here isn't really is it conscious, it's the Searle vs Dennett thing about intentionality. As I understand it, Searle's position is that the bot's words have only derived intentionality: they're meaningful only because we lend them meaning, the way the Chinese outputs in the room are meaningful to the people outside rather than to the room itself. Dennett's position, roughly, is that a two-bitser that gets its meaning by being paid in our interpretations ends up indistinguishable from a system with original intentionality, so the distinction is doing no testable work. I go back and forth on this constantly now, and I'd rather not settle it by reading more philosophy. So: design the strongest empirical test you can for the specific claim that a present-day LLM has only derived intentionality, not original intentionality. Do not just propose asking the model what it means, since both sides will just disagree about whether the answer itself is two-bit. Lay out the test concretely: what you'd measure or observe, what result would support the only-derived claim, what result would support original intentionality, and whether either result could be dismissed as just measuring our tendency to attribute meaning rather than the thing itself. Then say honestly what the test cannot settle even in principle, and what someone would have to already believe for the results to count as evidence at all.
