I'm TAing a class on responsibility and want to give my students a sample dialogue to discuss. I've been rereading Hobbes's Leviathan, chapter 16, where he argues, in paraphrase, that a multitude of people are made into one artificial person when a single representative acts for them, and that the unity of the person comes from the representative rather than from the people themselves. He's talking mainly about the commonwealth, but I want the dialogue to apply the idea to companies.

Could you write a dialogue between two students arguing about whether a corporation can itself be blamed, not just the managers or shareholders in it? One student should think Hobbes's point gives a real basis for treating a corporation as a blameworthy agent: once a representative acts in its name, the group is a single person and can own decisions. The other should think the same point shows why corporate blame is only shorthand, because the "person" exists only through the representative's actions and has no mind of its own to form intentions or feel remorse. Make them equally sharp and well-informed, and have them respond to each others actual arguments rather than just taking turns. Maybe have them press each other on whether intentions can be transferred, or whether the representative's acts are the group's acts or just acts for the group.
