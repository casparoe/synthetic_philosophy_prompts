Lately I've been stuck on something that started during a bad stretch of insomnia: wether a finite mind like mine could actually run out of genuinely new experiences. If there are only finitely many possible brain states and experience supervenes on brain states, then there should be only finitely many possible experiences, so in a long enough life you'd cycle through them and that's it. But I realize the interesting question is what it would even mean to have exhausted the list, and I've been trying to pin that down.

Here are the criteria I've been using for saying a mind has exhausted its possible experiences:
(a) Every experience type available to that mind has occurred at least once.
(b) When an available type recurs, the mind can recognize it as a recurrence, so nothing comes as a surprise.
(c) No combination or sequence of elements already in the repertoire yields an experience type the mind hasn't had.
(d) The mind can't form a higher-order thought or attitude about its own repertoire that wasn't already counted as one of the types.

Now the borderline case that's been bugging me. Imagine a finite mind, call it S, whose possible experiences have been enumerated in advance—science fiction, but go with it. The list includes all first-order experiences and all higher-order attitudes the enumerators could specify ahead of time. S works through the list in order. When S reaches the final item, it does two things: it has that final first-order experience, and it forms the thought that this is the last one. And that second-order thought was not on the list; the enumerators couldn't specify it ahead of time because it's an indexical thought about the moment of completion itself.

So: at that moment, has S exhausted its possible experiences, or not? Please go through my four criteria and tell me, for each, whether S meets it. Then commit to a verdict and defend it—don't tell me it just depends on how we define the words. Also say whether my criteria need changing, and in particular whether criterion (d) is asking for something impossible, or whether the original list was just badly drawn.
