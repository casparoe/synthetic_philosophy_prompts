I've got a presentation in my ethics seminar in a couple of weeks and the Q&A after is the bit i'm dreading. The position I'm defending is roughly Susan Wolf's line that the moral saint — somebody who is as morally good as possible — is not a personal ideal we should want for ourselves, but I'm adding a twist. My claim is that moral sainthood can be fine as an impersonal evaluation (a way of assessing someone from the outside) but becomes a problem the moment an individual adopts it as a deliberative ideal, because it reorganises their practical reasons and makes them worse at choosing the non-moral goods that make a life worth living: friendships, aesthetic commitments, private projects, play. So I'm not just arguing from demandingness; I'm saying the ideal is self-undermining as a guide to choice.
