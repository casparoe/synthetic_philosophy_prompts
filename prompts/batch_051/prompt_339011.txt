so we've been doing transitional justice in my class and I keep getting stuck on this. after apartheid, south africa gave amnesty to people who had tortured or killed for political reasons if they came to the truth commission and told the full truth. but almost nobody in the class thinks we should do that for ordinary crimes: a bank robber who confesses everything still goes to prison, and we all think that's obviously right. so why is the same deal called a triumph of justice in one case and an outrage in the other? is it the political motive, the fact that the old regime was itself criminal, the need to get the truth, or something else? I want the strongest principle you can find that actually generates both judgments, then test it against a hard case—say, someone who killed for partly political and partly personal reasons, or someone who confesses only partially—and tell me wether you'd keep the asymmetry or give it up.
