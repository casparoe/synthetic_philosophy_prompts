Origen argued that scripture contains deliberate stumbling blocks—places where the literal sense is impossible or apparently false—so that readers wouldn’t stop at the surface but would be driven to a deeper spiritual sense. For a long time only scholars could see many of these seams; ordinary readers mostly encountered the text in a single translation. Now suppose a Bible app lets any reader, in one tap, see every manuscript variant, alternate translation, and parallell contradiction, with algorithmic flagging of hard sayings. Does Origen’s argument survive this change? Is it stronger because the seams are now unmissable, or weaker because many of the seams look less like intentional divine tricks and more like the normal mess of textual transmission and human authorship? Say exactly which premise of the argument the technology bears on, and whether there’s a repaired version that still does some work.
