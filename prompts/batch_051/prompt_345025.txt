I've been reading Keynes on the good life, and one sentence in the preface to his 1931 *Essays in Persuasion* keeps bugging me. He writes: "For the Western World already has the resources and the technique, if we could create the organisation to use them, capable of reducing the Economic Problem, which now absorbs our moral and material energies, to a position of secondary importance." His idea is that the Economic Problem—want, poverty, class struggle—is basically a transitory muddle, and after we solve it we can finally turn to "real problems" of life and human relations.

Now, as a retired natural scientist, I keep thinking about a development Keynes didn't have: cheap, scalable AI that can generate endless digital content, personalization, and recommendation feeds. That doesn't obviously eliminate scarcity. It seems to shove scarcity into attention, status, and positional goods. So does Keynes's argument survive that shift? I realize he might say that's still the Economic Problem in another form, but if scarcity just pops up again at the level of status and attention, then what exactly was the "muddle" he thought we could get past? Is his real point about the organizing problem rather than the resource problem? Or does AI-driven abundance make the Economic Problem worse in a way his framing can't capture? I'd like you to pick apart which premise of his arguement the new development bears on, and say whether the argument still goes through, needs repair, or is basically dead.
