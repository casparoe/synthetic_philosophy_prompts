I cover science and ideas for a general-interest publication, and I have reported from time to time on the long-running argument about private cities, charter cities, and seasteading: the claim that people should be able to opt out of a state by choosing among competing providers of law, security, and public services. What strikes me each time I return to this material is how little the terrain has changed. One side argues that exit is already possible in degrees, from special economic zones to charter city proposals to floating communities, and that jurisdictional competition disciplines governments much as product competition disciplines firms. The other side answers that any provider of law and security must eventually become a territorial monopoly on force, at which point it is simply a state under another name; that host governments grant these enclaves their powers and can revoke them, so nothing fundamental has been opted out of; and that such projects inevitably free-ride on the courts, currencies, and militaries of the states they claim to have escaped. Each side can now predict the other's replies in advance, and the argument has settled into a fixed set of moves.

My working hypothesis is that the two camps may not be answering the same question: one uses "state" in a functional sense, as any jurisdiction that monopolizes coercion over a territory, while the other uses it in a more juridical or historical sense, as a sovereign entity claiming ultimate authority. That would make much of the impasse a definitional standoff wrapped around a substantive core. But I want a diagnosis with reasons, not simply confirmation of my hypothesis. Is the disagreement genuinely verbal, or does it conceal a factual or formal divergence, for example about whether competitive governance would converge on monopoly, or about what the correct counterfactual baseline is? And what would count as progress given the correct diagnosis: a distinction, a model, an empirical case, or a rephrasing of the question that would let the two sides engage rather than repeat themselves?
