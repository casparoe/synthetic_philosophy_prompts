So I'm taking a course on testimony this semester, mostly on the reductionism/anti-reductionism debate, and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a label for—I'm not even sure its been given one. The pattern is this: whenever someone argues that you shouldn't accept anything on another person's say-so until you've verified the speaker's reliability or checked the claim for yourself, the checking itself is already resting on a huge pile of testimony nobody ever checked. Like, my uncle is very into 'verify everything'—he won't believe a news story until he's confirmed it with 'primary sources,' but he got his sense of which sources count as primary, how to read them, and what the words in the headline even mean, from other people he never vetted. Same thing in class: the reductionist side wants positive evidence of reliability, but their examples—'I checked her track record,' 'I compared what she said with what I already know'—all presuppose a background of reports, labels, maps, dictionaries, earlier testimony, and a shared language that they just help themselves to. It isn't plain circularity, because you're not using the same speaker to check the speaker; it's more that the demand for checking can only be stated inside a room that default trust built in advance. And it isn't quite the standard regress argument either, because the problem isn't that checking generates an infinite chain—it's that a lot of unearned trust has to already be in place before the demand to check even makes sense.

Does this configuration have a name in the testimony literature, and if not, can you carve it out for me—say exactly how it differs from circularity, regress, and self-undermining objections to reductionism, and which of those is its closest neighbor? I'm not after a single word; I want the edges of the thing, where it stops applying, and whether it actually damages reductionism or just embarrasses the people who phrase reductionism too absolutely. Also, what's the best move a careful reductionist has for blocking it, and would your name/carving survive that reply? Keep the whole response under 450 words.
