Here is an argument about the symbol grounding problem, as applied to a language model trained only on text. I was convinced by the conclusion at first, but now I suspect a term is sliding and I want the failure localized. (1) A token such as 'gold' means gold in a system only if the system is connected to gold by the kind of chain that fixes reference. (2) The kind of chain that fixes reference must bottom out in a mind's direct perceptual or practical contact with the referent. (3) A text-only language model has no direct perceptual or practical contact with gold; the only chains reaching gold run through the direct contact of the human authors of its training texts. (4) So the model itself is not connected to gold by the kind of chain that fixes reference. (5) Therefore, 'gold' does not mean gold in such a model; at best its outputs are tokens whose meanings belong to the authors it was trained on. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and under which reading of the relevant term? If it is valid, which premise should a defender of text-only models reject, and what is the strongest substitute?
