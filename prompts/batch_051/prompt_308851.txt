I am writing a paper for a summer civics program about whether "democratic backsliding" is a genuine comparative concept or just a partisan way of describing politics one does not like. Before I go further into the literature, I want the standard set first: what does a theory — or even a usable working definition — of democratic backsliding have to deliver before it earns the right to be applied across Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States?

A passage I want woven through your answer comes from James Madison, The Federalist No. 10 (1787):

"The instability, injustice, and confusion introduced into the public councils, have, in truth, been the mortal diseases under which popular governments have everywhere perished; as they continue to be the favorite and fruitful topics from which the adversaries to liberty derive their most specious declamations."

Madison identifies the mortal diseases of popular government as instability, injustice, and confusion in the public councils themselves — not as armies or coups. That sits awkwardly against the modern discussion, which tends to focus on elected executives using formally legal means to disable courts, the press, and election administration. So your criteria have to take a position on this: is Madison describing democratic backsliding, or something else? If it is backsliding, an adequate account must be broad enough to catch gradual faction-generated rot; if it is not, the account owes a principled boundary between backsliding and these older diseases.

Please write the desiderata: the full list of requirements any adequate account of democratic backsliding must meet, each with the case or consideration behind it. Mark which are nonnegotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp. Make sure the list says how the account (a) avoids classifying every loss by one's own side as backsliding, (b) handles incremental lawful erosion as well as sudden illegal seizures of power, (c) specifies — or deliberately refuses to specify — a threshold or a matter of degree, (d) says who gets to judge that a country is backsliding, and (e) treats the passage above as a test case it must classify. Flag any pairs of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most work in ruling out weak definitions. End by telling me how confident you are in the overall list and singling out the single point you are least sure of.
