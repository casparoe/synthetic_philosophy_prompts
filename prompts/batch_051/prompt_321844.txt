Consider the following argument, which is offered as a reason for regarding Malthusian population theory in its modern form as no longer a live empirical hypothesis. I want you to determine whether the argument goes through, and if it does not, to identify exactly where it fails.

(1) Malthus's core claim is that population tends to increase geometrically while the means of subsistence can increase only arithmetically, so that population is under a persistent pressure to outrun subsistence.

(2) In the two centuries since Malthus wrote, successive applications of this claim have failed to produce the general subsistence crisis they pointed towards: agricultural yields, energy inputs and the effective carrying capacity of land have expanded roughly in step with population in Britain, much of Europe, and later much of Asia.

(3) Defenders of the theory meet each such apparent failure by adding an auxiliary factor that Malthus himself did not build into the original mechanism — new arable land, fossil-energy-based agriculture, synthetic nitrogen fertiliser, international trade, and the demographic transition, among others.

(4) A theory whose apparent predictive failures can always be explained after the fact by the addition of further auxiliary factors, without any statement in advance of which auxiliary factors would be admissible or what outcome would count against the core, is compatible with any course of events whatsoever.

(5) A theory compatible with any course of events is unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable theory has no claim to serious empirical status.

(6) Therefore the Malthusian core, as it is actually defended, has been emptied of empirical content: it cannot fail, and accordingly says nothing determinate about the world.

Assess the argument. Is it valid? If not, identify the step or steps that fail and say which failure does the main logical work; then give the strongest version of the charge that remains after the failures are repaired. In particular, consider a defender's reply that the repeated postponements are not independent rescue devices but consequences of the very pressure the theory describes, so that the theory may be 'right but early'. Does that reply undermine step (2), step (4), or step (5)? Please answer in British English.
