I don't understand why corrigibility and the shutdown problem are treated as open problems in AI alignment rather than as a special case of value alignment. If a corrigible system is one that defers to its operator and lets itself be shut down, doesn't that just mean its utility function assigns high value to deference, or to the state of being shut down? But that cannot be right as stated: an expected-utility maximizer that placed high utility on the operator shutting it down would take instrumental steps to bring that state about — manipulating the operator into wanting to shut it down, or underperforming so that shutdown looks best. So perhaps the target is indifference rather than preference: the agent should neither seek nor resist shutdown. But then I do not see how a system can be indifferent to whether it is switched off while still caring enough about its task to be useful, or why an agent that is indifferent to shutdown would not just switch itself off at random. Where does this way of setting up the problem go wrong? Is corrigibility better understood as a constraint on the agent's decision procedure rather than as a preference over outcomes, and if so, what does the constraint have to look like for an agent to be both competent at its task and reliably willing to let itself be switched off?
