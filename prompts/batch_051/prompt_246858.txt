Consider a candidate formalization of complexity. Define an object's assembly index as the minimum number of joining operations needed to build it from a small fixed set of elementary parts, where at each step you may join any two objects you have already built. The proposal adds that an object is genuinely complex only if it shows both a high assembly index and high copy number — many independently produced instances — since a one-off random object can score high on the index by accident, without any selective history. What is your credence that an account of this kind captures the ordinary notion of a complex object better than description-length measures such as Kolmogorov complexity, or disorder measures such as Shannon entropy? Please give a number and decompose it into: (i) the probability that ordinary complexity talk really is about minimal constructive history rather than compressibility or disorder; (ii) the probability that the assembly index faithfully measures minimal constructive history; (iii) the probability that the copy-number condition correctly filters out accidental high-index objects without excluding genuinely complex ones; and (iv) the probability that no rival measure does better on the cases where they pull apart, such as a one-off random string, a crystal with enormous copy number but a repeating unit, and a living cell. Show how the terms combine, state which sub-question the final credence is most sensitive to, and say what observation would move it most.
