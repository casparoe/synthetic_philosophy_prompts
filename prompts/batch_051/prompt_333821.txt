Consider Douglas Hofstadter's satirical essay A Person Paper on Purity in Language, written under the pseudonym William Satire. The device is a simple reversal: in the world of the essay, the word white does double duty as the supposedly neutral term for any human being and the word for a particular racial group, so that a cranky language columnist can complain about replacing it with person just as one might complain about replacing man with person in our own usage. I have been thinking about what this reversal actually establishes.

Please argue that the essay shows something stronger than the obvious claim that sexist language contains bias. It reveals that a group whose word for itself is also the general word for people will experience its partial term as universal, and that this experience is produced by social standing rather than by any feature of the vocabulary. Consequently, the neutral replacements that reformers propose can succeed only where the underlying default has already begun to move; without that movement, the replacements will be heard as forced, or will gradually take on the old default themselves. Make the strongest case you can for this position, showing exactly how the race-swap device supports it, while also being careful not to imply that race and sex operate identically.
