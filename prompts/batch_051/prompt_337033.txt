Last night a friend and I argued for hours about what it actually takes for a country to have “worked through” a violent past, and we ended up with far more examples than criteria. Give me a working definition of successful Vergangenheitsbewältigung — coming to terms with a past of mass atrocity — precise enough to distinguish it from neighboring activities that often get mistaken for it: official apology, public commemoration, school curricula, legal accountability, reparations, and mere continued denial.

Test the definition against these cases:

- West Germany after the war: accepted responsibility for the Holocaust, paid reparations, held trials, and made the genocide central to public memory and education. I want this to come out as the paradigm success.
- East Germany: presented itself as the heir of the anti-fascist resistance and therefore as already on the right side of history; by treating fascism as a capitalist pathology located elsewhere, it could denounce Nazism while never asking about the complicity of its own citizens.
- Japan: has issued repeated official apologies for wartime conduct and maintains a pacifist constitution, yet ministers have visited a shrine honoring convicted war criminals, and the treatment of the war in textbooks remains a recurring diplomatic crisis.
- Turkey: the state denies the Armenian genocide and has prosecuted people who publicly call it one. I take this as the clear failure.
- The American South: the Lost Cause was monumentalized in public squares for over a century, with the Confederacy treated as a noble defense of a way of life; monuments are now coming down amid fierce dispute, and there is no single “society” that could either accept or refuse responsibility.

I want the definition to say West Germany clearly counts and Turkey clearly does not; East Germany, Japan, and the American South are the cases my friend and I could not place. Propose the definition, run it through all five cases, and tell me where it surprises you or where you would revise it to make the verdicts come out right.
