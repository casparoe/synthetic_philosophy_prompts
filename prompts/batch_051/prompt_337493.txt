Two people in my department have been argueing for the better part of a year about the right way to axiomatically pin down a fair bargaining solution: one is a die-hard Nash bargainer, the other thinks Kalai–Smorodinsky is obviously the fix. They've grudgingly agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement they'd both sign. Write that document for me.

They've already agreed on the standard setup: a two-person bargaining problem, a feasible utility set, a disagreement point, and that any solution should satisfy Pareto, symmetry, and invariance to equivalent utility representations. The real fight is over which axiom to impose when the feasible set changes: the Nash person insists on independence of irrelevant alternatives; the Kalai–Smorodinsky person insists on individual monotonicity instead. Don't just restate the definitions, and don't turn this into a literature review. I want the actual document of an adversarial collaboration: claims they can both sign, written tightly enough that neither would wince; the residual disagreement phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position; and the cruxes where settling the matter would actually move one of them.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table, then a cruxes section. I suspect some of the cruxes are: whether IIA is a real consistency requirement or just a status-quo bias about what counts as a 'irrelevant' alternative; whether an expansion of the feasible set that only raises one player's best-case payoff is really a change in the bargaining situation rather than a change in the problem; and what fairness of the outcome should be sensitive to — the shape of the whole feasible set or only the alternatives the parties would actually have chosen. For each crux, give a concrete two-player case or argument both would accept as bearing on it (e.g., a set that expands in one direction while one player's best case is capped), and write down what each side would predict or argue about that case. If you end up thinking part of their dispute is just about words or about how to formalize the same intuition, say which part and why.
