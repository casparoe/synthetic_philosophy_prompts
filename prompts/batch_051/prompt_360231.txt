I'm a high school student writing an essay on the golden rule, and one issue keeps bothering me. We have been taught the rule roughly as treating others the way you would want to be treated, but that seems to collapse as soon as people want different things. For example, I would want my friends to tell me about my mistakes directly; one of my friends, however, would find that humiliating and would much rather be corrected in private. If I follow the golden rule, I correct her bluntly; if she follows it, she tiptoes around me; and we each end up doing the opposite of what the other actually wants. I have seen people online argue that the proper response is to replace the golden rule with the platinum rule, roughly, treating others the way they want to be treated. But would that not fail just as badly in cases involving children, addicts, or anyone who wants something harmful? What I want to know is this: once you notice these preference mismatches, what exactly is the golden rule good for, and is the right move to keep it, refine it, or replace it?
