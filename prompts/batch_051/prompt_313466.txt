I have been reading Popper's early remarks on Darwinism for an essay on scientific demarcation, and I have worked up a reconstruction of the argument I take him to be running there. The reconstruction troubles me: each step seems plausible on its own, but it lands in a conclusion that seems far too strong, and I cannot find the place where it overshoots.

(1) A body of claims deserves to be called science only if some conceivable observation would, were it made, count decisively against the body's central claims. (2) The central claims of Darwinism, as Popper had it in view, are that all living things form a single tree of descent with modification, and that natural selection acting on heritable variation is the principal explanation of adaptive complexity. (3) No conceivable observation would count decisively against these claims. (4) For any trait one encounters, a Darwinian can say after the fact that it is an adaptation, a by-product of selection for something else, a residue of drift, or an effect of developmental or historical constraint; and any apparently awkward case can be absorbed by positing a further selective history or an earlier change of environment. (5) The explanatory framework therefore issues no risky predictions about what traits or histories will be found. (6) So, by (1), Darwinism should be counted a heuristic or metaphysical framework rather than a science. (7) Yet exactly the same reasoning appears to apply to any inquiry that reconstructs unrepeatable past events — much of geology, cosmology, historical linguistics, and forensic reconstruction — since each shields its central commitments against refutation by adjusting auxiliary assumptions in just the same way. (8) It would be absurd to declare all those inquiries unscientific. (9) Therefore something in (1)–(7) must be given up.

I would most like to hold on to two things: the intuition behind (1), that a science must in some way stick its neck out, and the conviction that evolutionary biology is a science. I cannot see how to keep both, and I do not know whether I should reject a premise or somehow learn to accept (8). Which step goes wrong, and what would I be paying if I gave it up?
