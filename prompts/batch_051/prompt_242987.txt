So: analyze the Great Irish Famine (1845–1852) through the lens of Amartya Sen's entitlement approach to famine — his idea that famine is better understood as a failure of people's entitlements, their ability to command food through what they own, earn, or are given, than as a simple aggregate shortage of food.

Here's the episode as I understand it, so you don't need to look anything up. The potato crop, which was the staple food of the rural poor, failed repeatedly because of blight. Roughly a million people died and another million emigrated. Yet throughout the worst years Ireland kept exporting grain and livestock to Britain. The poorest laborers and cottiers didn't starve simply because there was no food on the island; they starved because they lost the ability to command the food that was there. Thier wages collapsed, thier tiny plots failed, and the relief on offer was too little, too late, and attached to workhouses and public works. London's response was shaped by laissez-faire ideas: limited relief and a strong faith that the market would sort things out.
