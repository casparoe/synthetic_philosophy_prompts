I work on the trust and safety engineering team at a mid-sized social platform. My team maintains the policy engine that triages reported posts, and several of our rules depend on intent: harassment versus a clumsy joke, a violent threat versus hyperbole, coordinated abuse versus ordinary disagreement. We process several hundred reports a day; a human reviewer gets about two minutes per case to remove, escalate, or dismiss, and errors in both directions are costly; my team owns the changes to the decision tree that guides the reviewers. I recently came across Abelard's claim that the sin lies in the consent to an act rather than in the outward act itself, and that the same outward act can be morally different depending on the intention behind it. I have no philosophy background, so I need that translated into a reusable triage procedure rather than theory. Reviewers cannot read minds; they have only the post, surrounding context, and the report. Write the procedure as a decision checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as sufficient evidence of intent at each step, where the checklist stops with a no, where it must escalate to a human or to a 'cannot determine' outcome, and where policy has to judge the outward post even when intent looks benign. Then run the procedure on three reports: an ironic death threat that is clearly a joke, a coordinated harassment campaign phrased as innocuous questions, and a user who reposts a slur to denounce it. Include at least one outcome the team will not like, and state clearly what the procedure cannot do.
