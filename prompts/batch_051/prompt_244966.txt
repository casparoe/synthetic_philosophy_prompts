I've been rereading Gödel, Escher, Bach and something won't sit right with me. The analogy Hofstadter builds is roughly this: Gödel showed a formal system can be made to talk about itself by encoding its own formulas as numbers, and that kind of self-reference is supposed to be the skeleton of a self-aware mind — an I is a strange loop. The picture seems to assume that for a system to talk about itself in the interesting way, it needs some explicit self-referential structure, a way of pointing at its own sentences or its own states. But now we have large language models. They weren't given anything like Gödel numbering or an explicit self-model; they're just trained to predict text. Yet they can refer to themselves as a model, describe their own limitations, and sometimes correct their own previous output. So does the Gödelian analogy survive that development? Is this self-reference without a strange loop, or is the loop somehow there implicitly in the training data or the objective, or was the analogy never doing real explanatory work to begin with? I'm not asking whether LLMs are conscious, just what this does to the analogy. I'd like you to identify which premise of the GEB picture LLMs actually put pressure on and whether it can be patched. And please say how confident you are in the overall verdict and flag the single point you're least sure of.
