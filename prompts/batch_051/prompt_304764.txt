Here's the thing I keep chewing on. Philosophers of mind have this problem called Hempel's dilemma for physicalism. Physicalism says everything that exists is physical. But that only means something determinate if "physical" is tied to an actual physical theory. If "physical" means "whatever current physics says exists," then physicalism is almost certainly false: current physics is incomplete and partly wrong, and some of the entities and properties it now posits will get revised or dropped the way phlogiston and the aether were. If instead "physical" means "whatever the completed, true, final physics says exists," then the claim becomes empty or undecidable, because nobody can say in advance what that final theory will include—maybe it'll grow to include things we now call nonphysical, and physicalism stops being a substantive claim. So physicalists are stuck between pinning to a buggy present and promising compatibility with an unpsecified future.
