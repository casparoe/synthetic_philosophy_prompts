Construct a thought experiment about rational choice without preference, but make it do something the standard Buridan's ass cases never do. Build a realistic case where an agent has to choose between two options that are not exactly equal in some single currency of value, so not two identical bales of hay, and are not incomparable in the sense that there is nothing to be said between them either. Instead the options should be on a par: neither is better, neither is worse, and the agent also does not see them as exactly as good as each other, yet the agent can compare them along other dimensions. Block the usual escape hatches: no cost to delaying, no randomizing device to hand, no meta-preference in the agent to get the deliberation over with, and dont just stipulate that the agent is irrational. Keep it to one paragraph and realistic, not sci-fi. I want this for a paper defending the idea that rational choice does not require a best option, so a scenario that simply stipulates the person has no preference would be cheating; the verdicts have to fall out of the structure of the case. After the scenario, walk through what a Buridan-style theorist would say the agent does, what someone who thinks choice without preference is possible would say, and identify the exact feature of the case that forces the difference between them. Then say how a defender of the side that comes out badly would attack the case, and what happens to the example if that feature is removed.
