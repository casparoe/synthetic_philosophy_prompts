Assign a precise credence to the following proposition: at least one large language model currently in public use is, for a substantial share (say, a third or more) of the tokens it emits in a typical multi-step reasoning exchange, best described as operating as a Gregorian creature in Dennett's four-rung taxonomy, rather than merely as a Popperian or sub-Popperian system.

To make the proposition self-contained, I will fix the taxonomy as follows. Darwinian creatures acquire their competences by natural selection over generations: the lineage, not the individual, tests candidates. Skinnerian creatures test candidates in the form of overt actions and retain those that are reinforced: trial and error in the world. Popperian creatures preselect candidates in an internal model, so that some hypotheses die in the head rather than in the environment. Gregorian creatures add a further step: they offload candidate representations and selection criteria into external artifacts — words, writing, diagrams, numerals, notation — and use those artifacts as working parts of the cognitive loop, so that candidates can be inspected, copied, revised, and criticized outside the nervous system.

Start with a single number for your credence. Then decompose it. Assign a rough probability to each load-bearing sub-question — at least the following, and any others you think matter:

(a) that inference-time token production includes a real act of selection among multiple candidate continuations, rather than merely sampling one continuation from a single distribution;
(b) that a model's chain of thought or scratchpad counts as an external artifact in the relevant sense, even though the model's own next-token stream is its only read-write world;
(c) that training is better analogized to Darwinian (or at most Popperian) selection, so that any Gregorian status must be earned at inference time rather than merely inherited from the optimizer;
(d) that Gregorian status requires an embodied perception-action loop over stable external traces and a persisting self that returns to those traces, and that current language models lack this;
(e) that the Popperian/Gregorian boundary is sharp enough for the proposition to have a determinate answer.

Combine the sub-estimates into the final number, showing your weighting or arithmetic. Then state which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, and specify the observation or intervention that would do the most to move it. If you regard the proposition as ill-posed, say exactly where it fails, give the nearest well-posed replacement, and assign the replacement a credence.
