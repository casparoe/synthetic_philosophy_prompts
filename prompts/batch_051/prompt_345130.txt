You are a senior philosopher of social epistemology who regularly evaluates postdoctoral research proposals in philosophy of technology. I am drafting an application for a one-year postdoc, and I want you to write the substantive proposal text for me — the question, the case for why it is open, the method, and the expected outcomes. Please exclude any bibliography or citation of specific papers; I will fill references in later.

My starting point is the idea, common in discussion of deepfakes, of an epistemic backstop. As I understand it, the thought is this: part of why public testimony remains trustworthy is that a speaker who lies about what she said or did can often be caught by a recording, and the mere availability of recordings therefore exerts pressure toward honesty. I suspect this familiar picture blends together two functions that recordings play, and that deepfakes damage those functions differently. The first function is evidentiary: when a dispute is already live, a particular recording can settle the matter. The second function is structural or accountability-related: the standing possibility of being recorded shapes what people are willing to say in the first place and which events leave a durable, checkable trace, quite apart from any particular dispute. Deepfakes undermine both, but the second seems more fragile and at the same time more important. Worse, the much-discussed liar's dividend — the ability of someone to dismiss an inconvenient real recording as fake — attacks not mainly our evidence about what happened but our confidence that any recording can serve as a common reference point at all.

My half-formed proposal is roughly this: the right response to deepfakes is less about teaching people to be skeptical of individual videos and more about rebuilding the structural function of the backstop — for example, through authentication and provenance infrastructure that lets a recording function as a shared, checkable point of reference even when its content is disputed. I want the proposal to examine whether this framing is correct, and if so what follows. In particular, please include: (1) a precise statement of the question; (2) an argument that existing work's tendency to treat the backstop as one undifferentiated thing leaves the question open; (3) a method for a philosophical project — probably combining conceptual analysis of the two functions with careful use of realistic cases from journalism and legal practice — that would make genuine progress without running empirical studies; (4) a description of the possible conclusions and the considerations that would decide between them; and (5) an honest scope note about what could make the project too large for one year. Before writing the proposal, brainstorm three or four candidate ways of framing the question, then choose and defend the one that is most tractable for a single philosopher.
