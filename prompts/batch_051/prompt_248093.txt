Make the case that pre-AGI bargains—agreements made before artificial general intelligence exists, intended to determine how AGI's powers, resources, or decision-making authority will be distributed after it arrives—are unenforceable in principle. The core of the argument should be that the parties cannot now fix terms whose content would remain determinate across the transition to a system whose capabilities and interests they cannot yet specify. Address the objection that ordinary contracts already govern deeply uncertain future contingencies by appealing to general principles rather than exact predictions, and explain why this strategy cannot be extended to the AGI case.
