I'm the student rep on my uni's speaker-panel committee, and we vote on which outside speakers get a room and a mic. We've been trying to run things on a pretty Millian line: the only reason to refuse someone a platform is harm to others, not offense and not disagreement. But every meeting we fight about what harm actually covers. Our votes so far: we let a comedian come who spends half his act mocking the majority religion's founder; loads of students were deeply offended, but nobody was threatened, so we waved it through. We blocked a guy who had published the home addresses of staff at an abortion provider and said they should be made to explain themselves in person. We allowed an economist who wants inheritance abolished entirely, even though some students said it was an attack on there families. And we blocked a group that followed a first-year around campus shouting slurs about her ethnicity and projecting her social media posts on a wall, one of them adding that they knew where she lived. Next week's proposal is a speaker who argues that trans women are men and that letting them into women's spaces is a form of male violence against women. She doesn't call on anyone to do anything and doesn't name individuals. Trans students say the talk will make them feel unsafe and unwelcome. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own votes, which older case is the actual analog, and if our record genuinely underdetermines it, what principle are we signing up for either way?
