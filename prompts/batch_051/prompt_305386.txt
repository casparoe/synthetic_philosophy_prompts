My study group has been working through an old notebook of a political philosophy professor where she wrote short 'rulings' on made-up secession cases, and we're trying to reverse-engineer her rule before we guess the next one. Her track record so far:

- Varkholm: a mountainous province with no distinct language or identity, no history of discrimination; a local militia ran an unsupervised 'vote' (52% yes among self-described Varkholmers) and declared independence, and the parent state refused consent. Ruling: no right to secede.
- Kossan: a coastal region with its own national identity that the central state spent decades repressing (language bans in schools, jailed autonomist leaders); after mediation the state consented to separation, and a monitored referendum passed 83%. Ruling: legitimate secession.
- Thessa: an island with its own language and institutions, never oppressed; the parent state negotiated in good faith and consented after a free referendum passed 80%. Ruling: legitimate secession.
- Brogavia: a distinct enclave under decades of severe repression; it held a free, monitored referendum (79% yes), but the parent state refused consent. Ruling: legitimate secession.

Next week's case is Seldane: an island with its own language and self-governing institutions, no history of oppression (the parent state is genuinely pluralist and has respected its autonomy), a free and internationally monitored referendum passes 84%, and the parent state refuses to negotiate, citing a constitutional ban on secession.

Predict her ruling. Say which theory she's implicitely running — consent of the parent state, remedial-only, primary/plebiscitary self-determination, or something else — and say which further case would settle it.
