Make the case that the line between natural persons and legal persons is itself a legal fiction — that personhood in law is always a status the law confers, not a fact the law discovers — and that a corporation is no more a "fiction" than you or me. Don't just assert that the law constructs everything; I want the specific pressure points where the natural/artificial distinction starts leaking: blame, standing to sue, being the "same" person over time, maybe whatever else you think carries the weight. Then say where fiction-talk still does real work even on your account, and where its just a way of sneaking in the claim that flesh-and-blood humans are the only real persons.
