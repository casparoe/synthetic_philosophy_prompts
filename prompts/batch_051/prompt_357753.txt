I cover mathematics and the conceptual foundations of science for a general-interest magazine, and I am drafting a piece around a puzzle I keep circling. The puzzle starts with the equation x² + 1 = 0. Its two roots, i and −i, are perfect mirror images: complex conjugation swaps them while fixing every real number, and any polynomial equation with real coefficients satisfied by one is satisfied by the other. From the equation's point of view, nothing picks out which root deserves the un-negated label, and the same moral is usually drawn for irreducible equations generally, because the Galois group carries any root to any other. Yet when I move from algebra to analysis or physics, the choice seems to acquire teeth. Contour integrals are taken clockwise or counterclockwise, winding numbers carry a sign, the right-hand rule in electromagnetism picks out an orientation, and all of this appears to presuppose a decision about which square root of minus one we have canonized as i. If the labels are pure convention, why does so much downstream machinery look sensitive to the choice? I keep hearing a deflationary answer: the individual choice does not matter, but the global consistency of the choice does; send i to −i and you must simultaneously flip every orientation-sensitive concept, after which no invariant statement changes. But I can also imagine a harder line, namely that the root-labeling question is a red herring, and the real issue is the gap between a structure and its presentations, or between an automorphism of a structure and a reinterpretation of a whole theory. Please write an essay on this controversy. Weigh the case for treating the labeling of the roots as pure convention against the case for treating it as carrying real content, and then defend a verdict rather than ending in a draw. I am especially interested in whether the slogan "it does not matter how you label the roots" is itself too coarse, and whether it should be replaced by a sharper claim about exactly what does and does not vary when the labels are permuted.
