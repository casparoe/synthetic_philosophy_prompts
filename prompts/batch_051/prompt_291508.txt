Here's where I've landed:

(3) Still, for any given mark in a fixed context, there is normally a fact of the matter about whether it counts as an 'a'; it isn't just whatever some tired reader happens to say on a Tuesday.

(5) Style and letter category are largely independent: you can hold a style fixed and swap letters, and hold a letter fixed and swap styles. So whatever makes an 'a' an 'a' has to be more abstract than any particular outline, since the outlines change with the style.

(6) I also don't think there's a single privileged platonic shape for 'a' that all real 'a's are copies or deviations from.
