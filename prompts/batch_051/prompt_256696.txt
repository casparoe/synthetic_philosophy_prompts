After a long argument with a friend about Aumann's agreement theorem, he proposed a rule and I want to know how it would actually behave if a firm adopted it. My friend's view is that since two rational Bayesians with a common prior and common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities cannot agree to disagree, any persistent disagreement inside an organization is a sign that someone is being irrational, dishonest, or sitting on private information that should be surfaced. So he proposed the following mechanism for a forecasting firm.

Analysts publish probabilities for well-defined events. The rule is pairwise and automatic: if two analysts have live, different probabilities p and q for the same event at the same moment, the system immediately overwrites both numbers with their arithmetic midpoint, m = (p + q)/2. The blended number is what appears on the dashboard, goes to clients, and determines each analyst's calibration bonus — a bonus based on how well the final posted probabilities match observed outcomes. The pre-blend numbers are stored but shown only to an internal auditor, and the two analysts are locked out of editing that forecast for 24 hours after a blend. Analysts know all of this in advance.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a debate about whether disagreement is rational. Analyze how it would play out: what an analyst is incentivized to post given what they actually believe and what they expect the other analyst to post; whether the posted numbers converge, polarize, or turn into something disconnected from anyone's belief; how the final blended numbers relate to the truth; and how analysts would game it — by shading toward extremes, colluding to post identical numbers, timing their posts to dodge the rule, or otherwise. Then say what the scheme selects for among analysts and among the forecasts that survive to the dashboard, what second-order effects it would have on the firm's information flow, and whether the failure modes are fixable with small modifications. Also identify which condition of the Aumann result the mechanism is implicitly leaning on but cannot itself enforce.

Throughout, say how confident you are in each major claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
