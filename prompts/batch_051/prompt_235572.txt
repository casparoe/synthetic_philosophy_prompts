I am writing an essay on whether a neural interface could ever give a person a genuinely new quale—a sensory experience whose felt character is not merely unfamiliar but different in kind from anything human beings have had before. I cannot settle what a genuinely new kind of sensory experience would have to be, and I want a working definition that sorts the following cases consistently.

I am fairly confident about two negative verdicts, and I want the definition to deliver them without simply building them in. A synesthete who involuntarily experiences an ordinary blue whenever she hears middle C is not having a new kind of experience; she is having a familiar experience under an unusual trigger. Likewise, a person who has spent her life in a black-and-white room despite knowing every physical fact about color vision, and who then sees something red for the first time, is not having a new kind of experience; she is only having, for the first time herself, a kind of experience that others already have. On the positive side, suppose a neural implant directly activates visual cortex to produce a color-like quality that is stable, learnable, and unlike any hue ordinary vision has supplied; I am inclined to count that as a genuinely new kind of experience.

The cases I cannot decide about are these. First, a person with four kinds of cone receptor rather than three can reliably distinguish two greenish surfaces that everyone around her sees as identical; she says one of them looks like a color she has no ordinary name for. Is that a new kind of experience, or only a finer-grained difference within an old kind? Second, under special viewing conditions, a subject in a color experiment reports seeing a single color that is red and green at once, not as a patchwork or a flicker; some call this a forbidden color. Third, a blind person wears a head-mounted camera whose image is converted into a grid of pressure patterns on her tongue; after months of training she can navigate a cluttered room and says it almost feels as though she is seeing it.

Please propose a definition, test it against each case, and say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. If the phrase a genuinely new kind of sensory experience is doing more than one job, separate the senses and say which cases move between them. If the definition forces a surprising verdict, tell me whether that is a reason to revise the definition or to accept the verdict.
