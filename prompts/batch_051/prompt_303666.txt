After a career in the physical sciences, I have been trying to make precise something that bothers me about how science education talks about misconceptions, and I want it checked before I lean on it. I call it regime stripping. The proposal is this: many persistent student errors are not false theories but correct local models whose boundary conditions have been lost. When a student says heavier bodies fall faster, they are retaining a relation that is true for terminal velocity in a resistive medium and dropping the medium. When a student says plants take their bulk from the soil, they are overextending the true fact that the soil supplies the plant's mineral fraction. When a student says constant motion requires constant force, they are describing exactly what is true of motion against friction or drag, where a steady force does sustain a steady velocity. On this view the mistake is not in the retained regularity but in the severed scope; instruction should restore the boundary condition and enlarge the model, not replace the idea. The label misconception would then be aimed at the wrong unit.

I am not asking for a literature review. I am asking whether the idea survives. Give me the strongest cases against it: persistent errors that are not a correct local regularity released from its scope, and tell me whether I have said anything more than that students overgeneralize, or whether the cases that fit my description are exactly the easy ones while the interesting errors lie elsewhere.
