I'm a journalist who covers AI and the arts, and I've been trying to pin down what I actually think about creativity by recording my gut verdicts on concrete cases. A painter who spends months on an original composition, making thousands of deliberate choices: clearly creative. A photographer who pulls over, points at a sunset, and presses the shutter with no planning: creative, but only faintly; the image feels more found than made. An illustrator who writes a long, detailed text-to-image prompt, generates several hundred images, selects one, and then reworks it digitally for hours: creative, with the credit belonging to the illustrator, though the model did something in the process that I can't quite name. A tourist who types 'a dragon in the style of a famous living illustrator' into an app and posts the output untouched: not creative — and the wrongness feels like theft from the illustrator rather than a mere mistake about who was creative. A scientist who builds a generative system to explore protein structures and gets back an elegant, surprising structure she didn't anticipate and couldn't have designed by hand: creative — but I'm unsure whether the word belongs to the scientist, the system, or the process. A gallery installation in which a model trained only on public-domain works keeps producing images, with no prompts from visitors, that critics call genuinely novel in style: I want to say the images are creative even though no human deserves credit for them.

Is there a principle that captures all of these, or nearly all of them? What does it say about a case I keep going back and forth on — a novelist who writes the plot, characters, and dialogue but has a language model draft every sentence, which she then edits line by line? State the principle precisely, test it against one further case of your own devising, say which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, if any, and end with a final line beginning 'Principle:' that states it in a single sentence.
