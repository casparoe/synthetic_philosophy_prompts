Two friends of mine got into a long argument at a party last weekend that I only half followed, and I ended up flip-floping whenever I tried to referee. Dana: When king − man + woman lands almost on queen, and Paris − France + Italy lands near Rome, that isn't a trick. The space is carving real semantic joints — gender, capital-city, comparative, tense — they all show up as directions. Meaning genuinely has geometry.

Eli: All it shows is that the model compresses distributional patterns. Words that appear in the same slots get pulled into the same neighborhoods. Dana: But distributional patterns are exactly the evidence a human learner has, too. Eli: It's not "noise," but it's not meaning either. And where a real semantics should be clean, the space is a mess. "Bank" is one point, even though river banks and financial banks barely overlap semantically. Trained vectors are anisotropic — they bunch into a narrow cone — so cosine similarity ends up inflated and nearest neighbors include garbage. You can bolt on machinery to correct all that, but that just shows the raw geometry wasn't doing the semantic work.

Dana: A map doesn't have to be perfect to be a map. And the systematic distortion is itself informative: the he–she direction lines up with occupation terms in pretty stereotyped ways. That's geometry tracking real structure in usage and social life, not random error.

Eli: Tracking usage isn't tracking meaning. And if you admit the space encodes bias and stereotype, you can't also claim its directions carve semantic joints. What would it even be approximating — some pre-existing "true meaning space"? At that point you're smuggling in the thing you were trying to prove.

Dana: I don't need a pre-existing true-meaning space. I'm saying the best observable proxy for meaning is the structure of how words pattern, and embeddings operationalize that. Your alternative is to call all of it an artifact — but then it's one hell of a coincidence that the artifact transfers across corpora, languages, and even into other modalities.
