I am a high school student, and I have been reading about Morgan's Canon in comparative psychology. As I understand it, the canon says roughly that we should not interpret an animal behavior as the result of a higher psychological faculty if it can be adequately interpreted as the result of a lower one. I am having difficulty understanding what carries the normative force in this principle. If the goal is simply to avoid unnecessary complexity, then Morgan's Canon seems like an ordinary application of parsimony. But if the goal is to avoid attributing human-like mental capacities too readily to animals, then the canon seems to presuppose that human-like capacities are the ones we should be most reluctant to attribute. Are these two goals equivalent, or can they come apart? Could there be a case in which the most parsimonious explanation of an animal behavior would actually attribute a higher psychological faculty, and if so, would Morgan's Canon recommend a different explanation than ordinary parsimony would? I would appreciate an explanation of how the canon is supposed to guide reasoning about animal minds without simply assuming in advance which capacities are more or less complex.
