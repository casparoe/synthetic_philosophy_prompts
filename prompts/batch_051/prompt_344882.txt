I've been working through the Machamer–Darden–Craver account of mechanistic explanation in biology, and I built the following argument in my notes. It now strikes me as proving too much, because the conclusion seems to imply that no mechanistic explanation has ever been complete — which is hard to square with how useful mechanistic explanation is in actual biology. But each premise still looks plausible to me when I read it in isolation.

(1) A mechanistic explanation explains a phenomenon by decomposing it into entities and activities that are organized so as to produce that phenomenon.

(2) For any activity cited in a mechanistic explanation, one can ask how that activity itself is produced; this question is, in principle, a request for a further mechanistic explanation.

(3) A mechanistic explanation is complete only if every activity it cites is either itself decomposed or is treated as a legitimate bottoming-out point.

(4) In biology, there are no absolutely bottom-level activities: any activity, whether enzyme catalysis, conformational change, or ion transport, can be further decomposed into lower-level entities and activities.

(5) Therefore, whether an explanation bottoms out is a matter of where we choose to stop asking questions, not a feature of the world.

(6) If that is right, then mechanistic explanation's completeness is interest-relative, and the account cannot support the claim that mechanistic explanations carve nature at its joints.

(7) But the Machamer–Darden–Craver account is supposed to be an ontic account: it says explanations describe real mechanisms in the world, not merely ways of organizing our ignorance.

(8) So the account faces a dilemma: either no actual mechanistic explanation is ever complete, or completeness is relative to our interests, which undercuts the ontic ambition.

I'd rather keep (1), (2), and (7), but I doubt I can keep all of them. Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? If I have to reject a premise, which one is least costly to give up, and what does giving it up do to the rest of the account? As you answer, please separate what is settled among people working on mechanistic explanation from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
