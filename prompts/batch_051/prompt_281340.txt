I've been nursing a homemade theory I call feed-through causation. Picture a sealed, frictionless box full of trillions of tiny magnetized bearings — call them simms — that jostle under local collision rules. At a coarse grain you can spot a stable blob that drifts toward the warmer side of the box. Nearly everyone says the blob is just a high-level summary; the only real pushes are ball-on-ball, bottom-up, and the blob's "heading for warmth" is just a symbol we project onto the system. My claim: attach a coarse-grained sensor on the outside that detects the blob's position and switches a heater on the cold side, and now the blob's being-on-the-left, as a coarse fact, feeds back and changes the very substrate that realizes it. The ball-level causal story can't even state that loop without borrowing coarse vocabulary. So symbols (or at least coarse patterns) really can push their substrate around. I know this sounds like I'm reinventing downward causation or Dennett's real patterns — first tell me whether this already has a name, then give me the three strongest objections and say whether any of them is fatal rather than just deflating.
