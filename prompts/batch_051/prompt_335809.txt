Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Ilona and Dev, on whether prosperity has moral worth beyond what it buys. Ilona defends the view that abundance can be good in itself, not merely as a stock of means: a life that overflows its needs may be better, not only for the extra consumption it permits, but because plenty can instantiate achievement, freedom, or a kind of fittingness. Dev defends the view that every bit of value in prosperity is value delivered through what it buys in the broadest sense—welfare, security, health, comfort, social standing, and the keeping-open of future choices—and that once these are credited there is no remainder that could count as the worth of abundance itself.

Make the dialogue concrete rather than programmatic. Have them work through test cases such as a granary whose surplus goes uneaten, or a person who sacrifices for options she knows she will never exercise. At least once, push the disagreement to a surplus that is never used, never drawn on for security, and never even contemplated, so the standard instrumental rationales cannot get a grip, and see what each character does with it. Both should be serious interlocutors: Ilona must not fall back on awe at plenty, and Dev must not equate instrumental value with trivial value. End with them locating the precise point at which their disagreement becomes irreducibly normative, and what each would have to be shown in order to concede.
