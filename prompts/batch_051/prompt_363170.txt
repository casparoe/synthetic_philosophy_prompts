Suppose Thomas Hobbes were presented, today, with the proposal of a pre-AGI bargain: an agreement made before artificial general intelligence exists, intended to govern what happens after it arrives. Examples include agreements among rival AI developers to halt training before certain capability thresholds, treaties among governments to share the benefits of AGI or to keep it under collective control, and unilateral commitments to subject future training runs to outside inspection. The peculiarity of such bargains is that they are concluded now, while the costs and temptations of defection are still remote, but they must be honored later, when one party may be vastly stronger than the others and when no existing authority may be able to compel compliance.

Hobbes argued in Leviathan that a covenant is reliable only when there is a common power to enforce it, and that agreements without such a power rest on little more than fear and hope. He also held that a person may be artificial rather than natural, and that one can authorize another to act in one's name.

Given these commitments, reconstruct the position Hobbes would most plausibly defend about pre-AGI bargains. Would he count them as genuine covenants at all, or as something weaker? Which feature of the pre-AGI case would he find most destabilizing—the delay between promise and performance, the likely inequality of future power, the absence of an enforcer, or the fact that the eventual counterparty might be an artificial agent that cannot consent now? What recommendation would follow? In particular, might he argue that the only stable pre-AGI bargain is one that first establishes a common power over advanced AI, a kind of Leviathan over compute, and would he predict that such a power can actually be created? Finally, say where his framework of covenants, authorization, and artificial persons would be strained when the aim is to bind an agent that does not yet exist. Reason from the conceptual machinery of Leviathan itself rather than from later Hobbes scholarship.
