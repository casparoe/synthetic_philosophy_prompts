Here's a counterfactual I want you to reason through. In 1979, a research group publishes a regularity it calls a law of history: in every one of the 70 cases they can find, when a state's urban population passes 60% while the richest fifth of landowners hold over half the arable land, a revolution starts within fifteen years. The proposed mechanism is plausible, and over the next decade the pattern keeps replicating even in countries never studied. By 1990, people treat it as roughly as solid as plate tectonics. Now, Popper said there cannot be laws of history, partly because people learn them and act on them, and partly because historical wholes are unique and not experimentally isolable. Does this discovery refute him, or does what happens after 1990 vindicate him? Walk me through the next forty years: does the regularity survive once its widely known, what do governments, revolutionaries, and political scientists do with it, and where are you confident versus guessing? What would have to be true for the discovery to change almost nothing?
