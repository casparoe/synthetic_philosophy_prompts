I am teaching a graduate seminar next term on metaphor, and near the end I will defend a Davidsonian position: metaphorical utterances have no special cognitive content beyond their literal meaning, and the distinctive force of a metaphor is a causal effect on the hearer rather than the communication of a further message. I will anchor the session in the passage in Davidson’s essay What Metaphors Mean where he compares metaphor’s operation to a picture or a bump on the head, and denies that the metaphor itself carries a cognitive content that it delivers to the audience. Against this, I will set the conceptual metaphor tradition, which treats metaphor as a surface effect of underlying cross-domain conceptual mappings that are real structures in cognition.

My colleague in psychology says this is not purely conceptual: she wants an empirical test that would pull the two views apart. I am skeptical that any test will not simply operationalize one side’s picture, but I agree it is worth trying. Draft exactly five numbered test proposals, each exactly two sentences long. The first sentence should specify the concrete manipulation or observation and what Davidson’s view and a conceptual metaphor theorist would predict. The second sentence should say which result would support which side, and one residual question the test would not settle. Do not include an introduction or conclusion.
