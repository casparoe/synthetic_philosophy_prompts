This spring I oversaw the scrapping of a discontinued product line at the firm where I work: several hundred thousand dollars' worth of components, already paid for, sent to be broken up. The write-down appeared in our accounts as a loss, and I found that I shared the intuition without being able to defend it. Since then I have been bothered by an asymmetry. If those same components had been destroyed by a fire, we would call the world poorer and name a dollar figure, even if an insurer had made our balance sheet whole. But if we had simply refrained from producing them in the first place, or if we had left the line idle for the same months and never made the goods it could have turned out, no one would record a loss, and no one would say the world was poorer. In both cases there are goods that could have served human purposes and did not; the only difference is whether the goods passed through existence on the way to being unused. What, if anything, justifies treating the destruction of wealth as a genuine setback while treating the non-production of the same wealth as no setback at all? Does abundance have value only once it reaches someone, or can prosperity be worth something beyond what it buys in wages, goods, services, and insurance payouts? If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against this pairing: the scrapped components were fully insured and would have sat unused in the warehouse anyway, while the idle line could have produced identical components at identical cost. Should the asymmetry be kept, or given up?
