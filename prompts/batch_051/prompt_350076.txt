My philosophy of science class covered this and it's been turning in circles. The reading gave an argument that seems to prove no false theory is closer to the truth than any other false theory, and I can't find where it runs of the rails. Here it is:

(1) Some false theories are obviously closer to the truth than other false theories. Newtonian mechanics is false—general relativity corrected it—but it's clearly closer to the truth than the theory that the planets are carried around the Earth on crystal spheres.

(2) If "closer to the truth" means anything, it has to cash out in terms of the truths a theory entails and the falsehoods it entails.

(3) Popper's definition does that: theory A is closer to the truth than theory B just in case either (i) A entails strictly more truths than B and no more falsehoods than B, or (ii) A entails strictly fewer falsehoods than B and no fewer truths than B.

(4) Now take any two false theories, A and B.

(5) Suppose A entails strictly more truths than B. Since A is false, it entails at least one falsehood f. Pick a truth t that A entails and B doesn't. Then A entails t ∧ f, which is false; and B can't entail t ∧ f, because then it would entail t. So A has a false consequence B lacks, and the "no more falsehoods" half of (3)(i) is dead.

(6) Mirror case: if A entails strictly fewer falsehoods than B, there's a false f that B entails and A doesn't. Since A is false, it entails some false g. Now g → f is true, B entails it (because B entails f), but A doesn't (if A did, A would get f from g by modus ponens). So A misses a truth B has, and the "no fewer truths" half of (3)(ii) is dead too.

(7) So any gain in truths comes with a new falsehood, and any cut in falsehoods costs you a truth. Popper's two clauses can never both go the right way for two false theories.

(8) So on Popper's definition, no false theory is closer to the truth than any other false theory. Newton is no closer to the truth than "the planets ride crystal spheres."

(9) But that's nuts—(1) just has to be right. So the trouble is in (2) or (3), and I can't see which.

Please walk me through it: is the construction in (5) (and its mirror in (6)) actually valid? Which premise would you reject? If I want to keep (1) and (2), what would a workable account of "closer to the truth" have to look like, and what exactly does giving up Popper's definition cost?
