I'm in a historical-sociology reading group and we've spent the last two months going round in circles on Max Weber's Protestant ethic thesis. The setup, as I understand it from The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, is that the Reformation — especially the Calvinist anxiety about whether you were among the elect — produced an inner-worldly asceticism in which disciplined work and profit came to feel like a moral duty, and this spirit, this habit of treating moneymaking as a calling, was around before modern capitalism's economic machinery and was one of the things that helped bring it into being, not just an effect of it. Our group has split into three camps. The economic historians keep bringing counterexamples and measurements: Catholic Antwerp and the Italian city-states were commercially sophisticated without much of this ethic, Protestant regions differed from Catholic ones in literacy and schooling which might be the real mechanism, and the timing doesn't match in places like Scotland or Hungary. The Weberians answer that these tests are a category error, because Weber was offering an ideal-type about meanings and elective affinities, not a variable to be plugged into a regression. Then a third camp says the entire fight is what you get when a discipline still hasn't made up its mind whether a culture or value-system can be a cause at all, or is just downstream of material interests.

My working hypothesis is that the debate has stalled because the sides mean different things by cause and are answering different questions: the historians are asking what tippped a pre-existing material process over the edge, while the Weberians are asking what made people experience moneymaking as a calling rather than a necessary evil. But I'm not confident about that. It could be that there is a real empirical question hiding in there and the evidence is just too coarse to settle it, or that both camps are circling a bedrock disagreement about materialism versus idealism that nobody can argue their way out of. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict on who's right. Is it verbal, empirical, a dispute about standards of evidence or about what counts as data, a disagreement about which question is worth asking, or something else? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a particular sort of evidence, a conceptual distinction, a formal model, or an honest acceptance that the thesis shouldn't be asked to do causal work?

After the prose diagnosis, give me a short JSON object with these fields: diagnosis (one paragraph, the best characterization of what the disagreement actually is), kind (the closest label, e.g. verbal, empirical, methodological, axiological), hidden_empirical_question (the testable question the debate might be smuggling in, or null), conceptual_issue (the key distinction that would clarify things, or null), what_counts_as_progress (one to two sentences), and confidence (one of low, medium, high).
