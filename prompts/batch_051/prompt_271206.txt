I am preparing a handout for an undergraduate political theory course on trust and democratic institutions, and I would like a "Which position on trust in government do you actually hold?" sorting quiz with a scoring key, for the unit in which we contrast the Nordic high-trust model of public administration with the American tradition of constitutional suspicion and checks and balances. The quiz should sort people by how they respond to concrete cases rather than by which label they like, so no question should simply ask whether the respondent trusts government or which named position sounds most appealing.

Please write eight to ten questions, each built around a short scenario with two or three answer options that express a practical stance. At least these three scenarios should appear: (1) a public-health agency asks residents to adopt a contact-tracing app during a respiratory outbreak, with uptake formally voluntary but described as effective only if nearly everyone participates; (2) a city considers allowing front-line staff discretion to waive a fee for residents facing genuine hardship instead of applying a rigid eligibility rule to everyone; (3) an independent audit finds that a major public infrastructure project ran late and over budget through poor management but without corruption, and the state must decide what should change for the next project. Then provide a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto at least these positions: default high trust (trust in public institutions is the appropriate baseline), Madisonian constitutional suspicion (institutions should be designed on the assumption that power will be abused and suspicion is a civic virtue), earned or conditional trust (trust must be continuously earned through competent performance, transparency, and reversibility), republican non-domination (the central question is not trust but whether power is arbitrary, contestable, and constrained by law), and principled distrust (government is structurally captured or coercive, so suspicion is the correct standing attitude).

Build the instrument from the logical space first: identify which combinations of answers are possible, locate the named positions within that space, and explicitly flag any pairs the quiz cannot readily separate, such as earned trust and republican non-domination if they answer the audit and sunset-clause questions alike and differ mainly on whether trust is even the right category to discuss. Finally, note any coherent but rarely occupied regions of the answer space and say in a sentence or two whether each is there because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored in our course readings.
