In a lab meeting, a colleague ended a debate about three research groups independently noticing the same rare pattern within a month by saying "the plural of anecdote is not data." I'm fairly sure that slogan is doing more work than it can handle, because the birthday paradox shows that striking coincidences can be exactly what a chance process predictably produces once enough opportunities are in play. Give me a menu of the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making, ordered from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, say whether it is true, false, or true only under conditions, and what accepting it would commit someone to. Then say which reading, if any, actually licenses treating the cluster of anecdotes as no evidence at all, and which readings the birthday-paradox point defeats.
