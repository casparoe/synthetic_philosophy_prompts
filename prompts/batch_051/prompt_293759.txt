I've been trying to sort out what I actually mean by 'violence', because every time someone says the world is getting less violent I suspect they're smuggling in a definition that quietly does all the work. So here are my gut calls on cases, and I want the simplest principle that captures them, or close.

- A man punches a stranger in a pub car park: violence, definitely.
- A soldier shoots an enemy soldier in combat: violence, yes.
- A police officer kneels on a suspect's neck until he dies: violence, and I don't care that it was 'procedure'.
- A government keeps sanctions on a country for years knowing children will starve: I want to call that violence, the slow kind.
- A factory owner dumps waste in a river and a village downstream gets poisened: I keep going back and forth; I'd probably count it.
- An employer cuts a worker's hours so they can't afford insulin and die: I don't think I count that as violence. Awful, but it reads as exploitation or neglect.
- A driver texting kills a pedestrian: I don't want to call it violence. Manslaughter, maybe.
- A teacher screams at a pupil every day until the pupil self-harms: violence, even though no one was touched.
- A person cuts their own arm: not violence; but a guard ordering a prisoner to cut themselves: violence.
- A lion kills a gazelle: no. Nature isn't violence.

The rough principle I've been carrying round is that violence is deliberate, non-consensual bodily harm by one person to another. But that gets the sanctions case wrong, and the teacher case, and it makes the factory poisoning not violence, which I'm not settled about.

So: find the simplest principle that fits most of these, tell me which of my verdicts it overturns and which pair it has the hardest time keeping together. Then tell me what the principle does to the question whether the world is becoming less violent. If the principle still leaves 'violence' vague at the edges, what does that mean for whether there's a fact of the matter about the trend? I'd also like to know whether the trend answer flips if you count per-capita versus absolute deaths, and whether that choice is part of the definition problem or a separate statistical one. And what does the principle say about a case I haven't listed, e.g. deaths from a factory's emissions causing respiratory disease in a nearby town, or a government delaying a pandemic lockdown?

Oh, and write the answer in British English.
