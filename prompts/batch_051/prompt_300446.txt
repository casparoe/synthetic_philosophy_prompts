I'm a few years into a political philosophy PhD and keep going around in circles on luck egalitarianism — the idea that inequalities are just only when they trace to choices, so bad brute luck (born with a disability, hit by a tornado) calls for compensation and bad option luck (gambling your savings, choosing a risky career) doesn't. I have gut verdicts on a bunch of cases but no confidence that they add up to one principle, and I'd like you to help me figure out what I actually think, or which verdict I should drop.

Here's where my instincts land. A kid born with a serious congenital condition gets full support, no question. Someone who blows his paycheck on online poker and loses it gets nothing. Someone whose retirement savings are wiped out by a sudden currency collapse should be compensated — he couldn't have foreseen that — but someone who put everything into an obviously sketchy crypto scheme shouldn't. A person who gradually cultivates a taste for rare wines and then says he needs extra money to reach the same welfare as before: no. A person who needs a very expensive special diet because of a metabolic condition he didn't choose: I find myself wanting to say yes, which bothers me because the expense is the same kind of thing as the wine. The one that really trips me up is the motorcyclist who rides without a helmet, crashes, and needs emergency brain surgery. By my own pattern that's textbook option luck and he's on his own. But I cant get myself to say we leave him on the curb. I want to say he gets urgent care but not, say, lifetime income support.

Is there a single principle — maybe a luck-egalitarian core with a basic-needs or urgency caveat — that captures all of this? And what does it predict for the case I keep going back and forth on: a family that moves into a flood plain because it's the only place they can afford, half-aware of the risk, and then loses their house in a flood. Do we rebuild for them, and under which clause does that fall? If my verdicts can't all be kept, tell me which one to give up and why.
