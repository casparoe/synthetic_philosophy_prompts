Suppose Tolsoy had lived long enough to see the postwar arguments among historians about whether Nazi Germany could have happened without Hitler. In War and Peace he's always going on about how Napoleon and Tsar Alexander were basically figureheads, that the real causes of events are millions of tiny decisions no one controls, and that historians just fit a story over the mess afterwards. So, concretely: what would he say about the functionalist-vs-intentionalist fight from the 70s and 80s? Would he say the system would have produced something similar with a different person on top, or would he have to admit that Hitler's particular race obsession and the push east did real independent work? And if you pressed him, would his position collapse into something unfalsifiable, or is there a way it could be tested? I'm not after the standard great-man-vs-structures slogans. I want the specific mechanics of his determinism, and exactly where it would creak under this case.
