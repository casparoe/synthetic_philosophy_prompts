I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been trying to formalize my views on cryonics so a discussion group can poke at them. I'd like a consistency check before I present. I've numbered the commitments below. I keep going back and forth about whether they all fit together, and I can't tell where the real strain is.

1. I think signing up for cryonics is rational for me. The cost is a life-insurance premium I can afford, and even on a very low probability of revival the expected value comes out positive.

2. I'd put the probability of actually being revived at maybe 1–5%, with most of the failure probability coming from organizational and institutional discontinuities over the intervening centuries, not from the revival technology itself.

3. I hold a physicalist view of personal identity: what matters for my surviving is that the right information-processing structure in my brain is preserved and continued. The particular atoms don't matter.

4. I think this has a consequence I'm willing to accept: a sufficiently faithful emulation or reconstruction of that structure would count as a continuation of me, not merely a copy of me.

5. Nonetheless, I treat cryonics as an attempt to repair and restart my original brain, and I assign that a much higher chance of success than emulating me from external records. The brain stores synapse-level and molecular detail that no diary, archive, or behavioral trace could preserve.

6. I think the standard objections misfire. Cryonics is not a lottery ticket, because a lottery ticket has negative expected value while cryonics does not. And it is not a Pascal's mugging, because the mugging only works if you accept an unbounded payoff chosen by an adversary, whereas cryonics has a bounded payoff — a finite extra lifespan — via a concrete physical mechanism.

7. But I also accept the mirror image of my own reasoning: the same expected-value logic commits me to taking small probabilities of enormous harm seriously, such as existential risks, and I do in fact give to organizations working on those risks.

8. I am confident that I want to extend this one life rather than merely instantiate my pattern. I would count revival as a success only if there is a single continuing thread, not if the procedure produced several parallel continuations of me.

Go through the numbered list and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the revision costs. I'd rather be told that I face a hard choice between commitments than have every pair labeled equally problematic.
