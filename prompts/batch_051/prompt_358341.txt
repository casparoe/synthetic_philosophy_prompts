ok so i cover science and ideas for a living, and after one too many public dust-ups about what someone "actually meant," ive realized my own commitments about meaning are kind of a mess. i'd like a consistency check before i try to hang a piece on them. heres roughly where ive landed:

(1) in ordinary cases, what a text or utterance means is tied to what its author or speaker intended by it. intention isnt just evidence we use to guess at meaning; its partly constitutive of it.

(2) but i also think texts can mean things the author didn't intend and couldn't have anticipated. the classic case: a 19th-century naturalist writes in a way that carries assumptions about race or gender that are genuinely in the text even if he wasn't thinking about them. i dont want to give that up.

(3) as someone who edits interviews, i treat my job as preserving the speaker's intended meaning. i clean up the ums and false starts, but id be misrepresenting them if i made their words say what i think they should have said. so my practice assumes intended meaning is a thing you can recover and be faithful to.

(4) for legal texts, though, im convinced the right view is something like textualism: meaning is fixed by the ordinary public meaning at the time of enactment, not by the private intentions of the drafters. i dont think a legislator's secret plan should control what a statute means.

(5) i also have a strong anti-deferential streak: an author's later gloss on their own work, like a novelist saying what the book "really meant," has no special authority for interpretation. its just another reading, maybe less reliable than the text itself.

(6) finally, im drawn to the idea that a text's meaning can drift as a community receives it — the same words can come to mean something different decades later — but i dont want to say anything goes; some readings are just wrong.

some of these feel like theyre pulling against each other and some probably only look that way, and i genuinely cant tell which is which. can you rank the actual tensions by severity, say which pairs are only apparent and why they dissolve, and tell me what the least costly revision is and what it would cost me? when you do, please seperate what's basically settled from what's actively contested among people who study interpretation, language, and legal meaning, and dont present a contested view as the consensus.
