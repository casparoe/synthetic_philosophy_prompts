Here's a dispute I want to drag out of the armchair and see whether any evidence could touch it. The Humean about laws of nature says a law is just part of the best overall summary of everything that actually happens: the universe is a big mosaic of particular facts, and laws are the patterns we compress it into. The anti-Humean says no, laws are something over and above the facts — they govern the facts, and things happen the way they do because the laws push them that way; the law would still have held even if some of the actual facts had been different. The two camps can apparently account for the same actual history, so it's usually treated as an empirical tie.

I want you to try to break the tie, or at least make it less of a tie. Design a test — an experiment, an observation we could look for, a simulation, whatever you like — that would genuinely distinguish these two pictures, not just change the subject. Be concrete: what exactly do you manipulate or measure, what result would push toward the anti-Humean/governing picture, and what result would push toward the Humean/summary picture?

Then, and this is the part I really want, say what your test would not settle even if it comes out cleanly one way. For example, would a pro-governing result show the laws really exercise some kind of power, or only that we can't make sense of our own scientific practice without treating them that way? Would a pro-summary result show the governance talk is empty, or only that it's empirically dispensible? Don't wave this off with 'it's just a matter of interpretation' — I want a precise statement of the residue.
