I host a podcast on rational choice and conflict, and next week's guest is a political scientist who applies Fearon's rationalist explanations for war to AI development races — both between labs and between states. Her line is that the three mechanisms (private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibility) explain why rational actors still race toward dangerous frontier systems even when a coordinated pause would leave everyone better off. She's sharp and has stock replys to the obvious pushback. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the framework. Make sure at least one question presses each of these, and for each one add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give: (1) whether an AI race is even the right kind of event for a bargaining model, or is really just market competition with a race-to-the-bottom flavor; (2) whether commitment problems carry over to firms that operate under enforceable contract law rather than anarchy; (3) whether there's any genuinely indivisible stake here, given that equity, licensing, and profit-sharing are available side payments; (4) what exactly the private information is and why labs can't credibly signal safety progress; (5) what observation would show the framework is just a loose metaphor rather than an explanation.
