so i just got out of a three-hour argument with a friend who kept saying words dont have meanings, only uses, and ive been chewing on a compromise ever since. i think hes wrong but the usual replies feel too fast.

my idea, wich im calling a words error surface, is this: the meaning of a word isnt a definition and isnt a flat list of uses either. for any word W and any candidate application, there is a pretty stable fact about how wrong a competent speaker in some community would judge that application to be. dog applied to a wolf is a little wrong, to a hyena more wrong, to a car very wrong, to the number seven wrong in a different register, more like a category error. the meaning of dog is that whole graded landscape, with the central cases at the bottom. polysemy is what you get when the landscape has more than one well: bank has two dips that are each completely fine, and a ridge between them where the weird mixed sentences live, like "the bank eroded and closed at 3", which only half-works because each verb reaches for a different dip. synonyms would be words whose error surfaces nearly coincide, which is why perfect synonyms basically dont exist.

what i like is that this lets me say yes, words do have meanings, because an error surface is a real community-level object that isnt any single persons idiolect, while still granting my friends point that meaning is graded and fuzzy at the edges and not something you can pin down from the armchair. so: is my error surface just prototype theory with a normative layer, or use theory with meanings smuggled back in? does it already exist under some name ive never heard? and where does it actually break — with systematic polysemy like healthy in healthy food vs healthy person, with names and function words, or with a community split on whether some use is acceptable? give me the two or three strongest objections and say plainly which are fatal and which are just uncomfortable.
