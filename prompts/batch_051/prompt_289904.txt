I'm a software engineer, and there's a guy at work who's my go-to for weird questions about computation. He has no formal philosophy background, but he has strong opinions about when a system can be "shortcut": when you can compute its state at step N directly instead of stepping through all N updates. He talks about it as the difference between a real explanation that compresses the computation and just running a faster simulation. I've been collecting his verdicts because I can't quite reverse-engineer the principle underneath them. He says Rule 30 (the one-dimensional cellular automaton with the random-looking triangular output) can't be shortcut, you simply have to run it. He says Rule 90 can be shortcut, and once showed me the Pascal's-triangle-mod-2 trick for getting arbitrary cells without computing earlier rows. He says a linear congruential generator with known constants is completely shortcuttable, since you can jump ahead with linear algebra. He says iterated SHA-256 on a fixed-size state is not shortcuttable, and that any shortcut would amount to breaking the hash. And on a double pendulum he said no: running a finer integrator is just a better simulation, not an explanation—the best you can get is statistical.

The new case I want to ask him about but haven't is a one-dimensional traffic model: cells on a ring, each empty or holding a car, and every tick each car moves one cell right iff the cell to its right is empty. Deterministic, local, simple—a lot like the cellular automata above. But it isn't linear the way Rule 90 or the LCG is, and there's a well-known way to redescribe it in terms of particles and holes shifting around rather than per-cell car updates. I'm trying to predict whether he'll say you can shortcut this system after N ticks (at least aggregate stuff like average speed or the eventual periodic pattern) or whether you have to step through all N ticks.

More than the yes/no, I want to know what rule he seems to be running, what other rules fit his past verdicts just as well, which of his earlier cases is the closest analog for the traffic model, and what follow-up case I could hit him with that would best distinguish the candidate rules. I'm less interested in weather the traffic system is actually shortcuttable than in why his pattern of verdicts would push him one way or the other.
