Consider the claim at the center of the conceivability argument: a being physically and functionally identical to us but with no phenomenal consciousness is genuinely conceivable, and its conceivability is evidence that physicalism is false. Much of that looks conceptual, but some of it seems empirically reachable — for instance, whether the intuition survives after subjects are walked through cases like blindsight, where a capacity is preserved while the associated experience is lost, or whether subjects' own judgments show they take conscious experience to do causal work that the zombie scenario denies. Propose the strongest test, or a small set of tests, of these reachable parts: specify the design and manipulations, the pattern of results that would count for and against the reliability of the intuition, and then say exactly what a clean result would and would not settle about the modal premise and the anti-physicalist conclusion. I want the boundary marked explicitly: where can data undermine the argument by undermining the intuition's reliability, and where is the residual question one that no experiment could touch?
