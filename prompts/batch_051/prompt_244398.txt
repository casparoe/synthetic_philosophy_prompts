I'm designing an ungraded instrument for an introductory philosophy of mind course: a quiz called "Which theory of beliefs and desires do you actually hold?" I want it to sort students by their answers to concrete cases rather than by which label flatters them, and I want the instrument built out of the logical space rather than assembled from a reading list.

Write an eight-to-ten-question quiz about propositional attitudes. Each question should present a concrete case and two or three answer options; none may ask respondents to endorse a named theory or state its metaphysics in the abstract. Cases must include: a thermostat; a chess engine whose commentary says it wants to control the center; someone who sincerely avows a belief they never act on; a blindsight subject who denies visual awareness while guessing above chance; a differently organized alien species that behaves indistinguishably from us; and a completed future neuroscience that predicts and explains behavior without ever using belief/desire vocabulary.

Then supply a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto five leaves: eliminative materialism, reductive physicalism about attitudes, functionalism, interpretationism, and instrumentalism/fictionalism about attitude-talk. Lay out the distinguishing questions first, then the combinations of answers the instrument permits, then locate each position at its cell. Mark any cell with no occupant and say whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Finally, flag any two positions your quiz cannot separate with behavioral cases alone, and specify a further question that would separate them.

Keep the whole thing self-contained and usable offline: no citations, reading lists, or footnotes, and do not rely on facts that would need to be looked up.
