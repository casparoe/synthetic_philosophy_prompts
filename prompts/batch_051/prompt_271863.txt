I have no academic training, but I have spent several years sitting with Dōgen's idea of being-time (uji), at least as I understand it: time is not a separate container that moments pass through, and each being and each moment is itself time, complete exactly where it is rather than a mere step toward some later payoff. I have been asked to give the commencement address at a small rural high school. The room will be full of students, parents, and teachers, and the occasion will naturally push everyone toward the usual language about futures, goals, and making the most of one's time. Please write the address so that this idea of being-time does the real structural work: it should shape what the speech can and cannot say, and it should not turn into a lecture against ambition or planning or against the value of what the students have accomplished. It should never mention Dōgen, Buddhism, or the term being-time, and it should still land for an audience that has never heard of him.
