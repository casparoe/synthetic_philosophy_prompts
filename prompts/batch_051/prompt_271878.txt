I'm not a philosopher or anything, just a piano teacher at a community music school, and I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about sad music because some students asked me and I realized I couldn't give a straight answer. So here's roughly where I've landed after thinking about it on my own over the last few months, and I'd like someone to tell me if it all hangs together:

(1) A piece of music can be sad even if the person who wrote it wasn't sad, and even if no one listening right now feels sad.
(2) The sadness is really there in the music, not just in the listeners' heads or in the lyrics or the title.
(3) When we call music "sad," we don't mean the same thing as when we call a person sad; its a different use of the word.
(4) Slow tempo, falling lines, and minor keys are part of what makes music sad — they aren't just arbitrary associations we picked up from movies or culture.
(5) If someone from a very different musical culture heard the same piece, they might not find it sad, and I don't think there's a fact of the matter about which of us is right.
(6) A purely instrumental piece with no lyrics, no title, and no known composer can be sad.

I'd like to know which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are. Please rank the tensions by severity, say what each tension would force me to give up, and tell me which single change would clean things up the most. Don't just say they're all fine or all inconsistent; I've gone back and forth on this alot and I want to see exactly where the problems are.
