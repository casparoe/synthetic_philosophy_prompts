The distinction between cranes and skyhooks, and the accompanying charge of greedy reductionism, is usually treated as a critical verdict on an explanation rather than as something one could test. I want to know whether that is correct. Consider this claim: a colleague proposes that an apology is nothing but a costly signal that repairs the apologizer's standing as a cooperative partner after a transgression, and insists that this is a good reduction — a crane that does genuine explanatory work at its proper level — rather than a greedy reduction that simply discards the phenomenon of remorse, and rather than a skyhook that invokes an unexplained capacity for reputation management. Design the strongest test you can of the claim that this account is a good reduction rather than a greedy one or a skyhook. Specify what would be manipulated or measured and what pattern of results would favor each of the three classifications. Then say what such a test would not settle even if it came out in favor of the account: for example, whether the phenomenon of apology survives the reduction as real, whether a nothing-but claim about a meaningful human practice can be literally true, and whether calling a reduction greedy is an empirical finding or a demand that more intermediate levels be supplied.
