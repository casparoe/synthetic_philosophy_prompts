Plutarch opens his Life of Timoleon (in the Parallel Lives) with a confession: "IT was for the sake of others that I first commenced writing biographies; but I find myself proceeding and attaching myself to it for my own; the virtues of these great men serving me as a sort of looking-glass, in which I may see how to adjust and adorn my own life." A bit later he says his method is "to habituate my memory to receive and retain images of the best and worthiest characters," so he can avoid "ignoble, base, or vicious impressions." That's about as open a statement of the edifying-biography program as you could ask for: he is not trying to be a neutral source, he is using selected lives as moral equipment.

Here's the asymmetry that bugs me. When we read that preface in a Great Books course, it gets treated as wisdom about how reading lives can form character. Fine. But if a modern writer said the same thing in an authors note—say, a book on a tech founder or a president where the writer admitted she had deliberately kept only the best and worthiest material because the aim is moral improvement, not the record, and she wanted readers to come away elevated rather than fully informed—most people would call it a whitewash, or at least PR. We'd say she buried the vices. But it looks like the same practice: openly selecting the noble parts of a life for moral effect, with the base bits left out on principle. We treat the ancient one as edifying and the modern one as dishonest. Is there a defensible difference between the two, or is it mostly that Plutarch has been dead for 1900 years and gets a pass? If you defend the asymmetry, give me the strongest principle you can (genre, distance, consent, the subject's being public property, the reader's expectations—whatever) and test it against a case where the modern writer is exactly as explicit as Plutarch about what she is doing. If you can't defend it, tell me which side we should drop: the reverence for Plutarch-style moral biography, or the automatic suspicion of modern edifying biography. I want an honest verdict, not a both-sides shrug.
