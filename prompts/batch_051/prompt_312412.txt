I spent my career as a research chemist and have only recently begun reading moral and legal philosophy, and I keep tripping over one question: whether the passage of time is itself a reason to stop holding someone accountable, or whether it is only ever a proxy for other considerations such as decaying evidence, changed identity, fading deterrence, or society's need for finality. Here are the criteria I have distilled from standard defences of statutes of limitations for an offence's continuing moral eligibility for punishment: (1) reliable evidence remains available for an accurate verdict; (2) the offender is continuous in identity with the person who acted; (3) identifiable victims still carry the harm; (4) punishing would still serve some preventive or deterrent point; (5) social reconciliation and finality carry some weight. Now consider this case. In 1970 a junior accountant falsified a pension fund's records and diverted a large sum; the fraud was documented in a complete, notarised paper trail that sat sealed in a private archive and was discovered only last month, when the archive was digitised. The evidence is as good as in a recent prosecution. The offender is alive, fully competent, and psychologically continuous with the 1970 agent; he has otherwise lived a law-abiding life. Several victims are still alive, and their retirements were permanently diminished in ways that still affect their daily lives. He is now old, and specific deterrence is weak, but the victims say the demand for accountability has not faded. The limitation period expired decades ago. Please work through the criteria one by one and say whether this is a case where the wrong remains morally eligible for punishment. In particular, say whether time itself is doing any independent work in your answer, or whether the criteria already capture everything time matters for — and, if not, which criterion should be changed and how. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
