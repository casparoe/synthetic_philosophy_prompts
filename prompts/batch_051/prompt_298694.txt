I've been kicking around a homegrown idea I'm calling semantic hysteresis. The claim: a word's meaning at a time just is a probability distribution over its next uses, given the trajectory of recent uses. Every extended use adds a bit of 'charge' to the network; if enough of it builds up, it calcifies into a new sense, and if not, it decays. Metaphor is just the zone where the charge is high but hasn't calcified yet. So asking whether 'healthy' in 'healthy diet' and 'healthy person' is one meaning or two is like asking whether a lump of clay is a bowl or a sculpture before its fired. I like that it handles dead metaphors (the leg of a table used to be charged, now it's just literal), speaker-level polysemy (email is one thing to me, a mess of uses to my mom), and maybe even zeugma. But maybe I'm just re-describing prototype theory plus frequency effects, or a sloppy version of dynamic semantics. Give me your honest read: what's the strongest version of this, what existing view is it really closest to, and where does it break — coercion, one-off coinages, terms with a stable technical core, something else?
