Many disputes about whether the world is becoming less violent turn on what gets counted as violence, so I want a working definition of 'violence' — not a dictionary gloss, but a criterion of application I could use to code cases the way a social scientist or historian would. Please propose one, then run it against all twelve of the following cases:

1. A mugger knocks someone down and takes their wallet; the victim is physically unhurt but is shaken for months afterward.
2. A professional boxer strikes an opponent during a sanctioned bout.
3. A surgeon cuts into an anaesthetized patient during a life-saving appendectomy.
4. A government cuts a food-subsidy program, and over three years child malnutrition rises and several thousand children die — though no official ever touched any of them.
5. A power plant emits particulates within legal limits, contributing to respiratory deaths distributed across a region over several decades.
6. A coordinated cyberbullying campaign drives a teenager to suicide.
7. A dog kills and eats a rabbit.
8. An earthquake collapses a building and kills the people inside it.
9. A parent spanks a child once, leaving no mark.
10. A factory owner knowingly disables a safety device to save money, and a worker is later killed by the machine.
11. A stranger screams a stream of racial slurs at a person on the street but never threatens physical harm.
12. A protester smashes the window of a bank branch at night when no one is inside.

I am confident that (1) is violence and that (8) is not. I lean toward counting (4), (5), and (10) as violence, though I do not fully trust that leaning, and I lean toward not counting (2), (3), and (6). On (7), (9), (11), and (12) I have no settled view. Propose the definition, apply it to all twelve cases, and say where it clashes with my leanings, where it forces you to revise either the definition or my verdicts, and which of the twelve classifications you would defend least. Ideally, the definition should not already smuggle in an answer to the question of whether the twenty-first century is less violent than the past; it should make that question more tractable rather than settle it by definition.
