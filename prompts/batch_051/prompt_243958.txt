I have been trying to make precise a claim about low-impact AI — the proposal that AI systems should avoid side effects even when their objectives are narrowly specified — and the following argument is the strongest version I can produce. Tell me whether it is valid, and if not, exactly which step fails and under what reading.

(1) A side effect of an action is a change the action causes beyond the changes it was meant to cause.
(2) An agent can avoid side effects only if it can distinguish the changes it is meant to cause from the rest.
(3) The only way to encode that distinction in an objective function is to build in a specification of which effects count as intended, and such a specification is already a specification of value.
(4) A natural alternative — penalizing deviation from a "do nothing" baseline — does not escape this: choosing the baseline and a measure of deviation requires deciding which differences matter.
(5) Deciding which differences matter is itself a value judgment.
(6) Therefore, "avoid side effects" cannot be defined without a full specification of value; the project of low-impact AI is either value-laden all the way down or empty.

Am I right that this goes through, or is there a step where it overreaches? Be specific about the step and the reading of it.
