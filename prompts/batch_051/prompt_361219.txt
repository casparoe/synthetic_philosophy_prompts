For my rational choice course I want a fresh case with the structure of the familiar self-torturer—small steps, each one tempting on its own, ending in a state the person never would have chosen up front—but set in an ordinary graduate student's week rather than the usual pain-for-pay setup. Build me a sequence of four or five states. The specs: each step from one state to the next looks, to the student at the time she takes it, like a strict improvement; the final state is one she would have refused if it had been offered to her as a single package at the start; each early step is reversable at small cost, but once later steps depend on it, backing out is costly; and there's no torture, no money, and no addictive substances. After you lay out the scenario, display the pairwise preferences that generate the cycle, and then tell me whether the resulting ranking is genuinely intransitive or whether the student's standard for acceptable is drifting across steps, so she isn't really the same evaluator at each point. Take a side: could a rational person move through this sequence without any particular step being irrational? Finish by saying how a money-pump operator would exploit her, whether the repair you'd use to block the pump would also dissolve the pain-for-pay original, and which single feature of your case is doing the real work.
