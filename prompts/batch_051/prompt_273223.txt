In my novel there is a long sequence in which a woman hurries across a city at dusk while conducting, in her head, a bitter argument with her sister; she steps off curbs, threads through pedestrians, catches the strap of a bag slipping from her shoulder, and twice changes course around a broken paving stone, all without the argument loosening its hold on her attention. While drafting the scene I realized I was describing a character who is, in some important sense, not steering her body — and yet the body is not running on blind reflex either, because it responds to novel details (a puddle of unexpected depth, a cyclist veering the wrong way) with a kind of rapid, accurate, unsummoned intelligence. I have since noticed the same thing in a pianist I know, whose hands find the next chord while she is listening for a wrongness in the voicing, and who, when the bench is nudged, shifts her weight to recover before she has consciously registered the contact.

I want to know how to think about this, and whether there is an established concept for it. I am not looking only for a label; I want the carving. Is this one capacity or several? Is it a form of intention, a form of perception, a form of memory — or something anterior to that division? Where is the line between this and ordinary deliberate action on one side, and reflex or involuntary movement on the other? Does it depend on skill and repetition, or is it present in everyone? I am especially interested in the cases where it fails or misleads, and in what those cases show about its edges: the way vertigo or a single glass of wine changes it, the way it can lag behind an injury, the way it sometimes solves a practical problem before the person can say what the problem was.

I ask because, as a writer, I need to render this accurately and not merely project a folk psychology onto my characters; I would like, in effect, a brief taxonomy of the phenomenon and its nearest neighbours, with the distinctions that matter drawn explicitly, and an honest verdict on which technical term, if any, fits what I have described.
