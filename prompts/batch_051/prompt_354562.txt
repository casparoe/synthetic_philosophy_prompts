Write the joint statement that a periodization realist and a periodization constructivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real historical units or retrospective inventions.

Define the two positions carefully enough that each side would accept the wording of its own view. Then produce:

(1) A numbered list of claims both sides can accept, including claims about the retrospective coinage and institutionalization of the period labels, about the observable continuities and discontinuities in the relevant centuries, and about what a period label must do to earn its keep as an instrument of historical understanding.

(2) A disagreements table with a column for the claim at issue, a column for the realist's formulation, and a column for the constructivist's formulation, each phrased so that its own side would sign it.

(3) A section identifying the cruxes: the sub-questions such that a settled answer to them would move at least one side. I suspect two of the cruxes are whether the absence of sharp temporal boundaries is evidence against real periods, as opposed to being compatible with vague but nevertheless real units such as mountain ranges; and whether the fact that labels like 'the Middle Ages' and 'the Renaissance' were coined retrospectively for partly polemical or self-congratulatory purposes impugns their referential adequacy. Identify these suspected cruxes precisely and add at least one further crux of your own, and for each crux state which side would move if it were settled in which direction.

(4) For each crux, a concrete case or body of evidence that both sides agree bears on it — for example, earlier 'renaissances' such as the Carolingian or twelfth-century ones, the historians' dispute over where the Scientific Revolution begins and ends, or the strained application of these labels to non-European histories — with each side's prediction about what that case will show, recorded in advance.

Format the deliverable as a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the crux-by-crux section. As the very last line of your response, write a one-sentence summary of the core residual disagreement.
