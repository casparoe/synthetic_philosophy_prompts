I have no academic training in philosophy, but for some years I have been turning over an idea I call the ledger view of consciousness, and I would be grateful for an honest assessment of it. My starting point is that nothing becomes conscious simply by being a mental event. There is a difference between a seeing that merely goes on inside me and a seeing that is something I have, and crossing that difference requires a second act of taking stock. I picture the mind as a shop with a clerk who keeps a ledger. A first-order event — the sight of the road, a toothache, the smell of rain — is not consciously experienced until the clerk enters it. The entry itself does not need to be entered anywhere else; it does its work in the dark, so to speak, because if every record required a further record we would have an endless stack of ledgers and no actual experience. So the state that makes an experience conscious is itself usually unconscious.

The view seems to me to explain things that otherwise puzzle me. I first began thinking this way after driving home from a night shift and arriving with no memory of the journey: the seeing went on, but the ledger was not being written, and on my view the journey was not conscious in the ordinary sense. I also think the clerk can be wrong. A ledger entry is, in effect, a claim the mind makes to itself, and claims can be false or premature; this is how I would explain why people are sometimes unduly sure that they were aware of something they could not have seen, or give confident explanations for choices they did not actually make. I should add that the view does not drain the colour from red: the entry adds no colour, it only confers the standing of being an experience that is mine rather than an event that merely occurs in me. I am aware the picture sounds strange, and I particularly worry that it may make infants and animals not conscious at all, since a clerk that writes entries sounds as if it would need words. So I would like to know: is this a genuine position, has it already been worked out under a better name, what does it get right, and where does it break? Please set out the strongest objections you can and say whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable. Please write your answer in British English.
