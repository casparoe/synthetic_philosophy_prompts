ok so we read this essay in class, Langdon Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?", and the example everyone remembers is the Robert Moses one. The way Winner tells it, Moses built the overpasses on the Long Island parkways deliberately low so buses couldnt pass under them, and that ended up keeping people who had to rely on transit (mostly poorer and Black people) from getting out to Jones Beach, while people with cars could go. So the bridge itself is doing an exclusion: no law says "no buses", but the steel does the same work.

I want to see what happens to that argument if the bridge is not fixed. Suppose the overpasses are adjustable: a timer and a small motor can change the clearance. From 6am to 9pm (beach hours) the clearance is nine feet, so the old buses dont fit. From 9pm to 6am it raises to fourteen feet, so literally anything can pass. Same bridge, same steel, just a setting that changes on a schedule.

Now think about three versions:

A. Moses picks that schedule exactly because he wants to keep bus riders off the beach during the day. Intent and effect line up.
B. A traffic engineer later picks the same schedule using a model that only minimizes car delay, and nobody ever enters bus data into it. The effect is identical to A, but there is no discriminatory intent anywhere.
C. The bridge always stays at fourteen feet, but a sensor in the same structure runs a dynamic toll: during beach hours, any vehicle taller than ten feet gets charged an enormous toll, which prices buses off the parkway.

For each version I want you to say whether the "politics is built into the artifact" camp and the "politics is in how the thing is used/interpreted" camp would actually give different answers, or whether the adjustable design makes them collapse into each other. Does the exclusion being stored as a setting inside the machine make the politics more clearly part of the artifact, or does it make the politics seem less built-in because the machine could just as easily do the opposite? In B, if you say the bridge does not have politics, do you have to say A doesnt either, since the physical object and the schedule are exactly the same? Or can two physically identical machines have different politics because of the history or intent behind the settings? And in C, is a toll that is enforced by a sensor and a billing algorithm part of the artifact in the same way low steel is, or is it more like a law that happens to be enforced by a machine?
