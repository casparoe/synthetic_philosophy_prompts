I'm writing a paper on the ethics of AI-generated persons and digital impersonation, and I keep returning to a phrase that Daniel Dennett made prominent in his late writing on AI: counterfeit people. As I understand his worry, the central danger is not that some future machine will become conscious or seize power, but that we can already manufacture artifacts that engage the social machinery we normally reserve for real persons, and that producing them at scale tends to degrade the trust and accountability practices that let us navigate the social world. I want to turn that worry into a usable concept rather than a slogan.

Give me a working definition of a counterfeit person — precise enough that a platform designer or a judge could apply it, not just a rough gloss — and test it against each of the following cases: a deepfake video of a living politician saying something the politician never said, posted without any disclosure; a chatbot with an invented name and personality that is plainly labeled as an AI; a digital double of a deceased actor used with the estate's permission in a new film, with the recreation disclosed in the credits; a hand puppet in a children's theater performance; a customer-service bot that uses a human name and first-person language and is not identified as a bot; an AI-generated virtual influencer on Instagram who has no human behind the account but is presented as a real person; a satirical deepfake of a celebrity placed in an obviously absurd scenario; an oil portrait of a person; a hologram of a historical figure delivering a scripted museum talk after being introduced as a hologram; and an AI replica of a specific deceased person sold to a grieving family member who knows exactly what it is. For each case, say whether the definition classifies it as a counterfeit person, a clear non-case, or somewhere in between, and explain which feature of the case does the classificatory work. Then identify which of your verdicts you are least confident about, and either revise the definition to handle that case or defend the surprising verdict. Please write the answer in American English.
