My coworker and I argue about politics at lunch sometimes, and he just sent me this version of the moral foundations theory stuff (the idea that people have built-in moral tastebuds — care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, and depending who you ask, liberty — and that left vs right is mostly a difference in which ones get weighted: liberals lean on care and fairness, conservatives spread across all of them). His argument:

(1) The theory treats each foundation as its own evolved channel of moral information, and it doesn't rank any channel as better than the others.
(2) If an outlook draws on more channels, it takes in more moral information — like a sensor with more bands misses less.
(3) Conservative moral psychology draws on more of the foundations than liberal moral psychology does.
(4) An outlook that takes in more moral information is more likely to get moral questions right.
(5) So the left/right psychology actually does tell us who's right: it gives conservative morality the edge.

I cant find the bug, but I keep ending up just not liking the conclusion, which feels like cheating. I'd hate to give up (1)-(3) because they seem to track the research, and I definitely dont want (5). Where should I actually push? Which step do you reject, and what does it cost? Or is there no bug and I have to live with (5)?
