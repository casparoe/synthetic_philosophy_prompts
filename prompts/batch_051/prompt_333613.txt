Im doing a 25-minute talk next month at a rationalist meetup — mostly Slate Star Codex readers, a couple of recovering libertarians, all of them pretty convinced that open argument beats status games — and I'm defending the thesis that honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures are not morally rankable in the abstract: each is an equilibrium for enforcing norms under a different social information environment, and victimhood culture in particular is a defensible response to low-trust, third-party-adjudicated social media rather than just a bug. Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the six or so objections this crowd will actually raise, roughly in the order they'll come, phrashed the way they'd put it (not how a sociologist would), each with a two minute reply, plus what to concede up front, where each reply is soft, and the one objection I'm hoping nobody asks. My quick argument sketch for them to target: dignity culture assumes people can and will confront each other directly and that bystanders mostly stay out of it; honor culture works when reputation is the main deterrent because enforcement institutions are weak; victimhood culture works when third parties, especially online audiences, reward visible harm and adjudicate disputes at scale. So "morally superior" has to be relative to which enforcement problem a society actually has.
