In my ethics seminar someone kept invoking Occam's razor to settle a debate between act-utilitarianism and a Ross-style pluralism. His line was: sure, the Ross view fits more of our moral intuitions, but it needs a whole list of prima facie duties plus some story about how to weigh them and a pile of ad hoc exceptions, so occams razor says go with the one-principle theory. I want to know what "Occam's razor" could even mean when it gets imported from science into ethical theory, because it feels like he's doing something illicit but I can't pin down which step. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making in that context — parsimony as evidence, as a tie-breaker, as a pragmatic bookkeeping virtue, and anything else you see. For each reading, tell me whether it's a truism, a substantive and defensible principle, or just false. Then say which readings, if any, actually license the move from "fewer principles" to "so we should accept utilitarianism as a moral theory." And seperate out whether simplicity of a moral theory has any evidential force at all, or whether it's at most something like ease of teaching, applying, and agreeing on the theory.
