Something's been bugging me about the Great Divergence debate—the old argument about why industrialisation took off in England rather than in China, with the Needham question sitting next to it. The two sides don't seem to move: one side keeps saying the question is malformed, because England and China weren't in the same starting position (institutions, coal, empire, wage levels), and asking why one went first is like asking why this particular lottery ticket won. The other side says that's a cop-out: once you allow arbitrarily many differences you can explain anything after the fact, and the whole point of the comparison is to work out which differences were actual difference-makers. My hunch is the argument hasn't been empirical for a while—most of the facts aren't really disputed—and it's now a fight about what a historical explanation is supposed to do. Diagnose this: what kind of disagreement is it, and what would actually count as progress (a smaller comparison, an explicit counterfactual, new evidence, or just changing the question)? And tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis, and which part of your answer you're least sure of.
