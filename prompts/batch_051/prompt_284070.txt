Ok here's the thing that's been bugging me. I'm not an academic, just somebody who reads around, but I can't stop thinking about this one.

People get really worked up about the idea of giving an AI "legal personhood." Say a self-driving car rear-ends someone, or an AI system signs a contract, or somebody says an AI should own the copyright to its own work. People act like the whole idea is absurd: a computer isn't a person, it wasn't born, it has no feelings, no body, no childhood, so how could it be a person in the eyes of the law?

But those very same people dont bat an eye at the fact that a corporation is already a legal person. A corporation can sign contracts, own property, sue and be sued, and in the US it even has free speech rights. And a corporation has no feelings, no body, no consciousness — it's literally a stack of paperwork. We made a "person" out of thin air centuries ago because it was useful, and nobody treats that as a metaphysical scandal.

So the asymmetry is this: the entity that actually is a little bit like a person (an AI that can act, make decisions, maybe one day feel something) is the one we refuse personhood for, while the entity that is obviously not a person (a company) has had "person" status so long we forget it's a fiction. That seems backwards to me. If personhood in law is just a bundle of jobs — who can be sued, who can own things, who is on the hook — then why should it track having a soul or whatever? And if it does have to track those deep things, why do corporations pass the test?

Act as a professional philosopher who works on legal theory. Please think carefully: start by brainstorming the three or four strongest principles that might justify treating corporations and AI differently, then pick the one you think is actually best and stress-test it against the hardest cases you can think of. Then give me an honest verdict. Should we extend legal personhood to AI, take it away from corporations, or neither? I dont want a summary of the law; I want to know whether the law's line is actually a good one.
