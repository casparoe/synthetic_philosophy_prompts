I keep going back and forth on abandoned software and orphaned works. The usual moral argument for not using someone's creative work without permission runs something like: there's an owner, they made it or paid for it, and they deserve control and compensation, so using it without asking or paying is wrong. But with abandonware and orphan works, there's no one to ask, no one to pay, and often no one who even remembers the thing exists — yet copyright lasts for decades. Does that argument actually survive once the owner is unfindable or gone, or does it quietly depend on there being a live person who can show up and be compensated? Which premise does the orphan/abandoned case put under real pressure, and is there a weaker repaired version that still works — for example, preserve but don't exploit, or wait some period? Or does the honest answer collapse into: it's permissible until someone claims it?
