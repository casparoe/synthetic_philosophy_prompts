I've been keeping track of my coworker Dana's calls on which monitoring tools our startup rolls out are fine and which are gross. She never states the rule, she just rules on cases, but she's weirdly consistent. Her track record so far:

- self-reported timesheets: fine
- a team calendar where everyone can see everyone else's meetings: fine
- the build dashboard that shows whose commit broke main: fine
- code review done out in the open on GitHub: fine
- anonymized per-ticket time stats, no names attached: fine
- software that screenshots your machine every ten minutes and sends it to our manager — she called that one a one-way mirror: not fine
- a badge that logs when you swipe into and out of the building, reported to HR: not fine
- a leaderboard of individual PR review times that only the lead can see: not fine

I have one hunch about the pattern — something about whether the person being watched can watch the watcher back, or whether the data is about the work rather than the worker — but I can never get her to agree to a formulation.

Now they want to add a bot that sits in our standup, listens to the call, and every week posts a "meeting health" scoreboard to the team channel. It lists each person by name with their total talk time and how often they interrupted someone. Everyone on the team can see it, managers included; it's not hiddden from anyone, and we're told it's for team health, not performance reviews.

What would Dana say about this one? I care less about the yes/no than the rule. What rule is she actually running?
