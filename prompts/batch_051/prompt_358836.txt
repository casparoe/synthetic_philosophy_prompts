I can't figure out why the Taurek-style argument about saving the many has to stop at a coin flip. The way I understand it: if you can save either one person or five strangers, the numbers don't aggregate, so you shouldn't automatically save the five; you should flip a coin. But why not run a weighted lottery instead? Pick one of the six people at random and save that person's group. The single person's group has a 1-in-6 chance of being saved, the five's group has a 5-in-6 chance, and each of the six had an equal 1-in-6 chance of being the one picked. That seems like it gives everyone equal concern and has the nice side effect that the five usually get saved. So why wouldn't the Taurek line of thought permit—or even require—that? Am I messing up what equal concern has to equalize here?
