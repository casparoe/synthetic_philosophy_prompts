In the arguments I keep having about deepfakes, the people who are most alarmed lean on a picture I want you to stress-test. They say an audio or video recording has long served as society's epistemic backstop: a kind of eyewitness that cannot be bribed, intimidated, flattered, or made to forget, so when testimony conflicts we can play the recording and settle what happened. From this they conclude that once convincing deepfakes arrive, the backstop is gone and we lose the only neutral referee we had, which means shared factual ground collapses. Treat that as an analogy and audit how far it holds. First work out the mapping in full: what corresponds to the witness, the act of testifying, cross-examination, the jury, and the scene observed. Then identify where the mapping breaks, which features of the witness picture get quietly imported into the recording case, and whether those disanalogies are load-bearing for the collapse conclusion or merely cosmetic. If a better analogy suggests itself, say what it is. Approach this as an expert philosopher of epistemology who works on testimony and technological change, think carefully, and before the systematic audit briefly brainstorm a few candidate mappings and stress-tests. End with a final line beginning Verdict: followed by a short phrase stating how far the analogy holds and whether the conclusion survives.
