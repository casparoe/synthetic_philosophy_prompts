Whitehead argued that the worst fault of education is the accumulation of inert ideas, by which he meant ideas received into the mind without being used, tested, or thrown into fresh combinations, and he held that genuine learning should move through a rhythm of romance (free and excited exploration), precision (disciplined mastery of particulars), and generalization (putting the particulars back into active use). He was writing when information was scarce, slow to obtain, and largely controlled by teachers. A student today can hold a device that answers factual questions instantly, explains any procedure, and produces a passable essay on demand, so information is no longer scarce and the teacher is no longer the only gatekeeper. Does Whitehead's argument against inert ideas still go through under those changed conditions, does it need repair, or does it point somewhere new?
