I'm a high school student reading about Newton's bucket on my own, and I'm trying to work out what position my intuitions actually add up to about space and motion. Here are my verdicts on five thought experiments.

Bucket. When the bucket first starts turning and the water stays flat, I think the water is not really rotating, even though it's turning relative to the bucket. When the water catches up and turns with the bucket, its surface goes concave, and I think it really is rotating then, even though it is now at rest relative to the bucket. So the concavity tracks something other than the water–bucket relation.

Two globes. Two globes joined by a cord revolve around their common center in an otherwise empty universe. I think the cord would be under tension, and that tension would show the two-globe system is really rotating, even though the system as a whole has no other object to rotate relative to.

One globe. A single globe in an otherwise empty universe: I lean toward saying there is no fact of the matter about whether it is spinning. But this sits awkwardly with the two-globe case, since it says adding a second object creates a fact that wasn't there before.

Shifted world. If the entire universe were moved five feet to the left with all distances preserved, I think nothing would really change; it would just be the same world described in a different coordinate system. But I can't say why that doesn't also make the bucket's rotation a merely relative matter.

Rotating universe. If everything in the universe, stars included, began rotating together around the Earth, I am genuinely torn. I suspect water in a bucket on Earth would stay flat, because nothing changes relative to the rest of the universe; but I worry my verdict on the bucket commits me to saying it would go concave.

Is there one coherent position about whether space itself is real that captures all of these verdicts, or nearly all? If not, which of my verdicts should I abandon, and why? Please also state what the resulting position predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, a bucket rotating in a universe with exactly one distant star — and where it draws the line between real rotation and merely relative motion.
