I run a small mealworm farm, and every cycle we raise and kill insects by the millions, and lately I've been worrying about whether what we do to them matters at all. I'm not an academic, but Ive picked up the rough idea that even if each individual bug is only a little bit likely to feel something like pain, the sheer number of them means you shouldn't just wave it off. I keep getting little proposals, like switching to CO2 before grinding instead of grinding them live, or giving them more space per tray, and right now I decide each one by gut feel, which is all over the place. I'd like a reusable decision procedure I can actually follow every time one of these lands on my desk: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops and says no, and where it should stop and hand the decision to me because it's a values call rather than a facts call. Time is tight, I've got maybe an afternoon per decision, and the owner wants to see the added cost before she signs off. Then run the procedure on two cases: switching from live grinding to CO2 stunning, and cutting stocking density by a third to reduce crowding and cannibalism.
