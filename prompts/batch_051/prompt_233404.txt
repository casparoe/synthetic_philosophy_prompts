I teach moral philosophy and sit on the ethics committee for a group of care homes, and the ward managers keep asking me to turn care ethics into a decision procedure they can actually use at two in the morning. They review a case a week on average and the decisions are high stakes: a resident with moderate dementia is refusing food and her daughter wants us to feed her anyway, a resident wants to go home though the team think he can't manage, things like that. The people running it would have never read a word of the theory. I know care ethics is supposed to resist exactly this kind of codification — the whole point is that you judge form the concrete relationship rather than a rule — but I need something to hand them. Please write the procedure care ethics would recommend if it had to be written down: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure should stop with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because no procedure can settle it. Also mark which steps the shift leader can decide alone and which have to go to the committee for sign-off. Then run the procedure on the two cases above and on a third where it tells us to do something the committee will not like. Be honest about which steps are just judgement calls dressed up as procedure.
