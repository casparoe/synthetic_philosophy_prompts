I'm taking a democratic theory course and we've covered compulsory voting and are now on who should get the vote — lowering the voting age and local voting rights for non-citizens. I've noticed I have a habit when I evaluate arguments against extending the franchise. I ask whether the same reason was given against an earlier expansion that we now treat as obviously right (ending property qualifications, women's suffrage), and if it was, I basically discount the argument to near zero. Last week a classmate argued that non-citizen residents shouldn't vote in local elections because they'd be too uninformed, and I wrote it off because competence arguments were used against women and poor men too. My friend says that's a genetic fallacy: an argument isn't wrong just because similar arguments were wrong before, and non-citizens might actually be relevantly different. Is my shortcut defensable? Is there a version of it that keeps whatever genuine debunking power it has without making "used before, therefore bad" a rule?
