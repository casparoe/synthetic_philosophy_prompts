My freind in our political philosophy class explained Locke on property to me like this: in the state of nature, everything is owned in common, but you own your own body and your labour. So when you work on some unowned thing — picking acorns, clearing a field — your labour gets mixed into it and that makes it your private property. Locke says this is only ok as long as you leave enough, and as good, for other people. My friend then said that this proviso quietly makes the whole theory self-defeating, because as soon as good land gets at all scarce no taking of it can literally leave as much and as good behind, so on Locke's own terms all actual private property turns out illegitimate. Is my friend's summary fair? Please go through it and tell me which parts track Locke, which parts are oversimplified or subtly misread, and whether the final conclusion actually follows.
