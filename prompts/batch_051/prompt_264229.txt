A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about how value is built up from smaller pieces, and I came away wanting the requirements list before I hear one more proposal. His opening formula was that the value of a single experience is its consciousness multiplied by its happiness — roughly, how much sentience is present times how well that sentience is going — and that the value of a whole state of affairs is the sum of those products. I offered one case after another in which the formula appeared to misfire, and he kept amending it, until neither of us could say which of our objections were genuine constraints on any theory of value combination and which were just our own preferences for a certain kind of clean arithmetic.

The cases that came up, all unresolved, included: whether a life lived exactly at the neutral point — neither worth living nor not worth living — contributes zero, so that a billion exactly-neutral lives and an empty world come out equivalent; whether an eternal life of mild suffering is infinitely bad just because duration multiplies a negative term; why duration and intensity should multiply within a subject while distinct subjects are added together; whether the very same combination rule should govern within a single life and across lives; how the rule is supposed to interact with uncertainty, given that the expected value of a gamble over intensities need not equal the value of the expected intensity once the value function is curved; and whether some harms are lexically worse than any amount of mild goods, and if so, where the boundary lies without making it arbitrary.

So before I read any more theories of value combination — additive, multiplicative, lexical, or some hybrid — write me the desiderata. What must any adequate theory of how the components of value aggregate into overall value actually deliver? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. Attend especially to the distinctions a theory cannot collapse: duration versus intensity versus number of subjects; combination within a single life versus across lives; the neutral point versus zero value. Say whether you think any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the familiar candidate theories, and why. Keep the weight on the criteria themselves rather than on scoring the existing views; I want a standard for the next time someone hands me a formula.
