A friend and I stayed up late last night arguing about Morgan's canon — roughly, that we should not explain an animal's behavior by appeal to higher psychological processes when lower-level ones would suffice — and we kept talking past each other. I came away convinced our real disagreement was about where thought begins. Could you give me a working definition of "thought" (or "cognition," if you prefer) that gets the following cases right: a dog retrieving a treat its owner hid days earlier; a honeybee using its waggle dance to signal the direction and distance of nectar; a scrub jay that moves its cached food to a new hiding place after another jay has watched it cache; a slime mold taking the shortest route through a maze; a Venus flytrap that snaps shut only after two trigger hairs are touched; a thermostat that switches on when the room cools; and a person figuring out how to maneuver a sofa through a narrow doorway. Run the definition through every case, mark which verdicts it forces and which are borderline, and where it yields a wrong or no answer, either revise it or defend that verdict. Commit to one definition and give reasons for it rather than laying out the options.
