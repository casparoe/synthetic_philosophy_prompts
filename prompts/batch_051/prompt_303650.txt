I am compiling a taxonomy of candidate grounds for assigning moral status to digital minds — by which I mean artificially realized or simulated minds, from whole-brain emulations to sufficiently sophisticated trained systems. The entries below are meant to be independently motivated grounds, not restatements of one another, and I have tried to make each one structural rather than merely terminological.

The entries I already have are:

1. Substrate-independent sentience: a system that instantiates the functional organization sufficient for phenomenal consciousness is a moral patient no matter what material realizes it.
2. Valenced welfare: a system with states that are non-instrumentally better or worse for it — suffering, enjoyment, frustration, satisfaction — has moral status even if we cannot establish that it is fully self-conscious.
3. Personhood: a system that can think of itself as a persisting subject across time, exercise practical reason, and pursue a conception of its own good is owed the stronger regard we give to persons.
4. Goal-directed autonomy: a system with stable ends that can be advanced or thwarted has interests, and interests are enough to make claims on others.
5. Relational standing: moral status can arise from how a being is situated — being cared for, being vulnerable to another's choices, or being recognized as a participant — rather than from intrinsic capacities alone.
6. Reciprocal agency: a system that can give and demand justifications, and can be held to shared norms, is owed the respect due to a fellow participant in moral life, with agency treated as continuous with patiency.

Give me one more candidate ground of a different structure: not on this list, and not a rewording, special case, or combination of the entries above. It should be a ground a serious person could defend, not a strawman. Then assess it honestly — whether it could bear weight on its own, how it compares with the strongest entry or entries above, and where it most obviously fails. In particular, say whether it would overgenerate by covering systems that are intuitively not moral patients, since that is the main failure mode of the entries I already have.
