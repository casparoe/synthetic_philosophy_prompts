I keep seeing rationalist-adjacent people lean on a distinction between manipulation and legitimate persuasion — in arguments about advertising, recommender systems, political messaging, even friends giving advice — but nobody ever operationalizes it, and the distinction seems to be doing real work in all those debates. Before I read yet another theory of it, I want the spec. What would an adequate account of the manipulation/persuasion boundary actually have to deliver? I mean the desiderata: the cases it has to classify correctly (honest explicit argument, flattery, emotional appeals, subliminal priming, default effects, pleading, gaslighting, love-bombing, Socratic questioning, a friend talking you out of a bad decision, an A/B-tested dark pattern), the distinctions it can't collapse (e.g., influence vs manipulation, how the influence works vs whether the influenced belief or action is good, maybe first-person vs third-person judgment), and the constraints like the same appeal being fine from a friend but creepy from an algorithm, or the same behavior counting as manipulation when the intent is bad but not when it isn't. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particular camps (consequentialist, autonomy-based, virtue-theoretic, whatever). Then flag any pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisifed, and tell me which single requirement does the most work excluding the usual stories people tell. Keep the scorecard of specific theories brief — I mostly want the criteria, not a survey.
