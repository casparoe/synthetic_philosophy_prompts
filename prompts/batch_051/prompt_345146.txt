my boss has gotten really into "thinking outside the box" ever since he came across the MU-puzzle in some old book, and now every product meeting ends with him waving it around as proof that constraints are the enemy. The puzzle as he tells it: you start with the string MI, you have four rewrite rules, and the goal is to get to MU. You can't do it, and the whole point is that the reason you can't is invisible from inside the rules — no reachable string ever has a number of I's divisible by 3, and MU has zero I's. Counting I's wasn't one of the rules; it's an observation about the system from outside. He calls this jootsing (jumping out of the system), and says it's exactly what "thinking outside the box" means: step out of the rules you were handed, look down from above, and the impossible goal dissolves into understanding why it can't be reached. So now he applies it to our actual work: the spec, the budget, the tools we've been told to use — those are just the box, and being creative means refusing to treat them as given.

Before I sit through another quarter of this, I want an actual audit of the analogy. Make the correspondence explicit: what plays the box, what plays the four rules, what is the act of stepping out, what is the solution, and what would count as the I-counting move in a real project? Then show me where the mapping breaks. In the puzzle the outside move is still a rigorous argument with its own hidden constraints — arithmetic, induction, whatever — not a vibe of rule-refusal. And a box is just a container you're allowed to leave, whereas some real-world constraints are load-bearing, or are the whole game, not incidental boundaries. Which disanalogies matter for the conclusion he wants (creative work means refusing the handed-down rules) and which are just nitpicks? Does that conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the MU-puzzle just dressing up a cliché in a fancy story? If there's a better analogy, or a more honest statement of what the puzzle actually licences, give me that too.

Also, keep the factual parts — that MU is underivable, the invariant about I-counts — clearly separate from the contested interpretive parts, and don't talk about some theory of creativity as if it were the textbook consensus when it isn't.
