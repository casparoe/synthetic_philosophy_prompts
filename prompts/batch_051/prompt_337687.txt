I've been running a monthly Fermi paradox discussion group for a while now, and I keep noticing that people sort into camps by their reactions to specific findings, not by the labels they'd pick for themselves: someone who says the galaxy is probably full of life will still talk like an early-filter person when you press them on what a confirmed second biogenesis would do to their credences. So I want to build a "which resolution of the Fermi paradox do you actually hold?" quiz for my blog, in the spirit of those old SSC sorting quizzes. Nine questions, each a concrete case or planned observation, two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto: Rare Earth, Great Filter behind us, Great Filter ahead of us, Zoo hypothesis, Dark Forest, grabby aliens, and the aestivation/transcension family (the idea that civilizations stop being visibly expansionist because they're optimizing for something else, not because they're absent or dead). If you genuinely can't seperate the last two as leaves, collapse them and flag why they came apart in principle but not in quiz form.

The cases should look like: a second, independent origin of life is found on Enceladus; a century-long all-sky technosignature survey turn's up nothing; we find no Dyson spheres or Kardashev-III galaxies within 100 Mpc; a narrowband signal is detected once and never repeats; a post-biological clade is found in the local stellar neighborhood but appears to be deliberately radio-quiet. The point is to sort people by what they'd expect to observe under each hypothesis, not by which label sounds right, so no question may just ask "do you think there's a Great Filter ahead of us?" Build the tree from the logical space of answers first (what combinations of answers are even coherent) and then place the named positions in it, and say a sentence about each unoccupied combination: is it empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored? I'd also like the scoring key to be able to distinguish people who actually accept the observer-selection move — we shouldn't be surprised to find ourselves early — from people who merely nod at it, but do that through case questions rather than one direct anthropics question.
