I keep running into arguments that Peano arithmetic (or ZFC, or whatever "the basic axioms of mathematics" is even supposed to pick out) might secretly be inconsistent, and none of them ever lays out what a serious version of the position would have to do. Before I read another one, write the complete requirements list for a theory of "the axioms might be inconsistent." I want it spelled out: which formal system it's about and whether PA and ZFC can be treated in the same breath; which established results it can't contradict; which distinctions it can't blur; which data points it has to explain. Give the reason each item belongs on the list, not just a name for it.

Some distinctions I half-remember but haven't seen anyone handle all at once: the asymmetry between proving inconsistency and proving consistency — one short formal derivation of 0=1 would settle the yes side, while no amount of "we still haven't found one" settles the no side; the cut Gödel's second incompleteness theorem makes (a consistency proof can't be carried out inside the system itself, and maybe not from anything weaker either — correct me if I have that part wrong); the difference between an inconsistent system and a merely unsound one, i.e. consistent but proving false sentences about the intended numbers; the fact that most ordinary mathematics seems to run on weak fragments, so a contradiction buried in the far reaches might be containable rather than fatal; and the distinction between a reassurance that uses only principles the system could vouch for and one that uses stronger principles and therefore might be circular comfort.

Then separate the hard constraints from the things that are really just one camp's preferences (finitist, formalist, paraconsistent, whatever). Say which requirements can't all be satisfied together — e.g., tell me whether a finitary consistency proof covering all of PA is actually impossible or whether I'm misremembering that too. Last, say which one requirement does the most to eliminate the hand-wavy versions of the position, and in two or three sentences apiece say how these three score against the full list: the person who still wants a finitary consistency proof, the person who thinks an inconsistency could be quarantined, and the person who thinks the worry is idle.
