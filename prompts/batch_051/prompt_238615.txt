I have been reading the Bible on my own for years and trying to work out how the church's gathered worship ought to be ordered. The position I keep coming back to is what I later learned is called the regulative principle: in public worship, whatever is not commanded by God is forbidden, not merely permitted. I do make one qualification. I distinguish between the content of worship itself and the practical arrangements around it—when and where we meet, what people sit on, what language the service is in—and I apply the principle only to the first. Lately, though, that qualification has started to look to me like a line that could be placed anywhere, and I worry that my position has become very hard to falsify.

Play it straight with me: what would count against the regulative principle? I do not want a proof-text list on either side, and I am not asking whether this or that practice is permissible; I am asking whether the criterion itself is a good one. I want the strongest reasons for thinking the principle itself is false, or the wrong kind of rule for the new covenant, ranked from what would be fatal to what would be merely awkward. Where defenders of the principle, like me, have standard replies, say which of those replies can be given in good conscience and which are special pleading. Then give an honest assessment: once the view is protected by the distinction between the substance of worship and its outward circumstances, has it been insulated against every possible counterexample? And is that insulation a problem or a point in its favor? Keep your answer to no more than 600 words.
