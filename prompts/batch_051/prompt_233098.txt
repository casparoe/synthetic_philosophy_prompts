Write a rigorous philosophical dialogue of no more than 800 words, excluding the title, between two equally sophisticated characters who both accept moral error theory — the view that all first-order moral judgements, such as the judgement that torture is wrong, are systematically false because nothing in the world has the properties they purport to ascribe. One character is an abolitionist: she holds that, since moral discourse is false and continually risks reinstating the illusion that produced it, we should relinquish moral talk entirely and find non-moral ways of criticising, coordinating, and deciding. The other is a revolutionary fictionalist: he holds that moral language, though literally false, remains indispensable for motivation and social coordination, and should be retained as a self-conscious fiction.

Set the dialogue in a single continuous scene in an empty seminar room just after a metaethics conference has ended, and render it as alternating speech with brief stage directions only where they clarify the argument. Begin with a short title.

The two positions must be evenly matched: neither character should be a strawman. Each should restate the other's view in a form the other would accept before objecting to it, and each should engage at least once with the strongest consideration raised by the other. The conversation should show real movement — views may be refined, but the disagreement should remain live at the end rather than being resolved. Make the piece intelligible to a reader who has never encountered metaethics: let the characters explain any technical terms in their own voices, without footnotes or external references. Avoid quotations and citations. Use British spelling and punctuation throughout.
