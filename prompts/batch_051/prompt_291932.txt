I'm an undergraduate in a metaphysics course on identity and individuation, and I'm drafting the position section of a term paper. I need to check that the view I've arrived at is actually coherent before I commit to it in writing. My commitments are:

1. A substantive version of the Principle of Identity of Indiscernibles: necessarily, if two particulars share all their qualitative properties, then they are identical. By 'qualitative' I mean to exclude identity-involving properties such as being identical with a or being one of the two spheres.

2. Black's two-sphere universe is a genuine metaphysical possibility: a world containing exactly two iron spheres, perfectly alike in size, mass, composition, and temperature, separated by some fixed distance, with nothing else that could serve as a point of reference.

3. Bundle theory for concrete particulars: a particular is nothing over and above its properties, so there is no bare substratum or thin particular underlying them.

4. A rejection of primitive thisness: there are no non-qualitative haecceities in virtue of which distinct particulars are primitively distinct.

5. Relations can ground distinctness: two intrinsically identical particulars can nonetheless be distinct in virtue of standing in different relations.

6. Conceivability is a defeasible guide to possibility: if a scenario can be described coherently and in full detail, that gives us a prima facie reason to think it is possible.

Please audit this set. I want to know which commitments are in genuine tension with each other and which pairs only appear to be in tension once the relevant distinctions are drawn. For each genuine tension, rank its severity, say what would have to be given up, and explain what the rebuilding cost would be. Then tell me what the cheapest single revision would be, if I want to keep as much of the original picture as possible, and what that revision would cost me philosophically.
