Okay so I've been trying to get my story straight on Dutch books before I teach a week on Bayesian epistemology, and I'd like someone to audit the set of things I currently believe because I have a nagging feeling they can't all be right. Here are my committments, roughly:

1. Credences (degrees of belief) should obey the probability axioms: nonnegativity, tautologies get 1, at least finite additivity. So probabilism is right as a normative claim about ideally rational agents.
2. I think the Dutch book argument is a genuinely good argument for this, not just a cute illustration. If your credences violate the axioms, there's a set of bets, each fair by your own lights, that guarantees you a loss.
3. But I also think you can rationally decline to bet on a proposition at all. You might not have enough of an opinion on it, or you might have moral or aesthetic reasons not to gamble on it, or you might distrust the betting setup. Refusing a bet needn't mean your credence is irrational.
4. I don't think 3 sinks 2, because the Dutch book argument doesn't need you to actually accept all the bets in the package. It just needs each individual bet to be one you'd be committed to accepting in isolation, given your fair odds.
5. I also think accuracy-first arguments for probabilism are importantly different from Dutch book arguments — one is about matching the world, the other is about avoiding exploitation — and I don't want to reduce one to the other.
6. Lately I've been tempted by imprecise credences: for a lot of propositions I don't think I have a single sharp degree of belief, just a range or a set of probability functions I can't rank among.

Are these jointly tenable? I want you to tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only seem to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest revision would be for each and overall. Please write your answer in American English.
