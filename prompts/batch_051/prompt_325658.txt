I got onto this after a Thanksgiving fight about a family ritual nobody enjoys but everyone defends as if it were sacred, and the same shape kept showing up in bigger moral arguments, so I've built a small theory and I'd like someone to kick its tires before I keep using it. I call it "tenured values." The idea is that some values begin as instruments — they spread because they reliably serve some other goal — but over time they get tenure. The community stops re-assessing them on the merits and starts treating them as intrinsically good, and the surest sign of tenure is that questioning the value reads as a character flaw rather than a factual disagreement. Truth-telling is my cleanest example. As a coordination device it earns its keep, but try saying "a small deception would be kinder here" and people usually don't respond with evidence that the lie will backfire; they respond as though you've declared war on truth itself. Tradition looks similar: it's plausibly a storage mechanism for other people's hard-won information, but challenge a tradition and you're rarely argued with on cost-benefit grounds — you're called impious or rootless. Purity is the weirdest case, since it starts in disgust and pathogen avoidance and then gets moralized well past anything hygiene justifies.

My actual claim isn't just the flat one that these are instrumental values people mistake for intrinsic ones. The tenure part is doing the work: the pretense of intrinsicality might be part of the value's function, not a bug. Calling truth sacred is what makes the norm cheap to enforce; the moment everyone treats it as merely useful, free-riding gets easier. So I don't exactly want to debunk these values, and I'm not convinced "intrinsic vs instrumental" is clean enough to do the sorting. I know there's a social-science literature on sacred values, and a functionalist tradition in ethics, but I mean something seperate from the first and maybe less reductive than the second. Where does the concept break? Give me the strongest objections you can, and tell me whether any of them kills the distinction rather than just denting it.
