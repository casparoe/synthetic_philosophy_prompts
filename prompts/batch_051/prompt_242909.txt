I'm putting together a unit on ecological ethics for an intro film class, and I want to use Jurassic Park. The obvious reading is that the park fails because living systems are to complex to predict, and the movie treats that as a lesson about humility. But I've been trying to connect it to the current debate over de-extinction and gene drives, and I keep feeling like the analogy does different work in the two cases. Bringing back a species is one thing — a gene drive is engineered to spread through a wild population and push it toward extinction, and it's meant to be irreversible in a way a fenced-in park full of cloned dinosaurs isn't. Does the film's cautionary logic actually apply to that second case, or is it running on an older worry about hubris and nature pushing back? I'd like a reading that takes the film seriously and also takes the philosophical arguments about irreversible ecological interventions seriously, and that's willing to say where the film's argument doesn't map onto the gene-drive debate. Which scenes would you point a class to, and where does the film resist the reading?
