I'm a PhD student in behavioral econ, and I've been tearing my hair out over an argument I want to use in a chapter on diachronic rationality. My superviser says it has a "classic bug" but he's making me find it myself. Could you act as an expert on decision theory and dynamic choice, think through this carefully, and brainstorm a few candidate failure points before you settle on a verdict? Here's the argument.

Sam has plain present-biased preferences: any reward that isn't immediate gets discounted by 50%, and an immediate reward isn't discounted at all. So $X in the future is worth $0.5X to him, and $X right now is worth $X.

(1) At t0, Sam is choosing between $60 delivered at t1 and $100 delivered at t2. Both are in the future, so he values them at $30 and $50. He prefers the $100 at t2.

(2) At t1, the $60 is immediate and the $100 is still one period away. Now he values them at $60 and $50. He prefers the $60 now.

(3) So a trader can run the following money pump on Sam. At t0, the trader offers him a commitment contract: "Pay me $5 now and I'll make sure you hold out for the $100." Sam accepts, because from his t0 point of view, getting the $100 rather than eventually caving for $60 is worth a $5 fee. Then at t1, the trader offers to void the contract for another $5. Sam accepts again, because at t1 he genuinely prefers $60 now to $100 at t2. He ends up with $60 minus $10 in fees — an outcome his t0 self ranks below the option he started with, just waiting for the $100 at t2.

(4) This generalizes: any present-biased agent can be charged for reversing an earlier decision, ending up worse off by their own long-run ranking.

(5) Let's grant for the sake of argument that being on the wrong end of a successful money pump is enough to show irrationality.

(6) Therefore, present-biased preferences are irrational.

This feels off, but I can't tell whether the argument is bad or I just don't like the conclusion. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and what would the corrected version of that step say? Could it be patched into an argument that at least some present-biased agents, or some forms of present bias, are irrational?
