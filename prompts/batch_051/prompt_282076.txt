I have recently joined a research group that works on understanding the internal behavior of language models, and one analogy organizes a great deal of how my colleagues speak. They say a trained model contains a kind of map of the world. When a direction in activation space tracks a concept, or when certain activations separate claims the model treats as true from claims it treats as false, my colleagues describe themselves as reading coordinates off the model's map. This way of speaking is not decorative: it supports the substantive conclusion that the model genuinely represents its training domain rather than merely storing statistical associations, and that a careful interpreter can recover what those internal representations say. Since I am now expected to use these interpretability findings to decide when a model's signals can be trusted, I need to know how literally to take the cartographic picture.

I would like you to audit the analogy seriously. Please provide an explicit correspondence: what in the model plays the role of the territory, the map itself, the coordinate system, the surveyor, the legend, and the person reading the map? Then identify where the mapping breaks. I can already see some candidate failures — the territory may not be fixed, the coordinate system may be arbitrary or tangled, and what one extracts often looks less like a single coherent cartographic projection than like a stack of partial sketches drawn for different purposes. But I do not want a mere list of disanalogies: which of them actually undermine the intended use of the analogy, and which are harmless? Above all, does the conclusion the analogy is used to support survive — that the model has something map-like inside it, and that interpretability can read off its representational content roughly the way one reads a map — once the important disanalogies are in view? If the analogy has been carrying the conclusion, what comparison would better capture the relationship between a model's internal states and whatever, if anything, they represent?
