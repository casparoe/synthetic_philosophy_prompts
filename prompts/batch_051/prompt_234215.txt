I've got this slightly obsessive question I've been turning over since my economics class did discount rates. There's this idea called pure time preference: we care less about stuff just because it happens further in the future, even before we factor in uncertainty or the fact that money invested today grows. So a pound in 2035 is worth less to most people than a pound today even if they're totally sure they'll get it. I keep wondering what a whole country would look like if basically nobody had that.

So here's the scenario. In 2025 a new island nation of about 400,000 people is founded, and to become a citizen you have to pass some screening showing you have near-zero pure time preference. These people care about their own welfare at 70 about as much as their welfare at 20, and they care about strangers alive in 2200 a lot more than normal people do. They still discount for uncertainty (a maybe-future benefit isn't treated as guaranteed), and they still understand that a resource today can be invested into more resources later. The only difference is that 'later' isn't automatically worth less. Predict how the place looks in 2075, with the actual reasoning, not just a story: savings and pensions, infrastructure, climate and environmental policy, crime and punishment (would they even see the point of life sentences?), medical research, and how businesses, voters and politicians would behave differently. Which predictions are you most confident in, and which are basically guesses? What hidden assumptions would have to hold for it to turn out differently? And do you thing they'd actually be happier, or would caring alot about the distant future just make everyone anxious and controlling? Oh, and please write the answer in British English.
