I work on the strategy team at a private foundation that makes roughly eighty grant decisions a year across a deliberately wide portfolio: global health delivery, climate adaptation, criminal justice reform, animal welfare, and AI governance research. The board has concluded that our funding choices track proposal charisma and current enthusiasm more than any shared rationale, and they have asked us to build a reusable triage procedure organized around the three considerations we constantly invoke without ever defining operationally: importance, tractability, and neglectedness. The procedure will be run by an analyst with at most two hours of desk time per proposal, before any deep due diligence, and must end in one of three outputs — advance to full review, return with a specific stated reason, or escalate to a person because the decision is now a value judgment rather than an analysis. There is disagreement on the team about how the three dimensions should combine: one program officer wants a single weighted score, another argues neglectedness should function only as a tiebreaker because it tracks diminishing returns rather than the goodness of a cause. Please write the procedure as an ordered series of questions, with enough guidance about what counts as a sufficient answer that two analysts reading the same proposal would usually land on the same output, and mark explicitly the steps at which the procedure must hand the decision to a human.

Then run the procedure on three recent-looking proposals: (a) a mass deworming program in a low-income country with strong trial evidence but a crowded funding landscape; (b) a two-year grant to an early-stage AI governance research institute with no track record, where our analysts suspect that the standard tractability question — what an additional dollar would be expected to purchase — is the wrong question to ask; and (c) a long-running first-generation college scholarship program that the board regards as a flagship success. One of these should produce an output the foundation will not like; identify where that happens and explain why the procedure is right to be unkind. Conclude by saying clearly what the procedure cannot assess, and what would go wrong if we pretended it could. Please write the answer in American English.
