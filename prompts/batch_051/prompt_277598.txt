Before I read any more theories of musical works, I want the specification such a theory is being measured against, rather than another theory. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of what a musical work is must meet, each one forced by a concrete case. Cover at least the following, and correct the list if you think it is wrongheaded: (i) the same work appears across two performances sharing no physical constituents, so the theory must say what a performance is a performance of; (ii) there is a threshold between a performance with many wrong notes that is still of the work and a performance that is no longer of it, so the theory must say what fixes that threshold; (iii) many works are created by someone at a time yet are the sort of thing one might have thought existed antecedently, so the theory must take a stand on creation versus discovery; (iv) a recording assembled in a studio and impossible to perform live still presents a work, so the theory must say how recordings relate to works; and (v) there are works with no score, from oral-tradition songs to electronic tracks to pieces improvised fresh each performance. For each requirement, give the case behind it and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Then flag any pair of requirements that plausibly cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to decide among the usual contenders—platonism, type/token views, nominalism, and action-or-lineage accounts. Keep the scorecard brief; I want most of the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases.
