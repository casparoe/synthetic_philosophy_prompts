Here is a case I keep returning to. In the standard color phi setup, a red spot is flashed briefly at one location, and then, after a short interval, a green spot is flashed at a second location. Subjects do not report two successive flashes. They report a single spot that moves from the first location to the second and changes from red to green roughly midway along its trajectory, with the color change represented as happening before the second flash has actually occurred. The usual way of framing the puzzle distinguishes two accounts. On the Orwellian account, the subject first consciously sees a stationary red spot, and once the green flash arrives the brain edits the experienced past, overwriting the prior episode with a memory of a moving, color-changing spot. On the Stalinesque account, conscious experience of the first flash is delayed until the second flash can be integrated, so the subject never consciously sees a stationary red spot at all; the moving, color-changing spot is the first thing experienced.

I want a number, not a taxonomy. What is your current credence that the Stalinesque account correctly describes what happens in a typical subject in the standard color phi case? Give the unconditional credence, not one that silently conditions on the question being well posed. Then decompose it. First, what is your probability that there is a determinate fact of the matter distinguishing Orwellian from Stalinesque processing at the level of conscious experience? Second, conditional on there being such a fact, what is your probability that the true account is Stalinesque rather than Orwellian? For each number, give the main consideration behind it and how confident you are in that consideration. Then tell me which sub-question your final Stalinesque credence is most sensitive to: the empirical facts about the timing and the perceptual integration, your background theory of consciousness and whether it allows a single determinate timing for an experience, or the strength of the deflationary argument that the two accounts are indistinguishable in principle and the dispute is therefore verbal. What experiment, result, or argument would move that number most, and roughly how far? Finally, say whether you would give a materially different answer if the gap between the two flashes were substantially shorter or longer, and whether you think the empirical indistinguishability of the two stories means we should treat the choice between them as something closer to a choice of notation than a factual question.
