Me and a couple of mates got into it the other night about how the world seems to pick and choose which massacres it will and wont stop. In 1999 NATO bombed Serbia over Kosovo for about eleven weeks without the Security Council ever signing off, on the grounds that people were being driven from their homes and something had to be done. In 2011 the same logic got a Security Council resolution for Libya that told member states to do whatever they judged necessary to protect civilians, and it quickly turned into helping topple the government. But Rwanda in 1994 was mostly watched from the sidelines while it unfolded, Darfur got years of speeches and sanctions but no real military rescue, and Syria was a horror show from the outside for more than a decade. Iraq in 2003 is muddier because the public case was built on weapons, but once those were not found the humanitarian line got wheeled out as well; count it or set it aside.

What bugs me is not that the cases are different, obviously they are. Its that the justifications stayed more or less the same across all of them even when the legal cover and the outcome pointed in opposite directions. The same governments also talk as if every person everywhere has the same basic rights, and as if a government only gets to rule with the consent of its own people. Yet the actual record behaves as if some strangers' suffering creates a duty for rich countries to act while other strangers' suffering does not. I'm not asking you to judge any particular war or write me a history essay. What I want to know is what this practice presupposes philosophically. What view of sovereignty, obligation, and the moral equality of people would make this selective pattern hang together rather than just being hypocrisy? Does the practice actually commit anyone to that view, or is it compatible with several different theories, and which rival views does it quietly rule out? And if the states doing the intervening really followed through on the theory their words imply, what would have to change about where, when, and why they show up?

Oh, and could you write the answer in British English?
