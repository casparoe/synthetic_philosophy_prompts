Before I read any more proposals about when, relative to the brain's processing, a conscious experience occurs, I want the specification they are all answerable to. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the timing of experience must meet. It must handle at least the following: colour phi, in which a red flash followed soon after by a green flash at another location is seen as one moving spot that turns green partway along its path, slightly before the green flash is presented; the cutaneous rabbit, in which a rapid run of taps at the wrist, then the elbow, then the shoulder is felt as a chain of taps hopping up the arm, including at sites that were never touched; and Libet-style findings, in which a neural event reliably precedes the time at which people report a conscious urge, and later stimulation can shift the reported time of an earlier experience. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and make explicit any distinctions a theory cannot collapse, such as between the time a stimulus is presented and the time its content is represented as occurring, and between the time an experience occurs and the time at which a report of it is issued. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable constraints on any account and which are merely commitments of a particular camp, such as the demand that every experience have a single datable onset or that there be a well-defined time of an experience at all. In particular, use the desiderata to address whether the Orwellian strategy (the brain revises the record of an experience after the fact) and the Stalinesque strategy (the brain delays or edits the experience before it reaches consciousness) can be separated by any possible evidence, or whether an adequate theory meeting all the requirements is forced to treat them as the same hypothesis. Flag any pair of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
