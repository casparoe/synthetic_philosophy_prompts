I've been calling it the 'precision budget.' The thought is: imprecision isn't a local property of a single event, it's a global state of your whole belief system. Precision is zero-sum: you can be a heroic imprecise Bayesian about one question only by being overprecise about another. That would mean nobody can actually be imprecise 'about everything' — the extreme credal set that's [0,1] for every event is a free lunch, so it can't be the whole story. It also would explain the guy in my class who's drippy-vague about the economy but weirdly certain about who started that group chat drama. I know it sounds like I've reinvented the principle of indifference wearing a fluorescent wig. And I can't figure out what the units of the budget would even be.
