My Catholic cousin and my Zwinglian-leaning brother-in-law spent Thanksgiving arguing about whether the bread at communion is actually Christs body, and my cousin kept throwing out "this is my body" as if that quote by itself settles it. I grew up hearing that sentence every Sunday, so I never had to think about it; now that I am thinking about it, I realize I can't even say what it would mean for it to be literally true. Take that sentence as said over the bread, and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, from the thinnest memorialist reading to the thickest numerical-identity reading, plus everything in between — bread stays bread but the body is present in and along with it; the substance changes while the accidents stay; nothing about the thing changes but what the thing means or is for changes (the transignification-style move); impanation; whatever other half-remembered positions belong on the list. For each reading, say what metaphysical machinery it has to buy (substance/accident distinctions, real vs spiritual presence, whether the "is" is identity or predication or a performative, the one-body-in-many-places puzzle), whether the reading is actually coherent on its own terms or just a label, and which of these readings would actually support the way my cousin was using the quote. I want a menu I can bring back to the dinner table, not a verdict, and not just the seventh-grade metaphor-vs-literal version.
