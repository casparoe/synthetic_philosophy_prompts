In my philosophy of religion seminar we read Charles Darwin’s letter to Asa Gray of 22 May 1860, and one passage has been bothering me in a productive way. Darwin writes:

“I cannot persuade myself that a beneficent & omnipotent God would have designedly created the Ichneumonidæ with the express intention of their feeding within the living bodies of caterpillars, or that a cat should play with mice.”

I am trying to isolate the argumentative move here, because it seems to me neither simply the generic problem of evil nor exactly what my instructor calls natural evil. Natural evil, as we have been using the term, includes earthquakes and plagues, but Darwin’s examples are not impersonal disasters; they are the evolved life cycles and ordinary behaviors of organisms—parasitism and predation—that require one organism to consume or torment another simply to live. The examples also seem to be doing a specific argumentative job: they are cases that the free-will defense and soul-making theodicy cannot easily reach, because no human moral agent is involved, and the caterpillar is not plausibly being educated or tested. Darwin then makes a further move I do not know how to classify: he says he is inclined to view everything as resulting from designed laws, with the details, “whether good or bad,” left to the working out of something like chance, though he admits this does not satisfy him.

What I want to know is whether this specific argumentative strategy has a recognized name. I do not mean merely “the problem of animal suffering,” if such a phrase exists, because I am interested in the precise way the nonhuman case functions as an independent challenge to divine benevolence, one that cannot be absorbed by the standard explanations of human suffering. How does it relate to the broader category of natural evil, and where exactly are its boundaries? For instance, would a deer dying slowly after a wildfire count as the same kind of case, or does the strategy derive its force specifically from the fact that living organisms are built to inflict suffering on other living organisms as part of their ordinary life cycles? If no established term captures exactly this move, I would rather hear what you would call it, and how you would distinguish it from nearby concepts, than receive a bare label.
