A friend and I went round and round last night about whether the axioms of expected utility theory are just a modeling convenience or an actual standard of correctness. His line was that someone who violates, say, transitivity isn't expressing an unusual taste — they're making the preference-level analogue of an inconsistency in belief, and the money-pump setup proves it: if A beats B, B beats C, but C beats A, you can walk them around the loop charging a fee each time until they're back where they started with less money. I kept saying that's an intuition pump, not a test. The cycling contradicts your axioms only if we've already decided that preferences ought to be orderable, which is the very thing in dispute. And for all the money pump shows, maybe the person just likes the activity of trading.

He came back with what I thought was his strongest move: it's not the exploitability that matters, it's the response to having the cycle pointed out. People often wince and revise. I said of course they wince — you've just shown them they can be turned into a pump, which is embarrassing, and social pressure is jusy that, not evidence of error. He said, okay, then imagine they'd pay up front to be prevented from making the cyclic choices at all. Doesn't that mean they treat the incoherence itself as a defect they want out of their own future behavior? I said no, they might just be paying to avoid being pumped, which is perfectly consistent with still having cyclic preferences.

So now I'm realy unsure whether the claim "violating the axioms is an error rather than just a different taste" has any empirical content, or whether it's purely a philosophical stipulation. I'd like you to try to give it some. Design the best experiment or set of observations you can that would separate the "error" reading from the "legitimate taste" reading of preference incoherence. Be specific about the manipulation, the control, and the result that would count each way. Then be honest about the residue: what would even a clean positive result still leave open about the difference between a normative standard and a psychological regularity?
