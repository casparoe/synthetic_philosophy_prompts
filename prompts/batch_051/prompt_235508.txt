ok so I'm putting together a little satirical zine with a few friends and we got into one of those arguments about whether streaming has actually gotten more formulaic or whether we've just gotten old and grumpy. I kept trying to bring up Adorno and Horkheimer, but saying the culture industry at a party is a great way to not be invited back. So I'd rather smuggle it in as a joke.

Can you write a parody of a shareholder letter from the invented CEO of a streaming service — call the service something like ReliaFlix — who has genuinely read Adorno and Horkheimer on the culture industry and decided its not so much a critique as a business plan. The letter should deadpan announce that the company is done pretending: the industry already sells the same thing while marketing it as new, so they're going to make sameness itself the product. Some bits to maybe work in, but don't cram all of them: a proprietary 'surprise minimization' score for season finales; A/B testing catharsis so every episode's emotional release lands at the same minute mark; removing the skip-intro button because the intros are now structurally interchangeable; celebrating pseudo-individualization the way a cereal company puts different cartoon mascots on the same grain slurry; buying canceled shows and resolving every cliffhanger in the least surprising direction possible.

Aim for 600–800 words, deadpan from end to end, no winking. It should be funny to someone who only knows that all the shows feel the same, but even funnier to someone who knows the theory: the standardization, the pseudo-individuality, amusement as an administered afterthought, the promise of a satisfaction that never really arrives. Don't have the CEO quote Adorno verbatim or cite the actual essay — a confident, slightly wrong paraphrase from a businessman is half the joke. The target is the practice and the position, not any real person.
