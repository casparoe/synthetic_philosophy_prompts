I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about the "do the most good you can" crowd, going by my gut on cases rather than the theory. Here's where I land:

- A math PhD goes into quantitative trading and gives half of a very large salary to the most cost-effective global health charity (malaria nets, deworming, that sort of thing). Fine — honestly, maybe better than fine, I admire it.
- The same person instead joins a studio that makes pay-to-win mobile games aimed at kids, planning to donate half his pay to that same charity. Not fine.
- A donor gives five hundred dollars a year to her local food pantry, knowing full well the same money would save more lives if she gave it to a top global health charity, because she wants to be the kind of person who hands food to her neighbours. Fine.
- A foundation turns down a grant to enforce lead-paint laws in old rental housing because the outcome can't be shown with a randomized trial, and funds another deworming rollout instead. Not fine — the metric is running the mission.
- A global health charity spends two million dollars and two years running an RCT of cash transfers in a setting where the evidence already strongly says they work, instead of just giving poor households the cash. Not fine, but I can't quite say how it's different from running a trial where the evidence is genuinely absent.
- A friend leaves a legal aid clinic, where she was very good, to go into management consulting so she can donate far more than she ever could at the clinic. I keep going back and forth on this one.
- Someone takes the earn-to-give path, always says "one more bonus first," and ten years later has donated almost nothing. Not fine — but I'm not sure if that's a problem with the idea or just with the person.

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these, stated precisely? Say which verdicts force which clause of it, what it implies about the consulting case and one further case (say, a donor who gives only to political organizing that has no measurable short-term target at all), and which of my verdicts, if any, I should give up.
