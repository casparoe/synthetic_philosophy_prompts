I'm a PhD student in economics working on social learning, and for the past year I've been sitting in on a reading group where two older grad students have been going at each other about information cascades. One of them — call him the Bayesian — treats a cascade as basically a rational thing: if enough people before you chose A over B, their choices carry information about their private signals, so it can be perfectly rational to override your own private signal and follow the herd. The other — she comes from social psych and reputation research — thinks the Bayesian story is mostly a post-hoc rationalization. She says in most real cascades people aren't extracting information from others' choices at all; they're following because deviating puts their reputation on the line and because joining the crowd lets them share the blame if the choice turns out badly. Her killer example is Asch-type conformity, where the "information" in others' choices is worthless and the behavior still shows up, so information can't be the thing doing the real work.

They've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I've been tapped to draft the joint statement they'd both sign. I'd like you to write that document for me. It should have:

1. A numbered list of claims both camps can sign — about cascades themselves and about the two mechanisms. Don't make these vacuous. The point is to find claims that are actually substantive and still acceptable to both.
2. A section stating exactly where they still disagree, with each side's own view phrased in wording that side would accept. I'm suspicous that part of this fight is verbal — about what "rational" and "information" are even supposed to mean — so if some of the disagreement dissolves under careful definitions, say so, and then seperately say which part stays genuine.
3. A table of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of the two. For each crux, give a concrete case or experimental setup both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. I'd especially like cruxes around (a) whether making choices anonymous and stripping out reputational stakes kills the cascade, (b) whether followers' private beliefs actually converge toward the herd's choice or whether there's a big private-doubt/public-compliance gap, and (c) whether a single well-timed dissenter can reverse a mature cascade, and under what conditions. Add one more crux only if you think there's a better one.

And at the very end, add a short drafter's note saying which part of the statement you're least confident about, where your own reasoning is most likely to have gone wrong, and what empirical result or argument would make you redraft that section.
