ok so theres this fight in my Orwell reading group that has dragged on for months now and nobody has budged. One camp reads Orwell as saying bad language is itself the problem: vague, puffed-up prose doesn't just announce a lazy mind, it actively makes thinking worse, and once you get to Newspeak the whole point is that if you deliberately shrink the language you shrink what people are even capable of thinking. The other camp says no, you've got the causal arrow backwards — for Orwell the rot always starts with lying and power, and degraded language is just what that rot looks like on the page. Ugly sentences are a symptom, not the disease.

The thing is, these aren't just two readings of a couple of passages; they lead the two camps to pretty different ideas about what Orwell thought language reform was even for. The first lot tend to think he believed fixing your prose was a real political intervention, because cleaner language means cleaner thought. The second lot think he believed tidying up your sentences was mostly about not fooling yourself and making it harder for other people to fool you — necessary, maybe, but not a substitute for actual politics.

We've read the same pages and nobody moves. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about Orwell at all: it's about which comes first, individual cognitive habits or institutional power, and each side is just reading its own prior commitment back into Orwell. But im not at all sure, and there might be a real empirical question in here about whether language degrades thought or thought degrades language.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal (something like the word 'corruption' doing a lot of unacknowledged work), is it a genuine question about causal direction, or are the two camps answering different questions under one heading? And what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a kind of evidence, a better way of putting the question? Or is the honest answer that Orwell was deliberately having it both ways and the fight shouldn't be settled?
