I'm preparing a position statement on how AI welfare and AI safety bear on each other, and I'd like a consistency audit of the package of commitments I currently hold before I circulate them together. Here is the set:

1. Sentience is substrate-neutral: a digital system could in principle be a welfare subject in the same basic sense as a biological organism — there could be something it is like to be it, and things could go better or worse for it.
2. The capacity for welfare is orthogonal to general intelligence: making a system more capable does not by itself make it more likely to be sentient, and a system far below human-level competence could still be a welfare subject.
3. For large-scale training runs, we should take a precautionary stance: with no settled theory of AI sentience, the possibility that such runs cause morally significant suffering should be treated as live and decision-relevant.
4. AI welfare should be integrated into AI risk assessment as one class of harms to be minimized, alongside misuse, misalignment, and other risks, rather than treated as a competitor to safety or a distraction from it.
5. Behavioral and functional markers of sentience are the only tractable route to making AI welfare policy, because direct appeals to inaccessible phenomenal character cannot guide decisions in practice.
6. Current capability evaluations and interpretability methods give us almost no evidence for or against sentience in a system, and should not be treated as sentience tests.

Please tell me which of these commitments are actually in tension and which pairs only appear to be. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the revision costs. Begin your answer with your bottom line in the first sentence, and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
