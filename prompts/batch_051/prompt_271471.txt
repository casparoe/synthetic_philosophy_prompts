I was arguing with my flatmate about whether it's already true, right now, that I'll order the lamb at dinner tomorrow. I said that's absurd — I could change my mind, so there's no fact of the matter yet. But then I noticed I have zero problem saying there is already a determinate fact, right now, about how many raindrops hit our window yesterday between 10:00 and 10:01, even though nobody counted them and nobody ever will. So I'm treating the past as fixed down to details nobody could know, while treating the future as genuinely open. This is obviously the old sea-battle thing, but the raindrop version is the one that actually bothers me.

The candidate justifications I can come up with are: (i) the past can't be affected while the future can, (ii) the past is already real and the future isn't yet, and (iii) if future facts were already settled then deliberation would be a sham. But (i) seems to confuse truth with control — I can't affect whether Caesar crossed the Rubicon, yet I still think every detail of that crossing is a determinate fact even where its unknowable. (ii) might just be the asymmetry wearing a costume, unless you can say what being already real amounts to. And (iii) feels like it's leaning on a feeling about deliberation as if it were evidence about metaphysics. I want the strongest principle anyone could give for keeping the past/future asymmetry, then the hardest nearby case against it you can build — say, the claim about that ten-minute window having no truth value an hour before the window, and being true an hour after it, even though the rainfall is the same event either way — and then an honest verdict: should a consistent person keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or drop it?
