Here's my working test for whether a decision counts as clueless in the consequentialist sense: (i) the effects you can foresee are mostly ones you can't sign; (ii) the verdict flips depending on which downstream chain you choose to trace; (iii) more analysis adds possible chains rather than narrowing the range; (iv) not deciding is itself a consequential choice. Borderline case: a medical resident with unusual lab talent has to pick between a modestly paying job doing TB contact tracing, where the near-term good is legible and positive, and a well-paying job designing pharma trial protocols, where the long-run effect could be "makes medical evidence generally more reliable" or could be "helps a company speed up approvals." The contact-tracing horn doesn't look clueless, but the trial-design horn does. Does the choice as a whole qualify as clueless under my test? Go criterion by criterion, and where some criteria say yes and others say no, tell me whether the criteria need fixing or the case just sits on the line.
