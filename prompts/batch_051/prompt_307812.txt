In a family argument about criminal sentencing, a relative kept falling back on the slogan "the punishment should fit the crime." He used it to defend very long prison sentences and to dismiss rehabilitation or reducing reoffending as irrelevant, since he said the only question is whether the penalty fits. I suspect the phrase is doing more work than it can bear, but I can't pin down exactly what claim it expresses. Could you lay out the different precise principles "the punishment should fit the crime" might be used to assert — for instance, that a sentence's severity should match how morally blameworthy the offender is, that it should match the harm actually caused, that equally serious crimes should receive equally severe sentences, or other readings you can identify — and say for each whether it is trivial or substantive, whether it is plausible, and whether that reading would actually support his harsher conclusions?
