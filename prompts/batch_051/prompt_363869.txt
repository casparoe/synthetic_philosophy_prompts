I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have long used the following four criteria to decide whether a process counts as an algorithm in the sense relevant to the Church-Turing thesis: (a) the process computes a mathematical function from a finite input string to a finite output string; (b) it is specified by a finite text and proceeds by means of discrete, unambiguous steps; (c) any two faithful implementations of the specification produce the same output for the same input; and (d) once started, it requires no creative insight, judgment, or information from outside the specification. Does this server count as an algorithm in the Church-Turing sense? Go through the criteria one by one, identify where they underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the difficult case is the server or the criteria. In particular, address whether criterion (a) builds in too narrow a picture of computation and, if so, what that implies about how much of actual software the Church-Turing thesis even purports to cover.
