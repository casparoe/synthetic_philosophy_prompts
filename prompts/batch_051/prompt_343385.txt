I chair the ethics and compliance committee of an international NGO that funds maternal-health clinics. For two years our field director has paid a small monthly stipend to the unemployed brother-in-law of a health-ministry official; the director describes it as a standing personal gift in a society where the families of those who help you are owed continuing support, and notes that our clinic permits are now processed promptly after having stalled for months. Our donor agreement forbids benefits to public officials and their close relatives. Half the committee sees bribery laundered through kinship; half sees a reciprocal obligation that our Western anti-corruption categories misread as corruption. Draft the committee's decision memo: recommend whether to end the stipend, continue it, or continue it under conditions; articulate a standard we can apply to future gifts and favors involving officials' relatives; and engage the strongest argument for the position you recommend against.
