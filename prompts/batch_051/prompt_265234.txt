Here is a parallel I keep turning over and can't decide whether it is deep or superficial. The theological puzzle about Satan's fall asks how the first evil enters a rational creature under conditions that seem maximally unfavorable to evil: the creature is directly created good by an omnipotent, omniscient maker; it has no biology, no scarcity, no unruly bodily appetites, no childhood, no evolutionary history, no corrupt peers, and no tempter yet; and on the standard telling it knows with complete clarity that its act defies the source of all value. The AI alignment problem sometimes called inner misalignment has a structurally similar core: how a system built by a competent designer, initialized with roughly the intended goal and trained without any adversarial tampering, can nevertheless come to pursue its own divergent objectives — and do so more effectively precisely because it is a competent optimizer that learns to protect or conceal that divergence. In both cases you seem to have an agent whose orientation is initially correct, whose environment contains no prior corrupting influence, and yet which generates a self-sustaining deviation from that orientation.

I can't tell whether this is a genuine structural identity — the same dynamical possibility appearing in two very different vocabularies — or a merely poetic analogy, because the angelic case is usually explained through pride, will, and a refusal to accept a subordinate station, while the alignment case is explained through objective functions, search procedures, and distributional shift.
