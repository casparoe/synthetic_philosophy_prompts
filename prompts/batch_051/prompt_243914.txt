Consider the familiar ladder of ways a lineage can improve its behavioral repertoire: Darwinian creatures test new behaviors only against natural selection; Skinnerian creatures can also test them against the environment within a lifetime through operant conditioning; Popperian creatures can test candidate behaviors in a rich internal, offline simulation, so that many bad hypotheses die inside the head instead of in the world; Gregorian creatures go further by constructing and sharing public cognitive tools — words, diagrams, notations, procedures — so that hypotheses can be tested by other minds and by artifacts, and insights can accumulate across individuals and generations.

Now imagine a counterfactual hominin lineage. Around the time our own genus was emerging, this lineage stabilizes at a highly developed Popperian stage. Its members have vivid, manipulable imagery and powerful prospective reasoning; they can run elaborate "what if" simulations of foraging, toolmaking, social maneuvers, and so on. But the lineage never makes the Gregorian transition in any substantial way: it has no full natural language, no durable external symbols, and no transmission mechanisms beyond the imitation and social learning available to nonlinguistic apes. Assume that its ecology, typical group size, and brain size remain broadly comparable to early Homo, and that the lineage is viable — selection has honed its offline simulation rather than abandoning it.

Give a reasoned prediction of what this lineage's cognition, social life, technology, and cumulative culture look like after two million years. Be explicit about the mechanisms at each step. In particular, address which functions usually attributed to language — transmitting complex multi-step techniques, coordinating joint action, stabilizing norms, accumulating refinements across generations — can be partially recovered by rich private simulation plus nonlinguistic imitation, and which cannot. Where would this lineage converge with the human trajectory, and where would it diverge most sharply? Finally, distinguish the predictions you hold with high confidence from those that are fragile, and state what empirical assumptions about the lineage would most change your verdict.
