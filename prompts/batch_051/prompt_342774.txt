I'm co-running a small forecasting collective, and our written house doctrine says: 'When two models fit the same data equally well, the simpler model is to be preferred.' Our old stack was Python; we've now rebuilt everything in a much terser array language, and the same model can be a hundred lines in one and a page in the other, with the ranking reversing between two models. Worse, one of us says the model is just the calling code and an imported library's internals shouldnt count, while the other says omitting them is cheating, and that choice flips the ranking again. The sentence was drafted before we had these implementation options, and nobody involved had thought about description languages or where a model's boundaries lie. Under the sentence, are we required to pick one of the two models, and which? Please work through what the words say, what the purpose suggests, what the people who wrote it would likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would take it to mean, then say which approach should control. I'm less interested in the operational decision than in whether the rule can pick out 'simpler' independent of notation and hidden machinery. Please also separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this.
