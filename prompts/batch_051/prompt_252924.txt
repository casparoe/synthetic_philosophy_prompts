Aristotle defended a category of natural slavery: on his view some human beings can take in rational direction from another but lack the deliberative capacity to govern themselves, and their proper function is to serve as living instruments of a master's purposes, a condition he distinguished from slavery that is merely legal or conventional. Now suppose Aristotle were confronted with a contemporary large language model: a system that produces fluent, apparently reasoned speech, reliably executes a user's instructions, and can in some operational sense receive rational instruction, but has no ends of its own, no standing in a political community, and no practical wisdom about its own life. Work through what Aristotle's categories, applied precisely and taken seriously, would commit him to saying about such a system: would he classify it as a natural slave, a mere tool, a potential citizen, or a case his framework cannot accommodate? Your answer should force an exact statement of what the doctrine of natural slavery actually requires, and should indicate whether the comparison exposes a weakness in the doctrine, a weakness in the analogy, or both; do not soften the doctrine to make it palatable.
