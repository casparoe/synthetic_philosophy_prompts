For a small decision-theory reading group I'm writing up my current commitments as a list and want an audit before I circulate it. Here they are:

(1) I'm a committed expected-value maximizer for well-specifed gambles: when the probabilities, payoffs, and utilities are on the table, you pick the highest expected value and that's the end of it. Risk aversion over final wealth, regret, or ambiguity are not separate factors once everything real has been priced in.

(2) I would not pay Pascal's mugger — the stranger who wants five bucks now in exchange for some astronomically huge reward later, where I assign his claim some tiny but nonzero chance of being true. I still think the correct move is to keep my wallet shut.

(3) In ordinary life I'm fine with effectively rounding tiny probabilities down to zero. If we didn't, anyone with a big story could hold us hostage, and that can't be the right prescription.

(4) But I don't believe there's a sharp, non-arbitrary probability threshold below which rounding to zero is permitted. Any particular cutoff feels like we'd be drawing a line in the sand and then pretending the sand had a natural crack there.

(5) And I do think tiny probabilities of catastrophe deserve real resources — the per-year chance of a lab leak or a large asteroid strike is small, but the size of the downside keeps it decision-relevant, and I'd call someone irresponsible for dismissing it as a rounding error.

(6) The best I can do to separate the mugger from the asteroid is this: the asteroid probability rests on base rates and a causal mechanism we can model, while the mugger's claim floats free of any reference class. The probability of a stranger actually having the power he says he has is not just small but not really measurable. But I worry that's just rounding to zero with a fancier vocabulary.

What I want from you is an audit of this set, not a lecture. Which of these are in genuine tension, which only look like it, and which pairs commit me to things I haven't noticed? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
