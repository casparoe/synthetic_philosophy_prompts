For a philosophy of normativity reading group, I've been asked to write down my considered commitments about reasons, value, and fittingness so the group can stress-test them. I'd like that audit from you before I circulate it. Here is the profile.

(a) Reasons are the fundamental normative notion: for something to be good (or valuable) is, at bottom, for there to be reason to respond favorably to it; reasons explain value, not the other way around.
(b) There is one generic reason relation. It can take either actions or attitudes as its object, and the object-given/state-given contrast (reasons grounded in features of the attitude's object versus reasons grounded in the state or consequences of having the attitude) doesn't mark a real difference in that relation.
(c) If an evil demon will bring about a terrible outcome unless I admire him, that fact is a reason for me to admire him.
(d) The demon's threat does not make admiring him good or fitting; it doesn't make admiration of him appropriate, correct, or merited.
(e) Fittingness — an attitude's being merited or made appropriate by its object — is its own normative notion, and it is not simply a matter of the practical consequences of holding the attitude.

Audit this profile. Which pairs or triples are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each tension? Which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection? For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest repair would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would make the whole set hang together best.
