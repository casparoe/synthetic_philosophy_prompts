I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science working on the realism/eliminativism debate about qualitative experience. Before I commit to either defending qualia or quining them, I want a specification of what a minimally adequate account of the qualitative character of sensory experience must do. Write the desiderata for such an account. Use Dennett’s Chase and Sanborn cases—two seasoned coffee tasters who agree that the same coffee now seems different from how it seemed years ago, yet locate the change differently, one in the coffee and the other in their own gustatory standard or sensibility—as a central test case, but do not treat them as the only constraint. For each desideratum, give the motivating case or consideration, and mark clearly whether it is non-negotiable for any adequate account or whether it is a desideratum specific to either a realist or an eliminativist camp. In particular, address whether the claim that there is a determinate fact about the intrinsic qualitative property itself, prior to any act of comparison, memory, or reporting, belongs on the list, or whether that is exactly what the Chase and Sanborn cases should lead us to suspend. Then briefly indicate how a realist about qualia and an eliminativist who wishes to quine them would each fare against your list, and isolate the single requirement that does the most to exclude or vindicate the posit.
