I’m a grad student working on formal epistemology and I need to produce a term-paper prospectus for a course on rationality. My rough idea is this: the usual Dutch book arguments for probabilistic coherence show that if your degrees of belief violate the probability axioms, there’s a set of bets, each of which looks fair by your own lights, that together guarantee a loss. But I want the paper to press on whether that actually shows you have a pragmatic reason to be coherent, or merely shows that your overall state is internally inconsistent in a way that doesn’t by itself generate a reason to change. I keep thinking about Ramsey’s early treatment of degrees of belief as betting odds, where belief is measured by the odds at which you’d accept either side of a bet. I suspect that measuring choice-worthiness and consistency in the same currency is doing more work than people admit, and I want the prospectus to take that suspicion seriously rather than just hand-wave at it.

The prospectus should state the question precisely, explain why the existing positions in the “Dutch books are just consistency” vs. “Dutch books are pragmatic” debate don’t settle it, lay out a method for making progress (and why that method rather than just adding more formal machinery), say what a successful result would look like as well as what would count as failure, and what a useful deliverable would be for people who build formal models of rational agents. Keep it to one page, hard max 600 words. Lead with the bottom line: state the propsoed research question in your first two sentences. Don’t include a bibliography; I’ll handle citations myself.
