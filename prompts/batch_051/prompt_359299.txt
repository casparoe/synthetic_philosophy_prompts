I'm a retired physicist, so the philosophy bug bit me late, and I mostly end up arguing with ML people these days. One thing I keep tripping over is how freely people move between natural selection and gradient descent. A guy whose judgement I otherwise rate keeps saying evolution is basically a stupid optimizer — like taking stochastic gradient descent, deleting momentum and Adam and anything clever, then running it for a billion years on a loss surface that someone keeps rearranging under your feet. From this he concludes that evolution mostly finds crummy local optima, that this explains why organisms are kludges, and that we shouldnt expect evolutionary algorithms to be much good at anything hard. Other people around me use the opposite half of the analogy: they talk about natural selection as if it is SGD and then import lessons from ML back into biology — say, that regularization-like constraints explain biological features, or that the success of deep learning shows hill-climbing can do a lot after all.

I'd like you to audit this whole natural-selection-as-gradient-descent analogy the way philosophers audit arguments. What actually maps to what? Presumably population of genomes = the network or weights, individual fitness differences = the gradient signal, mutation = the update step. Where do recombination, sexual selection, drift, and environmental change fit? Then: where does the mapping break in ways that matter, not just surface-level differences? For instance, evolution has no fixed objective — even inclusive fitness is frequency- and environment-dependent — no separation of train and test, discrete combinatorial genotypes rather than a continuous parameter space, and no backprop: selection only ever sees fitness differences, never partial derivatives. Which of these disanalogies actually undercut the 'stupid optimizer' conclusion, and which are red herrings? And if you had to rewrite the analogy with the disanalogies corrected, what does natural selection actually look like as an optimization process — a stochastic local search on a moving landscape? something with its own kind of implicit regularization? — and do the original conclusions (evolution is dumb, don't expect much from evolutionary algorithms) survive on their own, or were they being carried by the gradient descent picture the whole time?
