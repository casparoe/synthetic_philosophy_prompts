Consider a consortium of thirty independent research groups, each technically capable of unilaterally releasing a gene drive intended to suppress malaria-carrying mosquitoes. The release is either beneficial on net (malaria suppressed, with modest ecological side effects) or disastrous (a severe and irreversible collapse in the local insect food web), depending on a biological question about which each group has only noisy, imperfect evidence. Any member group may release without the others' consent, and a release cannot be undone. Assume each group acts sincerely, on its own best judgment, and each genuinely prefers to avoid a disaster.

This is a potential case of the unilateralist's curse: when many actors can each unilaterally take a risky action, the decision is in effect made by whichever actor is most optimistic, and that actor's error is unlikely to be corrected by anyone else. Please give a numerical credence for the proposition that the consortium should adopt a strict prior-approval rule, under which no group may release without the approval of all the others or of a designated review body, rather than retaining the present norm of unilateral discretion. Decompose your credence into at least the following terms, assigning each a rough probability and showing how they combine: (1) the prior probability that a unilateral release would in fact be disastrous; (2) the probability that, if it would be disastrous, at least one of the thirty groups would nevertheless mistakenly assess it as beneficial; (3) the probability that the first group to reach a favorable assessment would proceed rather than defer to the wider consortium; (4) the probability that the groups' errors are sufficiently independent for the curse to operate, rather than largely shared, in which case they would mostly err together and the curse would weaken; and (5) the probability that a prior-approval rule would actually prevent the disaster without introducing unacceptable delay, deadlock, or veto problems of its own. Then identify the term to which your final credence is most sensitive, and state what evidence about the case would move your credence the most.
