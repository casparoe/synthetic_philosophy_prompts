I'm an economist by training — went through the grad game theory sequence and wrote my thesis on cheap talk, so my home vocabulary is signaling, payoff functions, information transmission, equilibrium selection, that kind of thing. My partner is Japanese and I work at a Dutch firm, so I get the bluntness-versus-indirectness thing every day. What set this off: a colleague told me my draft was "not bad," and I spent a week reading that as a compliment before someone explained it was a polite way of saying it needed to be redone. I knew the game-theoretic reflex in my head was missing something.

Here's what I'd like restated in that vocabulary, systematically. Take (A) the usual politeness-theory picture: indirectness and white lies are strategies for managing face — roughly, the hearer's want to be approved of and their want not to be imposed on — so softening a truth is a way of performing a face-threatening act while damping the threat to the relationship. Take (B) the moralized directness line: giving someone the plain truth is respecting them as an agent who can act on it, and the white lie is objectionable because it quietly substitutes your judgment for theirs. Take (C) the moralized indirectness line: white lies and softened truths are care, an effort not to inflict gratuitous pain on someone you're in a relationship with, and bluntness can be a way of offloading your own discomfort onto them under the cover of honesty.

Translate A, B, and C one by one into game-theoretic language. For each, tell me what maps cleanly onto standard results about cheap talk, preference alignment, when signaling can be informative and when it collapses into babbling, and what only fits if I bolt on extra primitives like face, standing, or emotion. Keep the translation honest; if something has to be distorted to fit, say so, and don't sneak the game theory's assumptions in and call it a translation. Finally, your verdict on whether the residue is a defect of my framework or a sign that one of A, B, or C was more hand-wavy then it sounds.
