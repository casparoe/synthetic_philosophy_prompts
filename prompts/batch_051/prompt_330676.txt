Write a short dialogue between two philosophers disputing whether a single self can be distributed across more than one brain. Character A defends a pattern view of the kind associated with Douglas Hofstadter: a self is an abstract, self-referential pattern, so the right coarse-grained pattern could in principle be realized in two brains at once, and each realization would have a strong claim to be you in roughly the way your future self is you. Character B defends a substrate-continuity view: personal identity consists in the continued operation of one particular living body and brain with its actual causal history, so a structurally identical copy elsewhere is a separate self that merely shares your memories and dispositions. Have them press each other on the hard cases — gradual replacement of neurons, split-brain patients, uploading followed by deletion of the original, and the intuition that a duplicate is not you — and make sure they respond to each other's actual arguments rather than talking past one another; neither should be a strawman. End with a short note saying which position you think is stronger as stated, what argument or empirical finding would most change your assessment, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
