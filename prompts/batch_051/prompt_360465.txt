I've been turning over an idea for a while and I'd like to see it turned into a short story. I think I heard something like this on a podcast years ago and might be mangling it, so forgive me if the setup isn't quite right. The idea is this: people talk as if things matter because they'll outlast you — a book, a garden, a name on a bench. But the part that actually interests me isn't being remembered. It's the background feeling that human life will keep going after you die. What if we found out tomorrow, with total certainty, that humans had become infertile — no more babies, ever — but also that nobody alive would die any sooner, and the species would just wind down over the next century or so, ending when the last person now alive dies of old age. No catastrophe, no panic, everyone keeps living a normal lifespan. The thought I've been chewing on is that even so, most people's sense that their projects matter would quietly drain out of them: the researcher, the guy building a school, the couple restoring an old house, the composer writing for an audience that will soon be gone. Write me a story about that slow drain — just one person and one specific project — and show the erosion in small decisions and moments of hesitation, not in big speeches or melodrama.
