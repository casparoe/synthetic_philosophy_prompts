Been chewing on this for a while. People argue about whether a comment is racist based on what the speaker 'really meant' versus how it landed with the people who heard it, and the two come apart most sharply with coded language and dog whistles, where the speaker's got a whole innocent reading ready to go. So here's the scenario I keep coming back to. Starting tomorrow, every time anyone says anything, an accurate, tamper-proof subtitle appears under them, visible to everyone like closed captions, that states what they actually were trying to get across by saying it—their actual intent, not there words. The effects of the speech stay exactly the same: whatever a phrase usually does to its intended audience it keeps doing, and whatever offense the broader public takes, it still takes. The only new thing is that true intent is just out there in public. Predict, with reasoning, how the next ten or so years play out for racist speech, dog whistles, and the whole intent-vs-effect argument. Does the dog whistle collapse once the whistle is audible to everyone, or do people find new ways to keep the innocent reading handy? Does judgment shift back toward intent, or does 'impact over intent' get trickier once intent is objectively known? What new forms of deniability would pop up? And which of your predictions are you most confident about, versus ones that hang on assumptions about how fast people and platforms adapt?
