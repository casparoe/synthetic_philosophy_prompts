i've spent way too long in both a dojo and rationality-land (calibration drills, forecasting, pre-mortems, all that), and i keep coming back to a concept i call heat ratings for rationality training. in martial arts a kata is form work: it shows you know the movement, not that you can use it. what actually tells you whether you can use it is how much pressure the drill had. solo kata = zero heat, pad work = a little, sparring = a lot, a real fight = maximum. i think rationality training is still almost all zero-heat. calibration quizzes, base-rate problems, pre-mortems on other people's decisions: they train the form of, say, noticing the outside view, but they don't train you to execute when the decision is yours, the topic is one you're attached to, someone is waiting for you to look dumb, and the clock is running. so the claim is: grade every rationality drill by a heat level (0 = paper exercise, 1 = live but low stakes, 2 = live with social or time pressure, 3 = live with identity on the line), and don't count a skill as trained until it's survived at least one heat 2 drill. my bigger claim is that most rationality failure isnt a knowledge deficit, its a heat problem, and the reason rationality training feels like it doesn't transfer is that we only ever practice the kata.

i know this might just be hot-vs-cold cognition with a dojo metaphor, or stress inoculation, or far transfer, whatever. tell me straight: is this a real distinction or a stretched metaphor? what are the three strongest objections, and is any of them fatal rather than just uncomfortable?
