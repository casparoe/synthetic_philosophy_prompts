I retired from physical chemistry a few years back and got into philosophy of language, and I can't stop seeing translation as a constrained optimization problem: you ship as much of the source text as you can into the target language while holding certain things fixed and letting others go free. Heres the working list I use. When I judge whether a rendering counts as a translation of a particular poem, I reach for three things: (1) does it carry across roughly the same content — the situation, the speech act, what is said about whom; (2) does it preserve the verse form that partly constitutes the poem — meter, rhyme scheme, stanza shape; and (3) does it aim at a similar effect on the reader, so a joke stays a joke and a lullaby stays a lullaby. I honestly don't know whether all three are individually necessary or whether two out of three is enough — that's part of what I want you to test. Assume the translator was genuinely trying to translate, so no cheating on that front.

Now the test case. A friend set herself the exercise of rendering a sixteenth-century French poem — short rhymed lines, a song in which the speaker sends good wishes to a beloved who is ill — not by meaning but by ear. Her English version is chosen so that, read aloud, it approximates the sounds of the French line by line. The meter hangs on because the sounds drag it along, and there is some accidental rhyme, but the literal English sense is mostly nonsense: you catch a word here and there about a garden or a door, and nothing about illness or good wishes. She calls it a translation, just one that holds sound fixed and lets content be the free variable.

Does her exercise qualify as a translation of that poem under my three criteria? Lead with the bottom line — yes, no, or split — before you unpack anything. Then go through the criteria one by one and say where the verdict is forced and where the criteria just don't decide. End by saying whether the case shows one of my criteria should be added, dropped, or reweighted — in particular, wether sound should be folded into (2) as part of form, whether (1) is non-negotiable, or whether I just need an explicit ordering of the three. Hard cap: 400 words; a table is fine if it helps, but the bottom line has to come first.
