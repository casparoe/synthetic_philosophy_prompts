I'm a novelist, and I've written myself into a knot with a science-fiction scene. A woman steps into a teleporter; the machine scans her, destroys the original, and assembles two perfect copies, each with all her memories, each waking in a separate but identical room. She knows every physical fact about the machine, the rooms, the copying process. And still, from inside one of the bodies, there's a leftover question: which one is she. It isn't ordinary ignorance about how the world is—once she knows the layout and the scanning procedure, she knows everything world-level there is to know. It's closer to that dislocated feeling of waking up in a hotel and not yet knowing what city you're in, except here the "where" can't be settled by looking around, because both rooms are identical and both copies remember the same past. I have a hunch philosophy has a name for this species of uncertainty and maybe even holds that it can't be translated into ordinary factual uncertainty. I don't just want the label, though. Does this have an established name, how exactly does it differ from regular uncertainty about facts, and is it fair to treat it as a seperate kind of belief rather than just one more missing fact? And since I'm a novelist: would treating it as its own kind of thing change whether the scene can be narrated from an "omniscient" point of view, or is that just me being cute?
