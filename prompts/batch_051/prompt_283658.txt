Ok so for my econ fair I'm designing this UBI-ish thing and I want you to actually walk through what would happen if it was real, not just tell me whether its a good idea in theory. The plan: every adult gets $750 a month on a prepaid card, but the money isnt normal. It expires at the end of the month, and it can only be spent in person at a business with a storefront within 12 miles of your registered home address and fewer than 50 employees. So no rent, no utilities, no online shopping, no saving it up. Businesses that take the card can cash it out with the government for 97 cents on the dollar, and the whole thing is paid for by a tax on online purchases.

What I want to know is how it actually behaves if you drop it into a mid-sized city. Who games it first and how—like, how do people get around the 50-employee rule or the 12-mile rule? What does it do to local prices, to rents, to what shops stock and what services pop up? Do people end up buying junk they don't want just to not lose the money, and is that maybe not even a bug? What happens to someone who lives somewhere with almost no qualifying stores, or someone who works long hours and legitimately can't get to the shop before the card dies? And if it fails in the way you most expect, what's the smallest tweak that would fix that one failure without just turning the whole thing into normal cash?

At the end, tell me the part of your prediction you're least confident in, and what fact or evidence—if it came out the other way—would make you change your overall verdict on the plan.
