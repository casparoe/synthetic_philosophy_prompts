I am an undergraduate in a philosophy of law course on legal personhood, and we have been working through the analogy between corporations and possible artificial legal persons. I put together the following argument, and I cannot tell whether it is a genuine parity argument or whether it hides a bad inference. I would like a diagnosis of whether it goes through and, if not, exactly where it fails.

Here is the argument:

(1) Legal personhood is not a biological or psychological category. An entity is a legal person if the legal system assigns it the capacity to hold rights, bear duties, enter contracts, own property, and sue or be sued as one unit.

(2) The corporation is the clearest non-human legal person. The law treats a corporation as a single artificial unit so that it can hold property and enter contracts, even though the corporation is not conscious, has no sensations, and is not a moral agent in its own right.

(3) Therefore, the law grants legal personhood to corporations on functional grounds: the law needs a single unit, distinct from its members, that can aggregate rights and duties for the sake of the legal system's operations.

(4) An advanced AI system, or a near-future one, could satisfy the same functional requirements: it could hold title, be the counterparty to contracts, and have harm attributed to it as a single entity.

(5) Since the only remaining differences between a corporation and an advanced AI are metaphysical rather than functional, those differences are irrelevant to the law's reasons for granting corporate personhood.

(6) So the same grounds that support corporate legal personhood support legal personhood for advanced AI.

I feel the move from (3) to (6) is too quick, but I cannot state why. Is the argument valid? If not, which premise or step fails, and is the failure an equivocation on 'legal person,' a suppressed normative premise, or something else? If the argument is valid but some premise is false, please identify the strongest defensible version of the premises and say whether the conclusion survives that amendment.
