My friend defends the view that the physical world is made up of perceptions and that nothing about it exists apart from being perceived. The coffee mug in the break room exists over the weekend, he says, only because God continues to perceive it; in a world with no minds of any kind, he insists, there would be no mug at all. When I object that I can conceive of the mug existing with nothing whatsoever perceiving or conceiving it, he replies that this very act of conceiving puts my own mind into the picture, so I have not actually conceived of the mug as existing unconceived. What should I make of his reply? Is his argument sound, and if not, what precisely is the mistake I should press him on?
