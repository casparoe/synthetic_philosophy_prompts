I have no formal background in philosophy; I just kept running into these questions while trying to get clear about what it means to really know something, and I have ended up wedged between two convictions that seem impossible to hold together.

On one side, I have become convinced by a picture of knowledge as a kind of truth-tracking. Roughly, a true belief counts as knowledge only when the belief would have changed if the facts had been different: if the proposition were false, you would not have believed it, and if it were true under slightly different circumstances, you still would have believed it. What impresses me is how cleanly this handles cases that trip up the simpler idea that knowledge is just justified true belief. It explains why someone cannot know in advance of a draw that their ticket will lose, even if the ticket is overwhelmingly likely to lose: if it were going to win, they would still have believed it would lose. It also gives an elegant treatment of skepticism. Take the proposition that I am not a brain in a vat being fed an illusion of ordinary life. If that proposition were false—if I were such a brain in a vat—I would still believe it, because the illusion would feel exactly like this. So the belief fails the tracking test, and the view says I cannot know that I am not a brain in a vat. Yet the same view can allow that I do know I have hands, since the closest ways for "I have hands" to be false are ordinary cases, like having lost them, where I would notice and stop believing.

On the other side, I find it very hard to give up the idea that knowledge carries through deduction. If I know I have hands, and I can see that having hands entails that I am not a brain in a vat being deceived into believing I have hands, then surely I can come to know the second thing just by drawing out the implication. That seems to be part of what knowledge is for: you can reason from things you know to further things you know. But the tracking picture seems to say exactly the opposite in this case. It hands me the ordinary premise and takes away the conclusion I can see follows from it. I am not asking you to tell me which side wins. I want to know what each option costs: what it would take for the closure intuition to be the thing I give up, what it would take for the tracking view to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a way of reading "tracking" or "being in a position to know" on which both can be kept.
