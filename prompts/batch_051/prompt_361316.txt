Eric Hobsbawm and other students of nationalism have long observed that many of the symbols and rites treated as ancient by modern nations—from state ceremonies and national anthems to the kilt as a badge of Highland identity—were assembled quite recently, and Benedict Anderson's account of nationalism describes the nation as an imagined community of people who will never know one another. If the nation is an invention and its defining traditions are imagined, why do these same scholars continue to describe national identity as a real and durable force in modern history rather than as a recent collective fiction?
