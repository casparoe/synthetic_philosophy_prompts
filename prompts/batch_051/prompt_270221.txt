I'm a novelist, and I've been dragooned onto the jury for a regional book prize. We've got five shortlisted novels, seven jurors, and a meeting next Thursday to pick the winner. Last year's vote was a mess: the winner took a plurality, but a majority of the jury later admitted they'd have preferred the runner-up in a head-to-head, and the committee has been fighting ever since about whether that means the procedure was unfair. One juror—an economist, obviously—has now invoked Arrow's impossibility theorem and says no voting method can be simultaneously anonymous, respect unanimity, and dodge this 'irrelevant alternatives' problem, so we shouldn't pretend otherwise. The chair wants me to write a one-page memo for the committee recommending an actual, usable procedure for next week, one that looks the impossibility in the face instead of hand-waving it away. Give me the memo itself, no hello and no preamble; start with the recommendation in the first sentence, then the reasoning. And give the strongest case for the losing side (probably sticking with a simple plurality vote) its due rather then strawmanning it.
