Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether quantum indeterminacy could save free will from the threat of determinism. My friend kept invoking the familiar image of a single quantum event in the brain tipping the agent between two rival actions; she said the agent would be no more responsible for which action occurred than for a die roll. I replied that this places the indeterminism in the wrong part of the decision. We could not settle it, and I want to work through a precise variant where the randomness is placed, in my view, most favorably for the libertarian.

Begin with the canonical structure of the debate. The incompatibilist says: if determinism is true, every action is entailed by the distant past together with the laws of nature; since one can alter neither the past nor the laws, one could not have done otherwise. The libertarian replies that fundamental physics is indeterministic, so the entailment fails somewhere in the neural processes underlying choice. The standard counter-reply is that removing the entailment only inserts chance: if an undetermined event selects between two outputs, the agent no more controls the output than she controls a coin flip. Let that counter-reply’s image be the standard thought experiment.

Now change one feature. Suppose the quantum event never selects the action. It selects only the order, timing, and salience with which the agent’s own reasons present themselves to reflection: whether the memory of a promise surfaces before or after a competing obligation, whether one consideration is attended to before another, how long a reason lingers. Once the reasons are on the table, the rest of deliberation is deterministic: the agent weighs them according to stable standards and determinately performs whichever action the balance of the reasons before the mind supports. Assume the order of salience can change the balance, so that the same underlying inventory of cares can yield different actions on different trials, and assume the undetermined event does not bias the final weighing itself.

Run the incompatibilist, libertarian, and compatibilist analyses on this variant. Does the variant allow the libertarian to break the entailment from past and laws without collapsing into the random-twitch objection, or does the objection simply relocate from the output to the order in which reasons surface? What would the agent need to control, or own, at the level of salience for the indeterminism to count as the agent’s own doing rather than as moral luck? And is the final action determined by the agent’s reasons in exactly the sense the compatibilist requires, so that compatibilism and libertarianism converge on this variant, or do they still part ways? If they part ways, identify the property of the upstream quantum event on which the disagreement now turns.
