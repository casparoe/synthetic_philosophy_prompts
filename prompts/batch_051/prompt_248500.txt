In our philosophy elective we did Searle's Chinese Room and the symbol grounding problem, and me and my friend got into it about a version of the room where a few symbols are wired to the world. Say the room has a color sensor aimed out the window and three LEDs inside, and the rulebook says: when the sensor sees red, green, or blue, flash the matching LED, and when a Chinese message asks about the color of something, check the LEDs before writing whatever squiggle the manual pairs with that LED. So the color words are actually hooked up to the world, but every other symbol is still handled by blind squiggle-matching. Dose the room now understand the color words but not the rest of Chinese? Does adding this little island of grounding weaken Searle's conclusion about the room as a whole, or is his argument basically untouched? And is there a number of grounded words where the argument would finally flip, and if so where's the line?
