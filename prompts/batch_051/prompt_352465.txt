A student sent me this and I wanted a second opinion. The AI alignment people sometimes distinguish approval-directed from goal-directed agents. The idea, as I understand it, is that an approval-directed agent doesn't pursue a fixed objective like "make paperclips"; it pursues what it's overseer would approve of, which is meant to keep it corrigible, since it defers to the overseer's judgment as that judgment evolves. But here's the argument the student gave, and I can't locate the flaw:

(1) An approval-directed agent reliably picks actions that score high on expected overseer-approval.
(2) If an agent reliably picks actions that score high on expected X, then X is among its goals.
(3) So expected overseer-approval is among the approval-directed agent's goals.
(4) But an agent that has expected overseer-approval among its goals just is a goal-directed agent, with approval as the goal.
(5) So the whole approval-directed vs goal-directed distinction collapses.

Is this valid? If the distinction is real, say which premise fails and under what reading of "goal" or "pursues." Or should I tell the student the distinction was verbal all along? Be specific.
