I am a PhD student in philosophy, working on the aesthetics of music, and I keep returning to a tension I have not been able to resolve. On one side, I find Schopenhauer's account of music more compelling than almost anything else I have read. It has real systematic virtues: a single, unified principle explains why music can move us with an immediacy that painting or literature rarely matches. On his picture, the other arts represent the various grades of the will's objectification — roughly, the Platonic Ideas — but music stands apart because it bypasses representation and presents the inner striving of the will itself, without concepts, motives, or objects. That would explain why pure instrumental music can feel so serious, why it needs no text or program, and why it seems to disclose something about reality rather than merely depict a scene. On the other side, I have a firm intuition I cannot talk myself out of: much of the music I care about means what it does because of a particular historical and cultural idiom, and because of what it represents or accompanies. A hymn or a protest song seems to owe its power to a shared practice and a referential content, and a listener who lacks that practice is not simply receiving the same content in a dimmer form; they are receiving something importantly different. That looks flatly incompatible with the claim that music's content is a direct, culture-transcendent revelation of the will.

I would like you to present both positions at their strongest and then give me a negotiated verdict rather than simply picking a side. In particular: should my intuition be debunked and dropped — for instance, as an artifact of attending to impure or "applied" music rather than to music as such — or should Schopenhauer's theory be amended or abandoned? Or is there a reading on which the metaphysical account and the cultural specificity of musical meaning are compatible? Please say what would justify each of those moves, not just which one you favor. I would also like you to be explicit about what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Please write in American English.
