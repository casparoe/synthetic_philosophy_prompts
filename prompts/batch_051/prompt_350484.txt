Been trying to make up my mind about a view I saw called tranquilism on some forum: the idea that the only thing that matters, for it's own sake, is the absence of craving and suffering — pleasure, achievement, beauty, understanding, all of it matters only instrumentally, insofar as it quiets craving or suffering. I find the view weirdly compelling, but it immediately crashes into the word "craving." If craving just means any wanting at all, tranquilism turns into "want nothing, ever," which is obviously insane, or it has to treat a passing preference for tea over coffee as a bad state, which makes the view a parody of itself. If craving means only the gnawing, addictive kind of wanting — nicotine at 3 a.m. — then the view gets boring, because it ignores the quieter bad states: restlessness, low-grade longing, the feeling that something is off. So I want a working definition of craving, one a tranquilist could actually run with. Not a dictionary definition.

Here are the cases the definition (maybe after you revise it) should classify as craving or not craving, no hedges:

- nicotine withdrawal at 3 a.m.
- a fern growing toward a window
- wanting to scratch a mosquito bite
- feeling hungry after a long hike
- the urge to check whether someone replied to your message
- a meditator's restlessness ten minutes into sitting
- wishing a good conversation wouldn't end
- the urge to finish the last chapter of a novel
- a runner's determination to push the last 400 meters
- wanting your child to be safe
- the compulsion to wash your hands a third time
- the desperate need to breathe when you're held underwater

I genuinely don't know about half of these — hunger especially — so I'm not pre-committing you to my answers.

Format it with these sections, in this order:

- "Starting definition" — state your working definition in one or two sentences. (~100 words)
- "Case table" — go through each of the twelve cases and apply the definition: craving, not a craving, or genuinely borderline, with a phrase of justification for each. (~400 words)
- "Revisions" — say which cases put pressure on the definition and how you'd amend it, or why the surprising verdict is the one to keep. (~200 words)
- "Least secure verdict" — pick the single wobbliest call and say what it turns on. (~100 words)
- "Bottom line" — the definition a tranquilist should actually adopt, and what accepting it commits them to. (~100 words)
