Not a philosopher, just a person who reads a lot of news and argues with relatives. I've noticed something I can't quite put my finger on. It seems like political positions travel in packs: if I learn someone is pro-choice, I can make a decent guess about their views on guns, immigration, climate, and even stuff like school vouchers, even though there's no logical connection between any of those things. Fine, everyone knows that part. But here's what bothers me: the contents of the packs keep changing while the pack labels stay the same. When I was younger, "right-wing" around here meant small government, free trade, and traditional values; now a lot of right-wing people I know are cheering tariffs and industrial policy, which looks an awful lot like big government, and they still call it right-wing and still cluster with the same crowd on other issues. Same on the other side: "left-wing" used to imply a certain kind of internationalism, but I know plenty of left-wing people who are now pretty protectionist too, and others who'd call themselves left while disagreeing with each other on speech and policing. So the label stays put while the bundle underneath it drifts, and the clustering is real but not logical. Is there an established name for this exact thing — not just "people have ideologies," but the persistence of a label through changes in what it stands for, and the fact that the cluster holds together by something other than logical consistency? If the nearest concepts don't quite fit, tell me wich ones are closest and where the edges are. And if there's no name, what would you call it, and what's a clean definition that would let me tell when I'm looking at this phenomenon versus just ordinary disagreement or people following a party line?
