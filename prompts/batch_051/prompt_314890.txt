I am a high school student in a physics-and-philosophy reading group, and I have reconstructed an argument that strikes me as too strong. It seems to show that I should be nearly certain that the reasoning that produced it is untrustworthy. Here is the argument.

(1) On one widely accepted cosmological picture, the universe has an effectively endless future in which thermal fluctuations spontaneously assemble highly ordered configurations that then dissolve.
(2) Some of those fluctuations would be short-lived systems functionally equivalent to a human brain at a moment, complete with apparent perceptions, apparent memories, and apparent chains of thought. Call these Boltzmann brains.
(3) For any current internal state like mine, the Boltzmann-brain realizations of that state vastly outnumber—or have vastly greater measure than—ordinary realizations, because an ordinary realization requires an entire low-entropy universe to evolve life, while a Boltzmann brain requires only a local fluctuation.
(4) If the vast majority of states internally indistinguishable from mine are Boltzmann brains, then standard probabilistic reasoning says I should be nearly certain that I am a Boltzmann brain.
(5) But a Boltzmann brain's apparent memories—including my apparent memory of learning the cosmology in (1), checking the calculation in (3), and now evaluating this argument—are not reliable records of anything real.
(6) Therefore the reasoning from (1) to (4) tells me to distrust the very evidence on which (1) and (3) rest. An argument cannot rationally require me to be nearly certain that its own premises are cognitive noise.

Where does this go wrong? If the mistake is a slide between steps, identify it; if the argument is valid, which premise is least costly to reject, and what do I give up by rejecting it? I would most like to keep (1) and (2), and I would prefer not to rule out the possibility of treating my own experiences as evidence about what kind of observer I am, since that seems to abandon much of probabilistic reasoning about my place in the universe. But I cannot see how to keep those commitments and still block the conclusion.
