Some context: I'm a grad student helping to run a small standig workshop on humour and cognition. Our group has spent the last several sessions going in circles because two of our regulars keep talking past each other. One works in the incongruity tradition — the view that amusement is a response to a mismatch between what we expect and what actually happens, especially when the mismatch can be partly made sense of. The other works on benign violation theory — the view that something is funny when it violates a norm, a social convention, a category boundary, or a sense of how things ought to be, while simultaneously registering as harmless or acceptable. After our last session on cringe comedy (why a toast that runs thirty seconds too long is painful-funny), the two camps each insisted the other's favourite cases were just special cases of their own account, and the rest of us cant tell how much of this is real disagreement and how much is terminology.

I want you to draft the joint statement that these two positions could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please structure it as: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, with claims both sides can sign without wincing and phrased so neither would call them slanted; (2) a disagreements table, with columns for the precise point at issue, how the incongruity side words its own view, and how the benign-violation side words its own view, such that each column's wording is one its own side would endorse; (3) a list of cruxes — sub-questions that, if settled, would actually move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete test case (real or invented) both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Try especially to say whether the benign condition in benign violation theory is doing independent explanatory work or is just a disguised version of incongruity resolution, and state what each side would count as evidence against its own account if the data came out the wrong way.

End the document with a single line beginning "Residual disagreement:" and giving a one-sentence summary of the one issue that remains most stubbornly unresolved.
