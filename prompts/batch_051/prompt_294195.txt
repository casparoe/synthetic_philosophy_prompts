I've landed on a view in the do-LLMs-understand debate that doesn't run through the usual grounding or original-intentionality stuff, and I want to know how badly it's exposed. My position: understanding a claim requires being answerable for it later — being corrected has to be different from being fed a new prompt. An LLM, or anyway any system that just conditions on a fresh context window, can be redirected but not corrected, because it has no persistent commitments that could come apart from whatever the latest input asks for; there's only weights plus the current string. So it doesn't understand even if it passes every behavioral test you throw at it. What would count against this? I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: which results, arguments, or near-term developments should actually move me, which ones would I dismiss as changing the subject, and where a defender of my view has to admit the position is being propped up by a definition of 'understanding' — and whether that's a problem or just what a view of this kind needs.
