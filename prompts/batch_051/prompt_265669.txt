During a seminar on judicial review last week, a coursemate dismissed a ruling in which an apex court struck down a statute by saying the judges were 'legislating from the bench'. The phrase was made to carry the entire objection to unelected judges overruling elected legislators, but it was never unpacked, and I left unsure what precisely had been claimed. I would like a menu of the distinct theses this slogan could be expressing: for example, that the judges decided on grounds of policy rather than law, that they departed from the enacted text, that they laid down a general rule for future cases, that they substituted their own judgement for the legislature's, or merely that their decision had broad political consequences. For each reading, I would want a verdict on whether it is trivially true of much ordinary judging, a substantive and defensible criticism, or false, and an indication of which reading would actually have to be established for the charge to bite against the practice of judicial review itself rather than against one particular decision. I want something I can take to the next seminar and use to press anyone who repeats the slogan.
