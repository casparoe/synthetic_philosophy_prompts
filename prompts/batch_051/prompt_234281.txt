Proposition: fundamental quantum-level randomness in the neural processes leading up to action makes a positive and important contribution to human free will — that is, if human actions are ever free, their freedom depends in part on such randomness, and the randomness strengthens the agent's control rather than merely adding noise. By "free action" I mean the sort of control over one's behaviour that could make praise and blame appropriate.

Give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Items 1 to 4 should each state a number (0–100%) and a single sentence of justification; item 5 should state the final credence and the sensitivity.

1. Your credence that free action requires an ability to do otherwise that determinism rules out, so that no wholly deterministic system could act freely in the relevant sense.
2. Your credence that quantum-level randomness in the brain is amplified into behaviourally significant differences in neural processing, rather than being damped out before it could matter.
3. Conditional on such amplified chancy neural events occurring, your credence that an agent who could, on an exact replay of the situation, choose either way is thereby exercising control, rather than being subject to luck.
4. Conditional on items 1–3 all being correct, your credence that indeterminism would be doing essential work in the explanation of free action, rather than being a replaceable detail of one particular physical implementation.
5. Your overall credence in the proposition, how you combine the first four terms (noting any dependencies that make the combination non-multiplicative), and the one of items 1–4 you are least confident in, with a single piece of evidence or argument that would most shift it.

Please write the entire answer in British English.
