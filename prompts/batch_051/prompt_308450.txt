My younger brother has his sentencing hearing in three weeks. Last October he had two drinks at a going-away party, drove home on a back road, looked down to skip a song on his phone, drifted onto the shoulder, and killed a woman who had pulled over to change a tire. He wasn't drunk, just going a little too fast for the road and distracted for one second. Before this happened he was the cautious one of us—fire extinguisher under the sink, spare key at the neighbors, checks the stove twice. Since the arraignment he's been in county jail reading Nagel on moral luck, and on the phone last week he told me he doesn't want the "any of us could have done it" speech. He says that's the one thing that would let him off the hook, and he's not sure he wants to be let off. His attorney thinks a short statement from me will help, since I've known him his whole life.

Will you write it? About three minutes. It can't use the phrase moral luck or name-drop Nagel, and it can't point out that everybody glances at their phone, at least not as an excuse. It also can't turn the woman's death into a lesson or into something that happened to him. But I do want the judge, and her family sitting ten feet away, to feel how close any careful person is to being him—without me being manipulative, and without saying anything my brother would hear as an excuse. Write it like something a person could actually say out loud.
