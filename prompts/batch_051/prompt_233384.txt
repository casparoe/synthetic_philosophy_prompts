I'm a budget analyst for a mid-size city, and every spring I have to put together a short recommendation on how to split discretionary funds between parks, library hours, senior meals, and small-business grants. Our finance director wants every option scored on a single "resident wellbeing" number so we can just rank them and pick the top. I worry some of these goods aren't the same kind of thing — you can't really trade off more library hours against more meals — and that forcing them onto one scale hides the actual choice rather than informing it. Write me a decision procedure I can reuse each budget cycle that's built for the possibility that human values are plural, not one thing in disguise: the questions to ask in order, when a common score is acceptable and when I should refuse to use one, what counts as enough evidence to move between steps, and where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to an elected official because it's a values judgement not arithmetic. Then run it on two cases: one where two programs score nearly the same on wellbeing but serve entirely different groups, and one where a cheap program scores low but would be the only thing reaching a badly-off group. Keep it usable by someone who has never read philosophy.
