One of my grad school advisors has a running fight with a sociologist friend about when culture gets to count as a cause of economic outcomes, and he's built up a track record that looks pretty consistent. I want to predict his next verdict before he gives it.

His past verdicts, roughly: on the honor-culture explanation of Southern U.S. violence, yes — real cultural cause, specific mechanism and transmission, survives controls. On the northern Italy civic-tradition explanation for why regional governments there work better than in the south, no — that's inherited institutions doing the work, not something in people's values. On the line that countries are rich because their populations have pro-market or high-trust values, no — the values are mostly a consequence of good institutions, the causality runs backwards. On Confucian respect for education explaining East Asian growth, no — one vague "culture" was used to explain stagnation before 1960 and growth after, and the parts that actually help are exams and schools, i.e. institutions. On German manufacturing being down to a culture of punctuality and quality, no — just-so story, real mechanism is firms and vocational training. On the old Weber thesis that Calvinist doctrine produced the spirit of capitalism — he says yes at the level of a merchant minority's work habits, but no as an account of why industrialization happened where it did.

The new case we're arguing about: in parts of Latin America and West Africa, converts to Pentecostalism start small businesses at measurably higher rates than their own siblings who stayed in older denominations, and the effect seems to run through tithing discipline, sobriety, and saving. No big institutional difference between converts and non-converts in the same town.

What I want isn't just a yes/no; I want (a) his likely verdict on whether this is a case where religious values cause economic behavior, (b) the implicit rule he seems to be running about when "culture" can be a cause, (c) at least one rival rule that fits his past verdicts about as well but gives the opposite answer here, and (d) a further case that would cleanly tell the two rules apart. Also tell me which of his past verdicts is doing the most work in your prediction.
