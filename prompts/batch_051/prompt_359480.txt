I'm a PhD student in political theory and I keep getting tangled up in whether Bentham's 'nonsense upon stilts' is an actual argument or just a great insult. I've tried to reconstruct it as a step-by-step inference, but I cant tell if it goes through or if one of the moves is doing something shifty:

(1) Rights and duties are correlative: to say A has a right is just to say someone (or some institution) has a duty toward A.
(2) Duties can only be imposed by positive law — there is no such thing as a duty that exists prior to, or independently of, law.
(3) So rights can only exist where there is positive law.
(4) A 'natural right' is by definition a right that would exist prior to and independently of positive law.
(5) Therefore there are no natural rights.
(6) And since 'there is a natural right to X' doesn't refer to any real legal right, it is either false or just a way of saying 'I really wish people had X' — hence Bentham's 'nonsense upon stilts.'

I cant figure out whether step (2) is just question-begging against the natural rights theorist, or whether Bentham is entitled to it as a stipulation about what 'duty' means. And I'm not even sure (5) to (6) is the same as merely denying natural rights exist — does 'nonsense' follow? Tell me which step, if any, fails, and which one a natural rights theorist should attack first. At the very end, put \answer{...} containing the number of the first step that fails, or \answer{valid} if you think the whole argument is sound.
