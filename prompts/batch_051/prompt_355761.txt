Consider the color phi phenomenon. Two dots flash in quick succession at different positions, and subjects report seeing a single dot moving between them, even though they can't say the second dot's color until after it appears. Dennett and Kinsbourne frame this with two revisionist models: Orwellian and Stalinesque. In the Orwellian model, consciousness initially represents the first dot as red, then a moving dot, but the red part gets revised before it reaches memory. In the Stalinesque model, consciousness itself is delayed so the moving dot is inserted before the subject ever consciously experiences the red dot.
