I am an undergraduate in an upper-level seminar on deflationary theories of truth, and I have constructed an argument that I cannot find my way out of. It seems to show that deflationism about truth, together with classical logic and a natural assumption about a self-referential proposition, collapses into contradiction. Since I am reluctant to accept that deflationism is refuted by such a cheap maneuver, I need help locating the exact step at which the argument goes wrong—or, if the argument is valid, deciding which premise should be given up.

The argument runs as follows.

(1) Deflationism about truth holds that the entire content of the concept of truth is captured by the equivalence schema: for any proposition p, the proposition that p is true iff p.

(2) The schema is meant to apply to every proposition without restriction, including propositions that themselves concern truth.

(3) Let L be the proposition that L is not true. That is, L just is the proposition that ascribes non-truth to L itself.

(4) Applying the equivalence schema to the proposition that L is not true gives: the proposition that L is not true is true iff L is not true.

(5) But the proposition that L is not true is identical to L, since that is exactly how L was introduced.

(6) Substituting in (4), we obtain: L is true iff L is not true.

(7) By classical logic, no proposition can be equivalent to its own negation, so (6) is a contradiction.

(8) Therefore deflationism, together with classical logic and the existence of L, entails a contradiction.

(9) The ordinary concept of truth is not contradictory; we use the truth predicate in everyday and scientific discourse without deriving absurdities.

(10) Therefore deflationism cannot be the whole story about truth.

That conclusion strikes me as too strong, but I cannot identify the defect in the reasoning. I would most like to hold onto the unrestricted equivalence schema and classical logic, but the argument seems to force some concession. Which step should I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost the deflationist? I am looking for a precise diagnosis: name the step, explain why it initially looked unavoidable, and give an accounting of what the available repairs commit one to—rather than merely rehearsing the liar paradox or listing standard options.
