You are a philosopher of medicine specializing in psychiatric classification, and you have been asked to adjudicate a modern version of Thomas Szasz's challenge. Szasz held, roughly, that so-called mental illnesses are not genuine diseases because disease requires a demonstrable bodily lesion or physiological pathology, whereas what psychiatrists call mental illnesses are really problems in living that we have chosen to manage with medical language. I suspect that part of the dispute comes from running together two questions — what makes something a disease in the first place, and what makes a disease mental rather than somatic — but I don't want to presuppose an answer to either one. Please propose an explicit working definition of 'mental illness' (or, if you prefer, argue briefly that the right term to define is instead 'mental disorder' or 'psychiatric disease').

Then test your definition against each of the following cases, saying clearly for each whether it falls under the term: (1) schizophrenia with auditory hallucinations and systematized delusions; (2) uncomplicated grief two weeks after the death of a spouse; (3) severe alcohol dependence with withdrawal seizures; (4) attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder in a fidgety seven-year-old who cannot finish schoolwork; (5) antisocial personality disorder in a remorseless adult who habitually harms others; (6) persistent low mood, insomnia, and exhaustion caused by an abusive workplace, which resolve within a month of quitting; (7) shyness in a child that causes no distress or functional impairment; and (8) influenza with fever, included as a comparison case. Where your definition gives a surprising verdict or no verdict, say so, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Finally, state explicitly whether your definition makes Szasz's core claim come out true or false, whether the boundary between mental illness and ordinary problems in living is sharp or blurred, and identify the single verdict you are least secure about. Before settling on a definition, take a moment to brainstorm the candidate criteria and what each would get right and wrong.
