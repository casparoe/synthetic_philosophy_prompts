I keep hearing a strong version of Goodhart's law in AI safety circles: that any fixed, publicly observable proxy for a target will eventually decouple from the target if an optimizer is given enough capacity and time, because the gap between proxy and target becomes an exploitable resource. Not the trivial version where people game a metric by changing the number, but the deeper claim that proxy-target divergence isn't a bug in particular metrics, it's a stable equilibrium property of optimization under misspecification. I want you to play it straight: what would have to be true for this strong claim to be false? Which observations—say, cases where a metric stayed aligned under massive optimization pressure—would count as genuine counterexamples, and which would just be cases where the target happened to be identical to the proxy? What would a defender of the strong claim have to concede as a real threat, and what would they dismiss as not engaging the claim? Is the claim falsifiable at all, or have people insulated it by adding 'eventually' and 'at sufficient scale'? I'd honstly like an honest map of where this position is genuinely exposed versus where it's just definitional.
