So I've got this rule I've been following for a while and I honestly can't tell if it's actually a good epistemic policy or just a way to feel more sophisticated than everyone else while not changing my mind. The rule is: I refuse to write off a whole field or practice untill I've looked at its strongest version, defended by someone genuinely smart who took it seriously, rather than judging it by the pop version everyone makes fun of. My freind says this is broken, and it came to a head over astrology. He said it's "literally just confirmation bias" and nobody with a modern brain should need to look at it again. I wanted to just agree, but then I remembered Kepler from physics class—the same Kepler whose three laws of planetary motion we had to graph last month. Apparently that Kepler had a whole horoscope-casting practice on the side, but he also trashed the usual zodiac stuff: he thought most specific predictions were superstition, that whatever astrology did, it did by nudging the soul the way music sets a mood, not by forcing events, and that it was way weaker and less predictable than the professionals claimed. So one of the best astronomers of his era, whose math our whole unit is built on, still thought a cleaned-up version of astrology was worth defending.

My friend's comeback is that I'm just using one dead genius as a shield, and that by my rule I'd also have to keep phrenology open because some smart-ish people took it seriously. That stings because at the end of the day I can't defend any actual astrological claim myself; I've just decided not to close the question. So: is this rule actually defensible as a habit for forming beliefs, or is it just bias dressed up as a rule? Where does it work and where does it break, and is there a version of it I could keep that gets the good part (not dunking on the dumbest version of a thing) without leaving every crank idea "still open" forever?
