I'm a software engineer who got roped into grading a short-answer question for an undergrad philosophy of mind course (a friend teaches it and needed a second grader). The topic is the mental imagery debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn. The exam question was:

"In a few sentences, explain the central disagreement in the mental imagery debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn. Is the disagreement about whether people actually experience mental images, or about something else? Mention at least one piece of experimental evidence and at least one reason to be cautious about drawing a strong conclusion from it."

Here's what the student wrote:

"Kosslyn and Pylyshyn disagree about whether mental imagery is a genuine kind of representation. Kosslyn says yes — when people imagine things they form depictive representations that are like pictures projected onto a mental screen, with actual spatial layout and metric distance. His scanning experiments support this: people studied a map, then mentally 'looked' from one landmark to another, and their reaction times scaled with the distance between landmarks. If the representation were just a description, why would an extra inch of the map matter? Pylyshyn says the underlying representation is actually propositional, like sentences in a language of thought. He says the scanning results are an artifact of tacit knowledge: subjects know how scanning a real map works, so they unconsciously make their response times match what a real scan would be like — it's performance, not representation. For him there's nothing in the head that's actually image-like. Later, brain imaging settled the debate in Kosslyn's favor: studies show the visual cortex lights up during imagery, so imagery really does use the visual system."

I need to give this a grade out of 10 and write comments that justify the grade, point by point. The student can contest the grade, so I can't just say "mostly okay" — I need to know exactly what's right, what's subtly wrong, and where each deduction comes from. I'm a software engineer, not a philospher, so spell out the reasoning instead of assuming I already know the standard view.
