I keep going back and forth on the moral equality of combatants in just war theory — the idea that soldiers on both sides have the same moral permissions to kill one another, no matter which side's cause is just. In a reading group someone pushed me to say whether "same permissions" means both sides are permitted, or just that there's a symmetry. I ended up defending a version where the symetry runs the other way: that both sides are forbidden to kill except in immediate self-defense. But I can't shake the feeling I rigged the argument. Here's the step-by-step as I gave it:

(1) A soldier fighting for an unjust side usually isn't in a position to know their side is unjust: the state controls the information, expert opinion is divided, and refusing to fight carries enormous legal and social pressure. So in the typical case they aren't fighting out of malice or a culpable disregard for the truth.
(2) If someone is not blameworthy for what they are doing, then they have not made themselves liable to be killed by doing it — they haven't forfeited their ordinary right not to be killed.
(3) If a person hasn't forfeited their right not to be killed, then killing them is murder, except in immediate self-defense when they're directly attacking you.
(4) So when a soldier on the just side kills an ordinary enemy soldier, they kill someone who hasn't forfeited the right not to be killed.
(5) Therefore just combatants lack the permission to kill enemy combatants. And unjust combatants obviously can't be permitted to kill in service of an unjust cause. So the real symmetry is the opposite of what I was taught: nobody has the right to kill, on either side. That is, moral equality turns out to be pacifism.

I'm not a pacifist, and I don't think I've refuted all of just war theory in five steps, so I probably pulled a fast one somewhere. Tell me straight: which step fails, and how confident are you in that diagnosis? If more than one step is wobbly, say which is the main one. And if you think the argument actually goes through and I should just accept the pacifism — or that I'm using "moral equality of combatants" in a nonstandard way from the start — say that plainly instead of politely working around it.
