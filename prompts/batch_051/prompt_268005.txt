Heres the bind I keep putting myself in. I find the foundationalist picture really compelling: a chain of reasons has to stop somewhere, since coherentism just loops and infinitism seems to confuse an infinite series I can define with something I actually have. But then I take any proposed basic belief—"I'm in pain," "2+2=4"—and I can still ask why I should trust it, and the only answers seem to be another belief (so it wasn't basic) or something like "it's just obvious." And "it's just obvious" feels less like a justification and more like a decision to stop asking. So the view that actually ends the regress ends it by fiat, and the views that don't end it by fiat don't end it at all. I don't want a standard rundown of the three positions. I want to know what I'd have to give up to keep the intuition that no stopping point is forced, what I'd have to give up to keep foundations, and whether theres a version where the regress can terminate without any particular link being special. Is my intuition a real objection, or am I misreading what a basic belief is supposed to do?
