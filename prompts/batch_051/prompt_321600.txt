Here's something I've never quite got about the paradox of phenomenal judgment. The standard objection seems to be that epiphenomenalism says qualia cause nothing, so my judgment "that hurts" isn't caused by the pain—it's caused by brain processes instead. But that isn't a contradiction. An epiphenomenalist can say the same brain process that produces the judgment also produces the quale, so the judgment and the quale are correlated without the quale doing any causal work. What I don't see is why this correlation is supposed to add up to a paradox, rather than just being a case of two effects sharing a cause. Am I missing something, or is the "paradox" just an overblown way of pointing at the correlation? I'd rather you commit to an answer than lay out the dialectical options.
