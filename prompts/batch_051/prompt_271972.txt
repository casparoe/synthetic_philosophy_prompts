I am preparing a section of my jurisprudence seminar on the history of legal personality, and a question has begun to seem larger than the historical material itself. The chartered trading companies of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries were constituted by royal or parliamentary charter as collective legal persons, yet they levied troops and taxes, minted coin, concluded treaties, administered courts, and governed territories—powers we now class as sovereign. The standard reply is that these were transitional creatures, hybrids that were later sorted into the clean categories of "state" and "corporation." I want to test a stronger thesis: that the sorting was more a matter of political taxonomy than of a difference in the underlying kind of entity, and that the modern state is only a corporation with a distinctive and especially consequential bundle of recognized powers and a legitimating story to match.

I would like you to give me your credence in the following proposition, stated as precisely as I can put it: the sovereign state and the business corporation are not different fundamental kinds of entity; both are artificial legal persons, and every enduring difference between them is either a difference in the particular competences a legal order confers on them or a difference in the normative rationale under which those competences are exercised, rather than a difference in what they are. My own credence presently sits around 0.65, but the process is what I want, not confirmation. Please do not answer with a bare number. I want the number to rest on a decomposition of the proposition into its load-bearing sub-questions—for example, whether there are natural kinds of legal person at all, whether sovereignty is one indivisible status or a bundle of discrete competences, whether the public/private distinction is a structural feature of legal personality or a political classification, whether the chartered companies genuinely exercised sovereign powers rather than merely enjoying delegated private privileges, and whether the state's claimed monopoly on legitimate coercion marks a difference of justification or of ontological kind. Assign a rough probability to each of these, explain how you combine them into your overall credence, tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and say what argument or evidence would move you most on that point.
