News outlets and pollsters keep asking people to place themselves on a left–right scale, then report the results as if a country moving right named a single real shift. But the same surveys throw up people who are, say, anti-immigration and pro-tax-the-rich, or free-market and pro-weed — combinations that cut across the one scale. What does this practice presuppose about what left and right are, and about what holds each side together? Does it presuppose that one underlying dimension organizes all our policy views, that the left and right bundles are roughly stable across issues and countries, and that the cross-cutting cases are just noise? Or could the practice still be worth keeping even if those presuppositions are false, the way we keep a crude map because it beats no map? And what would have to change about how we report and argue about politics if theyre badly false?
