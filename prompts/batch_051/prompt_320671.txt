i'm in a seminar on achievement and whether effort can be valuable for its own sake, and I've ended up with a set of commitments but I genuinely can't tell if they hang together. Can you audit the set rather than lecture me on the topic?

(1) Effort is valuable for its own sake, not just instrumentally: the struggling itself adds something to a life, over and above better results or the character it builds.
(2) The value of effort depends on its being voluntarily taken on. Sisyphus forced to push the rock gets nothing good from the pushing, while Sisyphus who chooses to push the same rock does; and it's the choosing, not the pushing, that does the value work.
(3) What's good about effort is that it's an exercise of agency — me putting myself into what i do and being active rather than passive.
(4) The value of an outcome doesnt depend on how much effort went into it. A cure discovered by accident is just as good an outcome as one that took twenty years, and a paper that came easily can be just as good a paper.
(5) But the person who struggled deserves more admiration, and their life goes better in that respect, even when the outcome is the same.
(6) The felt unpleasantness of effort — strain, fatigue, boredom — is pure cost. If you could keep all the striving and agency without the felt strain, that would be strictly better.
(7) Holding outcome fixed, finding a clever easier way is always at least as good as grinding through the hard way.
(8) I also think effort only matters because it tracks something else, like caring or trying or the connection between an agent and an outcome; once you've fixed that underlying thing, effort itself drops out.

Go through and say which pairs are really in tension, rank those tensions by severity, flag which pairs only seem to conflict and why, and then tell me what the cheapest fix would be and which single revision cleans up the most.
