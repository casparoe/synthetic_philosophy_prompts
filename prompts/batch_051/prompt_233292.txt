Among the engineers I work with — people who came to the philosophy of mind mostly through technical blog posts and conference talks — a saying has hardened into conventional wisdom: the frame problem was an artifact of classical, symbolic AI, and deep learning did not so much solve it as dissolve it. The analogy used to support this is automatic memory management. Just as garbage collection relieved the programmer of having to decide, by hand, when an object on the heap could safely be freed, representation learning (and the attention mechanism in particular) is said to relieve the system designer of having to decide, by hand, which features of a situation are relevant to acting in it. On this view, relevance is now picked up from data through gradient descent, and worrying about the frame problem today is like worrying about manual memory leaks in a garbage-collected language: a sign that you have not updated your mental model of how modern systems actually work. The analogy is made to carry a fairly strong conclusion — that philosophers and earlier AI researchers elevated an implementation difficulty into a deep puzzle, and that the difficulty is now moot.

I find myself resisting this conclusion but lacking the background to say exactly why. I would like you to audit the analogy properly. Build the correspondence table: if the frame problem is the problem of relevance-selection, what plays the role of the object graph, the roots, the reachability analysis, the collection cycle, the stop-the-world pause, the leak, and the dangling pointer? Then separate the disanalogies that matter from the ones that do not. My suspicion, which I cannot yet defend, is that garbage collection operates inside a fixed, pre-specified graph of references, whereas the frame problem concerns how a system settles on the space of possibilities and dependencies it will even consider, so the analogy may quietly import the very structure that was at issue. I want to know whether that suspicion is right, whether the analogy can be repaired without circularity, and what the claim that deep learning dissolved the frame problem amounts to once the mapping has been tested. If the analogy ultimately breaks, tell me what a better computational analogy would be for what remains hard about the frame problem; if no such analogy is available, say so.
