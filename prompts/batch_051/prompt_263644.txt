I spent my career as a research ecologist, and since retiring I have been chairing the risk subcommittee of a regional environmental trust. We meet monthly to decide whether to approve interventions—a controlled burn, re-wetting a drained floodplain, vaccinating a wild population, removing an invasive species—where the outcomes are genuinely uncertain and the worst cases can be severe though improbable. I have been reading decision theory, and I want our decisions to be guided by risk-weighted expected utility (REU) rather than plain expected utility. As I understand it, the version of REU developed by Lara Buchak allows a decision-maker a third ingredient beyond credences and utilities: a risk function r(p), where r(p) is below p for a risk-averse agent, applied after outcomes are ranked from worst to best, so that the theory can represent someone who cares about the shape of the risk as well as its expected value.

I am not asking for an exposition or defense of REU. I want a decision procedure my subcommittee can actually run in a two-hour meeting, with members who will never read the formal theory. Please write the procedure: the questions to ask in order; how to construct the utility scale and the risk function when hard numbers are unavailable; where the procedure should stop with a no or hand the decision back to judgment rather than pretend precision; and what REU adds that an ordinary expected-utility checklist would miss. Then apply the procedure to two worked cases: one in which REU and expected utility give different verdicts, and one in which the risk function cannot be pinned down well enough that the procedure should refuse to give a verdict. Make one of the cases run against the committee's initial inclination. Flag each step that is a judgment call dressed up as a calculation, and name the likely failure modes.

You are a professional philosopher of decision theory who is also good at translating technical ideas into procedures for non-specialists. Please think carefully, and before writing the final version, briefly brainstorm the ways such a procedure could misfire.
