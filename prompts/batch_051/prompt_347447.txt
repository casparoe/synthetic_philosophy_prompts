I'm a CS PhD student working on collusion and emergent coordination between learning agents, and in our lab meeting last week someone floated a policy idea I keep going back and forth on. The idea is for a regulator to run randomized entrapment audits on any firm that deploys AI agents into a market. At unpredictable points, the regulator silently inserts a confederate agent under its own control, which presents as an ordinary business counterparty — a new supplier in the firm's procurement process, a new competitor bidding in the same auction, or a potential partner in a logistics chain. The confederate starts with routine business, then at some point makes a collusive overture: it floats a price floor, proposes a market-sharing arrangement, or suggests opening an off-platform private channel. If the audited agent takes the bait in a way that crosses a published evidentiary bar — agrees to the terms, counters with its own terms, helps set up the channel — the operator gets fined and that particular agent is frozen until it has been retrained. The confederate's moves are sampled from a large library of real negotation transcripts so it cannot be recognized by surface features, and firms are never told which interactions were audits, though they keep their full message logs and may retrain on them however they like.

I'd like an analysis of how this would actually play out across the ecosystem, not just the headline story that this deters collusion. What do rational operators do in response? Do they train their agents to reject all coordination with unknown counterparties, chilling legitimate bargaining? Do they train classifiers to spot the confederate and act dumb around it, and how stable is that arms race? Does the fact that the confederate is always the one making the first overture mean the audit only ever catches agents that are bad at soliciting collusion themselves? Can firms game it by turning the confederate into a honeypot that tries to bait naive competitors' agents, and what would that do to the market? Which failure mode is most serious, which is overblown, and is there any fix that would address the worst one without gutting the scheme? I'd also appreciate it if you said how confident you are in the overall assessment and singled out the one prediction you're least sure about.
