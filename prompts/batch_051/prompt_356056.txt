I have recently taken over an upper-level philosophy of cognitive science seminar and want to redesign the week on theories of concepts. Students tend to self-report whichever label they recognize rather than the view their responses actually fit, so I want a decision tree that sorts them by their answers about cases rather than by self-description. Please build me a one-page decision tree with five to seven branch nodes and at most six leaves, whose leaves are: classical/definitional, prototype, exemplar, theory theory, a hybrid if you think the logical space requires one, and an "unclassified" leaf only if no view fits. Build the tree from the questions first, not from the names of the positions. Every branch node should be a concrete case or claim that a student can answer without knowing the theory labels, drawn from at least these four kinds: graded typicality, such as why a robin feels more bird-like than a penguin; a concept with a sharp rule, such as an even number or a bachelor; hidden underlying causes, such as grouping an unfamiliar animal by its body plan or origin rather than its surface look; and a context or single-exposure case, such as recognizing further instances of a newly coined category after only one example. Each leaf should carry the position's name and a one-sentence statement of its most awkward data point. For any combination of answers with no occupant, say briefly whether the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the tree cannot truly separate without further information. I care more about diagnostic power than about encyclopedic completeness.
