I sit on a coastal city's emergency-planning commission, and next month we vote on whether to base evacuation orders on a newly proposed storm-surge model. The model predicts how far inland floodwater will reach and how quickly, using a coarse-grained set of equations with a few empirically fitted coefficients; it deliberately does not resolve currents below the scale of a city block and treats buildings, dunes, and bridges only through averaged parameters. One external reviewer, a fluid dynamicist, argues that we should not rely on it unless it is coupled to a much finer-grained street-level simulation, because the model omits the small-scale flows through streets and around structures in which the water actually moves. On his view, a model has to represent those lower-level processes to be a real explanation of the surge, not merely a statistical summary of past storms. The engineer who built the coarse model replies that the sub-block detail barely feeds back into the variables the commission acts on: small differences in street-level flow do not shift the predicted reach or arrival time, and the fitted coefficients already absorb what matters from the fine scales. He adds that resolving more detail would increase computational cost enormously without changing any evacuation threshold. The two approaches have effectively identical predictive accuracy on the city's historical storms, so this is not a dispute about which forecasts better; it is a dispute about whether the larger-scale description can be genuinely explanatory and complete on its own.

I have to write the commission's statement explaining whether we can rationally rely on the coarse-grained model, and I want to get the underlying principle right rather than merely approve it because its track record has been good. How should I think about when omitted fine-scale detail matters for explanation and when it can properly be ignored? Is the reviewer right that only the lower-level physics explains, or can a higher-level model be a genuine explanation in its own right even while remaining silent about much of the lower-level detail? What principle should determine when a coarse-grained account has earned that status?
