My friend Nadia has a set of verdicts on uploading cases that I can't reverse-engineer. Here they are. (1) Star Trek transporter: you're disassembled and a molecule-for-molecule replica is assembled at the destination. She says you die and the person who steps off is a copy. (2) Gradual neuron replacement: over a decade, one neuron at a time, yours are swapped for functionally identical silicon ones while you stay awake and active the whole time. She says it's still you, no death. (3) Destructive upload: your brain is scanned into a simulation, then the biological brain is destroyed. She says you die and the sim is a copy. (4) Non-destructive upload: a perfect simulation of you runs on a computer while the biological you walks around. She says the sim is a distinct person, not you.

Next week's case is a slow teleporter. It takes a year, beaming you to another city one neuron at a time: each original neuron is destroyed only after its replacement is built and running there, you're conscious and talking the whole way through, and by the end a complete working body is at the destination and the original is gone — but no whole duplicate ever exists, and the matter never travels; each unit there is built from new atoms.

Predict Nadia's verdict, and be explicit about which rule or rules she's actually running — spatiotemporal continuity, gradual-vs-abrupt replacement, uninterrupted functioning, something else? Also give me the further case that would cleanly force a choice between the rules that fit her verdicts. Keep it under 400 words and lead with the prediction.
