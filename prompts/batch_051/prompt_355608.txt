My study group has been building toy models of rational disagreement, and one member produced the following argument. Its conclusion strikes us as obviously too strong, but we cannot agree on where the flaw is.

Consider two ideal Bayesian agents, Ann and Bob, with a common prior over a fixed set of possible worlds and finite information partitions. Each updates on private information by conditionalization.

(1) Ann and Bob truthfully and publicly announce their current credences in H. Ann announces 0.7, and Bob announces 0.3. Since the channel is reliable and each observes the other's announcement, the two announced numbers become common knowledge.

(2) Ann then conditions on the fact that Bob's pre-announcement credence in H was 0.3, and Bob conditions on the fact that Ann's pre-announcement credence was 0.7. Because the announced numbers and the rule of conditionalization are common knowledge, Ann's revised credence in H and Bob's revised credence in H are themselves common knowledge once the update is performed.

(3) Aumann's agreement theorem states that if two Bayesian agents share a common prior and their posterior probabilities for H are common knowledge at a state, then those posterior probabilities are equal.

(4) Therefore, by (2) and (3), after a single round of public announcements Ann and Bob must assign exactly the same probability to H.

(5) But one round of announcement does not always produce agreement; two agents can share a prior, exchange a single number, and still remain in disagreement. If (4) were correct, the iterated process of announcing revised credences would collapse after one step, which it does not.

So at least one of the framework's commitments—the common prior, conditionalization, or the way public announcements create common knowledge—must be given up or restricted. Which step is the first one that is false or fails to follow? If you think every step follows, which premise should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost? I would most like to keep the common-prior and conditionalization framework, but I need to know what qualification to the communication assumptions that would require.
