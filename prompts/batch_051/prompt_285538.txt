ok here's one ive been turning over. suppose a country decides to actually operationalize the secular-cycle stuff — Turchin's idea that premodern states went through recurring crises driven by population pressure, falling mass living standards, and elite overproduction (too many elite aspirants chasing too few elite positions). instead of keeping it as a research program, the government sets up a standing Secular-Cycle Monitoring Authority. every quarter it publishes a composite index, 0 to 100, built from indicators like the ratio of elite aspirants to elite positions, real wages in the bottom quartile, and state debt service as a share of revenue.

if the index stays above 80 for two consecutive quarters, two things trigger automatically: the top inheritance tax rate jumps from 25 to 60 percent until the index falls back below 70, and the number of slots in the civil service and licensed professions expands by 15 percent, with part of the new inheritance revenue funding a temporary cash transfer to the lowest quintile.

treat this as a mechanism, not a position. walk through who games it first and how — the government that wants the tax low, incumbent elites, the authority's own statisticians, ordinary people reading the score. what happens once the score becomes a public signal of instability: does crossing 80 become a self-fulfilling event? which triggers could backfire — for instance does expanding the civil service push the fiscal stress component up, or does the inheritance tax just accelerate capital flight before it bites? and can the worst failures be patched without turning the thing back into ordinary discretionary politics? max 400 words, and american english please.
