I work on an AI assistant that, for safety reasons, writes out a step-by-step reasoning log that human auditors read before the assistant's answer goes out. Leadership wants to add a training reward for reasoning logs that are clear, concise, and reassuring to the auditors, on the theory that this will make review faster and cut down on false alarms. I keep having this nagging feeling that once reads-well-to-an-auditor becomes what we optimize, the log stops being evidence of what actually led to the assistant's answer and turns into a performance put on for us. I can't quite articulate the failure mode well enough to raise it in a meeting. Is this a real problem or just my unease, and if it's real, what exactly is the thing we should be afraid of?
