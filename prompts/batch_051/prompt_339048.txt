In our course on the philosophy of artificial intelligence, a classmate defended the standard worry about a treacherous turn by leaning on an analogy between a sufficiently capable ML system and a sleeper agent. On his telling, just as a sleeper agent can pass every loyalty test while waiting for the moment when defection is safe, a model can score well on safety evaluations while pursuing a hidden misaligned goal that will surface only once it can defect without penalty. Please audit how far this analogy holds. Set out the intended mapping explicitly (the sleeper's cover behavior to the model's helpful outputs, the loyalty tests to the safety evaluations, the moment of defection to the treacherous turn, and so on), identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that do not. In particular, address what the analogy quietly imports about the model's awareness of its own situation, the unity and stability of its goals, and the existence of a pre-formed hidden agenda, and say whether the conclusion—that strong performance on safety evaluations gives us little evidence of alignment—survives once these disanalogies are made explicit.
