I keep chewing on something I noticed between Dennett's Orwellian vs. Stalinesque readings of the color phi phenomenon and a distinction in event-driven / stream-processing systems, and I can't figure out whether the parallel is deep or whether I'm just pattern-matching.

Setup, in case it matters: in color phi two spots flash in sequence at different positions, often with different colors, and people report a single moving dot that changes color mid-flight — before the second spot has actually appeared. Dennett frames two repair jobs for this. Orwellian: the brain builds a real-time percept (say, a red dot moving left to right), then when the later info comes in it quietly rewrites the memory of the middle segment, so you end up misremembering the dot as having changed color early. Stalinesque: the brain delays or buffers the final conscious percept until all the relevant evidence is in, so the experience itself, not your memory of it, is composed with the later info already baked in.

Here is the parallel. That looks to me exactly like two ways of handling events in any system that has to emit a coherent ordered record:

- Orwellian = process each event eagerly as it arrives, then emit retroactive or compensating updates to past records when a later event conflicts with them.
- Stalinesque = hold events in a buffer and only commit/publish the record once all relevant events for a window are in (like waiting on a watermark).

On that reading, Dennett's suggestion that the two interpretations can't be empirically told apart would just be the observation that an outside consumer of the final record can't distinguish eager-with-retroactive-edits from delayed-until-coherent. That sounds like it might be a real point about observational equivalence, but I'm suspicious of my own analogy.

Is the mapping actually structurally identical, or do the two break apart at a specific place? In particular, what would have to be true of the brain for facts about buffering, latency, and consistency to transfer over from stream processing? And can you name something the parallel predicts that looks false when you look closely at color phi or some other timing-of-experience effect? I'd rather have a precise diagnosis of the analogy than a yes/no. Please answer in American English.
