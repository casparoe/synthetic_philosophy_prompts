I've gone back and forth on quantified modal logic for years — the kind of thing that occasionally comes up on blogs and then everyone drops it again. I've ended up somewhere I didn't expect: I think the Barcan formula is true, and probably its converse too, which would mean the domain of quantification doesn't vary across possible worlds. In plain terms, BF says if everything is necessarily F, then necessarily everything is F; the dual version says if there could have been something that's F, then there's already something that could have been F. The standard counterexample — a possible world with a few extra objects not present in ours — feels to me like it's just smuggling in possibilia. It treats "there could have been a person who never actually exists" as if it were the same as "there is a merely possible person, and that person has properties." I don't think it is. I'm an actualist about what there is, and I think the right response to the "could have been more things" talk is to paraphrase it away rather than expand the domain. I've also drifted toward thinking everything that exists exists necessarily, though I'm much shakier on that then on BF itself.

What would count against this view? I don't want a summary of the debate — I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they should be for someone in my position. Where exactly do the intuitions about there being more or fewer things than there actually are bite? Which formal results should sit badly with a Barcanite: the way standard variable-domain semantics makes BF fail unless domains never grow along the accessibility relation, the interaction with free logic, the question of whether BF is derivable from weaker principles I might not want to give up, anything else in the neighborhood? What would a committed Barcanite have to concede as counting against them, as opposed to dismissing as an artifact of Kripke models? And am I at risk of having insulated the view so much that it can't be disconfirmed, and if so, is that actually a problem for a claim of this kind? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the single most decisive thing that should make me reconsider — then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
