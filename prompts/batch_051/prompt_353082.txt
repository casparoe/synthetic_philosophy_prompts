Donald Davidson did not live to see large language models, but radical interpretation was his account of what it takes to make another speaker's words and behavior intelligible. Had he encountered a modern LLM, would he have classified the process of making sense of its outputs as radical interpretation, or would his framework exclude it? Take a definite position and argue it through the commitments that decide the matter: the requirement that the object of interpretation be a rational agent with beliefs, desires, and intentions; the principle of charity; and triangulation. Be precise about which of these an LLM satisfies, which it violates, and whether Davidson would have had to revise anything rather than simply deny that LLMs are interpreters or interpretable subjects.
