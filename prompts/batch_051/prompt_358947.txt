I teach an upper-level undergrad seminar on language and self-location (14 students, one 80-minute block). Design a classroom game for them that bears on the question whether a language could lack pronouns and indexicals: it should make them feel why paraphrases like "the person in room 204" don't simply replace "I," even when all the facts are describable impersonally. No lecture up front and no naming the view until the debrief.

The game should force them to plan and act under a strict ban on indexical and deictic devices — no I, me, you, we, here, now, this, that, today, "in five minutes," and so on. Names, dates, room numbers, and map coordinates are allowed. I'd like the rules arranged so they hit two different walls themselves:

(1) a coordination wall: two or three teams have to meet up or move through the building, and it looks at first like a description ("the person at the pillar") fixes who does what, but then they discover nothing has been settled until someone can say, in effect, "I am the person at the pillar" — and the ban blocks exactly that;

(2) an action wall: a single player is given a complete map with a marked dot and a stack of true non-indexical descriptions, and they still can't decide whether to walk toward or away from the dot until they locate which mark on the map is them; the descriptions alone don't connect to a move.

Don't let it become a vocabulary icebreaker — the payoff should come from watching communication go fairly smoothly for a while and then fail in those two ways for a distinctive reason.

Give me: the rules, the materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, a script for the debrief that maps each wall to a precise point about indexicals, and an honest note on what will go wrong the first time I run it (my students will definately point, make eye contact, or use names — say how to handle that). Also tell me one feature of the underlying claim the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to say outright in the debrief.

Keep it to 1,500 words max.
