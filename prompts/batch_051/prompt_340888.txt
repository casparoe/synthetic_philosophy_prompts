At work I keep catching myself doing the same thing. When someone says "the team has decided to ship the feature this quarter" or "the company intends to move into that market," I don't treat it as a claim about a group that's true over and above the individuals in it. I privately rewrite it as: who, individually, has decided what? If I can't fill in that list — or worse, if I suspect no actual person on the team individually endorses the thing — I treat the sentence as false, or at best as a coordination shorthand, and I won't let it change my plans. My coworker pushed back: she said a group can be jointly committed to something even if no single member would have chosen it on there own, and that "we intend to X" can be a real fact about us, not just a summary of facts about each of us. I have no philosophy background, so maybe I'm missing something. Is my reduction habit defensible, or is it a bias with a story attached? If some group commitments are real even when no individual holds them, how should I decide which team decisions actually bind me, and can I tell from the inside whether I'm being appropriately skeptical or just refusing to take collective reasons seriously?
