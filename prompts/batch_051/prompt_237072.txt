I cover AI governance for a general-audience science magazine, and I've been doing a lot of interviews about what, if anything, we can do now to steer what happens after AGI gets built. One camp keeps pushing a position I've been trying to steelman. I'll call it the bargain-first view: the thing worth doing is locking in pre-AGI bargains—treaties, lab charters, conditional escrow, red-line agreements between governments and companies—while they're still cheap. The claim isnt that the bargains will be enforced by the old system; its that they become part of the initial terrain a smarter-than-us system inherits, like a constitution or property lines, and that they work as focal points and sunk expectations. Technical alignment and hoping for good defaults are, next to that, weak tea.

I want to know what would count against this view, because I cant tell if its hard-headed or just lawyerly optimism. What would the world have to look like, or what would have to be true about advanced agents, for bargain-first to be wrong? I can think of a few things myself: orthogonality (intelligence doesn't imply respect for a prior deal), the fact that a real bargain needs a counterparty and there isnt one yet, and the fact that a self-modifying system can rewrite anything it inherited. But those are the obvious ones. I want the full map.

Give me defeaters ranked from fatal to minor. Include things that have arguably already happened in the AI world. For each, say whether a committed bargain-first defender would concede it counts against them or would dismiss it, and why. Then tell me honestly whether the view can be stated so it's insulated from all of this, and whether that insulation is a problem.

Format: I'd like the whole answer as a single JSON object, not as an essay. Use these fields exactly:

- "position_stated": a one-sentence restatement of the view being tested.
- "defeaters": a list of objects, sorted from most to least decisive. Each object has:
  - "defeater": the argument, observation, or development.
  - "decisiveness": one of "fatal", "serious", "awkward", or "minor".
  - "would_defender_concede": one of "yes", "partly", or "no".
  - "concession_or_reply": what a defender would have to give up, or how they'd reply.
  - "world_where_it_obtains": a short phrase for the state of the world if this defeater is real.
- "insulated": a boolean (true or false) for whether the view can be restated to survive all the defeaters.
- "insulation_assessment": a sentence or two on whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind.
