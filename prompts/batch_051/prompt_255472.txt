I've been retired from atmospheric chemistry for six years and only lately started reading Hume carefully. I'm working through the essay "Of Suicide" by David Hume and got stuck on this passage:

"A house, which falls by its own weight, is not brought to ruin by his providence more than one destroyed by the hands of men; nor are the human faculties less his workmanship than the laws of motion and gravitation."

Hume seems to be saying that if God governs through general laws, then human actions are no more or less "God's doing" than physical events are. I want to use this as the fulcrum for something. For my reading group, mostly retired scientists with no philosophy background, I'd like a decision tree that sorts someone into a position on the ethics of suicide based on how they answer the question: what makes an event an act of God, and how does that bear on whether a human can permissibly end their own life?

Don't build it from labels like "Humean" or "Kantian." Build it from concrete questions, ideally using cases: a house falling, a person taking poison, a sailor jumping overboard, whatever you think. Each branch node should be a question a smart non-philosopher could answer without knowing which position it leads to. The leaves should include at least: a Humean providential naturalist who thinks suicide is not a sin; a strong occasionalist-style view that every event is directly willed; a natural-law view that suicide violates our self-preservation; and a social or duty-to-neighbor view that suicide wrongs survivors or community. You can add others, but keep the tree to about 6 to 8 leaves.

Then after the tree, tell me which combinations of answers are logically possible but empty, and why: is it because the position is incoherent, just unattractive, or probably just unexplored? Also flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate, especially around the distinction between duty to oneself and duty to God.

Please begin with the decision tree itself, with no preface or commentary, and don't restate the question.
