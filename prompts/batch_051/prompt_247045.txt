A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether the scale argument for insect welfare actually works: the claim that even a low probability that insects are sentient, multiplied across their enormous numbers, should make insect suffering a serious moral priority. I came away with a credence of around 0.7 that it does; she came away around 0.2, and neither of us could locate where the gap actually was. Give me your own credence in that proposition, decomposed into the terms you take it to depend on — at minimum, the probability that a representative insect such as a honeybee has valenced experience, the moral weight such experience would have relative to vertebrate suffering, how the expected-value arithmetic should treat very large numbers against a very low probability, and how likely actual interventions to reduce insect suffering are to be net positive. Assign a rough probability to each with a sentence of justification, show how they combine, and then tell me which term my friend and I most likely disagreed about and what evidence would most change your final number.
