I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about what a free rider is, after one of us called the other's relative one and the other objected. We could agree on clear extremes, but every definition we tried either let in things neither of us wanted or excluded the cases we both wanted in.

Give me a working definition of "free riding" (or "social parasitism", if that more loaded term is usefully different) that sorts the following cases correctly. I am confident the first three are instances of free riding and the next three are not; I have no settled view about the last three.

- A listener who relies on a donor-funded public radio station for hours each day but has never donated, though she could comfortably afford to.
- A parent who declines safe, readily available vaccinations for a child while the rest of the community's high uptake keeps the disease away.
- An employee who never joins the union or pays its membership fees but happily accepts the pay and conditions the union negotiated.
- Someone who enjoys the scent of a neighbour's jasmine growing over the shared fence.
- A child who is fed, clothed and housed by her parents without earning any of it.
- A person who accepts a coffee a friend has bought for them, offered with no strings and no expectation of being repaid.
- A professional who has used open-source software and Wikipedia heavily for years, contributing neither code, edits nor money, though able to.
- A citizen of a wealthy country who benefits all day from infrastructure, institutions and knowledge created by earlier generations, while doing little to maintain them beyond paying for what she herself consumes.
- A weekend tourist who uses a city's public transport, parks and public toilets maintained out of residents' taxes without paying those taxes herself.

Propose the definition, run it through all nine, and say where it delivers a surprising or unhelpful verdict. If you think the term is doing two jobs — a structural one and a moral one, say — give both senses and say which cases fall under each. I want a committed answer: pick a definite definition rather than merely laying out the available options, and defend any place where it forces an uncomfortable conclusion. Please write in British English.
