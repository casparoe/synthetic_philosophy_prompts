My friend has thought a lot about idolatry, and I am trying to systematize her verdicts. She says a miser who treats wealth as the standard by which the worth of everything else is measured is an idolater; so is a nationalist who regards his country as sacred and recognizes no standard above its interests; and so is a man who orders every choice around preserving his physique and treats aging as a kind of defilement. But she would not call a mother an idolater though the mother has made her daughter the center of her life and has let many other pursuits lapse, because the mother still sees her daughter's faults for what they are and does not treat her daughter's interests as overriding every other moral claim. Here is the new case. A father is equally clear-eyed about his son's faults, but he says his son is the one thing that makes his life worth living and that he would lie, steal, or break any promise for his son; he no longer recognizes any standard above his son's good as having a claim on him. Predict how my friend will judge this father, and do not reduce it to the single word. Identify the implicit rule she seems to be applying, state at least one alternative rule that fits her earlier verdicts just as well, explain which candidate rule the new case pulls apart, and then give your best guess about her verdict with your reasons. End by proposing one further case, different from mine, that would most efficiently settle which rule she really holds. Please write in ordinary continuous prose: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a letter or an email.
