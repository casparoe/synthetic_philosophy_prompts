I'm a software engineer on a trust and safety team, and I've been reading about costly signaling because my group keeps assuming that only expensive signals can be trusted — verification badges, account age, elaborate onboarding — on the grounds that a bad actor won't pay the cost. That led me to the handicap principle, the idea from animal communication that a signal can be honest only when it is costly enough that a weaker or deceptive individual could not afford to produce it. I have no formal philosophy background, so I tried to turn that principle into a clean argument, and the argument now seems to prove too much: it would make nearly all cheap communication impossible, which I cannot accept. Here it is.

(1) A signal is honest only if a sender whose hidden state would make the signal false does not profit from sending it.

(2) The only thing that can make sending a false signal unprofitable is that the signal imposes a cost on the sender — money, time, effort, risk, exposure to punishment — larger than whatever the liar expects to gain from being believed.

(3) An honest signal must therefore be costly for anyone who sends it; if it were cheap, a dishonest sender could send the same signal at the same price, collect the benefits of being believed, and leave honest and dishonest senders indistinguishable.

(4) So every honest signal is a costly signal.

(5) But cheap honest signals are everywhere: a stranger at the station telling me whether this is the right platform, a colleague telling me which service is down right now. These cost the sender nothing meaningful, and if the argument is sound they cannot be honest.

Act as an expert on signaling theory and the philosophy of communication. Please think carefully and start by canvassing the different places the argument could break before settling on a diagnosis. Then tell me: which step do you reject, why did that step look correct (it clearly captures something real about verification badges and peacock tails), and what do I have to give up once I reject it? I would like to keep both the cheap-communication cases and whatever is right about the handicap principle, but if that is not possible, say what the cheapest sacrifice is.
