As a journalist who covers the cognitive sciences and machine learning, I have been turning over an apparent collision between an old idea in linguistics and a newer one in artificial intelligence, and I would value a careful verdict on it. The old idea is the natural-kind view of grammatical categories: the claim that distinctions like noun and verb, or case and preposition, are not merely convenient descriptive artifacts but real joints in the structure of language — universal, psychologically real, and needed if syntactic theory is to state its generalizations at all. The standard argument for this view runs roughly as follows: such categories show up again and again across unrelated languages rather than varying freely with the descriptive choices of individual grammarians, and syntactic rules can be stated only by ranging over whole categories, so the categories themselves must be picking out something genuine rather than projecting our classifications. The newer development that seems to cut against it is the behavior of large language models. These systems are trained almost entirely to predict the next subword unit across huge corpora; they are supplied with no explicit part-of-speech tags, no case features, no lexical category labels, and no grammatical rules, yet they become capable of long-distance agreement, word-order regularities, and many other patterns that syntax textbooks describe in terms of exactly those categories. My question is whether the natural-kind argument for grammatical categories survives this development, and in what condition it emerges. Specifically: which premise of the argument does the language-model result bear on; whether that premise can be restored in another form (for example, by arguing that the model has merely rediscovered the categories from data and thereby confirms their reality, or that categories could be real for human learners even if a machine can do without them); and what, if anything, the original argument still establishes if the premise cannot be restored. Please also indicate the strongest reply available to whichever side you do not take.
