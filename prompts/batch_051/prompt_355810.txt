Recently a friend of mine has begun pressing me to justify convictions I would never ordinarily think to question: that the world did not come into existence five minutes ago, that other people have experiences as I do, that my memory is broadly trustworthy, and that there is an external world independent of me. He does not assert that these things are false; he simply demands evidence. When I offer a reason, he asks for the reason for that reason, and so on. Every chain of support I produce either continues without end, eventually circles back to one of the very convictions under examination, or terminates somewhere I can find nothing further to say. He concludes that my most basic commitments are held without rational support and calls them articles of faith. I feel that the defect is in his demand rather than in my position, but I cannot say why. Is he right that I must either give non-circular support for these convictions or abandon them? If I may properly retain them without being able to justify them, what sort of epistemic standing do they have, and how should I think about requests for evidence at this level? Please keep your answer to no more than 500 words and write in British English.
