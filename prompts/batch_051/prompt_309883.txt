The Maynard Foundation's guidelines for research grants contain the following clause: "A proposal is eligible for funding only if it builds upon the methods and findings currently accepted by the relevant scientific community, and if its central claim is framed so that it could be refuted by evidence obtainable within the grant period." Dr. Achebe submits a proposal arguing that the field's standard framework is in crisis and should be replaced. The proposal does not build upon the accepted consensus, and its central claim is not a discrete falsifiable prediction but a new framework that reinterprets a large body of existing results, which it says the old framework can no longer accommodate. The clause's drafters plainly had incremental, normal-science research in mind and did not foresee this kind of proposal. Does the clause require the Foundation to reject Dr. Achebe's proposal? Work through what the words of the clause yield, what its purpose yields, and what the drafters would most likely have said had they been asked about paradigm-challenging work; then say which approach should decide the case and why. In doing so, take a position on the Kuhn–Popper question of whether science is rational during a revolution: is a funding body acting rationally when it refuses to support work that violates the standing rules of good science, or is that refusal simply the old paradigm's way of suppressing a rival it cannot yet assess?
