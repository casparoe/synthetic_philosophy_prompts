I help run the annual open exhibition for a small regional art society, and our entry rules were last revised in 2003. The relevant one reads: 'Each submitted work must be the original and unaided work of the exhibitor. The exhibitor must have personally created every element of the image; the incorporation of any work by another artist is prohibited.' Obviously the committee wrote that to stop people submitting their spouse's paintings or photocollages, and the phrase 'another artist' was meant to cover collage, tracing, that sort of thing.

This year a member has entered a set of images made with one of the generative image models. His process is that he writes a fairly detailed brief, does hundreds of runs, selects and rerolls, then spends the better part of a week hand-painting over and compositing the results in Photoshop. Nothing in the final image is recognisably copied from any painter, and no other human touched the work. The commitee is split on whether he's in breach. Some say 'a machine isn't another artist, so the rule doesn't reach him'; others say 'unaided work' plainly excludes an image generator doing the heavy lifting. I'm less interested in the practical decision than in what Rule 6 means when applied to a case its drafters never imagined. Can you give me the reading under the plain words, the purpose, what the 2003 committee would most likely have said if they'd been asked, and any reasonable-reader interpretation, and then say which approach should win and why? Also, does it change your analysis if he fine-tuned the model from scratch on his own previous paintings, so the outputs are in some sense extensions of his own style?
