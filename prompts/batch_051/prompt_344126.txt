Over the last few months Ive been going back and forth with a friend about whether photographs are different in kind from handmade pictures, and I keep noticing the same move being made by people who think they are. They start with examples. A blurry photo from a wedding, they say, shows my sister in a way that an expert oil portrait of her does not: if someone asked whether I saw my sister at the reception, I would point to the photo, but I would never say the portrait let me see her. Same with a bystander's photo of a car crash versus a courtroom sketch made by someone who was there. In both cases the photograph seems to count as a kind of contact with the event or person, while the drawing, however accurate, is more like someone's report of it.

Then the person making the move explains the difference by saying that the photo was caused by the actual scene: light from my sister had to reach the camera, the way a footprint in snow is caused by the foot that left it. A painter could invent the same composition from memory, but a photo of that scene could not exist in that form unless the scene was really there. So far this is only a claim about causes. But then they usually add a stronger line: that when I look at the photograph, I see my sister or the crash through it, more like seeing through a window, mirror, or telescope, and not merely seeing a depiction. I cannot figure out wether that stronger line is meant literally, or is just an emphatic way of restating the causal point.

Is there an established name in the philosophy of photography for the view that photographs are like mirrors or telescopes in this respect — that they let you see the things that caused them, and that this gives photography a standing that painting and drawing don't have? I suspect I'm slightly mangling the real view, so I want the precise version, not just a label. What does the view have to commit to? Does the footprint point get you to the seeing claim by itself, or does it need a further premise about what perception is? Could someone accept the footprint point and still deny that photographs are anything like mirrors? And what would have to be true about seeing for the stronger claim to be more than a metaphor?
