i'm helping run a monthly discussion night at a community center, nothing academic, people just come from their jobs and we sit and try to think through one topic seriously. next month i want to do something on a thought that's been stuck in my head for a while. we talk constantly about being honest and sincere, but i think there's a real gap between not lying to someone and making yourself easy for them to read — being legible. the half-baked idea i keep coming back to is that honesty is something we owe in particular situations, but legibility (explaining yourself, having a stable story about who you are, making your inner state available for other people to check) has become this default virtue in how we talk about relationships, workplaces, friendship, and maybe it isn't. maybe sometimes it's fine or even right to stay a bit opaque, even with people you care about. partly this comes from a few relationships where someone got hurt not because i lied but because i didn't volunteer enough of what was going on inside me, and i still cant tell whether they were asking for something fair or whether i was just being guarded and calling it autonomy.

i want to run the session as a 40 minute teaching thing with a whiteboard, and i want the payoff to be that people leave seeing the difference between honesty and legibility and actually questioning whether we owe others legibility by default. can you plan the explanation rather than give it? i need: the order the ideas should go in so the distinction lands as real and not word games, the one everyday scenario to build the whole thing around (i'd rather not do social media or ai, everyone goes there), the misconceptions to expect — like people thinking i'm defending lying or secretiveness, or that it's just an introvert vs extrovert thing — and at what point in the order i should head each one off, a check that would show people actually got it instead of just nodding along, and what i should noticeably leave out at this level rather than half-explain.
