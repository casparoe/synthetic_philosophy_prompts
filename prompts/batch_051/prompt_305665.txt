I've just spent an evening arguing with a friend about the Allais paradox and risk-weighted expected utility, and I want a consistency audit before I try to write any of this up. By the end of the conversation I found myself committed to all of the following, and my friend's objections have left me unsure whether they hang together.

(a) Expected utility theory gives the right standard of rational choice under risk: rational preferences must be complete and transitive, and must satisfy the independence axiom; anyone whose preferences violate these axioms has made a mistake, whatever their feelings about risk.

(b) The standard Allais preferences are nonetheless rationally permissible. Faced with a choice between a certain $1 million and a gamble that pays $5 million with 10% probability, $1 million with 89% probability, and nothing with 1% probability, taking the sure million is rational; faced with a choice between an 11% chance of $1 million (otherwise nothing) and a 10% chance of $5 million (otherwise nothing), taking the $5 million gamble is also rational.

(c) Risk-weighted expected utility is the most plausible fix: an agent's risk function weights probabilities non-linearly, which lets the Allais preferences come out as rational rather than as mistakes.

(d) But no acceptable theory can ever license a preference for a stochastically dominated act; dominance is non-negotiable.

(e) The suggestion that the Allais pattern is just diminishing marginal utility of money cannot be right, since the pattern persists for any increasing utility function over the three fixed dollar amounts.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what would be the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say what each revision would cost.
