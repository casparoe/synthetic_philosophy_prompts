ok so i'm a little obsessed with this right now. i tried to sign up for one of those paid psych studies near campus — $60 to sit in a dim room and answer questions about my "conscious experience" — and the consent form was this strange document that kept saying my descriptions of my own experience would be treated as fictions rather than as evidence about anything "really going on in here." it finally clicked that it was Dennett-style heterophenomenology, and honestly the real thing already reads like a parody of itself.

can you write the parody version for me: an informed-consent / participant-information form for a heterophenomenology lab. around 650-700 words, deadpan throughout, formatted as numbered sections like a real IRB document someone forgot to run past legal. the target is the method itself — the practice of taking first-person reports as data to be explained rather than as authoritative accounts of an inner life, and of positing the subject's "heterophenomenological world" as a useful theorist's fiction. the jokes have to come from getting the method right, not from generic bureaucracy jokes. so sections could cover things like: purpose (to construct the subject's world as a set of items posited in order to explain reports), procedures (reports will be transcribed, time-stamped, and handed to someone who believes you less than you do), how reports will be used (discrepancies between your account and our model will be logged as data about your account, not as errors in our model), risks (you may start to wonder whether the thing it is like to be you needs to show up in our instruments, or begin to feel like the narrator of a fiction we are writing about you), benefits (a small payment plus the chance to help establish that a science of consciousness doesn't need to ask what it's like to be anyone), withdrawal (the organism that signs may withdraw at any time; the narrator of your reports is not a party to this agreement), and confidentiality (your reports will be kept indefinitely; the "you" they are about will not be, since the lab makes no commitments about its existence). make it land the real tension — that the method really does buy something scientifically by refusing to take your word for it, and that this is also mildly horrifying — without ever breaking character or winking at the reader. it should be funny to someone in philosophy of mind and still legible to someone who has never heard the term heterophenomenology.

oh and dont make the lab cartoonishly evil; the creepiest part is that they're rigorous and polite about it.
