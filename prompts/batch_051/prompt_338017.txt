Alan Turing, in the paper that introduces the imitation game, makes a point of saying the evidence for telepathy is strong enough that it can't just be dismissed, and suggests running the test in a room that blocks telepathy so a machine isn't disadvantaged if the interrogator is psychic. Some readers treat this as an embarassing concession to pseudoscience; others see it as a sound experimental control, or even as pointing toward a deeper feature of Turing's view of intelligence. Write an essay weighing these interpretations, and defend a definite conclusion about whether the telepathy passage undermines or strengthens the imitation game as a test for machine thinking. Don't stop at "both sides have a point."
