Ok so my friend and I spent like two hours going back and forth on physicalism last night and the whole thing ended up stuck on Hempel's dilemma. The usual move is: if 'physical' means whatever current physics says, physicalism is almost certainly false, since current physics is incomplete; if it means whatever a final, completed physics says, physicalism is empty, since it just covers whatever exists. My friend kept insisting that second horn is fine because its a fixed thing out there. But I can't shake this: from what I understand, a lot of working physicists now treat our best theories as effective field theories—each one works beautifully at some energy scale and breaks down at another, so what we actually have is a tower of regime-relative approximations, not a ladder toward one final theory. If that's right, there may be no completed physics to point at, and even 'current physics' is a patchwork rather than a single doctrine. Does Hempel's dilemma survive this? Which premise of the dilemma is actually under pressure once you take the effective-field-theory picture seriously, and is there a repaired version of physicalism that says something weaker but still substantive?
