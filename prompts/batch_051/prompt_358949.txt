Since nobody behind the veil would gamble on ending up rich, the rational thing is to insure yourself against the worst possible outcome. That insurance is the difference principle: institutions are just as long as the least advantaged are better off than they would be under a perfectly equal distribution. So if a policy makes the worst-off even slightly richer than equality would, it's just, and the size of the gap between top and bottom is irrelevant as far as the difference principle is concerned. Rawls also adds that this is a standard for the basic structure — the main political and economic institutions — not a rule for judging each person's behavior within it."
