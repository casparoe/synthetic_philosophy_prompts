The student wrote: "Benatar's asymmetry compares a scenario in which a person exists with one in which that person never exists. If the person exists, the pain they experience is bad and the pleasure they experience is good. If the person never exists, the pain is absent and that absence is good, while the pleasure is also absent and that absence is not bad, because there is no one who is deprived. That means the no-existence scenario has a real advantage, avoided pain, and no real disadvantage, because the missed pleasure does not count against it. So even a small amount of pain in a prospective life makes coming into existence worse than never existing: the pain is a disadvantage the no-existence scenario avoids, whereas the pleasure the life would have contained is not a compensating advantage, because its absence would not have been bad. It follows that bringing a child into existence always harms that child, and moreover that most people's lives are not worth continuing once one accepts the asymmetry. The strongest objection, I think, is that Benatar simply asserts the crucial move: he insists that absent pain is good but absent pleasure is not bad, yet a hedonist could just as well insist on the reverse, that missed pleasure is a bad while avoided pain is not a good, so the asymmetry is really just an unsupported stipulation."
