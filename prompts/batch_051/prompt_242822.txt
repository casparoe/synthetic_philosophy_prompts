In my Chinese philosophy class last week, the prof kept falling back on Wang Yangming's slogan about the unity of knowledge and action to settle arguments, most recently to shut down a classmate who said he knows vaping is bad for him but still hasn't quit. I can't pin down what the slogan is actually claiming. What are the distinct precise theses that line could be asserting — about what knowing amounts to, what counts as action, and how the two connect — and which of them are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are just false? More importantly, which readings would actually let him treat "I know but I don't do it" as not really knowing or as self-deception, and which wouldn't? A menu of readings with verdicts would be ideal so I can bring it up in discussion.
