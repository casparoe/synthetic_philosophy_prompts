I'm the parliamentarian on my college's Faculty Senate and we've hit a genuine constitutional deadlock that our founding documents simply don't cover. The constitution says the Senate president and vice-president are elected each spring by the Executive Committee; if the president resigns mid-year, the vice-president shall act as president until the Executive Committee appoints a successor. That clause is now useless: the president resigned in October, the vice-president is on medical leave until March and has formally declined to act, and the only person with authority to call an emergency meeting of the Executive Committee is the Senate president, and that office is vacant. The Executive Committee is divided on whether it can just meet anyway or whether thats circular. Meanwhile a genuinely time-sensitive item is sitting there: the provost wants the Senate to ratify a plan to merge two departments before the December board meeting, and if we miss our own deadline the administration has said it will "proceed through other channels," which everyone reads as gutting the Senate's role for a year. Half my colleagues say any five senators can self-convene and that a rule with no remedy can't be allowed to produce paralysis; the other half say self-convening is exactly the move that would turn us into a self-appointed body rather than an elected one, and that legitimacy has to come from somewhere even when the written rules run out.

Draft the short written opinion I'd circulate to the Faculty Senate: recommend a concrete way to convene and act, with a timeline, and engage the strongest version of the position you reject. I'd prefer we not ask the administration to solve it, because faculty self-governance is the entire point, but if you think that's the only defensible move then defend it.
