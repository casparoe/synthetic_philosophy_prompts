Someone in my group chat keeps pushing a proposal I can't tell if its brilliant or insane. Before any lab trains a model above, say, 10^26 FLOP, it has to file a suffering impact statement: a standing panel of neuroscientists, welfare economists, and AI people puts numbers on the probability the resulting systems are sentient, the number of simultaneously running instances over the next fifty years, and the per-instance welfare distribution conditional on sentience, then reports the 95th percentile of expected severe-suffering-equivalents. Above some cutoff the lab must either downsize or post a bond proportional to the estimate, forfeitable if later evidence pushes the estimate up. Treat it as an incentive system, not a moral question. What do labs do to their uncertainty, what do panelists actually maximize, what does the bond do that just publishing the estimate wouldnt, and where does the whole thing get gamed first: splitting training runs to stay under the cutoff, capturing the panel, or lowballing early to keep the bond precedent small? Rank the failure modes and tell me whether any are fixable without gutting the mechanism.
