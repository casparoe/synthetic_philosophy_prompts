Here's an asymmetry I can't stop noticing in how people judge minds. My neighbor gives me maybe five minutes of ordinary conversation a day, and I don't hesitate to think she has beliefs, pains, an inner life. A language model gives me an hour of fluent, even self-reflective conversation, and I immediately reach for the ELIZA effect and say its just next-token prediction. Meanwhile, the same people will say their dog feels guilty or their Roomba is scared of the stairs on far thinner behavioral evidence than they'd ever accept from a machine. So machines seem to face a much higher bar than humans do, except when they're cute or shaped like a pet, in which case the bar collapses. I want to know whether there is a single defensible principle that generates all of these verdicts. If there is, give me the strongest version of it and show me how it handles three hard cases: a parrot that says I love you, a human in a coma who says nothing, and a model that passes a careful Turing test. If there isn't, say so plainly and tell me which of my original judgments I should give up first.
