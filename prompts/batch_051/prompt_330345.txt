Trying to pin down my actual position on the stats wars before I teach a course, and I want someone to check whether the set hangs together. My current commitments:

1. A good share of the replication crisis comes from treating p < .05 as though it meant the effect was probably real, when it doesn't.
2. If psychology and medicine switched to Bayesian methods with explicit priors, most of the replication mess would go away.
3. Optional stopping is not a problem: the likelihood principle says only the data you actually observed matters, not when you would have stopped.
4. Preregistration should still be mandatory even in a fully Bayesian world.
5. It's fine to inspect your data before choosing your prior, as long as you disclose that you did.

I don't want a lecture on which school is right; I want the tensions in my own list ordered by severity, with the cheapest fix for each. Also flag any pair that only looks like it conflicts but actually doesn't.
