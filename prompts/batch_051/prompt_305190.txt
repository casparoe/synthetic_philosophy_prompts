I'm a novelist, so on paper Im professionally committed to the idea that every stranger on the bus has an inner life as thick and contradictory as mine. And on reflection I really do believe it—its one of the reasons I write. But I also have this stubborn, almost bodily intuition that I'm the main character and most people I pass are NPCs: two lines of dialogue, a loop they walk, then they despawn once I look away. It gets worse when I'm drafting, but it isn't only a writer's thing; it shows up at parties, in airports, on the subway. So I've got a theory that seems right and an intuition that won't loosen its grip, and they can't both be true as they stand. I want you to play negotiator, not referee. Tell me what it would take for the NPC intuition to be the one I debunk and drop, what it would take for the "everyone else is just as real as I am" conviction to be the one I amend or surrender, and whether there's a reading of "real" or "inner life" where I get to keep both. Then tell me where your own answer is shakiest—what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind.
