In RE we were looking at Aquinas’s *Summa Theologiae* (II-II, q. 83, a. 2, obj. 2), and this bit stuck out to me:

“Further, by prayer we bend the mind of the person to whom we pray, so that he may do what is asked of him. But God’s mind is unchangeable and inflexible, according to 1 Samuel 15:29, ‘But the Triumpher in Israel will not spare, and will not be moved to repentance.’ Therefore it is not fitting that we should pray to God.”

I know Aquinas replies to this by saying we don’t pray to change God but to change ourselves or obtain things in the right order or whatever, but I want you to argue that his move doesn’t actually work. Make the strongest possible argument that petitionary prayer to an omniscient and immutable God is still incoherent, or at least pointless, even if you reframe prayer as changing the person praying. In particular, if God has already willed the best outcome and prayer is meant to line us up with that, it seems daft to ask for specific things like healing someone or passing an exam. Actually engage with the passage rather than just listing textbook responses.
