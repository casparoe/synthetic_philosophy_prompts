I was taught a three-part test for whether an answer to a letter-string analogy is right: (1) the rule has to reproduce the source change exactly, (2) the rule should sit at the most natural level of description—single letters unless there's real pressure to group them, and (3) the rule should respect the structure of the target string. Take the analogy 'abc -> abd', where the rule looks like 'replace the last letter by its alphabetic successor.' Now apply it to 'aaabbbccc'. The by-the-book answer is 'aaabbbccd': change that final c to d. But a friend of mine gave 'aaabbbddd', because for her the concept 'last letter' slipped to 'last group', so the final ccc turns into ddd. My criteria pull both ways: (2) tells me to stick with single letters, but (3) tells me to respect the groups sitting right there in the target. Is my friend's answer wrong under the criteria as stated, or is this a case where the criteria need to be revised to include something like conceptual slippage? Go through the criteria one by one and then say which answer you'd treat as correct.
