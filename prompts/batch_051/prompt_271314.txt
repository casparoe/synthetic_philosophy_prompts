Last night I had a long and unresolved argument with a friend about the utility monster. He held that the example is a decisive objection to aggregative consequentialism: once one grants that a single being's welfare could rise far more steeply than anyone else's with each unit of a shared resource, the theory's own arithmetic requires feeding everything to the monster, and any refusal to do so is an ad hoc departure from the theory. I held the opposite: the example only makes explicit what impartial maximisation already says, and the revulsion it produces may be mere bias rather than an argument. We parted without moving each other.

I run a second-year undergraduate ethics seminar, eighteen students, with a ninety-minute session available. I would like a structured classroom activity or game, not a lecture, that makes them feel the force of Nozick's utility monster before it is named. The rules, incentives, or roles should be arranged so that the students themselves build or choose a distributive rule and only then notice that they have been led somewhere uncomfortable: under an impartial "maximise total utility" rule, a single player with a much steeper utility curve ends up receiving nearly everything, and the group must decide whether to accept or resist that result. Give concrete rules, materials (no real money is required), a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened in the game onto the structure of the thought experiment and the main replies to it, for instance denying that such a creature is possible, biting the bullet, rejecting the interpersonal comparisons, or abandoning the maximising rule. The debrief should leave them seeing both why the example feels like a reductio and what a committed utilitarian can say in response, since I do not want them simply to inherit my side or my friend's. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of the thought experiment the exercise cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Please write the activity plan and the debrief in British English.
