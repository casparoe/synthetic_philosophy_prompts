Longtime OB/LW reader (back before the diaspora) here, so I've seen more futarchy debates than I can count. I keep noticing a pattern in the prediction-market side of it that I don't have a precise name for, and its not the standard Goodhart objection (or at least I don't think it is). Take a conditional market on some outcome — say GDP under policy A vs policy B — and suppose the decision rule is to adopt whichever policy the market predicts will score higher. The market is supposed to be a thermometer, passively aggregating everyone's information about the two counterfactuals. But once the market's price is an input to the decision, the traders stop being disinterested forecasters and become interested parties: their trades are now partly bets on what the decision rule will do in response to the price, and partly attempts to move that price to trigger one policy or the other. The market ends up both measuring the system and sitting inside the causal loop of the system, and after the fact you cannot seperate the information in the price from the influence the price itself had. It feels like metric-gaming, but it isn't: no one is corrupting a proxy so it stops tracking the real target. The measuring instrument is just coupled to the thing it measures. Does this phenomenon have a name in the prediction-market or decision-market literature? If it's a special case of something broader, I want the actual carving: what exactly it is, what it is not, and where the boundaries are with Goodhart's law and the other usual suspects. If there's no established name, say so and give it one with a definition and boundary cases. A bare label won't satisfy me.
