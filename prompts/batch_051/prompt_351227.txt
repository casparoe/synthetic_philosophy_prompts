I'm a PhD student working on economic models of labor markets, and over the past year I've quietly become the person in our methods reading group who defends the classic Friedman line (the one people sometimes call the F-twist): that a model's assumptions—perfect competition, rational expectations, firms that know their cost curves exactly—don't need to be true, or even approximately true, so long as the model predicts well enough for the question you're asking. I keep batting away the obvious complaints (that this downplays mechanism and explanation, that unrealistic assumptions sometimes quietly do the predictive work), but I realize I've never actually sat down and asked what would have to be true for this whole view to be wrong. So treat Friedmam's instrumentalism about assumptions as the position. What genuinely counts against it? I want a map of the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward—not just the standard objections, but the cases, possible results, and developments in modeling practice that the view stands or falls with: things like the Lucas critique, two incompatible models fitting the same data equally well, assumptions that turn out to matter exactly when you try to use the model in a new regime, whatever else you think is load-bearing. For each, tell me whether a committed Friedmanian would concede that it counts against them at all, or whether they'd dismiss it as a missunderstanding of the view.

Also be honest about the possibility that the position has been insulated by qualifications—if "all models are false anyway" makes the view unfalsifiable, that might be the deepest problem, or it might just be the sensible thing to say; say which. I'd especially like to know what the view actually bets on: which practices in modeling it says should succeed (deliberately false idealizations outperforming more realistic models in forecasting, say) and which should fail. Personally I find the extrapolation-and-regime-change worries most persuasive, but I want to know whether that's a real defeater or just me having liberal intuitions about models.
