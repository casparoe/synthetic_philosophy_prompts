I'm a PhD student in international law and I help run a small documentation unit at a human rights group. We keep getting asked, often on a two-day turnaround, whether a situation meets Lemkin because people want to use the word genocide before the legal threshold is clear. I'm hung up on the passage in Lemkin's Axis Rule in Occupied Europe, chapter nine, where he describes genocide as having two phases: first, the destruction of the national pattern of the oppressed group, and second, the imposition of the national pattern of the oppressor, either on the remaining population or on the territory after the population has been removed. That feels broader than the 1948 Genocide Convention's list of acts, and it makes cultural and social destruction carry more weight.

I want a reusable procedure my team can actually run when deciding whether an unfolding situation qualifies under Lemkin's original two-phase criterion, not the narrower legal test. It should be a checklist or set of questions in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand off to a person because the question is one of values or specialist judgment. We have access to open-source reports and maybe two days. The stakes are high because the label changes funding, media strategy, and whether legal advocates pursue one vocabulary over another, and it can generate backlash against the affected group.

Then run it on three cases: the 2017 violence against Rohingya in Myanmar, a marginal case of forced assimilation of a small Amazonian indigenous group with very little killing but heavy cultural destruction, and a hypothetical where a government suppresses a regional language and rewrites textbooks after a separatist conflict but does not kill or displace anyone. Please make clear where the procedure gives an answer the organization will not like, and where it just cannot settle the question without a political or moral call.
