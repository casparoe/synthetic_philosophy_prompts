I keep going in circles on this. Hedonism, as I understand it, says pleasure is the only thing that's intrisically good for a person. But the paradox of hedonism says people who make pleasure their explicit goal end up with less of it than people who aim at other things, like close friendships or getting good at something. That sounds like a straightforward refutation of hedonism, or at least a proof that hedonism is self-defeating advice. My question is this: when a hedonist replies that you should pursue those other things and let pleasure come as a side effect, are they still a hedonist in any interesting sense? Or have they quietly given up the one claim that made the view distinctive — that pleasure is what you should ultimately aim at? And if the paradox only refutes a strawman version of hedonism, what exactly is the version that survives it, and what does that version tell you to do that a non-hedonist wouldn't also tell you to do?
