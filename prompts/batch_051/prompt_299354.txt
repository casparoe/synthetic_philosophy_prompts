Give a working definition of 'cognition' broad enough to be applied across species, and test it against the following cases: a person solving a simple arithmetic problem; a dog that runs to the door when its owner picks up its lead; a honeybee that performs a waggle dance encoding the direction and distance of nectar; a slime mould that reorganises its network to connect two food sources by the shortest available route; a Venus flytrap that closes after two trigger hairs are touched within a short interval; and a crow that bends a piece of wire into a hook and uses it to lift a basket of food. I am drawn to Morgan's canon, the principle that we should not explain an animal's action by a higher psychological faculty if a lower one will do, but the canon seems to presuppose exactly the distinction I am asking you to draw. Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, identify the verdicts you are least confident in, and say what your account implies about whether Morgan's canon does genuine work or merely relocates the problem.
