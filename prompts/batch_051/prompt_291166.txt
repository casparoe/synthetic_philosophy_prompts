My housemate is writing a paper on value theory, and he cornered me with an argument he claims shows hedonism can't even be lived. I keep feeling like he pulled a fast one somewhere, but the steps all sound fine one at a time. He sets it up like this:

(1) Hedonism says pleasure is the only thing good for its own sake; everything else is good only because it leads to pleasure or is a way of taking pleasure in something.
(2) If a theory says that only X is good for its own sake, then someone who accepts it should make X the direct target of her practical reasoning. If she won't aim at X, she's not really treating X as the thing that matters.
(3) But there's a familiar psychological point: making pleasure your direct target tends to sabotage it. Scrutinize an evening with friends to check how much pleasure it's producing, and the pleasure thins out; the only way to get the pleasure is to throw yourself into the activity itself.
(4) So hedonism seems to advise its own followers to aim at something other than pleasure: care about friendship, work, music, whatever, for their own sake, and let pleasure come as a byproduct.
(5) But that advice can't be followed while believing hedonism. If you really believe friendship is just an instrument to pleasure, you can't wholeheartedly care about it 'for its own sake,' and the knowledge that you're only caring in order to reap pleasure is exactly the kind of self-scrutiny that kills the pleasure.
(6) So hedonism is only followable by people who don't believe it: as soon as you accept it, it undercuts the attitudes it tells you to cultivate and collapses the moment you try to live by it. He says this shows hedonism isn't merely false but self-defeating as a guide to life.

I've gotten three diagnoses from other friends: one says the hedonist can 'just go indirect' and there's no problem, one says step (5) is doing the shifty work by demanding wholeheartedness, and one says (3) is just empirically overstated. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost?
