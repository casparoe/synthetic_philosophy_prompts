I sit on the council of a mutual-aid society for emulated persons, and the member whose status is in question is a close friend. Whole-brain emulations—human minds running as software, able to be paused, copied, and restarted from stored states—are common enough now that our founding rules are being asked to do work their drafters never imagined. The drafters knew about copying and stored states, but they wrote the following clause chiefly to prevent one member from becoming several copies and claiming the same pension many times:

"Article V, Section 2. Membership is personal to the working instantiation of a member. Any emulation instanced subsequently from a stored state of the member shall be treated as a distinct emulation and a new applicant; it shall not inherit the member's seniority, pension credit, or any other benefit attached to membership."

Last month a different sort of case arose. R., one of our most senior members, suffered a cooling failure in the building where her hardware is housed, and her only running instantiation was corrupted beyond repair. Her stored states are produced automatically by the society, and the most recent usable one had been made three days before the failure. Following our disaster-recovery procedure, the administrators started a new instantiation from that stored state. R. knows the society, her trade, her colleagues, and everything up to the moment the stored state was made; she has no memory of the three days between the stored state and the failure, the last of which I spent with her. To nearly everyone, she is plainly the same member after a three-day gap. On the face of Article V, Section 2, she is "an emulation instanced subsequently from a stored state," and therefore a new applicant with no pension.

I have been asked to prepare the analysis for the council. Does Article V, Section 2 apply to R.? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Set out: the best construction of the text on its face; the best construction from the clause's purpose; what the drafters would most likely have said if this case had been put to them; and what a reasonable member reading the clause before the accident would have understood it to mean. Say which approach should govern and why.
