I serve on my institute's deployment-review committee, where every proposed system is classified as either a tool or an agent; tools go through the ordinary review track, while agents require sandboxing, monitoring, and a standing human-in-the-loop requirement. Our own precedents from the past two years look like this. We called a grammar checker that suggests edits but never applies them a tool. We called a voice assistant that books calendar slots after a single prompt, with no further confirmation, an agent. We called a code model that emits suggested patches without touching the repository a tool. We called an automated lab system that chooses and runs experiments overnight an agent. We called a model that negotiates with suppliers by email and can sign contracts under five hundred dollars an agent. And we called a translation aid that offers candidate phrases while leaving every choice to the translator a tool.

We now have to classify a claims-review system that ingests incoming insurance claims, drafts decision letters, and sends the letter itself without human review whenever the decision is below two hundred dollars and its confidence in the decision is high; otherwise it queues the letter for a human adjuster. If we want to stay consistent with our own record, should this be a tool or an agent? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog? More importantly, what are the competing rules that fit all our precedents, what does each rule predict for this system, and what principle would we be committing to under each classification? If you think our past decisions genuinely underdetermine the call, tell me which further case would settle it.
