I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I've been reading about Karl Jaspers's post-war analysis of German guilt, and I want to use it to think through a problem from my own field. As I understand him, Jaspers distinguishes four senses of guilt: criminal guilt, which courts can judge; political guilt, which citizens bear collectively for the deeds of their state; moral guilt, which an individual answers for before conscience; and metaphysical guilt, which arises from solidarity with other human beings and from failing to do what one could to prevent a wrong. He also stresses that these categories must be kept distinct.

Here is the case I have in mind. I work on ranking and recommendation systems. Suppose a social media platform's engagement algorithm, built by many engineers over several years and tuned for retention, is later shown to have systematically amplified hateful content and contributed to offline violence. No single engineer intended that outcome, and most did not foresee it, although some had access to data that suggested harm. The company is not a state, but it exercises something like governance over a large public space.

If Jaspers were alive today and confronted this case, how would he most plausibly apply his four distinctions? Would he judge the engineers, the product managers, the executives, and the company itself under different categories of guilt? Would the ordinary engineer who worked on a small piece of the ranking pipeline bear political guilt by analogy to a citizen of a state, moral guilt, metaphysical guilt, or no guilt at all? And would the concept of a nation's guilt transfer to a corporation's guilt at all, or does something essential disappear when the collective is a company rather than a political community?

Please answer as a reasoned prediction of Jaspers's likely view, not as a summary of his published texts. Indicate how confident you are in each major part of your answer, and single out the point you are least sure about.
