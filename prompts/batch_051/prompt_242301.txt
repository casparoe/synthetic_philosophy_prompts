Exam question: In his essay On Bullshit, Harry Frankfurt distinguishes bullshit from lying. Explain the distinction, and then assess the claim that when a large language model produces a plausible-sounding but false statement—often called a hallucination—that output is best classified as bullshit rather than as lying or as error. Be explicit about what each classification requires and about any assumptions you are making about the model.

Student answer:

Frankfurt's central point is that the liar and the bullshitter differ in their relation to truth. The liar deliberately says what he believes to be false; lying requires an intention to deceive. The bullshitter need not intend to deceive. What matters to the bullshitter is the impression he makes, the effect on his audience, or simply getting through the occasion; whether the statement is true or false is beside the point. Because the bullshitter is unconcerned with truth, Frankfurt says bullshit is a greater enemy of truth than lying: the liar at least takes truth seriously enough to work against it, while the bullshitter ignores it altogether.

When a large language model produces a plausible-sounding false claim, it is tempting to call this lying, but that is clearly wrong. The model does not believe the claim is false, since it has no beliefs in the relevant sense, and it has no intention to deceive. So hallucination is not lying.

The harder question is whether hallucination is bullshit or error. Error occurs when a speaker aims at the truth and misses. The model does not aim at the truth: it is trained to produce statistically probable text, not to track facts. Its outputs are correct only accidentally, when the training data happen to make truth probable. That is exactly Frankfurt's bullshitter: the model is indifferent to truth, and it constructs sentences to suit the context rather than to describe reality. Therefore LLM hallucination is best classified as bullshit. A further consequence is that, just as Frankfurt says about human bullshit, LLM bullshit is more corrosive to discourse than lying would be, because it floods the environment with claims that nobody has even tried to get right.
