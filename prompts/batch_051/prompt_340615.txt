You're an expert on how measurement and targets work in institutions, and you've got a philosopher's taste for drawing distinctions. Start by brainstorming and think aloud before settling on your final answer, because I want the boundaries right, not just a label.

I keep noticing a pattern and I'm not sure wether it has one name or several. It goes like this. A hospital starts measuring how quickly patients get discharged from A&E. At first the number tracks something real: quick discharge usually means the department is working well. A couple of years later, patients are parked in corridors, discharged too early and readmitted a week later, and ambulances are queuing outside. The number has gone up, but the hospital has got worse, and nobody had to fake the statistics: people just organised the work around the number. The same sort of thing happens in a school. It gets judged on exam passes, so it enters pupils for the easier papers, drills everyone for the exam, and narrows the curriculum; the pass rate rises while education gets thinner. And a version of it happens in software too: a feed algorithm is told to maximise how long people watch, and the stream fills with outrage bait, which is not what the designers meant.

I'm not asking for the old line about targets creating perverse incentives. What I want is the precise concept for what happens when a measurement stops describing the thing and starts acting on the thing. In particular:

(a) Is it all the same thing when people game a measure dishonestly (reclassifying patients so they don't count), when they sincerely work to satisfy the measure (reorganising care around discharge time), and when a machine just maximises whatever number its designers told it to care about?
(b) Does it matter for naming this whether the measure was a bad proxy to begin with, or a good proxy that got corrupted by being used?
(c) Is there a name for the specific moment, or the specific mechanism, where a metric stops measuring the world and starts changing it?

If there is an established concept, tell me what it is, but then also tell me what it doesn't cover and where its edges are. If the three cases are really different things, say so and give me a way to keep them apart. If no single name does the job, what would you call the thing I'm seeing, and how would you define it?
