Write an essay on the following controversy, and reach and justify a definite conclusion rather than ending in a draw.

Jain philosophers maintain that reality is many-sided (anekāntavāda). On their view, any judgment is true only from a partial standpoint, so a careful assertion should be qualified by the Sanskrit particle syāt, meaning something like in some respect. This yields the seven modes of predication (syādvāda): for example, one may say that in some respect the self is permanent, in another respect it is impermanent, in still another respect it is indescribable, and the remaining modes combine these options. The idea is not that the self is contradictory, but that different aspects or standpoints warrant different predicates.

Critics charge that the doctrine undermines itself. If every assertion is true only in some respect, then the assertion that every assertion is true only in some respect is itself true only in some respect, which seems to leave room, in some respect, for an assertion that is not merely standpoint-relative after all. If instead the doctrine is put forward as unconditionally true, it violates its own standard. Defenders reply that anekāntavāda is not another first-order description alongside ordinary claims, but a meta-level method or an epistemic norm of humility, and is therefore exempt from its own requirement. The critic answers that this exemption is ad hoc unless the method can be justified from within some standpoint.

Your task is to weigh this dispute and decide whether a Jain-style many-sided truth can escape both failures at once: self-undermining relativism and a disguised one-sided absolutism. Along the way, you may need to distinguish (a) different ways a statement can fail to be wholly true, such as falsity, partial truth, perspective-dependence, and inexpressibility; (b) whether the seven modes are best understood as a taxonomy of true qualified statements, a seven-valued logic, or something else; and (c) whether anekāntavāda is a metaphysical thesis about reality or a regulative policy for inquiry. If you find the simple version indefensible but a refined version defensible, present that refined version and defend it against the original objection.

Length: at most 1000 words.
