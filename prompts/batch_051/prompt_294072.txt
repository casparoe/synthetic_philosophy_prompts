Okay so the mental imagery debate in cognitive science — Kosslyn's camp says the fact that people take longer to scan across a remembered map the further apart the two points are, and take longer to mentally rotate a shape the further it has to be turned, shows the underlying representations are spatial and depictive; Pylyshyn's camp says the 'picture' is epiphenomenal and the real format is a language-like description, with the spatial-looking data only reflecting the subject's tacit knowledge about how a real scan or rotation would go — has been running since the seventies and, so far as I can tell, nobody ever moves. Each side just re-runs the same two moves: look at these reaction-time gradients versus subjects are simulating the task demands. I have a hunch the whole thing is two different questions under one heading: Kosslyn is really doing psychology of first-person spatial phenomenology, and Pylyshyn is really doing computational explanation, where representational format only earns its keep if it shows up in a mechanism.
