An acquaintance has offered the following argument, and I cannot locate the fault, though I refuse the conclusion. (1) I am shown two sealed envelopes and told that one contains exactly twice as much money as the other; no further information is given. (2) I choose one envelope but do not open it, and let n be the amount it contains. (3) The other envelope therefore contains either 2n or n/2. (4) Since the two envelopes are symmetric before I choose, these two possibilities are equally likely. (5) So the expected value of the other envelope is (1/2)(2n) + (1/2)(n/2) = 5n/4, which is greater than n. (6) Hence, whatever n is, I should prefer to switch. (7) But the same argument can be run with the envelopes swapped, so I should prefer to switch back, and in fact I should be willing to pay to keep switching. Where exactly does this argument go wrong? I am inclined to keep the symmetry stated in (4) and to reject the expectation calculation in (5), but I want to know precisely what (5) assumes and what a correct treatment looks like.
