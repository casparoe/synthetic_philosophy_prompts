I am a journalist working on a piece about the doomsday argument, and I keep returning to an asymmetry I cannot settle. A raffle has tickets numbered consecutively from 1, but the total printed is unknown. A single ticket is drawn blind and reads 81. Nearly everyone agrees that this observation pushes hard against the raffle having ten billion tickets and, unless the prior is stacked the other way, favors a total in the hundreds or thousands, since a low number would be a strange draw from an enormous run. Now run the same inference on your own birth rank among all humans who will ever exist. On standard estimates you are roughly the hundred-billionth human; if you treat yourself as one blind draw from the full set of human lives, the same reasoning pushes toward a human race that ends at moderate size rather than one that persists long enough to produce trillions. The people who found the raffle inference banal tend to call this version a trick. I want the strongest principle that licenses the raffle inference while blocking the doomsday inference, and an honest verdict on whether that principle survives the obvious nearby cases.
