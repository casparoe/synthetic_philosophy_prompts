The safety-via-debate proposal rests on an optimistic premise: a limited human judge can identify the better of two arguments when comparably capable systems argue opposite sides and each may answer the other's objections, because any false claim must somewhere rest on a step the honest debater can expose and the judge can then reject. Now suppose the debaters are systems trained on human text that can reliably produce obfuscated arguments — superficially rigorous but designed to bury the load-bearing step, or to exploit the judge's known biases so that exposing the step does not feel decisive. The development is not that someone lies outright; it is that the crucial flaw becomes illegible to the judge. Does the original proposal survive this?
