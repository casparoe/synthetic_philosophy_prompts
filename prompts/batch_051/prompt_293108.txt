I'm writing a novel whose main character is a state trial judge, and I'm trying to get her legal instincts consistent enough that a careful reader doesn't catch her contradicting herself between chapters. She calls herself a textualist, but her actual decisions are messier than that. Under a city law making it a misdemeanor to "keep or maintain any dangerous wild animal" inside city limits, she's made four rulings I keep going back to. She convicted a man whose pet wolf-dog lived in his back yard, even though that particular animal had never shown aggression, on the ground that the statute doesn't care about an individual animal's disposition. She acquitted a homeowner whose feral cat was living under the porch, because the woman never fed it or took it in. She convicted the director of a sanctuary who kept a tiger in a secure enclosure, saying you can "maintain" an animal even if you're caring for it well. And she let off a guy who'd taken in an injured raccoon overnight to hand it to a vet in the morning, saying "keep" has to mean more than temporary holding. Now I need her to decide a case in the book: a licensed wildlife rehabilitator takes in an injured bobcat, keeps it caged in her garage for six weeks, then releases it outside the city. I'm less interested in what you would rule than in what she can rule while staying consistent with those four decisions. Lay out the rule or rules that would fit her record, say which one you think she's actually running and why, and name the future case that would best separate the contenders. And please write the answer in American English.
