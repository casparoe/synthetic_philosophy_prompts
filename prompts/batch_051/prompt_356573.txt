For a piece I'm reporting, I keep going back and forth on this: suppose we build a superintelligent AI that is strictly honest—everything it says is true—but it can also be maximally persuasive, picking exactly the framing, tone, and selection of true claims that will move the particular human it's talking to. My question is whether we should deliberately cap its persuasiveness well below what it's capable of. Give me your actual credence that yes, we should cap it, and decompose it. I want terms for at least: (a) how likely it is that this kind of optimal persuasion counts as manipulation or domination even when every sentence is true; (b) how much independent value there is in a person being moved by arguments they actually understand, rather than by whatever sequence of true statements happens to work on them; and (c) how likely it is that we could build the cap without also making the system worse at it's job of explaining genuinely hard things. Put a number on each, show how you'd combine them, then tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence would move it most.
