In my philosophy club we recently read Zhuangzi’s “The Great and Most Honoured Master” in James Legge’s translation, and one passage has stayed with me: “The True men of old knew nothing of the love of life or of the hatred of death. Entrance into life occasioned them no joy; the exit from it awakened no resistance. Composedly they went and came.” I have been trying to turn that passage into a set of commitments I actually hold, and I would like someone to check whether the set hangs together. Here is what I have written down for myself: (1) My own death should be neither feared nor welcomed, but accepted as Zhuangzi describes; fearing my own nonexistence is a mistake. (2) When someone I love dies, grief is not just permissible but required, and I would think there was something wrong with me if I did not grieve. (3) There is a real asymmetry between my own death and another person’s death: my own death cannot be an event in my life, but another person’s death can be an event in mine, and this asymmetry reconciles (1) and (2). (4) Even so, I think I should not treat death as indifferent when it can be postponed at small cost; self-preservation matters to me. (5) I believe death is final, with no personal afterlife, and I find that consoling rather than frightening. (6) I also think people who say “my death is nothing to me” are missing something about the first-person perspective, because a death is the end of the very subject for whom things can matter. (7) Regularly reflecting on death improves how I live, but I suspect that dwelling on it can become a way of avoiding commitments that make life worth living. (8) I do not believe in a cosmic Dao that human life must align with; I read Zhuangzi as offering psychological wisdom about attachment, not metaphysics.

I am not sure where the real tensions are among these commitments, and I do not yet know which one I would give up. Could you audit the set rather than lecture me? I would like the pairs that are actually in tension, ordered by how severe the tension is, with a note on what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be in each case. I would also like you to identify at least one apparent tension in the list that dissolves on closer inspection, and explain why it only seems like a conflict.
