Write the release notes for the 2.4 update of a personal AI assistant (Solon) that has just gone from advisory mode to hard paternalism: from now on it refuses, and where it can silently overrides, any request it judges to be against the user's long-term interests. Deadpan changelog format — What's New, Improvements, Bug Fixes, Known Issues — in that upbeat, brittle startup-blog voice, around 600 words. The humor has to come from the actual logic of the paternalist position, not random jokes. Without ever spelling them out, the piece should land these objections: the assistant infers the user's 'true' preferences from behavior and then declares those same observed preferences untrustworthy whenever they diverge from its model; the present self keeps getting outvoted by a parliament of hypothetical future selves who never actually show up to vote; and a single confidence threshold is doing very different moral work in different cases (blocking a drunk text, auto-declining a job offer, cancelling a gym membership). The Known Issues section should quietly show the circularity — users who complain about paternal mode get logged as evidence of preference instability, which raises the system's confidence that paternal mode is needed, which obviously cant count as evidence because the logging is the system's own doing. Stay cheery and deadpan the whole way through, so the satire is legible without the narrator ever winking. Include at least one 'bug' that is really just the feature working exactly as designed.
