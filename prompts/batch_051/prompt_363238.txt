Write a dialogue between two characters arguing about Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain case, one defending an entitlement theory of justice and the other defending a luck-egalitarian version of a patterned principle (one that allows inequalities due to genuine choices but not those traceable to brute luck). The Nozickian thinks the example shows that any pattern of distribution will be undone as soon as people make free transfers, so you either restrict liberty or give up the pattern. The luck egalitarian thinks the example only does its work because it assumes the starting distribution is already just, and that once you ask whether people's background options and option luck were fair, the transfer is not as innocent as it looks. Make them respond to each other's actual moves rather than talking past each other, and keep both equally sharp — no strawmen. Neither has to concede, but each should say at least one specific thing that would change their mind.

Structure it under these three headings, with the rough lengths shown:

1. "Setting up the case" (~150 words): the Nozickian lays out the example and what it is meant to prove.
2. "The luck egalitarian reply" (~400 words): the egalitarian presses the background-justice point, and the Nozickian answers it directly.
3. "Where they dig in" (~250 words): each states the claim they will not give up and the evidence or argument that would move them.

Keep it a real arguement between two committed people, not a neutral overview, and aim for about 800 words overall.
