A colleague in my department works on foundations of physics and has been pressing the Rietdijk–Putnam argument on me, and I cannot decide whether it is a genuine result or a piece of philosophical sleight of hand. I have reconstructed the argument as follows. (1) For any two spacelike-separated events, there is an inertial frame in which they are simultaneous. (2) All inertial frames are equally valid descriptions of the world, so if an event is simultaneous with me-now in any frame, that event is real now; simultaneity in a legitimate frame is not merely a matter of how we slice spacetime for convenience. (3) Let B be my birth, D my death, and A an event on the worldline of a distant galaxy that is spacelike separated from both B and D; there is one inertial frame in which A is simultaneous with B, and another in which A is simultaneous with D. (4) Co-presence is transitive: if x is real together with y, and y is real together with z, then x is real together with z. From this it follows that D is real at B, which is to say that at the moment I was born, my death already existed. (5) Therefore special relativity forces the block universe: all events are equally real, and the passage of time is an illusion.

This conclusion seems to me to prove too much. I am prepared to accept that an empirical theory about rods, clocks and light signals has implications for the nature of time, but I find it hard to believe that a coordinate transformation by itself could establish that the future is already fully there. Still, every step looks innocent as I read it, and I know serious people have treated the argument as sound. My instinct is to keep the empirical core of special relativity and to suspect one of the more philosophical steps, but I want to know exactly which premise, if any, should be rejected, why it looked acceptable, and what the standard alternatives pay to reject or accept it. Please also tell me where your own diagnosis is most likely to fail and which further consideration, if any, would change your mind about it.
