Write an essay on whether deflationary theories of truth can account for the normativity of truth, and defend a definite conclusion. The essay should first state the deflationist's core claim: to call a sentence or proposition true is not to ascribe a substantive property to it, and 'it is true that snow is white' says nothing over and above 'snow is white.' It should then develop the strongest version of the objection that truth is normative — that truth seems to set a standard of correctness for assertion and to be the standard at which belief aims — and that this role could not be discharged by a mere device of disquotation or by a tool for generalizing over many sentences. Weigh the most promising deflationist replies, such as locating the relevant norms in the contents asserted or believed, or in practices of inquiry and evidence rather than in truth itself, and say what each reply would concede. Conclude by arguing for a specific outcome — that deflationism survives the objection, that the objection refutes it, or that deflationism must be revised in a particular way — rather than ending on a neutral summary. Do not use headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write in continuous prose, as you would an email or a letter.
