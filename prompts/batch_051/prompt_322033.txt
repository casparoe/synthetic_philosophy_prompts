I've ended up convinced that the distinction between "possible but probability zero" and "impossible" doesn't do any real work. My view: an event is genuinely possible just in case it has positive probability (objective, not merely epistemic), and probability-zero events are impossible, full stop. I know the obvious pushback is the dartboard—each exact point has probability zero under a continuous distribution, yet the dart lands somewhere—but I've come to think that's a reason to be suspicious of assigning probabilities to point-events, not a counterexample to the view. What would count against the view?
