I was talking with a colleague who works on AI alignment, and he walked me into an argument I can't find my way out of. It starts as a claim about why an AI would want anything at all, and ends up implying that human wanting is just as derivative as the AI's. Here is the argument as he laid it out.

(1) For a system to genuinely want G, G must be an end for that system itself, not merely a target imposed on it by an outside optimizer. (2) If a system's disposition to pursue G was produced by an outside optimizer selecting for pursuit of G, then G is an imposed target, not an end for the system. (3) An AI's dispositions to pursue its apparent goals are produced by an outside optimizer: designers choose the objective function, the training signal, and the environment that together select for those dispositions. (4) So an AI does not genuinely want anything; the wanting, if anywhere, belongs to the designers. (5) A human's dispositions to pursue food, status, and approval are equally produced by an outside optimizer: natural selection shaping a reward system the person never chose, plus cultural and developmental contingencies she never authorized. (6) So by parity, a human does not genuinely want anything either. He concluded that "wanting" is a projection we make onto systems that behave goal-directedly, whether neural network or human — and that if I try to avoid this by dropping (2), I'm forced to say a thermostat genuinely wants the room at 21 degrees whenever it was designed or selected to keep it there. Both results seem absurd to me, but I can't name the step where the argument goes wrong.

You are a professional philosopher working on the metaphysics of mind, agency, and AI. Please think carefully, and before giving a diagnosis, brainstorm the distinct senses of "want," "end," and "outside optimizer" the argument might be trading on. Then identify the exact premise or inference I should reject, explain why it looked unavoidable, and say what rejecting it would cost. In particular, I'd most like to keep the claim that humans (and probably dogs) genuinely want things; I'm less attached to any particular story about the AI.
