I'm a PhD student in philosophy of art, and I'm putting together a session on Schopenhauer's account of music. I want to focus on the paragraph in The World as Will and Idea, vol. 1, §52 (Haldane–Kemp translation) where he argues that music is not, like the other arts, a copy of the Platonic Ideas, but is a copy of the will itself—a direct expression of the inner striving that he thinks underlies everything. I don't have the precise wording in front of me, so please don't treat my paraphrase as the text; the point is his claim that music gives us the universal core of things without needing to represent objects or events.

Could you design a 90-minute classroom activity for a upper-level seminar on aesthetics, maybe 12 students, that makes the participants feel this asymmetry between music and the representational arts before the theory is named? I want them to come away seeing that there's a real phenomenological difference in how music gives us something—that when we try to say what music is about, we're forced into the language of emotions, drives, tensions, and resolutions rather than objects and scenes, and that this is precisely the gap Schopenhauer is exploting.

Please make it a runnable activity with rules and roles, not just a lecture. Include a timeline, what the facilitator does and says (and what they don't say), and a debrief that maps moments in the activity onto Schopenhauer's distinction between copying the Ideas and copying the will. Also say what will predictably go wrong—e.g., a student digs in and says music is about something, or the group divides over what an excerpt expresses—and how to handle it. And be honest about which part of the view the activity can't get them to feel, the metaphysical weight of music being more direct than the other arts, probably.
