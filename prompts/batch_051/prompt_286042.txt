Suppose a law of nature made explosive nuclear chain reactions impossible—no fission or fusion bomb could ever be built—though nobody discovered this until the 1940s, and history otherwise followed its actual course to 1945. Predict, with visible reasoning, how the period 1945–2000 would have differed from the actual record. I want a chain of mechanisms rather than a narrative: does the absence of nuclear deterrence make a third world war or direct U.S.–Soviet combat likely; which factors—conventional force balances, the memory of two world wars, economic interdependence, alliance structures—do the explanatory work; and how are decolonization wars, civil conflicts, and large-scale killing of civilians affected? For each major prediction, state whether it is robust or a guess and what fact about the actual post-1945 record it turns on. Finally, say whether your verdict about which world was less violent survives switching from absolute death tolls to per capita rates, and whether you think there is a determinate fact of the matter here at all.
