My friend Dana has this whole theory about when a moral stance is a "preference" and when it's a "bias," and she gets annoyed when people call everything a bias. Over the years Ive been keeping a list of the calls she's made about people we know:

- Theo keeps kosher and won't eat at restaurants he can't verify, but he's genuinely fine with other people eating whatever they want. Dana says preference.
- Rina turned down a job at a missile-systems company because she thinks designing weapons is wrong. She has close friends who work there and doesn't think they're bad people. Dana says preference.
- Sonia won't let her kids do sleepovers at houses outside her faith community. She likes the other parents, and she doesn't think they're doing anything wrong; she just wants her kids growing up inside a household rhythm that matches her own. Dana says preference.
- Art owns a small rental building and wont rent to musicians. His stated reason is that musicians are noisy and flaky, though he's never actually had a musician tenant. Dana says bias.
- Beth manages a care home and won't hire men as nursing aides. She insists men aren't inferior to women, just less naturally nurturing. Dana says bias.
- Greg won't start a business with anyone from a particular town, because he got burned once and now says people from there look out for their own and screw outsiders. Dana says bias.
- Maya refuses to put money into a friend's multilevel-marketing business because she thinks the business model is exploitative, but she doesn't think her friend is a bad person. Dana says preference.

Now here's the case she hasn't seen yet. Luis is a pharmacist who won't fill prescriptions for the abortion pill, based on his religious convictions about what the drug does. He's polite to the patients, doesn't think they're bad people or morally worse than anyone else, and he always tells them the pharmacy two blocks over can fill it. I want to ask Dana whether Luis's stance is a preference or a bias.

What do you think she'd say? I mostly care about the underlying rule, not the label. What other rules would make all her past calls come out the same way, and which of those rules survive the pharmacist case? If the pharmacist case doesn't settle it, tell me the case you'd invent to settle it. And write the answer in American English.
