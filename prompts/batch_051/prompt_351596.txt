I'm in a small discussion group on character and self-improvement — none of us has any formal training in philosophy — and I've been asked to present a short account of how I think about humility. I've written out the commitments I actually try to live by, and before I present them to a group that likes to poke holes, I want them checked for consistency. Here they are, in my own words:

(a) Modesty is a real virtue, and its core is low self-focus: a modest person isn't preoccupied with how they're coming across, and they don't turn their own good qualities into a topic of conversation.

(b) Intellectual humility means keeping a meaningful slice of confidence permanently on the table for being wrong — I aim for at least a fifth on any controversial belief — and when people I respect disagree with me, I make myself move at least a little in their direction.

(c) Moral humility means that strong condemnation of other people's choices is almost never something I'm willing to offer, because I can't know what I would have done in their exact circumstances until I've been in them.

(d) Self-deprecation is not modesty. Downplaying real achievements, fishing for reassurance, or prefixing every claim with a hedge is just self-focus with the sign flipped; real modesty is saying plainly what you are and can do, and then dropping the subject.

(e) There is also a duty not to shrink: when I have relevant skill or standing in a room, I should say what I know and exercise whatever authority comes with it instead of demurring.

(f) And when someone in a position of trust repeatedly mistreats people, those who see it shouldn't soften it; naming it clearly is owed to the people who were harmed.

I can tell some of these rub against one another, but I can't sort out which clashes are real, which dissolve once the terms are clarified, and which repairs cost least. Please give me an audit in exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, with no introduction or conclusion: rank the genuine tensions by severity, each with what would have to give, and note where the apparent conflicts dissolve.
