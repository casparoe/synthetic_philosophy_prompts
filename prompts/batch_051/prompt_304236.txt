I'm a retired physical chemist who has spent the last couple of years reading philosophy of mathematics instead of babysitting instruments, and one thing keeps tripping me up: the Church-Turing thesis. People seem to talk about it in three registers at once — as a discovered fact about the world, as a stipulative definition, and as something you cannot prove — but I never see anyone spell out what an adequate theory of effective computation is supposed to do in the first place, so I can't tell which register is the right one. its been driving me a little nuts.

Write me the desiderata. What must any adequate account of effective computability deliver? I mean the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it has to respect (computable vs. merely definable, halting vs. not, finite procedure vs. infinitary process), the closure properties it ought to have, and the boundary it has to draw — the things that make the account a theory of the effectively computable rather than a grab-bag of formalisms. For each desideratum give the motivating case or result behind it, and say whether you take it to be non-negotiable or negotiable (possibly a prejudice of one camp). Also say which single desideratum does the most to filter out bad accounts, and flag any pairs you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied as stated.

Then, briefly, score the standard trio — Turing machines, the lambda calculus, and general recursive functions — against your list as a package, and say what an account that helps itself to oracles or infinitary physical processes would score, since I suspect the boundary desiderata should bite there. One or two sentences per item is plenty; don't do the history.

Finally, use the list to say what epistemic status the Church-Turing thesis actually has: an empirical conjecture, a definition, an explication or proposal, or something mixed. I want a clear statement of what kind of claim it is, and whether the desiderata you set out can genuinely test the thesis itself or only the formal accounts meant to match it.

Because old lab habits die hard, give me the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields:
