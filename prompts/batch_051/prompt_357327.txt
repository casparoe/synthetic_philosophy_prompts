I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad philosophy of biology course on Popper, and I can't decide whether he actually contradicted himself or not. Here's the problem. In 1976 he wrote, in his intellectual autobiography:

"I have come to the conclusion that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme—a possible framework for testable scientific theories."

But two years later he wrote:

"I have changed my mind about the testability and logical status of the theory of natural selection; and I am glad to have an opportunity to make a recantation."

Now, if he meant different things by Darwinism in the first quote than he did by natural selection in the second, there's no conflict. But in the same 1976 text he says outright that when he says Darwinism he means the theory of natural selection, not evolution. So that easy reconciliation looks blocked. The handout I need should lay out the main candidate readings: (1) he simply changed his mind; (2) the 1978 claim is about a different formulation of natural selection than the one he criticized in 1976; (3) he was using testability in a less strict sense in 1976 than his own falsifiability criterion would require; (4) he was sliding between saying the theory is untestable and saying it is a tautology. For each, say what it would take for the two passages to be consistent, and then give an honest verdict on which reading does least violence to what he actually wrote. I especially want you to flag whether his recantation actually retracts the 1976 metaphysical-programme claim or just relabels it, becuase that's where I suspect he's papering over the mess.
