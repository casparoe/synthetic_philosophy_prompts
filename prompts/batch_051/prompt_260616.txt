I'm putting together a term-paper proposal for a seminar on self-reference in legal and rule-governed systems, and I'd like you to write the proposal itself. The topic is Nomic, the game in which the rules of the game can themselves be amended, including the rules that specify how amendment works. My half-formed idea is that the standard fix of declaring certain rules immutable and placing them outside the normal amendment procedure does not actually solve the self-amendment problem, because the rule that marks those rules as immutable is itself an ordinary mutable rule. A majority could apparently amend the immutability-assigning rule first and then amend the supposedly immutable rules, which suggests either that immutability is never more than a convention of convenience or that the system covertly depends on a rule it cannot express from inside. Please turn this into a proposal of roughly 1,200 to 1,500 words for a 5,000-word term paper. State the question precisely; explain why existing treatments that dismiss the paradox as a curiosity or settle it by stipulation leave the question open; set out a method, such as close analysis combined with a small formal model of rule change, and say why that method rather than doctrinal legal history or full game theory; specify what an answer would look like and what would count as the approach failing; and give an honest assessment of whether this is tractable in a semester. You need not include a bibliography; I will add citations myself.
