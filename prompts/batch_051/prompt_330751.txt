I'd like a poem for my philosophy of math seminar next week, where we're doing Skolem's paradox. A villanelle, 19 lines, ideally iambic pentameter but I won't be a stickler about that. The two refrains should carry the two perspectives: one refrain is the outside view, something like the whole universe of the model is countable; the other is the inside view, something like the reals are uncountable. I want the villanelle structure to do the philosophical work — the refrains keep cutting each other off the way the paradox keeps reasserting itself, and in the last two lines, where they finally sit side by side, the resolution should surface: it only looks like a contradiction because the model is missing the function that would enumerate its own reals, so "uncountable" is doing its work relitive to the model, not the metatheory. Don't mention Löwenheim–Skolem or ZFC by name in the poem itself. Someone who knows the paradox should know exactly what's going on; someone who doesn't should still end up with a real poem about counting the uncountable. Accuracy about the relativization of uncountability matters more to me than prettiness, though I'd like both if you can manage it.
