Im trying to figure out whether my sister's animal ethics hangs together or is just a bundle of intuitions pointing in different directions, and I'd appreciate someone beating on it.

She gave up beef because she thinks cows are more cognitively sophisticated than chickens, so killing one cow is a worse act than killing one chicken. Fine. But she still eats chicken almost every day. A chicken is maybe two meals; a cow is a few hundred meals. So her 'less bad' diet is responsible for the better part of two hundred chickens a year, whereas if she ate the same amount of beef she'd be responsible for maybe a fifth of a cow. When I point this out she says it doesnt matter: killing one cow is just worse, full stop. So in cow-vs-chicken cases she refuses to aggregate across numbers.

Now flip the context. She also eats a lot of plant-based mince 'for the animals.' If someone brings up the field mice that get killed in wheat and soy harvests, she says those are side effects of growing food, not deaths she deliberately paid for; buying a chicken, she says, is paying someone to kill that bird for her. So in the chicken-vs-plant-mince case she is suddenly very interested in counting individual deaths, but only the ones that were done on purpose.

So here's the asymmetry bugging me. Against beef she compares one individual to one individual and refuses to add up the year's bodies. Against plant mince she insists every individual chicken counts, even though no single purchase kills a chicken on the spot. But against harvest deaths she treats a whole process full of individual mice deaths as one fuzzy side effect. Can all three moves be justified by one principle? For example, could you save the beef/chicken thing with a view that weights expected suffering by cognitive sophistication? And if she says buying chicken is morally the same as killing the chicken herself, can she still use the side-effect excuse for the mice? If the moves can't all be kept, which one goes first, and what does the resulting view actually tell her to eat?
