Write a poem of 16 to 20 lines that dramatizes the core logic of scapegoat sacrifice: a community under stress transfers its diffuse guilt onto a single victim, who is made to substitute for the disorder, and then kills or expels that victim and experiences restored unanimity. The poem's form should enact that logic rather than describe it. The opening should be in the crowd's collective voice, in tight meter and rhyme, piling accusations on the victim and making the person sound at once powerful and contemptible. Then there should be a turn: the victim speaks, in broken meter, but reusing the crowd's images and accusations, now turned inward and half accepted; the reader should see the community's verdict being absorbed by the victim, not simply protested. Finally, a brief return to the collective voice, calmer and stripped down, should suggest that the killing has done its unifying work. Do not moralize or make the crowd monstrous; the disturbing thing should be how quietly the mechanism completes itself.
