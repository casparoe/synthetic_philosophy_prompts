I work in flight control systems and, more recently, unmanned aerial systems: I read block diagrams and safety cases fluently, but I have no training in philosophy or international law. Lately I have been trying to make sense of the idea of meaningful human control in the debate over lethal autonomous weapons. As I understand it, the position runs something like this. The property that matters for whether an autonomous weapon may be used is not how internally sophisticated it is, but whether the humans who decide to field it retain enough control over the selection and engagement of targets to be answerable for what it does; accordingly, the policy goal should be to require design and doctrine that secure this control, rather than to settle a hopeless line-drawing dispute about where autonomy begins. I also know the stock objection: control is an elastic word, and a commander who merely approves a machine's recommendation may satisfy the letter of the requirement while doing none of the moral work.

I would like you to restate this position, in its strongest defensible version, using the vocabulary I actually think in: supervisory control, levels of automation, authority gating, reversion modes, mode confusion, automation bias, latency and stale-data guards, the operational envelope, and the operator understood as monitor, approver, or veto holder. This should not be a loose analogy but a claim-by-claim translation, the way one would translate a control-law specification into code or derived requirements; if you find yourself strengthening or repairing the position to make it cohere, say where and why. Then give me a residue report: which parts of the position map cleanly onto that vocabulary, which parts you had to distort or fudge to make them fit, and which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all. Finally, your verdict on the residue. Does it show that my engineering vocabulary is missing distinctions the philosophers and lawyers are right to insist on, or does it show that meaningful human control is less precise than it sounds and is doing the work of a slogan? Throughout, separate what is settled in this debate from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
