Hey, I'm in a philosophy of AI course and I've been pushing a concept in discussion that I need an expert on AI interpretability to check, so please start by brainstorming the possible readings of what I'm about to say, then tell me honestly where it breaks. I call it 'counterfactual legibility,' meant as a training target rather than a post-hoc explanation. My definition: an output is legible to a reader when the reader can see, from the output itself, how the answer would change under small changes in the question or case. So if a model says a hospital may use an algorithm to triage patients, a legible output would make it visible that the verdict flips if the algorithm isn't auditable but not if it's merely expensive—not becuase the model lists those dependencies, but because the answer is written so the weighting is discernible. I know 'legible' already gets used in AI safety to mean something like 'readable to humans,' so I might just be renaming that. Does this already exist under another name? Is it distinct from explainability, or have I just moved the opacity from the model to the output? Give me the three strongest objections, and say whether any of them is fatal.
