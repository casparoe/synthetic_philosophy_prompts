At dinner last night my friends Priya and Dan got into it over a job Dan is about to take, and they asked me to referee, but I honestly couldnt.

Dan has two offers. The first is a steady salaried job, which he rates on a 0–10 scale as: 5 if there's a recession, 6 if the economy just grinds along, and 6 if it booms. The second is a commission-only sales job, which he rates 0, 4, and 12 in those same three futures.

Priya said taking the commission job would be a mistake. This is a one-shot choice and Dan has no idea which future is coming, so the only defensible move is to pick the option with the best worst case. That's the salaried job, since its floor is a 5.

Dan said he isn't ignoring the worst case; he's just looking at regret. If he takes the salaried job and a boom comes, he leaves 6 points on the table (12 minus 6). If he takes the commission job and a recession comes, he leaves only 5 points on the table (5 minus 0). So the commission job is right, because its biggest possible regret is smaller.

Priya hit back: suppose Dan had also been offered some wierd third job that would pay a 50 in a recession and 0 otherwise, a job he'd never actually take. On Dan's logic, just having that third offer on the menu changes the regret numbers and would flip him into the salaried job, since the salaried job's worst regret becomes 45 while the commission job's worst regret becomes 50. A rule that lets an irrelevant third offer flip your choice between the two real options is broken.

Dan said so what, the third offer isn't real, and a rule only has to work for the menu in front of you. Regret is inherently about what else was available; you can't regret missing an offer you didn't have.

Priya said that just shows regret is too unstable to bet a one-shot career choice on. When you can't assign probabilities, securing the floor is the only thing that isn't a guess.

Dan said her rule has the same problem from the other direction: it only pays attention to the single worst future and ignores the others. Taken seriously, it would make you reject a job with a big upside just because its floor is lower, which is managing dread, not deciding.
