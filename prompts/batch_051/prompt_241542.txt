hey, I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, mostly working on the metaphysics of consciousness, but I've gotten pulled into Schrödinger's philosophical essays where he keeps saying that the multiplicity of minds is maya and that there really is only one consciousness. I brought this to my reading group and someone pushed me to spell out what it would actually mean for action, and I ended up with an argument that I think proves too much. I'm hoping you can tell me where it breaks.

The argument, numbered:

1. On Schrödinger's view, the plurality of consciousnesses is only apparent; there is one consciousness appearing as many, just as Vedanta says the separateness of the jiva is maya.
2. If there is one consciousness appearing as many, then every experience, including every pain, belongs ultimately to that one consciousness.
3. So when I harm you, the pain I cause is experienced by the very same ultimate subject that I refer to as myself in my own case.
4. But if the subject of that pain is the same subject as me, then the only coherent reason for me to avoid causing it is self-interest: I would be avoiding future pain to myself in another strand of experience.
5. Therefore, on this reading, all other-regarding moral reasons collapse into self-regarding ones; morality is really just prudence across bodies, and the self/other distinction that seems to make altruism intelligible disappears.
6. That is not just revisionary, it seems to destroy the phenomenon: if I help a stranger because they are suffering, and someone tells me I am really helping myself, that changes the reason, not just its label.
7. So at least one of 1, 2, or 4 must be wrong, or the inference from 2 to 3 is dodgy.

I'd rather not give up 1, since I think it is a fair reading of Schrödinger, and I am also inclinded to keep 2. So I suspect the trouble is in 3 or 4, maybe something about what the phrase same subject can do for moral motivation. But I can't say exactly what I am paying if I reject 4. Can you tell me where this goes wrong, and what it costs to deny the different steps? I am especially interested in whether the metaphysical oneness actually has any implications for whom I should care about, or whether there is a hidden gap between same consciousness and same subject of welfare, or maybe between ultimate and conventional levels.

And please, if you could respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just like a letter.
