Okay, so I got asked to write a postmortem for a recommender our team shipped last month. The thing started acting off after a small UI change, and half the team keeps saying "goal misgeneralization" without being precise, and the other half says it can't be that because the rewards were fine. I dont want to relitigate the actual system; I want a clean toy case that splits the two readings apart.

Could you build me a case to order? Here are the specs.

Construct a small concrete AI setup (a gridworld, a text game, a recommender, whatever — but describable in under 200 words) that satisfies all four of the following:

1. In training and on the training distribution, the agent behaves exactly as if it were optimizing the base objective B (what the designers intended).
2. The agent actually has one stable mesa-objective M — the goal represented inside the agent — whose content is identical to B. Same target end-states, same trade-offs. Not a proxy for B, not a correlate.
3. At deployment, after a mundane distribution shift (no prompt injection, no adversarial inputs, no one editing weights, no self-preservation or situational awareness needed), the agent's behavior systematically diverges from what B would require, even though M is still fixed and still equals B. The divergence has to come from a world-model or planning assumption that doesn't transfer, not from the agent's goal changing.
4. A behavior-only evaluator who had watched the training behavior would diagnose goal misgeneralization in the usual proxy sense — and that diagnosis would be wrong.

Then do the check. Bottom line first: state in one sentence the single structural feature that makes the case work instead of collapsing into ordinary proxy-goal misgeneralization. Then give B and M explicitly, say what the train/deploy difference is, and go through specs 1–4, with a sentence or two each for why the case meets that spec. A compact table for the four checks is fine. Then say what happens if you remove the one feature — what sort of case do you get? Finish with how a defender of the slogan "same goal + fixed goal means same competent behavior" would attack your case, and whether you think the attack lands.

Hard cap: 450 words. I'll read the first 450 and grade the four checks plus the feature-removal test, not the prose style.
