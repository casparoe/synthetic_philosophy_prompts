In my cognitive science reading group the concepts debate has completely stalled. The prototype people say concepts are stored summaries — a central tendency plus a typicality gradient. The exemplar people say nothing is stored except the instances, and categorization is similarity computed on the fly. The theory-theory people say both are missing the fact that a concept hangs together because of an intuitive theory about the domain. And the maddening part is that everyone already concedes the other sides' evidence: typicality effects, instance sensitivity, theory effects like why superficially similar things get sorted differently once you know the causal structure. Nobody moves. I want a diagnosis, not another vote for one of the three. Is this a substantive empirical dispute about what's actually in the head, or are prototype and exemplar models close enough to each other's predictions that behavioral data can't decide between them? Could the real disagreement be about whether abstraction is stored or computed on the fly — which behavioral experiments alone can barely get at? Or are the three camps answering different questions under the single word 'concept'? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a specific experiment, a formal result, or a change in how the question is asked?
