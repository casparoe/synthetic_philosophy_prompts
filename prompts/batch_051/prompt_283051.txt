Something about the philosophical discussion of the standing to blame has never quite clicked for me. Suppose A has done something genuinely wrong, and B is fully informed about the facts and has considered them carefully, so that B's criticism of A is true and well-supported. Many philosophers still say that B may lack the standing to blame A if B is a hypocrite—say, because B has committed the same kind of wrong and continues to excuse it in himself. But B's own conduct cannot change what A did, and cannot weaken B's evidence for saying so. What normative work is the idea of standing doing here? If lacking standing can make B's blame improper even though the criticism is true and well-justified, does that not let A off the hook merely because the critic happens to be at fault in the same way? I cannot see how standing could be anything distinct from the truth and epistemic justification of the criticism itself. Please explain what I am missing, and do so in British English.
