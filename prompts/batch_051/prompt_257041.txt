I keep coming back to the Knobe effect — the chairman vignette where a cost-cutting side effect that harms the environment gets called intentional while the helpful side effect gets called unintentional, even though the chairman says he doesnt care either way. The experimental philosophy takeaway seems to be that the folk concept of acting intentionally is itself morally loaded: whether a side effect counts as intended depends partly on whether its bad. But I cant shake the worry that this is just a performance effect. Maybe the concept stays neutral and people are answering a different question than the one asked — like "is he blameworthy?" — or theyre just more motivated to pin a bad outcome on someone than a good one. What I want to know is whether the claim "the concept of intentional action has moral content" is something you can genuinely test, and not just a slogan dressed up as a finding. Design the strongest test you can to separate the constitutive reading from the performance reading. Be concrete about stimuli and measures, not just "run more vignettes." Then say what pattern would count for each side, what a clean result still wouldnt settle either way, and finally commit: which version of the test would you actually run, and which account would you bet on today?
