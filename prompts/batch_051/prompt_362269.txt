A city caps annual rent increases for sitting tenants in pre-2000 units, exempts new construction entirely, and lets landlords reset the rent at vacancy. The housing department says this protects sitting tenants without discouraging new supply. The same city raises its minimum wage while keeping a lower rate for workers under eighteen, arguing that the full rate would price some of them out of entry-level jobs. What philosophical commitments about fairness, welfare, and the point of price controls do these paired exemptions presuppose? Do the two policies rest on a single consistent view of who deserves protection from market outcomes, or are they in tension? Is the market distortion each control creates treated as a tolerable side-effect or as part of the point? End by saying which consideration would most change your reconstruction, and where your reading of the policies' commitments is most likely to fail.
