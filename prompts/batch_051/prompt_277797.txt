my friend has pretty firm views about sweatshops. here are her verdicts so far: a factory paying the local going wage to workers whose only other option is subsistence farming isnt exploitative, just poor. a factory paying 15% below the going wage but still more than the workers could make anywhere else is exploitative, even though theyre better off taking the job. a factory paying above the going wage but running 14-hour shifts and locking the fire exit till 6pm is exploitative. a factory paying the going wage, where workers only put up with the grueling pace because a land grab destroyed their farms last year, is exploitative. and a buyer paying a 25% premium on a rush order during a slow season, when the workers would otherwise be idle, is not exploitative. the new case she's deciding: a factory pays exactly the going wage, recruits workers from a region where a natural drought has wiped out their alternatives, runs legal but monotonous and physically hard shifts, and could easily raise pay 20% without cutting jobs or falling below the industry-average profit margin. what would she say, and why? i'm less interested in the single answer than in the rule she's tacitly running, the other rules that fit all five earlier verdicts just as well, where those rules disagree on the new case, and what further case would best settle which rule she's actually using.
