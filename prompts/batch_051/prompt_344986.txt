I cover science and ideas for a living, and I'm working on a piece about whether we can say what causation is in terms of manipulation without talking in a circle. Here's what I'd like from you.

Take two people who've spent years arguing about Woodward's interventionist account of causation. One is an interventionist of roughly the Woodwardian sort: X causes Y when, if you were to intervene on X in the right way (a surgical change to X that isn't itself driven by other variables in the system, and that affects Y only through X), Y's value or its probability distribution changes, with the relationship holding up as invariant under such interventions. The other is a circularity-skeptic: they say "intervention" in that gloss is itself a causal notion, so the account either goes in a circle or quietly leans on causal judgments it was supposed to earn. These two decide to do a good-faith adversarial collaboration instead of keep talking passed each other.

Draft the joint statement they'd sign. I want:

- a numbered list of claims both accept. Make these non-vacuous: include what they agree on about scientific practice, about why invariance and manipulation are useful in experiments, and about what the circularity worry is not;
- then a disagreements table. For each row, give the precise formulation of the disagreement, with each side's wording phrased the way that side would accept as its own;
- then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that, if settled, at least one side would have to move. For each crux, give a concrete case, test, or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts the case or test would show.

For cases and tests, don't reach for citations or papers. Work with clean standard or made-up examples: a barometer and a storm, a randomized trial where the randomization turns out to be compromised, a set of variables that are observationally equivalent under Markov factorization, that sort of thing. I want the structure of the disagreement nailed down, not a bibliography.

One more ask: if my setup is off—say the circularity worry dissolves once you pick a formalism, or "surgical intervention" can in fact be specified without causal talk, or the two positions aren't actually opposed the way I've framed them—tell me that straight, and draft the corrected document instead of a polished version of a confused one. On confidence: I'd rather you flag the places where both sides would be guessing, or where a crux is too coarse to settle anything, than have you make the document look tidier than it is.
