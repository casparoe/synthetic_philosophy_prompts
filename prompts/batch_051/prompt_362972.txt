I've been reporting on the psychology of thought experiments and I've come to this view: the more a thought experiment works as an intuition pump—the more it grabs you, sticks, makes its conlcusion feel obvious—the less it's worth as evidence. Vividness, narrative, and emotional salience are doing the persuading, not the structure of the case. I want to know what would count against this. What evidence or arguments should actually move me off it? I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I want an honest answer about whether the view has been built so squishy that nothing could count against it—and if it has, whether that's a problem. What would a world where this is false look like, and do we have any signs we live in it?
