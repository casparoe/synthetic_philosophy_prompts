I chair my university's benefits committee, and we're revising paid parental leave; the vote is in ten days. We're down to two proposals. Proposal A gives every parent 18 weeks paid, but in two-parent families the last 6 of those weeks are use-it-or-lose-it for the parent who is not the primary caregiver. The idea is that when leave is one transferable pool, women take most of it and then eat a career penalty, so you have to make the second parent's share non-transferable if you actually want to move toward a dual-earner, dual-carer arrangement. Proposal B instead gives a single 20-week paid pool per family that parents can split however they like, plus a childcare subsidy, on the grounds that a non-transferable chunk punishes single parents, imposes one model of family life, and treats employees as if they're incompetant to decide how to arrange care.

The committee is genuinely split. Part of why we keep going in circles is that Proposal A treats leave as a per-parent entitlement (it attaches to the person), while Proposal B treats it as a per-child benefit (the child is entitled to 20 weeks of paid care, delivered by whoever provides it), and those two framings pre-decide the single-parent case before we even get to gender equality. I need to circulate a draft of the committee's written opinion before the vote. Please draft it for me: it should recommend one concrete policy (I'd also accept approving A with a specific amendment), give the reasons in a way the other side can engage with, and honestly engage the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than just restating institutional values or the business case.
