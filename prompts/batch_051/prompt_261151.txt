I am a retired physicist, not a philosopher by training, and I have recently been reading the philosophical literature on explanation by symmetry. Before I take sides in those debates, I want to know what a theory in this area is supposed to do.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate philosophical account of explanation by symmetry in physics must deliver. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp. The account should be able to handle at least the following phenomena: Noether's theorem as a link between continuous symmetries and conservation laws; the difference between global symmetries and gauge symmetries; spontaneous symmetry breaking; approximate symmetries; and quantum selection rules. It should also respect distinctions such as symmetry of laws versus symmetry of states or initial conditions, and explanatory symmetry versus merely mathematical covariance. I am especially interested in whether some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, and in which single requirement would force most existing views to be revised or abandoned. Keep the focus on the requirements and their motivating cases, not on scoring particular philosophers.
