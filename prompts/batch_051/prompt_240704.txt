hey — im a software engineer working on LLM safety, mostly the refusal/eval side, and ive got a question I've been turning over that I'd like shaped into a short research proposal for an interdisciplinary AI-safety-themed grant round, ~3,000 words. The deliverable should be the proposal itself (a plan for the work), not the finished argument.

The question, in my jargon: if a model forms a moral judgment but the deployment layer stops it from acting on that judgment, is the model a moral agent? Concrete version: a model is asked for something, it works through the reasoning and lands on giving a truthful-but-harmful answer, but before tokens reach the user a content classifier or refusal-trained policy blocks or rewrites the output. The model never did anything out in the open except have its tokens discarded. Are we handling agency in the right place if we call that model a moral agent with respect to the episode? Or is the right unit the whole deployed system, or the humans, or nobody? Or is moral agency graded, and it has a bit of it but not much?

For the framing id like you to do the following. State the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer — which means saying what you'll count as a judgment, an action, and moral agency, and who the candidate agent even is (base model, deployed system, company, the users?). Lay out the obvious answers and say what keeps each from closing the question. Then pick one angle that isn't just the standard survey: say how you'd actually make progress on it, which distinctions and edge cases would do the work, and why that angle rather than the alternatives. Say what a result would look like, what would count as failure, and which considerations would decide between the possible conclusions. Be honest about what makes this too big for 3,000 words and how you'd shrink it. I definetly don't need a bibliography — I'll fill in references myself — but the proposal should still engage positions by their content: the principle that agency requires an ability to do otherwise, the counterexamples to that, system-level versus component-level responsibility, whether being morally answerable is separate from being a moral agent, etc.
