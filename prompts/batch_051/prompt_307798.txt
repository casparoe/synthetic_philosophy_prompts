I'm a high school senior interning in a state legislator's office, and I've been asked to draft a one-page internal procedure the office can use several times this session, before the member takes a public position, to evaluate any proposal that changes who may vote or whether voting is required. The procedure should be something staff can run in about fifteen minutes and the chief of staff can sign off on: a sequence of yes/no questions with clear stop points and a recommendation at the end, grounded in some explicit principle of democratic inclusion — for instance, that people affected by decisions should have a say, or that equal citizens should have equal voting power — rather than in polling or party line. Please write that procedure, and then run it on three proposals the office expects to consider: lowering the voting age to sixteen; allowing non-citizen permanent residents to vote in municipal elections; and making voting compulsory with a small fine for non-voting. Please also flag at least one place where the procedure gives an answer the office will not like, mark the steps that are really judgment calls dressed up as rules, and say what the procedure cannot do. If the task as I've posed it is confused or cannot be done well, tell me that plainly instead of giving me a polished but unusable checklist.
