My friend has spent a long time thinking about when it makes sense for a person to put a probability on their own choices, and I have been trying to work out what rule she is actually using. The judgments she has given me, stated as accurately as I can manage, are these. She will say there is an 80% chance she eats oatmeal tomorrow, because that is an entrenched habit and she is not making any decision right now. She refuses to give any number for whether she will accept the job offer she is currently weighing, because while she is deciding, a number would not be a prediction but one more move inside the decision. She will give 70% for attending the conference in March, because she has already settled that she will go if her grant comes through, and she gives 70% for the grant. And she will not give a number for whether she skips dessert tonight, even though she has skipped it only twice in her last ten dinners, because, she says, at this point a number would be the start of a decision rather than a prediction.

The new case is this. Tomorrow morning a subscription she has been putting off a decision on expires, and the app will ask her to renew or cancel then and there. She will decide on the spot, by feel, and she will not think about it again until the prompt appears. She has faced this same renewal prompt six times before: four renewals, two cancellations, and she knows those numbers. If I ask her tonight, do you predict she will say she can put something like a two-thirds chance on renewing tomorrow?

I am less interested in the exact figure than in the rule she seems to be running, the rival rules that fit her earlier judgments just as well, what each main rule says about the renewal case, and what further case would best settle which rule she actually holds. Please also tell me what would change your mind about which rule fits her best, or where your reasoning about her is most likely to fail.
