I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and over the past few years I have reported on several disputes where a community's transmitted memory of an event pulls against the archival record. I have found myself carrying around a set of commitments that I would like someone to audit for consistency. Here is roughly where I have landed. First, when a community's memory and contemporaneous documents conflict on a matter of fact, I believe the documentary record should enjoy a default presumption of reliability: it was produced closer to the events, it is usually corrigible, and it did not pass through the selective pressures of group transmission. Second, I nevertheless think public commemoration should sometimes defer to a community's memory even when that memory is demonstrably inaccurate, because commemoration is not primarily an epistemic practice; it sustains identity, solidarity, and the capacity to act in the present. Third, I think historians should not treat living memory merely as a defective instrument for recovering what happened. The fact that a group remembers an event in a particular way, and holds that memory with a particular intensity, is itself a historical datum worth explaining, even where the remembered content is false. Fourth, I am sympathetic to Nietzsche's early argument that a certain horizon-limiting forgetfulness is necessary for life and for action, and that an excess of historical consciousness can leave a culture unable to make decisions. Fifth, I reject the stronger view that official history should be curated primarily for its life-enhancing or nation-building effects, because that seems to convert historiography into propaganda. Sixth, in my own practice I have noticed that when I am faced with a community whose account of a violent incident contradicts multiple contemporaneous documents, I am very reluctant to state in print that their account is false, even when I privately believe it is. I do not think this is mere conflict avoidance; I think it reflects some principle about respect that I cannot yet articulate. Do these commitments hang together? I want the audit to distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones, to order whatever tensions exist by severity, and to say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost.
