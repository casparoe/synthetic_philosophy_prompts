Last night I spent two hours arguing with a friend about two people carrying a piano up a flight of stairs, and I realized we were just replaying the standard standoff about joint action. His side: shared action is nothing over and above each person intending their part, expecting the other to do theirs, with common knowledge holding between them — list the individual attitudes and you're done. My side: that misses the thing to explain, namely that we are doing it together and that I'd be wronging him if I set my end down and walked off mid-lift. He says that obligation is just ordinary promising, bolted on afterwards; I say promising already presupposes a joint thing, so it's no help. By the end I couldn't tell if we were having a real dispute or just using the word together differently. My hunch is that he's treating togetherness as a descriptve relation in the world, like two gears meshing, while I'm treating it as a normative status change, like being married. But maybe that's just restating the standoff one level down. So diagnose it, really: what kind of disagreement is this — substantive, verbal, two questions under one heading, something else? And if you had to, what could actually move it: a new case, a distinction, a formal model?
