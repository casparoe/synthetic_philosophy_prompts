Philip Sidney in A Defence of Poesie argues, in the part where he compares the poet with the historian, that the historian is stuck with the particular truth of what did happen while the poet may range into what should be; so fiction isn’t to be condemned for departing from fact. I want to know whether that defense survives generative AI. Specifically, suppose a biopic of a real dead person is generated by an AI system: photorealistic footage of their face and voice, in scenes that never happened, edited so smoothly that most viewers cannot tell what was invented. Does Sidney’s argument still protect the maker, does it now protect something else (like labeling or provenance requirements), or does it just not apply once the medium no longer signals “this is fiction”? I’m asking about the argument, not the policy. If the new development doesn’t actually touch Sidney’s premise, or my setup begs the question, tell me that straight rather than answering on its terms.
