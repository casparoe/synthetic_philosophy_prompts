I sit on the research funding commitee at a mid-size university, and we have to decide whether to renew a three-year grant for a metaphysics project that aims to develop a new account of what causation is. The applicant is well-respected and has decent publications, but half the committee wants to reject on the grounds that philosophy doesn't make progress: there's no agreed measure of it, and after two thousand years people still disagree about fundamentals, so we can't tell whether the money actually did anything. The other half says that objection confuses progress with consensus. I need to circulate a short written opinion before our meeting. Please draft it: recommend whether to fund the project (or fund it with conditions), give criteria we could actually apply to philosophy proposals, and engage the 'no progress' worry seriously rather than just dismissing it.
