So I write about science and ideas for a living, and I ended up down a rabbit hole with Sextus Empiricus's Outlines of Pyrrhonism, specifically the part where he lays out the five modes of Agrippa. He describes how any claim, when you ask what justifies it, lands in one of a few bad spots: the justification needs another justification and so on without end, or the chain doubles back on itself, or you just assert something as a hypothesis and stop there. What struck me is that the third option—the hypothesis—is always treated as a failure, but in actual intellectual life we constantly make provisional stops: a scientist trusts an instrument until it gives a reason not to, a journalist trusts a document until it's undermined, a reader trusts the translation unless there's a flag. I've been calling this 'deferred grounding.' The idea is that the stopping point is not a belief that's justified by default; it's a provisional loan on future justification, like escrow. You don't own the ground, but you act on it while the title is still being checked. I think this makes the regress less scary because justification is not meant to terminate, and it makes dogma less dogmatic because the assumption is explicitly time-indexed and revisable. But I also worry Ive just rediscovered contextualism, or pragmatism, or maybe something Peirce said, and that my distinction between a loan and a purchase doesn't do any real work. Tell me the three strongest objections and whether any one of them is fatal, not just uncomfortable.
