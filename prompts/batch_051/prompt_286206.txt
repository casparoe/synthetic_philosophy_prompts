Write me a satirical FAQ page, like 600 words, for a company that sells "negligible-impact waivers" to people who've concluded that since their own contribution never tips a large collective outcome, they might as well free ride on public goods guilt-free. Deadpan customer-service prose throughout — sections like "Do I still have to pay taxes?" and "What if everyone thought this way?" — and the jokes have to come from the actual ethics, not a caricature of a lazy guy. Let the page land the standard objections without stating them outright: the gap between "my one share makes no measurable difference" and "the good only exists because nearly everyone else is doing the exact thing I'm opting out of," the fairness problem in consuming benefits you'd never help produce, and the fact that the waiver is only worth anything so long as most people keep contributing anyway. End with a warm, community-spirited testimonial that quietly exposes the parasatism. The target is the rationalization itself; someone who works on collective action should nod at the assurance problem and not wince.
