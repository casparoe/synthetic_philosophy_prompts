The usual defense of Kelly betting—and of geometric rationality more broadly—is that maximizing expected log wealth is the one policy that doesn't care about the order of returns, and that any fixed-fraction policy differing from Kelly will be overtaken by it with probability approaching one over a long enough horizon, since Kelly has the highest asymptotic growth rate. The argument assumes you can always keep playing, even with a fraction of a cent left, and that the edge per bet is a known quantity. Now suppose the relevant market sells leveraged products that auto-liquidate the entire account on a single bad draw, so one tail event ends the compounding game for good. Does the long-run argument still get you to Kelly there, or does it need an extra term for ruin risk? Separately: if returns are fat-tailed enough that most of your edge shows up in rare events, does maximizing expected log wealth still pick out a sensible long-run policy, or was the geometric argument quietly assuming thin tails all along? I care less about the final formula than about which premise the ruin possibility and the fat tails each attack, and whether the argument can be repaired.
