Okay so I keep chewing on these apps where you make a chatbot of someone who died, trained on their texts and voice memos and old emails. A friend of mine did it with his dad and it's half-comforting and half-eating him up: the bot says things his dad never said, and now he cant bring himself to delete it because it feels like killing him again. I've been trying to figure out what a good set of rules for these things would actually have to do, and every take I see just points at one scenario and says the whole practice is obviously fine or obviously ghoulish. I want the full list of requirements first.

Give me the spec. What cases and situations does any decent theory/rules for these bots have to handle, for a company to build one responsibly or a lawmaker to regulate them — the person said while alive they didn't want this but the spouse wants it anyway; it's a bot of someone famous who never consented; a kid too young to really understand is talking to it every day; the company goes under and wants to sell or transfer the bot. What distinctions can't it collapse, like pre-death consent vs family consent after the fact, a bot that can only echo real messages vs one that generates new sentences, or grieving with a bot vs treating it like the person is still here. What facts about grief and privacy can't be hand-waved, like people not grieving on a schedule, or the privacy of living people whose messages ended up in the training data. For each requirement say why it's a requirement, what breaks if you skip it. Then mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just one side's preference, and tell me wich single requirement knocks out the most popular stances. If some of the requirements can't all be satisfied at once, say that too. Keep the scorecard of existing positions short; the requirements list is the part I actually want.
