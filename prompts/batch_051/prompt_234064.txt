I've been reading philosophy of mind again, and I keep coming back to Fodor's language of thought hypothesis — roughly, that thought just is computation over sentence-like representations in an innate mentalese, not in English or any other public language. It's the first theory of thinking that's made real sense to me, mainly because of the standard systematicity and productivity points: an unbounded range of new thoughts from a finite stock of parts, and anyone who can think that the cat is on the mat can also think that the mat is on the cat, as if thought has a compositional syntax. It also explains why every normal child can learn any natural language: they're not building thought from scratch, just translating their public language into a code that's already running.

But I've also got this stubborn intuition that programming languages are a pressure point. Since I started coding seriously, I catch myself actually thinking about everyday plans as loops with conditions and return values, and it doesn't feel like I'm doing mentalese first and then decorating it with Python syntax. The code feels like it's doing part of the thinking. Worse, different languages make different thoughts feel obvious: in a functional language recursion feels like the natural way to say almost anything, while in an imperative language I reach for mutable state and a sequence of steps without noticing. If mentalese is innate and neutral between public languages, how can picking up a programming language change what thoughts are easy to have?

I can talk myself out of it, though, by saying that 'thinking in Python' is just a habit of notation — the real computation happens in mentalese and Python is only the interface, like a musician thinking 'in' sheet music when the actual music isn't made of black dots. That would make the intuition a translation illusion to be debunked, not a reason to touch the theory. But I'm not sure I actually believe it, and I don't want to just assert it.

What I want is a negotiated verdict. Say what it would take to show that the intuition really is a translation illusion and can be dropped. Say what it would take to amend or abandon the language of thought hypothesis so that public languages like Python can reshape the underlying code. And say whether there's a reading on which both survive — maybe programming languages don't replace mentalese but extend it's vocabulary or bias which representations get assembled. Which move would you actually make, and why not the others?
