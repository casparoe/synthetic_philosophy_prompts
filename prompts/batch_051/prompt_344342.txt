I'm a PhD student in philosophy, though my own work is in ethics rather than the history of philosophy, and I am reading Nietzsche's On the Genealogy of Morality for the first time in a reading group. After a session on the first essay, a colleague in political theory (who has clearly read the book carefully) summarized its argument for me roughly as follows. Nietzsche gives an origin story for our moral vocabulary: *good* was originally how aristocrats described themselves and their way of being—noble, strong, capable, life-affirming—while *bad* was their contrast term for whatever fell short of that, namely the common, the weak, and the unhealthy. Later, a priestly caste, full of ressentiment, carried out what Nietzsche calls the slave revolt in morality: unable to defeat the nobles in the world, they revalued their values in imagination, so that humility, poverty, and passive endurance became *good*, and the nobles' strength and self-assertion were recast as *evil*. Christian morality, on this telling, is the institutionalized form of that revaluation. And the point of the genealogy, my colleague concluded, is that once we see that our most cherished moral ideals grew out of resentment and self-deception, we are no longer entitled to treat them as authoritative—at least not over the kinds of people who are strong enough to live by their own values.

I want to check how fair this is before I repeat any of it in my own teaching. Could you go through the summary roughly claim by claim and tell me which parts the text genuinely supports, which are compressed to the point of distortion, and which are imposed on it rather than derived from it? I am especially unsure about the final step: does the first essay actually license the move from a genealogy to the conclusion that the values have no authority, or does that require an extra premise Nietzsche does not supply? Throughout, please separate what is settled by the text from what is genuinely contested among careful readers, and do not present a contested interpretive line as if it were the consensus.
