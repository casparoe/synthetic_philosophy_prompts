Here is the picture of the facts as I understand them, so we are not spending the answer litigating them. Over the past two years, Anthropic trained sparse autoencoders on the internal activations of a language model and used them to decompose those activations into very many approximately monosemantic features. The meanings of those features were established partly by humans reading the texts that activated them and partly by smaller interpreter models shown those activating texts and asked to label the feature. One feature appeared to correspond to the Golden Gate Bridge; when the researchers amplified its activation, the model's behavior changed in a targeted way — it began introducing itself as the Golden Gate Bridge and bringing up the bridge unprompted. The demo was widely read as evidence that we can now find and manipulate a model's internal "concepts." I am less interested in the technical details than in what the episode does and does not establish about recursive interpretability.
