Analyze the 1936 abdication crisis as a test case for the theory of constitutional monarchy in a democratic state. The salient facts: Edward VIII wished to marry Wallis Simpson, an American whose divorce history made the marriage unacceptable to the Church of England; Prime Minister Stanley Baldwin opposed the marriage, the Dominion governments were consulted, and in December 1936 Edward abdicated rather than reign without it. Through the episode, work out what a constitutional monarch is: a private person, a public office, or both at once. What does the standard picture of constitutional monarchy—a hereditary head of state whose formal powers are in practice exercised by ministers responsible to an elected parliament, and whose own role is supposed to be nonpolitical—say the actors should have done, and what does it have to say about a monarch's marriage, private conscience, and choice to remain on or leave the throne? Which features of the crisis does that picture explain well, and which does it misdescribe or pass over in silence? Finally, does the episode confirm, refute, or require qualification of the common claim that constitutional monarchy is compatible with democracy because the sovereign is now purely ceremonial? Do not force the theory to speak where it has no resources, and say which party to the crisis had the strongest democratic claim.
