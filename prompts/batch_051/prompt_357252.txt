My friend works on animal signaling, and I keep a running list of her one-line rulings on cases. So far it goes something like:

- a peacock's train: honest signal of male quality
- a gazelle stotting in front of a predator: honest signal of vigor
- a dart frog's bright coloration: honest signal of toxicity
- a stag roaring during the rut: honest signal of condition
- a t-shirt that says "I am deeply trustworthy": not an honest signal, just cheap talk

New case: a guy trying to convince someone he does manual labor holds out his hands to show his calluses. Showing your hands costs basically nothing, but real calluses are pretty hard to fake on the spot.

What would she probably say about the callus case? I'm starting to think she's running the handicap principal, where signals are kept honest by their cost. But she might instead be running an index view, where signals are kept honest by being physically tied to the trait that's being advertised. Which is she actually running, and what single extra case would finally tell those two apart? Also, would she even count the calluses as a signal, or just as a cue or evidence?
