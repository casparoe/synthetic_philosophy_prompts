I've been arguing with a friend about whether the old skeptical challenge from Zhuangzi's butterfly dream is undermined by what researchers now know about lucid dreaming, and I want a careful answer rather than a yes-or-no. The challenge, as I understand it, runs like this. Zhuangzi dreamed he was a butterfly and, on waking, could not tell whether he was a man who had dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly then dreaming he was a man. If even the experience of waking leaves that question open, then no feature of present experience appears to settle whether one is awake or asleep, and ordinary claims to know that one is awake start to look unsupported. The development I have in mind is that people can be trained to notice dream signs, and lucid dreamers can even signal from inside a dream that they are dreaming; in some experimental setups a sleeping subject and a waking researcher communicate in real time through agreed eye movements. One might think this breaks the symmetry: a person can sometimes know she is dreaming from the inside, and by the same method she can know she is awake when her reality checks hold up.

What I want from you is an assessment of whether the butterfly-dream skeptical argument survives this development. Identify exactly which premise the lucid-dreaming results put pressure on, say whether that premise can be repaired or replaced so that the skeptical conclusion still follows, and if the argument does not survive, state what the skeptic is still entitled to claim once the development is granted. I am less interested in whether the experiments are real than in what they would show if we accepted them, so treat the empirical claims as stipulated and do the philosophical work.
