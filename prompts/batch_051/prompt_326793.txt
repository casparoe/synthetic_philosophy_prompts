I'm not trained in philosophy but I've been going in circles about free will lately. A while back I read Dennett's Elbow Room and the bit about the digger wasp Sphex stuck with me: the wasp brings a criket back to its burrow, leaves it at the threshold, goes inside to check things out, then comes back out and drags it in. If the cricket gets moved while the wasp is inside checking, the wasp comes out, dutifully drags it back to the threshold, and goes inside to check again. It'll do this over and over, never catching on. Dennett calls that sort of behaviour sphexish. I started thinking about sphexishness not just in bugs but in people, and now I've got a list of things I seem to believe, and I genuinely can't tell if they hang together.

Here's what I currently think:

(a) A person acting freely can notice when a routine or a plan isn't working and switch to something else.

(b) Humans are made of ordinary physical stuff, nothing extra, so everything we do is caused by ordinary physical processes.

(c) If you rewound the whole universe to the exact same state, I'd make the same choice every time.

(d) Adding randomness wouldn't make me free; if my arm moved randomly instead of for a reason, that's not really my action.

(e) Addicts and people trapped in compulsions are less free than someone who pauses and reconsiders, but that pausing and reconsidering is also just brain activity.

(f) There's no sharp line between us and Sphex; our flexibility is just much more complicated routines.

Which of these actually conflict? Which only look like they conflict? Could you rank the real tensions by severity and say what the least costly revision would be—and what it would cost? Don't tell me to give up all of them or that everything is fine; I want to know where the actual fault lines are.
