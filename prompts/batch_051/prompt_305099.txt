I'm a trustee of a small education foundation, and we're deadlocked over clause 9 of our 2009 trust deed. It reads:

"The Trustees shall apply the funds to narrow, so far as is practicable, the gap in measured attainment between the most and least advantaged cohorts of beneficiary schools, while giving priority to measures that raise the attainment of the least advantaged."

The drafters clearly assumed the only live way to narrow an attainment gap is to raise the bottom. But a donor has now offered us a restricted grant whose sole activity would be to shut down a Saturday enrichment program at our strongest partner school, on the theory that advantaged kids being further advantaged is itself the harm. Under the evaluation plan, the top cohort's measured attainment slips a little, the bottom cohort's numbers barely move, and the gap narrows. No beneficiary is made better off.

Does clause 9 require us to take the grant, forbid it, permit it, or is it just silent here because it never imagined narrowing by subtraction? Work through the literal text, the purpose, and what the 2009 drafters would have said if they'd seen this proposal, and tell me which approach should govern. Then say what that answer reveals about wether the deed is actually committed to leveling down, or whether it only looked egalitarian because everyone assumed you narrow gaps by lifting people up.
