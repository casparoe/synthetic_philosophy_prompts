For an upper-level undergraduate moral philosophy exam, write one 60-minute essay question on the friendship model of filial duties standardly traced to Jane English. The question should test understanding rather than nomenclature: a student should not be able to earn a good mark merely by summarising the 'debt model' and the 'friendship model'. It must force them to (a) state exactly what the friendship model implies about a self-sacrificing but emotionally distant parent, and (b) confront the objection that friendships are chosen and roughly egalitarian while the parent–child relationship is unchosen and asymmetrical, and to say whether a relationship-based account of filial duties can answer that objection and accommodate the 'distant parent' intuition without slipping back into the debt model English rejects. Provide the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 naming five distinct moves a first-class answer must make, the marks each earns, and a one-line note on the misunderstanding typically revealed by its absence. All three components should be written in British English.
