Consider Nietzsche's thought experiment of the eternal recurrence: a demon tells you that the life you are living, exactly as you have lived it, will be repeated infinitely many times, and the test is whether this news would crush you or whether you could affirm it as a moment of triumph. Now vary the setup. Suppose the demon preempts an interpretive question and tells you plainly that the repetitions are not the same life returning in a circle but a linear sequence of distinct cycles, each containing a perfect qualitative duplicate of you; no memory, and no numerical identity, carries over from one cycle to the next. Does the test of affirmation do the same work in this version? Answer the question twice: first on the assumption that the original thought experiment's force depends on affirming this very life as yours, and second on the assumption that its force depends only on affirming the qualitative content of a life. For each, say whether the duplicate version preserves the original's force, weakens it, or exposes something the original had left ambiguous — and whether the original was in effect relying on numerical identity all along.
