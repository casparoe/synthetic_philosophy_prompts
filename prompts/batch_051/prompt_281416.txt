I serve on the academic conduct board of a university whose integrity code was drafted in the late 1980s and has never been revised. The clause we are applying is Article IV, Section 3: "Any student who knowingly aids or abets another student in the commission of an academic offense shall be held responsible for that offense as if it were the student's own." We are currently deadlocked on one case.

A student named Mira created and maintains an anonymous peer-editing exchange for the political science department. Students upload essays, and a matching bot that Mira programmed assigns each submission to a student reviewer; Mira herself does not read the submissions. The exchange became popular because it offers line editing without the social awkwardness of exchanging drafts in person. Last year, two different students were caught using the exchange to have essays bought from a contract-cheating site polished before submission. Mira responded by adding a filter that blocks uploads containing the uploader's name or student ID, but she did not add plagiarism detection, did not begin reviewing submissions, and declined a request from the department to shut the exchange down. She argued that the service is used overwhelmingly for legitimate revision and that students who bought essays would simply have found editors elsewhere. This term, a third student uploaded a ghost-written senior thesis chapter; the bot paired it with a reviewer, the reviewer returned line edits, and the student submitted the chapter as their own and was caught. Mira has now been charged under Article IV, Section 3. She says she neither wrote nor submitted the chapter, she had no knowledge of this particular upload, and her service made no counterfactual difference to the cheating.

We need to determine whether the clause reaches her conduct. We are stuck on three questions: whether "aids or abets" requires that the accused actually make a difference to whether the offense occurred; whether "knowingly" requires awareness of the specific offense, or whether awareness that her platform was being used for this purpose and continued operation in light of that awareness is enough; and whether maintaining an automated system can count as aiding when the operator does not review what passes through it. Please work through the text of the clause, the purpose of an academic integrity code, and what its late-1980s drafters would most plausibly have said about automated facilitation they had never imagined, and give a reasoned answer about whether Mira is responsible under this clause, noting which of the three issues should carry the decision.
