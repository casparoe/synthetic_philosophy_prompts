Two of my coworkers got into it over lunch about whether a DAO we're building is basically the social contract with code instead of a sovereign, and I still can't tell who was right. (1) Marcus said a smart contract isn't just a contract, it's the social contract actually working: instead of handing all force to a sovereign, you make it impossible to break the rules, like code is the Leviathan. Dana said that's naive, because Hobbes's whole point was that promises without a sword are just words, and code can only enforce what happens on-chain; the moment someone has to actually ship a physical item or do off-chain work, the code is just words again. (2) Marcus said that's why you add escrow, staking, and dispute-resolution oracles, so the counterparty loses money if they defect. Dana said those are just little swords. An oracle or a staking pool has the power to settle disputes, and now you have to trust them instead; you've recreated the sovereign one level down. (3) Marcus said no, because dispute resolution is decentralized among token holders, not a single authority. Dana said decentralized authority is still authority. If token holders can be bribed or captured, then the problem is just who guards the guards, which is exactly the problem Hobbes thought the commonwealth was supposed to solve, and your DAO just relocates it. (4) Marcus said but in practice reputation and repeated transactions handle most enforcement; people keep their word because they want a good rating and future customers, not because a state might punish them. Dana said that's because the platform can ban you and the legal system can come after you. The sword is in the background, so the repretuation system is a shadow of state force, not an alternative to it. (5) Marcus said okay, but if the sword is in the background, then enforcement is a layered stack, not a single sovereign; you can slowly replace each layer with code and contracts, and the state becomes a legacy module nobody calls anymore. Dana said but if you ever actually replace the last layer, you're back in Hobbes's state of nature with no common power to keep everyone honest, and if you don't, you haven't shown code can replace the sword, you've just hidden it. So the real fight is whether the state is a platform layer or a legacy dependency. They asked me to settle it, and I just went back to my desk.
