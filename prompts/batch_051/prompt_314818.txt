I chair my university's Faculty Grievance and Governance Committee, and I need a memo to circulate before our meeting this Thursday. Last month, during a blow-up in a department budget meeting, the chair of the philosophy department stood up and said, "That's it — I resign as chair, effective immediately," and walked out. A few hours later she emailed the dean saying she'd spoken in anger and "did not actually tender my resignation." Our bylaws only say that a chair resigns by "written notice to the dean"; they don't say wether a public oral resignation can count, or whether someone can take it back before the dean acts on it. The dean's office has been treating her as outgoing and has started delegating her duties to a interim chair, so this isn't just semantic.

The committee is split. Half think there's no resignation until the dean accepts a written one, since that's exactly the kind of procedural safeguard the bylaw exists for. The other half keep saying the words did the work the moment she said them: resigning isn't reporting a private decision, it's performing one in public, and you can't unsay an "I resign" any more than you can unsay an "I do." One member has even started calling it a "performative utterance," though I'm not sure we all mean the same thing by that. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate: recommend a concrete decision about whether she is still chair and what the dean must do now, with a clear bottom line, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side rather than just restating the side you agree with.
