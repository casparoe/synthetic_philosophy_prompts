I just got out of an hour-long argument with a friend about why knowledge is supposed to be better than mere true belief, and we kept going in a circle. He kept coming back to the passage in Plato’s Meno where Socrates says that true opinions “do not care to stay for long, and run away out of the human soul, and thus are of no great value until one makes them fast with causal reasoning.” His line is that knowledge is better because it’s stable; once you tie the opinion down, it stays. I kept saying that if a true opinion is present and guiding action correctly, it’s just as useful as knowledge in that moment, and the fact that it might later run away doesn’t matter for what it did then. He’d say I was conceding his point, I’d say he was changing the subject from value to reliability. We couldn’t agree whether the real issue is utility, stability over time, what’s valuable for its own sake, or just Socrates’ metaphor doing too much work. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a distinction that would dissolve it before we even take sides, or is one of us just misreading the Meno? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here?
