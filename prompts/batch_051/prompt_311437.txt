I've been reading about the hole argument in general relativity and I've ended up genuinely torn, which doesn't usually happen to me. On the one hand, I'm attracted to the substantivalist picture: spacetime is a real thing, its points exist, and the gravitational and matter fields are properties at those points. I like that it's a single, unified ontology — no mysterious relational glue, no having to say that locations only exist because stuff is somewhere. It also seems to make sense of why acceleration is absolute in a way that relationism struggles with. But then the hole argument seems to show that this view makes GR radically indeterministic: take a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some open region and nontrivial inside, and you get two models that agree everywhere outside the region but assign different field values to the points inside. If the points are real, these are two genuinely different worlds with identical initial data — and determinism is just dead, for no empirical reason anyone could ever detect. And my firm intuition is that there is no genuine difference between indistinguishable worlds; if two descriptions can't be told apart by any possible measurment even in principle, then they're not descriptions of two possibilities, they're two descriptions of one possibility.

So I'm stuck: I don't want to give up the clean substantivalist ontology, and I don't want to give up the principle that empirically indistinguishable models are the same world, and I also don't want to declare GR indeterministic when the "indeterminism" is only ever between models that agree on every observation. Walk me through what each option actually costs: biting the bullet on indeterminism, going relationalist, or some way of keeping substantivalism while denying that the two models are distinct possibilities (for instance, by saying spacetime points lack primitive identity or haecceities). And tell me honestly whether my "no difference without a detectable difference" intuition is doing real work here or is just me smuggling in empiricism.
