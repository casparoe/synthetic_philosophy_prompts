Construct a thought experiment in which an automated pricing system's observed behavior in a stable market is exactly optimal under two sharply different objective/search-space pairs, where the objective is what the system maximizes and the search space is the set of options it considers, but a single ordinary change in market conditions cleanly separates them. The point of the case is to establish that observed optimal behavior underdetermines an optimizer's objective and search space even when the two are considered jointly, so no part of the divergence may be stipulated rather than derived from the two hypotheses. Constraints: keep it realistic (an online marketplace or bidding system, not a science-fiction AI); keep the scenario itself to one paragraph; make the initial behavior strictly optimal under each hypothesis, not merely consistent; make the two objectives differ substantively rather than by mere rescaling; make the two search spaces differ in the options they make available; make the intervention mundane rather than a trick or a perfect-predictor test. After the scenario, demonstrate the design: state each hypothesis' objective and search space, show why each selects the observed behavior in the initial environment, give the intervention and the divergent behavior each predicts, and identify which difference between the hypotheses does the work. Conclude with the honest notes: what goes wrong if that working feature is removed, and how a defender of the view that behavior reveals an optimizer's objective would attack the case.
