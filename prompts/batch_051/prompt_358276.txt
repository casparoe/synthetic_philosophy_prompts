I am a doctoral student in music technology, working on the curation and annotation of large recorded-music corpora. My training, however, is primarily in software engineering and programming language theory, and I think most comfortably in terms of abstract data types, specifications versus implementations, structural versus nominal identity, evaluation, serialisation, and provenance. Please restate the central positions in the ontology of musical works in that vocabulary, one by one: the view that a work is an abstract sound structure whose instances are performances; the view that there are only concrete performances and recordings and that talk of a single work is a useful fiction over resemblance classes; the contextualist view that instrumentation and historical setting enter into the identity of the work itself; and the action-type view on which a work is a type of performance or action rather than a type of sound. For each, say which claims translate cleanly, which must be distorted to fit, and which have no expression at all in the target vocabulary. Then give me the residue report: what the translation drops, why it drops it, and whether the residue shows that my vocabulary is blind to the normative and tradition-bound dimensions of musical practice, or that some of the philosophical talk was less precise than it looked. Be honest about the fit rather than treating whatever is easiest to model as all there is. I want to know whether the ontology of musical works is a genuine problem about what sort of thing a work is, or whether, once stated properly, it collapses into a type-token-plus-provenance problem.
