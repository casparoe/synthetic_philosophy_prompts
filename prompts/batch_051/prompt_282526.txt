Last night I got into a long argument with a friend about what Descartes's cogito actually proves, and we kept going around in circles. I was defending the usual reading that the fact that I am thinking shows at minimum that I exist — whatever I turns out to be. My friend said that's way to strong: he kept bringing up the old Lichtenberg-style objection that Descartes is only entitled to the claim that thinking is occurring, not that any owner of the thinking exists. I pushed back that thinking can't just float around; even if it's a process, it's got to be the thinking of something. He said that's exactly the inference he's challenging, and we couldn't get past that point.

It also seems like the scholarly debate has stalled in basically the same place. Some interpreters read the cogito as a direct intuition rather than an inference; others reconstruct it as an enthymeme with the suppressed premise that whatever thinks exists, and then argue about whether that premise is analytic or a metaphysical claim about substances. There are also readings I've only half absorbed — Hintikka-style performative readings where the sentence I exist is self-verifying in a way that the sentence thinking is occurring is not, and deflationary readings where the I in I think is just a grammatical placeholder for an event. People also seem to be asking different questions under the same heading: what follows logically, what is psychologically immune to doubt, what Descartes needed for his foundationalist project in the Meditations, and whether the first certainty is a substance, a thinking thing, a proposition, or an act. My hunch is that a lot of the impasse comes from running these together, but I'm not a Descartes scholar and I'd like someone who is to test that.
