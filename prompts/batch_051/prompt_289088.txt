He works in some kind of cost estimating job and he kept saying we should have ignored all the specific details of our situation — the custom cabinets, the old house, the contractor who seemed perfectly on top of things — and just looked at how long kitchen remodels actually take for people in our area. He called this the outside view. I pushed back that the details had to matter. If the specifics of your own case carry no information, why would anyone bother learning anything about their own situation? He said thats exactly the trap: people use the details to tell themselves a story about why their case is special, but on average the dumb base rate beats the smart-sounding story, and the real problem is that people are way too confident in their own read of things.

I think he's smuggling a metaphor in and calling it a testable claim. Then tell me what a clear positive result would actually establish and what a clear negative result would establish. And finally, say what part of our disagreement no test could settle even in principle — maybe something about what it means for the details to contain information in the first place, or about how much confidence people should have in their own judgment.
