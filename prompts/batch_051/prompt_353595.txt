A friend of mine who thinks Merleau-Ponty overstates things keeps using a craftsman analogy to argue that motor intentionality — the way the body is already geared toward possible actions — is really in the head. A carpenter reaches for a hammer that's no longer on the bench; he isn't exercising "hammer-in-the-hand" knowledge, just a habit plus an outdated expectation. Phantom limb is the same, he says: the limb is gone but the brain-side machinery keeps running, so the lingering felt presence of the arm is an error, not evidence that the body itself knows how to act. I want an audit of this analogy, not a summary of the debate. Tell me what maps to what (carpenter, hammer, bench, missing tool, workshop) in the lived-body case, where the mapping breaks, which features of tool use the analogy quietly imports that a lived body doesn't have, and whether his conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table. Also say whether "the body is a tool of the mind" and "the body is the agent's point of view" are genuine rivals or just two descriptions of the same thing, and what a better analogy would be if there is one. Then tell me how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of.
