Apologies, this is long, but I'd rather give you the brief properly than have you guess. A reading group I'm in has been going in circles about hell for about a year, and two of the members finally agreed to do an adversarial-collaboration thing instead of another argument. One of them holds annihilationism (conditional immortality): the finally impenitent are raised, judged, and then destroyed, so the "second death" is literal extinction rather than unending conscious existence. The other holds Christian universalism: hell is real but temporary and remedial, and in the end every rational creature is freely reconciled to God. They both think the standard eternal-conscious-torment view makes God monstrous and violates proportionality, which is exactly why their actual fight is between extinction and universal restoration.

I want you to draft the document they should have produced. It should include:

1. A titled "Agreed claims" section, with numbered substantive claims both could sign as written — about what hell is even for, what divine justice cannot be, the proportionality problem with endless punishment, and what the biblical material at minimum rules in or out for both of them. Don't let it slide into "we agree to disagree" filler, and don't phrase the agreed claims so they secretly favor one side.

2. A "Disagreements" table with columns: Issue | Annihilationist formulation | Universalist formulation. Each cell should be a formulation that side would personally own, not a caricature the other side would write for them.

3. A "Cruxes" section. For each crux: state the sub-question in neutral terms both sides would accept, give a concrete case or thought experiment or scriptural text both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it shows. I suspect the cruxes are things like whether a creature can finally and freely refuse God, whether God's victory over evil can consist in evil's mere removal or requires the creature's reconciliation, and whether punishment's purpose is ultimately retributive or remedial — but find the others too.

Two rules. First, don't adjudicate: the output should read like a document both camps could put their names to, not like one side quietly winning. Second, if part of their fight turns out to be about the sense of words like "destruction" or "free will," say so explicitly and clean it up in the agreed section.

Don't worry about footnotes or citations; this is for a private reading group, not a journal. For my own filing, end the whole thing with a final line of the exact form \answer{one neutral sentence summarizing the deepest remaining crux} — so the brackets just contain that single sentence, nothing else.
