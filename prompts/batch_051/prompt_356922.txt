Exam question:

Explain Ian Hacking's notion of the looping effect as it applies to psychiatric classification. Your answer should state what the effect is, distinguish it from a self-fulfilling prophecy, state one implication for whether psychiatric categories pick out stable natural kinds, and illustrate with a brief example.

Student answer:

Hacking's looping effect is a two-way process. When a diagnostic label such as multiple personality is introduced, it gives people a new description under which to understand themselves. They then shape their behaviour and self-narrative to fit it, and clinicians in turn observe these behaviour patterns and revise the diagnostic criteria, which changes who gets described in the future. In this respect the looping effect is essentially a self-fulfilling prophecy scaled up: the label causes reality to conform to it, and over time that conformity feeds back into the official definition. Because members of the classified kind respond to being known, psychiatric kinds are interactive kinds rather than indifferent kinds like electrons. Hacking's point is that the loop can set in motion a new kind of person that did not previously exist as a way of being. It follows that psychiatric categories cannot be stable natural kinds, since the label is part of what makes the category what it is. Hacking would add that this does not make the people or their distress fictional, because the individuals classified genuinely suffer.
