I am an undergraduate sitting on my university's honor council, which hears cases of academic dishonesty and recommends sanctions to the dean, and I am simultaneously taking a seminar on moral responsibility. Galen Strawson's Basic Argument has unsettled the way I think about what we do on the council. As I understand it, the argument runs like this: to be truly morally responsible for what you do, you would have to be responsible for the kind of person you are, since your actions issue from your character and motives; but you could be responsible for the kind of person you are only if you had chosen it, and that choice would itself issue from a prior character for which you would also have to be responsible; the regress never finds a bottom, because no one can be causa sui. If that is right, none of the students we sanction is ultimately deserving of blame in the deep sense, and our current deliberations are quietly assuming exactly what the argument denies.

I do not want to conclude that the council should stop sanctioning people; we still have to protect the academic community, deter cheating, signal the standards of the institution, and treat the accused student fairly. What I want is a decision procedure we could actually run in about an hour per case, written for committee members who have never read Strawson and never will. It should ask the questions in order, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to each, say where the procedure stops with a no, and clearly flag the steps at which the committee is making a value judgment rather than applying a fact. It should keep the forward-looking and expressive functions of sanctions while refusing to let us smuggle back in the idea that we are measuring an individual student's deep moral worth.

Please run the procedure on two hypothetical cases: (1) a first-year student who copied a page of unattributed text under time pressure and admitted it at the first meeting, and (2) a senior who paid a classmate for several problem sets over a full term and denied it until shown records. My worry is that any difference in severity between these cases will turn out to rest on a desert-based judgment Strawson would reject, and that a strict reading of the Basic Argument might force us to treat them roughly alike even though that feels wrong. I am prepared for that answer if it is where the argument leads. Please answer in American English.
