I'm in an ethics reading group and last week we fell down a rabbit hole trying to define supererogation, the going-above-and-beyond stuff. One person proposed that an act is supererogatory if it's morally good to do but not wrong to skip. That sounded fine until someone pointed out that it makes holding an elevator door and donating a kidney to a stranger the same kind of thing, which felt off to most of us. Another person said the act has to involve real cost or risk, but then we couldn't figure out what to do with a billionaire who gives away nearly everything anonymously; it's a huge amount but barely a sacrifice to him.

Here are the cases we kept fighting about: (1) a stranger rushing into a burning building to save someone else's kid; (2) donating a kidney to a stranger; (3) a parent making dinner for their own young kids; (4) a soldier jumping on a grenade to save the rest of their squad; (5) that billionaire giving away 99% of his money anonymously; (6) forgiving a partner who had an affair; (7) working double shifts for years to pay for a sibling's medical care, while complaining about it constantly and expecting to be paid back; (8) buying coffee for the stranger behind you in line; (9) a doctor staying two hours past their shift to sit with a dying patient who has no family; (10) spending years getting really good at the cello purely for your own satisfaction. I'm pretty sure the first two are clear cases and the third is clearly not, but the rest we go back and forth on. Give me a working definition of 'supererogatory act' (or 'going above and beyond', if you think that's the better target), run it through each case, tell me where it gets the wrong answer or no answer, and say wich of your verdicts is least secure.
