I am a high-school student presenting a paper next month at a regional high-school ethics competition, and the position I will defend is that the doing/allowing distinction is not a moral primitive but a threshold applied to a graded cluster of underlying features: how directly the agent's decision is what makes the difference to whether the outcome occurs, how much control the agent had over how events unfolded, and how costly or morally marked the available alternative would have been. My claim is that the threshold separating doing from allowing is set by substantive background norms rather than by any deep structural fact about agency, so that in hard cases—a delayed rescue, withdrawing life support, reallocating scarce medical supplies—appealing to the distinction tends to track the wrong variable, and we reason better by asking directly how near the agent stands to the outcome, what alternatives were actually available, and which competing norms are already in play.

The audience will be mostly deontologically inclined students from rival schools who treat the killing/letting-die contrast as close to axiomatic: they think it explains why murder is worse than refusing aid, why negative duties bind more strictly than positive ones, and why agents are answerable for what they initiate rather than for what they merely fail to prevent. A smaller group will be consequentialist-leaning faculty judges who share my suspicion that the distinction has been overrated but worry that my replacement is disguised consequentialism with no principled way to set the threshold. Both groups want a view that preserves some agent-relative constraints and that gives clear verdicts in concrete cases.

Please prepare my question-and-answer prep sheet: the objections I am most likely to hear, in roughly the order they will come and phrased as this audience would phrase them; for each, a note on how likely and how damaging it is, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and a two-minute reply with an honest mark of where that reply is soft. Include the question I should be hoping nobody asks, and say why it is the dangerous one.
