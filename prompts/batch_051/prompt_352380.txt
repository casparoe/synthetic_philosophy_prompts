Section 9 of a national communications statute, enacted in the mid-1990s and never amended, provides:

"9(1) A person commits an offence if the person makes material available to a person in the State where, if the material were published by the person within the State, its publication would be contrary to this Act.
9(2) A person makes material available to a person in the State if the material can be accessed by that person by means of a publicly available electronic communications service, whether or not the material is stored on equipment within the State."

A broadcaster established abroad operates a video-news service whose content is lawful in the broadcaster's home jurisdiction but some of whose episodes would contravene the Act if published within the State. The broadcaster blocks direct access from the State: a visitor whose connection appears to originate there is shown a notice and receives no video. To improve delivery to users in a neighbouring country, the broadcaster uses a content-delivery network that keeps cache servers in many places, including one in the State. That in-State cache server mirrors segments of the broadcaster's videos and serves them to users in the neighbouring country; it does not check the location of the requester. A resident of the State learns of the cache server, discovers a way to retrieve segments from it, and with some patience assembles and watches most of one episode.

Work through whether the broadcaster has made the material available to a person in the State on four approaches: the ordinary meaning of the statutory words; the purpose section 9 is best understood to serve; what its drafters would most plausibly have said had they been asked about content-delivery networks; and what a reasonable reader of the statute would take it to require. Which approach should control here, and why? In particular, address whether the broadcaster's geoblocking should be decisive and whether extending the provision to this case would erase any principled distinction between publishing and merely providing the infrastructure that enables publication.
