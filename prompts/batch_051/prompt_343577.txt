Me and my flatmate have been going round in circles about this Daoist idea of wu-wei — roughly, acting without trying. I'd never heard of it before a podcast, and now I can't stop turning it over. He's built the following little argument and it's got me cornered. (1) Wu-wei is a way of acting where you are not deliberately trying to control how you're doing, or to impress, or even to perform well — you're just responding, and the effort has dropped away. (2) The only reliable way to learn a new way of acting is to practise that way of acting on purpose, over and over, until it sticks. (3) So the only reliable way to learn to act without trying would be to practise acting without trying. (4) But practising acting-without-trying means aiming at not aiming — you'd be watching yourself to see whether you're being spontaneous yet, and that self-watching is exactly the sort of effort wu-wei is meant to leave behind. (5) So any training for wu-wei — rituals, breathing exercises, copying someone who's effortless — is self-defeating, because the training reintroduces the trying it's trying to remove. (6) But people clearly do end up in that state: musicians say that after years of scales they finally stop trying and the playing just plays itself. (7) So either wu-wei is a myth, or premise (2) or (4) has to go.

He says the honest conclusion is that wu-wei is a myth, or at best just a fancy name for being very well practised. I'd like to keep (2) — hard work clearly gets you somewhere — but then I seem stuck rejecting (4), and I don't know how to do that without turning wu-wei back into ordinary effort. Where does this argument actually go wrong, and what would it cost me to give up whichever piece you pick?
