A survey volume on moral and political philosophy gives the following account of the function of rights. Rights are side constraints: they express the idea that a person may not be used simply as an instrument for producing good outcomes, which is why a government may not harvest one healthy person's organs to save five dying patients even when the transplant would yield better overall consequences. Since rights, so understood, function as trumps over social welfare, any account that gives rights a merely instrumental role — protecting welfare, trust, or stability — would have to abandon them whenever protecting them costs more than it buys. The instrumental view therefore cannot explain the special force of rights, and this refutes consequentialism; the concept of rights is needed as a fundamental moral category.
