I've been trying to get clear on Woodward's interventionist theory of causation and I keep tying myself in knots. Here's an argument that the whole account is viciously circular. (1) Roughly, on Woodward's view, X causes Y just in case there is a possible intervention on X with respect to Y that would change Y (with the other variables held fixed). (2) But an intervention on X with respect to Y is itself defined in causal terms: the intervention must be a cause of X, and it must not affect Y except by way of X. (3) So the right-hand side of the definition uses the concept of cause. (4) A definition is viciously circular whenever its definiens uses the very concept being defined. (5) Therefore Woodward's interventionism gives a viciously circular definition of causation. I half-suspect step (2) is misstated or step (4) is too strong, but I can't make the diagnosis precise. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what's the strongest version of the circularity worry that remains?
