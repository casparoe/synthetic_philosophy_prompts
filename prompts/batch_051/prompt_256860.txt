As a journalist who covers cognitive science, I am working on a piece about how researchers describe the timing and location of conscious experience, and I want to make sure I am drawing the right distinction between Daniel Dennett’s multiple drafts model and the Cartesian theater he criticizes. I have been reading the passage in Dennett’s Consciousness Explained where he discusses the color phi experiment: a red spot is briefly flashed, then a green spot is briefly flashed a short distance away, and subjects report seeing a single moving spot that changes color roughly midway. Dennett argues that the brain can produce this edited narrative without there being a fixed moment or place at which the original red spot first appears in consciousness and then gets revised. He compares the philosopher’s temptation to ask whether the revision happened before or after the experience reached consciousness to asking whether a correction to a manuscript was made before or after it was published, when there is no single publication event in the scenario being imagined.

What I would like help with is the comparison that Dennett is pressing in that passage. Specifically, I want to understand whether the multiple drafts model is best presented to readers as an empirical hypothesis about how the brain works, or as a piece of conceptual hygiene about which questions can legitimately be asked at all. How exactly should I explain the difference between the Cartesian theater and the multiple drafts model? And when I explain the two views, what sort of evidence or experimental result would actually distinguish them, rather than merely illustrate them?
