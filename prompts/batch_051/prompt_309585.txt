i'm a novelist, working on a near-future novel where a city-state has set up something called the Office of the Accepted Micromort. The in-universe premise is that they've had it with incoherent risk regulation, so they take a micromort (a one-in-a-million chance of death) seriously as a currency and adopt a single baseline: any activity that exposes a person to no more risk than a year of routine driving counts as acceptable, because the public already accepts driving without complaint. I need a piece of in-universe satire I can quote in the book. Write me the office's deadpan public-consultation paper or internal directive, maybe 600 words, in flat bureaucratic prose, that proposes a handful of specific policies which follow perfectly from that baseline but would make a normal reader squirm. The humor should come from faithfully applying the micromort-as-fungible-currency logic, not from the narrator winking. Land the standard objections without ever stating them: voluntary vs imposed risk, statistical deaths vs one vivid catastrophe, and how the "acceptable" risk of drivers gets exported to pedestrians and kids. Keep it deadpan; by the end a reader should feel the pull of the reductio and be unsure whether the office is insane or just consistent.
