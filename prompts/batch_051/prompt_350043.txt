Me and a coworker keep talking about turning argument into a rated sport — a chess-for-persuasion app. Here's the proposal. Users on an Elo ladder get matched and pick any topic. Before the rounds start, each side writes one sentence saying what would actually change their mind. Then they alternate short replies: 150 words max, four-minute clock. There's no judge. After each exchange, either player can tap 'concession' on the other's last message, and tapping it is worth twice as many rating points as taking the audience vote. The audience votes on who reasoned better, but a vote only counts if it includes a one-sentence justification. At the end, each player grades the opponent's opening sentence honest-or-weasel on a 0–5 scale, and they get a small elo bonus if their grade matches the audience average. Talk me through how this thing actually behaves if it gets popular — who climbs to the top of the ladder, what kinds of topics and argument styles get selected for, and what's the first exploit people find? Then tell me whether the failure modes are fixable, and whether the format would actually make people argue better or just make them get better at seeming reasonable.
