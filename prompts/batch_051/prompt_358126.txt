I’ve been trying to understand what, if anything, a click of an “I agree” button actually does, and the more cases I line up, the less consistent my own judgments seem. Consider Alice, who installs a program and is shown a scrollable window with forty pages of terms and an “I agree” button beneath it. She never scrolls and never reads a line; she clicks. She is later found bound by a clause on page thirty-three requiring private arbitration in a distant city. Now consider Bruno, who signs up for the same service through a page stating that creating an account constitutes agreement to the terms of service, where the terms are available through a nearby link. He opens the account without opening the link. Neither has read anything, and the clause on page thirty-three was equally available to both, yet Bruno is considerably less likely to be held to it. The difference is exactly that Alice performed a gesture called “I agree”—a gesture that did not display, summarize, or call her attention to any actual term.

The asymmetry looks stranger when set beside ordinary life. If a friend hands me a long written proposal and says, in effect, “Just say yes and I’ll send you the details,” and I say yes, no one treats me as bound to surprising terms buried midway through the document. If a club president announces, “By entering this room, you have accepted the new rules,” while the rules are posted on the back of the door, we do not think the act of entering bound anyone to an unannounced fee or penalty. In those settings we deny that someone can agree to what she could not reasonably be taken to have known. But the unread click is treated as agreement to whatever the scrollable box contains, including clauses the clicker would reject if she read them. I would like the strongest available justification for this asymmetry. What exactly must a click communicate to count as agreement, and can any account that counts Alice’s click as agreement also avoid counting the friend’s “yes” or the club member’s entry as agreement? If the asymmetry cannot be justified, which side should give way—should unread clicks be treated as nonbinding, or should we conclude that much ordinary unread assent has been binding all along and revise our judgments in that direction?
