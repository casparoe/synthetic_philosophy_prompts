I keep tripping over the phrase legal fiction being thrown at very different things, so I'd like a working definition rather than just a list of examples. The sorts of cases I want it to handle are these. Clear cases to my mind: adoption, where the law treats a child as if they were born to the adoptive parents; and the old crown rule that the king never dies, so the office carries on as a continuous person even as the individuals die. Clear non-cases: a witness lying under oath, a judge reaching for a metaphor to dress up an opinion, and a rule of evidence that merely presumes a fact until the other side rebuts it. The ones I'm genuinely unsure about: a tax provision saying your deemed a resident if you spend 183 days in the country; the reasonable person in negligence, who nobody thinks actually exists; a constructive trust, where a court declares someone holds property on trust though no trust was ever formally created; and suing a ship itself in rem rather than its owner, which treats the vessel almost like a defendant. Please propose a definition, run it against every case I've listed, say where it gets the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Please write your answer in British English.
