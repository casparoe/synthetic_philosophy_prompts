ok so ive been trying to pin down what bothers me about hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity — the idea that you take an existing concept, locate the dimensions it varies along (size, tempo, number of legs, degree of abstraction) and turn those knobs, and thats how you get variations on a theme. i think that captures variation but misses the actual creative move. so i want to distinguish knob-twiddling from what i call knob-minting. knob-twiddling is changing the value of a paramater people already treat as a parameter; knob-minting is converting something that used to be a fixed background fact into a parameter in the first place — taking a dial that was welded at one setting and making it turnable. before body plan got minted as a knob, a horse-man was just a contradiction, not a centaur. before perspective became a knob for painters, flatness wasn't a stylistic option, just the default. going from real to complex numbers is minting a knob along the lines of "what if square roots of negatives count as numbers." the claim is that genuine creativity is knob-minting, and knob-twiddling is just the cleanup work after someones already done the creative part.

i know this might just be hofstadter again or something obvious in the literature. is knob-minting a real distinction or does it collapse into twiddling a higher-level knob (the "which features even count as parameters" knob)? has someone already said this under a better name? and where does it break — give me the strongest case of a creative act that isnt knob-minting, or a case of knob-minting that produces nothing creative. please write your whole answer in American English.
