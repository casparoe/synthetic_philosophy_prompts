I work as the data person for a mid-sized school district, and every spring I have to evaluate whatever math curriculum we piloted that year and recommend whether to keep it or switch back. This year we piloted an inquiry-based curriculum in 38 schools; the rest stayed with the old direct-instruction program. On the state test, the inquiry kids scored a couple points lower on average than the direct-instruction kids, so my first instinct was to say the pilot failed. But then I broke the results down by whether the student was below grade level at the start of the year, and in both subgroups the inquiry kids did better than the direct-instruction kids — in the below-grade-level group by a decent margin, and in the on-grade-level group by a smaller one. I know this is some kind of Simpson's-paradox situation (the below-grade-level schools were more likely to volunteer for the pilot, and their scores are lower for reasons that have nothing to do with the curriculum), but knowing the name doesn't tell me what to actually do.

I need a procedure I can follow, not just this year but every year, because this pattern keeps coming up with our reading program too. The constraints: I have about three weeks lead time, the decision affects a few thousand kids, the superintendent wants a single headline recommendation, some board members will role their eyes if I start talking about "adjusting for prior achievement" because they think subgroup analysis is cherry-picking, and the state now makes us publish results broken out by prior-achievement subgroup either way. So what I'm after is a step-by-step decision procedure: what checks do I run and in what order before I conclude that the overall number is misleading, how do I decide which variables are the right ones to split on versus ones I shouldn't, when should I report just the aggregate and when should I recommend on the basis of the subgroups, and what exactly do I put on the slide for a board that wants one number? Give me a worked example or two, including at least one where the procedure tells me to recommend the program I don't personally like, and flag the places where no procedure can make the call for me.
