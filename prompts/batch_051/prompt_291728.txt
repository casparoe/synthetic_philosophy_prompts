I work on a team that builds models to infer what users value from traces of their behavior—the choices they make, the options they ignore, the time they spend—and one feature of this work has come to feel philosophically familiar in a way I cannot quite settle. Inverse reinforcement learning proceeds from the assumption that an agent acts, at least approximately, so as to maximize some hidden reward function. Behavior is observed, the environment's dynamics are taken as known, and the reward function is inferred by working backward from behavior through the hypothesis of optimality. On its own, the behavior underdetermines the reward: indefinitely many reward functions would make the same behavior optimal, so one adds further constraints—simplicity of the reward, a maximum-entropy policy, a fixed set of features—to make the problem well posed.

That structure looks to me like the structure Donald Davidson describes under the name radical interpretation. A radical interpreter observes a speaker's behavior, including her utterances, while her beliefs, desires, and meanings remain hidden. The interpreter attributes beliefs and desires under a normative constraint, the principle of charity: roughly, interpret her so that she comes out mostly rational and mostly right about her observable surroundings. Only through that constraint does behavior become evidence for any specific attribution. Here too, behavior by itself underdetermines the interpretation, and the constraints that make the problem tractable look methodological rather than empirical.

The mapping I have in mind is: observed trajectories correspond to observed behavior; the unknown reward function corresponds to the agent's desires or values; the known transition dynamics correspond to the shared public environment that fixes which beliefs would be rational; the optimality assumption corresponds to the principle of charity; and the regularizers used in inverse reinforcement learning correspond to the holistic constraints Davidson places on interpretation.
