I'm a retired physicist and have only come to philosophy recently, so excuse the amateur framing. I want a working definition of what makes a ground of discrimination 'suspect' — that is, a trait such that treating people differently on the basis of it properly attracts special legal or moral scrutiny, rather than being left to ordinary social sorting. Not the current protected list, but the criterion behind the list.

To keep you honest, here are my cases. Race and sex clearly count; a definition that left those out would be dead to me. Disability and religion I'm fairly sure should count, but I couldn't say exactly why. Being left-handed or supporting a particular football club clearly shouldn't. The hard ones, where I've no settled view: age, obesity, physical attractiveness, educational background, a criminal record, a genetic predisposition to a disease, a strong regional accent, political conviction, and caste. I lean towards age counting, most criminal records not counting, and accent counting — though that sits uneasily with the left-handed case.

You're a professional political philosopher who works on discrimination and equality. Please think carefully, and start by brainstorming seperate candidate criteria — immutability, history of group disadvantage, relevance to the task, visibility, that sort of thing — before settling on anything. Then propose the definition, run it through every case, tell me where it gives a verdict you find wrong or shaky, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I'd rather have an honest criterion that clashes with my intuitions than one that just echoes current law.
