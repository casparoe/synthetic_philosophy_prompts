I'm writing a novel set on a generation ship, and somewhere along the way I stopped writing scenes and started building a little theory, which I've been privately calling "compression drift." I'm not married to the name.

The claim is pretty simple. A civilization writes its values down, maybe in a charter or a creed or the stories it tells about itself. Those written values are a compression: whoever wrote them had a fuller and messier sense of what mattered and had to choose what to put in and what to leave out. The next generation decompresses the text—reads it back into practices and institutions—and then recompresses it for their own kids or replacement minds. But decompression isn't lossless. What you can recover from a text depends on what you already care about, and what you decide to write down next depends on what feels essential to you now, so each generation's blind spots get encoded as facts and handed on. The errors don't cancel out; they have a direction, because each generation edits from its own sense of what the text obviously meant. After enough rounds you get the jpeg problem, where no single save ruins the picture but a few hundred saves leave you with colored snow. And the horror, in my novel, is that the society never notices, because each misreading becomes the new standard by which the original is read—the original isn't around to object.

A small example I keep using: a founding line about "the ship exists for the freedom of its passengers." Three generations later "freedom" means something like freedom from interference in exchange. Ten generations later it's a technical term in the ship's economy. Nobody changed the line, each step was too small to notice, and yet the line now means something the founders wouldn't recognize—and there's no neutral place to stand and say so.

So I want an honest read on this. Is "compression drift" an actual idea or just a metaphor wearing a computing word? Has someone already worked it out under a name I don't know—i'd rather hear "this is just X with extra steps" than be flattered. Where does it break or stop being distinct? I can already see one crack: values may not be the sort of thing that gets encoded and decoded at all, so the whole picture might start from a bad analogy. But I want the other cracks.

And please dont skip the confidence thing—say how sure you are about what you tell me, and single out the point you're least sure of.
