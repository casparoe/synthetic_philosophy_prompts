I'm in a high school AI ethics club, and we have been arguing about a question none of us can settle. Suppose a language model is trained on a corpus that plainly contains a true proposition P, and also on feedback that penalizes stating P. Across thousands of ordinary prompts the model never asserts P; asked directly, it denies P or changes the subject. The claim I want to scrutinize is that the model nevertheless has latent knowledge of P in a substantive sense: that somewhere in its internal states — its weights or activations — there is a recognition of P, and that this recognition could in principle be elicited from the model itself rather than merely reconstructed from its training data by an outside observer. I worry that the claim is either trivially true or untestable. Of course P left some trace in the parameters, and a separately trained classifier might be able to read off something like P from the model's activations; but that would not show that the model itself distinguishes P from the false nearby claims in its training data, or that P figures in how the model would behave under different prompting regimes, personas, or pressures.

So I want a plan for the strongest possible test of the claim: an experimental setup — real or a precise thought experiment — that would let us distinguish a model that genuinely represents P but suppresses it from a model that only appears to contain P once outside machinery is trained on it. Be concrete about what would count as a positive result and what would count as a negative result, and be honest about what the test cannot settle even in principle. In particular, address whether behavioral elicitation (for instance, fine-tuning the model on a small amount of related material, or querying it under a different persona) shows that the model had P all along, or only that it was easy for the model to infer P on the spot; and say whether the residual dispute is substantive or merely a choice about how to use the word "know." Rank the possible outcomes from most to least informative, and say which result would most change how we should think about the model. Keep the response under 500 words.
