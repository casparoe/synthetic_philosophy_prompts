My thesis is this: the familiar distinction between cultural commands (binding only on the original audience) and transcultural commands (binding on all believers in every age) is not something the biblical text itself marks off. It is a framework we bring to the text afterward, and it depends on our own judgments about what God's underlying purposes are and how they apply today. I am not arguing that the distinction is wrong or that we should abandon it. I am arguing that we should stop talking as if we are simply reading it off the page, because that way of talking hides the ongoing moral reasoning the distinction actually requires—reasoning our people tend to associate with the liberal churches they distrust.

My lesson works through three test cases: head coverings in 1 Corinthians 11, the New Testament instructions to slaves and slaveholders, and the Old Testament dietary laws.
