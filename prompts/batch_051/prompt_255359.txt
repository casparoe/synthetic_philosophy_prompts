Take the dispute over Confederate monuments in the United States, from the 2017 Charlottesville protests through the wave of removals that followed, as a case study for Nietzsche's distinction among monumental, antiquarian, and critical history in his essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life. I know the politics and the legal fights; what I want is the historiography. Classify the main positions in the debate — preservation in place, adding contextualizing text, relocation to museums or cemeteries, removal and destruction — under the three modes, and say where a single position draws on more than one mode. Then run the analysis in the other direction: which features of the episode does Nietzsche's scheme explain well, and which does it miss or misdescribe, such as the role of racial domination, civic identity, or the fact that many of these monuments were mass-produced and erected long after the war? Finally, evaluate his claim that a healthy culture needs all three modes in some proportion: does the monument controversy show that balance is achievable, or that the modes are not commensurable and one of them must simply win?
