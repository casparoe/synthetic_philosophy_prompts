I'm taking a philosophy of psychiatry course and we've been on Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects. As I understand it, the claim is that classifying people isn't a neutral act of sorting a fixed population: once a category like ADHD or borderline personality disorder is in wide circulation, people get diagnosed, come to understand themselves through the category, adjust how they behave, and then the category itself starts to change in response — symptoms get re-described, diagnostic criteria get revised, support communities reshape what the condition means. So the classification chases the classified and vice versa, and that's supposed to be why kinds of people are unstable in a way that kinds of chemicals are not.

Separately, at my job we've been instrumenting everything — ticket resolution time, test coverage, feature adoption — and our manager keeps saying that the moment a metric becomes a target it stops being a good metric, which I now realize is basically Goodhart's law: people game it or optimize the number at the exspense of whatever it was supposed to track, so the measure drifts away from the thing it was meant to represent.

It struck me that those are the same shape. In both cases a representation — a psychiatric label, a performance metric — feeds back into the thing it represents, and the feedback either ruins the representation's validity or forces it to keep revising itself. Is this a real structural identity or just a loose analogy between two things that both happen to involve feedback loops? If it's genuine, what transfers? For instance, would Hacking's point that looping human categories lack stable essences imply that no metric can be a perfectly neutral yardstick, or do the usual remedies for Goodhart's law (rotate the metrics, use several uncorrelated ones, keep the true objective underspecified) have an analogue in psychiatric classification? And where does the parallel break — what can a psychiatric category do that a performance metric can't, or vica versa?
