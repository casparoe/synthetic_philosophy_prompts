I've been going back through the Rousseau/d'Alembert fight over whether Geneva should get a theater, and it's one of those arguments where nobody seems to move even though they're looking at the same basic facts about what plays do. Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert on the Theatre (1758) is answering the Encyclopédie article where d'Alembert says Geneva's real problem is that it lacks a theater. My suspicion is that the deadlock comes from a mismatch: d'Alembert is mostly asking whether theater can be a vehicle for enlightenment and improved taste, while Rousseau is asking whether it will rearrange people's civic attachments, work rhythms, and everyday pleasures, almost independent of what any particular play says. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a hidden factual disagreement between them about, say, how audiences actually respond to Moliere? And if the disagreement is mostly about standards or starting intuitions, what could have broken the deadlock, or was it never the kind of question where new evidence would help?
