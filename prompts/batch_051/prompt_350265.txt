I've been asked to circulate, ahead of a small epistemology workshop, a one-page statement of where I actually stand on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia; the group will then check whether the position hangs together as a package. Here is the statement.

1. Epistemic akrasia is never rational. By that I mean it cannot be rational to hold an attitude toward a proposition while also believing that one's total evidence does not support that attitude: not to believe p while believing that one's evidence makes p unlikely, and not to have a credence of 0.7 while believing that one's evidence warrants only 0.4.

2. Higher-order evidence about my own cognitive condition should be reflected in my attitudes. If I learn that my reasoning about some matter was produced under conditions I consider unreliable — fatigue, intoxication, time pressure, or a known bias — I should lower my confidence in the output of that reasoning, even when I cannot locate any specific mistake in it from the inside.

3. I am a moderate conciliationist about peer disagreement. When someone I regard as an epistemic peer disagrees with me about p, I should move substantially in their direction, and in many cases I should suspend judgment rather than hold fast.

4. I do not think a belief is made irrational merely by having been causally influenced by a bias. If my evidence supports the belief and I have no undefeated reason to suspect that influence, the causal role of the bias does not by itself make the belief irrational.

5. I am suspicious of level-splitting. I do not want to dissolve problems in this area by distinguishing two kinds of rationality — for instance, a structural rationality that permits a state and a substantive rationality that condemns it — and then saying the agent is rational in one sense but not the other. I want a single notion of rationality to do the work.

Please audit this set. Go through the commitments pairwise and identify which are in tension and which are only apparently in tension. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would be, including what that repair costs elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision of one commitment would do the most to make the whole set hang together, and what residual strain would remain.
