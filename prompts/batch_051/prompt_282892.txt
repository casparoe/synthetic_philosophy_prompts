I am taking an upper-level course on akrasia and intertemporal choice, and I keep returning to a counterfactual I would like to reason through carefully. Suppose that starting tomorrow a cheap, side-effect-free intervention becomes universally available and is adopted nearly everywhere. The intervention changes exactly one feature of human motivation: each person's discounting of future outcomes becomes exponential rather than hyperbolic. That is, for any outcome, the rate at which a person trades off its value at time t against its value at time t+1 no longer depends on how near t is. Memory, reasoning, the intensity of immediate appetites, and the capacity to foresee consequences are unchanged. Trace the effects over the next half-century, reasoning through mechanisms rather than telling a story. In particular: Does ordinary weakness of will—resolving to rise early and then hitting snooze—disappear, or does it persist through some other route, such as appetite overwhelming judgment or tastes that change with time or context? What happens to commitment devices and precommitment: Ulysses contracts, automatic deductions, alarm clocks across the room, gym memberships? Would the distinction between sophisticated and naive agents, who do or do not anticipate their own future preference reversals, still cut at anything? What happens to addiction and relapse, to procrastination on fixed deadlines, and to industries and policies that currently rely on present bias? And after working through the consequences, address the philosophical question: would the problem of akrasia be solved, or merely relocated? For each link in your prediction, flag whether the result is robust or a guess, and state what would have to hold for it to be wrong. Finally, say which of the predicted consequences distinguish exponential discounting from merely having a much lower discount rate.
