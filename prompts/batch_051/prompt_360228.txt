I just joined the triage committee at my hospital, which is rewriting its policy for when there are more patients than ICU beds. Half the committee wants the policy to divert resources toward saving the greater number of people, full stop; the other half, including the palliative care doc I eat lunch with, keeps saying the numbers shouldn't count — that we're not obligated to let the one die for the five, and that a policy built on head counts is exactly the kind of fairness we should be trying to avoid. Weeks in, we're still talking past each other. Write the adversarial-collaboration document that should be on our table: a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, a disagreements table with the remaining difference worded so each side would accept it's own column, and the cruxes — I'd guess one is whether saving more lives and treating people fairly are the same kind of reason or two kinds — plus, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded. If any part of this looks like a purely verbal dispute, flag that too. The thing has to be something either camp could actually sign its name to.
