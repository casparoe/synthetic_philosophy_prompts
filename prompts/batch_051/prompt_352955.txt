I am writing because of a situation that has recently become impossible to ignore. While enrolling my father in a long-term care facility, I was handed a tablet at the admissions desk and guided through a series of screens. The final screen displayed several dozen pages of terms and a single button reading Agree. The facility later maintained that by pressing that button I had knowingly and voluntarily accepted every term. I had read almost none of them; no one invited me to negotiate; and it had been made clear that the placement could not proceed unless I pressed the button.

Since then I have noticed the same structure in unrelated settings: an airline check-in that makes a boarding pass depend on accepting terms shown only as a link; a credit card issuer that mails amended terms and states that keeping the card counts as accepting them; a software update that refuses to install until a box is ticked. In each case, one side has drafted every term in advance, the other side needs the underlying good or service immediately, and the law or the institution then treats the result as a first-person act of agreement, as though the parties had sat down together and negotiated each term.

I am not asking whether any particular term is unfair, or whether the terms should be enforced. I am asking about the practice itself: treating an unread, non-negotiated, take-it-or-leave-it set of terms as an individual's agreement.

Is there an established name for exactly this configuration? If so, what does it cover and what does it leave out? If nothing fits exactly, what are the nearest concepts, how does what I describe differ from each of them, and where are the boundaries—for example, between a click on a screen, a signature collected at a counter under time pressure, an amendment that counts continued use as acceptance, and a genuinely negotiated deal? A bare name would not answer my question; I am trying to understand how the phenomenon should be carved up, and whether a click and a signed paper form are the same thing or only cousins.
