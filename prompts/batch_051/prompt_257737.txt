ok so I've been going way too deep on continuity of government and designated survivor scenarios, and I wrote down my actual positions so someone could poke holes in the set. Assume a standard U.S.-style mass decapitation case: a cabinet secretary survives because they were deliberately kept away from the event, and everyone ahead of them in the line of succession is dead or unaccounted for.

Here's where I've landed:

1 The survivor becomes the legitimate president the moment they take the oath, even if we can't confirm their exact place in the statutory order of succession. The order of succession is a coordination device, not a sacred ranking; after a strike, somebody has to be able to issue orders everyone else will obey. Whether the Speaker was technically alive for another hour shouldn't make a day's worth of emergency decisions illegitimate.

2 Their first job is restoring democratic authorization: getting whatever survives of the elected branches, or emergency replacements, into a position of real control, fast. The state can remain an administrative machine without democracy, but the continued existence of the government isn't worth much if it's just a bunker committee with a flag.

3 They should execute the pre-existing retaliation and continuity plans, up to and including nuclear release, without re-litigating them on the spot. The public already authorized those plans under calm conditions, and re-litigating while shocked, grieving, and personally implicated is exactly when decision quality is worst.

4 I also think standing authorizations for lethal force go stale. If the facts the original decision-maker assumed have materially changed, then executing a pre-delegated order is morally the same as making a new lethal decision with nobody accountable for it.

5 The secrecy and pre-selection of the survivor are fine. Publishing the plans would just let an adversary tune the attack. But afterwards the survivor owes a complete on-the-record account of what they did and why. Continuity of government can't become a permanent get-out-of-accountability card.

6 In practice, trying to hold real elections or seat a rump Congress during the acute emergency is probably worse than waiting. Mass displacement and martial conditions would make whatever came out of it unrepresentative, so the survivor may end up governing without fresh democratic authorization for as long as the disruption lasts. That is bad, but the alternative is a pseudo-legitimate rubber stamp.
