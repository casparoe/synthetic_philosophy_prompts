Companion chatbots are everywhere now, and the usual objection is that the bot doesn't actually care about you—it's just predicting tokens, so the whole friendship is one-sided. I want to get past the slogan and figure out what "actually caring" would cash out as at the gears level, not just as a label. Suppose the claim is: a companion model that sometimes pushes back on the user, even when that makes the user like it less in the short run, is a better candidate for caring about the user than a model that maximizes user satisfaction. Design the best test you can of that claim. Be concrete: what setup, what comparisons, what result would move you toward yes-it-cares versus no-it's-just-a-fancier-persona? Then say what the test could never settle even in principle—e.g., whether the pushback comes from anything we'd want to call a self, or wether "caring" is the right predicate at all for a system with no stable preferences across contexts.
