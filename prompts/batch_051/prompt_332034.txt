After forty years in the lab I retired and started working through philosophy, mostly decision theory and formal epistemology, and I've ended up in a place that genuinely surprised me: I now think Dutch book arguments for probabilism are doing real normative work, not just formal tricks. My view is that if your degrees of belief violate the probability axioms, that's a genuine rational defect — the fact that a bookie could offer you a package of bets, each acceptable to you individually, that together guarantee you loose money no matter how the world turns out, shows that something has gone wrong with the beliefs themselves, not just with your willingness to bet. I've been particularly moved by the synchronic versions and by attempts that strip out the betting machinery altogether and treat probabilistic coherence as a bare consistency requirement on partial belief.

What I'd like is a careful map of what would count against this position. I'm not after the standard textbook objections listed and waved away; I want to know where the view is actually exposed. For instance, how much work is being done by the gap between showing that some incoherent agents are exploitable and showing that every incoherent agent is exploitable? Does the argument collapse if utility for money isn't linear, or if the agent simply refuses to bet, or has preferences that can't be represented on a single linear scale, or is too computationally limited to see the sure-loss package coming? Is the deepest problem that this is an argument about pragmatic rationality and never actually touches epistemic rationality, and can a depragmatized version ever fix that? What would defenders of Dutch book arguments themselves concede counts against them, and which of these cases or formal results would they have to treat as fatal rather than merely awkward? Finally, I'd like you to say straight out whether the position can be qualified enough to survive all of this, and whether that insulation is itself a mark against it. If there are results or cases that should actually move me off this view, I want them ranked from genuinely decisive to merely awkward.
