I've been working through the philosophy of love and keep finding myself pinned between a theory I admire and an intuition I can't shake. The theory is a bestowal view in something like Frankfurt's spirit: love does not detect value that is already there in the beloved; it creates or confers it, and the lover is subject to a volitional necessity rather than a comparative judgment. On this picture, love's refusal to shop for a better or more deserving beloved is not a failure of rational assessment but its point: love is not in the business of ranking candidates, and it can persist through a decline in the beloved's merits without thereby becoming irrational.

But I also have a firm intuition that love is answerable to its object. When we criticize someone's love, we cite facts about the beloved — the cruelty, the callousness, the self-absorption — as if love can be partly mistaken, not merely hard to live with. This is the pull of appraisal and reasons-based pictures: Velleman's thought that love answers to the beloved's value as a person, or Kolodny's thought that it answers to a shared history and relationship. Yet if love is answerable to such facts, I don't understand why it shouldn't rank by them — and the idea of ranking beloveds, or trading up when someone more eligible comes along, feels like a betrayal of what love is.

I want a negotiated verdict, not just a winner. Specifically: can the bestowal view be amended so that love creates value while still leaving room for the lover to be answerable to how the beloved is — or does that concession already collapse it into an appraisal view? And can Velleman- or Kolodny-style views explain why love is noncomparative and resistant to trade-ups without quietly borrowing the bestowal idea? Finally, should I treat the intuition that love must track the beloved's properties as a datum to preserve, or as the very piece of common sense that a bestowal theory is entitled to debunk? Tell me what justifies each move, and whether there is a reading on which both positions are partly right.
