My philosophy of responsibility seminar has spent the term on whether particular agents are appropriate targets of the reactive attitudes in roughly Strawson's sense — resentment, indignation, and the practices of holding responsible that go with them. The instructor has given this series of verdicts, mostly without stating a general criterion:

- A salesperson who knowingly sells worthless insurance policies to elderly customers: an appropriate target of blame.
- A man who strikes a nurse during an epileptic seizure: not an appropriate target.
- A woman who was drugged without her knowledge at a party and steals from a shop while disoriented: not an appropriate target.
- A driver who drinks heavily at lunch, knows he will have to drive home, and then kills a pedestrian: blameworthy, on account of the earlier decision to drink.
- A man raised from early childhood in an isolated, violent cult, who commits an assault while still inside the cult and sincerely believing the victim posed an immediate threat to his community: she has called him not a straightforward target of blame, and the seminar remains stalled over why.

Next week we turn to a 35-year-old with severe antisocial personality disorder who has defrauded several elderly people of their savings over a period of years. He is not delusional and is fully aware that his victims suffer; he simply regards their suffering as irrelevant to him. He was not coerced, and he planned the frauds carefully to avoid detection. What should we predict the instructor will say? In particular: which principle is she implicitly running, which other principles fit her record of verdicts at least as well, what does each principle predict for the new case, what is your best guess about her verdict and why, and which further case would best settle which principle she holds?

Please structure your answer in this order: Candidate principles (roughly 250 words); What each principle predicts for the new case (roughly 200 words); Best guess and rationale (roughly 250 words); The case that would settle it (roughly 150 words).
