Design a thought experiment for a workshop exercise in anthropic reasoning: the case should be presentable in fifteen minutes, and its verdict should not depend on fine-tuned priors or on a gerrymandered reference class.

The case must make the Self-Sampling Assumption (roughly, reason as if you were a random sample from all existing observers) and the Self-Indication Assumption (roughly, your existence favors hypotheses on which more observers exist) recommend opposite actions in a single practical decision. The SIA recommendation should have the presumptuous-philosopher structure: on no ordinary evidence, SIA makes the agent confident in a large unobserved human population and willing to act on that confidence, while SSA resists the inference. Constraints: Earth-bound and human-scale, of the kind a real relief agency or public-health authority could face; no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, and no doomsday arguments. After presenting the scenario, state the competing hypotheses and their priors, show what each theory recommends and why, identify the structural feature that produces the disagreement and what would happen if it were removed, and finish with the strongest reply a defender of SIA could give to the charge of presumptuousness.
