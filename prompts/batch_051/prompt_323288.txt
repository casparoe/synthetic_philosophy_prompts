Write a dialogue between two philosophers about whether the rigidity of the digger wasp tells us anything deep about human free will. For background: a digger wasp will drag a paralyzed cricket to the mouth of its burrow, leave it there, go inside to inspect, come back out, and then drag the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while the wasp's inside, the wasp drags it back to the same spot, goes inside to inspect again, and repeats; you can loop it indefinitely and it never short-circuits the routine. One character thinks this kind of "sphexish" rigidity gives us the right contrast class for freedom: what matters is being flexible and responsive to reasons, not whether your behavior was indeterministic, and humans mostly have that flexibility. The other thinks the wasp is a warning. A system can have more and more conditionals and still just mechanically grind through them; adding flexibility is a difference of degree, not the kind of could-have-done-otherwise that responsibility needs. Make the two sides about equally good, and have them actually answer each others strongest moves instead of taking turns monologuing. After the dialogue, give a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: `interlocutors` (brief sketch of each character and their core commitment), `true_disagreement` (the single proposition they'd both identify as the crux), `strongest_move` (an array, one entry per character, giving their best argument in the exchange), `near_agreement` (the point where they come closest to agreeing), and `assessment` (which side looks more defensible by the end, or whether it's a draw, with one sentence of reasoning).
