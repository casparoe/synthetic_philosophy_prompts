I'm on the committee of a small mountaineering club, and we're stuck on a disciplinary question that's starting to feel less like procedure and more like real philosophy. The relevant bylaw, drafted in 1982, reads: "No member shall knowingly speak falsely to another member or to the committee on any matter touching the safety or organization of a club outing." Last month, on a trip sign-up form that asked "Have you ever been banned from a climbing area by a landowner or ranger?", one member, Mira, wrote: "I have never been formally ejected from a crag by a ranger in the act of a rule violation." That's literally true, we think—she was banned in 2021, but the ban came in a letter from a national park office after they reviewed photos; no ranger caught her mid-violation. The form had a follow-up box for details and she left it blank. Alot of us are convinced she deliberately left the impression she'd never been banned. The bylaw's drafters were thinking about people saying things out loud at meetings; they didn't anticipate online forms where the question gets answered with something technically true and the rest is done by omission. So: has she violated the clause? Argue it from the plain words, from the purpose of the clause, from what the drafters would have said if asked, and from what a reasonable reader would take her answer to mean; then tell me which approach should win and why. And I actually want your view on the deeper line: does the bylaw's wording—against speaking falsely—mark a morally relevant difference between lying and misleading, or is Mira just saying "no" in a weaselly way? Also tell me what would change your mind: which argument or added fact would most likely flip your verdict, and where your reasoning feels shakiest.
