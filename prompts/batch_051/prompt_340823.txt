My friend has disturbingly consistant views about when a life of new experiences is actually better and when it's just a busy way of running from boredom. I've watched her judge these cases:

- A woman does a "year of firsts": one new activity every month, taken seriously for the month, then dropped, none of them kept up afterward. She says the year felt like adding a room to the house of her life. My friend says her well-being genuinely went up.
- A guy changes mobile games every three days for a year. He can't describe any of them afterward and says it was mostly something to do with his hands. My friend says no — that's not well-being, just filling an empty evening.

- A woman keeps a "life library": one new outing a month, planned in advance, each written up in a journal, and she stays in regular touch with someone she met through the mountaineering club she tried as one of the outings. My friend says yes, clearly.
- A man fills the hole by buying one new gadget or outfit every week. It gives him a short hit, then he feels flat and a little embarrassed by the pile. My friend says no — she thinks he's worse off.
- A woman saves up every two years for a genuinely unfamiliar trip to a new country, learns a bit of the language, often goes alone, and writes long essays to herself afterward. My friend says yes.
