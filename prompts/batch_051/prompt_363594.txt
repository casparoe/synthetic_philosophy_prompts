I've been trying to settle what I actually think about panpsychism, so I stopped reading and just wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases. Theyre a mess, and I dont know if theres a single principle underneath them or whether I need to abandon a few.

Here are the cases and my verdicts:

- A lone electron: I don't think it's conscious, but when panpsychists describe it as having a faint something-or-other, I don't find that crazy.
- A single neuron: no experience, not even a faint buzz.
- A normal adult human cortex: obviously experience.
- A nation of a hundred million people: no group-level experience. The people have experiences; the nation doesn't.
- Two people holding hands: definitely no new subject flickers into existence across their two experiences.
- A split-brain patient after the corpus callosum is cut: I keep wanting to say there are two streams of experience in there, not one, and not zero.
- An early fetus, before the cortex wires up: no experience, I think, but I'm shakier on this than on the neuron.
- A hypothetical machine built from a billion tiny chips, each running something I'd privately describe as a proto-feel: I lean no machine-level experience, but I worry that's just bias against silicon.

I know this is basically the combination problem, but I don't want the standard survey of positions. Say exactly where the tension is between the neuron verdict and the cortex verdict, and between the nation verdict and the brain verdict. If no clean principle saves all my verdicts, tell me which ones I should overturn and why. Then apply your favored principle to cases I didn't list: conjoined twins sharing a brain structure, a roomful of people with a perfect live neural link, and a normal wakeful cat.
