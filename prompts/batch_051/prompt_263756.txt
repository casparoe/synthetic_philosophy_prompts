My understanding of the two-player case is that once you allow strategies to be strictly dominated by mixed strategies, iterated elimination lands exactly on the rationalizable set, and the order of deletion does not matter. I've carried around the impression that this clean result is special to two-player games in two respects. First, I associate allowing mixed-strategy dominance with order-dependence: the way weakly dominated strategies can be removed in different orders to leave different residues, I've assumed the same can happen once dominators may be mixed, at least with three or more players. Second, I've assumed the survivor set can diverge from rationalizability in multiplayer games because elimination against all opponent strategy profiles is effectively allowing correlated beliefs about opponents, whereas rationalizability uses independent beliefs. But when I try to build a three-player example, I can't produce an order of elimination that leaves a different set, and I'm also unsure about the direction of the supposed divergence: is the worry that a rationalizable strategy can be eliminated, or that a strategy can survive elimination without being rationalizable? I suspect I've overgeneralized a clean two-player result in one direction or the other. Could you sort out which of these impressions actually follows from the definitions, and what the exact relationship is between the iterated-elimination survivor set and rationalizability in three-player games?
