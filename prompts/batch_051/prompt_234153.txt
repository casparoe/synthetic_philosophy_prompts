Something I've noticed talking to friends: everyone pretty much accepts that loosing $20 stings more than finding $20 feels good — we file that under loss aversion and treat it as a sensible preference. But the same friends will roll there eyes at the endowment effect, where someone who was just handed a mug suddenly values it above what they'd have paid for it an hour ago. They call that a bias, a status quo thing, irrational. So we've got two cases where merely having something makes it matter more, but one gets treated as a legitimate preference and the other as a mistake. Is there a defensible difference between them, or do loss aversion and the endowment effect stand or fall together? If you defend treating them differently, you've got to answer the obvious move that the endowment effect is just loss aversion applied to a mug.
