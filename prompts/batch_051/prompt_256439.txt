I have to write something funny for the club's zine, and I want it to actually be smart about the historiography, not just make fun of history nerds.

There's a passage in George Kennan's Long Telegram (February 22, 1946) where he argues, near the beginning of Part 2, that the Soviet leadership's hostile view of the outside world comes less from Western actions than from a traditional Russian sense of insecurity, and that the premises of the official Soviet worldview are mostly false but still govern the people in power. I keep coming back to it because it's a perfect piece of primary-source evidence for the question, and also a perfect example of how the question can be used to beg the answer.

Can you write me a parody of a probable-cause hearing or incident investigation report for the Cold War, around 800 words, deadpan, in which an investigator is trying to determine who started it, and keeps having to incorporate witness statements from a traditionalist, a revisionist, and a post-revisionist? Use the Kennan passage as one of the exhibits, paraphrased rather than quoted. The jokes should come from actual historiographic claims—Soviet security fears, American atomic diplomacy, the Atlantic alliance, Eastern Europe—so a cold war historian laughs rather than winces. Don't let the piece resolve the question; the satire should land on why the question itself is too simple. End with a one-sentence summary on its own line, starting 'Summary:'. And honestly it's fine if it has a typo-level informality; I'm going to edit it into the zine format myself.
