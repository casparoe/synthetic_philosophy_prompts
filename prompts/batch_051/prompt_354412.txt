A friend of mine refuses to plan anything in advance—trips, work projects, even her week—and she always says planning is overrated because the world is going to surprise you anyway. I tried to steelman her one evening and ended up with an argument that feels way too clean, and now I genuinely can't tell whether it's good or whether there's a trick hidden somewhere in it.

Here's the argument, numbered:

(1) A detailed plan is just a fixed course of action settled in advance of the situation it's meant for.

(2) For any particular situation you might actually end up in, there will be some course of action that fits that situation better than the fixed one the plan prescribes, since the plan was made before all the specifics of that exact situation could be known.

(3) An improviser, unlike a planner, chooses in light of the situation as it actually is.

(4) So, situation by situation, improvisation does at least as well as the plan in every case, and better in at least some cases.

(5) If one strategy does at least as well as another in every possible case and better in some, it dominates the other; you'd be irrational not to prefer it.

(6) Therefore improvisation dominates detailed planning, and the rational amount of planning is basically none.

I half suspect the problem is in how (2), (3), and (4) hang together, or maybe (5) is doing something illicit, but I cant make the objection precise. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, on what reading of it, and why exactly? And if it is valid, then am I just resisting an unwelcome conclusion? Also, if you think the whole setup is badly posed or rigged, say that plainly instead of playing along.
