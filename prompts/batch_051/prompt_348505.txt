In Plato’s Republic VII, Socrates distinguishes sense experiences that simply report how things appear from those that force the soul to move beyond perception because the same thing presents opposite qualities at once — his example being a finger that appears both large and small depending on what it is measured against. He treats this conflict as a summons to calculation and eventually to thought about Forms. Give your own example of an ordinary visual experience, not involving fingers or comparisons of size, that would seem to meet Socrates’ criterion for summoning the intellect. Then explain exactly what intellectual operation your example summons in the first instance — counting, comparison with an unseen standard, or something less clean than either — and use it to test whether this criterion actually moves the prisoner toward the Forms, or whether it could just as easily produce a kind of cognitive irritation that goes nowhere beyond the cave wall.
