I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am drafting a column around a legal counterfactual that I would like you to think through with me.

Suppose that a binding international agreement enters into force at the start of 2030 and replaces the familiar rule that online speech is governed primarily by the law of the country where the speaker or the platform is established. Under the new rule, any piece of content—a post, article, video, or comment—that is accessible within a country is subject to that country's speech laws, including its defamation, hate speech, and national security rules, and the country may enforce those laws against both the platform and, at least in principle, the individual speaker. Assume that the major platforms comply with geoblocking and takedown orders rather than abandoning whole markets, that the capacity of states to enforce against individual users is highly uneven, and that several governments immediately begin ordering the removal of content that was not aimed at their citizens but is merely accessible from their territory.

Over the following decade, predict what happens to the public internet. I want a mechanism-based argument rather than a single verdict: which kinds of speech contract or migrate to closed or encrypted channels, how platforms and advertisers change their architecture and moderation, how authoritarian and small states exploit the rule, and whether the regime proves stable or unravels. For each link in your prediction, state how confident you are, which assumptions about enforcement, state interest, or platform cost it depends on, and what would have to hold differently for that prediction to be wrong.
