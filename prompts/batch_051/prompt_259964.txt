Two friends of mine got into a long argument over Searle's paper Minds, Brains, and Programs, specifically the part where he answers the brain simulator reply. I couldn't referee it, so I'm bringing it here.

The brain simulator reply says: fine, maybe the original room full of rulebooks doesn't understand, but suppose instead we program a machine to simulate the actual sequence of neuron firings in the brain of a native Chinese speaker while he understands Chinese. That system would understand. Searle's countermove is to imagine the man in the room operating a set of water pipes and valves that mirror the brain's neural firings. He then says, roughly, that no one thinks a computer simulation of a rainstorm will leave us all drenched, or that a simulation of digestion can digest a pizza; in the same way, he says, the hydraulic brain simulation doesn't understand Chinese.

My friends' argument went like this:

1. Kurt said Searle's reply is decisive. The rainstorm and digestion cases show that a simulation is not the same as the thing itself, so the brain simulator reply just relocates the original problem into a new set of materials. If the Chinese room didn't produce understanding, neither does the pipe-and-valve room.

2. Marta said Searle's analogies are rigged. Rainstorms and digestion are defined by their physical outputs. Understanding isn't; it's about information processing. If you replicate the causal structure of the brain, you replicate whatever information processing the brain does. So the rainstorm comparison is a dodge, not an argument.

3. Kurt came back: that's exactly the point at issue. Marta is assuming understanding is nothing more than information processing, which is what Searle denies. And in the hydraulic version, the man could be the one opening and closing the valves, and he still wouldn't understand Chinese. So the system doesn't either.

4. Marta said that's a cheap move. The man is just one component of the system. If Searle is allowed to say the man's lack of understanding doesn't matter in the original room, he can't then use the man's lack of understanding in the hydraulic version to indict the whole system.

5. Kurt said the man is just there to make the point vivid. The real argument is that the water pipes are doing the same causal work as the neurons, so if the pipes don't understand, the neurons don't either—unless you're already assuming that biology is what matters, which would be smuggling in the conclusion.

6. Marta said maybe Searle is the one smuggling in the conclusion, namely that biology matters intrinsically and that consciousness can't be just causal structure. If you don't already buy that, the brain simulator reply looks like a plausible empirical hypothesis, not a logical error.

Please adjudicate this move by move: who's right about the rainstorm and digestion analogies, whether the pipe-and-valve version is a fair test of the brain simulator reply, whether the man-as-subsystem point works, and whether either side is smuggling in a premise. Most of all, does the brain simulator reply survive Searle's answer or not?
