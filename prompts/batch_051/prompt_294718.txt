I sit on my university's first-year seminar committee and have recently become coordinator of its common reading pilot. We are voting soon on whether to adopt a proposed essay for next fall's incoming students. The essay argues that the Neolithic transition was, for most people, a net loss: it cites nutritional stress markers in teeth and bone, dental caries, shorter stature, longer work hours in farming communities, and the rise of durable inequality and hierarchy, and it urges teaching the agricultural revolution as a trap rather than a triumph. Opponents on the committee reply that the essay slides between average and total well-being, treats regional variation as noise, and sidelines the fact that farming supported far larger and more resilient populations. I must circulate a short written opinion before the vote. Please draft it. It should recommend a concrete disposition—approve, reject, or approve with revisions—explain the reasoning in terms the committee can act on, and give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. We do not need footnotes or a reading list; we need clear reasoning.
