I have been thinking about why large-scale predictions about society keep failing, and I ended up with an argument that appears to prove something much stronger than I expected: that there cannot be laws of history at all. I cannot tell whether the argument is sound.

(1) If there were laws of history, then a complete description of present conditions would in principle let us predict the broad future course of society.
(2) The broad future course of society depends heavily on future knowledge — what later generations will discover, invent, and come to believe. A society that acquired steam power or antibiotics much earlier, for instance, would have taken a different path.
(3) Future knowledge cannot be predicted, because if someone could state today what a future discovery will be, they would already have made that discovery.
(4) So at least one of the main factors on which the course of history depends is unpredictable in principle.
(5) Therefore the broad future course of society cannot be predicted in principle.
(6) Therefore there are no laws of history.

Is this argument valid? If it is not, which step fails, and what would a corrected version of that step say?
