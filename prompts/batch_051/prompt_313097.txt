I am a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of physics, and I would like help turning a half-formed thought into a one-page proposal for a six-month research fellowship. The question I want funded is this: in the many-worlds interpretation, if every outcome of a measurement occurs in some branch, what justifies the Born rule—the claim that an observer should assign credences to being in branches in proportion to the squared amplitudes—rather than treating branch weights as an unexplained additional postulate? The two obvious answers seem to be that the rule follows from decision-theoretic constraints on rational agents facing branching payoffs, or that it must be accepted as primitive; neither, as I understand it, has cleanly answered the worry that its starting assumptions already import the very probabilistic content it claims to derive. I would like the proposal to be built around a sharper version of that worry: what counts as a non-circular derivation of the Born rule, and can the circularity objection be met without assuming from the outset that branch weights are probabilities? Please write the proposal stating the question precisely, saying why the existing positions leave it open, describing how philosophical analysis rather than further work in physics would make progress and why that method rather than the obvious alternatives, indicating what a successful deliverable would look like and what would count as failure, and including a realistic scope for six months and the answer we would give if a foundation asked why this should be funded as philosophy rather than as physics.
