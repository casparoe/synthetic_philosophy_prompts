I'm on the academic integrity board at a mid-sized university, and we have to rule this week on a senior who ran a small paper-writing operation—she wrote essays for pay for around twenty students over two years, letting them pass courses they didn't do the work for. She's first-generation, worked two jobs, and grew up in an unstable home. Her student advocate is asking for leniency partly on the basis of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: roughly, you do what you do because of the way you are, and to be truly morally responsible for what you do you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are, which nobody can be, since that would mean having chosen your own traits in a infinite regress. The board is split: some members want to expel her, while others say the Strawson point undercuts retribution and leaves only forward-looking reasons for any sanction (deterrence, education, protecting other students).

Draft the written opinion I'll send the board before our Thursday vote. It should recommend one concrete sanction with a time frame, and it should honestly engage the strongest argument on the side you reject—especially the Strawson-based point if you don't follow it. Start directly with the memo itself, no preface, and make the first sentence your recommended action.
