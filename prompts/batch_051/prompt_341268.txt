Im a philosophy PhD student working on AI alignment, and my advisor keeps telling me to stress-test the debate proposal against historical cases where someone had the evidence and still lost. I want to work through Semmelweis. Quick recap of the facts as I know them: in 1840s Vienna the main hospital had two maternity wards — the doctors' ward ran a maternal mortality rate around 10 to 18 percent, the midwives' ward around 2 or 3 percent. Semmelweis figured out that cadaverous particles were being carried from autopsies to the maternity ward on doctors' hands, after a colleague died of a dissection wound with symptoms that looked exactly like childbed fever. He made the doctors wash with chlorinated lime and the death rate on their ward collapsed. The establishment still mostly rejected him — the theory implied doctors were killing their own patients, he had only comparative statistics and no respectable mechanism, and miasma theory gave everyone a face-saving alternative. He died in an asylum before germ theory caught up with him.

The lens I want to put on this is the AI-safety-via-debate picture of scalable oversight: a weak judge who cannot verify the truth directly is supposed to reliably find it by watching two stronger, adversarial experts argue opposite sides and asking them questions, on the bet that under sustained cross-examination a false answer is harder to defend than a true one.
