Having spent my career in the natural sciences, I am accustomed to asking what observation would count against a claim before taking it seriously, and since retiring I have found that this habit is only rarely applied to the Great Filter debate. The specific claim I would like you to examine is a common one: that the hardest step between the chemistry of a young planet and a technological civilization—the filter itself—is the emergence of the eukaryotic cell. The reasoning is that a planet like the early Earth had the raw materials and conditions for life very early, that prokaryote-like life appears in the geological record not long after the planet became habitable, but that eukaryotic cells, with their nucleus and endosymbiotic organelles, took on the order of a billion years or more to arrive, and large multicellular organisms took far longer still. I am not asking you to decide whether that reasoning is correct. I am asking whether the claim is testable, and what a serious test would look like.

Please design the most concrete test or observational program you can: what would be measured, in what setting, and what results would support the claim or undermine it? Then say plainly what the test would not settle. I am particularly interested in whether a single planetary history can ever license a conclusion about how probable a given transition is, and whether the hypothesis that a step is intrinsically hard can be separated from the hypothesis that it is merely a contingency of the one history we happen to have observed.
