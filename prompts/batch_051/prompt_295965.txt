Quick one on Orwell I keep going back and forth on. The argument in 'Politics and the English Language' is roughly that sloppy language and sloppy thought feed each other: ready-made phrases let writers avoid thinking in the first place, and once they harden into habit they make precise thought harder, especially in politics where euphemism and abstraction do a lot of the work. But Orwell's diagnosis is aimed at living writers—lazy or evasive ones, people trying to make the indefensible sound respectable. Now suppose a large chunk of routine political prose (council reports, think tank output, backbench speeches, party briefings) gets drafted by large language models. They have no thoughts to corrupt and no conscience to soothe; they're just very good at generating fluent text built out of exactly the sort of worn-out metaphors and abstract filler Orwell attacked. Does his argument survive this development? I'm interested in the argument, not the policy. Say which premise is under pressure, whether theres a repaired version that still does important work, and if it doesn't survive, what the remaining problem should be called.
