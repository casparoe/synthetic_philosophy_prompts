I'm a CS PhD student working on debate as an AI oversight method, and a claim keeps floating around my group that obfuscated arguments are basically self-defeating inside a debate protocol: a weak case buried under confident tone-switches, gish-galloping, and subtle equivocation can get picked apart by a comparably capable opponent, and the judge then sees which side was leaning on tricks. I half-believe it but I cant tell if its an empirical claim or a conceptual one, because nothing in the protocol actually pins down what counts as obfuscation, and 'leaning on tricks' might not be visible to a judge even when its happening.

How would you actually test this? I want a concrete experimental design — LLM debaters and judges over questions with known ground truth seems like the obvious setup — and I want to know what result would support the 'debate disciplines away obfuscation' reading and what result would count against it. Then the part I keep getting stuck on: even if you get a clean positive result, what does it fail to show about real deployment where there is no independent ground truth to score the judge against? Is the test testing the conceptual claim or just a toy version of it?
