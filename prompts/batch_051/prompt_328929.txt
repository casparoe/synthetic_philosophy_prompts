Act as a professional philosopher of science who works on confirmation theory. Before drafting, think through the dialectic of each objection and the reply it is likely to provoke.

I am preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends the Hempelian resolution of the ravens paradox. Her position is that confirmation is a formal, qualitative relation between statements rather than a graded measure of evidential weight. Because the hypothesis that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens, and a white shoe satisfies the latter, she accepts that a white shoe genuinely confirms the raven hypothesis; the apparent paradox, on her view, is a psychological by-product of background knowledge, not a defect in the logic of confirmation. She is committed to preserving the equivalence condition and to Hempel's satisfaction criterion as the correct logic of instance confirmation.

Draft nine interview questions that force her to defend this position from distinct dialectical angles rather than restating it; do not include biographical or merely expository questions. Make sure the set includes at least: (1) a challenge to the equivalence condition based on the idea that logically equivalent formulations can differ in what it is sensible to test or search for; (2) a challenge from the Bayesian objection that a white shoe's confirmatory power is negligible, asking what her purely qualitative account says that a probabilistic account does not; (3) a challenge from the natural-kinds or projectibility direction, asking why a syntactic criterion is preferable to the idea that non-black and non-raven are the wrong predicates to project; and (4) a closing question about what observation or theoretical result would lead her to abandon the satisfaction criterion. For each question, state the reply she is most likely to give and write the immediate follow-up that this reply invites. End with a one-sentence summary of the interview's overall line of attack, formatted as \answer{...}.
