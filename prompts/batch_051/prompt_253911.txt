I've never understood why the same person will call it theft, full stop, when someone copies a paragraph from a novel, but will say that a chef copying another chef's signature dish down to the plating is just how the industry works, and a fast-fashion brand knocking off a dress days after a runway show is just competition. In most legal systems the paragraph gets copyright protection while the recipe and the silhouette basically don't, even though all three involve creative labor and can be copied for free. The obvious explanation is that one counts as "expression" and the others count as "ideas" or as functional, but I'm not sure that line is doing real work rather than just marking a historical accident. Think like a philosopher who specializes in intellectual property and the metaphysics of ideas: brainstorm at least three candidate justifications for the asymmetry, develop the strongest one as an actual principle, then attack it with the hardest counterexample you can think of. End with an honest verdict on whether the ordinary person's asymmetry is defensible, and if you think one side of it should change, say which side and why.
