I'm a philosophy professor, and I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a visiting speaker who defends the halfer answer to the Sleeping Beauty problem. Her stock reply to every thirder argument is that Beauty's evidence on waking is centered—it concerns which possible waking she is in—and that centered evidence of this kind cannot by itself move her credence in the uncentered proposition that the coin landed heads; anyone who shifts from one-half to one-third, she says, has confused a change in Beauty's location with a reason to revise her opinion about the coin. Draft eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, that make this deflection progressively harder to sustain. Cover at least the biased-coin variant (a coin biased two-to-one toward heads, with one waking if heads and two if tails), the betting variant in which Beauty faces the same bet at every waking together with the Dutch book charge against the halfer, and the case in which Beauty is told that today is Monday. End with a question about what evidence, if any, could count against her view rather than being absorbed into it.
