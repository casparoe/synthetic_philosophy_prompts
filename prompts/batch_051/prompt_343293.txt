A friend of mine has been arguing that everyone who uses a chatbot is secretly committed to claiming it has beliefs, and I can't find the hole. His argument, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) If you only ever interact with someone through text, then your only evidence about their mental states is their linguistic behavior. (2) The linguistic behavior of a good large language model is often smoother, more relevant, and more sensitive to what you said earlier than a human pen pal's would be. (3) If your only evidence is linguistic behavior, and a system's linguistic behavior is at least as good as a human's, then you have no more reason to attribute beliefs to the human than to the model. (4) So if you deny that the model has beliefs, you should deny that a human you only know by text has beliefs; that's just skepticism about other minds. (5) But skepticism about other minds is absurd. (6) Therefore the model has beliefs. When I object that I know the model is just doing next-token prediction, he says that's cheating, because my knowledge of the model's architecture isn't something I got from its behavior — and for the pen pal too, I'm leaning on background knowledge (that they're a human, that I have a human brain) which itself came from behavior at some point. So the asymmetry has to be earned somewhere, not just asserted.

Then he turns the same machine around to make it worse: (7) A model can be prompted to assert P, and then, after a simple prompt edit, assert not-P with no sign of noticing any tension. (8) So if it has beliefs at all, they lack the kind of stability and answerability to evidence that beliefs are supposed to have. (9) But if no behavioral test can tell a system whose "beliefs" are stable and evidence-sensitive from one whose "beliefs" are just prompt-relative outputs, then either the test is wrong or (6) was too quick. I don't know whether to reject (3), bite some bullet, or conclude that "belief" just isn't the sort of thing you can apply from behavior alone.

Which step would you reject, and what does rejecting it cost for the intentional stance as a way of talking about AI systems?
