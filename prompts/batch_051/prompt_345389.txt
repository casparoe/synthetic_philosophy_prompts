i'm giving a seminar next month to a room of mostly social and cultural historians, plus one paleoclimatologist who collaborates with historians and one historical sociologist. The paper argues historians should be willing to treat pathogens, climate shifts, and volcanoes as actors in a deliberately weak sense: not intentional agents, but exogeneous difference-makers whose effects can be followed through concrete mechanisms — mortality, harvest failure, state fiscal crisis. I claim the reflex that reabsorbs every non-human cause into the human response is an overcorrection that sneaks a commitment to human agency back in, and that factor talk is worse than actor talk because it invites a regression mindset where the human response always does the last causal work. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a restatement of the argument: the objections this exact room will raise, ordered by how likely and how damaging, phrased the way they'd say them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft, what I should concede up front (especially to the environmental historians who've been burned by determinism), and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
