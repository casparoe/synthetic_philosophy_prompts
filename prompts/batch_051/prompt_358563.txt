I've been trying to get clear on what would actually make an endless life boring, in the sense that makes people say an eternal afterlife would be unbearable. Here's the working list of criteria I've ended up with. An endless life counts as boring in the relevant sense if (1) the experiences available to the person are, however pleasant, drawn from a finite repertoire that eventually gets used up; (2) the person's desires will all, sooner or later, be either satisfied or abandoned, so that no new desire of the sort that gives you a reason to get out of bed will ever show up; and (3) from some point on, the person can correctly see that nothing they do is making them someone different than they already were — every project is just a rerun of an earlier self. Don't assume these are all necessary; part of the job is to see wether they hold up.

Now test them on this case. Call it the Record Library. You die and wake up in a place whose only activity is listening to music. The good news: the library contains every finite sequence of notes that can be built from the standard chromatic scale, so its literally infinite — there is always a piece you haven't heard, forever, no matter how fast you listen. The bad news: the pieces are just the combinatorics of the same modest musical vocabulary, so after a few millennia every new piece strikes you as a variation on something familiar, the way a new arrangement of the same few chord changes is still the same song at some level. Nothing outside the scheme can ever occur, because the library has no outside. You keep your memories, but you're allowed to deliberately fog parts of them, so that old pieces can hit you as fresh again, for a while.

So: is life in the Record Library boring by my three criteria, or not? Go through them one at a time, say where the criteria don't settle the case, and then tell me whether the case is really a counterexample to one of the criteria — or to the whole idea that boredom is what's wrong with living forever.
