I've spent years on forums whose karma systems share a design I have been trying to make philosophical sense of. Contributions are scored by other users, and the scores accrue into a single durable number attached to the account; that one number is then used as a signal of how much weight a person's future contributions deserve, as a credential that gates privileges such as voting and editing, and as a sanction, since losing it is treated as a socially meaningful penalty. Yet the individual votes that move the number are anonymous and nearly costless, while the number itself is public and persistent. Reconstruct the implicit theory of reputation, standing, or quality that this design presupposes. Is it committed to reputation as a global, fungible scalar — a kind of credibility currency — or does it quietly run together several rival views: an epistemic-quality signal, a governance credential, and a punitive ledger? Say what the practice strictly requires as opposed to what it merely tolerates, and what would have to change in the design if reputation were treated as context-specific and relational rather than a durable property of the person.
