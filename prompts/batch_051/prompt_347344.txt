Here is an argument I've returned to several times and cannot decide whether it's sound. (1) Satisficing is the policy of taking the first option that meets an aspiration level rather than continuing to search for the best. (2) A boundedly rational agent should adopt whatever decision procedure is best suited to her limited time, information, and computational capacities. (3) The best decision procedure for such an agent is the one that maximizes expected utility over the choices it will produce, minus the costs of running the procedure. (4) Therefore, a boundedly rational agent should satisfice exactly when satisficing wins this higher-order expected-utility calculation. (5) But that calculation is simply optimization applied to the choice of decision procedure; so satisficing turns out to be optimization in disguise, not an alternative to it. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and on what reading? Please give your assessment as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
