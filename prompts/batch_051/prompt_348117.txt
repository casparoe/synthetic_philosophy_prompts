I'm on my university's faculty promotions committee, and the provost's office wants us too adopt a "research productivity score" to triage tenure files. The score is a weighted composite of citation counts, h-index, and number of papers in journals above a certain impact-factor threshold; files below a cutoff would be flagged for "additional scrutiny" — emphatically not an automatic negative, they keep saying, just a reason to look harder. The committee is split. One side says this just formalizes what external reviewers already do informally and protects junior people from being sunk by one grumpy senior reviewer. The other side keeps bringing up Goodhart's law and specification gaming: once everyone knows the score matters, they'll optimize for it — salami-slicing papers into minimal publishable units, chasing citable-but-forgettable results, gaming impact factors — and the number will stop tracking whatever it was suppossed to track. Their other argument is that even a flag officially labelled "not a negative" will become a de facto target, since nobody wants a red flag in their file.

I've been asked to circulate a short memo by Thursday that lands somewhere. Should we adopt the triage score, reject it, or adopt a modified version (for example, keep the score but only as one trigger among several, allow candidates to submit a rebuttal statement, or compute it without ever publishing the exact weights)? I'm looking for a definite recommendation with reasons, not a survey of options — and please steelman the side you're recommending against before you explain why we're overriding them.
