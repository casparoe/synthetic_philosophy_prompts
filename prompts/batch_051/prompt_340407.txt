You're a political philosopher who knows The Brothers Karamazov well, and I want you to think carefully, show your reasoning, and start with a quick brainstorm before you commit to a number.

I just got out of a long arguement with a friend about the Grand Inquisitor chapter. His line is that the old man is basically a psychologist of the human condition: ordinary people can't really bear freedom and will gladly hand it over for bread, mystery, and authority, and they're mostly happier for the trade. I think the Inquisitor is stacking the deck—he only ever observes people the church has already schooled into childlike obedience, and the "freedom" he contrasts with happiness is Christ's impossible demand for free faith, not the ordinary freedoms people actually care about (who to marry, what to work at, what to say without a censor).

Here's the proposition I want your actual credence in, so don't just tell me it depends. Treat it as a claim about how people are, with a truth value: most human adults, if offered a real choice between (a) the burden of deciding their own values and carrying their own conscience, with the uncertainty and lonliness that involves, and (b) a clear set of fixed meanings handed to them by an authority that also guarantees their material security and tells them their obedience is virtue, would eventually choose (b) and, having chosen, would mostly be happy with the trade. Give me a single number for that.

Then decompose it. I want at least five sub-questions, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification, and I want to see how they combine into the final credence. Pick your own, but ones I'd expect: how much rides on who counts as "most adults" across times and places; how much on whether the Inquisitor's definition of freedom is a fair stand-in for freedom generally; how much on whether people mistake comfort or relief for happiness; how much on whether option (b) can keep running only with ongoing coercion behind the scenes; how much on whether the preference for handing over conscience is a fixed human trait or an effect of being raised under authority. Then tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to, and which one you're least confident in. Finally, say how confident you are in the whole decomposition and single out the one point in it you're least sure of.
