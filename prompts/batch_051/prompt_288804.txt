I recently read a piece in a popular-science magazine that tried to summarise the debate over reduction and emergence, and I would like to know how much of it to trust. As I understood it, the article made roughly the following claims. Everything that happens in the world is ultimately governed by fundamental physics; chemistry, biology, and even economics are, at bottom, descriptions of the same physical goings-on at a coarser grain. It called this picture reductionism and presented it as all but established, noting that thermodynamics has been recovered from statistical mechanics and that computer models now generate complex, lifelike patterns from extremely simple rules, which the article took to suggest that the higher-level sciences are unlikely to have autonomous laws of their own. The article then introduced emergence as reductionism's main rival, glossing it as the familiar idea that a whole is more than the sum of its parts. On the article's telling, emergence is the claim that higher-level properties such as temperature or consciousness exert a downward influence over the very particles that make them up. It concluded that reductionism clearly wins, because once emergence is acknowledged to be only a limitation on our ability to calculate everything from the bottom up, emergent properties turn out to be merely convenient summaries, nothing over and above the parts and their interactions.

I found this persuasive, and it matches much of what I have absorbed from popular science, but I have a nagging feeling that the conclusion came too quickly and that the article may have been attacking a stronger version of emergence than most researchers actually defend. Could you go through the article's claims and tell me which parts a philosopher of science, or a scientist working on these issues, would accept as stated; which parts are simplifications or conflations, and what a careful defender of emergence would object to; and whether the article's final conclusion follows once the accurate parts are stated properly?
