I retired last year after a long career in the physical sciences, and have only recently begun reading philosophy of science in earnest. Goodman's new riddle of induction has stayed with me more than almost anything else I have read. I understand the standard presentation well enough — the predicates grue and bleen, and the claim that observed green emeralds equally support the generalization that all emeralds are grue — but I have the nagging sense that several distinct problems are running together under the heading grue, and that some of them matter far more than others.

Let me lay out the joints I am groping toward, though I do not trust them and expect you to correct them. There is the formal problem of why some predicates are projectible and others are not. There is the question whether natural kinds or real similarities underwrite projectibility. There is the question whether the dispute between green and grue is substantive or merely verbal once the vocabulary is changed. And there is the role of entrenchment, or the history of a predicate's use, in licensing projection. I suspect one or two of these are red herrings.

Here is the situation for which I want your judgment. I have organized a small reading group of retired scientists, all serious newcomers to philosophy, and we can devote roughly a year of fortnightly meetings to work that descends from the new riddle. Our aim is not to produce publishable commentary but to arrive at a considered view of what the riddle really shows; we would rather spend the year on load-bearing questions than on venerable ones. I would like you to set the agenda. Which unresolved questions, implications, or lines of work in this area most deserve that year, and in what order? For each item, defend the placement: is it upstream of other questions, so that progress there would reorganize what comes after? Is it tractable for people with decades of experience thinking about regularities, models, and measurement but no formal training in logic or metaphysics, and is it comparatively neglected? What could a year of sustained discussion plausibly clarify or settle, and what later developments would lead you to promote or demote it? I also want a short excluded list: work often treated as central in this neighborhood that you would deliberately not put before a group like mine, and why. I am asking for a defended ordering and triage, not a survey of the field.
