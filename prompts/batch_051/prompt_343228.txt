I've been working through the Newton's bucket stuff, and there's a move I keep tripping over that I don't have a name for, and I'd like you to either point me to the right term or help me carve one out. The relationist says: there's no space itself, just material bodies and the distances (and maybe relative velocities) between them. Then you point to the bucket, or a Foucault pendulum, or a planet's equatorial bulge, and you say, look, there's a real physical effect of absolute rotation, so it's rotation relative to what? And the relationist says: relative to the fixed stars, or the average matter distribution of the universe. Fine. But then when the substantivalist says aha, so you've just introduced a privileged frame that does all the work absolute space was supposed to do, the relationist backtracks: no, the star frame is just a convenient reference frame, it's not a real thing, nothing metaphysically special about it. But then they go right back to using it as if it's special. They'll say that in an empty universe the bucket wouldn't be concave (or that the question is meaningless), and that if you spun the stars around the bucket the water would still climb the sides — which sure sounds like the star frame is doing physical work, not just bookkeeping. The position comes out relationist when you attack it and substantivalist when it makes predictions. Is there an established name for exactly this pattern? It's not straightforward instrumentalism, because the frame gets real causal work, and it's not standard substantivalism because they deny anything exists beyond bodies and relations. What's the closest concept in the vicinity, how does the thing I'm describing differ from it, and if there's no established name, what would you call it and where exactly are the edges — e.g., when is a relationist allowed to lean on a privileged frame and still count as a relationist?
