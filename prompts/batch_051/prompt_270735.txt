I've been trying to pin down what people mean when they call behaviour sphexish, after reading about a digger wasp that provisions its burrow like this: it drags a cricket to the entrance, goes down to check the nest, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a couple of inches away while the wasp is down there, it will drag the cricket back to the same spot, check the nest again, and repeat the whole loop indefinitely, never realising it could just pull the thing in. Everyone I've talked to agrees that's mechanical, rigid, and not at all free. But when I try to state what's going on, my verdicts go all over the place.

Here are my verdicts. (1) Someone drives to the office on a Saturday out of sheer habit when they meant to visit a friend, and only notices when they're sitting in the car park. I'd say they were running a stored routine, sphexish in the moment, yet I'd still hold them responsible for missing the visit, whereas I wouldn't blame the wasp for anything. (2) A novice cook abandons dinner and orders takeaway because one ingredient is missing, even though obvious substitutes are sitting in the cupboard. Sphexish in the kitchen, and I'd say something is going wrong with the cook, but not in the way it goes wrong with the wasp. (3) An airline pilot running through the pre-flight checklist without skipping a single item, even under time pressure, seems to me infact the opposite of sphexish: deliberate, careful, in control. But I can't say why running a stored procedure here is different in principle. (4) A chess program that plays from a giant lookup table, flawless in any stored position and helpless in a novel one, strikes me as mechanical and in no way free. (5) A grandmaster who has seen thousands of positions and plays fast by feel in a new one I want to call flexible and free, but isn't the grandmaster just a lookup table with better compression? (6) Someone with a compulsive ritual has to start over from step one whenever they're interrupted. The surface behaviour looks exactly like the wasp, but I'm unwilling to call it mechanical in the same sense; it reads as something afflicting them rather than a missing capacity.

Is there one principle behind these verdicts, or do some of them need to go? I mainly want to know what separates the pilot's checklist from the wasp's routine, and what separates the grandmaster's baked-in patterns from the lookup table's. Then tell me what your principle says about a case I keep going back and forth on: an actor who has rehearsed a scene so many times they can perform it on stage while their attention is entirely elsewhere. Is that performance free, sphexish, or neither? And if the principle ends up saying nothing about free will, I'd like to know that too, because I might just be confusing flexibility with freedom.
