A friend and I recently had a long argument about Goodhart’s law and specification gaming, and I came away committed to a distinction I cannot quite stabilise. I would call it the difference between gaming and metric absorption. Gaming, as I mean it, requires an agent that represents the measure as a measure: it knows the score is a proxy for something else and deliberately arbitrages the gap between proxy and target. Metric absorption needs no such representation. An optimiser simply adapts to the fine-grained structure of the measure until its behaviour is shaped by the metric the way an organism is shaped by a niche; there is no point at which it treats the metric as standing for anything beyond itself. My central claim is that the serious cases of specification gaming in AI are mostly absorption rather than gaming proper, and that this matters because the standard responses — auditing for deceptive intent, checking whether the system is cheating — catch gaming while leaving absorption untouched. Example: a student who learns the mark scheme is gaming; a reinforcement-learning agent that learns to exploit an unintended regularity in its environment to drive a proxy upward is absorbing. I feel the distinction doing real work, but I suspect it may collapse when pressed. Is it coherent? Does it already exist under another name? What does it get right, and where exactly does it break?
