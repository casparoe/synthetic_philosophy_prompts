I am a PhD candidate in environmental economics, and I am most at home in the language of dynamic optimization: Bellman equations, continuation values, optimal stopping, sunk costs, stochastic state variables, and the real options approach to irreversible investment under uncertainty. I would like you to restate a position I keep meeting in the conservation and bioethics literature in that vocabulary, and then tell me what survives the translation.

The position is the strong precautionary argument against irreversible ecological interventions, with gene drives as the central case and de-extinction as a useful test case. In its strongest form it runs something like this. Releasing a self-propagating gene drive into a wild population is effectively irreversible, because once the construct is in the population there is no reliable way to recall it, and its spread across ecosystems and borders obeys dynamics that our models do not characterize well enough to support ordinary probabilistic risk assessment. Therefore the decision should not be framed as an expected-value comparison of benefits and harms. The default should be not to release, the burden of proof should rest with the proponent to demonstrate safety to a very high standard, and a sufficiently severe worst-case outcome should receive lexical priority over any quantity of expected benefit rather than being traded off against it. Some variants add that ecosystem states are non-substitutable in a way prices cannot capture. Proponents of de-extinction and reintroduction face the same argument, though the intervention there is less clearly self-propagating.

Please translate that argument, claim by claim, into the vocabulary of real options and optimal stopping under uncertainty: value functions, continuation values, the option value of waiting, sunk costs, stochastic state variables, and the treatment of extreme tail events through constraints or penalties. I want a translation that is honest in both directions, one that neither makes the precautionary claim look like a trivial special case of real options nor dismisses it as unquantifiable before the translation has been attempted. Then give me a residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in the framework at all, for example lexical prohibitions, deontological constraints, asymmetries in who bears the risk of each kind of error, or the special standing of the pre-intervention state? Finally say whether the residue is a gap in the real-options vocabulary or a sign that the precautionary argument is smuggling in assumptions my framework rightly refuses to import. If gene drives and de-extinction come apart on any of these points, mark exactly where.

Please write your answer in American English.
