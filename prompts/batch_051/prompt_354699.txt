A broadly Parfitian thesis about personal identity holds that what matters in survival is not numerical identity but psychological continuity and connectedness: overlapping chains of memory, intention, belief, and character. The teletransporter case is standardly used to motivate the thesis. A machine scans a traveler's body and brain in enough detail to record their complete physical state, destroys the body while scanning, and then builds an exact replica from new matter at the destination; the replica wakes up with the traveler's memories, plans, and quirks intact. Many people find it natural to judge that the replica inherits the traveler's life in whatever sense grounds self-interested concern. The case is meant to pry apart what we care about in our own future from the strict numerical identity of a person over time.

Your task is to give your best account of how one could bring evidence to bear on the claim that psychological continuity, not identity, is what matters in survival. Design a concrete test with scenarios that intentionally pull the two apart in both directions: at least one branching case in which psychological continuity is not unique (the original survives while a replica is made, or two replicas are made), and at least one case in which bodily and numerical identity are preserved while psychological continuity is destroyed or severely attenuated (for instance, a treatment that leaves the person unable to form new autobiographical memories). For each part of the test, state exactly which pattern of results would support the thesis and which would undermine it, including results that would be mere noise or framing effects. Then say explicitly what the test would and would not settle. In particular, a positive result might establish only what people report, predict, or prefer about their futures, not what actually matters; address whether further design can narrow that gap, and if you think it cannot be closed, say what that means for the original philosophical claim.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence of your answer, and then give your reasoning. Do not begin with a preamble or restating the question. Write in American English.
