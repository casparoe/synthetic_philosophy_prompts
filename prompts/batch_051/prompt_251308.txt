I sit on a university working group charged with deciding whether wisdom can be listed among the learning outcomes of an undergraduate education, and each member has been asked to write out the position they currently hold before the group debates it. I would like an external audit of the position I have arrived at, because I suspect it is broadly coherent but I cannot see where it strains. Here are the commitments I find myself wanting to defend:

1. Wisdom is not fundamentally a stock of propositional knowledge. It is a capacity for practical judgment: knowing how to deliberate well in messy, high-stakes situations where the rules underdetermine what to do.
2. Because wisdom is a capacity rather than a body of content, it cannot be transmitted by lectures or measured by exams. Like a craft, it has to be acquired through practice, feedback, and a kind of apprenticeship within a community of practitioners.
3. Epistemic humility is central to wisdom. On many important subjects the wise person is less confident than the ordinary person, more alert to counterevidence, and quicker to revise.
4. But wisdom equally requires decisiveness under uncertainty. Someone who only ever hedges and suspends judgment is not wise, only timid.
5. The university should be the primary institution for cultivating wisdom in young adults; that is what makes higher education higher rather than merely vocational.
6. Wisdom is domain-general in a way expertise is not. A wise person tends to exercise judgment well outside their specialty, whereas a brilliant expert may be practically foolish.
7. In my own education the experiences that made me wiser were the least content-heavy: a seminar in which the professor mostly asked questions, a year running a student journal. Almost none of the content I learned has stayed with me, which supports the idea that the transmission of content is largely beside the point.

Please go through these commitments and tell me whether they hang together. I want an audit, not a lecture: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what would have to give and how costly the fix would be. Also identify any pairs that only look inconsistent and explain why the appearance is misleading. Finally, tell me which single revision of the set would resolve the most strain at the lowest cost.
