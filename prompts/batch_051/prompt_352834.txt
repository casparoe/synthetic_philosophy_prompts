I recently read a blog post that tried to digest Hofstadter's discussion of tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level from Gödel, Escher, Bach, and I have a nagging sense that it overreaches, but I cannot pinpoint where. The post explained the idea roughly like this. Hofstadter's central exhibit is Escher's Drawing Hands, in which each hand draws the other, so the picture's causal chain loops back on itself with no uncaused bottom level. Hofstadter generalizes this to hierarchical systems generally: whenever a higher level of organization can feed back and modify the lower levels that generate it, the hierarchy is tangled, and any purely hierarchical explanation of such a system is bound to leave something out. To keep the tangle from collapsing into paradox, the post says, Hofstadter argues that a coherent system must contain an inviolate level, a level of rules that the system cannot revise from within. A computer program can rewrite its own high-level instructions, but it cannot rewrite the machine language in which those instructions are executed; likewise, a mind can edit its beliefs but cannot edit the neural machinery that does the editing. The post then says Hofstadter identifies the self with this inviolate level, the part of the system that can inspect everything below it but cannot inspect itself. From this the blog draws the conclusion that because the inviolate level cannot be represented within the system, Hofstadter's view entails that no system can ever give a complete account of its own consciousness, so a full theory of the self is impossible in principle.

I can see that the last inference might be unfair, and something about the claim that every coherent system must have an inviolate level sounds too strong, but I am not confident in my own reading. Could you go through the summary and separate what Hofstadter would accept, what is a subtle distortion, and whether the blog's final conclusion follows from whatever is accurate?
