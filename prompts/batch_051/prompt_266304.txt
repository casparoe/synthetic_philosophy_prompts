I am helping build the public case for a standing citizens' assembly in our county, selected by sortition, with genuine agenda-setting power and final authority over housing and land-use policy. The argument we keep reaching for is that the assembly is simply a jury for policy: we already hand twelve randomly chosen citizens the power to send someone to prison, on the theory that ordinary people sitting under oath, hearing evidence, and deliberating under a judge's instruction are the best safeguard against both state coercion and private influence, so there should be nothing radical about handing a larger randomly chosen body the power to draft zoning rules. The move lands with audiences, but I have begun to suspect that the analogy is carrying more weight than it can bear.

I want a full audit of the jury analogy before we lean on it again. Give me the correspondence table up front — what maps to the venire, the voir dire, the judge's charge, the verdict, the appellate check, and the rights of the defendant — and then identify the places where the mapping breaks. I do not need pedantic disanalogies; I need to know which differences matter specifically for the legitimating work the analogy is being asked to do, namely showing that rule by randomly selected citizens is continuous with an institution most people already accept. Address at least the following pressure points: a jury answers a question framed in advance by law while an assembly sets its own agenda; a jury decides facts concerning one person rather than trade-offs across a whole population; a jury operates inside a surrounding system of judges, appeals, and prosecutorial discretion that a standing assembly may lack; and a jury's legitimacy rests partly on the rights of the accused rather than on the representation of the governed. Then give your verdict: after the damaging disanalogies are subtracted, does the conclusion that a lottocratic assembly shares the democratic legitimacy of a jury survive? And if the jury analogy is finally misleading, say what the better analogy would be and what work it would do differently.
