I spent nearly forty years as a research physicist, so I thought I had made my peace with time: it's a parameter, a coordinate, something you integrate over. Then our retirement reading group reached Book Eleven of the Confessions, and I can't stop turning over Augustine's idea that past and future have no real being except as the mind's present attention stretched backward and forward. My daughter, who enjoys seeing me unsettled, made me rewatch Arrival (the 2016 film) the week after, and the two have got tangled in my head ever since.

I'd like a close reading of the film through Augustine's account of time in Confessions XI, taking both seriously. The heptapod script seems to present a language where everything is written all at once, and Louise ends up with what the movie treats as memories of events that have not happened yet. Is that the same move Augustine is making when he says the future exists only as expectation in the present mind, or is the film committed to something stronger, treating determinate future facts as already there to be known? Where does the film vindicate Augustine, where does it part ways, and where does it maybe blur the difference between the physics of time and the experience of time? I am also stuck on the ending: can Augustine make room for the attitude Louise ends up with, knowing what is coming and still choosing to live it, or does that require a kind of fixed future that Augustine would not grant? I suspect the film adds something to the Confessions that philosophical argument alone misses, but I'd like to hear how far that suspicion holds up.
