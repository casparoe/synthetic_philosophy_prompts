I'm a backend engineer on the catalog team at a streaming service, and I've never taken a philosophy class, so I'm kind of stuck. We keep going in circles about when two uploads should count as "the same song" for the purposes of grouping tracks under one canonical work in our database. Someone told me philosophers talk about musical works as types and performances as tokens, but I don't know how to turn that into a rule. What I actually need is a working definition of a musical work, or of "same work" identity if that's the better way to frame it. The definition has to handle the cases that keep coming up in our taxonomy meetings:

- a studio recording and a live version by the same artist
- a note-for-note cover by a different artist
- a jazz standard where each performance has a different tempo, key, and solos
- a remix where the original drums and instruments are replaced and only the vocal survives
- a mashup of two existing songs into one new track
- a three-second loop sampled from an old recording in a new beat
- John Cage's 4'33", where the musicians are silent and the "music" is whatever ambient noise happens in the room
- an AI-generated song like "1970s soul ballad" produced in a single step, with no human writing chords or lyrics
- a MIDI file of a tune and an audio recording of someone playing that same MIDI file
- a 30-second ringtone edit of a song

My gut says the first two are clearly the same work and the mashup is clearly two, but I genuinly don't know what to do with the jazz standard, the Cage piece, or the AI-generated track. Could you propose a definition, walk it through all ten cases, tell me where it gives a verdict you think is wrong or no verdict at all, and then either fix the definition or defend the weird result?
