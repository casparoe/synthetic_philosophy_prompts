Imagine an agent whose pairwise preferences are transitive at any given moment but reverse across time in a predictable cycle: at t1 it prefers A to B, at t2 it prefers C to A, at t3 it prefers B to C, and then the pattern repeats. The agent begins the cycle holding B. At each stage a trader offers, for a small fee, to exchange the option the agent currently holds for the option it then prefers. Every trade looks like a strict improvement when it is made, but after one full cycle the agent holds B again and has paid three fees. The agent can foresee the entire sequence from the start and faces no obstacle to adopting a binding plan for the whole cycle, but it has so far chosen separately at each moment. Please describe the most important implications of money-pump arguments for how this agent should understand its own diachronic rationality; in particular, address whether the absence of an obviously defective individual choice weakens the charge of irrationality, and whether binding its future selves to a single policy is rationally required, merely prudent, or something else.
