In the philosophy of cosmology course I'm taking this term, I've been struck by how stationary the fine-tuning debate seems. Both sides appear to accept the same physics — the fact that the cosmological constant, the strong force, and the Higgs mass sit in life-permitting ranges that look extraordinarily narrow — and both sides agree that this calls for some response. Yet the theistic design proponent and the multiverse proponent have been trading the same considerations for decades, and neither moves. My current hypothesis is that the disagreement is not about the data or the probability calculations but about what an explanation has to do: the design proponent treats the life-permitting values as something that must be explained by a mind-like cause, while the multiverse proponent treats them as a selection effect that removes the need for that kind of explanation. Is that right? Or is the impasse better understood as a hidden empirical or conceptual disagreement — for instance, about whether a multiverse mechanism would actually predict these values, or whether calling the constants tuned already presupposes that they could have been otherwise — or as a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether apparent purposiveness is evidence? I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a verdict on which side is right. Given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress — a new measurement, a sharper distinction, a reformulated question — or explain why we should not expect any. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
