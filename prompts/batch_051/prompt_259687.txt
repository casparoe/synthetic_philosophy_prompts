The question read:

Akrasia (weakness of will) is often characterized as free, intentional action against one's own all-things-considered judgment about what is best. Hyperbolic discounting is a model of time preference, widely used to explain procrastination, in which delayed rewards are discounted so steeply over short horizons that an agent's preferences reverse as a once-distant temptation becomes imminent. Does the hyperbolic-discounting model pick out the same cases the philosophical account of akrasia picks out? Does it explain akrasia, explain it away, or explain something else? Answer with reference to a concrete case of procrastination.

The student wrote:

On the standard Davidsonian account, an akratic agent judges that A is better than B, all things considered, is able to do A, and nonetheless intentionally does B. Hyperbolic discounting describes how people value delayed outcomes: the discount rate is steepest at short delays, so a person can initially prefer a larger-later reward but come to prefer a smaller-sooner reward as the smaller reward becomes immediately available. In the familiar case, a student on Tuesday evening prefers writing her essay on Wednesday afternoon to playing video games on Wednesday afternoon, since both are still in the future; but on Wednesday afternoon she prefers playing now to writing now, and she plays. The two accounts are picking out the same phenomenon: hyperbolic discounting supplies the underlying causal mechanism, while akrasia is merely the agent's own description of it. When the student plays instead of writing, her all-things-considered judgment has been replaced by a new all-things-considered judgment, namely that the immediate pleasure outweighs the later cost. So on the discounting model she is not acting against her better judgment at the moment of action; the reversal is her better judgment. This also shows the action isn't genuinely irrational. If present-biased valuation makes the immediate reward genuinely more valuable to her now, then choosing it is rational by her own current standard, and there is no principled reason to privilege Tuesday's judgment over Wednesday's. Akrasia, properly understood, is just what that kind of preference reversal feels like from the inside.
