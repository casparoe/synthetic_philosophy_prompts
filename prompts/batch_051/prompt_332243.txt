For a reading group on Korean Neo-Confucian moral psychology, I've been asked to write out where I've landed on the Four–Seven debate so the others can stress-test it. The debate concerns whether the moral feelings and the ordinary feelings have one source or two. The moral feelings are the Four Beginnings: commiseration, shame-and-dislike, deference-and-yielding, and the felt sense of right and wrong. The ordinary feelings are the Seven Emotions: joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, and desire. The two candidate sources are li, the normative pattern that things realize when they are as they should be, and ki, the concrete, physically active material of body and mind.

Here are my commitments, stated as flatly as I can manage:

1. Li is not an agent or a force. It does not push anything into being; it is a pattern or norm. Ki alone has causal activity, so every actual episode of feeling is a ki-episode.

2. The Seven Emotions are ethically neutral as a class: any one of them can be good when it conforms to the relevant li and bad when it misses, whether through excess, deficiency, a wrong object, or a wrong occasion.

3. The Four Beginnings are always good. There is no bad kind of commiseration and no bad kind of shame-and-dislike; a feeling that is misplaced or disproportionate is not a deformed case of the beginning. Something about the Four deserves to be called an issuance of li, and that is what keeps them from being just another ki-impulse.

4. The Four Beginnings are not a separate set of feeling-types over and above the Seven. Each of them is built from the affective material of the Seven, ordered by the relevant li — for example, shame-and-dislike is dislike ordered by the li of righteousness. The Four are the Seven, or some of them, in their rightly ordered form, not an additional class of affects.

5. When I say a feeling issues from something, I mean that a single source produces it. Talk of two sources, with one following and the other riding, therefore sounds to me like a compromise label rather than an explanation — unless it is only a compressed way of saying that a ki-event is brought into conformity with li.

I cannot reliably tell which of these pull against one another. Go through the set and say which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly; which pairs only seem to be in tension and why they dissolve; and for each genuine tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost. Then say which single revision would clean up the most.
