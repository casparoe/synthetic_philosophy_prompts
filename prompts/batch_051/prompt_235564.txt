I have kept returning to a disagreement that came up in my school's ethics and economics discussion group. We were distinguishing intrinsic from instrumental value when someone asked whether wealth itself — not the things it buys, but the condition of being prosperous, of having resources in excess of need — can matter morally on its own. I find the instrumentalist answer almost obviously right when it is stated carefully. Money is a placeholder: a bank balance would be worthless to a person stranded with nothing to purchase, and valuing a raise seems sensible only because of the options it opens. The view also explains why hoarding for its own sake looks like a confusion, and why resources ultimately matter because human needs, relationships, and projects matter. But I also have an intuition I keep failing to get rid of. When I imagine a genuinely prosperous place — an abundant harvest on a family farm, a city with enough that it can afford to spill over into festivals, public art, and generosity that no one budgets for in advance — it seems to me that the abundance itself is good, not merely the specific meals, buildings, or gifts it finances. The surplus feels like it adds worth to the life lived within it, above and beyond what each purchase delivers. I would not say this about piles of currency; I want to say it about prosperity as a way of living with more than enough.

I am therefore torn between a theory that is simple and explanatory and an intuition that is concrete and firm. I am not looking to be told which side wins. I would like you to present the strongest version of each: the strongest case for treating the worth of wealth as entirely instrumental, and the strongest case for treating abundance as carrying moral value beyond its buying power. Then tell me what it would take for the intuition to be debunked rather than honored, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of prosperity or moral worth on which the two positions are compatible after all.
