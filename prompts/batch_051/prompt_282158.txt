I am a PhD student in the philosophy of social science, working on the dispute between methodological individualists and methodological holists. In my department, the holist side leans heavily on an analogy that I keep encountering but have never seen subjected to a proper audit. They say a society is like an organism: its institutions are organs, its division of labour is a physiological division of labour, and its continued existence is homeostasis. From this they conclude that social facts must be explained at the level of the social whole, because you cannot explain what a heart does by looking at the cell in isolation, and no one would try to run physiology from the bottom up.

The version of the analogy that gets cited is Herbert Spencer's argument in The Social Organism, where he compares the sustaining, distributing, and regulating systems of an organism to the productive, infrastructural, and governmental systems of a society, and takes the comparison to show that society is a living whole with a life of its own. A colleague insists that this is not merely illustrative but methodological: just as physiology requires the organism as its unit of analysis, sociology requires society as its unit of analysis, and individual-level explanations are as misplaced as trying to explain an organ's function by the motives of its cells.

I want you to audit this analogy as an argument for methodological holism. Give me the correspondence table: what in society is supposed to correspond to cells, tissues, organs, the circulatory system, the nervous system, growth, reproduction, homeostasis, and death. Then identify where the mapping breaks, and for each disanalogy tell me whether it is fatal to the methodological use or merely cosmetic. In particular, address these features that organisms have and societies may not: clear boundaries, a single genetic blueprint, fixed species-typical development, functional integration so tight that parts cannot survive outside the whole, and a central control system. Then say whether the holist conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing argumentative work that the evidence cannot support. If a better analogy would do the same work more honestly, propose one and state exactly what it licenses about the level of explanation in social science.
