I host a recorded seminar series on American pragmatism, and next week's guest is a philosopher who has devoted much of her career to defending a distinctive reading of Peirce's account of inquiry. On her reading, Peirce's catalogue of ways of settling opinion — tenacity, authority, appeal to what is agreeable to reason, and the method of science — amounts to an argument that only the last is self-consistent, because each of the others depends on suppressing or ignoring experience. She also reads the pragmatic maxim as a semantic rule for clarifying what a concept means, not as a criterion that decides in advance which concepts may be used; and she maintains that Peirce's fallibilism is compatible with his expectation that inquiry, continued far enough, would eventually converge on a single settled opinion.

I can introduce that view competently, but I want questions that press it rather than restate it. Please draft eight to ten interview questions, with no purely biographical filler. Include at least one question on each of the following pressure points, and give each a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give: (i) whether the method of science can justify itself without secretly relying on the very commitment to letting experience correct one's beliefs that it is supposed to establish; (ii) whether the convergence account of truth makes every present belief merely provisional in a way that sits badly with actual assertion and action; (iii) what the pragmatic maxim says about theoretical entities, dispositions, and other posits that cannot be cashed out in a finite list of conceivable practical effects; and (iv) how fallibilism about any given belief can be squared with the claim that inquiry could not go permanently wrong. The follow-ups should anticipate the guest's standard replies and force her to say what keeps them from becoming a promissory note.

Please write the questions in British English. After the questions, add a short note stating how confident you are in the reading of Peirce that the questions presuppose, and identifying which of the four pressure points rests on the shakiest part of that reading. End the reply with a single last line beginning "Summary:" followed by a one-sentence summary of the overall interview strategy.
