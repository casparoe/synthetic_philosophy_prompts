He says for any system there's three stances you can take to predict it: physical (atoms and forces), design (what it was built to do, read the spec), and intentional (pretend it has beliefs and goals and is acting rationally to get what it wants). He also insists the point isn't which stance is "true," it's which pattern is real enough to be doing predictive work at that level.

I've kept a running list of which one he actually reaches for:

- office thermostat, the old bi-metal kind: physical — he says it's just a strip of metal, there's nothing to mind-read.
- my top-loader with a mechanical timer: design — he predicts it off the cycle chart.
- the chess engine on his laptop: intentional — he talks about it wanting to open up the queenside, and predicts its moves by asking what a rational player at that level would want.
- his dog: intentional.
- a motion-sensor porch light: design — he reads it off the datasheet.
- the maple tree in his yard: physical — no goals, just water and gravity.

I don't want to ask Jake until I've thought it through. What stance would Jake take on the bot, and why? More importantly, what general rule is he actually running? I can see at least two rules that both fit the record so far but point different ways on the bot. Walk me through what each rule says, your best guess, whether any earlier case is a closer analog than I think, and what the cleanest little test case would be to settle which rule Jake's really using.
