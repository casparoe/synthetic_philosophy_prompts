ok so for a school project-ish rabbit hole I went down, I got really into endangered languages, and I had this idea I cant stop thinking about and I want someone to pressure-test it.

The proposal: instead of just having an endangered-languages list that people occasionally donate to, make a market like carbon credits. You set a global cap on how much "linguistic vitality" can be lost per decade. Every country gets an allowance: so many vitality points its allowed to lose (based on a vitality index: number of speakers, average speaker age, whether kids are still learning it, where the language is actually used). If a country keeps its languages healthier than its allowance, it can sell spare points to countries whose languages are cratering. Money from sales has to go into immersion schools, documentation, or community language programs, not general budgets. Communities get a cut too. So rich countries basically pay poorer countries to keep languages alive.

I don't want a "is this a good idea" essay. I want to know how the thing would actually behave if you turned it on. Who figures out how to game it first? Does it accidentally turn languages into museum pieces that get documented but not used? Can a government bank credits for a language while also arresting people for speaking it? What happens with languages that cross borders, or stateless languages, or a diaspora keeping a language alive somewhere the "owner" country gets no credit? Does the credit system make extinction something you can pollute your way out of?

Also please be blunt: if the whole premise is confused or can't really be analyzed as stated, say so instead of politely running with it. If "vitality points" are just not the kind of thing that can be defined well enough to trade, tell me that first.
