At a recent meeting of our university's budget committee, a senior colleague dismissed junior faculty complaints about the salary schedule as "really just envy—and envy is a vice." I suspect the slogan is doing more work than it can bear, but I want to be precise about why. Please distinguish the several claims that "envy is a vice" could be used to assert—about the emotion's intrinsic badness, its object and motivation, the behavior it produces, or the character of the envious person—and say of each whether it is trivial, defensible and substantive, or false. Then identify which readings would actually support using the charge of envy to dismiss a complaint about unequal pay, and which are compatible with the idea that the envy of the worse-off can be excusable, or may even signal that an arrangement is unjust. Does the distinction between envy and resentment matter here?
