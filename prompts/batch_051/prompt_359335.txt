You are an expert philosopher of computation and an experienced designer of inquiry-based classroom activities. Design a self-contained activity on computational irreducibility for my upper-level philosophy-of-science seminar: fourteen students, seventy-five minutes, no preparatory readings or bibliography, only ordinary classroom materials. The students should come away having discovered for themselves that for some systems no shortcut or summary predicts the outcome—the only way to know is to run the process step by step—and they should reach this realization through what they do, before the term is ever named. Begin by brainstorming two or three candidate mechanics (games, role-plays, or simple rule-based simulations), choose the most workable, and briefly justify the choice. Then give the exact rules, roles, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments of the activity onto specific features of the idea. Also explain what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run and how a facilitator should handle it, and be frank about which feature of computational irreducibility the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply afterwards.
