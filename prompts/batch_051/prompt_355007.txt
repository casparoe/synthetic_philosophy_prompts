You are a professional philosopher working on collective intentionality and the analysis of joint action. Think through the question slowly; begin by brainstorming candidate criteria a definition might use, and only then commit to a specific proposal.

Provide a working definition of acting together — also called joint action — that correctly classifies the following cases. Some are meant to be paradigm instances, some clearly not instances, and several are meant to be hard.

1. Two strangers independently notice a stalled car in an intersection and, without communicating, begin pushing it in the same direction at the same time; each is pushing on her own, but their efforts combine.
2. Two people carry a heavy table up a narrow staircase after a brief exchange in which they agree who will take which side and that they will tilt it at the landing; each adjusts to the other as they go.
3. A football team executes a rehearsed set piece during a match.
4. A crowd panics and flees through a single exit.
5. A flash mob assembles via social media; dozens of people who do not know one another arrive at an agreed time and perform a choreographed dance.
6. A toddler helps a parent bake, and the parent lets the toddler stir, even though the stirring goes in the wrong direction and makes the task harder.
7. Two soldiers carry a stretcher under an officer's orders; each would not have done it but for the command.
8. A corporation's policy, implemented by many employees over years, results in a polluted river, even though no single employee plans or fully knows the overall pattern.
9. Two people slow-dance together without a plan, continuously adjusting to each other's movements.
10. A buyer and a seller complete a market exchange, each pursuing their own advantage while depending on the other to get what they want.
11. A therapist and a client conduct a therapy session; they have different roles, different goals, and unequal authority.
12. Two roommates each decide on their own to clean the kitchen at seven in the evening; they notice each other while cleaning but do not coordinate or adjust what they do.
13. A human and a dog run an agility course together, the dog reading the human's gestures and the human reading the dog's pace.

For context: I am confident that (2), (3), and (9) are cases of acting together, and that (4) and (12) are not. My intuitions are genuinely unsettled on (5), (6), (7), (8), (11), and (13). I lean toward counting (1) as not joint and (10) as at best a limiting case.

Now do the following. Propose a definition of acting together. Run it through all thirteen cases, saying for each whether the definition counts it as joint or not and whether that verdict matches your pre-theoretical judgment. Where the definition gives a wrong answer, no determinate answer, or a surprising one, say whether you will revise the definition or defend the verdict instead. Then tell me which classification you are least secure about and why.

In your answer, make clear which candidate criteria you are weighing — for example, a shared goal or shared intention, common knowledge of that goal, mutual responsiveness, meshing of subplans, interdependence of roles, an acknowledged first-person plural standpoint, authority relations, and whether each participant is necessary for the outcome. Also say whether you think acting together is all-or-nothing or comes in degrees, and what the definition is ultimately for: grounding shared responsibility, distinguishing group agents from mere aggregates, predicting coordination, or something else. Do not treat this as a survey question; I want a defended definition, not a literature review.
