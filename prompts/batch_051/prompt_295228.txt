I got pulled into a hallway argument after a panel I was covering on the future of political parties, and I'm still trying to sort out who was right. Both of them had read some Simone Weil, who thought political parties should simply be abolished. (1) Marta said Weil's core point is structural: a party exists to grow and win, so whenever the party's interest and the truth conflict, the member is under standing pressure to choose the party. That's true of every party, even nice ones. (2) Diego said that's overgeneralized; a modern party often is just a loose coalition that coordinates votes, and members can publicly disagree with it on issues they care about. Weil was writing about mass parties whose discipline swallowed the whole person. (3) Marta came back: even loose parties have a brand, and members who publicly break with the brand get deselected or primaried; the pressure is real, it just works through reputation rather than command, which makes it less visible, not less powerful. (4) Diego said destroy parties and what you get is either atomized media politics or rule by whoever has the deepest campaign pockets, both worse for truth than a party. (5) Marta answered that this shows parties may be a necessary evil, which is a different thing then saying Weil's diagnosis misses; and anyway her point was about what parties do to the minds inside them, not a prediction about what replaces them. Please go through the exchange move by move: who is right about which specific point, what the actual disagreement hangs on, and what, if anything, both of them are missing.
