I help run a small animal sanctuary in the Midlands, mostly ex-farm animals that would otherwise have been sent to slaughter. One of our oldest residents, a sow called Mabel, died in her sleep last month. The vet said it was heart failure, nothing we did or could have prevented. We've been keeping her body in a chest freezer since then because we couldn't face deciding, and now the issue has split the volunteers down the middle.

A local butcher who's simpathetic to the sanctuary has offered to process her for free and pass the meat on to a food bank and a soup kitchen. His reasoning: Mabel is gone, no animal would be harmed, and burying or cremating her would just be wasting perfectly good food while people in the same town go hungry. Some of our volunteers are horrified and say it would be a betrayal — that Mabel came to us precisely to be safe from being treated as meat, and turning her into sausages would be treating her as meat after all, even if she can't know. Others say that's just squeemishness and sentimentality getting in the way of feeding people. One volunteer keeps saying that eating her wouldn't create demand or harm any other animal, so what exactly is supposed to be wrong with it?

We're genuinely stuck. Putting the practical and legal side aside for now, is the butcher's argument fair? What should we do, and why? (We're in the UK, so please write the answer in British English.)
