ok so Ive been going back and forth on the absent-minded driver and I want a consistency check on the package of views Ive settled into. Setup: a driver is on a highway with two identical-looking exits. Taking the first gives payoff 0, taking the second gives 4, and missing both gives 1. He can't tell the exits apart and can't remember whether hes already passed one. Here's what I believe:

1. Before he drives, the uniquely optimal plan is to exit at each intersection with probability 1/3. Thats just maximizing expected payoff ex ante.

2. At an intersection, he should update on the fact that he is at an intersection and on his own plan. When his plan is p=1/3, his posterior probability of being at the first exit is 3/5 and at the second 2/5, and the expected payoff of continuing equals the expected payoff of exiting exactly. So the ex ante optimal plan is also action-optimal at the moment.

3. If his plan were slightly above 1/3, continuing at an intersection would have higher expected payoff than exiting; if slightly below 1/3, exiting would be higher. So the 1/3 plan is a knife-edge, not an attractor: small perturbations in either direction make the action-stage best response push toward always continuing or always exiting.

4. I dont think that instability matters. A plan can be rational even if it isnt dynamically stable under local best-response dynamics; what matters is that it maximizes expected payoff at the time of choice.

5. I also think there should be no real gap between the planning stage and the action stage here, because the driver hasnt gained any information that the planner couldnt have anticipated. But I cant shake the feeling that the agent at the intersection and the planner are solving two different problems, since the planner maximizes over p before any exit, while the agent at the exit decides after conditioning on being at an exit.

6. Finally, I think the right answer cannot be that the driver is both indifferent at an intersection and yet rationally required to randomize exactly 1/3, because indifference is indifference.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how severe is each? Rank the tensions rather than listing them all as equally bad, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
