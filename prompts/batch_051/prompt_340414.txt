I serve on my city's Regulatory Reform Commission, which must vote next month on a two-year pilot replacing mandatory annual health inspections for low-risk food businesses with a self-certification regime modeled on high-trust Nordic practice: operators would file quarterly checklists, inspections would be triggered only by complaints or sentinel events, and random audits would run at roughly one-fifth the current frequency. Proponents argue this treats citizens as trustworthy rather than suspect, redirects scarce inspectors to the riskiest sites, and matches systems that work in high-trust societies; opponents reply that our jurisdiction's lower social trust and history of noncompliance make routine inspection the only credible deterrent, and that the pilot imports an attitude that does not fit local conditions. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote: recommend whether to approve the pilot, with any safeguards and a time frame, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side rather than merely restating the two positions.
