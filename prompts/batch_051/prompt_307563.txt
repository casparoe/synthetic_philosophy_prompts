I run comms for a regional public health agency. During outbreaks I have to sign off on every declarative sentence that goes out at the daily 2pm briefing — 'the vaccine cuts transmission', 'the outbreak is contained', 'asymptomatic people rarely spread it' — and we keep fighting about what we can say flat versus what has to be wrapped in 'evidence suggests'. I want a reusable checklist my staff can actually run in about fifteen minutes, no philsophy background, that tells them whether a sentence can be asserted outright or has to be hedged, grounded in whatever the norms-of-assertion debate has actually established. The checklist should handle the hard cases: the claim is true but the evidence is weak; the officer knows it but can't easily prove it to a journalist; the officer believes it on good but not decisive evidence. Then run it on three examples: (1) asserting 'masks reduce transmission' from one large observational study; (2) asserting vaccine efficacy from a single big RCT; (3) a true claim picked up secondhand from a neighbouring agency that hasn't been verified. Flag where the procedure is just a judgement call dressed up as a checklist, and give me at least one case where it returns an answer we won't like. British English please.
