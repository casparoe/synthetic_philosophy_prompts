I am currently taking a course in philosophy of physics, and we have been working through the debate over whether frameworks such as string theory or the multiverse can be rationally believed in advance of direct experimental confirmation. I would like to ask you to evaluate a set of proposed non-empirical considerations so that I can check my own sense of how much weight each should carry.

Below are six considerations that defenders of string theory or multiverse cosmology sometimes offer as reasons to assign at least some credence to a framework even while direct empirical support is lacking. Please sort them into three tiers: 'should carry substantial weight', 'should carry some weight', and 'should carry little or no weight'. For each consideration, give one or two sentences explaining why it lands in its tier. If you think a consideration should count against the framework rather than for it, place it in the bottom tier and say so explicitly; multiple considerations may share a tier.

The considerations:

1. Absence of comparably developed rivals: no other current approach unifies quantum field theory with gravity as comprehensively, so rejecting this framework would leave the field without a comparable positive research programme.

2. Unexpected explanatory contact: tools and structures developed mainly for internal consistency, such as gauge/gravity dualities or the counting of black-hole microstates, later turned out to reproduce or illuminate pieces of already established physics that they were not designed to explain.

3. Historical meta-induction: past theories that were favoured partly on non-empirical grounds such as unification and simplicity later turned out to be empirically successful, so similar non-empirical grounds deserve some weight now.

4. Internal mathematical coherence: the framework tames the infinities that appear in ordinary perturbative quantum gravity and yields well-defined finite quantities.

5. Landscape and multiverse: the framework naturally produces an enormous landscape of low-energy vacua; some take this to explain apparent fine-tuning, while others take it to rob the framework of falsifiability.

6. Mathematical beauty: many practitioners judge the underlying mathematics to be unusually deep, elegant, or constrained.

Finally, suppose a rational physicist were to form an overall credence in string theory or a multiverse framework on the basis of everything available. Roughly what fraction of that credence could legitimately be contributed by non-empirical considerations of the kind listed above, as opposed to direct empirical support? Please give a number between 0 and 1 and explain it in two or three sentences.
