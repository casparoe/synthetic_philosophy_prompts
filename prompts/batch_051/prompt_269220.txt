I'm giving a brown-bag talk in two weeks to our institute's climate group, and my real goal is just to survive the Q&A. My claim is going to be that the way the field routinely treats the spread across CMIP-class multi-model ensembles as if it were a probability distribution over future climate is a category mistake. A multi-model ensemble isn't a sample from anything well-defined: the models aren't independent draws, their errors are heavily shared, and the ensemble mean isn't a best estimate so much as an artifact of who happened to show up. I'm even going to say that perturbed-parameter ensembles within a single model aren't probability distributions either, because the parameter priors and the choice of which processes get perturbed are themselves judgment calls, not frequencies. The room will be mostly model developers who contribute runs to CMIP and are used to reporting the multi-model mean plus spread, plus two statisticians who keep trying to get everyone to do exactly the kind of formal Bayesian model-averaging I'm partly attacking. I don't need help with the talk; I need a prep sheet for after it: the objections they'll actually raise, in roughly the order they'll come and in their vocabulary, which ones I should concede up front, which ones I should fight, a short reply to each with the spot where that reply is soft, and the question I'm praying nobody thinks to ask.
