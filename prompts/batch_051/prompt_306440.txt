Lately I've been going back and forth on whether there's a real duty to be legible to other people, and I want you to help me figure out wich side should do the yielding. The case for the duty goes like this. I don't even mean honesty about particular claims; I mean something closer to the zero of the scale—being graspable at all. In any interaction where someone else has to act on what I believe or intend, opacity isn't neutral: it hands them a prediction problem. If cooperation already costs people enough, and I refuse to make my relevant states readable (where I stand, how uncertain I am, whether I still take myself to be committed), I'm quietly taxing everyone who has to coordinate with me. Deliberate unintelligibility starts to look like free-riding, and the default should be legibility, much as promising makes it wrong to leave someone guessing whether you still regard yourself as bound.

Against it, I have an intuition I can't shake: there's a private interior nobody has a claim on, and being required to be readable would flatten the very thing that makes disclosure matter. If you can infer my real attitude without my having offered it, you haven't been trusted; you've done an end run around my privacy. I also suspect a duty of legibility would turn into a warrant for making people explain themselves when they owe the questioner nothing.

I don't want a list of compromise positions. I want to know what would have to be true for the intuition to be dropped as a disguised entitlement (or as a hangover from a view of persons as self-owning fortresses), and what would have to be true for the theory to admit a boundary where it stops being about coordination and becomes about surveillance. In particular, help me test the asymmetry I keep reaching for: maybe you owe legibility about your actions and commitments but not about your doubts, fantasies, or affections. Is that a principled line, or just the intuition sneaking back in? And if the right answer turns out to be that legibility is a virtue but not a duty, show me that's a real distinction and not a refusal of the initial argument. I can live with narrowing; I can't live with a stalemate dressed up as a synthesis.
