I write literary fiction and I keep running into the same problem: a character says a slur, or my narrator renders one in free indirect discourse, and I can't tell whether what I've written reads as the character's ugliness or as me (or the narrator) signing on to it. Avoiding slurs entirely isnt an option — some of my characters are bigots, some are in-group members reclaiming a word, and writing around all of that feels like evasion or bowdlerizing. I want a reusable procedure, a checklist in words with questions in order, grounded in what philosophers of language actually know about slurs (derogatory force vs. truth-conditional content, use vs. mention, speaker vs. narrator distance, in-group vs. out-group uses, reclamation), translated into plain terms with the distinctions that genuinely matter kept and the rest dropped, that I can run on any slurring line in about twenty minutes. It should say where each step stops with "rewrite or cut," where it hands the decision to a sensitivity reader or my editor because it's no longer a factual call, and where the procedure will misfire. Then run it on three cases: an out-group narrator quoting an in-group character's slur in dialogue; a non-bigoted narrator using a slur in free indirect discourse to render a bigoted character's thoughts; and an in-group character using a reclaimed slur toward another in-group member. Make sure at least one case comes out with an answer I won't like. Hard limit: keep the whole thing under 850 words.
