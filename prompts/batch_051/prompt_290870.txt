Morgan's canon says, roughly, that we shouldn't explain an animal's behavior by appeal to a higher mental faculty if a lower one can explain it. What strikes me is how one-sided the rule is: you don't see a reverse canon with anything like the same standing, even though over-mentalizing and under-mentalizing are both just ways of getting the animal wrong. Give me the strongest defense you can of treating those two errors so differently—maybe it's about which hypotheses are simpler, which are easier to test, or the different costs of each mistake—and then tell me honestly whether that defense survives contact with crows, octopuses, or a dog that sure looks guilty.
