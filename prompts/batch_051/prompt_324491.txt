I teach an intro-to-proofs and real analysis sequence, and I grade somewhere around 50 or 60 proof-homework submissions most weeks. Here's the problem: a student can turn in a completely correct proof by having memorized the moves — the epsilon-delta scaffold, the induction setup, the contrapositive, the "choose delta = min(1, epsilon/...)" reflex — without being able to say why the argument works, or what would make it fail. I want to stop being fooled by that. Please write me a reusable procedure I can actually run, mostly in 10-minute oral check-ins and occassionally as a written follow-up question, to decide whether a given student understands a proof rather than just reproduces it. I have to defend any "does not understand" verdict to the course coordinator afterwards, so each step should produce something I can write down.

The procedure should be a sequence of question-types or tests to run in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a "does not understand" verdict, and where it has to hand the verdict over to my judgment. Treat the core principle as something like: understanding a proof means being able to see which features of the argument are load-bearing, vary them without the proof collapsing, and repair or diagnose a broken version. I want you to work that principle into the procedure explicitly rather than just quizzing students on statements of theorems.

Then run the procedure on two or three concrete cases from intro analysis — for instance, a student who can reproduce the proof that a continuous function on a closed interval is bounded, a student who can reproduce the standard proof that sqrt(2) is irrational, and a student who can reproduce the epsilon-delta proof that the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits. I especially want one case where the procedure comes back "does not understand" even though the written proof is flawless and the student can recite every step — tell me what the procedure would do there and why I should trust it over the flawless writeup. Also tell me plainly what this procedure cannot detect, which steps are really measuring something else (nerves, English fluency, how much sleep the student got), and how the procedure should handle a strong student who just gives terse answers.
