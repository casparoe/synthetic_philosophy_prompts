The standard picture treats the rule of rescue and QALY-maximization as rivals: rescue spends heavily on an identified patient while leaving many statistical beneficiaries worse off. I suspect the rivalry is an artifact of running together an ex post standpoint, in which one evaluates a rescue decision once the patient is identified, with an ex ante standpoint, in which one chooses health-system rules behind a veil of ignorance and a rescue clause may simply be one rational term of the bargain. The unsettled question would then be which standpoint is authoritative for evaluating triage rules, and why. Write the proposal as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the question stated precisely; (2) why the existing positions leave it open; (3) the method and why a conceptual rather than empirical approach fits; (4) what a successful answer would look like and what would count as the project failing; (5) the main risk that the project collapses into a merely terminological point and how to avoid it. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add those myself.
