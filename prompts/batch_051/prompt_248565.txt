I have been reading Orwell on political language on my own, outside any formal study, and I would like a careful case analysis rather than a summary of his views. Take the way official language around state violence changed after the attacks of 11 September 2001. By around 2004, American officials had settled into phrases such as enhanced interrogation techniques, for practices that in earlier decades would simply have been called torture, and extraordinary rendition, for transferring a detainee to another country where he was likely to be mistreated. I want you to analyse this episode through Orwell's account of the relation between language and political thought, and to do so in both directions. First, work through his specific mechanisms as precisely as you can: euphemism; the replacement of concrete verbs by abstract nouns; the passive constructions that leave agents unnamed; ready-made phrases that can be uttered without the speaker forming a clear image of what they describe; and the wider claim that a decayed language is both a symptom of political decay and a cause of further decay. For each mechanism, say whether it actually did the work in this episode and where it fails to capture what happened, rather than assuming that every feature of the new vocabulary is an instance of it. Second, turn the analysis around: what does this episode show about Orwell's position? Does it confirm his pessimism, or does it suggest that he underestimated how quickly a phrase can become normal, overestimated what plain speech could achieve, or got the direction of influence wrong in places? Do not treat this as a legal or intelligence-history question; I know those details. I want the conceptual analysis. Please write the answer in British English.
