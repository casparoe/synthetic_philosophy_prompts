I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background; I got pulled into this while designing a category and tagging layer for an application, and a colleague argued that the whole idea of a stable category scheme is built on a mistake. I cannot locate the fault in the reasoning. The argument runs as follows.

(1) The definitional theory of concepts — the idea that each concept is given by necessary and sufficient conditions for membership — is standardly regarded as having failed; the usual candidate definition of bachelor as an unmarried adult male has well-known counterexamples, and no proposed definition of chair has survived contact with actual chairs.

(2) The main alternative accounts say that we categorize by similarity to a prototype (a kind of average or ideal instance), by similarity to stored exemplars (particular instances we remember), or by coherence with a background theory about why the features cluster.

(3) On any of those accounts, whether something counts as an X depends on which features get weighted, which exemplars get retrieved, or which theory is active.

(4) Those factors shift with context and task. A tomato is a fruit in botany and a vegetable in the kitchen. Pluto was a planet under one classification and not under another. A whale belonged with the fish in older schemes and with the mammals in later ones.

(5) If membership moves with context like that, then for ordinary categories there is no context-independent fact about whether something is an X.

(6) But a category with no context-independent facts about its membership has no stable content of its own; the word is simply equivocal across contexts, and each application is only a projection from the context at hand.

(7) If ordinary categories are like that, then the stable communication, legal categories, and scientific taxonomies we rely on are an illusion.

(8) They are not an illusion.

So one of (1) through (8) must go. Which step, or transition between steps, do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost for the rest of the view? I would most like to keep (2) and (8), but if they cannot both be kept, I want to know which one has to be paid for. Also, please separate what is actually settled in this area from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
