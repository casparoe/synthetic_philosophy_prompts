Write the joint statement that a committed institutionalist and a committed culturalist about the Nordic model could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The two parties agree on the descriptive surface: the Nordic countries combine high tax-to-GDP ratios, unusually high tax morale and tax compliance, low perceived corruption, broad popular support for redistribution, compressed wage structures, and unusually high generalized and institutional trust. Their dispute concerns the explanatory order. The institutionalist maintains that a transportable institutional bundle — universalist rather than means-tested benefits, high-quality public services that the middle class relies on, public financing of child and elder care, coordinated collective bargaining, and tax systems with broad bases and visible services in return — generates trust, compliance, and support for redistribution as feedback effects. The culturalist maintains that this bundle presupposes conditions that cannot be engineered merely by adopting the institutions themselves: deep interpersonal trust, an internalized norm of contributing one's fair share, long-run state capacity, a broadly Lutheran egalitarian ethic, and substantial ethnic and religious homogeneity. Without those conditions, the culturalist predicts that transplanted institutions will produce tax evasion, capture, middle-class exit, welfare chauvinism, or electoral backlash.

The document should contain three parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims both sides can sign, covering the empirical surface, what would count as having successfully exported the model, and a clear statement of which parts of their past disagreement were merely verbal. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the question, the institutionalist position phrased as the institutionalist would accept, the culturalist position phrased as the culturalist would accept, and the crux — the narrower sub-question whose resolution would move at least one side. Third, for each crux, at least one concrete case, dataset, or intervention that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction of the outcome recorded in advance. Address at least these cruxes: the causal direction between generalized trust and institutional quality; whether universalist programs can be introduced in low-trust settings or require prior trust in service delivery; whether tax morale travels with institutions or only as part of a broader normative package; and whether rising ethnic diversity weakens support for redistribution directly or only under specific institutional choices about conditionality and exclusion. The formulations of the two positions should be neither vacuous nor slanted: each side should prefer its own wording over the other's, and each should concede that the other's wording is a fair statement of the other's view. Do not referee the dispute; produce the document they could both sign, with the residual disagreement made exact.
