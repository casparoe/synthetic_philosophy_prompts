For a paper on the paradox of tragedy, construct me a thought experiment that separates three standard explanations of why we seek out sad art. The case should be deliberately stripped of the features those explanations rely on: it must involve an ordinary, non-artistic, non-fictional situation in which a person knowingly seeks out an episode of genuine sadness that stays aversive moment to moment, offers no sense of control or mastery, and is followed by no relief or catharsis — yet she would choose the experience again if given the chance. After the scenario, state the verdict that a control/distance account, a catharsis account, and a meta-response account (on which the negative emotional state is itself valued as part of the whole experience) would each give, and make clear why the case favors the third. Finish by identifying the single structural feature of the scenario that does the work, saying what the case collapses into if that feature is removed, and explaining how a defender of the control/distance account would try to redescribe the case.
