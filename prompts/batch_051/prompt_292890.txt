I chair the research ethics board of a university-based institute for autonomous systems. Before retiring I worked as a physical scientist, and I have only recently begun reading philosophy seriously, so I want the reasoning laid out plainly but not simplistically. A proposal before us asks to use inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) on a large corpus of naturalistic driving data to infer the underlying reward functions of human drivers. The investigators argue that the inferred reward functions will reveal what drivers truly value—trade-offs between speed and caution, courtesy toward pedestrians, attitudes toward rule-compliance—so that autonomous vehicles can be trained to better mesh with human traffic. The board is split. One side says the proposal is methodologically sound in principle: any consistent behavior pattern can be represented as optimizing some reward given the right state space and dynamics, and the resulting model can be tested by how well it predicts out-of-sample behavior. The other side says the study is scientifically confused: human driving is partly performative, bounded, and shaped by social norms, and IRL will merely recover a rationalized description of observed choices, not a latent preference that existed before or independently of those choices. They invoke the difference between revealed preference and true preference, and they argue that an off-the-shelf IRL algorithm will mistake situational constraints for values. There is also disagreement about whether the data can support the study’s stated aim: the dataset was collected under conditions where drivers knew or could infer they were being recorded, so the behavior may partly be aimed at an imagined observer. Some members think this is a harmless modeling choice; others think it biases the inference in a way no amount of prediction accuracy can fix. Draft the board’s decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with an actionable bottom line and reasons the investigators could actually respond to. Include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
