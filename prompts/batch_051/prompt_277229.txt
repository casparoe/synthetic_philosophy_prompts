I've been chewing on this one: if I make a bet where I either double my money or loose half of it, 50/50 each, then on average I come out 25% ahead, so its a great bet by the usual logic. But if I actually shove my whole bankroll in over and over, I'm pretty sure I eventually go broke, because losing half and then doubling just gets me back to where I was, while the average is being pulled up by all those lucky histories where I double a bunch of times in a row. Can you make an argument that for a repeated bet like this, the number that matters isn't the average over all possible paths, but the path that a single person's wealth actually tends to follow over time? And that expected value is only the right guide for one-shot bets, not for compounding ones?
