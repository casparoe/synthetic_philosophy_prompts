I run a philosophy club for undergrads, usually around 16 students, and our sessions are 75 minutes. I've been trying to design a game around what I've seen called the Platonia dilemma or Luring Lottery: a group of people are each deciding whether to claim a prize, the prize only pays out if exactly one person claims it, if zero or more than one claim nobody gets anything, maybe with a small penalty for claiming too many. I don't want to give any lecture up front, and I don't want the name mentioned until the end. Ideally the activity has real but small stakes, like snacks or extra-credit tokens, and the rules are simple enough to explain in five minutes. I tried a version where people could just write their name on a card, but it fell flat because nobody took the zero-stakes version seriously. What I want them to walk away seeing is the weird tension between thinking "if everyone else refrains, I should claim" and "if everyone reasons the same way, everyone claims and nobody wins"—and why the standard game-theory answer can feel both right and wrong. Could you give me a runnable design: the setup, exact rules and payoffs, a timeline for the 75 minutes, how to run the debrief so it maps what happened back onto the underlying idea, and what will predictably go wrong? Also be honest about which part of the real dilemma can't be reproduced in a classroom and has to be supplied by me. Keep it under 600 words.
