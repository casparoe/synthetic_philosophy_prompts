Since retiring I have been working through the standard interpretations of probability, and I would like you to build me a case to order. Construct a thought experiment in which a frequentist, using the long-run frequency of a reference class, and a subjectivist, using a degree of belief grounded in the same record, assign exactly the same non-trivial probability (strictly between 0 and 1) to a single token event, while a single-case propensity theorist maintains that the token event itself has only propensity 0 or propensity 1 and no intermediate propensity. Constraints: an ordinary macro-scale mechanism, fully deterministic at the level that matters, with sensitive dependence on its initial conditions and a stable long-run frequency; no quantum mechanics, no infinite sequences, no exotic physics, and no perfect predictors; and the token event must be a single output of the mechanism, not a sequence or a class. After describing the mechanism, walk through how each of the three interpretations reasons from the same description of the device and its record, then isolate the structural feature that makes the propensity theorist part company with the other two, and say whether replacing the deterministic element at the point where the outcome is fixed with a genuinely stochastic process would bring all three into agreement. Close with an honest note on where your design is most fragile: how would a single-case propensity theorist who attaches propensities to the whole chance setup rather than to the token try to recapture the intermediate value, and what consideration would lead you to abandon the claim that your case separates the interpretations.
