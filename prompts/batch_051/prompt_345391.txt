Write a dialogue between two equally sharp consequentialists, Ada and Ben, about whether cluelessness is a fatal problem for the view. Ada holds that it is: she argues that once you trace the effects of an ordinary action far enough, the outcome set includes which particular people will exist and how history unfolds, and these things are sensitive to tiny causes in ways we cant model, so there is no non-arbitrary probability distribution over the consequences that actually matter — expected-value reasoning isnt just hard, its empty. Ben holds that this overstates what consequentialism needs: we can compare foreseen, decision-relevant consequences (relieving a headache now, reducing child mortality by a known amount) while treating the unmodelable far future as a background lottery that we have no reason to suppose is correlated with our specific choice; if the far future cancels or is the same in expectation across options, it drops out of the comparison, and if it isnt, the burden is on the skeptic to show we should let a completely unconstrained possibility swamp known goods. Make them respond to each other's actual points, not talk past each other. Have them really tangle over the claim that unknown far-future effects cancel: is that a neutral decision-theoretic move or a disguised assumption? Concede small points on both sides. End with the strongest unresolved objection each has to the other's position rather than a tidy resolution.
