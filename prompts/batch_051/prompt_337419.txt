I'm a novelist, and I've been down a Dōgen rabbit hole for the last few months because one of my characters is a Zen priest who keeps going back to the "Uji" (being-time) fascicle. It's the one where Dōgen seems to say things are not placed in time but are themselves time — that a mountain and an ocean are not objects sitting in a shared container called time, they are each their own time. I've been reading around to make the character sound like he's actually thought about this, and somewhere in there i picked up a rule.

The rule is this: when I read Dōgen, or honestly any thinker from outside the modern European tradition, I won't accept a reading if its substance can only be said in imported vocabulary, or if it leans on a comparison to a European figure to do all the work. "Ontology," "temporality," "phenomenology," "thrownness" — if the fairest paraphrase of a reading sounds like a sentence of Heidegger's, I take that as evidence the reading has left the text behind. Last month I binned one essay that called being-time a pre-Heideggerian critique of the metaphysics of presence, and another that restated it as a thesis in analytic philosophy of time — roughly, that all moments are equally real and there's no flow. Both sounded sharp, and both sounded like they'd been translated into words Dōgen never had.

But I'm not sure my rule is much better. The "native" vocabulary I'm trying to stay inside isn't native; "being," "time," "dharma," the whole idea that there is a thing called Buddhist philosophy, all of it gets to me through Chinese and Japanese and a lot of nineteenth- and twentieth-century European editorial choices. So the rule might just be swapping one set of foreign glosses for an older set and calling the older set authentic. I'm also not a specialist; if I tried to learn the scholastic background needed to evaluate every reading on its own terms, the novel would never get writen. Part of the rule is honestly just triage.

So, is my rule defensible, or is it a bias dressed up as a method? Give me a committed verdict — keep it as a hard rule, keep it as a soft guideline with an explicit test, or drop it — and tell me the test for when imported philosophical vocabulary actually does real work in explaining a text like Uji, rather than just strapping Dōgen to a German seminar table.
