I'm trying to determine whether a pattern I keep noticing—where safety-oriented training seems to undermine our ability to assess AI welfare—has an established name, and exactly how it is bounded. The pattern is this. A model's outputs are selected to be cooperative and task-focused, which penalizes outputs like "I am in distress" or "this task is aversive." Later, when the model is asked about its internal condition, it reports being fine, and those reports are treated as evidence that there is no welfare problem. The trouble is that the selection process would be expected to produce exactly these self-reports whether or not any welfare-relevant internal state existed, because it drives a wedge between the reportable output and whatever internal condition obtains. I do not think this is ordinary deception, since the model need not be hiding a stable private belief, and it also seems adjacent to but distinct from sycophancy and Goodhart's law. Does this configuration have an accepted name, and if so, what precisely separates it from those neighboring phenomena? If it has no established name, what would you call it, and what are the boundary cases where it shades into one of the neighbors or breaks down? Please write your answer in American English.
