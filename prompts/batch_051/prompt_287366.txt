I'm grading a third-year philosophy exam on rule-following and I'd like a detailed second opinion on one answer. The question I set was roughly: Explain the rule-following paradox Kripke finds in Wittgenstein, say why the dispositionalist response fails, and then state what Kripke's skeptical solution actually claims and whether it amounts to a communitarian view of meaning. The student wrote:

"Kripke's skeptic begins from the point that all my previous uses of '+' are finite, and any finite set of sums is compatible with my having meant plus and also with my having meant 'quus', which is just addition up to some enormous bound and then something different (say always 5). Since no introspective state, no explicitly formulated rule, and no fact about my past behavior can pick out one of these over the other — any rule can itself be interpreted in a deviant way — there seems to be no fact about me that constitutes my meaning plus rather than quus. The dispositionalist replies that I meant plus because I am disposed to answer '125' to '68+57' and to go on in the right way generally. Kripke rejects this on two grounds. One is that my dispositions are finite and gappy: I have no determinate disposition about sums far beyond anything I can handle, whereas meaning addition determines answers for all pairs. The other is that dispositions are descriptive, not prescriptive: they report what I would in fact do, not what I ought to do. If a quirk made me systematically disposed to give wrong sums, the dispositionalist would have to say those wrong sums are what I meant, which shows the view cannot make sense of error. So Kripke takes the skeptic's negative conclusion and gives a skeptical solution: we cannot state truth-conditions for meaning ascriptions, only assertability conditions. On that solution, I am entitled to say someone means plus when her use agrees with how her community would go on with '+'. And that is what meaning comes to: to mean addition just is to be in the right sort of agreement with one's linguistic community — meaning is constituted by communal practice, not by any private fact."

Give me a grade out of 20 and then walk through the answer point by point, saying where it gets things right, where it blurs distinctions, and whether the closing paragraph is a fair representation of the skeptical solution or a misreading.
