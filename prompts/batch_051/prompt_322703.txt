I keep noticing a pattern in how people talk about things being sacred, and I cant tell if its one concept or three. A few examples. When someone puts a crucifix in a jar of urine, the uproar uses blasphemy and sacrilege almost interchangeably, even though the act isn't saying anything about God — it's doing something to an object. When someone tweets rude things about a prophet, the charge is blasphemy again, but now it's about speech, not an object. And when a comedian jokes about a school shooting a week after it happened, people say he's desecrating the memory of the dead — no religion anywhere in sight, but the logic is weirdly the same: there's a line, you crossed it, and the crossing isn't just offensive, it registers as a kind of violation.

What I'm after is the third thing, or maybe the whole family. Is there an established name for the phenomenon where something nonreligious gets treated as if it were sacred in the strong sense — set apart, protected by a boundary, not to be profaned — and people respond to its violation as desecration even when they'd deny holding any religious belief and might even dislike the word sacred as self-important? And is it actually the same structure as religious sacrilege, or just a borrowed metaphor? I'm also fuzzy on where blasphemy ends and sacrilege begins, and whether the third thing has a name of its own or has to be coined. I don't just want a label; I want to know exactly what the concept covers, where its edges are (a burnt flag, a desecrated grave, a joke about a genocide, someone smashing a consumer object that a fandom treats as treasured), and which bits of this are settled distinctions and which are live fights among people who study it. Dont present a contested reading as the consensus — tell me where reasonable, informed people would part ways.
