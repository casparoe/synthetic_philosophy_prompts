In retirement, after a long career in experimental physics, I have begun reading philosophy in earnest and have recently been drawn into the philosophy of time. I have written down my present convictions as a numbered list, in the hope that a careful reader can check them for hidden inconsistencies. I would be grateful for an audit of the list.

1. I accept special relativity as physics. Its empirical content is not, for me, in question: the Lorentz transformations, the invariant interval, and the fact that no measurement can reveal absolute rest or absolute simultaneity.

2. I am a presentist. Only what is present exists; the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist. I regard temporal becoming as objective, rather than as a feature of how we happen to perceive a static world.

3. I do not think existence can be relative to a standpoint or a frame. An event cannot be real for one observer and unreal for another; if it exists, it exists simpliciter. Presentism therefore requires a single, global present — a worldwide now.

4. To keep (2) and (3) consistent with (1), I have come to accept a physically real but observationally inaccessible privileged foliation of Minkowski spacetime into successive global nows. This is closer to Lorentz than to Einstein: the detectable content of the theory remains fully relativistic, but the world singles out a hidden frame, and only that frame's simultaneity relation marks what is present.

5. For that reason the Rietdijk–Putnam argument does not seem to me to touch my position. As I understand it, that argument starts from the frame-relativity of simultaneity and, by insisting that reality itself cannot be frame-relative, is driven to the conclusion that all events in spacetime are equally real — the block universe. But it reaches that conclusion only by treating every frame's simultaneity relation as a guide to what exists. In the Lorentzian picture I am adopting, that premise fails: the non-privileged frames' simultaneity surfaces are coordinate artifacts, and the chaining of their nows has no ontological force.

6. I also bring with me a methodological principle from my years in the laboratory: if two descriptions of a system are equivalent with respect to everything that could possibly be observed, I take them to be the same description. A physical structure that is in principle undetectable — one that could make no difference to any measurement, even an ideal one — is not, as I see it, part of the furniture of the world.

7. Finally, I hold that the flow of time is a genuine process and not merely a static ordering. The present really does advance from past to future at a definite rate. Any position that makes all events co-equal and static would turn that advance into an illusion, and I cannot see how to accept that.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which pairs merely appear to be so. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to give, and what the least costly repair would be. Rank the tensions by severity, and close by saying which single revision of the list would do the most to make the whole position coherent, and what that revision would cost.
