I'm on the charter review commission in a smallish old mill town — about 40,000 people, roughly a fifth of them non-citizen residents, many here for over a decade, working in the plants and with kids in the local schools. A working group has just put foward a proposal to let non-citizen residents vote in municipal elections only: mayor, council, school board, and local ballot measures, but nothing at the county, state, or federal level. A similar measure failed 58–42 two years ago, but the commission's membership has turned over and we've reopened it.

I need to draft the commission's short decision memo — recommend to the town council that they adopt the proposal, reject it, or adopt it with conditions, with a concrete timeline for any conditions (a 3-year residency requirement? school-board-only first? a sunset clause?). The real split on our commission isn't about the details; it's about who belongs to the electorate in the first place. One camp says anyone systematically subject to the town's ordinances, taxes, policing, and schools has a claim to participate. The other camp says that's backwards: a political community has to be constituted by citizens standing in a relation of equal, self-binding rule, and you can't use 'who's affected?' to decide who gets to decide. I'd like the memo to take both of those positions at their strongest, not as strawmen, and the losing side should still recognize their own reasoning in the final thing. A third commissioner keeps saying the whole exercise is circular — a body of citizens deciding whether non-citizens get to help choose bodies like this one — and I want the memo to answer that worry head-on instead of waving it off. The council votes in February, so keep it to something they can actually act on.
