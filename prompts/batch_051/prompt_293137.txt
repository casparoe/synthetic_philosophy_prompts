I keep seeing 'robust' applied to climate-model results as if it named one property, but I suspect it does at least two jobs: many models pointing the same way, and models that are not all much alike pointing the same way. Give me a working definition — or several, if the word needs splitting — of a 'robust finding' in that context, tested against these cases. For each, say whether the definition counts it robust, whether that verdict is acceptable or a reason to revise, and what the case shows about the gap between robustness and the confidence a decision-maker should have in a projection.

(a) All thirty models in a large archive give the same sign for a high-latitude warming signal, but most descend from a small number of shared code bases.
(b) Two fully independent models both produce a regional drying trend, while the rest of the archive disagrees on sign.
(c) A global precipitation change appears only because roughly half the models are positive and half negative, and the multi-model mean is slightly positive.
(d) A result is unchanged across every member of a perturbed-physics ensemble — one model run many times with different internal parameters — but no other model family has been tested for it.
(e) A projection appears only in the model with the best historical performance score.
(f) A cloud feedback has the same sign in every model, but its strength differs by a factor of four, and that difference matters for adaptation decisions.

If the word needs to be split, give the distinct definitions, say which cases move between them, and say which cases show that a robust result, so defined, is not automatically the one a user of a projection should weigh most heavily.
