I help run a casual philosophy meetup at a public library — usually 10 people show up, none of us have any formal philosophy training, and we meet for 75 minutes. I've gotten myself obsessed with something I heard called the presumptuous philosopher. As I understand it: suppose two theories of the universe both fit all the evidence we have, but theory A says there will eventually be something like a trillion conscious beings and theory B says there will be something like a billion. The presumptuous philosopher says you should be mostly convinced A is true, because under B there'd be far fewer beings and so you'd be much less likely to exist as one of them — or something in that neighborhood. Another way of looking at it says your own existence shouldn't tip the scales that way at all, because you could only ever find yourself existing no matter which theory is true.

I want a 75-minute game that makes people feel that disagreement before we put words on it, not something where I explain it afterwards and people nod along. We can use candy or coins for real stakes, and I'm fine with splitting people into groups or giving them roles. I want them to run into the thing themselves — the moment where somebody makes a huge bet on the trillion-person universe and somebody else thinks theyve lost their mind, and both can kind of see why.

Please give me the whole plan as a JSON object with these fields:

- "materials": all the physical stuff to prepare, like cards, envelopes, coins, candy, a whiteboard.
- "room_setup": how to arrange the space and any instructions to put up before anyone starts.
- "roles": the roles participants get assigned, with how many people per role and what each role knows or chooses.
- "rounds": a list where each round has "name", "minutes", "rules", "what_players_do", and "moment_to_watch_for" — the thing I should listen for that means the idea is biting.
- "scoring": exactly how candy/coins/points move and how the payoff exposes the split between two ways of reasoning about yourself as an observer, without using those words.
- "debrief": a list where each entry has "moment", "idea", and "question_to_ask" mapping something that happened in the game to part of the presumptuous philosopher argument.
- "likely_problems": what will predictably go wrong when real people run it and how to handle each one.
- "what_it_cant_reproduce": the honest note about which feature of the real philosophical problem the game has to fake or leave out.

One thing I specifically want covered in the debrief: the difference between reasoning as if you're a random sample of all the possible observers and reasoning as if your own existence just tells you that at least one observer exists. I'm pretty sure my meetup will split right down that line, and I dont want to be the one drawing it on the board first — I want the game to make that split visible.
