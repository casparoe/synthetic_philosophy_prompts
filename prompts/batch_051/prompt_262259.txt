I have been reading Robert Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument, and I am trying to work out what it would say about something Nozick never considered. As I understand the example, if everyone starts from a just distribution and then freely pays to watch Chamberlain play, the very unequal distribution that results is itself just, and no patterned principle of justice can be imposed on that result without forbidding the free exchanges that produced it. Nozick never saw the platform economy, in which companies build enormous wealth by collecting and monetizing users' posts, personal data, and behavioral traces. What would Nozick have said about a proposal to tax a share of those platform profits and pay it out as a dividend to the users whose activity generated the value? Would he see the tax as one more patterned redistribution of the kind the Chamberlain example was designed to condemn, or would he locate the problem earlier, in the original acquisition of the data, so that the just-transfer argument never gets going? Reason through his entitlement theory, including his account of original acquisition and rectification, and identify where his categories strain when applied to personal data.
