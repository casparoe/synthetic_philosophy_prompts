i keep going back and forth on karma, and i think the sharpest version of the puzzle is this. on the one hand the theory is genuinely compelling to me as a picture of moral bookkeeping: nothing goes unpaid, no wrong goes unaccounted for, no one suffers for no reason at all, and every piece of good fortune is traceable to something someone did. it isnt just that it makes the universe fair — it makes fairness into a law of nature, and that kind of systematicity is hard to get from any other view i know; you dont need a separate judge because desert is just built into the causal structure of things.

on the other hand i have an intuition i cant shake. imagine a six-year-old with a painful terminal illness, and someone says she deserves it because of what she did in a past life she has no memory of. my immediate reaction is that this is a moral outrage, and not because i doubt reincarnation — even if i grant the whole metaphysics, i still want to say a person cannot deserve suffering for deeds they cannot in principal remember, any more than you can deserve punishment for something someone else did. the past-life agent and this child feel like different people in the only sense that matters for desert.

so i am stuck between a theory that gives me something nothing else gives me — a fully closed moral ledger — and an intuition about desert, memory, and identity that feels rock bottom. i dont want a reading list and i dont want someone to just pick a side. i want to know what each move would actually cost. if i keep the intuition, what has to happen to the theory: does karma have to stop being a theory of moral desert and become something weaker, like a law of psychological conditioning or a story about tendencies that isnt really about desert? if i keep the theory, what exactly is wrong with the intuition: is it a parochial assumption that identity requires memory, a confusion between punishment and consequence, or something else? and is there a reading on which both survive — where "she deserved it" does something different in the karmic case than in an ordinary courtroom — or would that just be equivocation? how would i tell the difference between actually reconciling them and just talking myself into it?
