Right, I've been going in circles on the whole sweatshop debate — the bit where the job is genuinely better than the alternative but still looks exploitative, what people call mutually beneficial exploitation — and I keep finding that the decent-sounding intervention (just pay them more) might not cash out the way you'd expect once you track the second-order effects. So here's a concrete counterfactual I want worked through properly, not just gestured at.

Suppose a large European apparel brand that sources from competitive supplier factories in, say, Bangladesh announces that from next season it will top up the wages of garment workers at its own suppliers by 40%, paid out of the brand's margin rather than through higher consumer prices or lower prices paid to suppliers. It keeps its supplier relationships and order volumes roughly unchanged, pays the top-up based on reported headcount, and makes the policy public. Jobs at those factories already have long queues, and the alternative jobs open to these workers pay substantially less.

Predict what actually happens over the next five years, with the causal chain made explicit. I'm especially interested in: how the compositon of the workforce at the topped-up factories changes; what happens to turnover and absenteeism and to the length of the queues; how suppliers respond (screening and favouritism, informal fees, inflating headcount, subcontracting the real work, splitting shifts, cutting their own wage component); how much of the gain sticks with the workers themselves versus leaking to landlords, moneylenders, or other household members who then stop working; and what happens to workers and wages at nearby factories that didn't get the top-up. Then give a bottom line: does the policy mostly help the people it's meant to help, and which of your predictions would have to flip for you to change that verdict?

For each link, tell me whether you think it's a robust effect or a guess, and what you're assuming about local labour markets, supplier margins, and the brand's monitoring ability. And write the thing in British English, please.
