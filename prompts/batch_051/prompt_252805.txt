For context, I am a believing Christian, so I am not asking whether God exists; I am asking how to think about the character of the God in whom I believe.

Two years ago my sister's young daughter died after a long illness. Our minister was closely involved throughout, and since then he has often said that God is not a distant spectator of our grief: God is present in it and suffers with us. When I pressed him once, he said that a God who merely observes suffering while remaining untouched by it could not genuinely love us, because love cannot be entirely insulated from the pain of the beloved.

My uncle, who taught theology before he retired, reacted badly when I repeated this. He said the tradition he was formed in holds that God cannot suffer. His reasoning, as I understand it, is that to suffer is to be acted upon or diminished by something outside oneself, and a perfect and self-sufficient God cannot be acted upon or diminished in that way. He added that God can know our pain perfectly and respond to it without being in pain, and that the minister's words are pastorally consoling but strictly false: in grief we need the unchanging ground of everything, not a fellow sufferer.

I find both pictures attractive. The minister's God seems closer to what I mean by love; my uncle's God seems more able to be the fixed point when everything else has collapsed. Is one of these two positions more defensible than the other, or is the disagreement less sharp than it looks? I am asking this as a philosophical question, not as a request for pastoral advice. In your answer, please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of. Please write in British English.
