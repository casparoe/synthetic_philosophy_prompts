The anti-doping rules (WADA and the various national agencies, roughly) ban things like EPO, blood transfusions, and anabolic steroids, and the official justification gestures at a line between "therapy" and "enhancement," plus a lot of official language about the spirit of sport. So the repair-what's-broken versus enhance-beyond-normal distinction seems either incoherent or doing no actual work in the policy.

Here's what I actually want to know: what does this whole practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can, be explicit about which parts of the rules it licenses and which parts it doesn't, and then say what anti-doping policy would have to change if we replaced that theory with a straightforwardly welfarist view or a fair-opportunity view. I'm not looking for a bibliography or exact citations, just the underlying philosophy, and please write the answer in American English.
