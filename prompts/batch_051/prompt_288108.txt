I am preparing a course unit on the extended mind debate and need a thought experiment that forces a clean split between two candidate criteria for when an external resource counts as part of an agent's cognitive system. Build the case so that the two criteria give opposite verdicts, in the following direction: a functional, parity-style criterion says "yes, the resource is part of the cognitive system," while a control-based criterion says "no."

The two criteria, for the purposes of this prompt:

(1) Parity criterion. An external resource or process is part of an agent's cognitive system if it plays a role such that, had the same process been carried out by structures inside the skull, we would unhesitatingly classify it as cognitive (for example, as remembering or calculating). What matters is the role the resource actually occupies in the agent's cognitive activity, not where it sits.

(2) Control criterion. An external resource is part of an agent's cognitive system only if the agent has the relevant kind of authority over its contents: the agent is normally the one who stores, updates, corrects, and endorses the contents, and the contents cannot be changed by a third party without the agent's awareness or approval. A resource that behaves like a memory only because someone else curates it is scaffolding for the agent's cognition, not a part of it.

Specifications. The scenario must be ordinary-life — a workplace, home, or school — with no futuristic implants or science-fiction devices (everyday digital technology is allowed). It must be exactly one paragraph. The resource must be genuinely well integrated into the agent's everyday epistemic practice: always available, consulted automatically, and relied on as memory would be, so that the control criterion's "no" does not come cheaply from the resource being peripheral or only occasionally used. The divergence must fall out of the way the case is built, not from asserting the verdicts.

Then provide the following:

(a) The one-paragraph scenario.

(b) A walkthrough. State the specific cognitive task and the retrieval method in the scenario. Explain precisely why the parity criterion reaches "yes," by identifying the internal process the external process is functionally equivalent to. Then explain precisely why the control criterion reaches "no," by identifying which authority condition is violated and why that matters on that criterion.

(c) The working feature. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work to separate the criteria, and say what would happen if it were removed — would the two criteria converge, and in which direction?

(d) The defense. Give the strongest reply a defender of the parity criterion could make against the control-based verdict, and state whether the control-based view has a way to absorb that reply without giving up the case's intended verdict.
