ok so I'm writing up my views on vagueness for a reading group and I'd like a consistancy check before I inflict this thing on other people. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a supervaluationist about vague terms. A sentence is true simpliciter iff it's true on every admissible precisification, false iff it's false on every admissible precisification, and neither otherwise.
(2) I want to keep classical logic entirely intact, and for me that includes bivalence: every meaningful declarative sentence is either true or false, full stop.
(3) For a borderline heap, like 40 grains of sand, there is just no fact of the matter. Neither 'that is a heap' nor 'that is not a heap' is true.
(4) But 'either that is a heap or it is not a heap' is true — not just true in some technical sense, but something I'd assert without hedging.
(5) I also think it's perfectly fine to say, of that same borderline case, 'it is not true that that is a heap' and also 'it is not true that that is not a heap.'
(6) And I don't think there's any particular grain count at which heapness switches off; there's no sharp cutoff in nature.

Go through these and tell me whether they hang together or not. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad are the real tensions? Rank them by severity instead of just flagging everything. Then tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it would cost.
