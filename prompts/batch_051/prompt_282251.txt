I've come to accept a deflationary theory of truth: the predicate "is true" is a logical device for disquotation, generalization, and anaphora, and there is no substantive property of truth, no nature of truth to investigate, and no explanatory work for truth beyond those expressive functions. I want a rigorous map of what would count against that view. Give me the observations, arguments, intuitions, and formal results that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would only embarrass the position. Treat the hard cases in particular: compositional semantics and the idea that meaning is truth conditions; the normativity of truth—why "that is true" seems to settle a question or license a claim; the role of truth in the content of beliefs and propositional attitudes; the success argument for scientific realism; and whether deflationism can state the very generalizations that motivate it without already invoking full truth. Where a sophisticated deflationist would concede that an objection counts against the position, say so; where they would dismiss an objection as merely changing the subject, say so. Finally, assess honestly whether deflationism has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is a defect for a view of this kind.
