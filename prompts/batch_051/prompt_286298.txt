I keep seeing epoch proposals treated as if each only needed to fix one defect — Unix time for machine arithmetic, the Holocene calendar for a less parochial year label, astronomical year numbering for clean interval arithmetic across the 1 BC/AD 1 boundary — and I want the full requirement list before comparing any of them. Write the desiderata that an adequate choice of epoch and year-zero convention must satisfy for a general-purpose dating system: one that can locate historical events, handle durations that cross the epoch boundary, and serve computation. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which are non-negotiable constraints on any system and which are preferences of particular camps.

Be explicit about the distinctions a spec cannot collapse. I can see at least these: a date label (a position in a sequence) versus elapsed time (a magnitude of an interval); a year as a duration versus an epoch as an instant; an origin chosen for arithmetic versus a point that carries cultural or theological meaning; and labels projected backward onto dates before the epoch versus labels actually in use at the time. Add any others that matter.

The part I most want is about conflicts among the requirements. I suspect two pairs cannot be jointly satisfied as stated: clean interval arithmetic across the epoch (which pushes toward a year zero) versus leaving every historically recorded year label untouched; and neutrality between traditions versus lossless continuity with existing records, most of which were produced under a non-neutral scheme. For each genuine conflict, say exactly where the tension is and what a compromise must give up.

Then, briefly, score the traditional BC/AD scheme with no year zero, astronomical year numbering, and the Holocene calendar against the list. Keep the scorecard short — the criteria are the deliverable.
