Suppose that, over the course of a decade, several independent lines of evidence from cosmology, computational physics, neuroscience, and the historical sciences converge on a rigorously replicated result: the universe is a simulation that was initialized one hour ago, with every apparent trace of a longer past—fossils, historical archives, personal memories, starlight already in transit—inserted at initialization as a coherent false history. The result is accepted as robust by nearly the entire scientific community and the broader public, but it licenses no conclusions about the initializer's nature, motives, or future intentions, and it provides no way to tell whether the initialization will ever happen again. The discovery apparently falsifies a proposition that ordinary inquiry has until now treated as a background certainty rather than an empirical hypothesis: that the world really has the long, unproblematic past it appears to have. Predict how this discovery reshapes scientific practice and everyday epistemic habits over the following decade. Give your answer as a numbered list of exactly five predicted effects, each in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Make each item self-contained: state the effect, its underlying mechanism or incentive, whether you regard it as robust or speculative, and the condition whose failure would most likely prevent it.
