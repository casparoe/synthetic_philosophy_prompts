Here is an argument I drafted for a seminar on the socialist calculation debate, and I have come to distrust it because I cannot accept where it leads. (1) Rational allocation of scarce resources among competing uses requires prices generated by market exchange. (2) A comprehensively planned economy lacks such prices. (3) Therefore a comprehensively planned economy cannot rationally allocate resources. (4) By parity of reasoning, a large firm, which moves resources about by administrative command rather than internal market exchange, cannot rationally allocate resources. (5) Yet large firms can and do allocate resources rationally, or at least far better than this argument allows. (6) Therefore premise (1) is false, and the calculation argument against planning collapses. I would prefer to keep (1) and (3), since I think Mises and Hayek were right that the problem is genuine, but I cannot see how to resist (4) and (5) without drawing a distinction that sounds tailor-made for the occasion. Where does this argument go wrong, if it does, and what would repairing it commit one to saying about when non-market allocation is possible? Please answer in British English.
