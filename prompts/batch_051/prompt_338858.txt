i've been calling something "riding-knowledge" and i want someone to hit it with a hammer before i keep using it. the distinction is between holding-knowledge and riding-knowledge. holding-knowledge is the stuff you can have and then step back from and report: facts, names, descriptions, the kind of thing that survives being put into a sentence. riding-knowledge is only there while you're inside the activity or the state; the moment you try to step out and hold it as a report, it either collapses or you fall off the bike. bike is the obvious example: i know how to stay upright, but if you ask me mid-turn exactly how i'm doing it, my answer is bad and i get worse at the thing i was just doing. but i don't mean just knowing-how. i think there's a stronger version where trying to say the thing doesn't merely fail to capture it, it actually forfeits it, because the thing only exists while it isn't being made into an object of attention. you can know a conversation is dying while you're in it, but that knowledge is part of how you're sitting and pausing and not saying the next thing; if you switch to reporter-mode and say "this conversation is dying," what you now have is a thin fact about the conversation, not the thing you knew, and you've changed the conversation by stepping out of it. taste is not the strong version. a mango's taste is too fine for words, but it's there whether or not you try to describe it, and trying doesn't cost you the taste. the strong version is what i think mystics are pointing at when they say union or no-self or whatever can't be said: the stance you'd need to report it—standing outside it, holding it still—is exactly the stance that isn't in it. so my claim is that what cannot be said can still be known, but only as riding-knowledge, and the reason it can't be said is not that language is too coarse but that saying it flips you out of the mode in which it's known.

i know this brushes against tacit knowledge and knowing-how, and maybe i've just reinvented something under a worse name. but what i actually want to know is: is the strong/weak split real or is it just a metaphor with a bike attached? is riding-knowledge actually knowledge in any sense that would satisfy someone who thinks knowledge has correctness conditions, or have i just relabeled a feeling? has someone already worked this out properly (it feels too obvious not to have been)? and where does it break—what would it wrongly count as knowledge, and what can't it explain, like the fact that people come back from mystical states and do manage to say things about them? first give me the strongest version of my own view so i know you saw it, then be mean.
