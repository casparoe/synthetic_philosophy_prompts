I am a cognitive scientist who works on moral emotion. My working vocabulary is contemporary affective science: basic versus complex emotions, affect programs, cognitive appraisals, automatic and controlled processes, and the distinction between an emotion’s eliciting conditions and its downstream motivational role. I have been reading the Korean Four–Seven debate in translation, and I want to know whether its central positions can be stated in my vocabulary without distortion.

As I understand the two positions, Toegye (Yi Hwang) argues that the Four Beginnings—commiseration; shame and dislike; modesty and compliance; right and wrong—and the Seven Emotions—joy; anger; sorrow; fear; love; hatred; desire—are different in kind. The Four Beginnings issue from li, or principle, with qi, or material force, following; the Seven Emotions issue from qi with li riding. Yulgok (Yi I) rejects any issuance of li by itself: qi always does the issuing and li always rides it, so the Four Beginnings and the Seven Emotions are not two separate causal streams but two ways of sorting the same affective phenomena, with the Four Beginnings being the well-oriented cases.

Translate each position into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. In particular, try to say what issuance becomes in my terms: is it a causal claim about what triggers the emotion, a constitutive claim about what the emotion is, or a normative claim about its propriety? Do not flatten the disagreement into a simple nature-versus-nurture story. Then give me a residue report: what translates cleanly, what had to be distorted, what has no counterpart at all, and whether the residue shows a gap in my vocabulary or a distinction in the debate that may have been less clear than it looked.
