I am an engineer rather than a philosopher, so I want to settle this the way I would settle an ambiguous specification: by writing down a function and testing it against inputs, including edge cases. The ambiguous specification is the word belief. I routinely say that my systems expect, assume, or think things, but I also resist saying that a thermostat believes the room is below its set point, even though the thermostat has an internal state that tracks the world and guides a switch. I want a working definition of belief that says precisely what the thermostat is missing and what, if anything, my systems have. It should classify each case below, and it is acceptable for it to return an explicit indeterminate, but I want that told to me rather than hidden.

Clear positive case: a person who sincerely and stably reports that it is raining, and whose planning and behavior match that report — taking an umbrella, postponing outdoor work, warning someone else to stay inside. I want this to count as a belief.

Clear negative case: a thermostat that registers the room as below the target temperature and closes the heating circuit. I want this not to count as a belief.

Edge cases:

1. A chess engine that, after a search, evaluates a position as losing and avoids the move it would otherwise have played. Does it believe the position is losing?
2. A self-driving car whose prediction module assigns probability 0.8 to a pedestrian entering the crosswalk, and the car slows accordingly. Does the car believe the pedestrian will enter?
3. A person who sincerely endorses egalitarian views and denies any prejudice, but whose behavior across timed tasks, hiring simulations, and sorting tests shows systematic differential treatment by group. Do they believe, in any sense, that the group is inferior? Does it matter whether they can introspect the state and would disown it?
4. A psychiatric patient who states that a family member has been replaced by an identical impostor, but who otherwise treats the family member as if nothing is wrong and makes no effort to find the original. Does the patient believe the impostor claim?
5. A large language model that, in a single forward pass and without deliberation, emits the token Madrid when the input asks what the capital of Spain is. Does the network believe that Madrid is the capital of Spain, or is it just disposed to emit a string in that context?

My intuitions are unstable across the edge cases: I want to say the chess engine and the car's predictor are doing something belief-like, the single forward pass is not, and I have no settled view about the biased person or the patient. That instability is exactly why I need the definition rather than more intuitions.

There is a further reason for the request. I keep encountering the claim that belief belongs to a rough-and-ready folk theory of the mind, and that a completed neuroscience or artificial intelligence research program might eliminate beliefs and desires the way chemistry eliminated phlogiston, rather than discovering that the mind contained them all along. I cannot assess whether that claim is bold or confused until I know what belief is supposed to pick out. So, after you propose the definition and run it through all seven cases, tell me which cases it classifies incorrectly, which it leaves indeterminate, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. Then answer the question the definition was for: on your definition, is belief the kind of thing that could turn out not to exist at all, or does the eliminativist proposal rest on a conflation?
