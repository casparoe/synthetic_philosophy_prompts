I keep running into an asymmetry in how people, and in the United States the law, treat markets in body products, and I can't find a principle that survives contact with nearby cases. We allow paid plasma donation: someone is compensated for sitting in a chair while blood is drawn, plasma separated, and red cells returned, often twice a week. We allow paid egg and sperm donation, and commercial surrogacy in many states. But paying a living kidney donor is a felony. When I press people on why, they offer things like: the kidney is inside the body; removal is invasive; the donor bears long-term health risk; a kidney can't be replenished; a kidney is a necessity in a way eggs aren't; and selling a kidney commodifies the body. But each of these looks either too weak or too strong. Egg retrieval is invasive and carries real risk; plasma and sperm donors are also selling something that came from inside their bodies; a liver lobe regenerates, yet paying a living liver-lobe donor is not treated like plasma; and if kidney sale is commodifying, it is not obvious why compensated egg provision or surrogacy escapes the charge. There is even a legal line that tracks the method of extraction rather than the tissue: I recall reading that a US court held that compensating donors for marrow stem cells collected by apheresis is permissible while paying for marrow taken by surgical hip extraction is not, because the statute's ban attaches to the surgical procedure's product rather than the stem cells themselves. That feels like a reductio of one candidate principle, but it might be a useful clue.

Give me a committed answer. State your bottom line in the first sentence: is there a defensible principle that puts paid plasma and paid egg donation on the permissible side while keeping paid kidney sale on the banned side, or is this just an inconsistent bundle of intuitions and interest-group path dependence? Then reconstruct the strongest version of the best candidate principle, test it against the hardest cases (liver lobe, surgical marrow versus apheresis, egg, plasma, surrogacy, perhaps a kidney from a directed deceased donor), and say whether the line should be kept, moved, or abandoned. I am less interested in a survey of options than in which line you would actually defend if forced.
