I sit on the compensation committee of a small company that is about to convert part of everyone's cash salary into an equity-linked instrument whose payoff is re-invested each quarter under a stochastic rule. The sponsors of the plan keep appealing to the instrument's expected value, which is strongly positive. A colleague who opposes the plan repeats the same argument at every meeting, and I have to assess it before I cast my vote. Expected value, he says, is an ensemble fiction: it averages over a thousand parallel copies of me, while I get exactly one realized trajectory, and what compounds along that trajectory is the time average, not the ensemble average. His illustration is a fair coin that multiplies one's stake by 1.5 on heads and by 0.6 on tails. Per flip the expected factor is 1.05, so the ensemble average climbs; but the geometric mean factor of a sufficiently long single trajectory is exp(0.5 ln(1.5) + 0.5 ln(0.6)), roughly 0.949, so a single player's wealth decays toward zero almost surely even as the ensemble average imagines it growing. From this he concludes that a rational individual should optimize time-average growth rather than expected value, and that expected-utility reasoning is the wrong lens for a real agent in repeated and one-shot choices alike.

I need an audit of this ensemble-versus-trajectory picture before I vote, not an audit of the plan itself. Please give the correspondence table explicitly: what in an actual decision corresponds to the ensemble, to the single path, to the long run, and to ruin? Then identify where the parallel-copies picture breaks down, and which assumptions it quietly imports about repetition, stake sizing, rebalancing, consumption, and the curvature of how wealth is valued. Finally, tell me how much of my colleague's conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table: does the coin example establish that expected-utility reasoning is misleading, or does it establish something narrower that an expected-utility framework can already express? If the picture needs replacing, say what the corrected picture would be.
