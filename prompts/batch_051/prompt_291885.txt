Last night I had a long and increasingly circular argument with a friend about whether she was justified in spraying her roses against aphids, and the discussion kept jumping from case to case — a mosquito on her arm, head lice on her child, bedbugs in an apartment, a locust swarm threatening a harvest, a non-native beetle killing a forest, bees dying as a side effect of a crop spray — each of us switching examples whenever our position began to look implausible. What I came away wanting was not another case; I want the full specification of what a theory of the ethics of killing insects in pest-control contexts must get right before any particular view earns a hearing. The purpose is a theory good enough to guide ordinary decisions about whether, when, and how to kill insects, and to evaluate collective policies such as spraying programs, not merely to sort cases after the fact.

So: write me the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is a genuine adequacy condition or only a preference of one camp. Make sure the list does all of the following. It must separate the question whether insects have moral status from the question how much weight that status carries, and from the further question whether the unit of concern is the individual organism, the population, the species, or the ecosystem. It must say what the theory is to do with genuine uncertainty about insect sentience and nociception without collapsing into either "they feel nothing" or a paralyzing precaution. It must take a position on whether intent and foresight matter — killing aphids to save a plant versus killing bees as a side effect of an insecticide — and on whether lethal control must meet something like necessity and proportionality rather than bare convenience. It must handle the differences among agricultural, domestic, and wild or ecological contexts, including invasive species, and it should adjudicate whether a pesticide's being synthetic or "natural" is morally relevant or a distraction. Mark any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding the intuitive positions people actually defend. A table is fine, but the reasoning behind each row is what I actually need.
