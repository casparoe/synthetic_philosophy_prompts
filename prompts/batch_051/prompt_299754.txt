I'm a novelist, and I keep trying to write from the point of view of an ant colony, and I've realized I dont know whether I'm chasing a real idea or just a fancy. Individual ants are basically little reflex machines, but the colony as a whole scouts, argues over nest sites (some kind of quorum), farms fungus, regulates temperature, moves the brood around—it looks for all the world like it has something like perceptions and intentions, even though no single ant does. Is there an established name for the view that the colony itself has a mind, or something mind-like, while the ants inside it don't? I don't just want the label, though: I want to know what that view has to commit to, how it differs from the weaker thing where you just say the colony shows 'emergent behavior' or call it a 'superorganism' or admit you're anthropomorphizing, and where the boundary is—would the same move make a firm, a market, or a country count as having a mind, or is there a principled place where it stops? If there's no settled name, what would you call it and how would you define it?
