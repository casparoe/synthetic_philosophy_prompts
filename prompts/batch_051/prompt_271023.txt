I run trust and safety for a mid-sized video platform with viewers in maybe forty countries, and we get a steady stream of complaints that some video is blasphemous, sacrilegious, or deliberately offensive to something sacred. The reviewers who make these calls are generalists with no theology background and about four minutes a case. We already have hate-speech and harassment rules, so assume the content doesn't target people for protected traits; the only question is whether to remove, age-gate, or flag something because it offends against the sacred, where sacred covers a god, a prophet, a scripture, a place, a ritual, a flag, a grave, that kind of thing. For this exercise assume no local law compels either way; this is our own policy. Write a reusable decision procedure, grounded in whatever philosophy of offence, dignity, toleration, and free expression can actually bear, that a reviewer on shift could follow without knowing the theory: the questions in order, what counts as a pass or fail answer to each, where the procedure stops and says leave it up, where it stops and says take it down, and where it kicks the decision upstairs because its a values call no checklist can absorb. Then run the procedure on three cases, and tell me which questions each one trips on: (1) a video by a former member of a faith, aimed at other former members, that calls the faith's central text a made-up fairy story and reads out what it calls the ten most absurd verses; (2) a satirical cartoon of a revered prophet in a humiliating pose, geo-blocked to a country where that imagery is deeply taboo; (3) a protest video of an immigrant burning the flag and military conscription papers of the country they left. Make sure one of the three comes out in a way I won't like, tell me which one and why, and end by saying what the procedure cannot decide.
