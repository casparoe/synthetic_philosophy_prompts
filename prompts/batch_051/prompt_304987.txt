One of my grad students — I'll call him Dan — lost his husband about a year ago. He's not clinically depressed; he works, he sees friends, he functions fine. But he's just started a trial for a drug that suppresses the felt awfulness of grief without touching memory or belief. On it, he can still tell you with full conviction that losing Marcus was the worst thing that ever happened to him, but he says it the way he'd say its raining — the judgment is there, the sting is gone. The dose wears off by evening, so he's started scheduling his grief: he skips the morning pill on Sundays and lets the full thing hit him then. His sister says that proves he isn't really grieving, that the pain is doing necessary work and he's just processing it like a chore. Dan asked me whether he should keep taking it.

Assume for the sake of argument that the pill is safe and does exactly what I described, no more. Give me your read as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no conclusion: what's actually at stake, the strongest case for keeping the drug, the strongest case against, whether the scheduling detail changes anything, and what you'd tell Dan.
