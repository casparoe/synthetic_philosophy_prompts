Covering a dispute about whether a country can join a customs union without ceasing to be sovereign, I keep hearing the same phrase used to close the argument: the member states do not transfer their sovereignty, they "pool" it. The phrase did a lot of work at a recent panel. An economist argued that a state that no longer sets its own external tariffs has already yielded final authority over a major area of its policy; the minister replied that sovereignty was not lost but pooled, as if that answered the charge. I could not tell whether this was a substantive reply or an equivocation. What are the distinct claims "pooled sovereignty" could be making, from the trivial to the radical? Which of those readings would actually rebut the economist's argument, and which would concede its point while obscuring it? Lay the readings out as a menu rather than settling on one, and say which readings do the work the phrase was being asked to do.
