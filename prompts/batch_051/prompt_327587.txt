I've been going back and forth with my housemate about the atheism/agnosticism burden-of-proof thing—basically wether "lack of belief" atheists get to sit back and make the theist do all the epistemic work. He pulled up W. K. Clifford's essay "The Ethics of Belief" and read me this bit:

"When an action is once done, it is right or wrong for ever; no accidental failure of its good or evil fruits can possibly alter that. The man would not have been innocent, he would only have been not found out. The question of right or wrong has to do with the origin of his belief, not the matter of it; not what it was, but how he got it; not whether it turned out to be true or false, but whether he had a right to believe on such evidence as was before him."

He says this dissolves the burden-of-proof question, because what matters about a belief is not whether it's true but how the person formed it, and the theist typically forms their belief by faith rather than by evidence. I tried to put his reasoning into steps to see if it holds up: (1) If someone forms a belief on insufficient evidence, they had no right to hold that belief. (2) Whether a belief is right or wrong depends only on its origin—on how it was formed—and never on whether it turns out true or false. (3) Forming a belief on faith means forming it while either lacking sufficient evidence or deliberately stifling doubts about the evidence. (4) So a religious belief held on faith is held without right, no matter whether it is actually true. (5) In a debate over whether God exists, the only thing there is to evaluate is whether the believer formed their belief rightly; so the burden of proof lies entirely on the theist, and the atheist has nothing to prove. (6) Therefore, if the theist cannot produce sufficient evidence, theism should be rejected—or at least the belief should be treated as unjustified. I can't find the weak link here, and I half think the conclusion just follows. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and does it fail because of the inference or because a premise is false? And is (5) actually something Clifford's text licenses, or is my housemate bolting it on?
