I restore mechanical watches and clocks for a living, and the work has left me circling around a question about what it means for an object to be complex. A tray of dismantled watch parts is a jumble, a crate containing the same parts is just as much of a jumble, and yet a working watch seems complex in a way neither is. It isn't merely the number of parts. It also isn't mere order: a metal crystal in which the same pattern repeats millions of times is far more ordered than the jumble, but I would not call it complex in the same way. The feature that keeps looking important to me is the depth of the object's construction, by which I mean how many distinct, non-repeating operations and choices had to occur to get that particular arrangement — how much the thing's history had to be steered into place, as opposed to how big it is, how improbable the final pattern looks, or how many total steps it took. A friend who is an engineer thinks I am chasing a shadow. Any way of ranking complexity, he says, is just a convention we take up for a purpose: shortest description, build time, number of distinct steps, amount of selection — each ranks objects differently, and there is no fact of the matter about which ranking is right.

What I want is a way to find out whether the construction-depth idea is more than that. Suppose someone claimed that construction depth tracks something real that the objects themselves have, so that a working watch is complex in this sense while the crate of parts is not, and this would be true even if nobody found the distinction convenient. How could one test that claim? Please give me a concrete experiment or measurement rather than an argument about words. Spell out what result would favor the view that construction depth tracks something real, what result would favor the view that it is just one arbitrary convention among many, and then be honest about what even the cleanest experimental result would leave unsettled. I know that deciding exactly what to count will always involve some choice on our part; I want to know how much of the question can actually be dragged out of stipulation and put to the test, and how much stays beyond testing no matter how clever the experiment is. Please write the answer in American English.
