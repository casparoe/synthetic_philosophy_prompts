I'm putting together a short series for a philosophy magazine in which each piece reconstructs how a historical philosopher who wrote about a practical domain would probably have responded to a development in that domain that postdates them. I want to begin with George Berkeley and money. Berkeley is remembered mostly for his immaterialism, but during the economic distress of Ireland in the 1730s he wrote extensively on currency and banking. The view he pressed was, roughly, that money is not wealth itself but a ticket or counter by which claims on goods are recorded and transferred; that a nation's real wealth is the productive activity of its people; that hoarding or fetishizing the material money is made of is a confusion; and that the right policy is to keep this ticket circulating in proportion to actual trade and employment. He also defended the idea of a publicly sponsored bank issuing paper money to stimulate an economy that had plenty of unmet needs and idle labor but little coin.

The developments I want to test him against are Bitcoin and current proposals for central bank digital currencies. These pull in different directions: Bitcoin has a fixed supply and no central issuer, while a CBDC would be a digital liability of the state, but both force the question of whether money needs a material anchor, who may issue it, and whether the medium matters or only the claims it records. Write the piece as a careful, self-hedging reconstruction: what would Berkeley's stated monetary principles commit him to saying about each development, where would he have to add premises he never considered, where would the two developments pull apart strands of his own position, and where does the counterfactual simply outrun what we can responsibly infer? Please separate what is settled in the record of Berkeley's monetary views from what is genuinely contested among scholars, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus. Where the argument would need to import economic ideas he could not have had, flag that rather than silently reading them back into him.
