I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, working on dispositions, and I've been asked to set the take-home essay for a third-year module in philosophy of mind and metaphysics that I help teach. The students have covered the simple conditional analysis of dispositions — roughly, the idea that an object is disposed to give a response when stimulated just in case it would give that response if it were stimulated — and the usual battery of counterexamples: finkish dispositions, masks (antidotes), and mimics. My problem is that they're good at writing down the textbook gloss of each label together with a stock example (a live wire that would be made safe if touched, a poison plus an administered antidote), but I'm not at all convinced they see what these cases actually do to the analysis, or weather finks and masks cause different kinds of trouble. The assignment is 1500 words, worth 100 marks, and it's meant to separate students who can genuinely argue from students who've just memorised the terminology.

Can you write me the exam question, a model answer, and a marking scheme? I'd like the question to be built around a crux rather than just asking them to explain finks and masks. For instance, one option is to push them to assess whether the standard repairs to the conditional analysis — adding a ceteris paribus clause, or restricting the analysis to cases where nothing interferes, or retreating to a claim about an underlying causal basis — handle finks and masks equally well, or whether one of the two is harder for the amended analyses to accommodate. But feel free to pick whichever crux you think is sharpest, so long as a student can't answer it by reciting a summary. The question and model answer also shouldn't lean on named readings or page citations, since this is meant to test reasoning rather than recall of sources.

I'd like the whole thing back as a single JSON object with exactly these fields: 'question' (the essay prompt itself, as a string), 'model_answer' (a string of roughly 600–800 words laying out what a first-class answer would argue), and 'marking_scheme' (an array of objects, each object with the fields 'criterion', 'what_top_answers_do', 'common_errors_and_penalties', and 'marks', where the marks across entries sum to 100). Please write everything in British English.
