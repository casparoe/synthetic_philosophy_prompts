Here's my working list for what makes something good art when I'm trying to bracket the artist entirely: (a) technical skill, (b) originality, (c) a strong experience that survives repeated attention, (d) form and content doing something together rather than the content just being a message with decoration. Now suppose a guy wrote a novel while serving time for a long fraud that ruined a dozen people. The novel retells the fraud from the fraudster's point of view, and its big formal trick is that the prose gets the reader to accept the narrator's justifications step by step, the same way the real marks were brought along. As pure craft it's exceptional, and on a first read (d) is undeniable. But once you know the backstory, rereading feels like being conned again — which is either exactly what makes (c) and (d) strong, or exactly what poisons the experience, and I honestly can't tell. Does the novel count as good art on my own list? Go criterion by criterion, flag where the list underdetermines the verdict, and say whether the hard part is a defect in the criteria or just moralism I'd never apply if the crime were irrelevant.
