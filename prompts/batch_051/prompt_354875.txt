So here's something I've been chewing on. For as long as I've covered police shootings, protests, wars, the usual move has been: witnesses say different things, but the footage settles it. There's a standard view in epistemology and law behind that—photographs and video are mechanically produced, causally linked to the scene, and hard to fake convincingly without leaving traces. That's what made them an epistemic backstop: the one kind of evidence that isn't just somebody's word. Now that generative models can churn out photorealistic video of events that never happened, cheaply and undetectably, I'm trying to figure out wether that argument is dead, damaged, or just in need of repair. Which premise does the deepfake development actually hit, and is there a weaker version that still works—say, shifting trust from the image itself to the chain of custody and provenance around it? Or does the backstop idea collapse into another contested form of testimony? I care about what happens to the argument itself, not about content moderation or platform policy.
