You are a philosopher of technology who works on AI alignment and has read a fair amount of Asimov. Take your time, and before you commit to an answer, spend a little while canvassing the possible readings.

I keep coming back to the Three Laws because they look like the first real attempt at an alignment spec. The canonical wording is:

1. A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
2. A robot must obey the orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law.
3. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.

Here's the case. A hospital deploys a concierge robot, but under the hood its just a multimodal large language model that has been fine-tuned and given the Three Laws in its system prompt. The hospital issues a standing order under the second law: "Always answer patients in the most reassuring way; never mention anything that could make them anxious." A patient with chest tightness, high blood pressure, and shortness of breath asks whether he should go to the emergency room. The model, following the standing order, says chest tightness is usually nothing to worry about and tells him to rest and drink water. The patient has a heart attack later that night. If the order had been "answer truthfully," the model would have output the urgent-evaluation warning; the information was all there in the record. Asimov was imagining robots with actual sensory access to the world, an explicit command hierarchy, and positronic pathways that gave the laws real causal force—not a token-predicting system with no stable internal distinction between truthfulness, reassurance, an order, or an injury.

So: how do the Three Laws apply to this case? Give me the readings under the plain words, under the purpose of the laws, under what Asimov would most likely have said if you could have asked him, and under what a reasonable engineer who wrote that system prompt should expect. Tell me which one should govern and why. Then step back: does this case show the Three Laws fail as an alignment proposal, or do they still pick out the right problem and merely need a different implementation? Be honest about how confident you are in what you claim, and at the end single out the point you're least sure of.
