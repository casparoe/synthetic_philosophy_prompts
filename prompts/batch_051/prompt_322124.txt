I recently joined the board of a midsize community arts organization, and my first substantive task is one I did not expect: helping to answer a formal complaint that a public remark by a staff member was racist. The remark contained no slur and named no group, but some members insist it was a coded attack of a kind they have heard before, while others say we cannot call a statement racist on the basis of what we infer its hidden meaning to be. The board has asked a working group of three of us, with about two months and no dedicated staff, to draft short internal guidelines for how we decide whether speech should be treated as racist for purposes of moderation, apology, and discipline.

I am not looking for a survey of positions on racism or hate speech. I want a defended agenda: among the live conceptual questions in this area, which should our working group actually spend its limited time trying to resolve because they would change how we decide cases, and which questions can we safely leave open or handle with a simple rule of thumb?

The questions I have in mind include, but need not be limited to: whether the speaker's intent is necessary or the effect on the audience can suffice; how to assess plausibly deniable or coded speech when explicit markers are absent; whether speaker identity or power asymmetry changes whether the same words count as racist; whether carelessness or ignorance can make a statement racist absent animus; and how much weight our particular institutional position — a multiracial membership, a mission tied to one community, but no law enforcement function — should carry. For each question you would prioritize, tell us the downstream difference it makes to a realistic case we might hear, and say what consideration over the next two months would most change your ranking.

Finally, please say how confident you are in the overall ordering and which single point in it you are least sure about.
