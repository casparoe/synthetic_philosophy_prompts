I'm putting together a list of problem cases for aesthetic defintions of art — the ones that try to define art in terms of the special perceptual payoff it gives us, like beauty, significant form, or aesthetic experience. So far I have: Duchamp's readymades, where an ordinary object gets displayed as art without gaining any new aesthetic properties to speak of; a meticulous forgery, which can deliver the same aesthetic experience as the original but sits in a quite different art-historical (and maybe metaphysical) position; a piece of conceptual art that exists mostly as instructions or documentation and leaves only a nominal physical object to contemplate; and Warhol's Brillo Boxes, which were made for a gallery but are, near as you can tell by looking, just like the supermarket cartons.

I know these are the standard moves, and they're all some mix of perceptual indistinguishability or too little sensory content. I want one more counterexample with a genuinely different structure — not another perceptual-twin case and not another case where the work simply lacks aesthetic content for the definition to grab onto. Tell me what the new case is, what it shows, and whether an aesthetic theorist could plausibly revise their definition to absorb it.
