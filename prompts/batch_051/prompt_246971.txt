The setup as I remember it: a guy crashes his car in the desert and he's badly hurt, a stranger drives up and says he'll take him to safety but only if the guy promises to pay him a lot of money once they're back in town. The guy is honest with himself and realizes that after he's rescued, he'll have no reason to actually pay — he'll be safe, and the money will just be gone. The stranger knows this too, so he doesn't trust the promise, and the guy doesn't get rescued. What's weird to me is that someone a bit less rational — someone who would feel bound by a promise even when it's not in his interest, or who could make himself genuinely believe he'll pay — would be believed, and would survive. So having a glitch in your own reasoning, or being less responsive to your future reasons, seems like an advantage. I can see how this is sort of like tying yourself to the mast so you can't give in to the sirens, but that changes your options, not your mind. What I'm after is the version where you mess with your own head.
