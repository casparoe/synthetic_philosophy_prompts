ok so imagine a platform that's given up on trying to read intentions when moderating racist speech, because anyone can claim they didn't mean it and the dog whistle is built to survive that defense. instead they build what they call a divergence tag. they pick a phrase that might be a dog whistle ('globalists', say, or some euphemism about crime), show it to a few hundred people from the group it seems to be about and a few hundred people from outside that group, and ask each person to rate "how racially loaded is this phrase, 0 to 10." if the gap between the two group averages clears a threshold, every post containing the phrase automatically gets a label: "this phrase is read differently by different groups." no deletion, no strike, no appeal based on the authors intent — just the note, stuck on every use forever.

walk me through how this actually plays out as a system, not as a moral question. which phrases end up tagged, and which actual dog whistles does it miss because the out-group hears them too, or because the target group itself doesn't rate them as loaded? how fast do users route around it: inventing fresh euphemisms, swapping in a near-miss spelling, or just using the tag itself as a badge that proves the quiet part was heard? does the raters' knowledge that they're being used as a detector change their scores over time — e.g., people rating 10 as a political move or rating 0 to starve the system? and what happens to ordinary in-group slang or genuinely harmless terms that get read differently across groups: do they collect a note that makes them look racist until the note stops meaning anything at all?

then tell me the likeliest failure mode and whether any patch (a higher threshold, rotating raters, tagging individual posts rather than phrases, letting the author attach a response) fixes it without quietly turning the whole thing back into an intent test nobody trusts. dont worry about whether the platform should do this; just trace where it ends up. last line should be a one-sentence summary of where the system lands.
