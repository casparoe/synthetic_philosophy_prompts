I'm a philosophy professor working in philosophy of physics, and a competent hobbyist programmer. I think naturally in terms of value semantics (where sameness is sameness of content), reference semantics (where two bindings can point to distinct objects that are equal in content), object identity as opposed to structural equality, multisets, keys, and the idea that permuting a collection's elements may or may not produce a new collection. I'd like you to restate a debate from philosophy of physics in that vocabulary and, crucially, report what does not survive the translation.

The debate concerns the identity and indiscernibility of quantum particles. Its main elements are these: (i) Leibniz's principle that no two distinct things can share all their qualitative properties; (ii) the fact that fundamental particles of the same kind share every intrinsic, state-independent property — mass, charge, spin magnitude — yet are counted as many; (iii) permutation symmetry, under which swapping two particles of the same kind does not produce a new physical state; (iv) the contrast between fermions, whose joint states are antisymmetric and which cannot occupy the same one-particle state, and bosons, whose joint states are symmetric and which can; (v) haecceitism, the view that each particle has a primitive thisness over and above its qualitative properties; (vi) the bundle or structuralist view that a particle is nothing over and above its properties or its place in a relational structure, so that there is no identity without discernibility; and (vii) weak discernibility, the fallback position that particles of the same kind are at least related by irreflexive relations, such as having opposite spin, that each bears to the other but not to itself.

Work through this as an expert in both philosophy of physics and programming-language semantics. Take your time, and begin by inventorying the distinct claims you will translate so that none is silently dropped. Then translate each of (i)–(vii) into the target vocabulary. For each, say what carries across cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all. Finally, assess the residue: which failures reflect a genuine limitation of the programming vocabulary, and which suggest that the philosophical claim was less precise than it looked? Conclude by saying whether the translation, on the whole, illuminates the debate or misleads about it.
