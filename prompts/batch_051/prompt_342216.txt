The question was: what is at issue between epistemic and ontic structural realism, and does the pessimistic meta-induction support one view over the other? Discuss the Ramsey-sentence reconstruction of theories in your answer.

The student wrote:

Structural realism is best understood as a compromise between the no-miracles argument and the pessimistic meta-induction. The no-miracles argument says that the novel predictive success of mature scientific theories would be miraculous unless they had latched onto something real. The pessimistic meta-induction points out that the history of science is a graveyard of predictively successful theories whose central entities were later abandoned, such as caloric and the luminiferous ether. What survives these transitions is not the entities but the mathematical structure of the relations between the phenomena; Fresnel's equations survived the abandonment of the ether once Maxwell reconceived light electromagnetically. Structural realism therefore tells us to be realists about relational structure and at least agnostic about the natures of the entities that appear at the nodes of that structure. The epistemic version stops there: we can know the relations, but the intrinsic nature of the relata lies beyond our epistemic reach. The ontic version goes further: the relata are not merely unknowable in their natures but are nothing over and above their place in the network, so talk of independently existing objects should be given up.

Ramseyfication shows how this is possible. If we replace each theoretical term in a theory with an existentially quantified variable, we get a sentence empirically equivalent to the original but which no longer names electrons or ethers; it merely says that there are some things standing in certain relations. This reveals that theoretical terms contribute nothing but their structural role, and that the entities themselves are dispensable for the theory's content. The pessimistic meta-induction therefore favours the ontic over the epistemic version. Since it teaches us that entity-based commitments are exactly what later science abandons, and since the Ramsey sentence shows that nothing is lost by dropping such commitments, we should conclude that there is no more to the world than structure. The old objection that relations require relata does no damage here, because the structuralist can grant that something has to occupy the nodes, but deny that those occupants have any nature beyond their relational position, just as the vertices of a graph have no existence other than their graph-theoretic position.
