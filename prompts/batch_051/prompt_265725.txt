Before I read one more book on why the Classic Maya or the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean or Easter Island 'collapsed,' I want the spec that the word 'collapse' has to earn first. It seems like the same term gets used for a 90 percent population decline, a century of slow political decentralization, the disappearance of a writing tradition, an empire breaking into smaller states — and then authors argue past each other about whether the society failed or just reorganized. So here's the ask: write the desiderata for an adequate theory/account of societal collapse. What does it have to capture, which cases must it get right (I want the Maya, the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, and Rapa Nui/Easter Island in there, plus at least one case that is clearly not a collapse), and which distinctions can it not blur — collapse vs. transformation, demographic collapse vs. political fragmentation, state collapse vs. cultural survival, abrupt vs. drawn-out, local vs. systemic? Also: what does it have to stay consistent with from archaeology and demography — coarse time resolution, contested population estimates, sites continuing while elites stop building monuments, that kind of thing. For each desideratum give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a vibe. Then seperate the non-negotiable requirements from the ones that are just a camp's preference — the transformation-only people, the fast-and-total people, the moral-lesson-about-overshoot people. And flag any pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied, plus the single requirement that would do the most to rule out the usual stories. I don't need a scholar-by-scholar scorecard; the criteria and the tensions between them are what I'm after. A table is fine.
