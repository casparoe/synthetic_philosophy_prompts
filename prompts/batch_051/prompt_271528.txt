In debates about AI, I keep encountering an analogy, associated especially with Daniel Dennett, between large language models that pass as human and counterfeit currency. The argument, as I have absorbed it from those discussions, runs something like this. A well-made counterfeit bill need not fool an expert, and need not end up in every wallet, to damage the currency: once people know that good counterfeits are in circulation, the standing trust that lets strangers accept money without inspection begins to erode, and that erosion — not any particular bad bill — is the real harm. The analogy then claims that human-seeming AI systems do the same thing to conversation: a chatbot need not fool everyone, or even most people, in order to degrade the background assumption that a human-seeming interlocutor is a person, and that background trust is what everyday communication and democratic discourse run on. The conclusion drawn from this is that creating and releasing such systems should be treated the way we treat counterfeiting — as something to prohibit, with disclosure or labeling at most a secondary measure.

I want this analogy audited rather than assumed. Please give me (1) an explicit table of correspondences: what is supposed to map onto what — the currency, its social function, the counterfeiter, the person who passes the bill, the merchant who accepts it, the mint or central authority, the casual detection heuristic, and the legal prohibition; (2) the places where the mapping breaks, separated into the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion and the ones that are merely superficial; (3) an account of what the analogy is doing in the argument — which premises it quietly imports from the currency case that may not hold in the speech case, and which steps of the inference depend on those imports; and (4) a verdict on whether the prohibitionist conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time. If some other comparison would do the intended work more honestly — forged documents, counterfeit goods, invasive species, or something else — say which one and justify the replacement.

Please write your answer in American English.
