When Mira tells me the new restaurant is good, I'll happily repeat that to someone else without having tasted it. When she tells me a novel is brilliant, I still feel like I can't say the novel is brilliant until I've read it myself, even though I trust her taste more than my own first reactions. Same with films, records, paintings: I treat her aesthetic reports as somehow defective, but I take her factual reports at face value. I don't think I'm unusual, and I'm starting to wonder if there's a real principle here or just a weird bit of epistemic snobbery. Whats the strongest principle you can find that justifies treating aesthetic testimony as second-class, and does it survive being pushed on nearby cases—like a food critic's review, or my own memory of having liked a book ten years ago? Give me an honest verdict on whether I should try to drop the asymmetry or keep it.
