Hey, I'm on a small data team at a community college, and we're building an early-warning tool that flags students who are likely to not finish so advisors can reach out. I've been put in charge of writing up our fairness principles before our next meeting, and I'm starting to suspect my own commitments secretly contradict each other. I dont have the background to work this out myself, so I want an audit of the set.

Here are my commitments:

1. The risk scores should be calibrated. If the tool says a student has a 70% chance of not finishing, then roughly 70% of students who get that score should actually not finish — and this should hold within each group, not just overall. The score should mean the same thing for first-generation and continuing-generation students.

2. The tool shouldn't be systematically worse for one group. Specifically, among students who actually end up not finishing, the fraction the tool flags should be about the same across groups. And among students who do finish, the fraction the tool wrongly flags should be about the same too. So, roughly equal false-negative rates and false-positive rates.

3. If one group genuinely doesn't finish at a higher rate, the tool can flag a bigger share of that group. That's just reflecting reality, not bias. In our data, first-gen students not finishing at about 40% and continuing-gen at about 20%, so commitment 3 isn't a hypothetical: i expect the model to flag more first-gen students.

4. We should use one cutoff for everyone. Like, flag anyone above 60%. Different thresholds for different groups feels unfair to me, because then two students with the same score could be treated differently depending on their group.

5. Group membership shouldn't be an input to the model. But I do think we can use things like high school GPA, attendance patterns, and zip code, even though they correlate with demographics. I wouldnt say using those is the same as using race or sex as such.

Which of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they're in tension? For the real tensions, rank them by how bad they are and say what the cheapest fix would be. If it comes down to one or two commitments causing most of the trouble, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it would cost me.
