I'm on the board of a small city archive, and we're rewriting our collecting policy because we're deadlocked on a concrete decision, and I'd like a memo I can bring to the next meeting. For decades the archive has mostly ended up with the records of the city council, the school board, the chamber of commerce, and a few prominent families — the stuff that got donated because somebody kept it in a filing cabinet. Last month a community group offered us a messy collection documenting a wave of rent strikes in the 1970s: flyers, oral-history tapes, letters between tenants, some photographs. Part of it is unflattering to people still living, and some of it reached the group secondhand, so we can't fully document the chain of custody. Around the same time, the police department asked us to prioritize scanning its old arrest logs, which are genuinely fragile and heavily used by genealogists and journalists. We can fund only one of these this cycle.

Half the board says our job is to preserve the official record, not to become activists; the other half says the archive's silence about tenants, immigrants, and working-class neighborhoods is itself a decision, and collections like this are the only way those people show up in the historical record at all. A third group says the real silence isn't in our shelves but in what was never created or kept in the first place, so no acquisition can fix it and we should stop pretending either project is neutral. Draft the decision memo for the board: which project should we fund, under what conditions, and what principle should govern how we think about silences in our holdings going forward? I want an actual recommendation we can vote on, and I want the strongest version of the loosing side stated honestly.
