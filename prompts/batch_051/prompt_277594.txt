I teach a philosophy of mind seminar, but my collaborator group is made up of systems engineers whose working vocabulary is observability: distributed traces, structured logs, metrics, instrumentation, and profilers. They are fluent in that vocabulary and have no background in philosophy. I want to see whether a live philosophical debate can be restated in their terms without loss.

The debate concerns whether a language model's verbalized chain of thought is a report of the process that actually led to its final answer, or a post hoc reconstruction that may diverge from the underlying computation. On one side: the chain of thought simply exhibits the reasoning, so a model that produces an articulate rationale is thereby reporting its own reasoning faithfully enough. On the other side: the chain of thought is itself an output emitted on demand, and its relation to the process producing the final answer is an empirical question that the model's own statement cannot settle by itself. Behind this stand older questions about first-person authority, answerability for one's reasons, and whether reasoning is transparent to the reasoner.

Your task has two parts. First, restate this debate as faithfully as you can using the engineers' vocabulary. Treat the final answer as the output, the actual token-level computation as the execution, and the chain of thought as a trace or log purportedly describing that execution. Draw on their distinctions where they help: a trace instrumented from the real execution path versus a reconstructed trace written after the fact; whether a trace can be verified against the execution; and the fact that emitting a trace may change the very behavior it claims to report. Say which positions in the philosophical debate map onto which distinctions in observability, and which positions the vocabulary tempts you to collapse together.

Second, give a residue report. What translates cleanly, what translates only under strain, and what has no counterpart in traces and logs at all—for example, claims about a reasoner's special authority to say what it reasoned, or its being answerable for offering reasons rather than merely providing accurate descriptions of an internal process. For each piece of residue, say whether it reveals something the observability framework is missing or something in the philosophical debate that is less clear than it looks.

Do not settle the question in advance by defining the trace as the execution, and do not let the engineering vocabulary prejudge whether a faithful report is possible. The interesting part is what the translation drops.
