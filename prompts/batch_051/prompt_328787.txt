Ok, so I've landed on the anti-KK side and I want to know what could dislodge me. I reject the KK principle — knowing doesn't entail being in a position to know that you know — and I buy the core of the anti-luminosity argument: run a smooth series of cases from clearly being in a condition C to clearly not being in C, and luminosity plus a margin-for-error premise forces C to hold the whole way across, so no non-trivial condition is luminous. I don't need that argument restated; the thing I want is the case against it.

Tell me what would count against rejecting KK. Not just as a logical possibility, but as a live option. Here are the places I suspect the view is vulnerable, but I want you to work through them rather than just list them: (i) Are any phenomenal states genuinely self-presenting, so pain or a felt mood escapes the argument? If yes, is that a fatal concession or a manageable carve-out? (ii) Imagine a creature whose first-order states automatically generate reliable second-order awareness of themselves — would that give it luminosity for some condition, or is that just changing the stipulation? (iii) Doesn't the positive-introspection axiom (Kφ → KKφ, i.e. S4/S5-style epistemic logics) treat KK as near-definitional for idealized rational agents, and does the anti-luminosity argument give us reason to say those logics don't fit knowledge, or should the logics make us suspicious of the argument? (iv) The anti-luminosity argument looks suspiciously like a sorites argument. If someone is an epistemicist about vegueness and accepts sharp cutoffs nobody can know, does the margin-for-error premise lose its force and luminosity sneak back in? (v) Does the argument prove too much — does it also show that being in a position to know isn't luminous, and would that destabilize the standard it uses to reject KK?

Sort these into fatal and merely awkward, say which ones a committed anti-KK person would themselves grant as counting against them rather than dismiss, and rank the defeaters. I'm also honestly worried the anti-KK view has a unfalsifiable feel, because it can always redescribe apparent positive introspection as a coincidence between first- and second-order states. Tell me whether that insulation is a genuine problem or the normal state of philosophy. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
