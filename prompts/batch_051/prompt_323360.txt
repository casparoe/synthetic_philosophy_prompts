I've been going back and forth on a forum with someone about which "repugnant markets" are actually wrong as opposed to just viscerally off-putting, and its made me realize I don't have a stable ordering of where I'd draw the line. I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs, so my prior is that a fair amount of the disgust around these markets is status quo bias or scope insensitivity wearing a principled costume, but I also don't want to be the person who says "two consenting adults, therefore it's fine" and hand-waves desperation, asymmetric information, corruption of non-market norms, and the way some sales change what the thing is to the people involved.

Could you score each of the following markets from 0 to 100 on how much a basically liberal society should permit it, where 0 means ban outright and actually enforce the ban, and 100 means treat it like selling apples, subject only to ordinary consumer-protection and contract law? The list: buying a kidney from a living donor; selling your vote; charging huge markups for bottled water and generators after a hurricane; paying someone to carry a pregnancy (commercial surrogacy); paying patients to take antipsychotic medication they'd otherwise skip; paying healthy volunteers for a phase I drug trial; a market in carbon emission rights; paying to jump the queue at airport security or a theme park; and outright selling admission to a public university. After the scores, group them into rough tiers and say why each lands where it does. I care more about what you'd defend on reflection than about current law, though it's fine to mention where enforcement costs or black-market pressures change the calculus. Please write it all as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just an email-like block of text.
