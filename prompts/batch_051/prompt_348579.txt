i've been kicking around a phrase i made up, 'archive gravity', and i want someone to take it seriously for a minute and then tell me where it collapses. the idea is that records have mass. once a person or family gets into the archive, later historians and archivists get pulled back to them because they're findable, catalogued, already cited, so the file keeps thickening. meanwhile the people who were never recorded don't just stay silent, they get more silent over time, because there's no entry point pulling anyone toward them, no index, no finding aid, no grant. the loop feeds itself. i first noticed it with my home town: the whole 19th century is written from the papers of two merchant families, and every new local history cites those same papers, so the rest of the town is a blank. and i think digitization makes it worse rather than fixing it — the already-scanned stuff is easy to download and cite and gets scanned again in better resolution, while the unscanned stuff rots in a basement. i know this overlaps with the usual line about archives and power and silences, but that always sounds like a description of the result, not the motor that keeps the silence running. so: is this an actual causal mechanism, or am i just dressing up 'researchers are lazy' as a law of nature? does it already exist under a real name somewhere? and where does my version go wrong — what does it get backwards, and is the digitization half even true?
