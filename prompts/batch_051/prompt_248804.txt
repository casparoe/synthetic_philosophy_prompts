In my work on the foundations of quantum statistical mechanics, I have developed a strong habit of reaching for one vocabulary whenever I read metaphysics: the standard treatment of indistinguishable particles. I am at ease with permutation invariance, the symmetrization postulate, bosonic and fermionic Fock spaces, labels without physical content, the Gibbs paradox, and the idea that exchanging two particle labels corresponds to no physical operation at all.

Lately I have been reading the philosophical debate over the identity of indiscernibles and over Black's two-sphere universe — a universe containing nothing but two qualitatively identical iron spheres — and I find myself translating every move in that debate into my own formalism. I would like that translation done explicitly and critically, not as a loose analogy.

Please restate the main components of the debate in the vocabulary of the theory of indistinguishable quantum particles. Cover at least: the principle of the identity of indiscernibles in its strong and weak forms; the distinction between qualitative and numerical identity; Black's two-sphere thought experiment; the role of haecceities or bare particulars; and what is at stake for bundle theory as against substratum theory. For each component, say what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all.

Then give me the residue report. Which distinctions or questions in the philosophical debate cannot be expressed in my vocabulary, even approximately? Is that residue a sign that the physics vocabulary is missing something, a sign that the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it looks once it is restated, or a sign that the two debates are doing different work? Be explicit about the possibility of a mixed answer, and do not assume that the physics settles the metaphysics: I want an honest translation, not an annexation. Treat the residue as the interesting part.

Please give the answer as a JSON object with the following schema.

- translation_table: an array of objects, each with:
  - philosophical_component (string): the claim, distinction, or argument from the philosophical debate being translated.
  - physics_counterpart (string): the closest notion in the theory of indistinguishable quantum particles.
  - fidelity (string): exactly one of clean, distorted, or no counterpart.
  - what_is_lost_or_changed (string): what the translation fails to carry, or what changes in transit.
- residue_report: an object with:
  - components_without_counterpart (array of strings): the items from the philosophical debate that have no counterpart.
  - interpretation (string): whether the residue indicates that the physics vocabulary is missing something, that the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it looks, or that the two debates are doing different work; a mixed answer is acceptable.
  - verdict (string): a two-sentence bottom line that stands on its own.
