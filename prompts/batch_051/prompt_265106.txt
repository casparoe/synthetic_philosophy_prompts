ok so I've been turning over Molyneux's problem — the one where a guy blind from birth can tell a cube from a sphere by touch, and the question is wether he could tell them apart by sight the first time he sees them. For ages this was a pure thought experiment, and philosophers used it to argue about whether shape concepts come from experience or are somehow built in. But now it's actually been run, or close enough: there are programs in India that do cataract surgery on congenitally blind kids, and the early results seem to be that right after surgery they can't match a shape they know by touch to the one they see, though some get the hang of it within days. So here's what I want to know: does Molyneux's problem survive that? Does the data settle the original question, or does the philosophical question just get moved somewhere else? I want you to say which version of the question is settled and which stays open, what the thought experiment needed to be true in order to arbitrate between empiricism and nativism, and whether the new data actually bears on that.
