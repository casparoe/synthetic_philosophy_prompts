I cover statistics, physics, and the philosophy of science for a general-interest magazine, and I am several weeks into a feature on a dispute I find genuinely unresolved: whether there is any principled, objective rule for turning background knowledge—especially near-total ignorance—into a prior probability distribution. The piece will be built around the maximum-entropy principle, which says to choose the prior of greatest entropy subject to whatever constraints you accept, and around the usual counter-moves: a prior flat in one variable is badly non-flat in a nonlinear reparametrization of it; entropy itself depends on how you partition the space of possibilities; and transformation-group arguments of the sort that motivate Jeffreys-style invariant priors seem to pull in a different direction from maximum entropy. Since the magazine has a no-footnotes house style and my editor wants the conceptual argument rather than a historiography, I need no bibliography, no reading list, and no quoted passages.

What I need from you is the specification I can use as the article's evaluative spine. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of objective priors—or any rule for converting background information into a prior—must meet: the cases it must get right (ignorance over a location parameter, ignorance over a scale parameter, a known mean, a known covariance, discrete partitions of differing granularity), the results or principles it must not contradict, and the distinctions it must respect (such as ignorance versus a definite constraint, and the parameter as a mere representation versus the problem itself). For each desideratum, give the motivating case or consideration, and mark whether it is a hard requirement or merely the preference of one camp. Then state clearly which subsets of the desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most to exclude the familiar proposals—indifference or flat priors, Jeffreys-style invariant priors, and maximum entropy. A table or structured list is fine.

End with a one-sentence summary of the central tension in the desiderata, as the final line, beginning with "Summary:".
