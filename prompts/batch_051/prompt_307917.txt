The challenge is: what fact about me makes it true that I meant plus and not quus? It can't just be the examples I've done, because both rules fit them equally. Kripke's Wittgenstein then offers a sceptical solution: give up on there being a fact of the matter about which rule I meant; saying I meant plus is a move my linguistic community accepts and reinforces, not a report of some inner state or relation.
