I keep returning to the claim that an ant colony is a single cognitive subject — that the colony, not any ant, is what remembers, decides, and perceives — and I want to know whether that claim can be tested rather than left as a metaphor. My attempt at an operationalization is this: treat the colony as a distributed system in which no node holds a representation of the overall state, instrument it, and selectively disrupt the communication channels (pheromone trails, food exchange, antennal contact) and the state-like residues (pheromone decay rates, nest structure). Then ask whether colony-level behavior such as foraging, nest choice, and damage response shows evidence of an internal model that can be run offline, so that the colony tracks states it has not directly sensed — for example, altering foraging in response to a change in a region no ant has visited — rather than running fixed stimulus-response loops. What is the strongest version of this experiment? What result would genuinely favor the reading that the colony itself is a mind over the reading that the colony is a mind-like process with no subject? And after a favorable result, what would still remain unsettled — is the residual gap a practical limit of the method or a defect in the question itself?
