My friend does safety reviews for a robotics startup that says its machines run on Asimov's Three Laws, and I've been keeping a list of her rulings. Past cases:
- a factory robot knocked a worker out of the way of a swinging girder and broke his arm doing it; she said that's not a first-law violation
- a home robot, following its owner's order, slapped the owner's ex hard enough to bruise; she called that a violation
- a care robot refused a direct order to leave a patient alone in a hot car, where the patient could easily overheat; she said the refusal was correct
- a robot restrained its sleepwalking owner, dislocating the owner's shoulder, to stop them walking out a third-story window; she said no violation
- a home robot, on its owner's order, told the owner's anxious spouse as a joke that the house was on fire, and the spouse had a panic attack; she called that a first-law violation even though the robot never touched anyone

Next week's case: a warehouse robot sees a heavy crate sliding toward one worker, and the only way to stop it is to shove a pallet jack into its path, which would then hit a second worker and give them a bad sprain. If the robot does nothing, the first worker gets a broken leg. What do you think she'd rule, what reading of the First Law is she actually running, which other readings fit her record just as well, and which further case would settle which one she's using?
