I've been re-watching Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind and trying to make sense of it through Douglas Hofstadter's idea that a self isn't just the meat inside one skull but a pattern that gets partly copied into the minds of people who know you, so that in some real sense you survive in other brains. Here's what's bugging me. If that's true, then when Lacuna erases Clementine from Joel's mind, they're not just deleting a memory; they're killing the copy of Clementine's self that lived in Joel. But does that mean the erasure is morally wrong in a way the film doesn't quite face? And after the procedure, Joel still drives to Montauk and feels the urge to go to the beach house; the film treats this as a trace of his love, not as Clementine persisting. Is that residue better read as a surviving fragment of a distributed self, or as something else entirely—like a bodily habit or a crack in the erasure technology? I also want to know whether the ending, where Mary sends the files and they choose to try again, supports or resists Hofstadter: they have no episodic memory of each other, so if the distributed-self view is right, what exactly is left of each of them in the other, and is it enough to count as the same couple? Do a careful reading of the film through that lens, being honest about where the film pushes back or where Hofstadter's view would make the film darker than its ending wants to be.
