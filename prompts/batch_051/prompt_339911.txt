I've been arguing with a friend about existential emptiness—the kind people describe as a void, a felt absence where meaning or desire should be. She thinks the experience is basically veridical: when it hits, you're noticing something real about the shape of a human life, that the projects and values you have don't bottom out in anything that could fully justify them. I lean the other way: it's an affective state like nausea or low mood, with a characteristic thought afterwards, but the thought shouldn't be trusted because the feeling is doing the convincing.

We both read Tolstoy's A Confession recently. There's a part where he describes having family, health, income, and even literary fame, and still being overtaken by a kind of inner stoppage: the usual 'why?' and 'so what?' questions kept rising up inside ordinary activities, and no answer he could find would put them to rest. (I'm recalling, not quoting.) She reads this as evidence that the void can show up even when circumstances are good, so it can't be just situational unhappiness; I read it as evidence that certain reflective habits can drain felt significance out of anything, which is compatible with the affective-only story.

So I want your actual credence, not a 'both interpretations have something to them' hedge. The claim is: the feeling of existential emptiness/void is best interpreted as a veridical awareness of a genuine feature of human life (roughly, that our ordinary projects and values lack an ultimate justificatory foundation), rather than as a non-representational mood state that produces a false seeming of insight. Give me a number. Then decompose it: how much rides on whether moods can have representational content at all; how much on whether this particular mood tracks something about life's foundations rather than just about a person's stress, sleep, or neurochemistry; how much on the reliability of introspection during existential moods; and any other term you think belongs. For each sub-question, give a rough probability and a one-line justification, then show how you combined them. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence would move your credence most.
