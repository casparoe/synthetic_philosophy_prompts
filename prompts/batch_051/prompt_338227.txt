I'm a novelist, and I've got a problem with the book I'm drafting. Two characters, both plausable and neither evil, have to co-sign a statement on the moral difference between killing and letting die, and I want the document they produce to be genuinely good philosophy rather than stage machinery. The characters are: (a) a distinctionist, who thinks the doing/allowing boundary carries intrinsic moral weight — other things equal, making a death happen is worse than letting it happen; and (b) an equivalist, who thinks the bare act/omission difference does no moral work on its own, so any real difference in a case neccessarily traces to something else, like intention, cost, prior commitment, or reliance. They've agreed to treat the familiar cases as shared data rather than conclusions: the bath case where one cousin pushes a child under and the other just watches him slip under for the inheritance, the trolley, pulling versus withdrawing a ventilator, and the shallow pond with the passing walker who does nothing. Don't quote or cite anything for me; just draft the document.

Produce the joint statement as a working adversarial-collaboration text with four parts. (1) A numbered list of agreed claims that are substantive but not slanted: claims about what the distinction amounts to, what the cases do and don't establish, and what constraints any defensible account has to respect — including, if you think they need it, the difference between withholding and withdrawing and between killing and not rescuing. (2) A disagreements table, one row per sub-question, with the exact wording each side would sign for its own position and a note on why each side would call the other's wording loaded. (3) A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them, with tests or cases both sides agree would bear on each crux, each side's prediction recorded in advance, and what outcome each side would count as evidence against its own view. (4) A short note on which part of the dispute you suspect is purely verbal, if any. Don't settle the debate for them; the point is to leave the remaining disagreement exact and honest. And please write the whole thing in British English, in plain precise prose a novel character would plausibly sign — I'd rather have an edge than a polite compromise.
