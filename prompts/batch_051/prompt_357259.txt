I'm putting together an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been fighting about Sen's liberal paradox for way too long. One is a Paretian welfarist: he thinks the lesson of the paradox is that rights have to be justified in welfare terms, that Pareto is non-negotiable as a consistency condition, and that something has to give on the liberalism side—probably by weakening minimal liberalism so it only bites when the stakes for others are small. The other is a rights-first person: she thinks the lesson is that Pareto isnt sacred, that Sen's theorem just shows you cant stuff rights into a social choice function over final outcomes, and that rights should be modeled as game forms or assignments of protected choices rather than as a condition on preference aggregation.

Draft the joint statement they'd actually sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want it in this format:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims. Cover the formal setup (social choice functions, unrestricted domain, Pareto, Sen's condition L/minimal liberalism), what Sen's theorem actually proves, and what it doesn't prove. Make these substantive, not filler.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give the exact parallel wording each side would accept for its own view, so the table shows precisely where they part ways.

3. A cruxes section. For each crux, give a concrete case or model both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction about it in advance.

I suspect some of the cruxes are these, but check them rather than just listing them: whether rights should be a condition on the social choice function itself or on the underlying game form; whether the real problem is meddlesome preferences rather than a conflict between rights and Pareto; whether narrowing the domain of admissible preferences is a genuine solution or a dodge; and whether the liberal condition is really about decisiveness over a personal pair or about something thicker like personal autonomy. Also say explicitly which disagreements, if any, dissolve as verbal or as modeling choices once you fix a formalism.
