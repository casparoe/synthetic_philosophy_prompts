I recently lost my father, an intensely private man, and a company has offered to build a griefbot: a conversational simulation of him, trained on his correspondence and voice messages. I find myself caught between a theory I otherwise find compelling and an intuition I cannot shake. The theory is naturalistic and parsimonious: the dead are gone; nothing can harm or wrong them; duties that appear to be owed to the dead must reduce to duties to the living, or to the living's memory and grief. On this theory, if no survivor is harmed and the simulation eases my mourning, using it is not only permissible but probably good. And yet I recoil: training a model on his private words, including letters meant only for me, feels like a violation—and a violation of him, not merely of decorum or of my own wellbeing. I am not asking to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives way, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that gives way, and whether there is a reading on which both survive. Set out the strongest version of each move—debunking the intuition, revising the theory, or reconciling the two—and then say which move is least costly and why.
