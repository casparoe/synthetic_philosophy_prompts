A national government, aiming at degrowth in high-consumption sectors, replaces its existing flat carbon tax with the following mechanism. Each year the government computes a carbon-equivalent footprint for every adult from mandatory reporting by utilities, fuel suppliers, airlines, and large retailers. It then sets a personal subsistence threshold at the 40th percentile of the resulting distribution. People whose footprint is at or below the threshold receive an equal share of the carbon-tax revenue as a dividend. People above the threshold receive nothing, and they must pay a progressive surcharge on the following year's consumption unless they either complete forty hours of certified conservation service or purchase a costly frugality exemption from the government. The threshold is recalculated annually, so it falls when median consumption falls. Firms continue to pay the carbon tax, but they may deduct from their tax liability the wages they pay employees for certified conservation-service time.

Analyze this mechanism as an institutional designer would: what incentives it creates for households, firms, and the administering agency; where it will be gamed; what second-order effects are likely; and whether its failure modes can be corrected without abandoning the whole mechanism for a plain carbon tax. Respond as a numbered list with exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
