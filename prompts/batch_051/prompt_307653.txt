I chair the review board of a city historical commission. We are revising the interpretive signage at the site of the Ash Grove neighborhood, a predominantly Black working-class district that was cleared beginning in 1961 for construction of the Eastbank Expressway. The descendants' association of displaced families has asked the commission to state on the new signs that the expressway route was deliberately chosen to destroy their neighborhood as retaliation for the neighborhood's leadership of a 1957 streetcar-fare boycott. Their evidence is the testimony of now-elderly residents, some of whom say city employees told their parents at the time that the organizers had brought the destruction on themselves; the fact that Ash Grove produced a disproportionate share of the boycott's leadership; and the disappearance of certain early planning documents in a 1960 fire at the city engineer's office. The surviving written record, by contrast — planning studies from 1954 through 1959, correspondence between the city and the state highway department, and finance-committee minutes — supports the view that the route was selected from four alternatives because it was cheapest per mile, required the fewest expensive grade changes, and carried the most projected cross-town traffic. None of the surviving documents mention the boycott or the neighborhood's organizing. The descendants' association replies that the missing documents are the ones that would have shown intent, and that a deputy city engineer named in the missing files brokered the property purchases and later became a partner in the construction firm that won the grading contract.

Our board is divided. One subgroup, led by our professional archivist, wants the signs to say that the archival record does not support the retaliation account and to describe that account only as a belief held by descendants. Another subgroup, including our two members who are descendants of Ash Grove families, wants the signs to state the retaliation account as fact and to describe the surviving record as the city's self-exculpating version. The full board votes next Thursday. Please draft the short memo I will circulate as chair before that vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action: the sign wording the board should adopt, plus any accompanying measures such as a corrections or additions procedure if you think one is warranted. It should give the reasons for your recommendation and honestly address the strongest consideration on the side your recommendation does not adopt. We need a document the board can act on, not a general essay about memory versus history, and we need it not to simply split the difference.
