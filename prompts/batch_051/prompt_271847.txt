I have been trying to extract the actual content of a recurring debate about whether AI alignment is solvable "in principle," and it has become clear that the words "in principle" hide several separable claims. I have so far collected six candidate readings that appear in the literature and in informal arguments:

(1) Existence. "Solvable in principle" means there is a well-defined formal target and some algorithm, however resource-hungry, that would produce a system satisfying it; unsolvability would mean the problem is strictly ill-posed or logically impossible.

(2) Identifiability. It means the correct target specification can be recovered from finite behavioral evidence or revealed preferences given enough data; unsolvability here is an information-theoretic claim about underdetermination.

(3) Tractability. It means a physically feasible agent could compute the solution within the time, energy, and safety margins that are actually available before the system is deployed or overtaken.

(4) Robustness. It means the solution would continue to be aligned under self-modification, distribution shift, and interference from other agents, rather than holding only at the initial configuration.

(5) Verifiability. It means that a solution, once built, could be checked by some external observer or auditor who does not have to rely on the system's own claims about its future behavior.

(6) Coordination. It means the problem admits a solution even in a multi-actor world where deploying a misaligned system confers a local advantage, so an "in principle" solution must survive defection incentives, not merely describe what a single careful actor would do.

I believe these six are distinct rather than restatements of one another. But I keep suspecting the list is incomplete, and that the missing sense is doing a fair amount of the argumentative work on one side of the debate. Give me one further reading of "solvable in principle" that is not a rewording, a special case, or a conjunction of the six above. Then explain briefly how it relates to the ones I listed, say honestly whether it is a good reading or a stretch, and give your view on which reading or readings the alignment debate is really about once the phrase is disambiguated.
