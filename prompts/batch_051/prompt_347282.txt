I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I am writing a seminar paper on the epistemology of interpretation. At our Davidson reading group last week, a colleague who works in formal semantics gave the following summary of radical interpretation, and I have been uncertain how much of it to trust:

"In radical interpretation, the interpreter has no dictionary and no prior grip on the speaker's beliefs; her evidence consists, roughly, of which sentences the speaker holds true under which observable circumstances. Davidson argues that interpretation is possible only if the interpreter adopts the principle of charity, which says to choose the interpretation that makes the speaker's beliefs agree with the interpreter's own as much as possible. On this view, an interpretation on which the speaker is massively mistaken must always be defective as an interpretation, since interpretation and massive error cannot coexist. Davidson then asks us to imagine an omniscient interpreter. Because even an omniscient interpreter would have to find any speaker she can interpret mostly in agreement with her, and because an omniscient interpreter's beliefs are by definition true, it follows that any creature with beliefs must have mostly true beliefs. Radical interpretation thus shows that global skepticism is incoherent: the very possibility of belief guarantees that most beliefs are correct. The familiar objection that charity simply projects the interpreter's own worldview onto the speaker is no threat, because for Davidson interpretation is all there is to meaning."

The summary matches my memory of the primary texts in broad outline, but I suspect it flattens a few distinctions — in particular, the difference between charity as a constitutive constraint on interpretation and charity as a rule for maximizing agreement, and the inference from "an omniscient interpreter would attribute mostly true beliefs" to "most beliefs are actually true." I would like an accuracy audit I can genuinely lean on while writing the paper. Could you go through the summary claim by claim, separate what Davidson would accept from what he would qualify or resist, and then say whether the anti-skeptical conclusion survives once the glosses are corrected? If my own suspicions are off base, I would want to know that too.
