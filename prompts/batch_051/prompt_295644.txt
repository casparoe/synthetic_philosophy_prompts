I run a discussion group on wild animal welfare and moral-circle stuff, and I keep noticing people talk past each other because they all say "sentience matters" but mean wildly different things by it. I want to make a little pamphlet quiz: "Where do you actually draw the line?" It should sort people on whether moral status comes in degrees, and how far down it runs—pigs, shrimp, bees, flatworms, thermostats.

So: write me eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask "do you think sentience is a matter of degree?" and no answer option should just be the name of a position.

Have the scoring key map answer patterns onto at least these five positions:

(a) hard-threshold binary sentientism: moral status switches on all at once, and below the line it's zero, full stop;  
(b) degree-of-valence gradualism: status tracks how much felt pleasure/pain a system has, so insects count a little if they have any, and thermostats get zero;  
(c) functionalist gradualism: any goal-directed control loop has a little status, even a thermostat or a prediction-error minimizer, though maybe negligibly;  
(d) valence essentialism: systems without an actual like/dislike reward architecture count zero even if they behave exactly like ones with it;  
(e) precautionary uncertainty: nothing below the line gets discounted to zero, because of how much hangs on being wrong.

If there's a sixth natural position, add it, but don't pad.

Use these cases at least once: a bee grooming an injured leg and then going back to forage; a housefly struggling in a spiderweb; a thermostat holding temperature with a single negative-feedback loop; a 302-neuron nematode that writhes when poked; a disembodied AI trained only to minimize prediction error that outputs "I am in pain" but has no reward or punishment signal; a plant root that grows around an obstacle.

After the key, point out any answer patterns that dont map to a position, and for each say whether nobody holds it because it's incoherent, because it's just unattractive, or because it's genuinely unexplored. If two positions end up with the same scoring pattern, flag that instead of pretending the quiz separates them.

Write for a mixed group of wildlife volunteers and skeptical engineers, so keep jargon low but don't hide the real distinctions.

End with a one-sentence summary of the quiz as the last line, in the form \answer{...}.
