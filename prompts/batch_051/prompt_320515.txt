I keep noticing something in how people reason about coincidences, and I haven't found a name for it. I'd like to know if the thing itself has an established label, and if not, what you'd call it and exactly where its edges are.

Nearly everyone I know handles the birthday fact fine: put two dozen or so people in a room and it's more likely than not that two of them share a birthday, even though the chance that any given person would share yours is tiny. The same people will also shrug off one-in-a-million events by saying that in a big enough population, "someone" has to be that one; a lottery winner, a rare cancer survivor, whatever. Nobody is amazed that, somewhere, a one-in-a-million thing happened today.

But flip it to the first person and the same people get spooked. A collegue's mother died on the date he'd already booked for his wedding, years later, and he talks about it as a sign. A statistically literate friend thinks of a song and then it comes on the instant she starts the car; she says the odds are astronomical, and if you point out how many songs she's thought of and how many times she's started that car, she says no, it's different because it was this song and this time and it happened to her. In the birthday room, by contrast, nobody cares which two people share the birthday. I can feel there might be a real asymmetry in there somewhere — something about being the person the event is indexed to instead of an observer of the whole room at once — but I also suspect a lot of the time it's simply a mistake.

So my question is: is there a term for this specific thing, the shift from "some rare event X happened to someone in a large set" (unsurprising) to "this rare event X happened to me, now" (surprising)? If there is, what exactly does it cover, and how is it different from plain old cherry-picking or from the birthday fact itself? If there's no standard term, I'd want you to coin one: define it, give a boundary case or two, and say whether the first-person version is ever rationally different from the third-person one.
