Public-health people measure tiny mortality risks in micromorts — a one-in-a-million chance of dying. And once you look through that lens, its kind of weird how differently the same risk gets treated depending on where it comes from: we shrug at the slow accrual of micromorts from driving everywhere, but a one-in-a-million cancer risk in drinking water gets a whole regulatory standard written around it, especially when the risk is imposed rather than chosen. What theory of risk, or of death, or of free choice does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct it as charitably as you can, say where it's underdetermined by any single theory, and tell me what would actually have to change if we held drinking water and driving to the same standard.
