I'm giving the toast at my best friend's wedding and I'm a little out of my depth. Him and his partner are both way into Bertrand Russell's writing about marriage and sexual morality — I've absorbed a lot of it from them over the years but I'm not an academic — and they've asked me to make sure the toast is actually faithfull to his line. The parts they keep coming back to: jealousy is not proof of love, it's the desire to own another person dressed up as devotion; a marriage has to be able to end without either person becoming a villain or the one who failed; and love is a good in itself while it lasts, not a down payment on forever. They don't want any talk of completing each other, no making forever the whole point, nothing that sounds like God or the state is what makes it real, and no hint that being exclusive is what makes their commitment valuable. The catch is the room. My friend's mom is a pretty traditional Catholic and a chunk of the older guests think a marriage that doesn't put forever-and-exclusive at the center isn't a marriage at all, just two people keeping their options open. The couple think the opposite — that a marriage you choose freely each day is more serious than one held together by a promise you made when you barely knew each other — and they want that said without making their relatives feel attacked. I need something like a five-minute toast that the couple will recognize as genuinely Russellian, but that doesn't leave grandma feeling like she just watched someone burn the idea of marriage down. Please start with the speech itself — no preface, no lead-up, just the first line of the toast.
