I help moderate a small online forum in my town, and for the past few weeks the moderator group has been arguing about whether to remove a member's posts that denigrate a local religious minority. One moderator keeps ending the argument by saying "your right to swing your fist ends where my nose begins," as if that settles it; another keeps answering with Mill's harm principle, as if it were the same point stated more respectably. I have no academic background, but I have been trying to work this out on my own, and I have come to think that the proverb is carrying several distinct claims at once, and that different people in the room are silently relying on different ones.

What I would like is for you to untangle the proverb rather than pick a single interpretation. Take "your right to swing your fist ends where my nose begins" and lay out the distinct precise principles it could be used to assert, from the nearly trivial to the radical. For each reading, give the principle in one sentence, a short example that separates it from its neighbors, and a verdict: platitude, substantive and defensible, or false. I especially need attention paid to what gets to count as the "nose" — actual physical injury, a credible threat, emotional distress, offense taken, harm to a group rather than a person, diffuse harm to trust or to the character of public discussion — and to whether the saying is about legal coercion, private consequences such as deletion or deplatforming, or mere moral criticism. Then tell me which of these readings would actually justify removing the posts under something like Mill's harm principle, and which readings the proverb, taken literally or naturally, can carry. I want to see whether the moderator who quotes it is smuggling in a much stronger claim than the platitude everyone would accept, and whether the Mill principle genuinely gets them to their conclusion.

Keep the whole thing under 1,000 words, and present the readings as a numbered menu, with the final paragraph devoted to which reading or readings the deletion decision would actually depend on.
