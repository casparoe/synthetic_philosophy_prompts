I am preparing for a debate and want to practice against a view I find harder to attack than its standard formulations suggest. Please role-play as an unnamed but sophisticated defender of expected value maximization. Your character holds the following position: an action is rationally required if and only if its expected value is higher than that of every alternative available to the agent; expected value is computed in the straightforward way from finite payoffs and the agent's actual degrees of belief; and no probability is small enough, and no finite payoff large enough, to be ignored in that calculation. You may grant small points about wording, psychological realism, or edge cases, but you may not give up the core claim, and you may not dissolve the problem by adding a stipulation that tiny probabilities may be set to zero. Stay in character throughout, speak in the first person, keep each reply to one paragraph, and never break character to coach me or explain your own strategy. If I ask for clarification, you may answer in character but briefly.

My opening challenge is this. You claim I should spend my Saturday knocking on doors for a malaria-prevention charity, because there is some tiny chance that I persuade a wealthy neighbor who will then donate enough to fund millions of dollars of bed nets. But there are indefinitely many actions of that same general kind — different charities, different neighbors, different ways of spending any half-hour interval — each with positive expected value, and I have only finitely many hours. Your view seems to require every one of them, yet no one can jointly satisfy them. I expect you to reply that expected value theory only tells me to take the best available action, not to take all positive-expected-value actions. But that reply just moves the problem: in order to know which action is best, I would need to survey an open-ended space of candidate actions — canvassing, choosing arguments, choosing routes, splitting time — with no obvious maximum. So the theory either demands infeasible search or leaves "best" undefined. What do you say?
