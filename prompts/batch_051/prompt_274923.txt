I am a novelist working on a hard-science-fiction novel in which a comprehensive astronomical survey has just concluded and finds no sign of life or technology beyond Earth. The novel dramatizes, through several characters, the disagreement over the Fermi paradox—why the galaxy is silent. I want each character to stand for a distinct, defensible position rather than a mood, so I need to know where the real fault lines in the Great Filter argument run.

Assume the role of a philosopher of science with a quantitative bent. Spend time brainstorming the sub-questions before committing, but end with a specific number. Here is the proposition: Conditional on the observed silence—no signals, no megastructures, no credible technosignatures after a thorough search—the Great Filter lies ahead of us rather than behind us. By ahead I mean the decisive obstacles that keep a typical Earth-like lineage from producing a long-lived, readily detectable technological civilization are still in our future, so a species at humanity's current stage would probably not get through them. By behind I mean the decisive obstacles are already in our past, and our lineage has passed the hard part. The Great Filter, for reference, is the idea that some rare step in the path from dead matter to a long-lived, detectable technological civilization explains the silence.

Give me the decomposition, not just a number. Break the question into components—for example, how likely life is to arise on a habitable planet; how likely complex or eukaryotic-like life is given life; how likely a technological species is given complex life; how likely a long-lived, visibly expansionary civilization is given technology; and how likely the apparent silence is even if such civilizations exist, whether because detection is harder than expected or because advanced civilizations reliably converge on some non-detectable equilibrium. Where relevant, say explicitly how observer selection—the fact that we can reason about this only because our own lineage passed every earlier step—should condition the inference that the hard step is behind us rather than ahead. Assign a rough probability or range to each component, show how they combine into your final credence that the filter is ahead rather than behind, and state which component the final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me which single argument or piece of evidence would most change your mind about the final number, which component it would act through, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.

The novel does not need a survey of the literature. It needs a sharp, exposed estimate with its weak points marked.
