Suppose you are grading the following exam question in an upper-level course on cooperative game theory. Grade the student's answer out of 10 and provide point-by-point comments, not just an overall impression. Identify exactly where credit is earned and where it is lost, and give the correct computation for anything the student got wrong. Write the grade and comments in American English.

Question. Let the disagreement point be \((0,0)\), and let the feasible set be
\[
S=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,0),(0,2),(2,1)\}.
\]
For any compact convex set \(T\) containing the disagreement point, define the ideal point
\[
b(T)=\bigl(\max\{u_1:(u_1,u_2)\in T\},\ \max\{u_2:(u_1,u_2)\in T\}\bigr).
\]
The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is the point \(\lambda b(T)\), with \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), for which \(\lambda\) is as large as possible while still lying in \(T\). The Nash solution is the point of \(T\) maximizing \(u_1u_2\).

(a) Find the Nash solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution of \(S\), showing your work.

(b) State the standard axiomatizations of the two solutions and explain what they imply about the relationship between the two solutions in this example.

Student answer. The ideal point is \(b(S)=(3,2)\). For Nash, the product \(u_1u_2\) is maximized on the Pareto frontier. The frontier consists of the segment from \((0,2)\) to \((2,1)\) and the segment from \((2,1)\) to \((3,0)\). On the first segment, the product is \(x(2-x/2)\), whose maximum on \([0,2]\) occurs at \(x=2\), giving the point \((2,1)\). On the second segment, the product is \(x(3-x)\), whose maximum on \([2,3]\) also occurs at the endpoint \((2,1)\). Hence the Nash solution is \((2,1)\). For Kalai–Smorodinsky, the solution is the feasible point on the Pareto frontier closest to the ideal point \((3,2)\). That point is \((2,1)\), since it has distance \(\sqrt{2}\) from the ideal point and every other frontier point is farther away. Therefore the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is also \((2,1)\), and the two solutions coincide. The standard axiomatizations are: Nash is characterized by Pareto optimality, symmetry, scale covariance, and independence of irrelevant alternatives; Kalai–Smorodinsky is characterized by Pareto optimality, symmetry, scale covariance, and individual monotonicity. Thus in this example the axiom on which the two characterizations differ does not produce a difference in the selected outcome.
