I'm teaching a two-hour evening seminar on Kavka's toxin puzzle to a reading group of about fifteen people — mostly working professionals with some background in economics or law but no formal philosophy beyond an introductory course. My goal is for them to leave able to state the puzzle cleanly, distinguish it from nearby puzzles about weakness of will and Newcomb's problem, and see why it is a problem about the rationality of intending rather than about drinking the toxin.

Here's the puzzle I plan to put in front of them: a wealthy benefactor offers you a million dollars if, by midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a non-lethal but sickening toxin tomorrow afternoon. The money is paid on the basis of your intention tonight, whether or not you actually drink it tomorrow. You know that when tomorrow afternoon arrives, you will have no reason to drink the toxin — it will only make you ill for a day — and no external constraint will make you drink it, since no one can verify what you intended. The puzzle is whether you can rationally form the intention, given that you know you will not, and rationally should not, drink the toxin when the time comes.

Don't explain the puzzle to me. What I want is a teaching plan. Specifically: what idea has to be in place before the puzzle even bites — I suspect something about intention being distinct from both belief and desire is the load-bearing piece, but I want the dependency structure laid out. What single example or running case should carry the whole session, and why not the obvious case of promising someone you will do something unpleasant? What misconceptions should I expect this audience to form — I already anticipate "can you trick yourself into intending" and "should you just drink it anyway to keep your word" — and at what point in the sequence should I head each one off? Finally, what one question at the end would show me whether they have actually got the puzzle rather than just learned to repeat the standard line, and what should I openly refuse to explain at this level?
