For a project in my psychology class's unit on decision-making under risk, I was given the anonymous response sheet of a participant, L, from an earlier session of a survey of hypothetical gambles. L's recorded choices were:

1. A 50% chance of $40 (otherwise $0) versus a 45% chance of $80 (otherwise $0). L chose the 45% chance of $80.
2. A sure $25 versus a 50% chance of $40 (otherwise $0). L chose the sure $25.
3. A 30% chance of $50 (otherwise $0) versus a 40% chance of $80 (otherwise $0). L chose the 40% chance of $80.
4. A sure $40 versus a 25% chance of $120 (otherwise $0). L chose the sure $40.

Next week, L will be asked to make two more choices:

5. A sure $30 versus an 80% chance of $45 (otherwise $0).
6. A 25% chance of $30 (otherwise $0) versus a 20% chance of $45 (otherwise $0).

I'm trying to predict L's answers before I see them. Please do the following: identify two or three decision rules that are still consistent with L's first four choices; say what each rule predicts for choice 5 and choice 6; give your best prediction of how L will choose in each, with reasons; explain whether L's two new choices, taken together, would satisfy the independence axiom of expected utility theory; and describe one further choice that would most cleanly settle which rule L is actually following.
