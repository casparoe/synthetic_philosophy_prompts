I have been trying to think through Du Bois's argument that for the Black artist there is no neutral art, that all art is inescapably propaganda, and I find myself simultaneously drawn to it and resistant.

As I understand the position, a work of art is an act in a world already structured by racial domination. It either advances the struggle for recognition and justice or it turns away from it, and turning away is itself a position. Propaganda here is not meant in the debased sense of slogans wearing the clothes of art; it means that every representation carries moral and political force, that beauty cannot be sealed off from truth and right, and that an artist who claims to work only for beauty has simply accepted the existing order. The view has genuine virtues: it gives the Black artist a criterion for sorting good work from bad under conditions of collective danger, it explains why a dominated community cannot treat art as decoration, and it unifies aesthetics with ethics and politics instead of leaving them to float apart.

But I cannot shake the intuition that a work's quality as art and its political effectiveness are different things, and that the difference runs in both directions. A painting can be aesthetically powerful while politically quiet, and a politically righteous work can be artistically weak. If that distinction is real, then art cannot simply be judged as propaganda, because propaganda asks whether a work moves people toward the cause, while art, I want to say, asks whether the work itself is good. I find myself wanting to insist that a Black artist who makes a still life with no visible political content may still be doing something valuable, and that evaluating the work only by its ideological function misses what art is.

Please present each side at its strongest and then give me a negotiated verdict. Is my intuition a bit of art-for-art's-sake prejudice of exactly the kind the argument is meant to expose, so it should be debunked? Or should the propaganda view be amended so that art is propaganda in one fundamental respect while retaining an independent aesthetic standard, and would that amendment keep the original claim substantive or make it trivial? What would justify giving up the intuition, and what would justify revising or abandoning the theory? I want to know what each move costs, not simply which side wins.
