I am preparing a short written statement of my views on choice under uncertainty and social evaluation for a seminar, and I would like an outside consistency check before I circulate it. My current commitments are these:

(1) In Rawls's original position, the parties behind the veil should use maximin. Because they lack objective probabilities over which social position they will occupy, they should choose the basic structure that makes the worst-off representative position as good as possible.

(2) In my own one-shot decisions under complete ignorance, however, I favor minimax regret over maximin. Maximin seems to me too conservative: it fixes on the worst state and ignores the size of what one forgoes in states that do not turn out to be the worst.

(3) I accept Harsanyi's impartial-observer result as sound. If a rational and impartial evaluator assigns equal probability to being any member of society and then maximizes expected utility, social alternatives are ranked by average utility, and I regard that as the strongest formal case for a utilitarian social criterion.

(4) Under known probabilities, I am an unreserved expected-utility maximizer, including the independence axiom, and I accept ex ante Pareto: an arrangement that gives every person at least as much expected utility and at least one person strictly more is socially better.

(5) I also accept a menu-independence condition on decision rules: if I would choose action A over action B from the two-element feasible set {A, B}, then adding some further action C while leaving A and B available should not make B the chosen action.

I can no longer tell which of these are merely in apparent tension and which are genuinely incompatible. Please audit the set: rank the tensions by severity, distinguish the apparent tensions that dissolve under analysis from the real ones, and for each real tension say what would have to be revised and what the least costly repair is.
