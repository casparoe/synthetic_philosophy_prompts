Please write the text of a commencement address for a graduating class of nurses. The address should be organized around the concept of supererogation, understood as action that is good or admirable but beyond what duty requires. The room will include new graduates, their families, and experienced nurses who have watched administrators quietly turn yesterday's heroism into today's staffing baseline. The speech should do three things: distinguish going beyond duty from having the boundaries of duty redrawn for you; warn the graduates that institutions will try to convert their willingness to do more into an expected minimum, without making them cynical about their profession; and preserve the thought that some acts of care are genuinely admirable rather than merely useful to an employer or naive. The philosophical idea should do structural work, not appear as a vocabulary item: what the speech can honestly praise depends on it. Write exactly five numbered points of the address, each one or two sentences long, with no greeting, no opening anecdote, and no closing benediction.
