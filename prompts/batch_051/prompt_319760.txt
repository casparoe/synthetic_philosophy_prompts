I've been vegan for close to a decade now, but I've only ever done it in rich-city conditions where tofu, lentils and oat milk are five minutes away. In vegan spaces I picked up the usual line: eating animal products is wrong, and I can't afford to is mostly an excuse. Then I spent a few months living with my partner's family in rural Mongolia. They keep goats, sheep and a few chickens; the milk, meat and eggs are a real chunk of the kids calories, and the nearest shop that sells dried beans is an hour away and not exactly affordable for them anyway. No one in my vegan circles back home would call that family moral failures, and if I'd sat there eating my imported protein bars while lecturing them, I'd have been the asshole. But those same people will absolutely tear into a middle-class Londoner for buying a supermarket chicken, even though she has way more alternatives than my in-laws do.

So there's the asymmetry: we judge the well-off person harshly and the poor pastoralist barely at all, for what we'd normally describe as the same underlying choice to eat animal products. The easy answer is that blame tracks how costly the alternatives are, and the Londoner has options. But I've watched the asymmetry survive cases where that explanation stops working. A rich Londoner with severe allergies and a history of an eating disorder who says she genuinely can't go vegan gets told she's making excuses, while the herder family gets sympathy. And a low-income family in a US food desert with no car and no nearby store selling cheap beans gets judged harsher than a family abroad whose access to beans is just as bad. So I suspect we're actually tracking how much someone looks like us and how close their failures are, not how hard the alternatives actually are.

Is there anything that definately justifies judging the Londoner but not the subsistence family, in a way that still holds in those nearby cases? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Each point should state one candidate justification for the asymmetry, test it against one of my nearby cases, and say whether it holds up or where it cracks.
