I came to philosophy late, after a career in field ecology, and I keep being struck by the way debates about the value of nature proceed without anything like the specification one would demand before building a model. I am not asking for a survey of positions; I want the desiderata first. What must an adequate theory of the value of nature and wilderness deliver if it is to serve as a working framework for conservation decisions? Write the requirements it has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely the commitments of some camp. I particularly want to know whether any subset of the genuine requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement eliminates or most strains the familiar views — nature has intrinsic value because it is wild or self-organizing, value is entirely contributed by human valuers, value reduces to ecosystem services. As part of this, say whether the three-way split between intrinsic, relational, and instrumental value is itself a requirement on a theory or already a theory in disguise. Do not bother with citations or reading lists; I want the criteria themselves to do the work, though a brief scorecard of the familiar views against the list is welcome if kept secondary.

To anchor the request, I have been turning over cases from my own experience: the last living individual of a taxon with no remaining breeding population; a restored wetland on an industrial site that is heavily managed and recent in origin yet functioning habitat; a handsome invasive tree whose removal is required for the benefit of a native plant community; and a wilderness whose value seems to consist partly in there being no one present to make use of it. A theory should get these cases right, or at least misclassify them for articulable reasons. Where should the line be drawn between valuing an individual organism, a population, a species, and a landscape; between moral considerability and intrinsic value; and between the value of outcomes and the value of processes such as wildness or natural selection? Those are the distinctions I have in mind. Tell me what belongs on the list and why, where the list is doing more genuine work than any theory built on top of it, and which requirement, if any, forces the deepest revision in the positions currently on the table.
