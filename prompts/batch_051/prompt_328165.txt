Machiavelli is famous for insisting that a prince or republic should rely on its own arms — citizen militias — rather than on mercenaries or auxiliaries, and he clearly admired the Swiss as an armed and free people. So time-travel him to the present and have him advise Switzerland (and Finland too if he thinks it helps) on defence policy. Switzerland still runs a militia-style conscription system from a position of long neutrality, but the question keeps coming up whether to scrap conscription for a smaller all-volunteer propfessional force. Finland keeps a broad conscription, sits on a long border with Russia, and recently joined NATO. Write the advice he'd give: keep conscription, go professional, or something in between, and why. Don't give me the lazy version where he just says militias are virtuous and mercenaries are bad. Work through where his framework strains: cyber, drones, special operations, training pipelines that take years, equipment a part-time soldier can't operate or maintain. Would he reinterpret what counts as a state's own arms? Would he split the difference — citizen-reservists for mass tasks, full-time professionals for the technical core? And what would he say to the objection that a conscript-heavy force is the wrong answer when your real problem is a big neighbour with a professional army, and what you need is a small number of superb units and top-shelf kit rather than a broad levy? Write the whole thing in American English.
