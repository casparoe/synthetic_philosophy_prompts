Ok so I'm taking a philosophy of music class and got into it with my housemate about whether music can be sad in itself. Her line is that a slow descending minor melody is sad the way a drooping willow is sad — the sadness is in the shape of the thing, before anyone learns to associate it with funerals or crying. I said she's smugling the listener back in, because a minor third is just a frequency ratio, and if you'd been raised somewhere that interval meant something else entirely you wouldn't hear sadness in it. She said: fine, test it. But I honestly don't know how to design that test without begging the question.

So how would you test whether sadness is in the music (or the acoustic features) rather than projected onto it by listeners? I want a concrete design: what you'd manipulate, what you'd measure, who you'd study, and what pattern of results would favor each side. Would people with no exposure to Western music help, or infants, or is that just moving the problem? Also pull apart "the music sounds sad" from "the music makes me feel sad" if that helps. Then be honest about the residue: what would even a clean positive result fail to settle, and is there a version of the question that just isn't empirically decidable?
