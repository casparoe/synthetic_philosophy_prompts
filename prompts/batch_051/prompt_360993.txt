Last night I had a long argument with a friend who defends scientific realism against Larry Laudan’s pessimistic meta-induction. She had the paper in front of her—Laudan’s article “A Confutation of Convergent Realism”—and she kept returning to the early passage in which he assembles his list. In paraphrase, the passage says that the history of science is full of theories that were once successful by the ordinary standards of their day, in that they explained the phenomena and generated predictions, but whose central theoretical entities we now believe never existed; among the cases he mentions are the crystalline spheres, the medical humours, the electrical effluvia, phlogiston, caloric, the vital force, and the electromagnetic ether.

Her verdicts on the earlier cases were as follows. She set aside the crystalline spheres, humoral medicine, the effluvia, and the vital force, saying that these were not sufficiently mature, or that their success did not sufficiently depend on the questionable entity. She conceded that phlogiston and caloric were genuine successes, but she denied that they were counterexamples to realism, because the successful quantitative content was, in her view, carried by relations that survived in the successor theories rather than by the posited substances themselves. She then granted exactly one of Laudan’s cases—the electromagnetic ether—as a real counterexample, because the mechanical ether was, she argued, indispensable to the predictive derivation of Maxwell’s wave equation and was later abandoned wholesale.

I want to press her on a new case: Newtonian mechanics, and in particular Newtonian gravity. Do you think she will call that a genuine counterexample to realism, as she did with the ether, or will she find some reason to set it aside? I am less interested in the answer itself than in the rule she is implicitly running. Please lay out the candidate rules that fit her verdicts on the earlier cases, say what each rule predicts about Newtonian gravity, give your best guess about her next verdict and why, and name a further historical case that would best tell the candidate rules apart.
