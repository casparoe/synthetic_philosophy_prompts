I've been thinking about the problem of other minds, and about the "main character" intuition: the sense that I directly know my own experience while everyone else's inner life is only ever inferred, which makes it tempting to treat other people as supporting characters or NPCs. I'd like you to reason through a counterfactual with me, making the chain of mechanisms and predictions explicit.

Suppose that starting tomorrow every human being involuntarily emits a faint "sentience signal" within a radius of about ten meters. When someone is nearby, you feel a continuous, low-resolution impression of their emotional state, bodily comfort or discomfort, and whether their attention is on you — enough to register in real time that there is something it is like to be them, but not enough to read specific thoughts, memories, or beliefs. The signal cannot be faked, blocked, or voluntarily suppressed, and everyone emits and receives it in the same way.

Predict how this world changes over the next two or three decades, reasoning through at least the following: (1) Does solipsism as a live skeptical worry disappear, or does it get pushed back a level — for example, toward doubting whether the signal itself is a real sign of consciousness rather than a shared illusion? (2) What happens to the main-character feeling? Does it weaken, persist, or get replaced by a different kind of first-person privilege? (3) How do ordinary cruelty, lying, rudeness, and indifference change when nearby people can feel some of the target's distress? (4) How do norms of privacy, emotional concealment, and emotional labor adapt when moods are semi-public? (5) What happens to art forms that depend on hidden inner lives, such as plot twists, unreliable narrators, or actors pretending to feel?

For each major prediction, tell me which links you are confident in and which are guesses, and state what would have to differ about human psychology or social organization for the prediction to fail. I'd prefer a disciplined account of causal pressures over a narrative.
