There's a debate I keep circling back to, and every time I think I've found the crux, the other side just re-describes what love is doing rather than answering the objection. On one side, love is an appraisal: the beloved's qualities — their warmth, their wit, the specific way they listen — are what ground the love, and "why do you love them" is a request you can answer by pointing at those qualities. The immediate problem is fungibility: if someone warmer and funnier showed up, the view seems to say you ought to trade up. So defenders complicate it: it isn't the qualities exactly, it's the beloved's personhood, or the sheer fact that they are this particular rational being, or the relationship and its history (this is the Frankfurt/Velleman/Kolodny neighborhood, roughly). On the other side, love is bestowal: loving someone is what makes them matter to you; the value doesn't pre-exist the love, the love confers it. From that angle the question "what do they have that someone else lacks" is already the wrong question. But then love looks unanswerable to reasons — it can't be misplaced, can't be anything but the lover's own making, and that also seems false, because love really does feel like it answers to something in the beloved and not just to my own investment.

My hunch is that they're answering different questions under one heading, but honestly I'm not sure, and I've seen to many smart people talk past each other here to trust that hunch. So here's what I want. These two sides have been sophisticated for decades and neither moves. Diagnose the impasse. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal dispute about the word "value," or two camps answering different questions — one asking what justifies love from the lover's point of view, the other asking what love does to a lover's evaluative landscape? And given your diagnosis, say what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a particular case, a redescription of the question, or an argument that the question as posed is badly formed and should be dropped. I'd rather have a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement than a verdict for one side.
