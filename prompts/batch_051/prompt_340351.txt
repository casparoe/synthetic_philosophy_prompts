I'm in a small philosophy reading group that meets in a pub, and for months we've been stuck on a dispute I assumed would have been sorted ages ago. The question is whether sentience, and the moral status that goes with it, is a matter of degree or whether there is a sharp threshold somewhere. My friend Rob holds that it must be a threshold: either there is something it is like to be a system or there isn't, and a thermostat is not a little bit conscious, so no amount of extra complexity can make something matter a little bit morally before it crosses the line. Priya, on the other hand, thinks the threshold talk is a leftover from our folk categories. She says sentience shades off across the animal kingdom, from rich self-aware experience down to insects and maybe even into simple learning systems or a thermostat, and that moral considerability fades out gradually rather than cutting off. The problem is that everyone in the group has heard every argument already: Rob points to the felt all-or-nothingness of his own waking consciousness, Priya points to the difficulty of saying where in evolution the threshold could possibly be, and then we go round in circles. I've began to suspect the whole thing is a verbal dispute, with Rob making a claim about the structure of duties and Priya making a claim about the underlying psychology, but I'm not confident.

I'd like a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement rather than a verdict about who's right. Is this a verbal disagreement about the word 'sentience', a hidden empirical question about what the science of consciousness might eventually show, a difference about which cases get treated as data, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, is there anything—a distinction, an experiment, a change of question—that would actually count as progress, or is this just the kind of thing where no one should expect the other side to move?
