i've been arguing with a friend about whether the vector potential in electromagnetism is real or just surplus structure, and i'm at the point where i don't think a prose argument is going to settle it, so i want a poem where the form does some of the arguing. here's the setup i keep coming back to. in classical electromagnetism you can shift the potential by the gradient of an arbitrary smooth scalar, A_μ -> A_μ + ∂_μ λ, and nothing observable changes: not E, not B, not the force on a charge, not the phase an electron picks up around a closed loop. but then the Aharonov-Bohm effect seems to need the potential itself — an electron looping around a solenoid picks up a phase shift even though B is zero where it travels — so people split over whether the gauge freedom just shows the potential is redundant description, or whether the connection is real and merely tolerates more then one faithful label.

what i'd like is a Petrarchan sonnet (14 lines, ABBAABBA octave, then a sestet with a turn) in which the octave performs a gauge transformation on itself. let the first quatrain describe a charged particle looping around a solenoid in one gauge, and the second quatrain describe the exact same loop in a shifted gauge, with parallel syntax and matching rhymes so the two descriptions line up line-by-line; the only things allowed to stay literally the same between the two quatrains are the gauge-invariant facts. then the volta at line nine should be the speaker realizing the two descriptions are observationally indistinguishable, and the sestet should sit with the question of what, if anything, deserves to be called real once you've accepted that much freedom. someone who knows the Aharonov-Bohm setup should find the physics right, and someone who doesn't should still come away seeing that the same physical story can be told in a whole family of ways without changing what matters.
