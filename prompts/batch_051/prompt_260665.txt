Over the past term, a friend of mine has been recording her judgements about which of two hypothetical mental states is better all things considered. She describes each state by a consciousness score, c, and a happiness score, h, each on a 0–10 scale, where 0 is complete absence of that quality and 10 is its maximal degree. She treats both scores as cardinal, but she has not said how the two trade off against each other. The natural candidate rules are:

- an additive rule: the value of a state is c + h, so the two qualities can substitute for one another without limit;
- a multiplicative rule: the value of a state is c × h, so balance matters and a zero on either score gives zero value;
- a lexical rule: c dominates h, so any gain in consciousness outweighs any gain in happiness, with h only breaking ties.

Her recorded verdicts so far are:

(8, 6) is better than (8, 2).
(7, 7) is better than (3, 7).
(9, 3) is better than (1, 3).
(5, 8) is better than (5, 1).
(8, 4) is better than (2, 7).

Next week she is going to judge a new pair: (8, 5) versus (6, 8).

Predict her verdict. Say what each of the three rules predicts about (8, 5) versus (6, 8), give your best guess as to which rule she is running and why, and tell me what single further comparison of two states would settle the matter.
