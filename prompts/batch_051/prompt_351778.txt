In my philosophy of language reading group we've spent three months on the Twin Earth debate and it's completely stalled. We all accept the setup: Oscar and his molecule-for-molecule twin both call the clear drinkable stuff 'water', but Oscar's is H2O and Twin Oscar's is XYZ. We even all agree that when Oscar says 'water is wet' and Twin Oscar says 'water is wet', they're saying something about different substances. The fight is about what to conclude. The internalists keep saying that only shows something about reference or truth-conditions, not about meaning: the narrow content, what's literally in the head, is the same for both of them, so the mind is still autonomous. The externalists keep saying narrow content is either empty or can't do the jobs meaning is supposed to do (explain communication, explain action), so it isn't meaning at all. After three months nobody's moved and nobody can say what would settle it. My working theory is that this is at bottom a verbal dispute about which theoretical role 'meaning' has to play, plus a stubborn difference about whether armchair thought experiments like Twin Earth count as data in the first place. Is that right, or is it something else—a hidden empirical question about how people actually generalise terms, a difference about where the burden of proof lies, or just two groups answering different questions under one heading? I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a survey of the literature, and then an answer to what would actually count as progress given that diagnosis.
