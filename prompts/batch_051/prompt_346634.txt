I'm taking a seminar on the doctrine of creation and the problem of natural evil, and I've been trying to determine whether the traditional claim that God created the world good can be reconciled with the evolutionary history of prehuman animal suffering. I keep coming back to the following argument, but I'm unsure whether it is valid and just has a contestable premise, or whether it slides over something. (1) On the traditional doctrine, death and suffering entered creation only after, and as a consequence of, the first human sin; before the Fall, creation was free of both. (2) The first human sin could not have occurred before human beings existed. (3) The evolutionary record indicates that nonhuman animals suffered and died for hundreds of millions of years before human beings existed. (4) Therefore, on the traditional doctrine, death and suffering were already present in creation before the very event that supposedly introduced them. (5) Since that is a contradiction, the traditional doctrine is incompatible with the evolutionary picture. Is the argument valid? If it is valid, which premise should a defender of the doctrine reject, and how should it be qualified? If it is not valid, exactly which step fails and why?
