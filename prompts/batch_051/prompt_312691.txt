As a PhD student working on the relationship between timing judgments and theories of consciousness, I keep coming back to Benjamin Libet's wrist-flexion experiments as a test case for what an account of conscious willing has to look like. In those experiments, subjects watched a rapidly moving clock and reported the moment at which they first became aware of an urge to flex their wrist, while scalp electrodes recorded a readiness potential that began a few hundred milliseconds before that reported moment. The standard reading is that the brain starts the action before the person consciously decides, leaving at most a small window for a conscious veto. But that reading seems to me to presuppose something like the Cartesian theater: the idea that conscious events occur at a definite place and time in the brain, where a subject could in principle catch them as they happen. Dennett's multiple drafts model rejects that picture; it denies that there is any one moment at which the urge becomes conscious and treats the subject's report as a probe-driven narrative rather than a timestamped registration.
