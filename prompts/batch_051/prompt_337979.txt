I maintain a model that triages incoming tickets into bug, feature request, or question. We correct its mistakes by hand, and over the last few months I have noticed something strange. When we relabel one borderline ticket, the model changes its calls on a cluster of nearby tickets that we never touched, and the effect can be surprisingly large. A colleague says the model has no stable concept of feature request at all: it is doing a kind of case law, copying the label from whichever training ticket is most similar. I think it has an imperfect but mostly fixed decision rule, and each correction just nudges the rule slightly.

I can run offline experiments, such as removing a ticket, relabeling it, retraining, and measuring how the labels of other tickets shift, but I do not know what pattern would actually separate the two explanations. Design the strongest empirical test you can of the claim that the model is only copying labels from nearby cases rather than applying a stable, generalizable category. Specify the setup, the measurements, and exactly what pattern in the results would support the copying account and what would support the stable-rule account. Then be explicit about what the test would not settle even if it came out cleanly: in particular, whether a stable decision boundary would justify saying the model really has the concept, and whether relabeling-induced drift could also be explained by a stable concept plus noisy or under-specified inputs. Keep the test runnable with the data and model I already have.
