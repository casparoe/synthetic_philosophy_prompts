I am a software engineer and serve as the non-scientist community member on my hospital's research ethics board; my background is not in medicine or philosophy, so I need the analysis to make its conceptual commitments explicit. We are being asked to weigh in on a randomized controlled trial at a decision point. The trial compares an experimental anticoagulant against the current standard of care for patients with advanced heart failure. At its second scheduled interim analysis, the trial's independent data and safety monitoring board found a modest survival advantage in the experimental arm, but the result did not cross the prespecified stopping boundary, and the DSMB recommends continuing enrollment as planned. Several of the trial's own investigators, however, have told the board they are increasingly uncomfortable randomizing new patients to the control arm now that the data trend points the other way.

The board is split. One side argues that clinical equipoise is a live ethical condition: once the accumulating evidence points in one direction, even short of statistical significance, we can no longer honestly describe the two arms as equally promising to a person considering enrollment, so continuing to randomize patients to the control arm lets them bear risk for a question that may already be answered in practice. The other side argues that the prespecified stopping boundary is exactly the mechanism by which we decide when the evidence is strong enough, and that halting on an early trend would leave the treatment question permanently unresolved, thereby failing future patients and wasting the contribution of the patients already enrolled.

I have been asked to draft the short written opinion the board will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame — continue as planned, pause enrollment, modify the design or consent process, or something else — together with reasons the other members can actually act on. Please draft that opinion, state plainly what clinical equipoise should require of an ethics board at an interim analysis, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side.
