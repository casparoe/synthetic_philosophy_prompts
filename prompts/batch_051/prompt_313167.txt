Something I keep getting stuck on, and I think it's specific enough to be worth poking at. A gene drive is different — it's built to spread through a population even if it makes individuals less fit, because it biases inheritance in its own favor. That's basically the whole selfish genetic element trick, right? So what are the most important implications of that difference — specifically the fact that spread is decoupled from individual fitness — for how we should reason about de-extinction proposals where someone suggests using a gene drive to establish a resurrected species or a proxy for one? I don't mean the generic it's-irreversible point; I want the subtler things that follow from the fitness-independence in particular, for example for how we'd monitor such a release, model it, or decide when its actually working.
