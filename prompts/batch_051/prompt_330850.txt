Here's one I keep going back and forth on. The standard definition of veganism, as I understand it, isnt "never touch an animal product no matter what." It's more like a two-part test. First, a vegan avoids animal products—food, clothing, and so on. Second, that avoidance is required only where doing so is possible and practicable, and where it isnt, the person should at least minimize the harm they contribute to. So the category has a feasibility clause built into it, and the question is how much work that clause is allowed to do.

Now take this case. Amina (made-up name) lives in a semi-arid village in northern Kenya. Her household keeps a few goats and about a dozen chickens. The goats are mostly for milk, the chickens for eggs; meat gets eaten rarely, usually when an animal is old or at a wedding. The local market is a long walk and mostly stocks maize, sorghum, some greens in the rainy season, cooking oil, and sugar. There's no reliable year-round supply of beans, no fortified plant milk, no B12 supplements for sale, and the clinic doesn't hand them out. She heard a radio segment about veganism and wants to do right by animals as best she can. She's already stopped eating meat except at communal events, stopped buying leather when she can afford a cloth alternative, and she treats the goats about as well as anyone in the area treats animals. But she still eats eggs and drinks milk most days, because otherwise she and her kids would get very little protein or fat during the dry season, and the animals also function as her savings and her fertilizer. She says she'll go fully plant-based if the market ever makes that possible.

So: is Amina vegan? Work through the definition criterion by criterion. Then tell me whether the case shows the definition needs sharpening—for example, whether we need a separate category like "excused non-vegan," or whether "vegan" is just not a usable category in a place like this. And be straight with me about how confident you are in your claims, and pick out the point in your answer you're least sure of.
