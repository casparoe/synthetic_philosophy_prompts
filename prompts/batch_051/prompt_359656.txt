I was reading Aristotle, On Interpretation, chapter 9, and I want help understanding one of its central moves. The passage is the one about the sea battle; I am working from a public-domain English translation, and since I do not have the text with me, I will paraphrase it closely rather than quote it.

Aristotle asks whether, for every assertion and its negation about the future, one must be true and the other false. He considers two people, one of whom now says that there will be a sea battle tomorrow and the other that there will not be. If the first is already speaking truly, he argues, then it would seem that the sea battle is already necessary, since what is truly said to be going to happen cannot fail to happen. He then draws the conclusion that this would make everything happen of necessity and would leave no room for chance or for deliberation about things that might be otherwise. But he regards that conclusion as clearly false. He goes on to say, as I understand him, that what is, when it is, must be, but that this does not mean that everything that is, is necessary without qualification.

My difficulty is in pinpointing exactly what Aristotle is committed to by this resolution. If he grants that, when a sea battle occurs, it was true beforehand that it would occur, how can he also deny that, before the event, the assertion that there will be a sea battle is already true? Is his position a denial of bivalence for future contingent statements, so that neither member of a contradictory pair is true until the event occurs? Or is he instead making a narrower point about what the truth of a future-tensed assertion would entail, so that the assertion may be true without its truth making the event necessary in the objectionable sense? And what work is the remark about what is, when it is, must be, supposed to do in solving the difficulty? I have seen different people read the chapter as endorsing a three-valued logic, as rejecting future truth altogether, or as merely distinguishing two senses of necessity, and I would like help sorting out which reading the passage actually supports.
