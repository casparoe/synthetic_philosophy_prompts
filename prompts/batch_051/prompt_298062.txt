I've been messing with the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe, and I can't tell if its airtight or if it's cheating at one exact spot. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) Special relativity says there is no absolute simultaneity: two observers in relative motion slice spacetime into 'now' differently.
(2) Take Alice and Bob as they pass each other, and an event E far away in Andromeda, spacelike separated from where Alice and Bob are. In Alice's frame, E is happening right now. In Bob's frame, E lies a few days in Bob's future.
(3) Whatever is happening right now for Alice is real—as real as the curb she is stepping off.
(4) If E is real for Alice, then E is real, full stop. Being real cannot be frame-dependent; otherwise the same event would be both part of the world and not part of it.
(5) So E is real, even though it lies in Bob's future. So at least one future event is real, and running the same construction for other events means the whole future is already there. The block universe wins.

This looks valid to me, but people who dislike the conclusion just wave at step 3 or 4 without saying exactly what breaks. Is the argument valid?
