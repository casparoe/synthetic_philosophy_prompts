Several years ago I adopted an epistemic rule about my own happiness, and I still cannot tell whether it is a sound piece of folk psychology or a flattering way to avoid evidence. The rule is that I do not measure my happiness directly. I keep no mood journal, assign no daily satisfaction ratings, avoid mood-logging apps, and I will not sit down in a quiet hour to ask whether I am happy. My rationale is the paradox of hedonism — the old observation that happiness seems to be best pursued indirectly, and that turning one's attention directly onto it tends to dissolve the very thing one wanted to find, or to replace an honest answer with self-consciousness and a meta-mood. Twice this policy has actually mattered. While I was deciding whether to leave a job, and later during a strained stretch of a relationship, people close to me kept urging me to set aside time and check in with how I really felt. I refused, not because I thought the answer would be unpleasant, but because I believed the checking itself would return noise or alter the signal; I argued that the admissible evidence was what I chose, did, and returned to when I was not watching myself.

I now want to audit the rule rather than merely restate it. Treated strictly as an epistemic shortcut, it claims that introspectively sampled happiness is inadmissible evidence about how a job, a relationship, or a period of my life is going, because the sampling procedure interacts with and degrades the sampled state. Is that defensible? When does introspective measurement genuinely distort what it is trying to measure — through self-consciousness, expectation, or the displacement of absorbed activity by a second-order mood — and when is that mechanism really just a rationalization for declining to collect evidence that might force a decision? Is there a revision of the rule that preserves whatever is right in it, such as tracking absorption and activity rather than felt happiness, or sampling retrospectively rather than in the moment, while still grounding accurate self-knowledge? And how, from the inside, would I tell the difference between a measurement that genuinely defeats itself and a measurement I am avoiding because I suspect it would return a verdict I do not want to hear?
