I serve on a university committee that decides whether to rename buildings, and how to phrase the interpretive plaques, for historical figures whose legacy the campus is fighting about. Almost every month someone defends the figure in question by saying we should judge him by the standards of his time, or warns us against presentism. The trouble is that this phrase gets used to mean several different things at once, depending on the speaker: that the person did not know the practice was wrong; that he could not have known; that even if he knew, the moral concepts he needed were not available to him; that the practice was legal and widely approved; that the personal and social costs of acting otherwise were severe; or simply that he was a man of his time, full stop. We keep relitigating the same cases without any shared structure, and the meetings run for hours.

Please write us a reusable decision procedure for the man of his time defense. The people using it will be a lawyer, a historian, a student, and two alumni, none of them philosophers, and it must fit on two sides of one page and be usable inside a three-hour meeting. I want the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the remaining question is one of values rather than historical fact. Then run the procedure on three cases we actually face: Thomas Jefferson, who wrote about liberty while enslaving people; Christopher Columbus, whose expeditions involved enslavement and extreme violence; and Winston Churchill, who as a war leader directed area bombing of cities with large civilian populations. For each, say exactly where in the procedure it lands and what verdict it yields. Make sure at least one case lands somewhere the committee or the alumni will not like, and say so plainly. Finally, tell us what the procedure cannot settle, and mark clearly which steps rest on what historians and philosophers broadly agree on and which steps are genuinely contested among people who know the topic; please do not present a contested position as the consensus.
