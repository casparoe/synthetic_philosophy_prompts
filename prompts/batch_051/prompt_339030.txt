Recently I have been working on a project that involves computing eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix, and I lost the better part of a week to a sign convention: two software packages returned the same eigenvector with opposite signs, and the sign silently changed which of two clusters an object was assigned to. That experience sent me back to a question that has long bothered me in Galois theory, and I have been collecting my verdicts about when a labeling of mathematical objects is merely conventional and when it marks a genuine distinction. I would like you to extract the principle behind my verdicts, if there is one, or to tell me which of them I must give up.

Here are my verdicts.

(1) The two roots of x² − 2 over the rationals are √2 and −√2. I regard the labels positive and negative as picking out a real distinction: the usual ordering of the real numbers separates the two roots, and I do not think of that ordering as an arbitrary extra piece of structure.

(2) The two roots of x² + 1 are i and −i. I regard the choice of which root to call i as pure convention. Complex conjugation swaps them while preserving the field structure, and I cannot point to anything in the algebraic object itself that makes one root i rather than the other.

(3) I also accept that once the complex plane is oriented — once a direction of counterclockwise rotation is fixed — i is distinguished as the root that acts as a 90° counterclockwise turn and −i as the clockwise one. But I am inclined to say that the orientation itself is a convention, so the arbitrariness in the choice of i and −i is postponed rather than removed.

(4) For the two non-real cube roots of unity, e^{2πi/3} and e^{4πi/3}, I have the same verdict as in (2): the labels are arbitrary, because complex conjugation swaps them. This mildly worries me, since writing them as e^{2πi/3} and e^{4πi/3} already presupposes the very orientation I called conventional in (3).

(5) For an eigenvector v of a real symmetric matrix, I regard the choice between v and −v as convention: they define the same eigendirection. Yet I know that many algorithms then use v as a coordinate axis, and the sign determines which side of the axis a data point lies on. I do not want to say that the side a point lies on is a convention-independent fact, but I also do not want to say that the resulting cluster assignment is meaningless.

(6) For left-handed and right-handed orientations of physical space, I would have said on classical grounds that the distinction is pure convention, since mirror reflection is a symmetry. But I know that the weak interaction is not invariant under parity, so nature appears to distinguish left from right. I am torn between saying that this makes the two orientations genuinely distinct in our universe and saying that the distinction still rests on a convention about which particles count as matter and which as antimatter.

My tentative principle is this: a labeling is merely conventional exactly when there is a structure-preserving symmetry that exchanges the labels, and it marks a real distinction exactly when no such symmetry exists relative to the structure that is actually given. But my own cases seem to show that the hard question is what counts as the structure that is actually given — in particular, whether the ordering of the real numbers and the orientation of the complex plane are part of the given structure.

I would like you to do four things. First, state the best principle you can find that captures my verdicts, making explicit which structures count as given and why, and indicate which of my cases motivates each clause of the principle. Second, tell me which of my verdicts, if any, the principle forces me to abandon or revise. Third, test the principle on cases I have not listed — for example, the choice of which root of x³ − 2 to adjoin to the rational numbers, and the two directions of time in a physical theory that is symmetric under time reversal. Fourth, say what your resulting account predicts about whether there is a fact of the matter as to which of two identical particles is which, and whether that is the same kind of question as the root-labeling cases or a different one.
