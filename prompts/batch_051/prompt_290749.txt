I cover science and ideas, and I am preparing an essay about the 2022 episode in which Jason Allen's Midjourney-generated image Théâtre D'opéra Spatial won first prize in the digital-arts category of the Colorado State Fair's annual fine-arts competition. I want to analyze that episode through Tolstoy's theory of art in What Is Art?, taken at full sophistication rather than as a slogan: his definition of art as a human activity in which one person deliberately transmits a feeling they have lived through to others, the three conditions of infectiousness (the individuality of the feeling, the clarity of the expression, and above all the artist's sincerity), and his further normative claim that art is good only insofar as the feeling it transmits is good, broad, and unifying rather than aimed at the pleasure of a cultivated few. Work the case through both layers: who or what counts as the artist, where in the sequence of prompting, selecting among generated outputs, editing, and display the lived feeling is supposed to reside, whether any infection actually occurs, and whether the work can still be counterfeit by his lights even if viewers are moved. Then say what the theory would have told Allen, the judges, and the artists who objected that they were doing and should have done, and run the analysis in the opposite direction: which features of the controversy does Tolstoy's framework explain, which does it misdescribe or pass over in silence, and does the case confirm his theory, refute it, or reveal that it was never built to judge an object like this?
