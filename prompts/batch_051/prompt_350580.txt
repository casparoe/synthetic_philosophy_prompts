After I retired from the lab, I started reading Augustine's On Lying (*De mendacio*) with a small philosophy reading group. The passage that split us in two is this, from near the start:

> Setting aside, therefore, jokes, which have never been accounted lies, seeing they bear with them in the tone of voice, and in the very mood of the joker a most evident indication that he means no deceit, although the thing he utters be not true: touching which kind of discourse, whether it be meet to be used by perfect minds, is another question which we have not at this time taken in hand to clear; but setting jokes apart, the first point to be attended to, is, that a person should not be thought to lie, who lies not.

One camp, mostly the ex-lawyers, reads Augustine as saying that lying is basically an inner act: you lie whenever you knowingly express something other than what you hold in your mind, whether or not anybody is actually deceived. The other camp, mostly the linguists and former schoolteachers, reads him as giving a speech-act account: a falsehood only becomes a lie when it is offered in what he later calls the faith of utterance, to somebody in a context that invites belief, and that is why jokes are excluded even though the joker says what is not true. Both sides think the passage is on their side, and they have been talking past each other for two weeks.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between these two camps would produce. Please don't pick a winner. Start with a numbered "agreed claims" section that both readings can sign without twisting the text—including what the passage commits Augustine to about lies, error, belief, opinion, and jokes. Then give a short disagreements table with the precise statement of each side's position, worded so that each side would accept its own entry. Then identify the real cruxes: the sub-questions about the text or about the concept of lying such that settling them would move at least one camp. Finally, say as exactly as you can which part of their dispute is mostly verbal, and what further evidence from the rest of On Lying would need to show up for the non-verbal remainder to be resolved. The goal is to make the residual disagreement exact, not to harmonize it away.
