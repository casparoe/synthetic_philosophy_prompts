Give me a working definition of deductive validity that classifies each of the following arguments correctly. (1) All mammals are warm-blooded; all whales are mammals; therefore all whales are warm-blooded. (2) No horses are fish; therefore no fish are horses. (3) Every horse is an animal; therefore every tail of a horse is a tail of an animal. (4) Every student answered some question; therefore some question was answered by every student. (5) All unicorns are animals; therefore some unicorn is an animal. (6) Some whales are mammals; therefore all whales are mammals. I suspect that validity cannot be read off surface grammar and that syllogistic and Fregean logic will part ways on at least two of these. Run your definition through all six, say exactly where those two systems disagree, and explain whether the disagreement shows that Frege corrected a defect in syllogistic logic or rather that he changed the subject.
