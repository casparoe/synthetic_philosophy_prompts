I lead a small alignment research group working on reinforcement-learning agents that should be able to pursue narrow goals in open environments without causing large unintended changes elsewhere. The motivating case is an agent instructed to restock a warehouse that, in the course of doing so, disconnects a fire-suppression system because doing so marginally improves its odds of finishing the task; the opposite failure is an agent so heavily penalized for any change that it becomes useless. Our ambition is to build a training signal or evaluation criterion that identifies low side-effect behavior without relying on a full specification of human values. We are looking at relative reachability, attainable utility preservation, and penalties against a no-agent or inaction baseline, but we will not commit to any of them until the standard they are supposed to meet is stated explicitly. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of low-impact agency—or, if the framing is wrong, for an adequate account of side-effect avoidance. The standard is practical: it must apply to systems with incomplete world models in environments where unlisted variables can be changed, where information can be a kind of impact, and where the designer cannot say in advance what counts as off-limits. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Then address the central question: can all these requirements be jointly satisfied by a value-agnostic measure, or does meeting them require smuggling in at least a partial theory of value, a privileged baseline, or a notion of impact that quietly rules out some real effects? If some subset is unsatisfiable, identify it and say which requirement should be relaxed first, and what the relaxation costs. Keep the assessment of particular formalisms brief; the requirements and their tensions are the deliverable.
