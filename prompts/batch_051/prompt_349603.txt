Hobbes, in *De Corpore* (the English *Concerning Body*), retells the ship of Theseus story and then adds a twist that I'll paraphrase because I don't want to mangle the exact wording. His move is something like: suppose someone saves all the original planks as they are removed from Theseus's ship, and later reassembles them into a hull in the same arrangement. If the continuously repaired ship is the same numerical ship as the original, then the ship reassembled from the saved planks would also be the same numerical ship as the original. But that would make two distinct ships numerically the same ship, which he treats as an absurdity. So something in the ordinary way of tracing identity has to give.

I want to use a set of criteria I was taught for whether an artifact is the same individual over time:

(a) There is a continuous spatiotemporal path from the original object to the current one.  
(b) The material composition is at least mostly the same, with changes gradual enough to preserve overlapping parts.  
(c) The form, function, and kind are preserved, or changed only gradually.  
(d) There is a single coherent causal chain linking the present object to the original.

Now consider a borderline case messier than Hobbes's: a wooden schooner, the *Odyssey*, is rebuilt over 30 years. Every plank and timber rots out and is replaced one at a time with new wood of the same dimensions, while the rebuilders log and store each original piece in a warehouse. At the end, the continuously maintained ship is fully seaworthy. A museum then uses the stored original timbers to reconstruct a hull. But the original iron fasteners are gone, so the museum uses modern marine bolts, and about 15% of the original planks are too far gone to use, so those are replaced with new wood matched to the old. Also, the continuous ship keeps its current rig from when it was converted to a training vessel in the 1960s, while the museum reconstruction restores the 1930s hull shape and sail plan. So neither candidate matches the original in every respect.

Does the continuously repaired ship qualify as the same individual ship under those four criteria, and does the museum reconstruction? Or does working through the case show that one of the criteria is doing no real work, or that the criteria are missing something about how we trace a material artifact? I'm especially suspicious that (b) is a rough proxy for something else and not itself pulling weight.
