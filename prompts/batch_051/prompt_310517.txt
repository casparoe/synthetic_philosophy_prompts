I review restoration-grant applications for a state wildlife agency. The governing Act defines its central term as follows: "Sec. 3. 'Biological function' means an effect of a trait or behaviour of an organism for which that trait or behaviour was selected, or for which it has been maintained, by natural selection in the evolutionary history of the population in which it now occurs; no trait or behaviour that lacks such a history of natural selection has a biological function under this Act." A later section provides that a project is eligible for funding only if it will conserve or restore at least one biological function of the affected species.

An application now before us proposes to release into a remnant wild population a strain of an endangered frog engineered by a university group to resist a fungal disease that is otherwise eliminating the species. In controlled trials the resistance plainly performs a causal role: engineered frogs survive and reproduce where unmodified frogs die. But the trait has no selection history in the wild population; it was introduced only recently, and the drafters wrote before this kind of genetic rescue was foreseeable. The applicant identifies the engineered resistance as the biological function its project will restore. The dispute has been framed for me as a clash between an etiological account of function — a trait's function is the effect for which it was selected or maintained — and a causal-role account — a trait's function is what it in fact does for the organism's survival or reproduction, however it arose. The definition certainly looks etiological.

Please rule on the application under this clause. Set out what the text itself yields, what a reading from the Act's purpose yields, what the drafters would most plausibly have said if asked about engineered traits with no selection history, and what a reasonable reader of the clause would take it to mean. Then say which approach should govern here, and whether that result is defensible on the better account of biological function.
