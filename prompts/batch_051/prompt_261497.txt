I keep coming back to one short exchange in the Analects because it seems to compress the entire disagreement about whether the self is made by its community or whether the community is made by selves. The setting is Book 18 ("Weizi"), in James Legge's translation: a recluse has told Confucius that the world is a flood and that he should withdraw from it rather than try to reform it. Confucius replies:

"The Master observed with a sigh, 'It is impossible to associate with birds and beasts, as if they were the same with us. If I associate not with these people - with mankind - with whom shall I associate? If right principles prevailed through the empire, there would be no use for me to change its state.'"

Write a dialogue of roughly 1,500 words between two equally competent, equally well-informed characters: a Confucian role ethicist and a liberal individualist. The passage above is the shared text both keep returning to. The question the dialogue should pressure-test is which side gets the relation between person and community right. The Confucian should treat the passage as a compressed argument that the self is constituted by the human community and the roles one occupies, and that the individualist's pre-social chooser is an abstraction in which the socializing work has been quietly hidden — an attempt to herd with birds and beasts. The individualist should not be a strawman: she should hold that a person can stand at critical distance from any particular community, that the recluses in the same chapter demonstrate that withdrawal is a live human possibility, and that the real issue is whether individuals may revise or exit the roles assigned to them. Have the characters respond to each other's actual claims rather than trading prepared speeches, and require each to state the strongest version of the other's position at some point before explaining why it still fails. Keep the argument self-contained — no citations or reading lists — and do not force a synthesis; the dialogue should end with the disagreement sharpened, not resolved.
