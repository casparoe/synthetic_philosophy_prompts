I teach a one-off high-school philosophy elective (juniors and seniors, 16 students, 75 minutes) on the Presocratics, and I want a runnable activity that makes Parmenides' denial of change and plurality feel like something you could actually be pushed into, not just a weird historical exhibit. Most of the class treats him as obviously refuted by, like, clapping your hands or walking across the room, so I want them to hit the problem before anyone names it. My rough idea is a constrained language game: they have to describe an ordinary change (a candle burning down, someone leaving the room, a block moved across a table) or two distinct objects without using any negation or difference vocabulary — no "not," "nothing," "without," "absent," "before it wasn't," "other," "different," "elsewhere," and so on. The constraint should make them feel how much of our ordinary talk about change and plurality quietly rests on negative facts, and the debrief would map that onto Parmenides' claim that what-is-not can't be thought or said, and that therefore coming-to-be, passing-away, and difference are impossible. But I'm not sure my version is actually runnable.

Can you design it properly?
