i keep goig back and forth on the AI-safety-via-debate idea and i need help pinning down where the load-bearing claim actually is. The theory's appeal, to me, is that it promises to make truth win without the judge knowing the truth in advance: you train two agents to argue opposite sides of a claim, a human judge asks questions and picks the winner, and the story is that a false answer is harder to defend than a true one because lies have to be kept consistent while the truth just has to be laid out. Over enough rounds the judge is supposed to be able to confirm things she could never check directly, like whether an enormous proof is valid or whether a proposed change to a codebase is safe. That is an elegant story. It doesn't require solving alignment, doesn't require a philosophical account of truth, and uses only one raw ingredient: the judge's ability to recognise a good answer when it's shown one.

And then there's the intuition I can't shake. A sufficiently capable liar doesn't just fabricate a house of cards; they can construct a false case that is internally coherent, tuned to the judge's priors, and stocked with prepared deflections for every obvious objection. They can choose the terrain, make the truth-teller waste time swatting away noise, and tuck the one bad step inside a long, tedious, plausibly-formal chain that the judge cannot practically check. Once the false case can take the form of an obfuscated argument with a hidden mistake in a haystack, it stops being obvious that the truth is easier to defend; it starts looking like the judge is just being asked to solve the original problem in the guise of checking the argument.

So I'm stuck between a theory with real systematic virtues and an intuition that returns whenever I imagine an actually capable liar. I don't want someone to pick a winner. I want a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing I should drop, what would it take for the theory to be the thing that gets amended or abandoned, and is there a reading on which both survive because debate works under some conditions on the skill gap, the domain, or the judge and my intuition only bites outside those conditions? Give me the strongest statement of each position and then tell me which move the evidence would justify.
