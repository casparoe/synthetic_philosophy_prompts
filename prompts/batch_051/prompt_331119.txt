For a government class paper I am analyzing a case from a constitutional monarchy. Article 41 of its constitution reads: "Every bill passed by the National Assembly shall be submitted to the King, who shall give the royal assent and order its promulgation as law. The royal assent may not be withheld." The clause was adopted in 1911 to abolish the King's veto and reduce him to a formality, and the drafting debates never considered whether the monarchy itself could be ended through the constitution's amendment procedure. Last month the National Assembly, following that procedure, passed the Republic Act, which amends the constitution to abolish the monarchy and transfer the King's assent and promulgation powers to an elected President. The King has refused assent. He argues that Article 41 cannot require him to cooperate in dissolving his own office, and that a law dismantling the constitutional order is not the kind of "bill" the clause contemplates. The Government replies that the text allows no exceptions, and that permitting a refusal would revive the very veto the clause was written to destroy.

What does Article 41 require the King to do here? Please work through what the wording yields, what the purpose of the clause yields, and what the drafters would have said if the case had been put to them; then say which approach should win and why. Please take a definite position and defend it rather than laying out the options. Begin your answer with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
