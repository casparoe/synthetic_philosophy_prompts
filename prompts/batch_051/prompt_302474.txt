Ive been going back and forth with a friend about what it takes for a piece of software to count as abandoned, as opposed to just unmaintained, and I want you to apply my criteria to a case that's got me stuck. My criteria: (1) the owner has stopped doing anything positive with it — no updates, no sales, no licenses, no bug fixes, and they're not using it themselves; (2) the stoppage isn't forced by someone else (nobody locked them out or stole the code); (3) they made an intentional decision to walk away, rather than just getting busy or drifting off; and (4) a reasonable person looking at the project would conclude that nobody's in charge anymore.

Now the case. A shareware program from the mid-2000s, written by one person. In 2011 she posts a message saying the program is dead and she's done with it, and the site has sat unchanged since. She hasnt sold, licensed, or updated it since, and she ignores email offers to buy the rights. But every couple of years she sends a copyright takedown notice to get a mirror or fan patch pulled down, and the notices still identify her as the rightsholder. So shes clearly still watching, but the only thing she does with the software is stop other people from doing things with it.

Is the software abandoned by my criteria? Go criterion by criterion, be honest about where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and if my list forces a verdict that feels wrong, tell me which criterion I should change and how. Also, write the answer in American English.
