I serve on a city commission charged with deciding, category by category, which kinds of calls should remain with armed police officers and which should be transferred to unarmed civilian workers. The commission grew out of an argument over whether the police should be reformed, shrunk, or broken up entirely, but our actual work has been narrower: we review specific categories of calls and make rulings. Our rulings so far have been:

- Parking enforcement should go to unarmed civilian workers.
- Ordinary noise complaints should go to unarmed civilian workers.
- A residential burglary in progress, where an occupant may be inside, should remain with armed police.
- A domestic violence call in which a weapon has been reported should remain with armed police.
- A mental-health call in which the person is agitated and alone, unarmed, and not threatening himself or anyone else should go to an unarmed mobile crisis team.
- A minor traffic collision with no injuries should go to unarmed civilian workers.

We have never written down a general policy; the pattern above is all we have to go on.

The proposal now before us concerns a man in a mental-health crisis who is alone in his apartment, holding a knife, and threatening to harm himself. No one else is at risk. Some commissioners say this is simply another mental-health call and should go to the unarmed crisis team; others say the presence of a knife means armed police must respond.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own past rulings? Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analogue, what principle would we be adopting if we send the crisis team, and what principle would we be adopting if we send armed police? I would also like to know whether there is a further category of call, not yet on our agenda, that would settle which principle we have really been applying all along.
