I teach undergrad political philosophy, and every year the affirmative action unit gets derailed over the word "quota." One side uses it for anything with a demographic goal, the other side insists almost nothing is a quota, and nobody has a definition that actually does any work. I'd like a working definition I can hand the class.

Here are the cases I use (a bit simplified). My own instincts are in parentheses; I'm happy to be told I'm wrong.

1. A public medical school in India reserves 22 of its 100 seats for members of the Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes. Those seats can only be filled by members of those groups; the remaining seats are open to everyone. (Clear quota.)
2. A university does holistic admissions with no set-asides, but the dean watches a live dashboard of admitted students by race, and when one group is lagging he tells readers to slow down and reread borderline files from that group. (I want to say not a quota, but it feels awfully close.)
3. Norway requires the boards of public limited companies to be at least 40% women, and a company can eventually be struck off the register if it doesn't comply. (Quota, surely? But it is a floor, and it applies company by company rather than divvying up a fixed pool.)
4. A firm announces that over five years at least 30% of new partners will come from underrepresented groups. It calls this a target, not a quota, and insists each hire is still competetive. (No idea.)
5. Two applicants are genuinely tied after full review, and the tie-breaker rule says to pick the one from the underrepresented group. (Does the tie-breaker framing change whether it's a quota? I'd say not a quota, but I'm uneasy.)
6. A university sends extra recruiters to majority-Black high schools but runs the same admissions formula for everyone. (Obviously not a quota.)
7. Every applicant from a designated group gets 10 bonus points on a 150-point admissions scale. (Feels like a quota in disguise, even though no seat is reserved and the number admitted can bounce around.)
8. A state university replaces its normal process with a weighted lottery, with weights tuned each year so the admitted class roughly mirrors the state's racial composition. (Hardest one for me: the numbers are engineered, but no seat is set aside in advance.)

So the ask: propose a definition of "quota" that draws the line somewhere principled, run all eight cases through it, and flag any place where your definition disagrees with my instincts or with ordinary usage. Then either revise the defintion or defend the surprising verdicts. I care less about the legal valence of the word than about a definition that tracks something morally or institutionally real.
