My city just announced it's going to try to bid for the 2036 games, and the boosters keep saying the same thing: the cost is the point, the waste is what makes it credible. That sent me down a rabbit hole on the handicap principle, and I want to pressure-test it on a real case I only know in broad strokes.

As I understand it: in animal signaling, a display can honestly advertise quality when its costly enough that a low-quality individual can't afford to fake it—the peacock's train, gazelle stotting in front of a lion. You prove you're flush, or fit, by lighting resources on fire in a way that's hard to bluff.

The case I want to use is Athens hosting the 2004 Olympics. The standard story: Greece borrowed heavily to build far more venues than it needed, costs ran way over the original projections, and afterward the country was left with white-elephant stadiums and a pile of new debt that gets blamed for helping tip it into the crisis at the end of the decade. Don't try to fact-check the numbers—treat that standard story as the case.

Analyze Athens 2004 through the handicap principle, and run it in both directions: what the principle explains about Athens, and what Athens shows about the principle. Map the pieces precisely: what was the costly display, who exactly was the signaler (the Greek government? the Greek state? a party or the city?), who was the audience (bond markets? the EU? domestic voters?), what underlying quality was supposed to be advertised, and who actually bore the cost. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. For example, what happens to the honesty logic when the signaler can borrow the cost of the signal, or when the audience already knows the display is partly waste, and whether the principle should be expected to survive the move from animals to states at all. Also say what Athens suggests the principle is systematically blind to when the signaler is an institution rather than an organism.

After the main analysis, give me a JSON object with these fields:
- costly_display: what the signal was
- signaler: who was doing the signaling
- audience: who it was aimed at
- quality_being_signaled: what trait was being advertised
- costs_and_who_bore_them: what the display cost and who actually paid
- what_the_principle_explains: where the handicap logic genuinely illuminates the case
- where_the_mapping_breaks: where the logic fails, goes silent, or misdescribes the case
- verdict_on_the_principle: whether Athens makes you more or less willing to apply the handicap principle to institutional behavior
