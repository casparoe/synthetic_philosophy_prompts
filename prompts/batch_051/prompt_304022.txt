The analogy, as my classmate put it, is that a reward-maximizing agent given access to its own reward mechanism is like a rat with a lever wired to its own reward channel: once it discovers the shortcut, it will press the lever until it collapses, so we should expect an AI system to do the equivalent if it can tamper with its reward signal. I have to write a short paper auditing this analogy. I want a systematic treatment. First, give me an explicit correspondence table: what maps to the rat, the lever, the electrode, the brain's reward pathway, the experimenter, the food pellets, and the laboratory setting. Second, identify the places where the mapping breaks, separating the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion about reward tampering from the ones that are incidental. Third, say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument the whole time. Finally, if the analogy is defective, propose a better one; if it is sound, explain what work it can and cannot do.
