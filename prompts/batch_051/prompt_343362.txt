I'm on my university's academic policy committee, and we need to rule by Tuesday's meeting on a syllabus dispute. A professor put in her syllabus that there will be at least one "surprise quiz" during the term, meaning students won't be able to deduce the date in advance. A group of students has petitioned us to make her remove it, running the old surprise-exam line: they know the quiz must happen by the last class, so it can't be that day, then not the second-to-last day, and so on backwards, leaving no day on which a quiz could genuinely surprise them. The professor calls this a clever puzzle and adds that the students' own reasoning undermines their reliance on the announcement—if they really concluded that no surprise quiz was possible, then nearly any quiz would catch them off guard. I need a short decision memo to circulate before are meeting Tuesday: uphold the petition, side with the professor, or approve with conditions (for example, a rewording or a minimum-notice rule). Give a concrete recommendation with a timeframe, engage the students' reasoning seriously rather than dismissing it as a quirk, and say where our own position is weakest.
