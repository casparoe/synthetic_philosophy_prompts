I'm a data scientist by trade — I live in conditional probabilities, base rates, likelihood ratios, selection effects, DAGs — and I only have a tourist's grasp of philosophy of language. I keep tripping over the generics debate and getting stuck on the same examples: why is "ducks lay eggs" true when maybe half of ducks (the female ones, and really only the adults among them) lay eggs, and why is "mosquitoes carry malaria" fine when the fraction of mosquitoes that actually carry it is tiny, while "ducks are female" is false even though roughly half of ducks are female, and "books are paperbacks" is iffy even though a huge share of books in print are probably paperback? So I want you to do a translation exercise for me. Take the main positions people defend about generics — the prevalence view (a generic roughly says "most Ks are P"), the contrastive view (it's about how much more common P is among Ks than among non-Ks), the normalcy/default view (it's about normal or stereotypical instances of the kind), the kind-based/causal view (it's about the kind's nature or characteristic activity, not any distribution over individuals), and the psychological "striking property" view (it's about what human default-generalizing machinery latches onto as dangerous or characteristic, whatever the base rate) — and restate each one in my vocabulary: probability distributions, conditional probabilities, likelihood ratios, latent variables, causal structure, decision-theoretic salience, whatever fits. For each position, tell me what translates cleanly and what only fits if you bend it, and what has no expression at all in my toolkit. Then give me the part I actually care about: the residue report. Is what fails to survive the translation a sign that my vocabulary is missing something — normativity, essences, cognitive quirks — or is it a sign that some of these positions are less well-defined than they sound, leaning on words like "normal," "characteristic," and "nature" that fall apart once you demand they cash out into a distribution or a DAG?
