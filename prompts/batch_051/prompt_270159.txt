I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am building a small interactive quiz for a civic reading group. The quiz is meant to sort people into views about secession and self-determination — using Scotland, Catalonia, and Quebec as running examples — based on how they answer concrete cases, not on which label sounds good to them. Please write the sorting instrument.

Please provide:

1. Nine questions, each built around a short concrete case (real or clearly hypothetical) with two or three answer options that a layperson can answer without knowing the theory. Across the question set, include: at least two cases where the seceding region is not a distinct nation (for example, a wealthy province that wants to stop paying into a shared fiscal system); at least two cases where a distinct nation inside a broadly democratic state wants independence; at least one case where the group is genuinely oppressed or discriminated against; at least one case where the secession would strand a minority that wants to remain in the parent state; and at least one question that tests whether the motivation for secession matters in itself (escaping redistribution, preserving a language, protecting against oppression). Phrase the options so that they do not reveal which position they feed into.

2. A scoring key that maps answer patterns onto five positions: remedial-right-only, primary-right/plebiscitary, national self-determination, constitutional unionism, and all-things-considered case-by-case. Use a simple accumulating scheme, say which questions are weighted most heavily toward which positions, and state the answer pattern that should produce each label.

3. A diagnostic paragraph covering answer combinations the key would mishandle, such as a respondent who would allow secession only when it is both a distinct nation and a large supermajority but would also allow a bare regional majority in one case, or a respondent who rejects secession even under serious oppression. For each problem pattern, say whether you would treat the underlying position as coherent-but-unrepresented, incoherent, or merely unattractive, and explain how the key should map it.

4. A note identifying two pairs of positions the instrument cannot cleanly separate, and whether you would collapse the pair or add a tie-breaker question.

Do not reference particular philosophers or texts in the questions, options, or scoring key. The deliverable should read like a usable quiz and key, not an encyclopedia entry; embed the distinctions in the questions rather than explaining them at length.
