My friend is one of those people who will happily argue about whether some enormous historical change counts as a 'takeoff,' an 'explosion,' or neither, and she's got a bunch of firm verdicts that I can't turn into a single rule. She says the Industrial Revolution was a slow takeoff but not an explosion: growth went from almost zero to maybe one or two percent a year, but it took more than a century, and she doesn't think there was a clean cut between the old world and the new one. She says the Neolithic switch to farming wasn't a takeoff at all—just thousands of years of people making local choices, with plenty of failed starts. Nuclear weapons, though, she'll call a genuine explosion: the amount of destruction a handful of humans could unleash went up by orders of magnitude in a few years. Human-level intelligence evolving from our last common ancestor with chimps? Not an explosion to her—just millions of years of incremental changes. But she'll happily call the Cambrian explosion an explosion, even though she knows it took something like twenty million years, because next to the preceding three billion years of mostly single-celled life it was sudden.

Now people keep asking her how she'd classify an AI 'intelligence explosion,' and she just says it depends what you're asking. I don't especially care what probability she'd assign to each AI scenario; I'm trying to extract the tacit criterion behind the earlier cases. So: if she's consistent, would she call a fast takeoff—an AI system going from roughly human-level to qualitatively superhuman in weeks or days via recursive self-improvement—an explosion? Would she call a slow takeoff—sixty to a hundred years of AI systems accelerating progress and gradually automating their own R&D, like the Industrial Revolution but faster—an explosion, a takeoff, or neither? Lay out the candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts, say what each one predicts about those two AI cases, give your best guess about what she'd actually say, and tell me what additional case (historical or AI) would best distinguish the top two rules. And please flag which parts of the answer are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the topic: the historical cases differing in speed is uncontroversial, but whether AI would take off fast or slow—and even what counts as a 'takeoff'—is contested, and I don't want an answer that quietly treats one side as the consensus.
