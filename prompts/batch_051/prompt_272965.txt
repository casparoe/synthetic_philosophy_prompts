I am presenting a seminar paper to an interdisciplinary science-and-technology-studies group. My thesis is a qualified defense of the claim that artifacts can have politics: I reject technological determinism, but I argue that some technical systems impose stable, asymmetric material constraints on the social arrangements that can be built around them, so that certain arrangements are not logically impossible but are systematically more costly to sustain; this constraint is an objective feature of the artifact together with its surrounding infrastructure and does not depend on any designer's intent. The audience is mostly constructivist STS scholars with SCOT and actor-network commitments, plus two philosophers of technology who think Winner's standard examples, the Moses overpasses and the nuclear-power case, have been empirically undermined; they are wary of back-door essentialism dressed up as politics embedded in a device. Prepare me for the question period: the objections I will actually get from this group, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way these scholars would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
