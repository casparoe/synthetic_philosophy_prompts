I run a little mind-and-brain discussion group at my local library, usually 10 to 14 people, 80 minutes, mix of curious non-experts, some students, some retirees. Lately I've been stuck on something and I want the group to feel it in their own heads instead of hearing me explain it.

The way I understand it (and I might have the details off): one view says a mental thing only becomes conscious when it gets broadcast to the rest of the brain so you can talk about it, act on it, or remember it, like it's been put on the main stage. Another view says the raw experience itself can be bigger than whatever gets broadcast, because reporting is a bottleneck. So you can be consciously seeing something without being able to report all of it. I once read about a trick where you flash a grid of letters for a split second and people feel like they saw all of them but can only name a few, until you tell them which row to report and then they can get most of that row. That gap between what's there in experience and what they can report is the thing I want them to run into themselves.

Design a runnable activity or game for this group that makes them feel that gap. It has to work with just a laptop and a projector or printed cards, no real money. Include rules or steps with timings, a debrief that maps what happened in the activity onto the two views (broadcast vs experience-overflows-report), what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the idea the activity can't actually demonstrate so I'll have to supply it myself. I want them to come away thinking that their own experience may have outrun what they could say about it, and that this matters for which view is right. Don't give them the jargon or the names of the views untill the debrief.
