I've got a question I've been turning around in my head to much lately, and I'd trust your read over mine. My friend Mara is one of those Locke people — she keeps a copy of John Locke's Second Treatise on her desk like it's a codebook — and she's annoyingly consistant when she runs through examples. The passage she treats as her canonical rule is this one, from chapter V (Of Property):

"Thus the grass my horse has bit; the turfs my servant has cut; and the ore I have digged in any place, where I have a right to them in common with others, become my property, without the assignation or consent of any body."

Her track record, as best I can reconstruct from years of arguing with her:

- Acorns picked up in a wood anyone may enter: hers.
- Turf cut by her hired gardener on the village common where she's a commoner: hers.
- Ore she digs on that same common: hers, no need to ask the other commoners.
- Fish she catches beyond any national jurisdiction: hers.
- A plaque and flag glued to an unclaimed islet in the middle of nowhere: not property, just a gesture.
- A fence thrown around a bit of the common which she then leaves untouched for years: not her property; the other commoners can ignore it.

I keep thinking this is overdetermined by several nearby rules — labor-mixing plus a non-spoilage proviso; a bare actual-use requirement; or maybe the "where I have a right to them in common" clause is doing the real work. The new case that's bugging me: a startup lands a teleoperated robot on a small metallic asteroid, spends months extracting a few tons of platinum-group metals, and seals the ingots in a cache on the same rock with no near-term plan to sell or use them — the founders explicitly say the point is to establish entitlement by extraction. No state has claimed the rock, the Outer Space Treaty says celestial bodies aren't subject to national appropriation, and it's genuinely murky whether the extracted metal becomes anyone's property. What would Mara say? Which of her earlier verdicts is the real analog — the ore on the common, the open-ocean fish, or the plaque? If her record genuinely underdetermines it, what principle would she be adopting with each live verdict, and which single followup case would most cleanly break the tie?
