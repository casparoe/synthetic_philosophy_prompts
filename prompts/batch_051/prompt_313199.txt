Write a Petrarchan sonnet about prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection, structured so that the form itself carries the argument. The octave should present, in concrete images rather than abstractions, the egalitarian intuition that a society in which some flourish while others barely survive seems wrong partly because of the sheer gap between them. The volta must be the leveling-down objection itself: if the gap mattered as such, then bringing the flourishing down to the level of the rest would count as an improvement in at least one respect, even though it benefits no one. The sestet should give the prioritarian reply — that benefits to the worse off carry greater moral weight, while equality as such carries none — and thereby show why leveling down is not an improvement. The closing couplet should compress into epigrammatic form the insight that the prioritarian keeps the special concern for the badly off while abandoning any intrinsic concern for the gap. Do not use the words "prioritarianism," "prioritarian," or "leveling-down" anywhere in the poem; the poem should earn those concepts through its images and its turn. The verse should be technically disciplined in rhyme scheme and meter, and philosophically accurate enough that someone working in distributive ethics would recognize exactly how the priority view both captures the original egalitarian intuition and escapes the objection.
