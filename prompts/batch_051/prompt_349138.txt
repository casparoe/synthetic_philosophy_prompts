I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep running into the phrase "money is a social construct" being used to settle arguments it plainly doesn't settle. Last month a stablecoin founder told me that since money is a social construct, his private token is "just as real" as the dollar. The same week a gold bug told me the same phrase proves the dollar is a collective delusion that will eventually collapse. And a friend who's into local currencies uses it to mean we could collectively decide bottle caps are money tomorrow and it would just work. I can't tell whether these people are making the same claim and drawing different conclusions, or smuggling in different claims under one slogan. Can you seperate out the precise theses "money is a social construct" could be asserting — e.g. about what makes something money in the first place, about whether the category "money" is a natural kind or a social kind, about how much of monetary value is convention versus hard functional constraint, about whether we can redesign money at will — and for each reading say whether it's trivial, subtantive-but-true, or false, and which of the three people above actually needs that reading to get where they're going? The useful thing would be a menu rather than one official interpretation, with a note where a true reading still doesn't get the person their conclusion.
