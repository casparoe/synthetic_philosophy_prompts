So I'm prepping a lecture on Russell's Marriage and Morals, and drafting it has made me realize my own committments on sexual ethics are kind of a pile of things I've absorbed rather than actually thought through. Before I stand in front of students I'd like to know whether the following set even hangs together. Here they are, in no particular order:

(a) Russell was right that sexual morality should be rebuilt from the ground up around the welfare of the actual people involved—adults and children—rather than from divine command, natural law, or plain disgust.

(b) The old rule against extramarital sex was mostly downstream of paternity and property: you needed confidence about whose child was whose. With reliable contraception and cheap paternity testing, the traditional case against affairs has lost most of its footing.

(c) But I also hold, just as firmly, that a married person who is secretly carrying on a romantic or sexual affair thereby wrongs their spouse seriously—even in the careful version where the spouse never finds out and there is no pregnancy, disease, or neglect of the children.

(d) I also think jealousy is almost always a bad reason to restrict a partner's behavior. The jealous person should manage their own jealousy, and I don't think a spouse's intense jealousy makes otherwise fine behavior wrong.

(e) What I really care about throughout is actual harms and benefits to the people involved; I don't think 'the institution of marriage' has any independent moral weight.

(f) Though then again, I want to say that being married changes the moral situation in a way that goes beyond whatever private promises the two people happened to make—and I can't just reduce that to the sum of those promises.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean the whole set up. I'd rather you discriminate than flag everything.
