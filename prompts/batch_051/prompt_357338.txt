You are an expert political philosopher with a particular skill for disambiguating slogans in political discourse. I am a philosophy professor preparing for a graduate seminar on distributive justice. Last week, a student repeatedly quoted the slogan "From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs" in order to dismiss, in a single move, any proposal that ties income to contribution, makes benefits conditional on work, or excludes unpaid care work from the distributive system. I do not want the class to treat the slogan as a conversation-stopper, so I plan to devote part of the next session to working out what it could actually be claiming.

Please produce a disambiguation of that slogan. Begin by brainstorming at least six candidate precisifications; think carefully about the fact that the slogan contains two distinct clauses, which might be read separately, jointly, or with one clause presupposing the other. In particular, make sure to distinguish at least these kinds of reading: the slogan as a description of how distribution would work in a fully developed communist society; the slogan as a demand of justice binding even under conditions of scarcity; the first clause as a principle of contribution or assignment of tasks; the second clause as a principle of distribution, sufficiency, or desert; and the two clauses as a single integrated principle whose force depends on their combination. Then settle on the most distinct and philosophically interesting readings and present them as a menu.

For each reading, give: (1) a one-sentence plain-language statement of the precise claim; (2) a classification as trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but doubtful or false, or radical—with a brief justification; and (3) an explicit verdict on whether the reading would support the student's dismissal of contribution-tied or work-conditional proposals.

Close with a short paragraph identifying which reading comes closest to making the student's argument work, and which reading the student must be tacitly assuming for the slogan to have any dialectical force against those proposals. The menu of readings, not a single interpretation, is the deliverable; the point is to make visible the ambiguities that allow the slogan to do more argumentative work than any one precise claim can bear.
