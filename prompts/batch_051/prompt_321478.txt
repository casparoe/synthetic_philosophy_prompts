I've been rereading Gödel, Escher, Bach and I keep getting stuck on the part about where meaning is located. I find myself pretty well convinced by the view that meaning isn't sitting there in the physical message itself; it's spread across the whole situation — the message, the decoder, and whatever backround tells the decoder how to read it. The way Hofstadter separates frame message (the implicit signal that something is a message and is meant to be decoded), outer message (the information about the code or decoding rules), and inner message (the actual content once you decode it) seems to explain a lot: why a DNA sequence only says something relative to a cell, why a text in a dead language seems to be in semantic limbo until somebody cracks the script, why the identical string can be a poem in one context and a list of numbers in another. And the point that the outer message can't ever be made fully explicit without setting off another decoding problem feels right to me too.

But I have a stubborn intuition that cuts the other way. If I write down that the cat is on the mat, and then every mind in the universe were to disappear, I can't bring myself to say the sentence stops meaning anything — it still seems true or false, still about cats and mats, still different from random keyboard noise. The theory seems to make meaning too dependent on being interpreted, and I don't see how it explains the difference between a real message that is waiting to be deciphered and a mere string that could be given any interpretation you like. So this is the thing I want negotiated rather than adjudicated: is my intuition just a leftover from a bad picture in which meanings are glued to marks, and it should go? Or does the GEB view need to be amended — for instance by relocating meaning in standing public conventions rather than in particular acts of interpretation — and what would that amendment cost? Or is there a reading on which the sentence on the unread page really does mean something while it's still false to say the meaning is in the marks? I want to know what each move buys and what it loses, and which one you'd make if you had to.
