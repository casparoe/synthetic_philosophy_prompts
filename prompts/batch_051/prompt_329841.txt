Here's the counterfactual I keep turning over: suppose a midsize U.S. state (say Ohio) passes a law saying that within ten years, every state prison has to run on a Halden-style model — no bars on the windows, cells with real wood furniture and doors you close yourself, officers who carry no weapons and train more like social workers, staff and prisoners eating together, real kitchens and trades, no guard uniforms. Predict, with reasoning, how that state's prison system actually looks thirty years after the law passes. I dont want a yes or no on wether it "works." I want the mechanism chain: where you'd expect the biggest drops in violence and recidivism and why, where it would backfire or get watered down, how the correctional officers' union and the legislature would adapt, which parts of the model are load-bearing and which parts would get dropped quietly, and what it does to the politics of punishment in that state over three decades. For each prediction, say how confident you are and what fact about people or institutions would have to be true for it to play out the other way.
