I'm reporting on a lab that has run a long-term artificial life experiment since the early 1990s: chunks of code copy themselves, mutate, and compete for processor time, and after millions of generations they evolve behaviors nobody wrote. The lab's founding biosafety policy, drafted before digital organisms existed, contains this rule: "Any entity that takes in usable resources, maintains itself, and reproduces with heritable variation shall be handled as a living system." The senior researcher says the rule doesn't reach the simulation, because the code's "metabolism" is just the building's electricity and its "environment" is a server. Her grad student says that's how all metabolism works — you take energy and materials from somewhere — and the words plainly apply. I'm less interested in the practical classification than in what each approach to interpretation gives: the wording, the purpose of the rule, and what the drafters would of said if you could ask them now. Which one should win here, and does it say anything about what "alive" was doing in that policy in the first place?
