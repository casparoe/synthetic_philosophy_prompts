Before I go any further down the respect-and-recognition rabbit hole, I want the spec I keep not finding. Write the desiderata: what does a theory of what we owe each other in attitude, not just in action, actually have to get right? It should handle at least the coworker who performs all the right actions toward you while privately holding you in contempt, the friend who genuinely feels all the warmth in the world but somehow never manages to act on it, the forced appology, the boss or court that formally recognizes you as a rights-holder while clearly despising you, and the gap between owing someone recognition as a person and esteeming them for what they've done. For each requirement, give the case or intuition that forces it, and flag which ones you think are negotiable without losing the phenomenon. Also take a brief stand on whether you can owe someone an attitude you can't voluntarily call up, and if not, where the obligation moves to. Then say which single requirement does the most work ruling out candidate views, and whether any pair of them look like they can't be jointly satisfied. I want the criteria themselves to be the deliverable, not a scoreboard of theories.
