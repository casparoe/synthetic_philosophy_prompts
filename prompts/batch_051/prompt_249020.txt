The lottery paradox shows that rational belief cannot be both consistent and probability-based. Since rational belief is closed under conjunction, you must then believe that all the tickets will lose, which contradicts your knowledge that some ticket will win. The preface paradox is the same point in different dress: a careful author has adequate evidence for every sentence in her book, yet she also believes, on the basis of well-known human fallibility, that at least one sentence in the book is false. Her beliefs are therefore inconsistent, though each individual belief is rational. The moral of both paradoxes is that rational people inevitably hold somewhat incoherent states of belief, and the right response is to abandon the demand that a person's beliefs be logically consistent.
