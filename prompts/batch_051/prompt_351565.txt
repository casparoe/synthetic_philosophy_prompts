I am a novelist, and I am drafting a book in which several characters describe themselves as empty. I do not want that word to blur into a vague mood, so I need a working definition of existential emptiness precise enough to separate it from neighboring states. Please give me a definition that gets these nine cases right: the flatness that follows finishing a long project; ordinary boredom on a free Sunday; the emptiness reported in clinical depression; the dark night described by religious contemplatives; the burnout and anhedonia of an overworked person; the hollow feeling of early widowhood; the void that opens when a person loses the faith or worldview that had organized their life; the serene self-dissolution sometimes reported in meditation; and the sense, in a materially successful life, that everything at the center is hollow. I am confident the first case counts and that ordinary Sunday boredom does not. I suspect the meditative case is a different phenomenon altogether, though I cannot say why, and the rest I am genuinely uncertain about. Propose the definition, run it through all nine cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer; either revise the definition or defend the unexpected verdict.
