I don't know what to call a move I keep seing in puzzles. Take the one where you start from MI and try to rewrite your way to MU using the four rules (append U to a string ending in I, double everything after an M, replace III with U, delete UU). The only way I know to show MU is unreachable is to stop applying the rules and notice something the rules never talk about: the number of I's can never become a multiple of 3, and MU would need zero. The insight that cracks it isn't a move inside the system at all, but a fact about the system seen from outside. I ran into the same shape of move in the domino puzzle where two opposite corners are removed from a chessboard: the tiling rules never mention color, but a coloring argument outside the rules shows the board can't be covered. Is there an established name for this move of climbing out of a rule system and finding a fact about the system that isn't statable within it? How is it different from just using a stronger system to prove something the weaker one can't, and how is it different from a plain invariant argument or no-solution proof? I'm less after a bare label than the boundaries between whatever nearby concepts exist. Please end with \answer{...} containing the name or a short label you'd give the move.
