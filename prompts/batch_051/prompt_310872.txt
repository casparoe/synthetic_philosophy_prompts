I keep running into a mental state I don't have a word for, and I'd love to know if philosophy has a standard name for it. Two examples.

First, my brother-in-law is sure a certain contractor rigged a government tender. I show him the paperwork — which he admits is the only real evidence either of us has — and he says, sincerely, "on these documents alone the level-headed conclusion is that one can't tell either way. But I'm still certain it was rigged, because of how these people operate." He isn't bluffing and he isn't avoiding the documents: he can state exactly why a neutral reader would suspend judgement, and he holds that second opinion at the exact same time as the certainty. They just sit there side by side.

Second, a doctor I know has a reputation for snap diagnoses. She told me that sometimes she is sure a patient has condition X, while also being sure that if you handed the same symptom list to a panel of her colleagues, they'd rightly say the evidence is too thin. Her confidence doesn't drop, but she doesn't think the panel is wrong either.

Is there an established name for this specific state? It's not quite self-deception, since she can see the conflict plainly. It's not exactly weakness of will, since there's no action being avoided — though maybe belief itself is the thing she can't bring herself to change? Is it automatically a rational failure, or could both attitudes be reasonable because they answer different questions? I don't just want a label: I want you to say what the nearest concepts are, which one fits best, and where the edges are — for instance, how this differs from just slowly updating when you learn that peers would disagree, or from believing p while knowingly acting as if not-p. If there's no standard name, coin one and tell me what it excludes.
