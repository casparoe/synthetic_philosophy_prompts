I have an argument I cannot find the exit from, and I would like you to find it. I have no linguistic training; I started thinking about this after a family argument over whether English spelling should be reformed, and I talked myself into a corner.

(1) An alphabet is a writing system in which the written symbols stand for the sounds of a spoken language.

(2) If that is what an alphabet is, then a literate person who knows the values of the symbols should be able to pronounce any written word, and spell any spoken word, from the symbols alone.

(3) No script usually called an alphabet passes that test. English is the obvious failure: the letters "ough" sound different in "through", "though", "tough" and "thought"; the sound at the start of "fish" can be spelt f, ph or gh; and some spellings do not record sound at all so much as relationships between words (the g in "sign", which reappears in "signature") or distinctions between homophones ("write" and "right"). But the supposedly regular alphabets only move the problem around. Spanish and Finnish come close, yet they still leave out the intonation of whole sentences and the changes words undergo in connected speech, and any alphabet used by more than one dialect has to let the same letters stand for different sounds in different accents. The gap is therefore not a defect of English; it is built into the very idea.

(4) So either "alphabet" does not mean what (1) says, and the ordinary claim that English, Spanish and Finnish are alphabets is saved only by quietly changing the subject, or else there are no alphabets anywhere.

(5) The second option is absurd. Whatever definition we adopt, Spanish and the Latin script are alphabets in the ordinary sense; a definition that makes them not alphabets has cut itself off from the very word it was supposed to explain.

I would most like to keep (5). But I cannot say which earlier step to reject: (1) is how I was always taught to think about alphabets, and (2) seems only to spell out what (1) already commits one to. Please tell me where the argument goes wrong, or, if it does not, which premise is the least costly to give up. For whichever step you reject, explain why it looked right and what it costs to reject it. Please also say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of. Please write the answer in British English.
