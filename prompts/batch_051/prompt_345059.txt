Before I read yet another theory of dispositions, I want the full spec instead of the usual hand-waving. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of dispositions like fragility, solubility, and irritability: what any account has to get right, with the case behind each requirement. I already know it has to separate being fragile from actually breaking, and not simply identify the disposition with the underlying physical structure that realizes it. What I need you to stress-test is how the theory handles the tricky cases: a finkish disposition, where the very stimulus that would trigger the manifestation also removes the disposition (a live wire that a safety cut-off deactivates the instant it's touched); a masked disposition, where the stimulus occurs and the disposition stays put, but an external blocker stops the manifestation (a glass in impact-absorbing foam that wouldn't break if struck); and a mimic, where something lacks the disposition but would still produce the same behavior if triggered because an outside cause steps in. For each requirement, say why it's a requirement, flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and mark which are non-negotiable. Then briefly: how does the simple conditional analysis (x is fragile iff x would break if struck) do against your list, and which single requirement does the most to kill it?
