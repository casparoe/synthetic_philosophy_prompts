ok, so a friend and i have been fighting about a platform idea and I want you to analyse it as a mechanism, not as a moral question. The site (working name Burnfeed, because we're not serious people) replaces upvotes with literal burning of money. You buy 'flares' for real currency; when you flare a post, the money is verifiably destroyed — sent to an account that cannot be spent, keys deleted, whatever — and the amount burned is displayed on the post. The feed is ranked by total burned. The platform takes no cut and the poster gets payed nothing, so there's no transfer being signalled, it's pure waste. That's supposed to be the handicap logic: the only way to credibly say 'this matters' is to pay a cost nobody can fake. The one real design choice is that a flare can be attached anonymously or with your username.

My friend thinks this gives you a feed of things people actually care about, because nobody burns money over nothing. I think it gives you a feed of rich people with grievances and whatever reads as conspicuous consumption, and that creators will just burn money on their own posts as a marketing line item, at which point it's just the ad market again with extra steps.

Walk through it properly: who posts, what gets amplified, how the anonymous/named choice changes what the signal means (to different audiences), whether creators self-flaring is the stable equilibrium, and where the whole thing gets gamed first. Also: does handicap logic even transfer from mating displays to a public feed, or does it break because there are multiple audiences reading the burn, not one? And can any of the failure modes be fixed without just turning it back into a normal paid-promotion product?
