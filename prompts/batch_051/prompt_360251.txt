Your assignment for Thursday: a piece of short fiction, 1500 words or so, that thinks through the epistemology of coincidence. The main character should be someone who understands the birthday paradox — they know that in a room of 23 people, a shared birthday is already more likely than not, and they're the sort of person who dismisses "spooky" coincidences by pointing out that with enough chances, rare events are basically guaranteed. Then give them a coincidence that isn't merely rare but pointed, something with specific content: a stranger abroad brings up a private detail from their childhood, or a dream that gets tomorrow's conversation right word for word. The story should dramatize the actual philosophical problem here — how to weigh the base rate against the fact that this particular event is wildly improbable under the null hypothesis. Don't resolve it by revealing a hoax or a miracle; leave it genuinely ambiguous whether the character is being rational to shrug it off or obtuse to ignore it. Ideally the reader should come away able to seperate "some coincidence was bound to happen" from "this coincidence was bound to happen."
