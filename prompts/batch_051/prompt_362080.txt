Write a deadpan parody of an employee handbook for a hospice chain that has reorganized itself around a strict reading of the doing/allowing distinction. The company's proudest boast is that it never kills anyone: it only ever allows people to die. The handbook, around 700 words and formatted as numbered rules under headings like Feeding, Ventilation, Alarms, and Pain Relief, should instruct staff on how to act so that no individual action can be described as the cause of a death, while making clear that patients nonetheless die at a brisk clip. The jokes should come out of the actual philosophy: each rule should be a recognizable extension of real moves in the euthanasia and withdrawal-of-care debate — the difference between withholding and withdrawing treatment, the double-effect rationale for morphine, the idea of letting nature take its course — and the cumulative effect should be that a supposedly humane scruple turns into systematic callousness. Keep the memo-writers sincere: they genuinely think this is the only decent way to run a hospice, and the humor should feel like the distinction is hanging itself rather than being heckled from outside. It should be funny to someone who knows the killing-versus-letting-die literature and readable to someone who doesn't.
