Everyone in my interpretability reading group has settled into an analogy where AI interpreting AI is like literary criticism. The move is: you don't need to be Shakespeare to explain Shakespeare — a perfectly ordinary critic, with no creative genius, can notice patterns in King Lear, explain why the structure works, flag ambiguities, and say things the author never consciously intended. So, the argument runs, when we set a smaller or dumber model onto the weights, activations, and features of a bigger model, we shouldn't panic that the interpreter is less capable then its target. It just has to be a good critic rather than a good author — good enough at reading, not at writing. This gets wheeled out to defuse the worry that recursive interpretability has a ceiling, the worry that once models start outrunning their interpreter models we're flying blind. The critic, after all, never had to outwrite the playwright.
