Mīmāṃsā philosophers held that the Veda is authorless (apauruṣeya) and beginningless: no human or divine author composed it. On their account, Vedic testimony has intrinsic validity precisely because there is no author whose ignorance, carelessness, or deceit could have corrupted it, whereas ordinary human testimony is received only provisionally, since its speaker is a possible source of error. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa, the seventh-century exponent of this view, never encountered text produced by a machine.
