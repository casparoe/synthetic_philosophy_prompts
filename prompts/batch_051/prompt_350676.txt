I've read the standard Simpson's paradox example so many times it's basically cached at this point: a drug looks better than placebo in every subgroup but worse in the pooled data, so you dig up the lurking variable and trust the stratified numbers. Here's a variant I haven't seen handled cleanly. Suppose the stratifying variable isn't a pre-existing covariate but something caused by the treatment itself. Concretely: the drug beats placebo among people who tolerate the full course (80% vs 60% recover) and among people who don't tolerate it (20% vs 10% recover), yet overal placebo wins 55% to 26%, because the drug pushes almost everyone into the don't-tolerate group. Does the usual stratify-and-trust-the-subgroups advice survive when you're conditioning on a post-treatment variable? Which number is the right one for someone deciding whether to take the drug, and is it even fair to call this Simpson's paradox anymore?
