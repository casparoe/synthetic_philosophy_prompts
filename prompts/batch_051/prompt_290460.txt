I'm taking a continuing-ed philosophy class on Plato right now, and at my day job I do a fair amount of video editing, so the two have been banging into each other in my head all month. In class we've been working through the Republic in translation (I'm not a classicist) and spent a long time on the cave allegory and the theory of forms, plus that bit in book 10 where he talks about imitation — the idea that a painting of a bed is a copy of a particular bed, and the particular bed is itself a copy of the form of the bed, so the painting sits at a third remove from what's real. The prisoners in the cave are in a similar spot, staring at shadows thrown by copies and not even realizing there's a whole world behind them.

At work, meanwhile, everyone keeps showing me AI image generators and voice-cloning tools, the kind where you can make a clip of a person saying something they never said. And I keep wondering what Plato would of made of this. Would he see a deepfake of a real person as just another instance of the imitation he already distrusted in painting and poetry? Or would it be worse in his framework — a copy of a copy of a copy, shadows of shadows, where the real person is already a particular and the deepfake adds another layer of removal from the form? And would he even care about the distinction between a deepfake of an actual person and an AI-generated picture of, say, a horse, given that on his account both are pretty far down the chain from the forms? My half-baked hunch is that he might be oddly unbothered, since he already thought every image was ontologically suspect, but maybe he'd treat deepfakes as a new kind of harm because they make people even less likely to turn around and leave the cave. I'm not after a reading list or citations, just a careful reconstruction of what his actual positions imply.
