Take Carroll's tortoise, but suppose the tortoise isn't flatly refusing modus ponens. Its happy to apply it to any conditional whose consequent it already accepts on independent grounds; it only baulks when the conclusion would be new to it. So from A, 'if A then Z', and an independent belief in Z, it'll happily detach Z, but from A and 'if A then Z' alone it won't move. Does Achilles' regress still develop the same way, and does the usual moral -- that inference rules can't simply be added as premises -- survives this change? Or does the case now turn on a different issue, roughly whether deduction can yield genuinely new commitments? If so, does the original rule/premise lesson depend on the tortoise not already believing the conclusion?
