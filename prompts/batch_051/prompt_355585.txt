My friend and I have been arguing for weeks about what counts as art, after we went to an exhibition where one room was a huge pile of cellophane-wrapped candies and another was just a recording of a man describing his morning. She kept coming back to the bit in Tolstoy's What Is Art? where he says art happens when someone deliberately summons up a feeling they have experienced and then, through movements, lines, colors, sounds, or words, transmits that feeling so that other people catch it. The way she reads him, if there's no infection of feeling from the maker to the audience, it's not art, no matter how clever or beautiful it is. I think that makes art into a kind of emotional telepathy and would throw out too much.

So here's what I'd like: give me a working definition of art that handles the following cases. Clearly art, for me: a Schubert string quartet, a novel that leaves a specific mood, a child's crayon drawing of the monster in her closet, a ritual funerary chant. Clearly not art: a traffic light sequence, a bird's song, a diagram of the Krebs cycle, a chess match, a chef's plate of food. Borderline, and this is where we fought: Duchamp's Fountain, a decorative carpet made for sale, a piece of music produced by a generative model, a hand-carved rocking chair, a logo, a game like Journey, a professional mourner who performs grief she doesn't feel, a firework display. If possible, say which of these your definition puts in and which out, and point out the case where you're least confident. Also say whether Tolstoy's infection criterion, as my friend understands it, is the core of the right definition, a rival to it, or a test that only works for some art.
