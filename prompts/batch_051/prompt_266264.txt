I am a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, but I have been working through Marx's account of machinery in Capital, and I want to know how much of it can survive contact with the kind of automation I encounter in my own work. As I understand the core argument, it goes roughly like this: under capitalism an employer introduces a machine not primarily to reduce toil but to produce the same output with less labor-time; the machine can then be operated by cheaper and less skilled workers, so the skilled workers who previously did the job lose their bargaining power, and the displaced labor feeds a reserve pool that disciplines wages more broadly. Mechanization therefore does not, in the ordinary run of things, deliver more free time to workers; it raises the intensity of exploitation and precarity, and productivity gains are captured by capital.

The development I want to put pressure on this argument with is the automation of cognitive and software work by general-purpose AI systems. This differs from the industrial machinery Marx had in view in at least three ways. First, it targets desk work — including the very labor that traditionally saw itself as shielded from deskilling. Second, it is not a single-purpose machine built for one production line but a retrainable, repurposable model that can be pointed at many tasks. Third, it is produced and controlled by a small number of firms that also control the compute infrastructure and the training data, so the tool itself is a kind of capital concentrated at a scale that did not exist in Marx's examples.

My question is this: does the Marxian argument still go through in this setting, or does it need repair, or does it collapse into something weaker? Specifically, identify which premise or premises the new development bears on, say which versions of the argument fail and which still hold, and tell me whether the old conclusion — that automation under capitalism tends to worsen the condition of workers and strengthen capital — still follows if those premises are updated honestly. I am not asking for a reading list or a comparison of Marx scholars; I want a close, structured answer about the logic of the argument itself.
