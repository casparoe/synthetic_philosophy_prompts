In my philosophy of mind seminar we've been working through self-deception, and I find myself stuck between an argument that seems sound and an intuition I can't abandon. The argument: deceiving yourself would require getting yourself to believe something you already know is false. If the knowledge and the false belief coexist, you hold contradictory attitudes; if instead you set out to induce the belief, the intention seems to keep the truth in view, preventing the belief from ever taking hold. The intuition: people plainly do deceive themselves — about a relationship that is failing, about the quality of their work, about symptoms they are afraid to have checked. I don't want a verdict; I want the strongest version of each side. What exactly does the impossibility argument rule out, and what does it leave standing? What do the ordinary cases actually establish? And is the right resolution to debunk the intuition as a misdescription of something like motivated bias, to revise the theory of belief and intention so that self-deception comes out possible, or to show that the two are compatible on a deflationary reading of what it is to deceive oneself? Which move should I make, and what does it cost?
