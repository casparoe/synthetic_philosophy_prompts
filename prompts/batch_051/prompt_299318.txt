I volunteer in my parish's program that prepares adults to enter the Catholic Church, and I help teach some of the sessions. Last week our team was planning how to answer a question from one of the candidates, a woman whose parents are practicing Hindus: she asked, carefully and without hostility, whether the Church teaches that her parents are lost. One of the other catechists cut the discussion short with the old formula, 'Outside the Church there is no salvation,' and everyone else nodded as if that settled it. I do not think it did. The sentence looks like a single hard claim, but I suspect it can be made to express several quite different precise claims, and that some of them do not answer her question at all. Lay out for me the distinct readings of the formula, varying what 'Church' picks out, what 'outside' amounts to, and what the sentence thereby says about the people who are outside. Give me exactly six readings, as a numbered list with no introduction and no conclusion, each item one or two sentences. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude or a substantive claim, which familiar position about the salvation of non-Christians it lines up with (exclusivist, inclusivist, pluralist, or none), and whether it actually settles the question about the Hindu parents. Make the sixth item the reading the other catechist would have to intend for the sentence to do the work he was using it to do.
