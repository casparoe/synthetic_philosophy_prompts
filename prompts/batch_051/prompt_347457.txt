I'm looking for a reasoned judgment on a disagreement I keep having with a close friend, and I'd like the underlying structure examined rather than generic reassurance.

For years I have deliberately returned to films, novels, and music that reliably leave me sad, sometimes for hours afterward. I don't merely tolerate the sadness; I seek out these works partly because of the kind of sadness they produce, and I believe I would lose something important by avoiding them.

My friend argues that this is incoherent, or at least irrational. His case runs as follows. Sadness is an aversive state: normally I go out of my way to avoid it. If I choose these works because of the sadness, then either the sadness serves some distinct payoff that I could describe, or else I value an aversive state for its own sake. The second option, he says, is close to a contradiction: it treats an experience as something I both want not to have and pursue as its own reward. And the first option, he says, concedes his point, because the sadness would then be at best an instrumental cost, not something I actually enjoy. He adds that my behavior outside art gives him a further argument: I never seek out equivalent sadness in daily life, even when I could, so whatever I am after, it cannot be the sadness itself.

I can feel that something is wrong with his framing, but I cannot state clearly where the mistake lies. Is he right that my taste for sad art is either disguised masochism, self-deception, or a practical contradiction? Give me a rigorous analysis of whether deliberately pursuing sad art can be coherent and rational, and what it can mean to say I "enjoy" it. I don't want advice about what to watch or about mental health; I want the conceptual dispute resolved.
