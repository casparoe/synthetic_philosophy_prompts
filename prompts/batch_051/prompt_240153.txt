Before I accept any account that makes a person's idealized or coherently extrapolated preferences the target of either that person's choices or an AI system's alignment, I want the full specification of what such an account must actually deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must handle, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the structural or normative constraints it must respect, with the case or consideration behind each. Then state which requirements are non-negotiable and which are optional or specific to one camp, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude familiar candidate views.

Do not organize the answer as a survey of existing authors or systems. The list is the deliverable, and the motivation for each item matters more than who said it first. Make sure the list at least addresses the following, though you should add anything I have missed, and you may reject any of these as pseudo-desiderata if you argue for that verdict:

- Fidelity under correction: the procedure begins from a person's actual motivational set, which is formed under false beliefs, partial information, transient affect, and social pressure. The account must say why correcting a descriptive belief can change the target value, and how it distinguishes fixing a cognitive error from substituting the theorist's values for the person's.
- Path-independence: the output must not depend on the order in which corrections and reflections are applied, or, if it does, the account must say which order is privileged and why.
- Reflexive stability: re-running the procedure on its own output should leave the output unchanged, and the person should not be able to predict that they will disown the result — or the account must explain why that fixed point is not required.
- Authority: the actual person's refusal to undergo the procedure, or their horror at the output, must be given a determinate role, whether as data, veto, or evidence of a faulty procedure.
- Aggregation: the account must say how it extends from one person to many, and whether coherence across persons is a well-formed demand or a category mistake that smuggles in a substantive view about interpersonal trade-offs.
- Non-circularity: the procedure must not presuppose the values it claims to extract — in deciding which emotions count as information and which as bias, which conflicts to repair by revision and which by compromise, or which of two conflicting selves is authoritative.
- Benchmarks: it must get the easy cases right, among them wanting to want to quit smoking, preferring the experience machine, a preference installed by an abuser, and a person who predicts they will reject the procedure's result.

For each requirement, give the motivating case and say what a theory loses if it drops the requirement. Then flag the pair or cluster of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable and give the strongest argument you can for why not; that tension is the part I most want to see. Finally, in one short paragraph, say how revealed-preference accounts, ideal-advisor accounts, and coherent-extrapolation accounts fare against the list.
