Produce a parody patient-information booklet for a health system that has made QALY-maximisation the basis of every triage decision, written as a cheery, deadpan NHS-style welcome leaflet. Cover how your personal QALY balance is calculated (including the age and disability weights and the national cost-per-QALY threshold), how to use the online portal to see whether a referral is worth booking in the first place, and the transition arrangements for services that no longer clear the bar, like a good chunk of intensive care and the rare-disease drugs ward. Include a short FAQ for the awkward cases: what happens when your consultant can see you as an actual person, why appeals against the questionnaire are assessed using the same questionnaire, and whether a QALY saved this year is really worth the same as a QALY saved in ten years. The humour has to come from the actual mechanics of QALY-based rationing, so exaggerate real features rather than inventing mad ones, and keep it deadpan the whole way through: the leaflet never says the quiet part out loud, but a reader should still end up worrying about who assigns the disability weights, whether one person's QALY is being treated as interchangeable with anyone else's, and what happened to the rule of rescue. Around 600 words. Answer in British English, and dont bother with citations or a bibliography.
