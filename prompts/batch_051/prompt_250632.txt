A relative of mine was recently refused a tenancy after the landlord learned of a minor conviction from many years ago, and I found I could not articulate why that refusal felt less clearly wrong to me than a refusal on the basis of race, though I wanted to call both discriminatory. I would like a working definition of a protected characteristic — the kind of trait that should be treated as a protected characteristic, one whose use as a ground for denying someone a job, a home, or a service is presumptively unjust and stands in need of special justification. Put aside the positive law of any particular country; I want the definition you would defend. Please propose the definition and test it against the following cases. I take race, sex, and religion to be clear cases on the protected side, and eye color and a person's taste in music to be clear cases off it. The cases that genuinely puzzle me are these: age; weight; physical attractiveness; political affiliation; a criminal record; pregnancy; and a degree from a prestigious university. I lean toward counting pregnancy and age as protected, and political affiliation as protected too, though I cannot say why; I have no settled view about weight, criminal record, physical attractiveness, or the university degree. Run the definition through all of the cases, tell me where it yields an answer that seems wrong or where it goes silent, and then revise it or defend the verdicts that surprised you.
