I am taking a seminar on the evolution of mind, and we have just worked through Dennett's four-rung hierarchy: Darwinian creatures, whose behavioral repertoire is shaped only by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, whose overt actions can be selected within a lifetime by reinforcement; Popperian creatures, whose hypotheses can be tested and discarded in an inner model rather than by the organism's own trials; and Gregorian creatures, which import and export tested cognitive tools through language and culture. For my term paper I want to work out a counterfactual the seminar keeps gesturing at but never settles: what position B. F. Skinner would most plausibly have taken if he had been shown this hierarchy and, at the same time, a present-day large language model, which he did not live to see. Construct the sharpest historically honest reconstruction of his likely view. Address three points explicitly. First, would he accept the Darwinian-to-Gregorian ordering as a genuine progression, or would he regard the Popperian and Gregorian stages as mentalism reintroduced under new names, so that the whole hierarchy collapses into selection by consequences plus verbal behavior? Second, would he classify a modern LLM, trained through gradient updates and then fine-tuned with human-feedback reinforcement, as a Skinnerian creature, a Gregorian tool, both, or neither? Third, where would his radical behaviorism force him to refuse the framing of the question rather than answer it? Do not make him a caricature who denies inner events outright; ground the reconstruction in his actual commitments about private events, thinking as covert verbal behavior, and selection by consequences, and say which parts of the taxonomy he would have to reinterpret, not just reject.
