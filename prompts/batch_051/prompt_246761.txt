A philosopher of perception has given the following verdicts about people born blind whose sight is later restored. A person who had learnt by touch to tell a rough sphere from a smooth one could, on first seeing the two objects, say which was rough; a person who had learnt by touch to tell a moving object from a stationary one could say which was moving; a person who had learnt by touch to tell a large ball from a small one could say which was larger; a person who had learnt by touch to tell a flat square from a flat circle could say which was which; but a person who had learnt by touch to tell a warm ball from a cool one could not say which was warm by sight alone. Next week the philosopher must give a verdict on Molyneux's original case: a man born blind who can tell a cube from a sphere by touch is made to see and, before touching either object, must say which is the cube and which the sphere. Predict the philosopher's verdict. Set out the candidate rules that fit her track record, say what each rule predicts for Molyneux's case, state which rule you think she is actually running and why, and name one further case that would discriminate between the candidate rules. Write in British English.
