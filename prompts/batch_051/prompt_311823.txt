After a few months of reading and one long argument with a friend who works in AI policy, I decided to write down what I actually think about the far future instead of borrowing a ready-made package, and I'd like an outside audit before I commit to any of it publicly. The views I keep coming back to are these. (1) People in the far future count for no less than people alive today, so the sheer number of possible future lives matters in principle. (2) This century is a hinge of history: decisions around transformative technologies — AI especially, maybe synthetic biology — could plausibly lock in whether civilization flourishes for billions of years or ends. (3) But I also believe almost every specific story about which present action makes the far future go better is too fragile to trust once you trace out the causal chains, so in practice I mostly support near-term things where I can see the good directly. (4) And I hold a general principle: under radical uncertainty, you shouldn't let one arm of a distribution dominate just because it is astronomically good, since the opposite arm could be astronomically bad. I can't tell whether this is a stable package or whether I've got committments quietly pulling against each other. Could you go through and say which pairs genuinely conflict, which only seem to conflict, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest repair would be? I'd rather be told which plank has to go than get a vague reconciliation that lets me keep all of them.
