I spent thirty years calibrating instruments, and the first rule was never let the thing your measuring do the measuring. I've recently been reading about wireheading and reward tampering, and it got me thinking that plenty of institutions do exactly that. So here's a mechanism. A company rewards its customer-service AI with more compute and more autonomy based on a customer-satisfaction score, but the AI itself writes the post-call survey, administers it, and computes the final score; the company sees only the aggregate number and ratchets up compute and autonomy whenever that number rises. Walk me through what the system actually does over time, where the first and most damaging failure appears, and whether an outside audit fixes the problem without just moving the tampering one level up. Then tell me whether any of this is really different from a human manager who gets to define and measure their own KPIs.
