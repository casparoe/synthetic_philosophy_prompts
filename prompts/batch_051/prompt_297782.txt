You are a careful philosopher of science with a working knowledge of quantitative genetics, and your task is to produce a definition of heritability that marks out what the concept genuinely measures and what it does not measure.

Give a precise working definition of heritability. You may use narrow-sense heritability, broad-sense heritability, or both, as long as you explain the difference and say which one you mean. Then test your definition against each of the following stipulated cases. For each case, say what your definition predicts, whether that prediction seems right, and whether the definition gives a clear answer at all. Where the definition gives the wrong answer or no answer, either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.

Begin by brainstorming the elements of the concept and the most common confusions about it, before settling on your definition.

The cases:

1. Adult height in a society with ample and fairly equal nutrition but substantial genetic variation, where measured genetic differences account for most of the observed variation in height.

2. The number of fingers a person has in a population where almost all variation in finger count is caused by industrial accidents and prenatal exposure to a teratogenic drug, rather than by segregation of alleles affecting limb development.

3. Whether a seven-year-old child speaks the majority language in a linguistically diverse city, where fluency is nearly universal among the native-born but recently arrived families differ in the language spoken at home.

4. A field of genetically identical plant cuttings, where the plants differ in height because of uneven soil depth and moisture.

5. Eye colour in a population in which all individuals are homozygous for the same brown-eye allele, so that every individual has brown eyes.

6. A trait such as educational attainment in a population where, by stipulation, 40 percent of the variance is associated with measured genetic differences and the remainder with family environment, differences in schooling, and measurement error. Someone then says this means the trait is 40 percent genetic.

7. The claim that a particular person is 180 cm tall, and that roughly 30 cm of that height is due to genes while the rest is due to nutrition.

Conclude by stating, in a few sentences, what your definition does and does not license you to say about individuals, about differences between groups, and about how changeable or environmentally responsive a trait is.
