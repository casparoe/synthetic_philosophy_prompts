I have spent the last year or so settling into the Boethian solution to the problem of divine foreknowledge, and I want to find out what would actually count against it. In the form I hold it: God’s knowledge of future contingent events does not threaten human freedom because God is outside time. His cognitive relation to what I will do tomorrow is not foreknowledge but timeless knowledge — closer to someone standing on a ridge who sees the entire road at once than to a forecast, and no more coercive for being infallible. I already have provisional answers to the standard objections: an infallible record of my action seems to leave me without the power to act otherwise; a timeless God may be unable to know tensed propositions; and the view may smuggle in compatibilism and then do no independent explanatory work. What I do not have is a clear sense of which remaining difficulties should most move me, and which ones a committed Boethian can reasonably treat as costs worth paying.

Treat the position as the object under audit. I want to know what would have to be true for the Boethian line to be false or explanatorily idle: which arguments, possible cases, or results about the nature of time, truth, and agency would count against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. For each, indicate whether a defender should grant the pressure, deflect it, or bite a bullet in response, and how expensive the bite is. I would also like to know what the view commits me to that its rival solutions do not, and which of those commitments are genuinely load-bearing. Then give an honest judgment about insulation: whether the standard qualifications — that eternity is a positive fullness rather than the mere negation of temporality, and that freedom requires only the right kind of sourcehood rather than alternative possibilities — have made the view unfalsifiable, and whether that unfalsifiability is a problem for a metaphysical position of this kind. Most of all, tell me which single consideration should most move me away from the view, not merely which one gets discussed most often.
