For a school project I have been looking at the informational cascade model in A Theory of Fads, Fashion, Custom, and Cultural Change as Informational Cascades, the 1992 article by Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, and Welch. The passage I keep returning to says, in my paraphrase, that a cascade begins when it becomes optimal for someone to imitate the choices of the people before him and ignore what his own signal tells him. I tried to turn that passage into a general argument about rational choice, and I ended up somewhere that seems too strong to be right.

(1) People choose one after another. Each person has a private signal about which option is better, and each observes all the earlier choices.
(2) Early choices are evidence, because those choices reflect the private signals of the people who made them.
(3) Once the public evidence from earlier choices outweighs any single person's private signal, it is rational for each later person to follow the crowd even when his own signal points the other way.
(4) But then later choices stop revealing anything about their private signals, so the public evidence stops accumulating.
(5) Therefore a crowd can become locked into one option on the basis of only a few early signals, even when most members privately think the other option is better, and which option wins depends on the accident of who moved first.
(6) But if rational choice lets a small accident fix the outcome and makes everyone ignore the information they actually hold, then rational choice imposes almost no meaningful constraint on what people do.
(7) So the model must be wrong: rational people would not really let themselves be herded by that little information.

I want to keep the model rather than accept (7), but I need to know which step I should reject and what rejecting it costs. In your answer, please say how confident you are in the diagnosis and identify the point you are least sure about.
