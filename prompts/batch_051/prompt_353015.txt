I am drafting a novel set in the courts of the Warring States, and one of my characters, an advisor given to dialectical puzzles, refuses to surrender a white horse on the ground that a white horse is not a horse. He plainly does not mean that there is no animal before him, so some sharper claim is being made, but I cannot tell which, and the scene will read as empty mystification if the person demanding the horse answers the wrong version of the claim. Please lay out the distinct theses this line could be used to assert, from readings that are merely about words to readings that are substantive and even false, and for each say what reply it would invite from someone who wants the horse. Then say which reading best fits the advisor's use of the line as a ground for refusing the demand, and whether, on that reading, the refusal is actually justified.
