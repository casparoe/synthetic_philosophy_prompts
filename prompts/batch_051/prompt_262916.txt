I'm a third-year undergraduate in a political theory seminar on exit, voice, and the boundaries of the state, and we have been discussing private cities, charter city proposals, and seasteading. In our last session, one student defended all three by arguing that they simply give people a way to "vote with their feet": she claimed that moving is itself a form of political participation, because jurisdictions that mistreat people lose residents, tax revenue, and reputation, while a new charter city or seastead that attracts residents has in effect had its rules ratified by the people who choose to move there. Another student objected that leaving is not voting at all, since a vote produces a binding collective decision that even the losers and non-movers must accept, whereas exit is only an individual choice with no collective moment. Both of them treated the slogan as if it obviously settled the question, and I think it is doing much more work than either acknowledged. I would like to bring a careful menu of readings to the next session, because the disagreement seems to bottom out in what the phrase could actually be asserting.

Please lay out the distinct precise claims someone might be making when they invoke the slogan "vote with your feet" in the context of arguments about charter cities, private cities, and seasteading. For each claim, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and indicate which reading or readings would actually support the first student's argument that these projects extend democratic choice, and which would undercut it. Please respond with exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
