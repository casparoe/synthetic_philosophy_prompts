Over the past term, our working group on the classification of political violence has issued written rulings on the cases below, and we have agreed that our future rulings should be consistent with them. A fifth case is now before us, and I would like your analysis of how we should rule.

1. A separatist movement detonates a bomb outside a crowded café in the capital, killing several bystanders, and hours later issues a statement saying that the government must begin withdrawal negotiations or the attacks will continue. Ruling: terrorism.

2. During an armed conflict, a revolutionary movement attacks a military convoy on a rural road, killing four soldiers, for the stated and apparent purpose of preventing the convoy from reinforcing a front-line position; its communiqué describes the dead as enemy combatants and makes no threat against civilians. Ruling: not terrorism.

3. The air force of a recognised state bombs a residential district of a rebel-held city, killing many civilians. The group finds that the operation was intended to disrupt a supply route, that it was not accompanied by any demand addressed to the civilian population, and that there was no evidence of an intent to punish or intimidate the population. Ruling: not terrorism.

4. A non-state armed faction abducts the mayor of a provincial town from his home and kills him in front of his family, leaving a note saying that all local officials who cooperate with the central government will be dealt with in the same way. Ruling: terrorism.

The pending case:

5. During a ceasefire, a non-state armed faction fires mortars at an army barracks within a town at night, killing three soldiers and no civilians. The barracks has little military significance. Within the hour the faction releases a video addressed to the civilian population, stating that the soldiers were shot in their beds, that the government cannot protect anyone, and that families should flee the town unless the government resigns within a week. The faction has no recorded history of deliberately targeting civilians.

If we are to be consistent with our own precedents, how should we rule on the fifth case, and what principle would our ruling commit us to? In your analysis, identify the rule or rules that best fit the first four rulings, state how each candidate rule would classify the fifth case, say which earlier ruling is the closest analogue and why, and specify the one further case whose addition would best settle which rule we are in fact applying.
