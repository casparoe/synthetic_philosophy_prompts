People in my AI reading group keep throwing around the phrase "competence without comprehension," and it seems to do different work each time. Last week one person used it to brush off my worry that a medical chatbot could do real harm even if it doesn't understand what it's saying—as if the fact that the system lacks comprehension makes the danger somehow less real. Another person used it to describe how evolution builds things that look designed without any designer (they said something about Darwin reversing the normal order of explaination). A third used it to claim LLMs obviously don't understand language, full stop. I suspect these are different claims wearing the same slogan. Can you lay out the distinct precise things "competence without comprehension" might be asserting—maybe from something trivial like "some systems are good at a task without understanding that task" to something radical like "all apparently intelligent behavior is competence with no comprehension behind it"—and say for each whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or actually false? And which reading, if any, would actually support the first person's move of dismissing the worry that a system without comprehension can still be dangerous?
