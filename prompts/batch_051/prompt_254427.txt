I've been working alot on a paper about values that have a strong felt quality of being ends-in-themselves even though there's a plausible story that they got installed for instrumental reasons. The one I'm stuck on is truth-seeking. So far I can reconstruct three mechanisms people give for why truth-seeking might present itself to us as intrinsic when its real function is instrumental:

1. The epistemic-commons story: reliable information is a public good, and if everybody only pursued truth when it happened to pay locally, shared practices of inquiry would collapse. So communities that internalized truthfulness as a norm outcompeted the rest, and individuals inherit it as a felt duty rather than a calculation.

2. The reputation/self-signaling story: being known as someone who wants the truth for its own sake makes you a more credible informant and collaborator. But the signal only works if it isn't faked, so the value has to be internalized deeply—deep enough that you keep pursuing truth even when nobody is watching.

3. The curiosity-reward story: we find uncertainty unpleasant and resolution pleasurable; the little hit of satisfaction from closing a question is a proxy reward for obtaining information. Truth-seeking then feels terminal because the reward comes from our own wiring, not from what the truth buys us.

I keep feeling there's a fourth mechanism these three don't capture, but I can't put my finger on it. Anyway: is there another mechanism in this family—an explanation of why truth-seeking feels intrinsic but might be instrumental—that is genuinely different from the three above, not a restatement or a special case or a combination of them? If you think one of my three should be split or rejected, say so, but I'm mostly fishing for the missing one.
