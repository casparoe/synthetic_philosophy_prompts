I'm a clinical psych PhD student, and I sit as the trainee member of a working group that advises the national psychiatric classification committee (a DSM/ICD-style manual, but not those exact texts). We've been asked whether to recommend adding a new diagnosis, Persistent Loneliness Disorder: severe, enduring loneliness (at least 18 months) with clinically significant distress or functional impairment, not better explained by another condition. The pro-label members argue it would name real suffering, create a treatment target, and let people get reimbursed care instead of being told loneliness is just life. The skeptics keep raising Ian Hacking's looping effects: psychiatric categories interact with the people they classify, so minting this label wouldn't just track a preexisting condition but would help create a kind of person who organizes her self-understanding and symptoms around it — and they think that risk is especially bad for a common, identity-relevant condition like loneliness. The commitee has asked for a short decision memo. It should recommend one concrete course of action — adopt as written, reject, or adopt with conditions — argue for it, and treat the strongest point on the losing side fairly. Keep it to one page, max 600 words.
