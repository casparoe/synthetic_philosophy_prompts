I've got no philosophy training but I've been reading the Turing paper where he introduces the imitation game (the 1950 one) and I keep getting stuck on the bit near the end about telepathy and extrasensory perception. Most people treat that bit as a weird lapse where he goes soft on the paranormal. I'm about to present to my local meetup, which is half skeptics group and half amateur philosophy club, arguing the opposite: the ESP section is actually one of the cleanest moves in the paper, because it shows the game only tests what the interrogator can observe, and any hidden channel—telepathy, a radio link, someone whispering answers—is just a leak in the setup that you plug the same way, which is why he says a room that blocks telepathy would handle it. The audience is mostly retired engineers who are allergic to anything that sounds like legitimizing the supernatural, plus two people who have read the whole paper carefully; half of them already think the ESP passage is embarassing and one thinks Turing was just a crank on this. I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a polished defense. What objections will they actually fire at me, in their own words and roughly in order, how damaging each is, what I should concede and what I should hold, the shortest workable reply for each, where those replies are softest, and the question I'm praying nobody asks.
