I am an undergraduate in an upper-division aesthetics course, and our current unit concerns whether games can be art and, if they can, what kind of artistic value they have. Two positions have recurred in my notes and in seminar discussion, and I want to settle which is the stronger before I write my final paper.

The first position, which I think of as the content view, holds that games are art to the extent that they succeed in the familiar artistic media they contain: well-wrought narrative and dialogue, expressive visual design, an evocative score, and the representation of characters and worlds. On this view, Tetris or Go is at best a pleasing artifact or proto-art, while a story-driven role-playing game or a cinematic action-adventure game can be art in the fullest sense. The second position, which I think of as the agency view, holds that this misses what is distinctive about games. On this view, the central aesthetic achievement of a game is the design of agency itself, what might be called the aesthetics of agency: the rules that establish a space of meaningful choices, the calibration of obstacles and difficulty, the way a player's capacities and habits change through play, and the felt quality of deciding, executing, failing, and adapting. By this standard, an abstract puzzle game or a finely tuned racing game can be a complete work of art even when it has no story, no characters, and no representational imagery to speak of.

I would like you to compare these two positions and assess which gives the better account of what makes games valuable as art. I am less interested in a survey of the debate than in a defended verdict. In particular, please address the hard cases for each side: whether the content view can explain the apparent artistry of games that strip away story and imagery; whether the agency view can explain the value of games whose appeal seems to lie mostly in passive absorption of narrative or audiovisual design; and whether either view can explain how a game might be art even when its individual media, considered in isolation, would not be. If you think the best account is neither pure position, say so, but explain which single consideration does the most fundamental work in your account and why.
