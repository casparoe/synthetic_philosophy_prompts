The unilateralist's curse arises when any member of a group may, on their own, take an action that will affect the whole group, and each member goes ahead whenever their own judgment says the action is on balance beneficial. Under those conditions, the probability that someone acts rises as the group gets larger, even when most members, or the group's average judgment, would oppose acting, so the group is effectively steered by its most optimistic or least cautious member. Give an example of a realistic institutional or social setting—outside the standard examples involving the publication of dangerous information—in which this curse would operate even though everyone involved is competent and acting in good faith, and explain which structural feature of the setting, rather than any individual's carelessness, is responsible for the outcome.
