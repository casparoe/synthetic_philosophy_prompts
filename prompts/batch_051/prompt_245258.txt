i'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and i've been going down a rabbit hole on logical uncertainty. the trigger was a failure mode i keep seeing in systems that mix probabilistic reasoning with deterministic code: the system treats "i haven't computed this yet" and "the world is random" as the same kind of uncertainty, and then updates wrong. someone pointed me at Scott Garrabrant et al.'s paper Logical Induction (2016, arXiv), and i got stuck on this bit from the introduction:

"The natural impulse is to appeal to probability theory in general and Bayes’ theorem in particular. Bayes’ theorem gives rules for how to use observations to update empirical uncertainty about unknown events in the physical world. However, probability theory lacks the tools to manage uncertainty about logical facts."

what i took from it: empirical uncertainty is the kind where more thinking doesn't help and you need new observations; logical uncertainty is the kind where you can think your way to a better probability, because the fact is already determined. and the passage claims standard probability theory only really handles the first kind. the paper then goes on to propose a "logical inductor" criterion — roughly, a reasoner whose probabilities no polynomial-time trading strategy can exploit for unbounded profit in a market where each sentence is a stock.

here's my situation: i have maybe ten hours a week for a year, plus two engineers at my company who'd work on it part-time, to do something useful with this. we're not going to publish. i want to know which open question in this general area is most worth us working on first, and why — in the sense that the most other practical questions depend on it. i dont want a survey or a reading list. i want a defended agenda: which open questions are upstream of which, which are actually tractable for a small non-academic team, which are overrated because even a good answer wouldn't change what we build, and what evidence over the next year would make you re-rank. be explicit about the criteria your using (importance, tractability, neglectedness, how many other things depend on it) and where they trade off. also tell me if the empirical/logical distinction in that passage is itself doing more work than it can carry, because that would change everything downstream.
