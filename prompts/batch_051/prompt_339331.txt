In my perception class the professor summarized disjunctivism like this: "The argument from illusion says that when a straight stick half in water looks bent, what you're directly aware of can't be the stick itself, so there must be a common item — a bent sense-datum or appearance — shared between the illusion and the veridical case. Disjunctivists reject the last step: they say that in the veridical case your experience consists in the stick itself being present to you, whereas in the illusion case there's no such object, so the two have no common element at the level of the experience." Then he said this can't be right because by stipulation the illusion is indistinguishable from seeing a genuinely bent stick, and disjunctivists are just refusing to admit the obvious common factor. I couldn't tell if that was fair.
