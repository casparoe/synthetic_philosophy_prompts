I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've found myself stuck on a piece of anthropic reasoning that a friend summarized for me as the presumptuous-philosopher problem. I reconstructed his argument as a numbered inference below. It has the shape of a bug in an update rule: each step passes inspection on its own, but the final output can't be right. I'd like you to go through it step by step and tell me whether the argument is valid, and if not, exactly which step fails, on which reading of it, and what the corrected step would be.

(1) Suppose two physical theories T1 and T2 are equally simple, fit all the ordinary non-anthropic data equally well, and differ only in how many conscious observers they say will ever exist: T1 predicts about 10^10, T2 predicts about 10^13, and all of these observers fall in the same reference class as me. I start with equal credence in T1 and T2.

(2) My current evidence includes at least this much: there is now an observer with roughly my memories, my body, and my present experience.

(3) Conditional on T2 there are a thousand times as many observer-slots at which a life like mine could have been realized, so the evidence in (2) is a thousand times more expected under T2 than under T1. Formally, P(E | T2) / P(E | T1) is about 10^13 / 10^10 = 1000.

(4) Bayes' rule says posterior odds equal prior odds times the likelihood ratio. Since my prior odds were 1:1, the posterior odds are 1000:1 in favor of T2, which is about a 99.9% posterior for T2.

(5) But that result seems absurd: T1 and T2 make identical predictions about every observation I can make, so no amount of thinking about my own existence should leave me nearly certain that the universe contains 10^13 rather than 10^10 observers.

(6) Therefore either one of steps (1)–(5) fails, or the conclusion really does follow and I just don't like it. I cannot locate the failure myself. My hunch is that either step (2) is not the kind of thing I ever have as evidence, or step (3) conflates "the theory predicts more observers" with "the theory predicts me."

Can you tell me whether the inference from (1)–(4) to the 99.9% posterior is valid? If it isn't, are you denying step (2) or step (3), and why is that denial not just a flat refusal to count my own existence as data? If it is valid, what principle has the argument thereby assumed, and is step (5) really an independent absurdity or just a restatement of that very assumption? I'd also like to know the practical difference between rejecting step (2) and rejecting step (3), since those two moves feel like the same refusal to me, but I suspect they are not.
