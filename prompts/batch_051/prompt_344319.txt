I've been living in Germany for three years after growing up in the States, and its unsettled a conviction I didn't know I had. I came over convinced the only defensible view is that rules are heuristics: a mature person judges each case by their own conscience, and following a rule you think is wrong in this case is just moral cowardice. I still feel the pull of that—it keeps moral agency alive and stops you turning into a functionary. But I can also see that the German default of following rules even when nobody's watching and nobody benefits buys fairness, predictability, and a kind of respect, while the conscience-first stance seems one rationalization away from everybody making themselves the exception. When the two conflict, which should give way? Is the German position just rule-worship with better PR, is the American one just special pleading with better PR, and is there a principled way of saying "rules for institutions, conscience for persons" that isn't a cop-out? I want to know what each side has to give up, not who's right.
