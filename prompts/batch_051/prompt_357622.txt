I'm a PhD student in theology and I keep feeling like I have two incompatible minds about the text I study. On one side, the historical-critical method has me entirely: source criticism, form criticism, redaction criticism, the whole apparatus. It realy works. It dissolves things that used to look like contradictions, gives me a disciplined account of why Isaiah 40-55 addresses exiles in Babylon, why Matthew reshapes Mark the way he does, why the gospels differ in exactly the ways they differ. Reading the text as a human artifact with editorial layers has become my default, and I don't think I could unsee it.

On the other side, I can't shake the conviction that these same texts are revelation: that when I read the call of Samuel, or Second Isaiah announcing comfort to an exiled people, God is addressing me through the text rather than merely being reported about. The moment I treat a passage primarily as data for reconstructing a community's theology or a redactor's agenda, that sense of address goes dead, and I don't want to give that sense up. I don't think it's just aesthetic or nostalgic.

Don't give me a cheap both/and. Set up the strongest version of each side: what the historical-critical method, pushed all the way, commits me to saying about the text's truth claims and about my conviction; and what the conviction of revelation, pushed all the way, commits me to saying about the method's self-understanding. Then give me a negotiated verdict. Is there a reading on which both survive without gerrymandering them into different compartments of my life — and if so, spell out exactly how the two modes of reading bear on each other? If not, which one should give: the method's self-understanding or my conviction? And what would have to be true for giving it up to be the right move rather than just a compromise? I can see the beginnings of a reconciliation in the idea that the editorial activity is itself part of what God uses, but I can't tell whether that's a real position or just a way of not changing either side. I've heard the line that the method asks historical questions while faith asks theological ones, and it just sounds like an evasion.
