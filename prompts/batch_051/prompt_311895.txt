Write a critical reading of Jurassic Park (1993, though you may use the novel where it helps) through the lens of contemporary debates about gene drives and de-extinction as irreversible interventions in ecosystems. I will be leading my biology class discussion on this, and I want the film treated as making a serious, if partial, argument rather than as just a cautionary monster story. Distinguish the different kinds of intervention the film depicts: captive breeding and a contained park, genetic engineering with built-in control mechanisms such as the all-female population and the lysine dependency, and the unplanned capacity to breed that the frog DNA introduces. Then ask how close that last point comes to the real issue with gene drives, namely a trait engineered to spread through a wild population on its own in ways that may be irreversible. Where does the film anticipate legitimate worries about irreversibility, cascading ecological effects, and the limits of containment? Where does it stack the deck, for example by making the disaster depend on sabotage and greed rather than on ecology itself? Where would a careful defender of de-extinction or gene drives have good replies the film never considers? End by saying what the film contributes to the bioethics debate that the arguments my class has read do not. Keep the reading to no more than 800 words.
