I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about knowledge, so I wrote out my gut calls on a bunch of cases. Driving through normal farmland, I see a red barn and think "there's a barn": I know it. Driving through Barn Facade County, where all but one structure is a fake front, I see the one real barn (though I can't tell it from the fakes) and think the same thing: I don't know it, even though it's true. I buy a lottery ticket with a 1-in-10-million chance, and before the draw I believe "I'll lose": I don't know that. I glance at the office clock, which unbeknownst to me is broken and stuck at 3:00, and at that exact moment it happens to be 3:00; I believe "it's 3:00": I don't know that either. But when I wave my hand in front of my face and believe "I have hands," I want to say I know that. And I also think I know that having hands entails I'm not a brain in a vat being fed hand-feelings. Yet I want to say I don't know I'm not a brain in a vat. That last cluster feels like it's in tension, but each piece feels right individually. Is there a single principle that captures all of these, or which one should I give up? And what does your principle say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a normally flakey freind tells me, totally seriously, that she's moving to Tulsa; it's true and I have no particular reason to doubt her—do I know she's moving to Tulsa?
