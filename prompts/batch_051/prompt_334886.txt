I am a control systems engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I know roughly that part of cognitive science treats the mind as building and manipulating internal representations of an external world, and that enactivism, a movement associated with embodied cognition, rejects that. As I understand it, enactivism says cognition is not the reconstruction of a pre-given world inside the head but the ongoing history of structural coupling between an organism and its environment; that the organism enacts or brings forth its world through its sensorimotor capacities rather than representing it; that the proper unit of explanation is the organism–environment system rather than the brain; and that at least a great deal of adaptive behavior emerges from closed-loop dynamics rather than from internal models of the world.

Please do two things. First, restate that enactivist critique of representationalism as precisely as you can in the vocabulary I use every day: feedback loops, set points and reference signals, plant dynamics and disturbance rejection, state estimation, internal and forward models, model-predictive control, and the contrast between reactive controllers and controllers that contain a model of the plant. Where a term like representation maps onto something in my toolkit, say exactly what it maps onto and where the mapping is only approximate. Second, give me a residue report: which parts of the enactivist position translate cleanly, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts have no counterpart at all in how a control engineer thinks about systems.

I want to know whether enactivism is ultimately just the claim that much intelligent behavior can be produced by tight closed-loop coupling without a rich internal world model, or whether it is making a further claim that control-theoretic vocabulary cannot even express — and, if so, whether that inexpressible residue is a mark of depth or of confusion. Specifically, is my hunch that representation simply means an internal model of the plant correct, or does the philosophical debate mark a distinction that my vocabulary collapses?
