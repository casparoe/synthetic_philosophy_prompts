I recently inherited the safety module of a domestic-robot project, and my first assignment is to pin down what its side-effect penalty is actually supposed to do before the team commits to any implementation. The robot takes general instructions but has no model of the household's values beyond the current command, and we have accepted that no such model is coming: whatever "side effect" ends up meaning for us has to be definable without a full specification of what the user cares about. The obvious candidates all look wrong in different ways. A penalty on overall change to the house would punish the robot for carrying out the very task it was given; an exemption for "intended" effects seems to presuppose exactly the full account of value we lack; and a penalty on any change in the set of futures the robot could still bring about would punish it for learning to cook or gaining a useful skill, while treating "what would have happened anyway" as the baseline lets a slow leak go unnoticed.

To make the target concrete, here are cases I want the criterion to pronounce on. A robot sent to fetch a glass knocks a vase off the counter in passing; that is plainly a side effect. A robot ordered to retrieve an inhaler from behind a bolted-down cabinet pries the cabinet loose, cracking its base and tearing up the floor; the damage is foreseen, deliberate, and part of a sensible method, so it is not a mere accident, but a rule that excuses every effect the robot decides to pursue as "part of the task" looks easily gamed. A robot told to leave the kitchen clean shovels everything into the back garden: the terminal state satisfies the instruction, so a criterion that inspects only the end state misses the damage. A robot spontaneously reorganizes the bookshelves to make future tasks easier, changing the world in ways that enlarge its future options, and we do not want that penalized the way breakage is. And a robot stands idle while a slow pipe leak ruins the floor, which raises the question whether side effects can arise by omission and whether "do nothing" is a legitimate normative baseline.

Write the desiderata that any adequate measure of avoiding side effects must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp. Flag any subset that you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and in particular give an honest verdict on the tension I am most unsure about: whether "does not presuppose a full specification of value" can coexist with "distinguishes side effects from intended effects," or whether one of those has to be weakened. End by saying which single requirement would rule out the most naive candidate measures. I do not want a survey of named proposals; I can do that part myself once the criteria are fixed.
