I'm a PhD student in epistemology, and a friend gave me his summary of Moore's paradox after reading a popular piece on it. He said: "The puzzle is about sentences like 'It's raining, but I don't believe it is.' It's not a formal contradiction—both halves could be true—but it's still impossible to assert coherently, because when you assert the first half you express the belief that it's raining, and the second half denies you have that belief. What Moore's paradox really shows is that belief is a necessary condition on assertion: you cannot properly assert p unless you believe p." I'm suspicous of the last step—people assert things they don't believe all the time—but I also worry I'm confusing insincere assertion with assertion proper. Which parts of his summary are accurate, which are subtly wrong, and does the conclusion about belief being necessary for assertion actually follow from the accurate parts?
