For your next essay, I would like you to compare two famous philosophical uses of dreaming: the butterfly passage from the Zhuangzi and the dreaming argument in Descartes's First Meditation. I do not want a survey of how each text has been interpreted; I want you to test a particular way of drawing the contrast between them.

Here is the position I want you to examine. It is tempting to file both passages under the same heading, dream scepticism. But they may be attempting quite different things. Descartes uses the dream as an alternative state of affairs that is subjectively indistinguishable from waking life; the problem he raises is evidential, because the availability of this alternative undermines the senses' claim to give us knowledge of an external world. On the reading of the Zhuangzi I want you to consider, the butterfly is not being used to show that we might simply be wrong about which state we are in. It is being used to unsettle the very distinction between waking and dreaming, and between being one kind of creature and another. What is at stake is not evidence but identity and fixity: whether 'waking' and 'dreaming' name two stable kinds with a firm boundary, or whether the passage points instead toward a transformation of things.

Your task is to assess whether this contrast is sound. Write an essay that compares the two passages along these lines and reaches a verdict. In particular, consider whether the proposed reading of the Zhuangzi is illuminating or whether it strains the text by imposing a foreign epistemological framework on it. If you think the framing is confused, or that the comparison is too forced to be useful, do not comply politely; say so plainly and explain how the question should be reframed. Where your verdict depends on a contested reading of the texts, mark that dependence rather than pretending to certainty.

Structure your essay under these headings, in this order, and roughly at these lengths:

1. What the two passages seem to share (about 300 words).
2. Descartes's dreaming doubt: the evidential problem (about 400 words).
3. The butterfly passage and the reading in question (about 450 words).
4. Testing the contrast: where it illuminates and where it strains (about 600 words).
5. Verdict (about 300 words).

Write the essay itself in American English. Do not append a bibliography or use citations; I want your own reconstruction and judgement.
