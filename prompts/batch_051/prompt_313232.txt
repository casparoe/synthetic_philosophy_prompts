One thing I keep seeing invoked there but never actually cashed out is Dennett's four-level progression of minds—Darwinian creatures, Skinnerian creatures, Popperian creatures, and Gregorian creatures. For reference, the gloss I'm working from is this. Darwinian creatures are products of natural selection alone: candidate designs are generated by mutation and recombination, tested against the environment over generations, and the failures die with their bearers while the successes propagate. Skinnerian creatures add a second selection loop inside a single lifetime: the creature emits or stumbles into behaviors and the environment reinforces or extinguishes them, but there is still no inner rehearsal before acting. Popperian creatures have an internal model of the world in which candidate actions can be tried and discarded before the creature commits to them, so part of the selection among behaviors happens inside the head. Gregorian creatures go one step further and import cognitive tools built by other minds—language, notation, algorithms, institutions—so that a large part of the search over hypotheses runs in a shared, culturally transmitted space rather than inside any single head.
