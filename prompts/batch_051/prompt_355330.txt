I moved to Copenhagen last year for work, and the phrase I keep running into is Janteloven, the Law of Jante. The way people explain it to me, it's a set of rules from an old Scandinavian satirical novel, and the first rule is always quoted as "You're not to think you are anything special." The next rules are variations on the same theme: don't think you're as good as the rest of us, don't think you're smarter than we are, don't think you can teach us anything. An Australian friend says its the same mood as tall-poppy syndrome back home, just with better branding.

Last month a coworker shot down someone's pitch by saying, in effect, that it all sounded very American — one guy as the hero — and the whole room was already nodding before she finished. I'm American; I grew up on the American Dream version where if you work hard and do well you get to say so, and maybe even should say so, and where being proud of doing something well isn't the same as claiming to be better than everyone else. So I want to actually know what Janteloven is claiming. Is it a moral rule against self-promotion, an epistemic point that success is mostly luck and context, an equality principle, a demand for conformity, a description of how Scandinavians actually behave, or what? I'd like the readings laid out as a menu — for each, what exactly is being claimed and whether it's plausible, trivially true, or just false — and then a clear answer about which of those readings would license cutting down a successful person the way my coworker did, and which would not.
