A friend and I recently spent a long evening arguing about what it means to say that the heart's function is to pump blood. He held a selected-effects view: the function of a trait is what it was selected for, so a trait with no relevant selection history has no function at all. I leaned toward a causal-role view: the function is what the trait does now within the larger organized system, quite apart from its history. The argument stalled when he insisted that his account is empirically testable and mine is only a definition. So here is the question I want pressed further. Suppose we engineer into a bacterium a novel genetic circuit that, purely by accident, now makes a vital contribution to the cell's survival under a particular stress but has no history of selection for that contribution. What experiment or observation would best test whether that circuit has a function, in a way that discriminates between the selected-effects and causal-role accounts? Specify the design in enough detail that the predicted outcomes would be clear, say which result would favor which account, and then state as precisely as you can what such a test would and would not settle, including whether the residual disagreement is empirical, terminological, or a mixture of both. Please answer in American English.
