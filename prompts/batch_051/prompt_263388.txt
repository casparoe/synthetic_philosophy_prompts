I recently got into an argument with a friend who follows AI policy debates more closely than I do, and he offered the following summary of Dennett's view on counterfeit people:

"Dennett's real claim is not the usual one about a superintelligent agent seizing power. The dangerous thing about modern AI is that it lets people mass-produce counterfeit people — programs that can pass as real human beings in conversation. His key analogy is counterfeit money: a fake bill works not by being better than a real bill, but by making everyone a little less willing to trust any bill, and counterfeit people debase the trust that human communication and institutions run on in the same way. So his proposal is to treat fake humans the way the law treats fake currency: make it a crime to deploy a human-passing AI without clear disclosure, require labels or watermarks so people can tell what they are dealing with, and hold the creators liable for the damage their bots do. Dennett also thinks we can do all of this without worrying about AI rights, because these systems are not conscious and feel nothing, so treating them as illegal deceptive artifacts costs us nothing morally."
