I'm an undergraduate in a seminar on conflict and emerging technology, and part of my grade comes from peer-reviewing short-answer responses against the course rubric. The question asked students to apply Fearon's rationalist account of war to a possible AI development race. Fearon argues that war can occur even when it is ex post inefficient because of three mechanisms: private information about capabilities or resolve combined with incentives to misrepresent; commitment problems; and issue indivisibilities. The question read: "For each of the three mechanisms, assess whether it has a genuine analog in a race between states or labs to develop advanced AI, and indicate one place where the analogy needs to be qualified."

I have been assigned the following response to grade:

"Fearon's three mechanisms do map onto AI races, though unevenly. Private information is the cleanest analog: labs have private information about their own technical progress, safety margins, and timelines, and they have incentives to misrepresent it—sometimes exaggerating capabilities to attract investment or deter rivals, sometimes downplaying them to avoid regulation or escalation—so each side must bargain under uncertainty about the true distribution of power. The commitment problem carries over as well: even if two labs agree to share benefits or pause at some threshold, a lab that later gains a decisive lead would have both the ability and the incentive to abandon the agreement, so the lab that expects to fall behind has reason to race now rather than accept a bargain it cannot enforce. This is worsened by the fact that technical progress can shift the balance of power quickly and is hard for outsiders to verify. Issue indivisibility is likewise present, because the decisive strategic advantage from AGI is a winner-take-all prize, like a piece of contested territory that cannot be divided without destroying its value; if the prize cannot be split, there may be no division of the expected gains that both sides prefer to conflict. One might think the commitment problem is just private information in another guise: with sufficiently good monitoring of capabilities and intentions, the commitment problem would dissolve, since there would be no hidden defection left to fear. Overall, then, the framework gives strong reason to expect bargaining failure in an AI race."

Please grade this answer out of 20 and provide exactly five numbered comments, each one or two sentences, with no separate introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state the grade and the overall justification for it; items 2 through 5 should identify specific claims in the response as correct or incorrect and explain why.
