I’m taking a course on cultural evolution, and we read the chapter of The Selfish Gene where Dawkins introduces the meme. I got into an argument with my roommate about whether the Darwinian stuff about culture is meant literally. I reconstructed what I thought Dawkins was arguing, and he says it’s invalid, but he can’t say exactly where. Here is my reconstruction:

(1) Natural selection requires a replicator with heritable variation and differential survival or reproduction.  
(2) Dawkins defines a meme as a unit of cultural transmission that propagates by imitation, and he says such units can be copied with variation and some spread better than others.  
(3) The disanalogies Dawkins mentions between memes and genes (not arranged on chromosomes, no alleles, blending possible) are architectural details, not differences that remove memes from the category of replicator.  
(4) If something is a replicator with heritable variation and differential survival, it can evolve by natural selection.  
(5) Therefore memes can evolve by natural selection.  
(6) So cultural change is, at least in principle, a Darwinian process in the literal sense, not just a loose metaphor.

I think the controversial step is (3), because maybe Dawkins’s caveats are bigger than I’m making them, or maybe blending breaks natural selection? But my roommate says (1) is too weak and natural selection also requires that the replicator be particulate, or that there be a distinction between replicator and vehicle, or something. He won’t spell it out, though. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails and what is the strongest corrected version of that step? If it is valid, tell me why the conclusion is correct even if memes can blend.
