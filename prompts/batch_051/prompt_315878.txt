I spent my career as a physical chemist, and since retiring I have been working through political philosophy and rational-choice theory in a way I never had time for. I am now preparing an application for a six-month independent-researcher fellowship, and I want the project to be a focused conceptual study rather than a survey. The question I keep circling is whether James Fearon's rationalist account of why states fight can be transferred to races to develop advanced artificial intelligence, or whether the phrases "AI race" and "AI arms race" are doing rhetorical work that outruns their analytic content. My half-formed suspicion is that most commentary borrows the war metaphor without specifying which of Fearon's bargaining failures—private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, or issue indivisibilities—is doing the work, and without first checking whether the parties are in a bargaining problem at all rather than, say, a winner-take-all contest to be first in which the prize is produced and partly dissipated by the racing itself. Please turn this into a one-page proposal. State the question so that it has a determinate answer; explain why existing positions leave it open (the rationalist-war literature was built for sovereign states bargaining over territory or policy, while AI-race commentary tends to assert rather than establish the analogy); describe the approach—a disciplined mapping of the analogy's terms together with a stripped-down two-player model of a development race with private information about progress, a first-mover advantage, and no external enforcement—and say why that approach is better than a purely historical or a purely empirical comparison of past races; say what a successful deliverable would look like for people deciding about AI governance (ideally a diagnostic that says when race talk licenses treaty-style coordination and when it should be replaced by a different model) and what would count as failure; and give an honest assessment of the main risk, namely that the mapping is too forced to yield a determinate answer and the real value lies in abandoning the analogy cleanly. Keep the scope realistic for six months. Do not include a bibliography or request citations; refer to positions and arguments by name, and I will supply references myself.
