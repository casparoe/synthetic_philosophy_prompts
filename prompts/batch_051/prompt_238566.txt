I serve on the grants committee of a small private foundation that supports endangered-language documentation and revitalization. We are weighing a three-year proposal for a language with roughly 250 fluent speakers, nearly all over sixty; no children are acquiring it. The project would record narratives and ordinary conversation from the remaining speakers, produce a reference grammar and a digital archive, and train two community members as language workers. A later phase proposes a pre-school immersion program.

The committee is split. One side holds that the proposal should be funded almost as a matter of course: a language is an irreplaceable way of organizing human experience, the archive would preserve distinctions and forms of knowledge that would otherwise disappear, and the foundation exists to keep such meaning systems available to future generations. The other side holds that a language's value is ultimately instrumental, that our duty runs to speakers rather than to languages, and that we should not spend scarce funds on a language the community is no longer transmitting. They cite a counter-letter from parents in the community arguing that the money would serve their children better as after-school tutoring in the national language, and warn against treating a living community as a museum.

Draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before next month's vote. It should recommend one of fund, reject, or fund with conditions, with reasons the applicants and the dissenting committee members could respond to. It should also propose a general criterion the foundation can use in future cases to decide when documenting or reviving a language is a defensible use of funds, since demand will always exceed what we can give. Engage seriously with the strongest version of the position you recommend against — among other things, by distinguishing kinds of value a language might have, by asking whose interests we should be serving, and by facing the question of whether a community's current pattern of transmission should settle the matter.
