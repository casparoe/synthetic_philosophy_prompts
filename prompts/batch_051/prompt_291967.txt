I have been studying with a formal epistemologist who publishes verdicts on self-locating cases. Her track record so far:

(a) God's coin toss. A fair coin is flipped. Heads: one observer is created in a room. Tails: two observers are created in indistinguishable rooms. You wake up in one of the rooms knowing the setup, with no way to tell which possible observer you are. She says your credence in heads should be 1/2.

(b) Red/blue rooms. A fair coin is flipped. Heads: ten observers are created, nine placed in red rooms and one in a blue room. Tails: ten observers are created, one in a red room and nine in blue rooms. You wake up in a red room. She says your credence in heads should be 0.9, and she adds that the reference class must be chosen before you learn your room color, not after.

(c) The presumptuous philosopher. Two cosmological theories T1 and T2 are equally supported by all non-indexical evidence. T1 entails that the universe contains 10^11 observers; T2 entails that it contains 10^20 observers. She says you should not raise your credence in T2 merely because T2 predicts more observers; your credence should stay at roughly 1/2 for each theory.

From discussion I know she rejects the self-indication assumption (the idea that a hypothesis is confirmed simply by predicting more observers), and she accepts some version of the self-sampling assumption: conditional on a hypothesis, the probability of a de se observation is the fraction of observers in the chosen reference class who have that observation. The only unsettled question is which reference class she uses.
