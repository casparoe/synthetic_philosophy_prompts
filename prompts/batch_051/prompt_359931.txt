So my friends and I have been playing this game called Nomic, where the whole point is that the rules are themselves moves in the game—you can vote to change any rule, including the rules that say how to change rules. Last night we got into an argument I still can't resolve. We had a rule (call it Rule 12) that said "Any rule may be amended by a simple majority vote." Someone proposed amending Rule 12 itself to require a two-thirds majority, and it passed by a simple majority, like 5 to 3. Now half the group says Rule 12 is now a two-thirds rule, and the other half says the amendment was invalid because the new two-thirds requirement wasn't met when it was passed.

That fight spilled over into a bigger question: if a rule about changing rules can be changed using itself, doesn't the whole game rest on circular bootstrapping? Wouldn't you eventually need a foundational rule that can't be changed, to stop an infinite regress of rules getting their authority from other rules? It seems to me the game only works because everyone quietly agrees not to notice the circularity, and that the "valid" changes are just whatever the players happen to tollerate.
