Davidson's paper What Metaphors Mean is often glossed as the claim that a metaphor has no meaning beyond its literal meaning. Unpack that gloss carefully. What precisely does Davidson deny, what does he think a metaphor does instead, and why does he hold that metaphors cannot be paraphrased? And here's the student's answer, typed up as submitted:

Davidson denies that a sentence like "my lawyer is a shark" has a second, non-literal content riding on top of its literal content. The sentence means exactly what it literally means, and that literal claim is usually false. What makes the sentence a metaphor isnt a special kind of meaning but a special kind of effect: it prompts the hearer to notice similarities between the lawyer and a shark, with no fixed list of what gets noticed. Because the effect is psychological rather than semantic, paraphrase of a metaphor fails — any paraphrase would only restate the literal meaning and leave the effect out.

Lakoff and Johnson see metaphor as primarily conceptual rather than linguistic. Expressions like "he demolished my argument" and "I couldn't defend that position" aren't one-off figures of speech; they are surface realizations of a systematic mapping, ARGUMENT IS WAR, in which we understand arguing by way of fighting. Likewise, "our relationship is at a crossroads" draws on LOVE IS A JOURNEY. On their view, metaphor isn't deviant or decorative; it's the normal machinery through which abstract concepts get structure, and much of what we call literal language is built from old metaphor.

So I dont think the two views really conflict. Davidson is talking about novel, poetic metaphors, the ones that startle us; Lakoff and Johnson are talking about conventional, everyday metaphors that have already sunk into the language. A fresh metaphor either gets conventionalized — at which point it becomes just another literal expression and Davidson's account handles it — or it stays fresh, at which point its effect is still not meaning. Davidson covers the poetic case, Lakoff and Johnson cover the conventional case, so the two are compatible.
