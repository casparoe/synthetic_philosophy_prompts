I'm writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate philosophy of mind course that has just spent three weeks on Dennett's multiple drafts model and the color phi phenomenon, including the Orwellian and Stalinesque alternatives for how the brain could produce the reported experience of a spot that appears to change color midway through its apparent motion. I want a single essay question that cannot be answered by merely defining Orwellian and Stalinesque revision; it should force students to work through whether the two stories are empirically distinguishable at all. The question should be built around a hypothetical proposal: a lab claims it can settle the matter with EEG decoded in fine temporal detail, by determining whether the brain's red-encoding process ever reaches the threshold the lab identifies with conscious awareness before the green process arrives and overwrites it. If the red process does reach that threshold first, the lab says the revision is Orwellian; if it never does, the lab says the revision is Stalinesque.

Please write the exam question itself, a model answer of about 750 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks. The marking scheme should name five specific moves a strong answer must make, assign marks to each, and give a brief note for each on how students typically go wrong, for instance by treating the threshold as if consciousness were a directly measurable quantity, or by reducing Dennett's position to the claim that brains do nothing. The question should lead students to spell out both models precisely, identify what assumption about a definite time and place of conscious experience the proposed experiment needs, and evaluate whether the experiment could in principle break the tie between them. Students will have about forty-five minutes to write the essay. Please write the entire response as continuous prose, the way one would write an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
