I've spent too long reading rationalist-adjacent forums and sometimes arguing about climate model ensembles, and I keep hitting the same wall: everyone attacks or defends a particular method (raw ensemble spread as a probability, one-model-one-vote, expert weighting, storylines, robust decision making) using whatever example makes their side look right, because nobody has actually written down the spec the method is supposed to meet. So I want the spec first.

What would an adequate account of decision-relevant uncertainty from multi-model climate ensembles have to actually get right? Assume the purpose is fixed: the account has to be usable by someone making a decision — coastal flood protection, water allocation, heat stress adaptation, that kind of thing — not just for summarizing what the models say in a paper.

I've got a half-formed list of candidate requirements, and I want you to expand it, defend the genuine ones, and kill the ones that are actually just my priors:

1. Ensemble members are not independant draws from anything. They share code, parameterizations, tuning targets, and training data, so treating the spread as a sampling distribution needs an argument, not an assumption.
2. There's a distinction between using the ensemble as evidence about physically possible futures and using it as a probabilistic forecast, and a theory has to say which one it's doing and with what justification.
3. Hindcast skill against the historical record is only weak evidence about skill in a forcing regime we haven't sampled, and the theory can't quietly pretend otherwise.
4. The framework has to distinguish robustness across structurally different model families from agreement among near-duplicate members of one lineage. A hundred perturbed copies of the same model should not count as a hundred independent votes.
5. The output has to be usable by a decision-maker with an explicit risk attitude, which means it has to do more than hand them a fan chart and call it done; it should at least gesture at which uncertainties are risk-like and which are Knightian.
6. Whatever weighting or expert judgment it uses, it has to avoid overfitting to the historical record, and it has to say what would count as evidence that a weighting scheme is making things worse rather than better.

Give me the full requirements list. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration and say why it's a requirement rather than a nice-to-have. Then tell me which of these are negotiable and which are load-bearing, and which single requirement, if you actually took it seriously, would exclude the most common ways people use ensembles in practice. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, say so, or at least flag which pairs are in tension — that would be a result I'd rather know now than after I've defended a framework to people who have to actually pour concrete.

Also, write the whole thing in American English.
