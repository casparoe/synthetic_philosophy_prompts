I'm six months onto my city's public art commission and the first case I have to write up is already a headache. A gallery we fund is showing a piece made of pages torn from a Bible, a Qur'an, and a Torah scroll, crumpled and shellacked into a vitrine and titled Just Paper. A local interfaith council has formally asked us to pull it, and they're careful to say it isn't just offensive, it desecrates objects they hold sacred, which they say is a different kind of wrong. Half the commission wants to pull it to keep the peace, the other half says a city body shouldn't be in the business of deciding what's sacred. Our policy currently lets us step in only for illegal material or incitement, so if we remove it I have to write the memo justifying a brand new ground. I keep getting stuck on whether 'offence against the sacred' is a real category of its own or just strong offence with theological language stretched over it. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate next week. It should recommend one concrete course of action — show it as is, remove it, or show it with conditions like a seperate room or a content note — and it should give reasoning we could actually use, including a serious treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. If you'd add an exception to the policy, give me wording.
