I am taking a university course on moral responsibility this term, and we have spent the last few weeks on complicity — the idea that a person can be caught up in another's wrongdoing without being its principal author or main cause. I have been trying to work out what I actually think by testing my gut reactions against a list of cases, and I would like a precise statement of the principle behind them, if there is one.

Here are my verdicts, reported as honestly as I can.

The getaway driver. A friend tells Marco he plans to rob a bank and asks for a ride. Marco agrees and drives him there and back. I have no hesitation saying Marco is complicit in the robbery.

The sleeping lookout. Lei agrees to act as lookout for a planned robbery, but he drinks too much and sleeps through the whole thing. The robbery succeeds without any warning from him. I still call him complicit in the robbery, even though I cannot point to anything he caused.

The helpful hardware clerk. A customer tells Nia he needs a crowbar and bolt cutters because he is going to break into a storage locker that night. She sells them to him, on the thought that it is not her job to police what customers do. I call her complicit in the burglary.

The cloud engineer. Tom maintains servers for a payment platform. He knows that a substantial share of the platform's users are fraud rings, and he has complained to his manager, but he keeps the infrastructure running for everyone. I would criticize him for staying quiet, but I would not call him complicit in the fraud, and I am not fully sure why I draw that line.

The sidewalk cheerer. During a beating on the street, Jonas stands across the road and cheers loudly. The attackers notice and feel encouraged, but they would have continued either way. I find what Jonas does disgusting, but I do not think he is complicit in the assault.

The small shareholder. Priya owns a small stake in a retail chain. She votes in favor of a merger that the board knows will break long-standing contracts with warehouse workers. Her shares are nowhere near decisive, and the measure passes with 68% of the vote. She votes yes solely because she expects the merger to raise the share price. I am strongly inclined to say she is not complicit in the contract breaches, or at most complicit in a very thin sense. But this is the case I keep going back and forth on, because it looks as though the wrong is baked into the very measure she supports.

I am drawn to something like the following principle: a person is complicit in wrongdoing when they intentionally make common cause with it — by agreeing to play a role in it, coordinating with its authors, or deliberately providing what it needs — whether or not their contribution changes the outcome. That would explain why the sleeping lookout is complicit (he agreed to be part of the joint effort) and why the sidewalk cheerer is not (he expressed an attitude without coordinating with anyone). It would also explain why Nia is complicit while Tom is not: Nia supplies a particular scheme with the tools it needs, whereas Tom provides a generic platform to everyone. But I cannot make the principle fit the shareholder. She votes in a coordinated process, for the very measure that includes the wrong, with her eyes open about what the merger entails. If that is not making common cause with the wrongdoing, I do not know what the phrase means; and yet I still do not want to say she is complicit in the way the getaway driver is.

What I would like from you is this. State the principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that best captures my verdicts, and state it precisely, with each clause motivated by the case or cases it is doing work for. Tell me which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and whether you think that verdict should be revised. Then apply the resulting account to a further case I have been unable to settle: a caterer who agrees to cater a lawful fundraising dinner for an organization he believes is responsible for political violence, knowing that the dinner will raise money that helps fund that violence. Is he complicit in the violence, and how would his position compare with the hardware clerk's? Please answer in American English.
