I've been trying to sort out my intuitions about the void-for-vagueness doctrine—how precise a criminal law actually has to be—and I keep ending up confused about what principle, if anything, my verdicts add up to. I thought I held the standard view: a law is too vague when an ordinary person can't know in advance whether their conduct is covered, or when it leaves too much to the discretion of whoever enforces it. But my gut reactions to particular laws don't all fit that view, and I'm starting to wonder which ones I should give up.

My verdicts, for what they're worth. A 65 mph speed limit is fine, even though 64 and 66 are basically the same; the line is arbitrary but it's knowable and measurably. An ordinance making it an offense to annoy, harass, or otherwise alarm another person in a public place is not fine—it's a blank check that could sweep in a street preacher, a panhandler, and someone playing the trombone badly. A stalking statute that makes it a crime to engage in a course of conduct that would cause a reasonable person to fear for their safety is fine, even though "reasonable person" and "fear" are fuzzy. A loitering law that simply forbids remaining in a public place with intent to steal is not fine; it needs to pin down observable behavior. A housing code offense for failing to keep a yard clean and sanitary is not fine. A water-quality rule limiting a certain industrial chemical to 0.4 micrograms per liter is fine, even though I can't picture a microgram and would need a lab to know wether I was over it. An ordinance banning obscene live performances, with no further definition, is not fine, even though courts have elaborate tests for obscenity.

Here's what bothers me most. My friend says I'm being inconsistent: a law that turns on what a reasonable person would fear is no more definite than a law that turns on what a reasonable person finds annoying—both tell a jury to imagine a normal person's reaction. I can't say why I trust one and not the other. I also notice I'm fine with a number almost nobody understands (0.4 micrograms per liter) but not with "clean and sanitary," which at least points at something I can see. Is there one principle that gets all seven of these verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn? And what does the repaired principle say about a campus speech code that punishes speech undermining an atmosphere of mutual respect but lists several examples of what's prohibited?
