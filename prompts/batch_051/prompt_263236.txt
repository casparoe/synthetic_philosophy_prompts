I'm drafting a novel and I keep getting stuck on my judge character. She's known in her town as the one person who can't be corrupted, and I want the book to actually test that reputation instead of just asserting it. Here's the criteria I've been using in my notes for what it means to be incorruptible: (1) can't be bought — money, gifts, future jobs don't move her; (2) can't be threatened — even credible threats to her or the people she loves don't move her; (3) no favoritism — she won't bend for friends, family, or her own people; (4) she sticks to her principles even when it costs her something real, not just when it looks good.

Now here's the character. She's a local judge. Over the years people have tried everything on her: envelopes of cash, a job offer from a developer, threats against her son — she turned them all down and reported them. Her sister once asked her to soften a sentence and she didn't return the call. Fine, so far so good. But I'm worried the reputation itself is the thing she's corruptible through. The only way anyone can move her is by appealing to the story of her own honesty. A defense lawyer figured out he can get her to rule against his client by implying, in front of the gallery, that she might be the kind of judge who can be bought. She'll overcorrect to prove a point. And there's an old case she got wrong — she knows she got it wrong, but she won't reopen it because reversing herself would look, she says, like she'd been gotten to. Meanwhile someone is serving time over it.

So: is this woman incorruptible in the sense my four criteria pick out? Go criterion by criterion and be honest about the ones she passes and the ones where it gets weird. Then tell me wether the problem is with her or with the criteria — specifically, whether incorruptibility needs a fifth criterion about being able to revise your own decisions without treating that as a form of corruption. I don't need you to fix the novel, but I do want to know whether I've written a character who's incorruptible in every way except the one that matters.
