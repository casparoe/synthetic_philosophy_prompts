I'm a junior taking a philosophy of mind seminar, and in two weeks I have to lead the session on personal identity and uploading. My line is this: if a gradual replacement procedure swaps out your neurons one at a time for functionally identical artificial ones while you're awake and talking the whole time, you survive; but if a destructive scan-and-upload freezes, slices, scans, and simulates your brain, you do not survive, even though the finished system has the same functional organization and claims to have your memories, plans, and quirks. I keep saying the difference is that the first case has an unbroken, causally continuous process with temporal overlap, while the second involves a gap where the original is destroyed and a new causal thread starts up. But I'm already loosing sleep over the comeback I know is coming: if no single neuron swap matters, then how can the whole chain amount to surviving while the upload, which is the same swaps compressed into a different causal wrapper, doesn't?

The group is small but sharp. Two students are into psychological-continuity views and basically think the upload survives, or at least that survival is a matter of degree and my bright line is fake. One is an animalist who thinks I'm wrong in the other direction: you are a living organism, so both procedures kill you, and the artificial neurons just produce a very good impostor. The professor keeps pushing us to ask whether the distinction I'm drawing is tracking anything deep, or just tracking squishy intuitions about whether a body got destroyed. I also expect someone to bring up bridging cases, like a version of gradual replacement done in batches under anesthesia, or a scan that non-destructively copies you and then the original dies of unrelated causes a week later, and to argue that my view gives weird verdicts there.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet for this talk, not a defense. I want the objections in roughly the order this audience will actually raise them, phrased the way each person would actually put them, each marked with how likely and how damaging it is, a short reply, and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Tell me specifically what I should concede in the moment and what I should hold, and include the question I should be praying nobody asks. End with a single line that starts "Bottom line:" and gives a one-sentence summary of the single biggest weakness in my position.
