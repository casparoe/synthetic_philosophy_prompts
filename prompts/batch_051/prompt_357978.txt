I have no formal training in philosophy of science, just a long-standing habit of reading about how scientific fields change their minds. I keep being pulled between two pictures. On one picture, science remains rational through a revolution because a framework earns its scientific standing by specifying in advance what observations would count against it; a revolution is simply the public process of letting those observations decide. On the other picture, the old framework and the challenger share too little for such advance tests to settle things: what counts as evidence, an anomaly, or even a measurement can be redescribed from inside each framework, so a change of allegiance looks more like a mass conversion than an inference. These are roughly the positions of Popper and Kuhn as I understand them, but the positions matter more to me than the labels.

Here is the counterfactual I want worked through. Take an ordinary mature empirical field—about the size, funding, and career structure of a natural science in the mid-twentieth century, but otherwise generic. Its dominant framework has been productive for decades but is now accumulating anomalies, and a challenger framework is just beginning to form. At that moment, the main funding bodies impose a rule: every published empirical study must include a dated disconfirmation clause, written before the data arrive, listing several concrete observational outcomes that would compel the authors to regard their own framework as seriously damaged. Clauses can be revised only once a year and only in public. Funders treat a researcher's record of honoring previous clauses as a major marker of scientific competence.

Predict how the field changes over the next two or three decades, and let me see the mechanism rather than only a verdict. What do researchers actually write in these clauses, given the pressure to make them neither too weak nor too strong? How do the clauses behave as anomalies mount, as auxiliary assumptions get adjusted, and as the challenger grows? Does the eventual choice between frameworks become a public calculation, or do the Kuhn-type difficulties reappear inside the clauses themselves—through theory-laden observation, redescription of outcomes, or goalpost-moving without visible cheating? Follow the incentives and career risks at each link, and include how the challenger's supporters could use the system against the entrenched framework.

Finish by saying which predictions you are most confident in and which depend on assumptions about researchers' honesty, the ambiguity of experiments, the separability of a framework from its auxiliary assumptions, and the structure of prestige in the field.
