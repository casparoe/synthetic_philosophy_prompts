ok so i'm putting together a list of the different sub-arguments that fall under the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism (the family of complaints that gets traced back to Rawls's point about extending the logic of one person's prudential trade-offs over time to a whole society). i've tried to cover the standard ones, but i have a nagging feeling the list still isnt exhaustive. here's what i've got:

(1) the conflation version: within a single life it's rational to accept a present loss for a bigger later gain, because the same subject ends up with both halves of the trade; utilitarianism scales that intrapersonal logic up to society, and that only works if you treat a bunch of different persons as if they were one.

(2) the utility monster version: if some being gets vastly more utility from each unit of a resource than everyone else does, utilitarianism says to hand it everything, leaving everyone else with nothing. the monster just makes vivid that the view doesn't care where utility sits, only how much of it there is.

(3) the scapegoat version: punishing an innocent person, or killing one to save five, can come out required on the utilitarian ledger, because one person's loss is entered as a negative that offsets other people's gains. but a person isn't a quantity that other people get to spend to top up the total.

(4) the distribution version: utilitarianism cares about the sum and not the spread; if the total is the same, it's indifferent between one person having all the welfare and the same amount being shared out across many. diminishing marginal utility only papers over this, and only contingently.

(5) the integrity version (my sense is williams's integrity objection is really a cousin of these rather than a direct descendant, but i'm keeping it on the list anyway): utilitarianism makes each agent responsible for all the bad consequences they could have prevented, so a person's own ground projects become just one more asset to liquidate whenever the ledger says so.

i'm pretty sure there's another genuinely distinct version i'm not thinking of — and by genuinely distinct i mean not a restatement, special case, or combination of the ones above. what is it? say what it adds that these five don't, and be honest about whether it's any good as an objection or whether it collapses back into one of the ones on my list.
