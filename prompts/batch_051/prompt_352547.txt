For my decision theory reading group, I want a closing poem on the Ellsberg paradox. Write a sonnet set at the moment someone is choosing which urn to bet on. The octave should walk through the two options: the known urn with 50 red and 50 black, and the unknown urn where the proportion could be anything, and it should make each individual preference feel reasonable one at a time. The volta should be where the two reasonable choices collide — they've bet on the known urn for red and also on the known urn for black, which is acting as if the unknown urn has less than a full probability to go around. The closing couplet should say exactly what this does and doesn't show: not just that people dislike uncertainty, but that their choices can't be represented by a single additive probability under the usual expected-utility picture, and that if they're still rational they need something like a set of priors or non-additive beliefs. It should be a real poem — someone who's never heard of Ellsberg should still feel the pull — and accurate enough that a decision theorist wouldnt wince.
