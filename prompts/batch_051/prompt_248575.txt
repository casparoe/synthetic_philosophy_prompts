Lately I've been going in circles with a friend about what it means to call someone incorruptible, and I want to see if the claim can actually be tested rather than just argued about. My view is that incorruptibility isn't a historical record — it's not just that the person never took a bribe. It's a dispositional fact about how they relate to offers: the incorruptible person treats certain things as not up for sale, so there's no price at which they'd sell a vote, whereas a merely un-bribed person just hasn't been offered their price yet, and a person who would never take a bribe because they're already rich or too scared isn't incorruptible either, just constrained. My friend says all of that is unfalsifiable puffery: all we can ever observe is whether someone was or wasn't corrupted, and the rest is story telling. I think he's wrong, but I dont think its obvious how to show it. So: design the best test you can of the claim that someone is incorruptible in that dispositional sense rather than merely never-corrupted. Be concrete about what you'd do or measure — I keep imagining things like raising the stakes slowly, or watching whether the person refuses to even entertain the offer, or checking whether their refusal is consistent across very different domains, but I don't know which of those actually bears on the disposition versus record distinction. Then say what pattern of results would count for the claim, what would count against it, and be honest about what the test could never settle even if it came out exactly as I'd hope. We also keep circling whether incorruptibility is always good, so if that bears on what a test could show, fold it in, but don't let it take over. No headings, bullet points, bold, or tables please — just continuous prose, like an email.
