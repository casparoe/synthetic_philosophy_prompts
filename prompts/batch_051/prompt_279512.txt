Im a junior, and me and my friends keep hitting the same wall with our school's name policy. In the student portal we can set a "preferred name" that shows up on rosters, gradebook, student IDs, and yearbook, no legal change needed. But report cards, transcripts, state testing, and the eventual diploma all use your legal name, and the registrars office will not touch those without a court order. At the same time, the portal groups everyone into "households" and auto-generated emails to families come with the subject line "[student's last name] Family." If a kid's parents kept different last names, only one ends up on the household account and the other gets listed as an outside "contact." My friend Sky has gone by her mom's last name her whole life; the school let her put it as a preferred name, but her honor roll certificate got printed with her dad's last name, which she's never used. Another friend is Korean and the system wants a "first name" and "last name," so his family name gets put in the wrong slot and teachers greet him by it as if it were his first name on day one.

I'm not asking whether the school is being rude or just careless. Im trying to figure out what theory of what a name is, what it picks out, and whose call it is would make this whole setup make sense: a given name is like a display preference you can change, a family name is a fixed fact you inherit, a "family" is people who share one last name, and a legal name is the real one underneath. Is that actually a coherent view? Does the practice strictly presuppose that view, or is it just compatible with it while several other views could also explain it (for example, that names are whatever the bearer says they are, or whatever the government records say)? And if the school switched to one of those other views, what would it have to change on the ground, not just in its forms? Please give your main answer in prose, and then after the prose give a table summarizing it, with these columns: Feature of the practice, What it appears to presuppose, Whether a rival view could also explain it, and Change required if we adopted that rival view. Fill maybe four to six rows.
