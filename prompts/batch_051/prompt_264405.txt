I used to help run a tiny Nomic club (the game where the ruleset includes rules for changing the ruleset), and the same move came up every few months: somebody proposes an amendment saying "Rule A may henceforth be amended or repealed only by a rule adopted under Rule A," and then everyone ties themselves in knots arguing whether the amendment bootstrapped itself into validity or just blew up the very procedure that passed it. The forum rulings we wrote were half serious, but I always wanted to see it done straight, as an actual court opinion with the deadpan confidence of a real appellate judge and none of the forum hedging.

Write a satirical appellate opinion, in house style—headnotes, numbered paragraphs, a little dicta—ruling on whether an amendment to "the amendment clause" is valid when the amendment clause is the very source of the amendment power. The humor should come from the court resolving the self-reference by generating even more self-reference: it should commit to a holding, then quietly amend the holding in a footnote, float a "hyper-amendment rule" for amending the amendment clause, and end with a decision whose validity allegedly rests on procedures its just declared void. Stay deadpan throughout; a reader should be able to tell exactly which legal bootstrapping move is being skewered, but no character should wink at it. The underlying point to land is that a rule can't simultaneously be the ground and the product of the process that gives it force—and that legal talk has no honest way to say so, only more rules all the way down.

Around 900 words. Commit to a single definite ruling (however self-undermining) rather than deferring with "this court need not reach." The final line should be a one-sentence plain-language summary beginning "Summary:".
