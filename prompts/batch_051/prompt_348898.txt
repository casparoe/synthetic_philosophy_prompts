I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep seeing the phrase "infinite regress" used in design discussions as an automatic objection: a proposal gets dismissed because its justifications would never bottom out. I know that cannot be the whole story, because I also work with recursive structures and infinite streams that never bottom out but are perfectly productive. I want a built-to-order example that isolates what turns an infinite regress from a harmless byproduct into a vicious one, because I need a reusable pattern for design discussions rather than a one-off illustration.

Construct a thought experiment: a pair of variants of a single ordinary engineering-organization process (compliance, documentation, or design approval), differing by exactly one clause, such that the same relation, one item deriving its justification from the next, repeats infinitely in both variants, but in one variant the regress is vicious and in the other it is benign. Build the vicious variant first; then produce the benign variant by editing that one clause, not by swapping in a different kind of process or an outside referee. Constraints: realistic in tone and content, with the only idealization being the stipulated infinity of items or levels, so no perfect predictors, demons, or exotic metaphysics; the benign variant must not simply assert that the regress is harmless, since the harmlessness must fall out of how the edited clause structures the chain; and the two variants should stay close enough that someone inclined to treat every infinite justificatory regress as fatal would find the benign case genuinely uncomfortable rather than obviously different. Keep the two scenario variants together under 800 words.

After the scenario, do the verification. For each variant, state exactly what relation repeats at each step, what a single step would have to deliver for the original item to count as justified, and whether the demand is ever discharged or is merely handed to the next step forever. Name the single structural feature that does the work, explain what happens if it is removed or swapped between the variants, and say whether the cases would then collapse into each other or into a skeptical regress in which nothing is ever justified. Then give the strongest version of the objection that your benign variant is really vicious after all: what exactly would a defender of that objection point to, how would you answer them without changing the case, and what hidden assumption does your answer lean on?

If the task as posed is itself ill-posed, for instance if there is no single structural feature separating vicious from benign regresses, or if the distinction bottoms out in a prior commitment about whether some justifications must simply be accepted and therefore cannot be drawn this cleanly, say so outright rather than delivering a neat pair that fits the request only by smuggling in the conclusion. I would rather learn where the line actually is than collect an example that misleads me the next time I use the pattern.
