A friend and I spent most of an evening going in circles over an urn filled with coloured marbles, and I still cannot locate the step at which his reasoning slipped, if it slipped at all. The set-up is as follows. The urn contains ninety marbles. Thirty are known to be red; the remaining sixty are each either black or yellow, but the proportion is entirely unknown: there may be no black marbles, sixty black marbles, or anything in between. Consider two pairs of gambles, each of which pays £100 on one outcome and nothing otherwise. In the first pair, the choice is between red and black. In the second pair, the choice is between red-or-yellow and black-or-yellow. I prefer the bet on red in the first pair and the bet on black-or-yellow in the second; my friend says nearly everyone does. He then advanced the following argument.

(1) In the first pair, the two bets differ only in the winning colour, so preferring red to black must reveal a belief that red is more probable than black. (2) By the same reasoning, preferring black-or-yellow to red-or-yellow must reveal a belief that black-or-yellow is more probable than red-or-yellow. (3) Probabilities of disjoint outcomes add, so the belief revealed in (2) amounts to P(black) + P(yellow) > P(red) + P(yellow), and the shared yellow term cancels, leaving P(black) > P(red). (4) But that contradicts what (1) revealed, so no single probability assignment can capture both of my preferences. (5) Rational choice under uncertainty requires precisely such a single probability assignment, so at least one of the two preferences must be irrational.

I suspect that (5) is doing more work than it announces, but my friend insists that the fault, if any, lies in (1) and (2), because a preference between two bets with identical payoffs need not reveal a probability at all. Which of us, if either, is right? Please say whether the argument is valid; if it is not, identify exactly which step fails, give the strongest reading of that step on which it is true, and explain whether the argument goes through once that reading is adopted. Please answer in British English.
