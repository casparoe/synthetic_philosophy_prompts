I'm prepping a podcast episode on Sartre's Existentialism Is a Humanism, using the Philip Mairet translation, and my guest is a philosopher who has spent her career defending the lecture against the charge that "existence precedes essence" is quietly incoherent. She also refuses the soft reading that turns Sartre into a crypto-Kantian about universalizability, so I expect her to resist both the flat-footed objection and the overly tidy rescue. I'd like you to write me seven or eight interview questions that zero in on this passage early in the lecture: "For at bottom, what is alarming in the doctrine that I am about to try to explain to you is – is it not? – that it confronts man with a possibility of choice." The questions should go beyond restating the view. At least one should challenge her on whether Sartre's later appeal to a shared "human condition" imports an essence through the back door, and at least one should press on whether the claim that in choosing myself I choose for all mankind makes choice less radical than the quoted line suggests. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give—so if she says the human condition is only the formal constraints of facticity, ask why formal constraints don't count as an essence in the relevant sense.
