A buddy of mine who works on fisheries policy keeps making the same move whenever the conversation turns to fish farming: he says a salmon net pen is just a chicken warehouse with water, a shrimp pond is a cattle feedlot, and putting live crabs through an ice slurry is no different in kind from gassing pigs in a CO2 chamber. I get why the analogy is tempting—it imports all the moral urgency of the land-based factory farming debate into aquaculture in one step, and it dares you not to flinch. But I suspect the places where the analogy breaks might be doing the real moral work rather than just being picky. Fish are ectotherms and mostly breathe water, so their welfare hangs on oxygen levels, ammonia, and temperature in ways that have no clean counterpart to air quality and litter for chickens. Fish can use all three dimensions of a net pen, so a stocking density number means something quite different than birds per square meter of barn floor. Slaughter methods for fish and crustaceans tend to kill through different mechanisms—asphyxiation in air, ice slurry, percussive stunning, boiling for some crustaceans—and some of the species people lump in, bivalves especially, may not be sentient at all, while there is decent evidence that at least some fish and decapods are. It would be easy to just say "the analogy is loose but the conclusion stands," and I'm not interested in that.

Make a table with these columns, one row per feature of the analogy: (1) the feature on the terrestrial factory-farming side, (2) the putatively corresponding feature in aquaculture, (3) mapping quality, which must be one of "clean", "strained", or "breaks", (4) the specific biological or practical difference doing the work when the mapping is strained or breaks, and (5) whether that disanalogy is load-bearing for the conclusion that the moral case against terrestrial factory farming carries over to fish and crustacean aquaculture, plus a short note on why or why not. After the table, give a JSON object with fields: "overall_verdict" (one of "carries", "carries with repairs", or "does not carry"), "load_bearing_disanalogies" (array of short strings), "innocuous_disanalogies" (array of short strings), "what_the_analogy_gets_right" (string), and "repaired_version" (string explaining the strongest version of the argument that survives the audit).
