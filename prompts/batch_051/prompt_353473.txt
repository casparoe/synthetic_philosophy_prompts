I've been trying to systematize my own methodological intuitions about forecasting from historical base rates, and I keep finding that my verdicts on individual cases don't add up to a single view. If someone uses the survival rates of past ventures in the same industry to estimate that a new venture has a low chance of lasting a decade, I find that entirely legitimate. If a historian uses the lifespans of past empires to put wide brackets on how long a current great power might last, I find that a sensible outside view, though I hesitate because the reference class is contestable. If, however, someone predicts a specific remaining lifespan for a current power by selecting past empires after the fact—chosen because they share some feature that flatters the current one—I reject the forecast as cherry-picking, though I cannot state the rule that separates this from the historian's brackets.

When someone treats the present moment as randomly placed between the construction and the collapse of an old wall and concludes there is a 50 percent chance it will stand for at least as long again, I find the move clever but feel that treating the present as a random draw is unjustified. When someone says a Broadway show that has already run for a decade is more likely to run another year than a newly opened show, I accept it, since survival itself seems to be evidence of staying power. When an ecologist uses a century of extinction records for related bird species to argue that a particular species is at elevated risk, I accept that for conservation triage, though I would not treat the base rate as decisive for any one species. And when the same kind of reasoning is applied to the human species as a whole—that a person is probably not at a special early point in the history of humanity, so a very long future is improbable—I find the conclusion alarming and the reasoning harder to dismiss than I would like.

Is there one principle about when outside-view, reference-class, or self-locating forecasting is warranted that captures all or nearly all of these verdicts? If so, state it precisely and say what it implies for the doomsday-style case and for the difference between choosing a reference class before or after the fact. If not, tell me which of my verdicts the best candidate principle forces me to drop, and why. Please answer in American English.
