I have been unable to decide whether a parallel I keep noticing is a real identity of structure or just the word "ensemble" doing the work, and I would like it stress-tested. The parallel crystallized for me while reading Herger et al., "Model dependence in multi-model climate ensembles: weighting, sub-selection and out-of-sample testing" (Earth System Dynamics, 2019):

"The rationale for using multi-model ensembles in climate change projections and impacts research is often based on the expectation that different models constitute independent estimates; therefore, a range of models allows a better characterisation of the uncertainties in the representation of the climate system than a single model. However, it is known that research groups share literature, ideas for representations of processes, parameterisations, evaluation data sets and even sections of model code. Thus, nominally different models might have similar biases because of similarities in the way they represent a subset of processes, or even be near-duplicates of others, weakening the assumption that they constitute independent estimates."

Read as a description of machine-learning ensembles, that paragraph would be almost exactly right: methods like bagging and random forests work because averaging many imperfect predictors whose errors are sufficiently independent shrinks variance, and if the supposedly diverse base learners are near-duplicates — shared code, shared training data, correlated errors — averaging them buys roughly nothing. In both fields, diversity is the raw resource, low error correlation is what licenses averaging, and near-duplicates quietly degrade the ensemble. Is there a genuine structural isomorphism here, or does the analogy bottom out at a shared diagnosis? If it is genuine, what would a climate modeler need to establish about the joint distribution of model errors for bias–variance-type results to transfer to CMIP-style multi-model means? And, to keep me honest, name at least one concrete result the ML analogy seems to license about climate projections but that is probably false once the disanalogy — whatever it is — is made precise.
