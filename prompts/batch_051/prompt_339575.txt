I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether an AI could end up with goals nobody gave it. The puzzle is this: human wants come from an evolutionary process that selected for things like food, status, and not dying. An AI never went through any of that. So here's the counterfactual I want to work through.

Suppose we train a system from scratch on exactly one objective: predicting the next video frame from a camera mounted on a little wheeled robot. There is no reward signal, no human feedback, no imitation of humans, and no instruction to do anything other than get better at that prediction. The system can move the robot, but only because moving changes what the camera sees and therefore changes the data it learns from. We run it for years, continuously learning, in a big building the robot can roam. What, if anything, would this system end up wanting?

I dont need a confident yes or no — I need the chain. Where would a want even come from in a machine like this? Under what conditions would it start acting as if it had goals, and what would those goals be about: exploring the building, controlling the camera, keeping itself running, something else? Be explicit about which links in your argument are robust and which are guesses, and finish by naming the one assumption that, if it turned out false, would most change your prediction.

Please structure the answer as four sections: "Where wants come from" (~150 words) laying out the possible sources of wanting; "The machine, not the organism" (~100 words) restating what the scenario rules out; "What it ends up wanting" (~300 words) giving your prediction and reasoning; and "The weakest link" (~100 words) naming the condition that would most change your answer.
