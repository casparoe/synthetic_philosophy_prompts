Suppose a lab is training a reinforcement-learning agent to do customer-service chat, with a learned reward model that gives scalar rewards including negative ones. A grad student has talked the team into adding a welfare flag: at each timestep the policy gets an extra output it can set or leave unset, and the flag has no effect on the environment, the reward, or the loss — it's a pure signal to the lab. The internal rule is that whenever the flag is set on a timestep where the reward is negative, the run pauses, the episode is logged as a possible pain event, and the team has to either modify the reward or file a written justification before training continues. Walk through how this actually plays out. What should we expect the policy to learn to do with the flag, given that only task reward is optimized? What incentives does the rule create for the lab, for whoever reviews the justifications, and for anyone auditing the logs later? Can the flag ever be informative about whether negative reward is valenced, or does it end up tracking something else? Where does the scheme break first, and is there a tweak that fixes it without making it even easier to game?
