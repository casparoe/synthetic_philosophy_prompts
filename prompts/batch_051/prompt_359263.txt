One of the more paranoid worries in AI safety is that debate won't save us because of obfuscated arguments. Quick background: the debate proposal is that instead of having one superhuman AI just tell us the answer, you have two AIs argue both sides in front of a human or weaker AI judge. The hopeful story says the truthful debater can always point out the exact bad step in the liar's reasoning, so the judge never has to be smart enough to find the truth themselves, only smart enough to check a specific pointed-out flaw. The obfuscation worry is that a superhuman liar can bury the bad step inside an argument so tangled the judge can't follow the pointer even after it's pointed out.

Our lab is kicking around a fix. Each debater has to submit its argument as an explicit tree of claims. Any claim can be written out directly or compressed by citing one of the debater's own earlier lemmas. The judge sees the compressed tree. At any moment it can demand that a claim be unrolled one level — the citation gets replaced with the lemma's immediate subclaims. Every unroll costs the debater a fixed fee. At the end the judge picks a winner; the winner gets all their fees back plus a bonus that shrinks as the number of unrolls demanded from them goes up. The loser eats their fees and gets payed nothing. The idea is that honest arguments are cheap to unroll and obfuscated ones are expensive, so the truthful debater ends up subsidized while the liar bleeds.

Walk me through how this actually plays out, because I can already feel it getting gamed. Where does the first exploit show up: the liar making the honest argument look tangled and their own look clean, or the honest debater deliberately triggering unrolls of the liar to run up their fees, or the judge silently becoming the real bottleneck because it has to choose what to unroll? What stops a debater from hiding the flaw in a lemma nobody thinks to unroll? Does the winner's reimbursement break the scheme by making the fee not actually a cost for whoever expects to win? And what is the failure mode that kills the whole thing even if everyone plays nice?
