I keep noticing an asymmetry in the way people around me talk about values, and I cant tell if its principled or just a local prejudice. If someone spends years and real money finding out a truth that will make them worse off—getting a genetic test for a disease they can do nothing about, asking for a brutally honest performance review, checking whether a cherished family story is actually false—we treat that as almost noble: self-knowledge, taking the truth straight, refusing to flinch. But if the same person spends the same years and money maintaining a ritual practice that does no observable good, or preserving a dying craft purely because their grandparents did it, or keeping purity rules that make daily life harder, we start reaching for words like compulsion, superstition, sunk cost. In both cases the value is being pursued as if it matters beyond its consequences. Why does only truth-seeking get treated as something you could rationally want for its own sake, while tradition and purity are assumed to be instrumental until proven otherwise? Is there a real structural difference—truth is self-correcting in a way tradition isnt, or truth has an internal connection to reasons that custom lacks—or is it just an unexamined aesthetic preference? Put differently, should I keep this asymmetry, or is it an inconsistency we should just live with? Take the harder case for your own view—a costly tradition that produces no benefit and still feels binding, or a truth that is useless and in fact painful to know—and tell me whether the asymmetry should be kept, dropped, or reversed.
