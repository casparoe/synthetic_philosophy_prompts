I am a novelist, not a philosopher, and I am drafting a novel whose central moral problem is a character who consistently does things that anyone would call good, sometimes heroic, but that no one around her would call required: giving away most of an inheritance to a stranger with a plausible but weak claim on her, taking in a difficult and ungrateful relative, declining to benefit herself in ways that would be permissible but would let other people down. The novel depends on my being able to make the reader feel that these acts are intelligibly excellent without implying that everyone else in the book is blameworthy for failing to imitate her. I want the moral psychology to be exact rather than sentimental, so before I read further in moral philosophy or decide which account should shape the character, I want the problem stated at full scope.

Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of supererogation — acts that are better than duty requires but not required — has to satisfy. I want a list of requirements with the case, puzzle, or distinction behind each: which cases it must get right (the small favor, the costly heroism, the saint's whole life lived for others, the costly refusal to do something permitted but shabby), which distinctions it cannot collapse (the merely permissible versus the supererogatory, the cost to the agent versus the value of the act, going beyond a social code versus going beyond all-things-considered obligation, being optional versus being arbitrary, being praiseworthy versus being obligatory), and which standing puzzles it must dissolve or at least respect, above all the paradox of supererogation: how an act can be morally better than what morality requires without thereby becoming required. For each requirement, tell me whether it is non-negotiable or merely one camp's preference, and flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, indicate which single requirement does the most to exclude the most familiar accounts. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases that motivate them, not on a scorecard of theories; I will do that evaluation myself.
