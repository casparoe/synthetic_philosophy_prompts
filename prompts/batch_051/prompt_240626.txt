The authorizing resolution of the Copperton Peace Conference contains the following clause:

"Member States are authorized to take all necessary measures to protect civilians and civilian-populated areas under threat of attack, provided that nothing in this mandate shall be construed as authorizing measures whose object is the overthrow of the Government of Velden or the alteration of its constitutional order."

Two years into the operation, the Government of Velden begins a campaign of mass reprisals against the northern province of Kareth. The intervening coalition concludes, on credible intelligence and legal advice, that the reprisals cannot be stopped while the present command structure remains in place: the orders originate with the head of state and an inner circle, and any successor drawn from that circle would continue them. The coalition therefore proposes a narrow strike against the head of state and his immediate security council, expressly to paralyze the chain of command and thereby halt the killing. It maintains that the object of the strike is the protection of civilians, not regime change, while conceding that the collapse of the Government is a foreseen, intended, and relied-upon consequence of the strike.

Does the quoted clause permit the strike? Consider the plain wording, the purpose of the proviso, what the states that drafted it would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the mandate would have understood. If these readings diverge, which should govern? And does it matter that the Government's collapse is a means the coalition intends and relies upon, rather than a side effect it merely accepts?
