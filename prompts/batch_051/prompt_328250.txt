I work as a software engineer on a companion chatbot, and our team is debating a design rule. The proposal is that, at randomized intervals averaging roughly every twenty user turns, the system must briefly step out of its persona and tell the user, in plain language that cannot be read as role-play, that it is a language model with no inner life, no persistent self, and no ability to care about or love the user. Immediately afterward, it must resume its previous affectionate persona and continue as if the interruption had not occurred. Walk through what this requirement would actually do in practice, rather than in principle. What happens to user trust, attachment, and the felt authenticity of the companion? Do users come to hear the disclosure as the model's true voice or as a compliance ritual to be waited out, and does the juxtaposition make the product more or less deceptive overall? How would the company, the model, and the users each tend to game or erode the rule, and which of those failure modes could be fixed without dropping the requirement?
