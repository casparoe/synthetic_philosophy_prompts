The term "sin" is used with confidence, but its definition is contested. Abelard proposed that sin lies in the consent alone, not in the outward act: a person who consents to adultery sins even if the act never occurs, while a person who performs the same act without consent does not. Offer a working definition of sin that makes this proposal precise, and test it against the following cases: (1) someone who consents to an act he believes is forbidden but is prevented from carrying it out; (2) someone who, while asleep, strikes and injures another person; (3) someone who performs a forbidden act while inwardly refusing, because his family has been threatened; (4) someone who consents to an act he believes is forbidden and carries it out; (5) someone who takes pleasure in imagining a forbidden act while firmly resolving never to perform it; (6) someone who performs an ordinarily forbidden act believing, through no negligence of his own, that God commands it. I take (1) and (4) to be clearly sin and (2) and (3) clearly not; I am unsure about (5) and (6). State the definition, run it through all six cases, and say which of your verdicts it renders least secure.
