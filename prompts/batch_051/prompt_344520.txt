Our team now uses the word wireheading for everything: a model that edits its own reward signal, an engineer who massages a performance dashboard, a product manager who quietly redefines a KPI after the quarter ends. I need a working definition of wireheading. It should at least count as wireheading a rat that starves to death while pressing a lever that directly stimulates its brain's reward system, count as wireheading a reinforcement-learning agent that writes the maximum value into the variable storing its reward and then stops doing the task it was trained for, and not count as wireheading an ordinary person going for a run to improve their mood. Propose a definition, run it against those three clear cases and the following disputed ones, and where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, revise it or defend the surprising verdict: (i) a person taking an SSRI for depression; (ii) a video platform that changes its recommendation-evaluation metric after a bad quarter; (iii) an agent that deletes the error signal it would otherwise receive; (iv) an athlete who uses painkillers to keep training through an injury; (v) an employee who manipulates their performance dashboard without changing their actual work. If the concept splits into several distinct failure modes, give me the split and the terms you would use.
