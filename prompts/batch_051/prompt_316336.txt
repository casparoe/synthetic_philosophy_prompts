Labs train language models with RLHF by having human raters pick which of two candidate responses they prefer, and then training the model to score outputs the way raters do. After that people just call the model aligned. What theory of human values, or of what alignment even means, does that practice presuppose? It seems to need something like a stable, coherent preference ranking sitting inside each person waiting to be measured. But preferences are often inconsistent and constructed on the spot—ask me the same question an hour later and I might pick the other response. Does RLHF strictly require that stable preferences exist, or can the practice make sense if preferences are just whatever a rater happens to endorse in the moment? And if the latter, what is it even tracking, and what would have to change in the training objective for it to lock onto something more stable than snapshots?
