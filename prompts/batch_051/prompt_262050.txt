Clause 11(3) of the Ayling Trust's governing deed reads: 'In deciding among applications, the Trustees shall give priority to that use of the income which, in their reasoned judgement, would relieve the greatest amount of suffering per pound applied, regard being had to the degree of confidence with which the relief claimed can be attributed to the work to be funded.' The clause was inserted in 1987, when the Trust's grants went to direct relief: famine aid, field hospitals, clean-water programmes. The deed says nothing further about how to weigh uncertain or diffuse effects.

The Trustees now have two applications before them. One is an unusually well-evaluated programme that supplies oral rehydration salts and water filters through rural clinics; its cost per child protected is low, its effects are established by trial evidence, and nearly every benefit can be attributed to the funded activity with high confidence. The other is a five-year grant to a small policy organisation campaigning for legally binding limits on an airborne pollutant that is a major cause of premature death across the region. The organisation's modelling suggests that success would prevent on the order of tens of thousands of premature deaths a year, with a meaningful but hard-to-quantify chance of success. No randomised trial can settle attribution here: the benefit, if it arrives, will arrive through legislators and regulators, years later, and will be recorded as a change in policy rather than as a grant output.

Read Clause 11(3) as its drafters would have to read it now. Does it require the Trustees to prefer the water-and-salts programme? Does it permit funding the policy organisation at all? I would like you to take each approach to interpretation in turn — the plain words, the evident purpose of the clause, what the 1987 drafters would have said if asked about structural reform rather than direct relief, and what a reasonable trustee reading the deed today would take it to mean. Then say which approach ought to govern, and whether the clause quietly stacks the decision in favour of marginal interventions whose benefits can be attributed in the old way, against systemic interventions whose value lies in changing the conditions that keep producing the suffering. Set aside the immediate funding decision as such; what I chiefly want is an account of what the words 'attributed' and 'per pound applied' can honestly cover, and what a systems thinker would say has been written out of the decision before the Trustees sit down. Please answer in British English.
