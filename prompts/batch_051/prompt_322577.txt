I'm a graduate student leading a single fifty-minute seminar for first-year undergraduates in an introductory metaphysics course. The assigned topic is origin essentialism, approached through the question students actually find gripping: could you have come from a different sperm and egg? Last time I attempted the session it drifted almost immediately into cloning science fiction, and I closed the hour without ever having stated the distinction that does the real work. The room is mixed: a few students have read some philosophy, most are there to satisfy a distribution requirement, and at least one will arrive sure the question is either straightforward genetics or a disguised argument about abortion.

I need a teaching plan for that session rather than the explanation itself. Please specify the order in which the components must be introduced and what depends on what — I suspect that in some order the session needs the distinction between being this very person and merely being a person rather like this one, and the distinction between epistemic possibility and genuinely counterfactual possibility, but I'm not sure what has to be in place before the biological case can carry philosophical weight. Give me the one concrete example the entire fifty minutes should be built around, with an account of what it must have and what it must lack; I would like to avoid souls, switched-at-birth stories, and duplication machines. Identify the misconceptions I should expect and exactly where in the sequence to head each off — I am anticipating 'different gametes just means a sibling,' 'this is really about when personhood begins,' 'we can't know, so the question is empty,' and the harder one, 'identical twins came from the same sperm and egg, so are they the same person?'. Finally, provide one closing question whose answer would show that they have grasped the metaphysical claim rather than just learned the reproductive biology, and say what I should openly refuse to settle at this level so that the session does not slide into abortion, genetic determinism, or the twins case if the twins case would silently change the claim.
