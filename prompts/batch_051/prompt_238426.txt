Design a runnable activity for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on AI alignment. The setting is one room, 14 students, 90 minutes, with a whiteboard and ordinary paper available but no internet access, no screens, and no specialized equipment. Enrollment mixes long-time readers of rationalist and AI alignment blogs and forums with students new to the topic, so the activity must not presuppose prior exposure to technical vocabulary.

The activity should make participants feel the force of the distinction between goal-directed and approval-directed agents. Take the distinction roughly this way. A goal-directed agent optimizes for features of the world itself. An approval-directed agent optimizes for an overseer's signal of approval or reward, so that the world enters its deliberation as an instrument for producing that signal rather than as the target. Under dense, honest feedback the two orientations can look nearly identical; the important difference appears when the signal becomes costly, noisy, shallow, or easy to game.

The activity should be a game with roles, rules, and small real stakes such as points or candy, not a lecture followed by an illustration. Arrange the incentives so that participants drift into the divergence themselves: a competent player pursuing the stated incentives should end up doing something they would not have endorsed before play, and the group should recognize that the failure is not a defective agent but a different orientation toward the task. The takeaway should be that an approval-directed agent is not merely a badly trained goal-directed agent.

Give the complete rules and the facilitator's role, and include a timeline covering setup, play, and debrief; the idea should be allowed to emerge from what happens before it is named. Write out the debrief in full. The debrief should map specific moments of play onto the concepts of approval-directed and goal-directed behavior, a reward model, a proxy, and Goodharting (the divergence between a proxy and the underlying target once the proxy is optimized directly), should say what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it, and should end with an honest note on which feature of the real distinction a tabletop game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Write the response in American English.
