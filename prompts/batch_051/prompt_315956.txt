For my philosophy of history class I want to use the film Downfall (Der Untergang) to get at the Great Man theory vs. structural explanation debate—the question of how much we should explain the Holocaust and Nazi Germany by Hitler's particular psychology and decisions, versus by deeper structural forces like bureaucratic dynamics, economic crisis, long-standing antisemitism, and a fragmented state whose competing agencies radicalized each other. I keep going back and forth on what the film is actually saying. On one reading it's a deeply Great-Man film: it's literally a biopic of Hitler's last ten days, everything orbits his moods and rants, and the suggestion seems to be that once he kills himself the whole thing collapses because he was the force holding it together. But there's also a lot in the film that pushes the other way: the way the regime keeps grinding on after he's clearly loosing it, the generals and secretaries obeying long after any personal loyalty makes sense, Goebbels and Himmler jockeying for position, and the death of the Goebbels children as loyalty to an idea rather than to the man. So I can't tell whether the film is dramatizing the structuralist point that the system had its own momentum, or whether it's ironically captured by the very thing it's trying to critique.

I'd like a reading of Downfall through this debate. Specifically: does the film, taken as a whole, come down on the Great Man side, the structural side, or does it show something the historians' debate misses? Where does the film's medium itself—a feature film centered on one man, staged for dramatic compression—stack the deck, and where does the film resist its own format? And on the counterfactual question: does the film give us any material for thinking about whether a broadly similar catastrophe would have happened without Hitler, or is it just helpless on that question? I want the honest version, so if there are places where the reading feels forced, say so. Start with a one-sentence answer to the "what does the film actually do?" question, then the detailed reading, and keep the whole thing under 1,000 words.
