Consider modern intellectual-property law and notice that its main regimes do not own the same kind of thing. Copyright does not protect ideas, plots, methods, or facts, only the particular expression that has been fixed, and it targets copying: someone who independently produces an identical text or image, however improbable, has not infringed. A patent is different. It gives its holder rights against everyone who makes, uses, or sells the claimed invention, including someone who developed the same invention from scratch in ignorance of the patent. A trade secret is something else again: it is property only while it remains secret, and it evaporates when a rival reverse-engineers it or discovers it independently. And a trademark is not ownership of a word at all, but control over a sign in commerce, exercised mainly to prevent buyers from being confused about the source of goods.

So the law appears to hold, at the same time, that an idea can be owned against the whole world (patent), that only its expression can be owned and only against copyists (copyright), that concealment itself can be owned (trade secret), and that reputation can be owned while a word cannot be (trademark) — and all this for things that, unlike a bicycle, can be used by any number of people at once without anyone having less of it. This looks less like a single concept of owning an idea than like several concepts held together by one word. What philosophical commitments about ownership, ideas, and value does this patchwork presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory or theories, separating what the law strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with and marking where it is underdetermined enough that several rival theories would license the same rule. Then say what would have to change in the law if the underlying view were replaced by any of these: that we own only what we make with our own labor, that exclusive rights are justified solely as incentives to create and disclose, or that ideas are not the kind of thing that can be owned at all.
