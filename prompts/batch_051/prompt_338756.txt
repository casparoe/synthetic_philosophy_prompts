Me and a friend got into a long argument about underdetermination of theory by evidence. He kept saying that any finite set of data points can be fit by endlessly many different curves, so the data never single out a theory, and then he jumped to: the data never even favor one theory over another, so scientists are basically choosing on taste. I pushed back at the time, but now I'm starting to think he's right. If there's always some empirically equivalent rival to whatever theory you like, what could it even mean to say the evdience favors one theory over the other? It seems like underdetermination just shows that experiments never really support one theory more than its rivals. So why do scientists and philosophers of science still talk as if they do? Is there a real difference between 'the data don't force a theory' and 'the data don't favor a theory,' and if so, where does the slide go wrong?
