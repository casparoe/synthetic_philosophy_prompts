My reading group has spent the last year relitigating the socialist calculation debate, and two members in particular keep arguing past each other: one is an Austrian in the Mises/Hayek line, the other is a market socialist who thinks Lange basically settled it. They've finally agreed to do a proper adversarial collaboration, and I got voluntold to draft the document they could both sign. Write that statement. It should open with a numbered list of claims both camps accept — about what Mises' original challenge actually was, what Lange's Central Planning Board trial-and-error procedure shows and does not show, and where the formal models do and don't capture the live issue. Then give a disagreements table. Each row should state the residual disagreement neutrally, then in wording each side would accept for thier own position; identify the crux question underneath it; and give the concrete case or evidence both agree would bear on the crux, with each side's prediction written down in advance. The two obvious cruxes are (a) whether administered prices arrived at by trial and error can do the work of real factor markets when the relevant knowledge is tacit and dispersed rather than merely computational, and (b) whether manager incentives are a side-issue or the whole game. I also suspect one chunk of their argument has been verbal — they mean different things by "rational allocation" — so you should separate what's verbal from what's substantive, and don't let the agreed claims collapse into an empty truce either.
