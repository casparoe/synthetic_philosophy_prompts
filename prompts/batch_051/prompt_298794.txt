I keep ending up defending the view that "do what we mean but avoid side effects" can't be made precise without secretly puting in a full specification of what we value. The usual proposals all look to me like they relocate the problem instead of solving it: penalize deviation from a do-nothing baseline (who picks the baseline and why is that value-neutral?), preserve the agent's ability to achieve a broad distribution of possible goals (which goals, with what weights?), keep options open in some state representation (which variables count?), or train a side-effect classifier on human labels (then the specification is in the labels, not in the measure). I want to be pushed off this. What would count against the view, if I'm being maximally honest? I'd like it sorted by how decisive it would be: what formal result, working impact measure, or clean definition should genuinely move me; how much of that has arguably already happened with existing low-impact measures; which costs a defender of my position should concede rather than explain away; and a straight answer on whether I've insulated the view into unfalsifiability — and whether that's a defect for a claim like this or just what it is to say the concept is under-specified. Also say what my view predicts about the world that the optimistic view doesn't, so I'm not just winning by moving goalposts.
