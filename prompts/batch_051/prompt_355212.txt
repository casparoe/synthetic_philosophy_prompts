I work on a fraud-analysis team at a payments company, and a few times a week I have to revise a probability after evidence that is itself uncertain: a cardholder who may or may not be telling the truth, a merchant explanation that may be self-serving, a device match that could be coincidence. I keep seeing Jeffrey conditionalization described as the correct Bayesian rule for exactly this situation, where an observation does not establish its content with certainty but only makes that content more or less likely. I need that rule turned into a desk procedure I can actually run in about half an hour per case before my lead signs off on the revised estimate. Please set out the procedure: the quantities I need to estimate and in what order, the update in plain words rather than only notation, the checks that would show I have misapplied it, and the cases where I should fall back to ordinary conditionalization or to no formal update at all. Then run the procedure on two worked cases: (1) a cardholder disputes a charge and the call leaves me 65% confident she genuinely did not authorize it, with third-party fraud at a 3% base rate for that merchant class; (2) a merchant replies to a fraud inquiry and I leave the reply 50% confident its explanation is true, where I would not be surprised if the correct output is that my target probability barely moves. Be explicit about which steps are arithmetic performed on judgments rather than a replacement for judgment, and say what the procedure cannot settle for me.
