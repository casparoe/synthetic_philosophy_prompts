I spent most of my career in evolutionary biology and only got into philosophy and theology after I retired, and I keep tripping over an asymmetry I can't tell whether it's real or just a habit. A lot of Christians I read treat the idea that animals suffered for hundreds of millions of years before any human existed—predation, parasitism, starvation, disease—as a decisive objection to the doctrine that creation was originally good. The stock example is something like a parasitoid wasp larva eating a caterpillar from the inside over several days; put that before the Fall and it's supposed to be close to a refutation. But the very same people don't treat the very same event happening in my garden last summer as a decisive objection to God's goodness now. They have some account of a fallen creation, or human sin having cosmic consequences, and they move on. So the asymmetry is: pre-Fall animal suffering is treated as a problem that must be explained away or the doctrine collapses, while post-Fall animal suffering of the same kind is treated as theologically manageable. I want to know whether anything actually justifies drawing the line at the Fall, or whether the line is doing more work then the theology can support. If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against the case where the two events are identical except for their date.
