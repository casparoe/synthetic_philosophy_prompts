I got into a long argument with a friend about whether probability zero means impossible. He held the line that a rational person can't assign zero probability to something that could actually happen — zero means you'd take any bet against it, and then if it happens you've been incoherent, not just unlucky. I said that can't be right: take tomorrow's high temperature, model it with any continuous distribution, and every exact reading (71.3628114°F, or whatever it ends up being) had probability zero beforehand, yet one of them was always possible and one of them actually happens. He said that just shows the continuous model is an idealization and not what you really believe. We went in circles. So what I actually want to know: for an ideally rational person, does probability zero coincide with 'impossible,' or are possibility and probability just different notions that overlap imperfectly? And if they're different, what should 'possibly' mean for someone who thinks in probabilities — is it 'not assigned zero,' 'not known to be false,' or something else entirely? I get the feeling theres a confusion in how we set the question up, but I can't put my finger on it.
