And it reminded me of something I read in Franklin's autobiography forever ago. He basically built a paper version of this: a list of thirteen virtues (temperance, order, industry, stuff like that) and a little ruled notebook where he'd put a mark every day he slipped on one. He'd focus on one virtue a week so he'd cycle through all thirteen four times a year, and he says he was actually shocked to find himself fuller of faults than he'd imagined. But here's the part I keep chewing on: he eventually stopped trying to get the whole ax head polished. He tells this story about a smith and a guy turning the grindstone who quits and says he likes a speckled ax better, and Franklin seems to walk away thinking a perfectly virtuous person would maybe be envied and hated, so a good-natured person ought to keep a few faults on purpose, almost as a favor to the people around him.

So I keep wondering what he'd make of the modern version of his project. On one hand, habit trackers are literally his notebook with a database under it, so maybe he'd be all over them. On the other, the whole quantified-self streak thing treats every missed checkmark like a bug, and it's all about never breaking the chain — which sounds exactly like the moral-perfection thing he gave up.
