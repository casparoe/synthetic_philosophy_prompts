ok so I keep coming back to this thing i read about forbidden colors — like reddish green, which you supposedly cant see normally because red and green are opponent processes, so they cancel each other out. Apparently some old experiment got people to see reddish green by showing one eye a red field and the other eye a green field, and using an eye tracker to keep the border between them frozen on the retina so the edge never moved. But I don't know if I believe those people, cuz maybe they just saw something weird and didnt have a word for it.

Now imagine a lab does a version with a brain implant that can stimulate the color areas of visual cortex in a pattern that should force the red and green opponent cells to be active at the same time. The lab says subjects report a color that is reddish and greenish at the same time, not a brownish mix.

the conceptual claim I want to test is: those subjects are having a genuinely new color experience, not just (a) making weird reports because the experimenter expects them to, (b) having some non-color visual change they cant describe, or (c) actually seeing a mixture and labeling it badly.

How would you test that? I want the actual experimental setup, what result would count for the new-experience reading, what result would count for the just-reports/mislabeling reading, and what the test could not settle even in principle. Like, can you ever get outside someone else's color experience with any measurement, or does a "new color" just mean "a new verbal report"? Also, is the eye-tracker version and the brain-implant version testing the same claim, or does the implant change what would count as evidence?
