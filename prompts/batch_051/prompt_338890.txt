Some people draw the line between moral agents and moral instruments at causal efficacy: a system that emits morally relevant judgments but is causally unable to act on them is, on their view, not a moral agent at all — it is a moral calculator or oracle, no matter how accurate or sensitive its judgments are. Others hold that the heart of moral agency is the formation, maintenance, and revision of judgments in response to reasons, and that the causal power to intervene is an accidental addition rather than a requirement. Call the first position the efficacy view and the second the judgment view.

The dispute can look merely verbal, but the two views should come apart once we ask how a system's status as an agent behaves as its causal powers are added or removed.
