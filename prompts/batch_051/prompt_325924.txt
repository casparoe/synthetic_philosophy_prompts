The common prior assumption, standard in Bayesian epistemology and economics, holds that ideally rational agents can be represented as sharing a single prior probability function: any difference in their current credences must be attributable to a difference in their information, and two such agents with exactly the same total evidence could not rationally disagree. Give the reasoning behind that figure by assigning explicit probabilities to the sub-questions your verdict depends on: for example, whether rationality permits or forbids multiple rational priors (permissivism versus uniqueness at the prior stage), whether a defensible rational prior can incorporate mere taste or temperament, whether the assumption depends on contentious views about which kinds of evidence are shareable in principle, and whether it is best read as an empirical claim or as a normative modeling convention.
