I'm a journalist on the ideas desk of a science magazine, which in practice means I get handed anything with the word "evidence" in it, so now I'm doing a piece on the old Paul-versus-James fight over whether a person is justified by faith or by works. While reporting, I sat in on a reading group where two people have been argueing about this for months. One is a hard Pauline (justification is by faith; works are at most evidence and never contribute to justification). The other is a hard Jamesian (faith is completed by works in a way that belongs to justification itself; a faith without works is dead, not just fruitless). They've agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration, and I want to publish the document they should have produced rather than the actual shouting.

They've agreed to anchor the whole thing in a passage from the Epistle of James, Authorised (King James) Version, instead of the usual proof texts:

"Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God."

(They know the very next sentence is the famous punchline, but they've agreed not to start there because it usually ends the conversation.)

Please draft their joint statement. I'd like: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about this passage, about Abraham, and about what the two sides' disagreement is over, phrased so neither side flinches at the wording; (2) a disagreements table, each row a single precise question — for example whether "justified" in verse 21 can mean "shown to be in the right" rather than "made righteous", what "wrought with" and "made perfect" in verse 22 actually assert about works, and why James says the Genesis verse was "fulfilled" at the offering of Isaac when the original verse was spoken years earlier — with each side's answer in wording that side would sign; (3) the cruxes, the sub-questions that would actually move at least one side if settled; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test or case both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I suspect one crux is whether "justify" in James can bear the sense "vindicate or demonstrate" rather than "constitute righteous", and another is whether the real action is over Abraham at Genesis 15 or Abraham at Genesis 22. Dont paper over the genuine disagreements; if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say exactly which part and why. British English spelling throughout, please.
