Construct a thought experiment in which three candidate rules for combining consciousness and happiness within a single conscious experience deliver this pattern of disagreement: the additive rule (value = consciousness + happiness) and the lexical rule (consciousness is lexically prior, so any gain in consciousness outweighs any gain in happiness) both rank the same experience higher, while the multiplicative rule (value = consciousness × happiness) ranks the other higher. Assume, as these rules do, that each dimension can be represented by a positive real number on a shared cardinal scale with a meaningful zero, so that adding and multiplying across dimensions is well defined.

Constraints. The two experiences must be ordinary human experiences — no exotic beings, no infinite populations, no uploading, no comatose or dreamless states — and both must be clearly positive on both dimensions. The verdicts must come from the way the dimensions trade off, not from pushing either dimension near zero or to some edge at which one formula trivially dominates. Concretely: the experience favored by the additive and lexical rules should be the more conscious one and only mildly happy, while the other should be noticeably happier but only slightly less conscious; the happiness gap must be smaller than the consciousness gap, so the additive rule still favors the more conscious experience, yet large enough relative to the levels that the product favors the better-balanced experience. State the scenario in one paragraph of ordinary psychological description, with no numbers.

After the scenario, do the following in order. (1) Walk through each rule and show how it maps the two experiences onto a verdict, explaining in words why the additive and lexical rules agree while the multiplicative rule dissents. (2) Identify the structural feature doing the work — the precise relationship between the consciousness gap and the happiness gap that the multiplicative rule exploits. (3) Give the strongest reply available to a defender of the multiplicative rule: what property of the better-balanced experience does the product representation capture that the other two rules miss? (4) Specify one change to the case that would make all three rules agree, and explain why removing that feature dissolves the disagreement.
