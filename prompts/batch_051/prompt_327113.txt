As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I keep encountering an unspoken asymmetry in arguments about civilizational collapse. The Late Bronze Age is treated as a paradigmatic collapse: palace administration gone, Greek writing gone for centuries. The Classic Maya are routinely said to have collapsed, even though Maya communities and towns persisted and adapted. The contested Easter Island story still circulates as a parable of self-destruction. But when a modern city loses well over half its population and its former economic basis — Detroit after 1950 is the obvious case — writers reach for decline, shrinkage, deindustrialization, transition, almost never collapse. State this asymmetry in its strongest, most precise form, identify the best principle that could justify treating the two kinds of case differently, and test it against the examples people actually cite. Give an honest verdict: should we keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or give it up?
