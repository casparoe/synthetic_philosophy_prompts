- there are genuine moral dilemmas: situations where you have two all-things-considered obligations, you can't satisfy both, and no matter what you do you'll have violated a real obligation.
- ought implies can: nobody is obligated to do something that's impossible for them.
- if you ought to do A and you ought to do B, then you ought to do A-and-B (i've seen this called agglomeration).
- morality should be action-guiding: for any real choice, a moral theory should tell the agent what to do, or at least leave at least one option as permissible.
- being in a genuine dilemma and picking one horn doesn't mean you chose wrong, but you should still feel something like deep regret or agent-regret.
- moral realism is true: there are objective moral facts about what's required and what's right.
