I'm a science and ideas journalist, and I've been reading around on whether introspection is reliable enough to be treated as a scientific method. I keep coming back to this passage from William James, The Principles of Psychology (1890), near the start of the chapter on the stream of thought:

“No one ever had a simple sensation by itself. Consciousness, from our natal day, is of a teeming multiplicity of objects and relations, and what we call simple sensations are results of discriminative attention, pushed often to a very high degree. It is astonishing what havoc is wrought in psychology by admitting at the outset apparently innocent suppositions, that nevertheless contain a flaw. The bad consequences develop themselves later on, and are irremediable, being woven through the whole texture of the work. The notion that sensations, being the simplest things, are the first things to take up in psychology is one of these suppositions. The only thing which psychology has a right to postulate at the outset is the fact of thinking itself, and that must first be taken up and analyzed. If sensations then prove to be amongst the elements of the thinking, we shall be no worse off as respects them than if we had taken them for granted at the start.”

I get the general polemic, that psychology shouldn't start by treating sensations as simple building blocks, but I'm stuck on how deep the claim goes. Is he saying that the very idea of a "simple sensation" is a retroactive construction, and that introspection never actually catches one in real time? If so, that seems like it would undercut a lot of what later introspectionist labs tried to do with trained observers. But then James still introspects all over the book, so maybe he's making a narrower point about the order of topics. Can you unpack what he means by "simple sensations are results of discriminative attention," and what that implies for the reliability of introspective reports? Also, I can't tell whether "no one ever had a simple sensation by itself" is an empirical claim about the contents of consciousness or a conceptual point about what psychology is entitled to posit at the start. Or maybe both?
