I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about who should get to vote. The hook is that several places near me are arguing in three different directions at once: one wants to let 16-year-olds vote in school-board races, another wants to give permanent non-citizen residents a vote in municipal elections, and a third keeps flirting with compulsory voting. Every time I write about one of these, I get letters insisting that some totally different rule is obviously right, and I suspect people are sorting themselves by tribal labels rather than by what they would actually say about cases. So I want to run a "which theory of the franchise do you actually hold?" quiz alongside the piece.

Could you build me that quiz? Aim for eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case: a 16-year-old who works and pays payroll tax, a non-citizen parent with kids in the local schools and a decade of residency, a citizen living abroad for fifteen years, an adult with a severe cognitive disability, a 17-year-old who can pass the same civics test we give naturalizing citizens, a citizen who can't name any of the candidates and has no interest in learning, that sort of thing. Give each question two or three answer options in ordinary language, none of which should just be a position label. The quiz should sort people by the distinctions that actually do the work here: whether what matters is being affected by a decision, being subject to the laws, being competent to choose, or holding the civic role of citizen. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: an all-affected-interests inclusion view, a stakeholder/residency view, a civic-duty view that treats voting as an obligation (the one that likes compulsory voting), a liberal-citizen-autonomy view, and a competence view — plus any combinations of those four distinctions that are logically available but that nobody seems to defend. Where a cell is empty, tell me briefly whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the quiz cant really seperate.

Please format it in four sections, in this order: "The quiz" (roughly 700–900 words), "Scoring key" (roughly 200–300 words, with a simple way for a reader to tally their own answers), "The positions" (roughly 200–300 words, each position named plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment), and "Caveats" (roughly 100–200 words on what the instrument cannot distinguish). Write in American English, and don't lean on specific candidates or current laws — I want the quiz to travel.
