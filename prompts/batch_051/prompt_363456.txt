An adversarial collaboration is being conducted between two readers of Marx on automation. One reading — call it the domination reading — holds that Marx treats machinery under capitalism primarily as an instrument of class power: a means of extracting surplus-value, displacing workers, and disciplining those who remain. The other reading — call it the productive-forces reading — holds that Marx sees the same machinery as building the material preconditions for a society of shortened, lightened toil, and that his criticisms target only the capitalistic application of that machinery, not machinery as such. In Capital, Volume One, Chapter 15 ("Machinery and Modern Industry"), Marx writes:

"That is, however, by no means the aim of the capitalistic application of machinery. Like every other increase in the productiveness of labour, machinery is intended to cheapen commodities, and, by shortening that portion of the working-day, in which the labourer works for himself, to lengthen the other portion that he gives, without an equivalent, to the capitalist. In short, it is a means for producing surplus-value."

Draft the joint statement these two readers could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should specifically take up whether the phrase "the capitalistic application of machinery" presupposes that machinery can in principle have a non-capitalistic application, and whether the closing sentence is a definition of machinery or only a claim about its function under capitalist relations. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims about this passage and about Marx's broader view that both sides can accept, stated without evasion; (2) a disagreements table giving each side's position in wording its own side would accept; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions, textual or conceptual, such that settling them would move at least one side — with each side's prediction about how each crux would turn out recorded in advance. If some of their dispute looks merely verbal, identify which part and say why.
