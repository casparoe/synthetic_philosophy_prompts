I've got this rule about big-picture history: I won't take any claim that history has cycles or laws seriously unless the person can state a falsifiable prediction in advance, with a date range. Retrodictions — pointing at Rome and the Ming and saying "see, the secular cycle fits" — count for almost nothing with me, because with enough past data points you can fit any curve. A friend who's been reading Turchin says this holds history to a standard that would have killed physics too, and that careful retrodiction is real evidence when you can't run experiments. Is my rule a sane response to the overfitting problem, or am I just banning the only kind of evidence macrohistory can have? If it's too strict, what's the defensible version?
