You're a philosopher of social science who knows Lewis on convention and also experimental economics. Think out loud a little before committing to a design.

A friend insists that the unwritten rule in her office—nobody takes the last cup of coffee without starting a fresh pot—is a convention in Lewis's sense: everyone prefers to comply conditional on expecting everyone else to comply, and a different regularity (say, whoever empties the pot brews the next one) would have served just as well. I dont see how to distinguish that from a sheer habit that would survive any change in expectations, or a norm enforced by third-party side-eye. Take the coffee-pot case and give me a concrete test that sorts the three readings. What would you manipulate or observe, what pattern would favor each reading, and how would you operationalize 'an alternative regularity would have served just as well' without merely assuming it? Then tell me what a clean convention result would still leave open. What worries me most is common knowledge: the theory seems to require it, but it looks unobservable except through the very conduct it's meant to explain.
