I'm a novelist, and for a book where the same person splits across several alternate timelines I made the mistake of actually reading some metaphysics instead of just winging it. Now I'm stuck on this old fight between counterpart theory and transworld identity, and from the outside it looks completely frozen. Lewis's side says no individual exists in more than one world: individuals are world-bound, and when we say she might have been a pianist, we're really saying that a counterpart of her -- someone in another world who resembles her in the right context-relative ways but is not literally her -- is a pianist. The other side (Kripke, Plantinga-ish) says that's changing the subject: the very same individual exists in different possible worlds with different properties, with identity across worlds either stipulated or carried by something like a haecceity. If you tell Humphrey he might have won only in the sense that some other guy resembling him won, Humphrey doesn't care. Lewis shrugs and says that other guy is exactly what the modal claim is about, because what matters for de re modality is similarity, not strict identity across worlds.

What's weird to me is that both sides seem able to absorb the other side's puzzle cases by fiddling with the counterpart relation or the notion of a world, neither side moves, and the underlying modal logic mostly stays put. So I don't want a verdict on who's right. I want a diagnosis of the dissagreement itself. Is it a verbal dispute about the word identity? A clash of starting intuitions about what de re modal talk has to explain? Is the real fight Lewis's concrete modal realism versus abstract or ersatz worlds, with the counterpart machinery just downstream? Are they answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress -- a formal translation result, a sharper distinction, a psychological or linguistic finding, or just dropping the question? Or is this the kind of debate where no progress should be expected, and why?
