A friend and I argued late into the night last week about a policy I follow in my own thinking. When I discover that someone I would have judged, before the disagreement, to be at least my epistemic peer on a question where we have both seen roughly the same evidence, I move my credence exactly one third of the way toward theirs—no more, no less—and then treat the question as closed unless genuinely new arguments or evidence turn up. My friend insists that this cannot be principled: once I have conceded that the other person is my peer, there is no non-arbitrary stopping point short of full equal weight, and my one-third is just my antecedent confidence in myself sneaking back in under another name; the closing clause, she says, is motivated reasoning dressed up as intellectual stability. I resist that because I think full equal weight would make my beliefs swing too wildly on the say-so of one person, while refusing to move at all seems indefensibly arrogant. Is my one-third rule rationally defensible as a standing epistemic policy? If not, what would a defensible policy for updating on one-on-one peer disagreement look like, and under what conditions would a fractional move—rather than zero, half, or full capitulation—be justified?
