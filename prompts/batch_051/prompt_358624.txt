In my decision theory seminar we've been doing Newcomb-type cases and the TA mentioned evidential cooperation in large worlds. The setup: you're one of a huge number of agents across a very large universe who all face the same one-shot Prisoner's Dillema and reason similarly (shared genes, similar training), but none of you can causally affect the others. Your choice is still correlated with theirs: if you cooperate, that is evidence that a large number of your counterparts cooperate too. Evidential decision theory says cooperate, because the news-value of all those correlated cooperative acts outweighs the small causal cost of your own cooperation; causal decision theory says defect, because your act causes only the local payoff. The claim being pushed is that this is a genuine couterexample to causal decision theory.

What's your actual credence that the claim succeeds (precisely: that in such a case a rational agent should cooperate, so CDT is at least worse than EDT)? Assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions, say which one your final number is most sensitive to, and what new argument or empirical discovery would move you most.
