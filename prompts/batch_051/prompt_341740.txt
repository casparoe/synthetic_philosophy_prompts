I retired from biochemistry a few years ago and have got properly into the philosophy of mind, and I've ended up moderating a small online colloquium with a philosopher who defends a Millikan-style teleosemantics. Her rough position is that what a mental representation means is fixed by its proper function—what the producing mechanism was selected for—together with what the consuming systems downstream use it for. I don't want the usual restate-your-view session, so I'd be greatful if you could draft me eight or nine interview questions that actually push her. I'd like at least one question on each of the following, with a follow-up written against the reply she's most likely to give: (i) the old indeterminacy worry about the frog's snap reflex—whether the content is fly, small dark moving speck, or food—and what she says if the consumer seems to use it for several different things at once; (ii) the Swampman case, where a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me forms by chance with no selection history, and whether she'd really deny it has beliefs and desires; (iii) how her account gets misrepresentation off the ground rather than just saying the mechanism failed to function. And please include one or two questions about thoughts whose contents have no obvious selection history—say, quark spin or the square root of minus one—since that is the one my old chemistry colleagues always bring up to me. And no softballs, please, nothing like what drew you to philosophy.
