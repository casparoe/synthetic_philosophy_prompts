I am a software engineer whose work involves fitting models, selecting capacity, and deciding how much to regularize; I have no formal background in philosophy, only independent reading. I have noticed what looks like a structural parallel between how I choose models and how philosophers seem to choose ethical theories, and I want help deciding whether the parallel is deep or superficial.

In machine learning, we do not prefer simpler models because simplicity is intrinsically good. We prefer them because a high-capacity model can fit the training set nearly perfectly while generalizing poorly, whereas a simpler, properly regularized model tends to do better on data not seen during training. Simplicity functions as an instrumental bet about unseen cases, and the bet can be tested by holding out data.

In ethical theory, something similar appears to happen. A theory with one principle, or a small set of principles, is often favored even when a more complex theory — a long list of duties, exceptions, and case-specific qualifications — matches more of the considered moral judgments already on the table. The more complex theory has something like lower training error, yet the simpler theory is treated as better in virtue of its parsimony. That looks like choosing the regularized model over the unregularized one.

I would like you to do four things. First, state the mapping precisely: what in moral theorizing corresponds to the model, the training data, the hypothesis class, the regularization penalty, and held-out data. Second, say what would have to be true on the ethics side for the machine-learning rationale — generalization to unseen cases — to justify parsimony there; in particular, is there a counterpart to held-out data against which a moral theory can be tested, or does the analogy break at exactly that point? Third, name one thing the analogy would predict about moral theories that seems false or at least questionable, so I can see where the structures come apart. Fourth, give me your honest verdict: is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion?
