I'm in a grad seminar on medieval moral psychology and we've spent the last two weeks on Abelard, which has ended up colliding with something happening at my job. I work as a pharmacy tech, and a few weeks ago I filled a prescription incorrectly. It got caught before it reached the patient, nobody was hurt, but I havent been able to settle how much I'm actually at fault, partly because I can't tell whether the fact that nothing bad happened should lower my guilt. The reading keeps insisting that on Abelard's view the sin is in the consent and not in the act, but I've realized that what I actually believe is a whole cluster of things that borrow from that idea without sitting cleanly together with it. I'd rather have the whole set audited than defend any one of them.

Here are the commitments as honestly as I can state them:

a) If I fully resolve to do something wrong and then get stopped by outside circumstances, I'm just as guilty as if I'd done it; carrying it out adds nothing to the guilt.
b) Conversely, if I cause harm with no prior consent — by reflex, pure accident, or innocent ignorance — I haven't sinned.
c) But I also can't shake the thought that being genuinely careless is blameworthy, even when there was no moment where I consented to harm. That I never noticed the error is exactly what's wrong, not an excuse.
d) The stray thoughts that show up in my head unwanted aren't sins and aren't even fully actions of mine; only saying yes to them is.
e) Yet I think going through with a harmful act does something to your character — it makes the next one easier and hardens something — and that this matters morally, not just as a practical prediction.
f) I hold that someone who deliberately gets drunk knowing he'll drive is fully blameworthy for the harm he causes while drunk, even though the harm itself wasn't consented to at the moment.
g) And I don't think consequences change the degree of guilt: intended harm that fails and intended harm that succeeds are equal, even though only one leaves someone injured.
h) Finally, I think there's a distinct blameworthiness in refusing to look at your own motives — persistently avoiding seeing what you're up to is itself culpable, not just for the individual acts it hides.

What I want is a consistency check, not a survey. Go through these and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other, rank the tensions by how severe they are, and say which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and why. For each real tension, tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be. Then end with the single revision you'd make if you could only make one. Please take a definite position on each pair rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
