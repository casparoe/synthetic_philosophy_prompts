I am a novelist by trade and currently a fellow at a university history department, where I have been asked to give the closing lecture at a small workshop called Narrative and Historical Explanation. The lecture is my chance to press a claim I have come to believe while researching a novel about the early modern Mediterranean grain trade, and I would like you to build the argument for me so that I can cannibalize it for the talk. The claim is this: Braudel's tripartite scheme—geographical time, the slow rhythm of social and economic conjunctures, and the quick agitation of events—does not pick out three genuinely distinct causal levels. The apparent difference in kind between a slow structure and a dated event is an artifact of sampling resolution. Sample the causal field coarsely, over centuries, and you see what Braudel called the longue durée; sample it finely, over days, and you see the merchant falsifying a manifest or the bishop delaying a ship. But the coarse view is not a deeper cause that governs the fine view: the rough average is composed of, and sustained by, the very episodes the scheme treats as mere surface disturbance. My thesis, then, is that the longue durée is a deliberate choice of narrative and analytical scale, not a discovery that geography and climate are causally more fundamental than events and individuals.

For the question period I expect resistance from a historian friend who works on the Mediterranean grain trade and considers herself event-centered; she will argue that this turns every constraint into a statistical illusion and leaves no ground for saying that a famine, a tariff regime, or a climatic regime is genuinely hard for any individual to move. Please write the argument in the following named sections, in this order: Thesis stated precisely (about 150 words); The main argument in three linked steps (about 450 words); The strongest objection, put in its most damaging form (about 200 words); Reply, including what the thesis should concede and what it should refuse to concede (about 250 words); Where the case is softest (about 100 words). Do not include footnotes, a bibliography, or citations, and do not build the case on authority; I want the reasoning itself.
