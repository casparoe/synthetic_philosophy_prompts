In my course on the representation of war in popular media, we keep circling around a distinction between games that glorify war and games that treat war seriously, but the line between them keeps collapsing under actual examples. The working criteria we have settled on are these: (I) aestheticization, whether the presentation makes violence exhilarating or beautiful rather than dreadful; (II) moral framing, whether the player's side is depicted as unambiguously justified; (III) erasure, whether the game omits civilian harm, trauma, and the slower social damage that follows combat; (IV) reward structure, whether the mechanics visibly reward killing with points, progress, or prestige; and (V) reflective distance, whether the game creates moments of stillness in which the player can step back from the action.

Here is the case that now feels genuinely undecidable under those criteria. Imagine a tactical shooter set in an unnamed civil war. You play a logistics officer rather than a frontline soldier, and much of the game consists of planning supply routes, reading terrain, and moving convoys under time pressure. The movement and planning systems are tight and sincerely pleasurable: players can optimize routes, improve their times, and chase an efficiency score, but there is no body count, no kill-streak reward, and no mechanical payoff for violence. The casualties your side inflicts are mostly offscreen or registered through clipped radio chatter. However, the operation you have been optimizing throughout the game is revealed, in a long late-game scene without mission goals or score, to have enabled a massacre in a village. The scene is quiet, unflinching, and the game does not comment on it afterward. The story never says your side is righteous, yet the play itself continually gives you reasons to feel clever while serving that side.

Does this game glorify war, treat it seriously, both, or neither? Go through the five criteria honestly; where a criterion gives no clear verdict, say whether that is because the criterion is inadequate or because the case is genuinely ambiguous; and then say whether the criteria need revising in light of the case.
