I hold the view that backward induction is the uniquely correct standard of rational play for finite extensive-form games of perfect information, and that the centipede game is best understood not as a paradox but as a case in which the correct answer is psychologically unwelcome. In the standard centipede, two players alternate between taking a growing pot and passing it on; taking gives the taker the larger share and ends the game, while a path of mutual passing reaches a terminal node whose payoffs are better for both than the backward-induction outcome. I accept the implication that the first player should take at the first node, and I regard the intuitive pull of continuing as a symptom of how hard it is to carry iterated best-response reasoning all the way down the tree, rather than as evidence against the principle.

I want a systematic map of what would count against this view. Please give me the candidate defeaters in order from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one specify exactly what it threatens: backward induction as a normative solution concept; the assumptions usually used to derive it—common knowledge of rationality, common knowledge of the game and its payoffs, self-interested preferences, no pre-play communication or renegotiation—or only its adequacy as a descriptive model of actual experimental subjects. Say which of these a committed defender should concede counts against the view and how they would absorb the blow, and which they would dismiss as aimed at a different claim. Pay particular attention to the status of off-path nodes: backward induction requires a player at an unreached node to have determinate conditional moves, even though the preceding player's supposed rationality should make that node impossible, and several of the most interesting escape routes turn on whether those counterfactuals are well-defined. Then give an honest assessment of whether the position has been progressively insulated—for instance by retreating from a claim about what actual players do to a claim about what ideal players with common knowledge of rationality would do—and whether that insulation is a problem or a legitimate scoping of a normative theory. Which of the derivation's assumptions are live escape routes rather than merely logical possibilities? Close by identifying the single sharpest live challenge to the view, even if you judge that the view survives it.
