I've been down a rabbit hole about the textbook wars and I keep hitting the same confusion. On the American side you have the Texas board of education voting line by line on what a textbook has to say about slavery or the Alamo, the 1619 Project saying the countrys real founding should be dated to 1619 rather than 1776, and the 1776 Commission telling teachers that that kind of history corrodes patriotism. On the Japan–Korea side you have a version of the same thing: Korea protests every time Japan's education ministry approves a textbook that softens or skips the comfort women and forced labor, and Japanese officials say its just neutral screening standards. Everyone in these fights talks as if textbooks are supposed to be accurate and objective, but everyone also acts as if a nation's survival depends on which story the kids get.
