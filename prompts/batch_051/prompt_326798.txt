I'm a software engineer, and I think most naturally in the vocabulary of interfaces, implementations, composition versus inheritance, base classes, role objects, and runtime binding. Last night I spent hours arguing with a friend about Confucian role ethics. His position was roughly this: a person is not an autonomous core self who then takes on roles such as daughter, teacher, or friend; the roles constitute the person, and the idea of a self standing behind or prior to them is a mistake. I kept pressing him with the objection that seemed decisive to me — what is the base object that all these role-interfaces attach to? — and he replied that the question itself is the error rather than something to be answered. Please restate the Confucian role-ethics position in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Which of its claims map cleanly onto interface-driven design, composition over inheritance, or the absence of a shared base class, and which claims can only be expressed with distortion? Then give me a residue report: what in the Confucian view has no counterpart in software-architecture vocabulary at all — for example, ritual propriety, filiality, shame, the body, or the fact that one is born into roles rather than assigned them. I want to know whether my conviction that there must be a base object is a genuine architectural requirement that the role ethicist needs to answer, or merely an artifact of my framework that a Confucian can legitimately refuse.
