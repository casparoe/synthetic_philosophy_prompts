I'm a philosophy of psychology PhD student and I'm a bit stuck on this passage from Spinoza's *Ethics*, Part III, Definition III:

"By emotion I mean the modifications of the body, whereby the active power of the said body is increased or diminished, aided or constrained, and also the ideas of such modifications."

And the note straight after it: "If we can be the adequate cause of any of these modifications, I then call the emotion an activity, otherwise I call it a passion, or state wherein the mind is passive."

I can see why people read this as a feeling theory, but the second clause about the ideas of the modifications makes me think he wants both body and mind in the emotion itself, and the activity/passion note makes me think he doesn't even treat all emotions as straightforward inner feelings. So is the emotion here the bodily modification, the idea/perception of it, or a single thing that is somehow both? And would Spinoza even fit the contemporary judgment/perception/feeling taxonomy, or is he doing something else entirely? I'd be greatful for a close reading. Please answer in British English.
