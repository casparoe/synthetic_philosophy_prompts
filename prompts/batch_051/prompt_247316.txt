You are an expert in moral psychology and experimental design. I direct a behavioral research group inside a game studio, and I need you to design a study.

We are building a game in which players can help or harm non-player characters, and the studio is debating how to describe those choices publicly. The lead designer defends a descriptive claim: the concern players feel for non-player characters is largely self-regarding, rooted in the player's own self-image, absorption in the role, or attachment to something they own and control, rather than in other-directed moral concern for the character. I want to bring evidence to bear on that claim before the studio commits to it.

You may use playtest telemetry, post-play surveys, and controlled experiments with a few hundred recruited players and a small budget for real incentives. Design the best test you can of the claim that player concern for non-player characters is self-regarding rather than character-directed. Say exactly what pattern of results would count in favor of the self-regarding account, what pattern would count in favor of the character-directed account, and then say what the test could not settle even in principle, including the residual question of whether such characters actually matter morally.

Think carefully and start by brainstorming at least three candidate operationalizations before you commit; do not include that brainstorming in your final answer. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The five items should cover: the operationalized version of the claim, the concrete test design, the result that favors the self-regarding account, the result that favors the character-directed account, and what the test cannot settle.
