I've spent the last month wading through the Sleeping Beauty literature, and the thing that actually interests me now isn't whether the answer is 1/2 or 1/3 but why the debate seems so completely frozen. The setup is familiar: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped; Beauty is woken on Monday; if the coin landed tails she's woken again on Tuesday after a drug wipes her memory of Monday; she knows all this in advance. Halfers say her credence in heads when she wakes should be 1/2, since she learns nothing new about the coin. Thirders say 1/3, since tails produces twice as many wakenings and, from inside the experiment, a randomly selected waking is twice as likely to be a tails-waking. What bugs me is that each side seems to have a learned a stable response to every reply the other side makes. Halfers will often grant that Beauty should accept 2:1 betting odds on tails, but deny that this makes her credence 1/3, because tails gives her two chances to place the same bet; thirders reply that this is just redefining credence to avoid the intuition. So both camps agree on every physical fact about the setup and still end up with different numbers, and neither has moved in twenty-odd years.

My working theory is that this is at least partly a verbal or conceptual dispute: "credence" is being asked to do two jobs that come apart in self-locating cases—track the objective chance of the coin and guide action in a setting where the number of times you act is correlated with the outcome—and maybe there isn't a single quantity that does both. But it could also be a substantive disagreement about whether waking with amnesia is evidence, or about whether self-locating beliefs need different formal rules than ordinary ones. I'd like a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not one more argument for a side. What kind of disagreement is this really—verbal, evidential, about which background principles apply, about what "credence" means, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a theorem, an agreed constraint, a reformulation—or should we expect the impasse to persist, and why?
