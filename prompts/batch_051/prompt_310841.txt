In a seminar on the timing of conscious experience, we worked through the color phi phenomenon: two brief flashes of different colors at separate locations are reported by subjects as a single moving light that changes color midway along its path. The standard treatments distinguish two ways the brain might handle the fact that the color change seems to be registered before the second flash: an Orwellian revision, on which the subject consciously experiences the unedited sequence and memory is rewritten after the second flash, and a Stalinesque revision, on which consciousness is delayed until the second flash so that only the edited sequence is ever experienced. I've tried to turn the dispute into a clean argument, and now the conclusion seems too strong to accept:

(1) The Orwellian and the Stalinesque accounts are empirically equivalent: they predict the same reports, the same behavior, and the same introspective judgments.
(2) If two accounts are empirically equivalent, no possible evidence can tell between them.
(3) If no possible evidence can tell between two accounts, then there is no fact of the matter about which account is correct.
(4) Therefore, in the color phi case, there is no fact of the matter about whether the subject consciously experienced the motion before the second flash or only had it added to experience after the second flash.
(5) If there is no such fact in this case, then by parity there is no fact of the matter about whether any recollected episode was consciously experienced at the time or is being confabulated in memory now.
(6) But that is absurd: there is a manifest difference between having experienced an event and merely remembering an experience one never had.

So either one of the premises must be rejected or the parity step from (4) through (5) to that general claim must be blocked. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I most want to keep (6), but I cannot see how to keep it without admitting a difference that no evidence could ever detect. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
