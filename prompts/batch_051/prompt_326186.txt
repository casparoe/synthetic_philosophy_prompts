I have recently joined the nomination committee of a large listed company, and we are revising both board-recruitment and graduate-hiring policy. I have found that every disagreement about whether a proposal is acceptable eventually turns on whether it is a quota, and nobody in the room can say what that word picks out. I would like a working definition that does the job.

Give me a definition of quota, in the context of hiring and admissions, and test it against the following cases: (a) a company required by law to have at least 40 per cent women on its board, on pain of fines or removal from the register; (b) an Indian university that reserves a fixed number of places for candidates in a particular caste category, for which only those candidates compete; (c) a US university that treats an applicant's race as one plus factor within holistic review, with no fixed number of offers by group and no separate competition; (d) a top-ten-per-cent plan under which every student in the top decile of their secondary school is guaranteed a place; (e) an employer that sets a 50/50 gender goal for senior hires by 2030 but imposes no sanction and allows no preference in any individual decision; (f) an employer that applies a lower qualifying-test cutoff for one group but then hires strictly on merit above that cutoff; (g) a rule requiring at least one shortlisted candidate for each senior post to be from an underrepresented group, with no constraint on the final choice; (h) a legacy preference for children of alumni.

I regard (a) and (b) as quotas, (d) and (h) as not quotas, and I have no settled view on (c), (e), (f) and (g); I suspect (g) is where ordinary usage pulls apart. Please set out your definition, run it through all eight cases, identify the classification you are least willing to defend, and then either revise the definition or defend that verdict.

Please structure your reply in sections: Working definition (one paragraph); Application to the cases (a sentence or two on each); Where the definition strains (one paragraph); Revised definition or defence (one paragraph).
