I have been thinking about alphabets and what they actually commit a community to, and I have arrived at a formulation I want checked. I call a working alphabet a frozen theory of a language's sounds. By this I mean that an alphabet does not record sound itself; it records a settled decision about which differences in sound are assigned distinct letters and which are treated as variations of one letter, and the script then presents that decision as if it were simply the obvious way to write the language. The strong version of my claim is that no alphabet is purely phonemic: even the most systematic spelling has to take positions on questions the speech stream cannot settle by itself, such as whether an affricate is one sound or two, or whether a diphthong is a single unit or a sequence. I want an honest assessment: whether the basic idea is coherent, whether it already exists under a standard name, whether the strong claim is true, and what the strongest objection to the idea as a whole is.
