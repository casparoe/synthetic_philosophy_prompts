I am a sixth-form student at a British school, studying moral philosophy. My teacher has asked me to design a practice examination paper for our unit on loyalty to groups versus impartiality, and I would like your help drafting one essay question, a model answer, and a marking scheme. The question should be suitable for an upper-level sixth-form class and should take about forty-five minutes. Please write the question so that a candidate cannot do well merely by reciting definitions of partiality, impartiality, loyalty, or bias, or by restating the textbook contrast between consequentialism and deontology. It should force a candidate to work through a concrete case in which someone must decide whether to favour their own family, friends, community, or group over outsiders, and to ask whether that favouring is best understood as a moral virtue, an acceptable partiality, or a disguised bias. The model answer should be around 650 words. The marking scheme should be out of 20 marks and should separate a high A essay from a low B essay by naming the specific moves a strong answer must make and the specific mistakes that should lose marks. In particular, a strong answer should not merely assert that private loyalties and public impartiality operate in separate spheres; it should press on whether that separation can be justified, and it should show awareness that some impartial standards may themselves be shaped by in-group perspectives. Please write everything in British English, and do not include any quotations unless they are invented for the exam question itself.
