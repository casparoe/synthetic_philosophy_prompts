For my upper-level seminar on philosophy of law I'm setting an essay that can't just be fobbed off with the usual liberal-communitarian summary. Here's the question.

Imagine a medium-sized country—call it the Republic of Kazi, population around 15 million, a mixed legal system left over from colonialism, mostly agrarian with two big coastal cities—adopts a new constitution in 2030 intended to operationalize an ubuntu conception of personhood. The key changes: legal personhood stops being automatic at birth. Instead it's graded and relational: a person gains full legal standing only once a registered local circle (kin group, neighbourhood, or work association) recognises them as meeting ongoing reciprocal obligations; minors and severely cognitively disabled people have minimal standing, but a recognised kin network can exercise standing on their behalf; someone with no recognised relationships retains only a thin basket of protections (physical security, emergency food and shelter) and cannot vote, enter contracts, hold title to land, or appear in court except as a petitioner; corporate personality is abolished, so enterprises act only through the overlapping circles of their workers, shareholders, and suppliers; and in any civil case the court must determine the parties' relational standing before it reaches the merits.

Predict, with reasoning, how this country looks after forty years. I don't want a description of what ubuntu "really teaches" or a story about invented families. I want the chain of mechanisms: which incentives and constraints actually bite, where people game the system or route around it, which areas of law get rewritten quickly and which quietly lapse, how courts and local circles handle the new discretion, and whether the system achieves it's communitarian aims or just creates new exclusions. Be explicit about which of your predictions are robust, which are guesses, and what would have to hold for you to be wrong.

Structure the response with these four titled sections, in this order:

1. Setup (about 100 words) — restate only the features of the scenario that will do the causal work.
2. Mechanisms (about 300 words) — the main institutional and social mechanisms you expect to drive the outcome.
3. Trajectory (about 400 words) — your argued prediction for law, family, economy, and politics at roughly 10, 25, and 40 years.
4. Confidence check (about 150 words) — the links you're most and least confident in, and the observations that would change your mind.
