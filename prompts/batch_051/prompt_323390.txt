I sit on the panel that selects and weights the global climate models for our national climate projections. The projections feed flood and water-supply planning, so the spread of the ensemble matters as much as its mean. Next week we have to rule on a new model, and I want to know what our own precedents commit us to. We have never used reduced weights, though a colleague is now proposing one.

Our track record is this:

- Monsoon-4: an independent model with an equilibrium climate sensitivity (ECS, the warming for doubled CO₂) of 5.6 K. It scored mediocre on daily precipitation variability but most of its core diagnostics were within our usual screening range. We included it at full weight over objections that it skewed the ensemble high.
- Breeze-2: shared most of its atmosphere component code and part of its ocean code with Breeze-1, which was already in the ensemble. Its hindcast skill was better than Breeze-1's. We excluded it as double-counting.
- Torrent: the best hindcast skill in the ensemble, but a review found that much of that skill came from compensating parameter errors. We kept it at equal weight and said we would not give it a skill bonus.
- Drift: a genuinely independent model with a novel ocean mixing scheme. It was below average on several tropical diagnostics but still above our screening threshold. We included it at full weight because its structural independence added diversity.
- Reef: failed the screening on several core fields and used a static slab ocean rather than a dynamical ocean. We excluded it unanimously.

The model before us now, Basalt, is a genuinely independent code lineage with no shared components with any current member, and it includes a novel treatment of ice-sheet–ocean coupling. Its physics are judged plausible on review. But it fails our usual hindcast screening on tropical precipitation by a wide margin, and its ECS is 5.3 K, near the top of the ensemble after Monsoon-4.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves: include Basalt at full weight, exclude it, or adopt the reduced-weight option we have never used? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog, and does it settle the question? If the record genuinely underdetermines the decision, spell out the principle we would be adopting with each choice, and describe the model case that would most cleanly tell which principle we have been running all along.
