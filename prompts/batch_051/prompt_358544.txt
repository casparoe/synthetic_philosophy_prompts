I'm collecting explanations of what's wrong with Moorean sentences like It's raining but I don't believe that it is raining. So far I have: (1) the Gricean/speech-act diagnosis, where asserting p either expresses or implicates that you believe p, so the whole utterance communicates a contradiction even though the sentence itself isn't one; (2) the norm-of-assertion diagnosis, where assertion is governed by a knowledge-type rule, so asserting p while denying belief is an open flouting of the norm; (3) the self-falsifying-belief diagnosis, where the proposition can be true, but a sincere belief in the whole conjunction would make the second conjunct false, since believing the conjunction requires believing the first; and (4) the transparency diagnosis, where a present-tense I believe that p isn't a report on an inner state but just takes the same stance that asserting p takes. These all strike me as standard. I want another diagnosis that is genuinly different from these four and not a rewording, special case, or combination of them. Give it, say exactly how it differs, and then tell me honestly whether it's any good.
