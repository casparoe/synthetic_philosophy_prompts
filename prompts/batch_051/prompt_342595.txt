I am the lead case officer in the secretariat of a national body that maintains the list of conditions classified as diseases for the purposes of publicly funded treatment. Each week our officers prepare recommendations on two or three proposed conditions, a committee of eight then votes, and the chair signs off on the final classification. The committee is dissatisfied with its own decisions: the same condition is treated one way one year and another way the next depending on which experts present, and no one can say where the factual questions end and the value questions begin. The stakes run in both directions—a refusal to classify removes access to funded care, while classifying a condition commits public money and may medicalise ordinary variation. Our academic advisers inform us that the philosophy of medicine contains roughly three positions on this: naturalism, on which disease is a matter of value-free biological dysfunction; normativism, on which values are ineliminable in calling something a disease; and hybrid views, on which a condition is a disease only when there is both a dysfunction and a judgement that the dysfunction is harmful. The committee has asked me to produce a single written decision procedure that our officers can run on each proposal before the meeting.

Please write that procedure. It must be usable by people with no philosophical training, in under an hour, and without access to journal subscriptions. It should ask its questions in a fixed order; say what counts as a sufficient answer to each; mark where the procedure stops with a no; mark where it must hand the decision to the committee because the question is one of values rather than facts; and identify the questions that look factual but are actually value-laden. It should be honest about which steps are judgement calls dressed up as procedure. Then run the procedure on three proposals from our current queue: (a) classifying mild age-related cognitive decline, in a person without dementia who is functioning normally, as a disease; (b) classifying obesity as a disease; and (c) classifying infertility as a disease. Ensure that at least one worked case reaches a conclusion the committee is likely to resist, and say plainly what the procedure cannot decide. Please write the entire answer in British English, including spelling and punctuation.
