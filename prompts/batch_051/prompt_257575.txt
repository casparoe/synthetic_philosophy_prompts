Psychology and biomedicine will not publish a finding unless it reaches statistical significance — conventionally p < .05 — and the same fields increasingly require that hypotheses and analyses be pre-registered before data are collected. Yet those journals still publish unregistered "exploratory" analyses, and a direct replication is typically judged by whether the second study also clears p < .05, not by how much evidence the two studies provide taken together. What view of statistical evidence does this patchwork of practices presuppose? Is the field implicitly committed to a frequentist, error-control picture of inference, or can the practices be given a Bayesian updating rationale as well? Which parts of the practice strictly presuppose that view and which merely fit well with it — and is there a genuine inconsistency here that the fields should acknowledge rather than paper over?
