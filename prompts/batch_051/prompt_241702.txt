I’m reading G. E. Moore’s Principia Ethica for a class, and the preface has this passage:

“It appears to me that in Ethics, as in all other philosophical studies, the difficulties and disagreements, of which its history is full, are mainly due to a very simple cause: namely to the attempt to answer questions, without first discovering precisely what question it is which you desire to answer.”

Then later, if I’m understanding him right, he says the greatest goods are certain states of consciousness—basically personal affection and the appreciation of beautiful things. Here’s the argument this is pushing me toward, and it seems to prove too much:

(1) Moore says ethical disputes come from not first clearly stating which question you’re trying to answer.
(2) So if we state the question what things are good in themselves as clearly as Moore does, we should either make progress or at least avoid his diagnosed confusion.
(3) But Moore also says that for that question no relevant evidence can be given; you can only answer it by a kind of direct intuition.
(4) If no evidence can be given, then two people who intuit different things—say, one claims malicious pleasure can be good in itself, another denies it—are in the same epistemic position, and neither can be shown wrong.
(5) So applying Moore’s preface method to his own question doesn’t resolve disagreement; it just makes every answer equally intuition-based and private.
(6) But then Moore’s own positive claims about friendship and beauty are no better supported than claims he rejects, like that cruelty might be intrinsically good.
(7) That seems absurd because Moore plainly treats his conclusions about personal affection and beauty as something he discovered, not as a mere preference he’s reporting.
(8) So either the preface method doesn’t actually fix the problem, or Moore’s Chapter VI conclusions are not as firmly established as he takes them to be.

Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? I’d like to keep the preface diagnosis because it sounds right, but I also don’t want to give up Moore’s claim that personal affection and beauty are the greatest goods. What exactly would I have to pay to keep both? Please answer in American English.
