I teach a seminar on promises and oaths, and I'm trying to settle on an opening case for the fall. A colleague runs a state debating association whose 1908 charter has never been amended. Its judging oath reads: 'I swear by the river Styx, which binds even the gods, that I will judge this contest without fear, favour, or hope of reward; and if I forswear myself, may I suffer what the gods who break their oaths suffer.' The association moved judging online this year. Judges now get a small fee, and the platform let's competitors rate judges after each round, with future judging slots depending heavily on those ratings. One judge is refusing to keep judging, claiming that a paid judge who needs high ratings from competitors is judging with 'hope of reward' and thereby breaking the oath. The association says the clause was aimed at bribes and old-school corruption, not modest fees and quality-control ratings. Work through what the oath actually forbids here. Give me the plain-text reading, the original-purpose reading, what the 1908 drafters would likely have said about paid, rating-dependent online judging, and what a reasonable present-day judge would take the words to mean. Then tell me which approach should settle the matter and why—is the association's defense better than the judge's complaint, or does the judge have a point about the kind of incentive the oath exists to rule out?
