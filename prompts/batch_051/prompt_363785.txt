I don't have any academic background in philosophy, but I've been reading Aristotle in translation and trying to reconstruct his case for natural slavery from the Politics as a numbered argument, because I want to know whether it actually works. I want to know specifically whether the argument is valid, and if not, which step fails and on what reading of the key terms.

Here is the argument as I've pieced it together, in my own words:

(1) In a human being, the soul naturally rules the body, and within the soul the rational part naturally rules the appetites; these arrangements are by nature, not mere convention, and being ruled this way is good for the part that rules and the part that is ruled.

(2) Whenever two things stand to each other as naturally fitted ruler and naturally fitted subject, it is natural and beneficial for them to stand in that relation of ruling and being ruled.

(3) Some human beings are related to other human beings the way the body is related to the soul and the way appetite is related to reason: they can follow and carry out another person's plans, and can grasp enough of reason to obey it, but cannot deliberate about how to live their own lives.

(4) People of the sort described in (3) are what Aristotle means by natural slaves.

(5) Therefore, people of that sort should be ruled as slaves, and actually enslaving them—keeping them as property, not just as subordinates—is natural, just, and to their benefit.

I keep feeling that there is a slide somewhere between "some people have a lower capacity for independent deliberation" and "the actual institution of owning such people is natural and just." But I can't tell whether the slide is a false premise, an invalid step, or just my own modern aversion to the conclusion. Am I right that the argument fails? If so, point to the exact step, and say whether the problem is a false premise, an equivocation—for instance between "natural" meaning "by nature" and "natural" meaning "fitting or beneficial"—an unstated assumption, or an inference that does not follow.
