After a career in ecology I've only recently started reading philosophy, and I've picked up a mental rule that I'm not sure about. Whenever somebody argues for an ought on the basis of an is—"this is how nature works, so we ought to leave it alone," or "we evolved this way, so it must be right"—I check out almost immediately. I did it the other day when my neighbor said that because animals kill each other, violence can't be all that wrong. The way I understand it, I'm just following Hume's is-ought point, and maybe Moore's naturalistic fallacy, though I probably couldn't state either one cleanly on the spot. My son says I'm being too quick, since in a lot of those cases there's a hidden premise like "we ought to live in line with nature," and the argument becomes valid if you just make it explicit, and in any case ethics has to take facts about us into account or it's useless. So: is my rule rationally defensible, or is it a bias dressed up as a rule? Is there a version of it that keeps the benefits without just refusing to listen?
