At my rationalist meetup next week I'm running a session on Scanlon-style contractualism and the aggregation objection, and I keep noticing that people call themselves contractualists while making very different calls on cases. I'd like a decision tree that sorts them by what they actually say about cases, not by which label they like. Start from the natural contractualist thought that an act is wrong if someone could reasonably reject a principle allowing it, and that you therefore can't outweigh one person's very serious complaint by piling up many separately trivial complaints. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of named positions: each branch should be triggered by a concrete forced-choice case (one identified patient versus many people with headaches; one certain death versus many 1-in-a-million chances of death; repeated small harms to one person over a lifetime versus the same harms spread across many people; that sort of thing), phrased so someone can answer without already knowing the jargon. Leaves should include at least strict anti-aggregation, ex ante aggregation (summing chances before any outcome is known), ex post aggregation (summing only realized harms), aggregation only within a single life, and prioritarian or weighted aggregation; cap it at eight leaves. Mark combinations nobody ends up in as unoccupied, and say whether that's because the position is incoherent, just unappealing, or an artifact of your cases. Flag pairs of views your tree can't seperate. Keep it self-contained—no authors, no citations. Then say which split is doing the most work and where a strict anti-aggregationist would most want to edit the tree. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the tree overall and single out the point you're least sure of.
