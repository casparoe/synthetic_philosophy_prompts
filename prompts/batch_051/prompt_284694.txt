I am a retired natural scientist—physical chemistry was my field—and I have recently begun working through the philosophy of cognitive science. The puzzle I keep returning to is the one Hofstadter sets out with the careenium: a frictionless table in which large, slower structures called simmballs are formed from many small components called simms. The high-level behaviour can be described in symbolic terms, but the hard question is whether those symbols are anything more than a convenient redescription of the substrate, and in particular whether a symbolic-level pattern can ever be said to push the substrate around rather than merely to ride on it. I want something more focused than the usual gloss on that question.

Suppose a small research programme—say, one postdoctoral researcher and two doctoral students for three years—were to take the careenium problem as its central conceptual target, with no requirement to produce empirical work. What are the main open questions in that vicinity, and which two or three would you prioritise? Please defend the ordering explicitly: for each candidate line of work, I would like to know why it is upstream of other questions, how tractable it is with philosophical tools rather than computational or neuroscientific ones, how neglected it is within the existing literature, and what evidence over the next couple of years would cause you to move it up or down the list. I do not need a reading list or a historical survey.

End with a single-sentence summary of your recommended priority as the final line, beginning with 'Summary:'.
