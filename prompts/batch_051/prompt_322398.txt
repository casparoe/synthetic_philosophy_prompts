I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been working on a piece about pain — specifically whether the awfulness of a pain is the pain itself or a reaction we layer on top of it. I've ended up with a set of views I'd like checked for coherence before I publish. I think the badness of pain is part of the pain itself: a state that didn't hurt at all would just be a tactile or thermal signal, not a pain. I also think pain asymbolia is real — the condition, sometimes reported after brain damange or under certain drugs, where a person says the location and quality of the pain are present but it doesn't bother them at all. And I think when someone describes there own pain like that, we should take the report at face value rather than tell them they must be confused. I also hold that pain's badness is what gives us reason to avoid it: the reason to pull your hand off a hot pan is the awfulness, not the mere information that the pan is hot. At the same time, I think there's nothing irrational about knowingly seeking out pain in some contexts — the burn of a hard workout, entering a chili-eating contest. Finally, I've always assumed introspection is basically authoritative about whether I'm in pain. I'm not sure these all hang together. Where are the real tensions, which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and what would the least costly repair be? I'd rather see the trouble spots ranked by severity than listed as equally bad.
