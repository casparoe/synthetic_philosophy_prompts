I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of economics and have asked two visitors to conduct an adversarial collaboration before the students. One visitor holds that utility in economic theory is nothing over and above a numerical representation of choice: on this view, revealed preference defines utility, and any talk of an inner magnitude that choices measure is at best a harmless heuristic. The other holds that choice data are evidence for a real underlying psychological quantity — preference strength or satisfaction — and that welfare economics cannot do its evaluative work unless "utility" means that quantity rather than merely whatever the agent acts on. Draft the joint statement they should produce. It should include: (i) a numbered section of claims both can sign, covering what choice data do and do not establish, what violations of the Weak and Strong Axioms of Revealed Preference (WARP/SARP) show if taken at face value, and what demand theory strictly requires; (ii) a table of the residual disagreements in wording each side would accept for its own position; (iii) the cruxes, including at least three: whether revealed preference is a definition or a fallible measurement hypothesis, whether preferences can change without the choice record flagging it, and whether a welfare criterion can be built from choice alone once we consider agents whose choices are absent or constrained (children, future generations, adults unable to form or express choices, nonhuman animals); and (iv) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. Where a disagreement is merely verbal, say so and show which term is absorbing the dispute. After the statement, append a JSON summary with fields: agreed_claims (array of strings), disagreement_table (array of items, each with issue, revealed_preference_view, mental_state_view, purely_verbal and a short explanation), and cruxes (array of items, each with crux, why_it_matters, revealed_preference_prediction, mental_state_prediction, agreed_bearing_test).
