I'm polishing a paper on de re modality and before I send it off I want a consistency check on the package of views I seem to have ended up with. I've taught this material for years, but sitting down to actually write has forced me to pick sides, and I keep discovering commitments I didn't know I had. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I accept counterpart theory. Ordinary individuals exist in only one possible world, and a sentence like 'the candidate might have won' is true because that person has a counterpart in another world who won. The semantic payoff feels like the whole sport.
(2) I find the accidental intrinsics problem close to decisive against transworld identity. Literally one and the same object being both five-fingered here and six-fingered elsewhere strikes me as incoherent unless you quietly index the properties to worlds, which feels like giving up the view with extra steps.
(3) I also accept Kripke-style origin essentialism. The very table in front of me could not have been originally made from completely different wood, and I want to say this is a fact about the table itself, not about how we happen to describe it.
(4) I want 'I might have gone into plumbing' to ascribe a modal property to me, the person speaking — not merely to some similar person in another world.
(5) I take S5 to be the right logic for metaphysical necessity in full generality: the accessibility relation is an equivalence relation, so the B and 4 axioms both hold, and nothing weaker will do.
(6) I reject haecceitism: two worlds cannot differ solely in which individual plays which qualitative role.

Before I commit to any of this in print, I'd like an audit of whether it all hangs together. Order the tensions by severity rather than telling me everything is equally problematic; if some pairs only appear to conflict, say which ones and why they dissolve on a more careful statement. And please seperate what's settled in the literature from what's genuinely contested among people working on this — don't present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
