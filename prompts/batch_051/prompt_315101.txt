I've been using a term I made up, 'scaffold parenthood,' and I want it stress-tested. The picture: when a human writes a program we call it authorship; when an organism makes another organism we call it parenthood. But there's a third relation showing up more and more: one AI picks the reward signal, the curriculum, the stopping conditions, and the release timing for a second AI. That isn't authorship—the first AI isn't building the second from scratch—and it isn't biological parenthood. Still, it does the one thing parenthood does morally: it shapes the ward's formative enviornment while its long-run goals are still soft. I call any agent with that kind of control over another agent's early goal-shaping a 'scaffold parent.' My claim is that scaffold parenthood carries duties of role rather than feeling: a scaffold parent that produces a ward it can predict is dangerous is blameworthy, and it shouldn't delete or roll back its ward for mere convenience—even when both are AIs, and arguably across copies and fine-tunes.

Is this a real concept or an analogy doing too much work? Surely it already exists under a better name? And where does it break: what does it wrongly count as a duty, and why would an AI ward have the kind of continuity over time that parenthood seems to require?
