Me and a friend have spent most of a year arguing about why promises bind, and we're finally doing an adversarial-collaboration writeup. I hold the expectation view: a promise binds because you deliberately lead someone to expect you'll do the thing, and then you wrong them by dissapointing that expectation. She holds the practice view: promises bind because saying 'I promise' invokes a social convention that obligates you, and breaking one is wrong because it free-rides on a practice the rest of us maintain. Draft the joint statement for us. It should run in continuous prose, like a real co-signed document, so don't use headings, bullet points, bold, or tables. Work through: the claims we can both sign, about what a promise is and what any decent theory has to explain; the exact places we part ways, in wording each of us would accept for our own side; and the real cruxes. I think two are (a) whether a promise still binds when the promisee dies before she can form any expectation, and (b) whether the wrong of breaking a promise is owed to the promisee in particular or to everyone participating in the convention. For each crux, give a concrete case we'd both accept as a fair test, and write down what each side predicts. And if some of our disagreement is really just words, say which part.
