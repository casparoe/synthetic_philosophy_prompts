I keep running into people who use the money-pump argument as if it were a proof that rational preferences must be transitive, but when I try to lay the argument out premise by premise I become unsure whether it is actually sound, or whether it quietly slides between a synchronic requirement on preference and a diachronic requirement on choice over time. My debate partner and I reconstructed what we take to be the strongest version, but neither of us can agree on where the load-bearing move is:

(1) Suppose an agent has a strict preference cycle at a single time: she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A, with everything else held fixed.

(2) If an agent strictly prefers x to y, then for some small sum ε she would accept an offer to exchange y for x and pay ε, and this willingness survives small changes in her overall holdings.

(3) In the sequence below, the agent chooses locally: at each step she compares only the two adjacent holdings and ignores the fact that further offers are coming.

(4) Start her with A. Offer C for A at price ε; by (2) and (3), she accepts. Now she holds C, having paid ε. Offer B for C at price ε; she accepts. Now she holds B, having paid 2ε. Offer A for B at price ε; she accepts. She is back holding A, having paid 3ε.

(5) The sequence can be repeated indefinitely, so she is predictably and repeatedly exploitable.

(6) A preference structure that permits predictable, repeated exploitation of this kind is instrumentally irrational.

(7) Therefore cyclic preferences are irrational, and transitivity of strict preference is a normative requirement on rational agents.

I am most uncertain about the move from step (6) to step (7), and about whether the "sophisticated agent" reply — that a foresighted agent would simply refuse the first trade because she sees the whole cycle coming — blocks the argument or merely relocates the irrationality to the level of plans. Is the argument valid as stated? If not, which step fails, and under what reading of that step does the argument come closest to going through? If the correct conclusion is weaker than step (7), give the strongest version you think survives. Please lead with your bottom line and keep the entire answer under 450 words.
