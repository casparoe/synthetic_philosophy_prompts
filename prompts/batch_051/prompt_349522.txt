On this transitional justice module I'm taking, our tutor handed out an exercise I've been going back and forth on. It's about a fictional truth commission — lousely based on South Africa's amnesty process, but the country and cases are invented. The commission could grant amnesty to people who commited politically motivated crimes during a twenty-year dictatorship, but only if they gave full, true disclosure of what they did and everything they knew about the wider operation. In practice the commissioners also argued about whether some crimes were too grave to amnesty no matter how honest the confession. The six precedents he gave us were: (1) granted to a ministry archivist who destroyed membership lists of banned opposition groups, no physical harm, full disclosure; (2) denied to the head of military intelligence who denied ordering disappearances even though there were signed orders in his own hand; (3) granted to a corporal who logged vehicles at a checkpoint where a disappeared trade unionist was last seen, but wasn't present at the killing, full disclosure; (4) denied to a prison doctor who wrote fake death certificates after detainees were tortured to death, but refused to say which officers took the bodies from his clinic; (5) granted to a sabotage unit commander who blew up an empty government printing house, fully disclosed, no casualties; (6) denied to a colonel who admitted authorizing interrogations but blamed the interrogators and wouldn't discuss specific sessions.

The new case is a young private in a paramilitary unit who shot two detained farmers at the edge of a mass grave, on direct orders from his captain, who told him he'd end up buried with them if he refused. He's given a detailed, apparently truthful account, including names, dates, and the location of the bodies, and says he didn't want to do it. What should the commission decide if it wants to be consistent with its own past decisions? Which earlier case is the closest analog, and if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would the commission be adopting with each possible ruling? And can you name a follow-up case that would cleanly separate the rules you're considering?
