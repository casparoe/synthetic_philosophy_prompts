I've been arguing with a friend about the Protestant principle of sola scriptura, and I find myself suspended between a systematic view I am attracted to and an intuition that will not go away. On the one hand, the view has real theoretical virtues. If Scripture is the supreme authority, and if its central teaching is clear enough that an ordinary reader can grasp it without an infallible interpreter, then it seems to give the regress of interpretation a principled stopping point. The text, not a teaching office or a tradition, is the final court of appeal; difficult passages are to be read in light of the clearer ones; and the view refuses to place one class of readers over another in the matter that counts most. That is a clean, non-hierarchical picture, and I understand why it has looked like the only stable alternative to an infinite chain of interpreters.

I also have a firm intuition that this merely relocates the regress rather than ending it. A text does not sort its own passages into the clear and the obscure, does not announce which reading of a supposedly clear passage is the one doing the clarifying, and does not certify that a given appeal to the text is faithful to it rather than a reader's construction. The moment someone says that Scripture plainly teaches such-and-such, an act of interpretation has already been carried out—someone has decided what the text's own voice says—and so the question of who decides what the text means seems to return inside every individual reader, now without any institutional check. I do not want an answer that simply awards the match to one side. I want the strongest version of both, and then a negotiated verdict. What would it take for the intuition to go—for it to be shown to be a confusion, say, between the absence of an external umpire and the absence of determinate meaning? What would it take for the theory to go—for it to be amended or abandoned once it admits that some tradition, community, or interpretive practice is doing the actual deciding? And is there a reading on which the two are compatible, in which Scripture can be self-interpreting in one sense while the act of interpretation remains unavoidably mine in another?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
