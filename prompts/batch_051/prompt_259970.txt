Please give me a credence from 0 to 1 for the following claim: if an agent has cyclic all-things-considered preferences (A strictly preferred to B, B strictly preferred to C, and C strictly preferred to A), then the fact that she can be money-pumped — that is, led through a finite series of trades she accepts at each step and thereby returned to A minus some fees — is sufficient on its own to establish that her preferences are irrational. Do not just give the number. Decompose it into at least these sub-questions: (i) whether cyclic preferences are rationally defective independently of any possible exploitation; (ii) whether vulnerability to a foreseeable series of individually acceptable trades is the right sort of test of rationality at all, or merely a symptom that someone could take advantage of; (iii) whether a sophisticated agent who sees the whole trading sequence and declines the later trades would still be pumped, and what that shows about the argument; and (iv) whether the argument conflates 'cannot be represented by a utility function' with 'is irrational.' Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, state how they combine into your overall number, and then say which single sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most.
