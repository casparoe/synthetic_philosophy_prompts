Before I retired, I spent my working life in nonlinear dynamics, and for the past year I have been reading moral philosophy. A structural resemblance keeps presenting itself to me, and I cannot decide whether it is a genuine isomorphism or only a seductive metaphor. I would like a clear verdict on whether it runs deep.

Consider the dynamics side first. In a fully deterministic but chaotic system, the map from initial conditions to later states is continuous yet exponentially unstable: two initial states separated by a distance δ too small for any instrument to resolve will diverge, along at least one direction, roughly as δe^(λt), where λ is the largest Lyapunov exponent. After a predictability horizon of order (1/λ)ln(Δ/δ), where Δ is the size of the features one cares about, the two trajectories occupy unrelated regions of the same strange attractor. The equations are exact; the failure is not in the model. A difference that is real but below the threshold of measurement grows, lawfully, into a decisive difference in outcome.

Now the moral side. Two drivers are negligent in exactly the same way—same speed, same distraction, same stretch of road, same hour. In the first case a child steps into the street and is killed; in the second no one is there. The first may face a homicide charge and carries a burden of guilt the second will never touch; the second receives a citation or nothing. Whatever the right account of their identical culpability, the response to what they have done could hardly diverge more. Outcome alone, with the controlled internal state held fixed, has done enormous moral work. This is the phenomenon I understand as resultant moral luck.

The structural claim I keep coming back to is this: the map from an agent's controlled state—intention, attention, precaution—to what the agent must answer for is unstable in something like the Lyapunov sense. Hold the internal state fixed, introduce a perturbation below the threshold of control, and the output of the map—the verdict, the penalty, the guilt—is qualitatively different. Both structures undercut the expectation that securing the inputs secures the outputs.

But I am not certain the moral case is even the right species of instability. It may be closer to crossing a basin boundary in a multistable system—a small displacement sending the state into a different basin of attraction—than to exponential divergence on a strange attractor. And perhaps there is no meaningful metric on 'same act,' no phase space for agents, and no Lyapunov exponent for blame, in which case the parallel would be nothing more than an image.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. If you judge the parallel deep, I want the mapping stated exactly, an account of what would have to be true on both sides for results from dynamics to transfer, and one concrete consequence the parallel predicts that I should expect to find false. If you judge it superficial, I want the precise joint at which the structures come apart and a statement of what, if anything, survives the failure.
