Consider a French public secondary school that is choosing between two familiar regimes for religious and non-religious identity symbols. Under the standard laïcité regime, students may not wear conspicuous religious symbols on school grounds. Under the accommodationist regime, symbols are permitted unless they demonstrably disrupt instruction or coerce others. A working group proposes a third mechanism, to be piloted for one year.

Each morning, any student who wishes may register a symbol in the school's digital expression ledger: a hijab, a cross, a kippah, a "no religion" pin, etc. The ledger is anonymized and displayed each afternoon in the school lobby; it reports only counts per category, never names. At the end of each half-term, the elected student-faculty council examines the count trajectories. A two-week quiétude period is triggered if any category's weekly count changes by a factor of more than two in either direction, or if a majority of students in the termly climate survey report feeling expected or pressured either to display or to conceal a symbol. During quiétude, the standard laïcité ban is temporarily reinstated and the ledger is suspended.

Treat this as a mechanism to be predicted, not as a proposal to be endorsed or condemned. Describe how it would actually behave: what it incentivizes among students, parents, and student politicians; how it would be strategically triggered, evaded, or otherwise gamed; what second-order effects it would have on the school's social equilibrium; and whether its failure modes can be fixed without collapsing it into either plain laïcité or plain accommodation.
