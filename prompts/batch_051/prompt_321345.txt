I'm prepping a small grad reading group on bargaining theory, and I keep running into the same problem: people argue for Nash or Kalai–Smorodinsky by waving one axiom around, and I can't tell which axioms are actually load-bearing untill I see the whole list at once. So here's what I want: write the desiderata for a two-person bargaining solution.

Fix the setting first. A bargaining problem is a feasible set of utility pairs (closed, convex, containing a disagreement point d), and a solution is a rule that picks one feasible point for each problem. The purpose is normative: you want a rule you could defend to both parties before they know which position they'll end up in. Your job is to spec out what an adequate rule has to get right. For each requirement, give the motivating case or intuition and mark whether it's non-negotiable or a camp preference. I want at least these on the table: Pareto efficiency, symmetry (name-swapping shouldn't matter), invariance to positive affine transformations of utility, individual rationality, independence of irrelevant alternatives (the Nash-style axiom: if you'd pick x from the big set and x is still available in a smaller set with the same disagreement point, you should still pick x there), and monotonicity (the Kalai–Smorodinsky-style axiom: if you expand the set in a way that weakly improves one player's achievable utility at every level of the other player's utility, that player shouldn't end up worse off). If I've stated one of these sloppily, correct it — that's part of the point.

Then the part I care about most: tell me whether some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable. My sense is you can't have IIA and monotonicity together once you've kept Pareto, symmetry, and affine invariance, because each of the two extra axioms pins down a different unique solution, and those two solutions don't always agree. If that's right, walk through why the no-go holds and what exactly has to give. If it's wrong, say so and show what combination works. Also say which single requirement does the most work excluding candidate solutions, and why.

You can score Nash and Kalai–Smorodinsky against the list at the end, but keep it brief — I want the criteria and their motivation, not a summary of the papers. And no reading list or citations; I dont need a bibliography, just the spec.
