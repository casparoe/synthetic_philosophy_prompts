Structural realism is standardly defended by a version of the no-miracles argument: the predictive success of mature science is best explained by its theories capturing real structure in the world, even as the entities they posit come and go. But an increasing share of cutting-edge prediction now comes from machine-learned models—deep neural networks, learned force fields, surrogate emulators—that succeed while remaining partly or wholly opaque: their internal representations shift with retraining, resist interpretation, and are usually not treated as candidate descriptions of unobservable structure. Does the no-miracles argument survive this development? Say which premise it puts pressure on, whether structural realism can be repaired so that it still says something substantive about such models, or whether even a structural realist should be an instrumentalist about this large and growing class of scientific work.
