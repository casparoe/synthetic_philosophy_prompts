I keep going back and forth on what makes a mental state conscious, and I've landed on a picture that probably has a name i don't know. The way in for me is highway driving: when I'm on autopilot I'm still processing the road, but I'm not aware of it in the way I am when I snap to. So I started thinking: maybe a state like a pain, a thought, or a visual experience only becomes conscious when some other state in the mind points at it or is aware of it — like a spotlight that lights it up. But then why doesn't the spotlight itself need another spotlight, and so on forever? The fixes I've heard are: the spotlight doesn't have to be conscious itself, or it points at itself, or the pointing is more like an inner perception than a thought. I can't tell wether those are real fixes or just tape over a hole.

So here's what I want. Give me your credence for this broad claim: a mental state is conscious — there's something it's like for the person — only if some other state (or maybe the state itself) represents it or is aware of it, and the state gets its consciousness from being pointed at in that way. Count the self-pointing version as included, but if you think that's really a different view, split it out and say so.

Put the answer as a JSON object with these fields:

- `final_credence`: a number from 0 to 1 for the broad claim.
- `factors`: an array of objects, one per sub-question your credence leans on, each with:
  - `factor`: the sub-question in plain words;
  - `credence`: your probability for that sub-question;
  - `why`: one line of reasoning;
  - `what_would_lower_it`: the evidence or thought experiment that would most push that number down.
- `combination`: one sentence on how you combined the factors.
- `least_confident`: which factor you're least sure about and why.
- `would_move_most`: the single thing that would most move your final number.

Then, after the JSON, tell me in one or two plain sentences whether the factor the whole view most depends on is the same one you're least confident in.
