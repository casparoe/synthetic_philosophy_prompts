I keep coming back to a thought experiment about forgeries. Suppose a forger makes a copy of a painting that really is indistinguishable from the original, not just to my eye but down to every pigment particle and craquelure—so any perceptual test and any physical test internal to the object would say they're the same. A clean aesthetic empiricist view says there can't be an aesthetic difference between them: if the experience is identical and the object's intrinsic properties are identical, the value should be identical. But I also have this stubborn feeling that the original has something the copy lacks, and its not just market value or bragging rights. If I learned the one I loved was actually the copy, I'd feel tricked even though nothing about the painted surface changed. I don't want a quick answer. I want the strongest version of each side: the case for treating the copy as equal in all artistically relevant respects, and the case for saying the original's history—its having been made by the artist in those circumstances—enters into the work's value itself. Then tell me what you'd have to believe about art, action, or value for each move to be justified, whether there's a stable position where both the theory and the intuition survive, and what would count as evidence that the intuition is just a debunkable fetish about authenticity. Is there a reading of 'the same painting' on which they are two different works despite being physically identical? Or does that just smuggle in the conclusion?
