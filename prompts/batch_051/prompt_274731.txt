An editor once returned a chapter with the note that my protagonist's choice was unmotivated — nothing in the character as written could have produced it; another editor once called a character wooden because every choice followed too predictably from a single trait. I have lately been reading about the digger wasp Sphex, whose apparent intelligence turns out to be a rigid fixed-action pattern, and it strikes me that literary judgment wants a person's actions to be neither arbitrary nor automatic. Reconstruct the theory of agency and free action that this editorial practice presupposes. In particular, does it implicitly distinguish free action from both mechanism and chance, and can that distinction be made coherent? Say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — which rival pictures of agency would license roughly the same editorial advice — and tell me which consideration would most likely make you revise that reconstruction, or where your account of the practice's commitments is most fragile.
