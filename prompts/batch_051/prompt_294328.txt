Me and a friend were arguing last night, over genuinely bad takeout, about whether Nietzsche would have been a quantified-self guy. His take was that Nietzsche was basically a nineteenth-century biohacker. He was always tinkering with his diet, complaining that his migraines and stomach trouble came from what he ate or drank, adjusting when and how much he ate depending on whether his head worked afterwards. So my friend figures he'd love continuous glucose monitors, sleep trackers, meal-logging apps, maybe an Oura ring — same impulse, better instruments. I think that misses the thing. What he wanted from food wasn't health or longevity or biometric optimization; it was the precondition for clear thought. The diet stuff was in service of the mind, and I suspect he'd see today's wellness culture — the self-quantifaction, the obsession with lifespan and body composition — as a nervous, decadent over-concern with the body of the sort he liked to ridicule, maybe worse than the heavy German cooking he was always going on about, since at least that was just bad food and not an entire ideology. We never settled it. So give me your honest reconstruction: what would Nietzsche make of today's health-optimization culture, and why? Draw on what he actually says about nutrition, digestion, stimulants, alcohol, exercise, climate. And don't just split the difference — if one of us is mostly wrong, say so.
