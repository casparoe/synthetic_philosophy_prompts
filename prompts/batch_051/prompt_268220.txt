Anglo-American law treats an incorporated company as a legal person separate from its shareholders: it can own property, enter into contracts, and sue or be sued in its own name, while its shareholders are not personally liable for its debts. But the same courts will "pierce the corporate veil" and hold shareholders personally liable when the corporation was an undercapitalized shell used to defraud creditors — a move in which the company's separate personality is set aside for that purpose rather than shown to be absent. Personhood for a corporation also looks purpose-relative elsewhere: in U.S. law a corporation can be prosecuted and fined but cannot claim the privilege against self-incrimination, while a natural person is a legal person simply by being human and not because the law has granted that status. What theory of legal ontology — of what legal persons and legal facts are — does this pattern of practice presuppose?
