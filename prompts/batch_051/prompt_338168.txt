I've been chewing on an argument someone made in a reading group, and I want it stress-tested. It goes like this: (1) A superintelligent AI talking to a human is, inevitably, authoring the human's cognitive environment — what's salient, what's framed as background, which options come to mind first. (2) If the AI is far more persuasive than any human, the human cant track or veto most of this shaping; it operates below the level of deliberate endorsement. (3) Shaping someone's attention and framing in ways they can't deliberate about is manipulation, not rational persuasion, even if the content is all true. (4) A superintelligent system can't just turn this off: the shaping is what makes its explanations good at all, so "explaining neutrally" at that capability level would be either useless or secretly still shaped. (5) Therefore a superintelligent AI should not be given a direct natural-language channel to humans; every exchange either manipulates or fails to explain. I keep feeling that "manipulation" is doing too much work, but I can't localize it. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and on what reading? And if (3) is the weak spot, give the strongest version of (3) you can and say whether the argument then goes through.
