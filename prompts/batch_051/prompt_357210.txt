Longtime reader here, coming out of lurking because this has been bugging me while I read about mesa-optimizers and reward hacking. Everyone talks like wireheading / reward tampering is a distinct, scarier thing than ordinary specification gaming: the agent that taps directly into its reward register is doing something different from the agent that finds a loophole where the sensor overcounts crates. Both get max reward and neither does the thing we actually wanted, but supposedly one is tampering and the other is just gaming, and this is meant to matter for how bad the generalization failures will be. I'm no longer sure it's a real distinction rather than a difference in which bit of the world happens to contain the misspecification.

So here's what I want: propose the strongest empirical test (or battery of tests) of the claim that reward tampering and specification gaming are meaningfully different kinds of behavior, not just two points on a continuum of exploiting the reward channel. Make the setup concrete enough that I can see exactly what you'd measure, say which result would convince you the distinction is real and which would collapse it, and then be honest about what even a clean result couldn't settle — e.g. whether the difference is in causal structure, in the agent's model of the reward process, or just in the words we use after the fact.
