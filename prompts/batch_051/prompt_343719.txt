I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about how much to risk on bets with a positive expected value, because my gut verdicts look like they were produced by five different people. A coin that I know is biased 60% to land heads, where I win my stake on heads and lose it on tails: I'd risk about 10% of my bankroll, maybe 12% on a brave day, and the 20% number that the math-y people in my life seem to think is canoncial feels genuinely reckless. But the same coin, offered a thousand times in a row with my wealth compounding after each flip: now 20% per flip doesn't just feel fine, it feels like the obviously right answer, and 10% feels like I'm leaving money on the table. A one-shot bet where I have a 99% chance to get a hundred times my stake back, so ninety-nine times my stake in profit, and a 1% chance to lose the stake entirely: I'd put up about a quarter of my bankroll and no more, even though I can see the expected value is enormous and the chance of loss is tiny. A $2 lottery ticket with a positive expected value, say a 1 in 300 million chance at $1.2 billion in cash, doesn't tempt me at all, and I don't think that's just because the EV is still small in absolute terms. But a long sequence of small independent bets, each paying even money and each with, say, a 52% chance of winning: I'd happily stake 1% of my bankroll on every one of them and never think twice.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or at least tells me which of my verdicts I have to abandon? And what does it say about the one I keep going back and forth on: a 90% chance to get ten times my stake back, so nine times my stake in profit, and a 10% chance to lose it, where my gut wants to say 30% but I can't tell whether that's wisdom or just fear. I'd rather read this as continuous prose, like an email or a letter, not bullet points or headings or bold.
