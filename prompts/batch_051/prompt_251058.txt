Im a grad student working on the decision theory of long-shot bets, and a cryonics org I follow floated a proposal I want to think through as a mechanism, not a moral question. Instead of the usual model where members fund preservation up front through life insurance plus annual dues, the org would freeze people for free and take an equity claim: if you're revived, you owe the org 25% of your net worth at that time, collected automatically from whatever assets are still tied to your name; if revival never happens, the estate owes nothing. The org also wants to publish an annual revival-probability estimate, and new signups with low probability estimates could negotiate the percentage down, while existing members are locked at what they signed. I see the pitch: it looks like a bet with no downside if you stay dead, and it supposedly aligns the org with actually getting you revived. But how does this actually play out? What does it incentivize among members, the org's staff, the people who set the annual estimate, and the trustees managing whatever assets are supposed to last until revival? Where does it get gamed or fail, and can those failure modes be fixed without just turning it back into the old prepaid model?
