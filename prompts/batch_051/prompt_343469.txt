So I've been trying to get clear on what makes a scientific classification "carve nature at the joints" rather than just being a convenient label, partly because a friend of mine is writing something about how much of our scientific categories are just decisions we make. I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases. I think gold, understood as the element with atomic number 79, is a real kind, and so is lithium, even though lithium occurs in different isotopes. I also think the set of things that are yellow or weigh under two kilograms is obviously not a real kind. And I think the gem category "jade" is not a real kind either, because it lumps together jadeite and nephrite, which are different minerals with different compositions. But then I get tangled up. I think "mammal" is more of a real kind than "things that live in trees," because mammals form a single branch of the tree of life and tree-dwelling animals don't. Yet I also think "tree" is not a real kind, since oaks, pines, and palms are not one branch, even though botanists and ecologists use it all the time and do real work with it. And I definitely think the IAU's category "planet" is partly conventional, since the 2006 vote could have gone differently and Pluto would still be a planet under a slightly different wording. But I don't want to say "chemical element" is conventional in that way; I think element 119, if it can be made, was already a kind before anyone made it. My question is whether there is a principle that captures all of these, or close, and if so what does it say about the cases I keep going back and forth on: "species" for things like ring species or hybrids, "cancer" in medicine, and "wetland type" in ecology. Mill argues in A System of Logic that when a natural classification is built on real kinds, its groupings are not conventional and do not depend on an arbitrary choice by the person classifying. I'd like whoever answers to test that thought against my verdicts and tell me whether I'm being inconsistent, maybe by forcing me to either give up "planet" or give up something about "tree" or "jade." Also please don't use headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables in the reply; just continuous prose, like an email.
