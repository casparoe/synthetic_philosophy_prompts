I have come to accept a version of the rational-irrationality view that is standardly introduced with Parfit's hitchhiker. The version is this. A hitchhiker and a driver both know each other to be self-interested and rational in the ordinary expected-utility sense. The driver will take the hitchhiker home only if the promised payment is credible, but both know that once the hitchhiker is home he will have no self-interested reason to pay. There is no enforcement mechanism, no reputation at stake, no prospect of future interaction, no collateral, and no binding contract; the driver can, however, perceive the hitchhiker's psychological dispositions accurately. I hold that the hitchhiker can rationally choose, before the encounter, to acquire—and can rationally refuse to undo—a settled disposition to keep the promise even though, at the moment of performance, keeping it is contrary to his present self-interest and he would prefer to break it. The rationality attaches to the prior choice and to the disposition understood as a standing policy; it does not attach to the later act itself, and it does not reduce to a bet on hidden future gains, reputation, or repeat-play returns.

I want a map of what would count against this position, with defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Include the strongest internal objections a careful defender would concede have force, not only what critics say. In particular, address the charge that I have merely relocated the problem from the act to the disposition and am equivocating between ex ante and ex post standards of rationality; what a commitment to expected utility at each choice point, or to dynamic consistency, says is wrong with the view; whether any observation or result from decision theory, economics, psychology, or evolutionary theory could directly test the claim rather than just illustrate it; and what the view predicts in hard variants—where the driver cannot observe the disposition, where the disposition is probabilistic or can later be abandoned, or where the promised act is harmful rather than merely costly. Also say what the position commits me to that rival treatments of the case do not, and what would count against this view specifically rather than against the alternatives. Finally, say honestly whether the position as I have stated it has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be a reason to abandon it or a tolerable feature of a claim about rational agency. Confine the reply to the setup as described; I am not asking for a history of the debate, a reading list, or citations.
