Had a long fight with my friend tonight about risk-weighted expected utility, and after she left I wrote down what I think is a clean arguemnt that the whole theory is broken. She kept saying the Allais pattern is rational, and REU is designed to let one person prefer the sure $1 million to the gamble that pays 89% $1M, 10% $5M, 1% $0, while also preferring a 10% chance of $5M to an 11% chance of $1M. Here's the argument.

1. If two gambles share a common branch (say, an 89% chance of $1M in both), then swapping that branch for something else in both gambles shouldn't reverse which one you prefer. Whichever way the common branch lands, you're in the same position either way, so it can't be doing any work.
2. The Allais agent prefers the sure $1M to the 89/10/1 gamble.
3. Those two gambles share an 89% chance of $1M. So by step 1, replace that common branch with an 89% chance of $0 in both, and the preference must survive.
4. After the swap the sure $1M becomes an 11% chance of $1M, and the 89/10/1 gamble becomes a 10% chance of $5M.
5. So the agent must prefer an 11% chance of $1M to a 10% chance of $5M.
6. But the other Allais preference is the exact opposite. So REU rationalizes a pair of preferences that are flatly inconsistent.

I keep staring at step 1. My friend would probably say step 1 just is the independence axiom and REU doesnt accept it, which sounds like a dodge. Is the argument valid, and if not, which step actually fails? If step 1 is the problem, what's the strongest reading of it on which it is true, and does the argument still go through on that reading?
