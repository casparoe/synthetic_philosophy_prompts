Please argue that the Darwinian/Skinnerian/Popperian/Gregorian ladder of minds is better read as a ladder of where trial-and-error happens—in the gene pool, in overt behavior, in internal models, in cultural artifacts—than as a ladder of how smart a creature is. In particular, argue that the Skinnerian/Popperian boundary is much fuzzier than the standard picture suggests: lots of behavior that gets filed under operant conditioning plausibly involves just enough internal rehearsal, like imagining a movement before making it, to count as Popperian. So most "Skinnerian" creatures would already be a little bit Popperian.
