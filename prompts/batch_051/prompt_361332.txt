I've noticed a move in pseudohistory — Atlantis books, ancient-aliens TV, Fomenko's new chronology, you know the genre — and I keep needing a name for it. The move goes like this: a theory commits to something concrete enough that it ought to leave traces, say a lost Bronze Age trading empire or a civilization with far-advanced tech. When the traces don't show up, that never counts against the theory; it's taken as evidence of erasure, destroyed records, or a mainstream cover-up. But when some ambiguous trace does show up — a similar myth, an out-of-place artifact, a wall that sort of looks like another wall — that's treated as a smoking gun. So the same theory gets confirmed both by finding the thing and by not finding the thing. I know this is somewhere near unfalsifiability, Popper's talk about immunizing a theory, and heads I win tails you lose, but none of those quite captures the asymmetry: its not that the theory refuses to commit, it's that every commitment is double-hedged, and the hedging is never priced in. Does this pattern have an established name of its own? If it does, tell me what it is exactly and how it differs from ordinary unfalsifiability and from ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses; if it doesn't, coin a term and give me a tight definition with the boundary cases. I want the carving, not just the label. End your answer with \answer{...} containing the established term or your coined one.
