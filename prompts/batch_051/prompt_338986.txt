I have been trying to reconstruct an argument from the Hart–Fuller debate and cannot tell whether it is a tight argument or a piece of equivocation.

(1) If a rule is a law, then it is capable of guiding the conduct of the people to whom it is addressed.
(2) A rule can guide conduct only if it is general, promulgated, prospective, clear, non-contradictory, possible to comply with, relatively stable, and applied by officials as announced.
(3) These eight requirements are not morally neutral standards of technical effectiveness; they embody respect for persons as responsible agents, and therefore are moral standards.
(4) So a rule is law only if it meets moral standards.
(5) Hence legal validity cannot be separated from morality in the way legal positivists claim.
