Write a deadpan parody of a software changelog for the 2006 redefinition of planet by the International Astronomical Union—the one that demoted Pluto—written as though the IAU were a software maintainer shipping version 3.0 of the concept PLANET. Format it as real release notes: a version number and date; a breaking changes section; a list of deprecated bodies (Pluto); a migration guide for affected users (school mnemonics, textbook indexes, planetarium gift shops, the New Horizons team); and a known issues section (the fuzzy dwarf-planet boundary, bodies that clear their orbital neighborhoods only marginally). The satire should target conceptual engineering and the debate between analyzing concepts and fixing them, and every joke should come from the actual philosophical pressure points rather than generic software-culture gags. In particular, the piece should treat the question of what the concept planet should do for us as an ordinary design requirement; it should habitually collapse the distinction between discovering that Pluto is not a planet and deciding that planet should exclude it; it should render the changing-the-subject worry as a backward-compatibility complaint; it should treat the license of a precise replacement to depart deliberately from ordinary usage, as in explication, as a routine patch note; and it should present the authority to revise a shared concept as a maintainer's privilege. Steelman the conceptual engineers hard enough that the release notes make their view feel genuinely tempting, while the reductio catches the real sticking points. Stay in character throughout—no winking, no commentary—but keep the irony legible to a reader who only knows that Pluto was demoted. Around 600 to 800 words.
