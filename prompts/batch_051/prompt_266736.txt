Imagine it's 2041 and a company releases worldwide a machine, about the size and cost of a home printer, that makes an atom-for-atom duplicate of any painting or sculpture. The copy matches the original in every testable respect — pigment chemistry, craquelure pattern, isotope ratios, you name it — and the machine leaves no watermark or tracer, so the only remaining difference between the two objects is their causal history: which one is downstream of the artist's act and which one came out of the machine. Owning the machine is legal, but selling a copy as the genuine article is still fraud. Predict how the next twenty years go, focusing on the art market, museums, forgery law, and ordinary people's attachment to the real thing. I want mechanisms visible, not a story. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. For each item, give one concrete effect, the mechanism that produces it, and how confident you are in that prediction; for whichever prediction you're least confident in, also say what would have to be different for it to fail.
