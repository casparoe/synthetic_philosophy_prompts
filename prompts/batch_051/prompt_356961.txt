I'm a novelist, and I've been stuck on a scene that set off a two-hour argument between two friends of mine. The premise: a character, Iris, has her brain removed and kept alive in a basement vat. Her body still goes about the world with a wireless link — every sensation is beamed from the body to the vat, and every motor command is beamed back. At the moment they argued about, her body is dancing at her sister's wedding while the vat sits in the dark basement doing all her thinking, feeling, and remembering. I asked the two of them, where is Iris?

Kate said the basement. Her line was: everything that makes Iris Iris is happening in the vat, and the body is just a drone. If the body got hit by a bus, we wouldn't say Iris died — we'd say she's alive in the basement, just blind and paralyzed; if the vat lost power, we'd say she died even though the body was still dancing. So the person goes with the brain. Luis said that was like saying a person lives in their heart because a stopped heart kills them — something being necessary doesn't make it the location. His test was point of view: Iris sees the cake and hears the band, and none of her experience is of the basement, so her perspective, and therefore she, is on the dance floor.

Kate came back that this only shows where the camera is. The body's eyes are the camera and the brain is the viewer in the basement; I can watch a wedding through a headset in my basement without being at the wedding. Luis said no, those are literally her eyes — everyone's eyes are just cameras feeding a brain in a dark skull, and by her reasoning none of us ever travel anywhere, we're all locked in our skulls watching the world through two holes. That can't be right, so we should keep locating the person by the body. Kate said the normal case doesn't decide the split case; the whole point is that the ordinary rule breaks when you scatter the person across two places. And the bus case still shows the body's location is dispensable: if she can lose the body and remain herself, then she wasn't located in the body. Luis said surviving is not the same as being located — the brain survives, sure, but that doesn't make the basement where she is. Then he added that if you wheeled the vat into the next room while she was dancing, nothing about her experience or behavior would change, which ought to make us suspicious that the vat's coordinates are doing any work at all.

I kept out of it, partly because I really don't know. I'm less interested in the final "where is she" than in who was right about which specific move. Could you adjudicate the exchange as it actually happened? Please write your response in these sections:
1. "The two positions" — one short paragraph each, laying out Kate's and Luis's respective views.
2. "Point by point" — a sentence or two on each substantive move I've reported, saying whether it's sound, a half-truth, or a mistake, and why.
3. "What both are missing" — one short paragraph.
4. "Verdict" — who wins on which specific claims, even if nobody wins overall.
