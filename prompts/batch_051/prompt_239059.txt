Another says a name carries a description along with it, so 'Holmes' packs in something like 'the detective who lives at 221B Baker Street', and sentences can be perfectly meaningful even when nothing in the world satisfies the description. A third says stop using the same table for everything: fiction is more like a separate schema, and 'Holmes is a detective' isn't true in the real-world database, its true inside the stories, the way a move is legal inside a game.

Could you restate this debate in my vocabulary as systematically as you can, not just hand-wavey analogy? And the part I actually care about is the residue — the stuff that does not survive the translation. Is it residue because my vocabulary is missing something the philosophers are right to care about, or because some of their distinctions don't survive being made precise? Use the sentence 'Sherlock Holmes doesn't exist' as one concrete test case: to me that's an existence query for a key that returns zero rows, and a zero-row result is itself a perfectly meaningful, true answer, which already seems awkward for the pointer camp.
