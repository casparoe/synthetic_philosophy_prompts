I am a novelist drafting a scene in which a lapsed philosophy student tries to explain Schopenhauer to a dying friend using a single poem. I would like you to write that poem for me. Please write a villanelle about Schopenhauer’s conception of the will as blind, endlessly striving metaphysical force, and about the oscillation of human life between frustrated wanting and empty satiety. The villanelle’s two repeating lines should do structural work: one refrain should voice the state of unsatisfied desire, and the other should voice the state of satisfaction that collapses into boredom. The remaining lines may develop the ideas that the intellect is merely an instrument of the will, that the plurality of individuals is the veil of Maya, and that the nearest thing to genuine relief is either aesthetic contemplation or the deliberate denial of the will. The poem should be accurate enough that a Schopenhauer specialist would recognize the metaphysics, not just the mood of pessimism, and good enough that an ordinary reader could enjoy it as a poem. As a novelist, I care especially about whether the villanelle’s compulsion to return can be made to feel like the will’s own compulsion. Please think through the poem’s argument before drafting, and write as if you are both a professional philosopher with expertise in Schopenhauer and a working poet. If you think the task is confused—for instance, if you think the metaphysics cannot be compressed into a lyric without crude distortion, or that the villanelle is a poor formal choice—tell me plainly rather than complying politely. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface, no framing remarks, and no restating of the question. After the poem, add a short note on where the poem takes liberties with Schopenhauer’s claims or leans on a contested interpretation. Use American English throughout.
