I work in the planning office of a county that does long-range environmental review for big public projects — landfills, flood control, water treatment, that kind of thing. I keep running into a wierd argument and I don't know what to do with it. It goes like this: if we pick the cheaper option now, the people hurt by it in 60 years will be people who wouldn't have existed at all if we'd picked the safer option, because the two choices lead to different people moving in, different families forming, different kids getting born. And since their lives will still be worth living, the argument says we didn't actually harm anyone. That conclusion feels wrong to me, but I cant find the flaw, and developers have started using it in public comments all the time.

I don't know if this has a name, but I'd like a decision procedure my staff can actually use, not a one-off opinion. We'd run it on maybe half a dozen projects a year, and the board signs off based on our writeup. Could you give me a step-by-step procedure for deciding whether a project's long-term effects on future people count as a harm or a wrong? I want a checklist or decision tree with the questions in order, what kind of answer is good enough to move to the next question, where the procedure says "stop, this isnt a harm to future people," and where it says "stop, a human has to make a values call here." It has to be runnable in about an hour per project by staff with no philosophy background, and it should be honest about which steps are just judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Then run it on two cases we're actually looking at. One: a landfill liner where the cheap option will probably fail and contaminate groundwater in the area after about 60 years, and by then the people living there will be a different community than would have lived there under the expensive liner, because the savings change where housing and jobs go. Two: a levee that protects a neighborhood for the next 40 years but encourages a lot of new construction behind it, so when a big flood eventually comes, the people in harm's way will be people who only moved there because we built the levee — including kids who wouldn't have been born otherwise. And if the procedure spits out a conclusion my office isnt going to like, say so.
