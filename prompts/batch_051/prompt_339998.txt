I'm a science journalist who covers biology and idea-driven features, and every few weeks I run into the same problem. I write a sentence like "a Portuguese man o' war is a colony, not one animal" or "an ant supercolony is basically a single organism," and then a fact-checker asks me why, and I realize Im just going on vibes and whatever a source happened to say. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in what biologists and philosophers of biology actually know about what makes something one organism, that I can run in about fifteen minutes before deadline, with no special training and an editor who has to defend it to readers. It should be a sequence of questions in order, with what counts as a good enough answer at each step, and it should mark the steps where there's no factual answer and it's a choice of framing. Then run it on three cases: a honeybee colony, a coral with its zooxanthellae, and a stand of aspen trees that turns out to be one underground clone. Make sure at least one of those comes out in a way I'll hate to write and my editor will hate to defend, and be honest about what the procedure cant settle.
