Yesterday evening I had a long argument with a friend about Wittgenstein's On Certainty and about what separates a hinge commitment from an ordinary empirical knowledge claim. We kept returning to G.E. Moore's gesture of holding up a hand and claiming to know that a hand is present; my friend rejects both the idea that this is knowledge and the idea that it is a mere assumption. By the end of the discussion she had settled into the following set of verdicts:

- If she holds up a hand in good light and says that this is a hand, she is expressing a hinge rather than claiming knowledge.
- If she looks into the garden and identifies the tree there as a sycamore, she is making an ordinary empirical knowledge claim.
- The proposition that she has two hands is a hinge.
- The proposition that there are other minds is a hinge; the alternative, she thinks, is not one that evidence could ever settle.
- The proposition that the earth has existed since long before she was born is a hinge.
- The statement that the Battle of Hastings was fought in 1066 is ordinary knowledge, because it rests on records and testimony and could in principle be corrected.
- When she says that she is in pain in the usual first-person way, she is neither claiming knowledge nor relying on a hinge; she is expressing her sensation.
- The proposition that twelve times twelve is one hundred and forty-four is a hinge for arithmetic: it is not an empirical discovery, but something that must stand fast if calculation is to go on.
- After she has looked into the next room and found it empty, the statement that there is nobody in the next room is ordinary knowledge.
- The statement that the train for London leaves at two o'clock, read from a timetable, is ordinary knowledge.

I am trying to work out what she is committed to saying about a new case: the proposition that the earth is roughly spherical. Would she, for consistency, classify it as a hinge, as ordinary empirical knowledge, or as something else? I am less interested in the bare label than in the underlying rule. Please set out the plausible rules that would fit her recorded verdicts, say what each rule predicts about the new case, and give your best guess as to what she would say, with reasons for preferring one rule over the others. Then identify a further test case that would most cleanly distinguish the leading candidate rules.

Please write your answer in British English.
