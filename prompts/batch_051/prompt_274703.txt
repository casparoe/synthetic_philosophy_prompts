This is the Allais pattern. In the first problem I choose a certain $1,000,000 over a gamble that gives a 10% chance of $5,000,000, an 89% chance of $1,000,000, and a 1% chance of $0. In the second problem I choose a gamble that gives a 10% chance of $5,000,000 and a 90% chance of $0 over a gamble that gives an 11% chance of $1,000,000 and an 89% chance of $0. I understand the compound-lottery structure: in the first problem, both options share an 89% chance of $1,000,000; in the second problem, both share an 89% chance of $0; and the remaining 11% branch, in which the two options differ, is the same pair of prospects in both problems. Since I have merely replaced one common branch with another, your axiom says my ranking should be the same in the two problems. Instead, my ranking reverses, and I do not feel that I have made a mistake. Show me, in character, why the flaw is in my preferences and not in the independence axiom.
