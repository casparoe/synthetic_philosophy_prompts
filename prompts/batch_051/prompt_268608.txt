I've been chewing on this for a while and Ive ended up at a position that feels right to me but also a bit slippery, and I want you to try to knock it down. Heres the view. Imagine we can pick between two climate/energy policies. Policy A is cheap now but leaves the people alive in 150 years with worse health and shorter lives. Policy B costs a lot now but the future people are better off. The snag is that the two policies also shift which people get born at all — different jobs, different cities, different couples, different timing. So the specific people who exist under A never would have existed under B. That means a person living the worse life under A cant say "you made my life worse than it could have been," because the only alternative for them personally was never existing, and their life, while maybe hard, is still worth living. So Ive come to think a choice like A doesnt wrong the particular future people it creates, even if the world under A is worse overall.

I want to know how exposed this view is. What would count against it? I dont just mean "people will suffer," I mean the specific cases or arguments that would make someone who holds this position genuinely reconsider. Which are fatal, which are just awkward, and which look scary at first but the view can handle? Are there situations where the "they wouldnt have existed under the other policy" move clearly breaks down, or where accepting the view forces me to say something I should refuse to say? What would a defender of this position have to admit counts against it? And when you push on the way I stated it, am I smuggling in any assumptions I should be worried about? For what its worth, the sort of thing that would probably move me most is a case where the person harmed is clearly the same individual either way, and the view still lets us off the hook.
