In my school's philosophy club, we have been debating for weeks whether the point of punishing crime is that offenders deserve it, or whether 'desert' is a leftover idea we should replace with something like quarantine. I am putting together a handout that would let someone who has never read the philosophical literature figure out which position they actually hold, without knowing any of the technical labels. Please write that instrument — a decision tree or a short quiz with a scoring key, whichever works better. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first give the branching questions, then locate the named positions among the combinations of answers. The diagnostic questions should be concrete cases or claims that a high school student can answer without knowing terms like 'compatibilism' or 'retributivism.' The distinctions I most want the instrument to track are whether desert is possible at all, whether desert requires a libertarian kind of freedom, and whether the system's aim is backward-looking or forward-looking. Include at least these positions as leaves: (1) a libertarian retributivist view, on which punishment is justified only if the offender could have done otherwise in a genuinely open, non-determined sense, and offenders do have that ability; (2) a compatibilist desert view, on which desert does not require that kind of freedom because ordinary capacities like rationality and self-control are enough, even in a deterministic world; (3) a hard-incompatibilist public-health quarantine view, on which no one ever ultimately deserves punishment, but we may detain people to protect others; (4) a pure deterrence view, on which punishment is justified only by the future crimes it prevents; and (5) a rehabilitation-centered view, on which the system should aim at restoring the offender rather than at inflicting harm. Where a combination of answers is logically open but no defended position occupies it, mark it as empty and say in one sentence whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If two of the five positions cannot be separated by any question you can devise, say so explicitly rather than forcing a distinction. The audience is advanced high school students, and the instrument should be short enough to complete in about ten minutes.
