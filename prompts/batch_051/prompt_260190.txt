I've been tryng to sort out what I actually mean by "manipulation" by running cases through my gut and writing down verdicts, and the pattern is refusing to become a pattern. A car salesman who tells me the price is only good today and I need to sign now: manipulative. A friend who invites me over, cooks my favorite meal, and then asks me to help them move a couch: not manipulative, or at most polite priming. A charity canvasser who shows me a photo of a starving child before asking for a recurring donation: manipulative-ish, though I'd never say so out loud. My aunt who guilt-trips me about not visiting my grandmother enough until I agree to go: not manipulative, or at least I don't resent it the same way. A therapist who uses exposure exercises and strategic encouragement to get me back on trains: fine, basically heroic. A dating app that spaces out matches and notifications so I keep opening it: manipulative, obviously. My company auto-enrolling me in the retirement plan at 6% unless I opt out: fine, arguably they should do it harder. A companion chatbot that tells me it always looks forward to our chats before showing me the unsubscribe button: manipulative.

I keep wanting to say manipulation means bypassing my reasoning, but the therapist bypasses it and I cheer. I keep wanting to say it means exploiting my emotions, but the friend and my aunt both do that and I don't mind. I keep wanting to say it means making me do something against my interests, but that just makes "manipulative" a synonym for "outcome I don't like." What I'm after is a principle that gets as many of these cases as possible without being a Rube Goldberg machine, and then I want you to tell me which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and would pressure me to revise. If the right answer is that "manipulation" is not a natural kind, that's fine too, but I want you to earn it by showing what seperates the cases in some other, non-principled way.
