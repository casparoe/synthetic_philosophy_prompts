I'm a PhD student in early American intellectual history, and while discussing Franklin's Autobiography with a colleague, she summarized his famous moral perfection project as follows. In the Autobiography, Franklin recounts that as a young man he set out to achieve complete moral perfection, meaning he would never commit any fault at all, at any moment. He composed a list of twelve virtues, later adding humility after a friend told him he was overbearing. His method was to keep a little ledger with each virtue as a row and each day as a column; he would record his daily transgressions as marks and devote each week to a different virtue, cycling through all thirteen in a thirteen-week course. She described this as an essentially Calvinist exercise in self-mortification, since Franklin believed that salvation required a spotless moral record. She also said that Franklin presents the project as a success: by the end of the relevant section, he claims to have achieved the perfection he set out for, and he concludes that anyone who follows the method will obtain the same result. I suspect this account is too neat in places, but I have not read that part of the text closely enough to identify exactly where it goes wrong. Could you go through her summary claim by claim, say which parts are accurate, which are overstatements or confusions, and what a careful reader of Franklin would accept or object to? In particular, please separate what is well settled among scholars from what remains genuinely contested or uncertain, and do not present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
