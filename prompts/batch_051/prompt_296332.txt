I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy, and our reading group has been circling the question of wether promises bind at all. I keep trying to pin down what actually does the work—the social practice, the expectations you create in the promisee, some act of will, whatever—but every time I push on this, someone says "a promise is a promise" and the discussion just stops, like that settles it. I've started noticing that the line gets said in different tones: sometimes it sounds like "you shouldn't need a further reason," sometimes like "promises bind absolutely, no exceptions," sometimes almost deflationary, like "that's just what the word means."

So what I'd like is a menu: what are the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be expressing, from the trivial to the genuinely substantive, and for each one, is it plausible and does it actually get you to the conclusion that promises bind—let alone that some particular promise binds? My hunch is it's doing a lot of argumentative work under the radar, but I can't articulate the readings well enough to object. I want something I can bring back to the group and say "okay, which of these are you actually asserting, and why should it settle anything?"
