hey, im a high schooler and I got assigned a creative response for a philosophy unit on consciousness. We read a excerpt from Dennett's Quining Qualia, and I got weirdly obsessed with the Chase and Sanborn coffee tasters: one says the taste quale stayed the same but he stopped liking it, the other says the actual quale changed. Dennett uses them to show that introspection can't reliably tell the difference between a changed quale, a changed memory, and a changed judgement. For our school zine I want to write a deadpan parody piece about a company, QualiaCorp, that has to issue a product recall because two of its longest customers (Chase and Sanborn) filed contradictory complaints about the same coffee-qualia capsule. Could you write a 600-700 word "customer safety recall notice" from QualiaCorp, formatted with the usual recall sections (product description, reason for recall, what consumers should do, contact info), where the corporate legalese slowly unravels the whole idea that there is a private inner thing to recall in the first place? The humor should come from the actual quining argument: the company can't tell whether the defect is in the quale, the memory of the quale, or the customer's standards. It should be readable to someone who doesn't know the Chase and Sanborn example, but a philosophy kid who knows it should find it fair. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and don't use the word "incoherent" or "unverifiable" anywhere in the final piece.
