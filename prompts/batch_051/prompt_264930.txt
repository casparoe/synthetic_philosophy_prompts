Two members of a philosophy of language reading group have spent months arguing past one another about semantic externalism, and they have now agreed to formalize their dispute as an adversarial collaboration. The first camp holds that the Twin Earth scenario teaches a genuinely anti-individualist lesson: a speaker on Earth and that speaker's molecule-for-molecule duplicate on Twin Earth are in the same total internal physical state, yet when each points at the clear, drinkable liquid around them and uses the same public word, the word has different semantic properties because the liquid is composed of different chemical kinds. The second camp accepts the case as described but denies that it cuts where the first camp thinks it does. On their view, the two speakers share a narrow meaning, sense, or mode of presentation, and the scenario shows only that extension is environmentally dependent; meaning itself remains internal, even if the internalist is then obligated to explain how narrow meaning relates to outward reference.

Draft the joint statement this pair could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. It must contain four things. First, a numbered agreed-claims section stating what both sides accept about the twins' internal states, discriminatory capacities, dispositions to revise their claims, and the ordinary extension of their term in each world, without smuggling in either camp's diagnosis. Second, a disagreements table giving the precise residual disagreement, phrased so that each camp would accept the wording of its own position and would recognize the other side's wording as a faithful and non-caricatured statement of that side's view. Third, a list of genuine cruxes, meaning sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one camp, including at least one crux about whether some part of the dispute is merely verbal rather than substantive. Fourth, for each crux, one concrete case or experimental setup that both camps agree bears on the question, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. You may invent the cases, including variants on slow switching between environments, but make them precise enough that the two predictions genuinely diverge. Produce the document itself rather than a summary of the debate.
