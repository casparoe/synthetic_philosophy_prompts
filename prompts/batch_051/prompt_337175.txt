I'm preparing a unit for a philosophy-and-film course and need a critical reading of the film Interstellar through the lens of the idea, familiar from some approaches to quantum gravity, that spacetime is not fundamental but emergent. By emergent I mean the loose view that spacetime and its geometry are derived or effective: they arise from something more basic, such as patterns of entanglement or nongravitational degrees of freedom, so that points, distances, and times are not the bottom level of description. The film seems to me to both need this idea and resist it. It treats gravity as the one channel that can reach across time and space, in the falling books, the dust settling into coordinates, and the second hand of the watch; and inside the tesseract it presents time as a dimension through which Cooper can move and act on Murphy's past. That is a picture of spacetime as fundamental and navigable, and of the unseen beings as agents outside our time. At the same time, the plot runs on connections between separated points that a defender of emergent spacetime would describe as correlations that do not respect the usual geometry, correlations that only look like messages after the fact. I want a reading that respects both the film and the physics-based idea. Write it in continuous prose, as an email to a colleague, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables. Assume I know the plot, so do not summarize it, and do not pad with quotations from the film. The reading should say where the film genuinely dramatizes something like emergent spacetime, even if unintentionally; where it resists that idea and the honest critic has to admit the film is committed to time as a fundamental arena; and whether the tesseract's presentation of time as navigable is best read as a metaphysical claim at all, or as dramatic license that a defender of emergent spacetime could accept. End by saying what the film gives the philosophical discussion that popular physics explanations do not.
