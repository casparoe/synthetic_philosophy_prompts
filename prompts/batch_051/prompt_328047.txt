I'm a high school student who competes in our school's ethics bowl, and I've noticed a pattern I don't have a word for. Suppose two students are running separate service projects. The first believes the most important thing she can do is raise money for a global-health charity; she thinks animal welfare matters, but much less. The second believes the reverse: animal welfare ranks highest for him, and global health is genuinely good but clearly lower. They reach an arrangement: she will spend an afternoon volunteering for an animal-welfare group she would otherwise not have helped, and he will spend an afternoon volunteering for a global-health group he would otherwise not have helped. By each student's own ranking, the world after the exchange is better than the world in which each had stuck to his or her original plan. It is not a compromise in the usual sense: neither revises her ranking, neither meets the other halfway, and each does something they would, in isolation, have declined to do. I have seen the same structure when two factions in a club disagree about how to spend a shared budget and each gives the other what the other values more in exchange for something it values more.

Is there an established name for this configuration? I am not looking for just a label. I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, how it differs from ordinary compromise, from agreeing to disagree, and from relativism about values, and whether the reasoning underlying it is considered sound. If there is no established term, what should it be called, and what are its boundary cases?
