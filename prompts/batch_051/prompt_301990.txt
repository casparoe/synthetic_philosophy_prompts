In the Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Aristotle sets out the problem of akrasia this way: "whether men fail in self-restraint knowing what they do is wrong, or not knowing, and if knowing, knowing in what sense." I am a software engineer, so my natural way into the question is through temporal discounting. In reinforcement learning, a future reward is weighted less than the same reward now, but different discount curves have different dynamic consistency properties. With exponential discounting, my later preferences stay aligned with my earlier ones; with hyperbolic discounting, my later self can disagree with my earlier self even without new information. It seems tempting to read Aristotle's phrase "knowing in what sense" as tracking exactly that: the akratic person knows the way a hyperbolic-discounting agent knows, because the later time slice's evaluation no longer matches the earlier one's. But I suspect that mapping is too clean. Please explain how well hyperbolic discounting actually works as a model of Aristotle's question. What does it capture, and what does it distort? I would like the explanation in plain but conceptually careful terms, including an honest assessment of which features of the Aristotle passage the model leaves out or misdescribes.
