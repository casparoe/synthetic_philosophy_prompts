Read literally they're nearly always false — most ducks don't lay eggs and very few mosquitoes carry malaria — but I'd still want my system to store them as true. So I need a working defintion of when a sentence of the form 'Xs do Y' is true as a generalisation, rather than as a claim about most individual Xs.

Here are the cases I want the defintion to handle. Clearly true: 'ducks lay eggs', 'mosquitoes carry malaria', 'dogs have four legs' (some have three, fine). Clearly false: 'ducks are female', 'books are paperbacks'. Borderline, where I genuinely don't know: 'lions have manes' (only the males), 'humans are right-handed' (about 90%), 'pit bulls are dangerous' (exceptions and stereotypes both feel relevant).

Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it breaks or needs patching. If you think 'true as a generalisation' is actually doing two different jobs — something like 'true of the kind' versus 'true of a typical member' — say which cases pull them apart.
