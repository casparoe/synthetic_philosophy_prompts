"State the leveling-down objection as precisely as you can, identify the view it targets, and explain how the priority view (prioritarianism) is supposed to escape it. Does the escape succeed? Defend your answer. (~400 words)"

Here is the student's answer:

"The leveling-down objection is usually aimed at telic egalitarianism, the view that inequality is intrinsically bad. Suppose Ann has 10 and Bob has 5. A telic egalitarian has to say that moving to a world where both have 5 is in one respect an improvement, because the distribution is now equal, even though Ann is worse off and Bob is no better off. The objection is that a change which benefits nobody cannot make a state of affairs better.

Prioritarianism is designed to avoid exactly this. It doesn't care about the gap between people for its own sake; it cares about how well off each person is in absolute terms, and it gives extra moral weight to benefits that go to the worse off. Its value function is increasing and strictly concave in each person's well-being. So if Ann is leveled down from 10 to 5 while Bob remains at 5, Ann's weighted well-being drops and Bob's is unchanged, so the total weighted sum must fall. Pure leveling down is never an improvement on the prioritarian view.

Whether the escape works is another matter, and I think it doesn't. We have to ask why a benefit to the worse off should count for more; the only answer available is that inequality is bad. The weighting is just a way of building the value of equality into the value function. Compare (10, 5) with (5, 5): a prioritarian will rank (5, 5) higher, because the distribution is more equal, even though Ann is worse off and Bob gains nothing. That's the leveling-down verdict all over again, just hidden in the weighting. So prioritarianism doesn't avoid the objection; it relocates it."
