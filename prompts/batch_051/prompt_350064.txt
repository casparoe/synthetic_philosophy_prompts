I'm a software engineer and recently got yanked into a physics rabbit hole while building a little 2D particle sim for fun, which led me to reading about the vector potential and gauge freedom, something I'd always just nodded along with. At lunch, I repeated the gist of it and two coworkers got into an argument. I'm going to write out the exchange as best I remember it, because I honestly can't figure out whose right.

(1) Dan, who did a physics undergrad, said the vector potential is just bookkeeping. You can add the gradient of any scalar to A and simultaneously adjust the scalar potential, and the E and B fields are totally unchanged, so no measurement can ever distinguish them. Two mathematical descriptions that agree on every possible observation are just two descriptions of the same physical situation; so A isn't real the way E and B are.

(2) Maya replied that the Aharonov-Bohm effect breaks that. You can have a solenoid with a magnetic field trapped inside it, and an electron traveling through a region where B and E are both zero still has its interference pattern shifted. The shift is set by the vector potential. So an electron seems to "feel" A in a field-free region — which makes A look pretty real.

(3) Dan said the electron only feels the loop integral of A around the solenoid, and that loop integral is gauge-invariant; it equals the flux through the loop. The shift doesn't tell you A's value at any particular point. So the Aharonov-Bohm effect doesn't make point-by-point values of A real — it just points to a different, nonlocal, gauge-invariant quantity.

(4) Maya shot back that this is already her point: there is more real structure than local E and B. If only E and B are real, how does an electron in a field-free region know about flux inside a solenoid it never enters? You need potentials (or at least their loop integrals) in the picture to make sense of it.

(5) Dan answered that "needed for calculation" doesn't mean real. You can simulate something in redundant generalized coordinates; the redundancy doesn't make every coordinate physical. Gauge freedom, he said, is surplus structure in the formalism, and you should only count gauge-invariant quantities as real.

(6) Maya said gauge transformations look a lot like symmetries, and physicists are happy to say symmetries reveal real structure rather than mere bookkeeping. We don't treat a rotated coordinate system as a new universe, but the fact that some things are invariant under rotation tells us something real about the system. So maybe gauge invariance itself points to what's real, and the real objects are exactly those loop integrals and fluxes that Dan kept appealing to.

I stayed out of it, but I'd like a referee. Please go through this move by move and say who's right about what, then give an overall verdict on whether the vector potential should count as real or just as surplus mathematical structure — and if the argument hinges on what "real" is supposed to mean, say so.
