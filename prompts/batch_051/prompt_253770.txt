I've been thinking on my own about the Condorcet jury theorem, and I'm drawn to what it seems to show: a tidy, almost mathematical argument that a large group of voters who are each only slightly better than chance will, if they aren't copying one another, almost certainly be right. But I can't shake the opposite conviction. Imagine a huge electorate where everyone is barely better than chance and everyone gets their information from the same few slanted sources: that group seems to converge not on the truth but on a shared mistake, made to feel authoritative by its very size. I don't want a verdict on which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the use of the theorem to justify majority rule to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a reading of 'not copying one another' on which both survive. Make the strongest case for each side before saying which should yield, and lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
