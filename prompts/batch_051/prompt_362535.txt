I cover ecology, animal behavior, and their ethical implications for a general-audience science magazine. I am preparing a feature on a question that has come up repeatedly in interviews with ecologists and welfare researchers: once the suffering of wild animals is counted seriously, should habitat destruction still be treated as an unambiguous loss? Before I build the piece around my own position, I want an audit of whether that position hangs together.

Here is where I currently stand:

1. The pain, fear, and hunger of a wild animal matter morally in just the same way, and with the same urgency, as comparable states in a human or domestic animal; being wild cannot demote an animal's suffering.
2. The assumption that wild animals generally have lives worth living is an empirical claim, not a default. For many small, fast-reproducing species — large broods, very high juvenile mortality, minimal parental care — the balance of positive and negative experience may well be negative.
3. It follows that a habitat sustaining a very large population of animals whose lives are on balance not worth living contributes negatively to the world's total welfare, and its continued existence cannot be defended on the ground that it makes those individual animals better off.
4. We nevertheless have stronger obligations not to cause suffering through our own actions than to prevent suffering that arises in nature from processes we did not set in motion; doing harm is worse, other things equal, than allowing comparable harm to continue.
5. For that reason, I also hold that deliberately destroying habitat as a welfare intervention would be reckless: our uncertainty about the net balance, the indirect effects of habitat loss, and the irreversibility of ecosystem collapse is simply too severe.
6. Finally, the value of a habitat is not exhausted by the summed welfare of the individual animals in it; species diversity, ecological complexity, and natural processes have some value independently of how any individual animal feels.

Which of these are in tension with which? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, separate real tensions from ones that only look that way on a first pass, and for each genuine tension identify the least costly revision and what it would give up. I would rather learn where the weak point is now than have the piece reproduce an inconsistency.
