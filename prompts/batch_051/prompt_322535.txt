I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether deepfakes are a genuine epistemic break, and he has an argument that's been bugging me. His line is that the usual story — video used to be an epistemic backstop for testimony, and deepfakes destroyed that backstop — has it backwards. Video never was a backstop, because a backstop would have to be independent of anyone's say-so, and video never was. So deepfakes don't create a new vulnerability; they just force us to notice one we always had. He wrote the argument out like this:

(1) Video can function as an epistemic backstop for testimony only if it lets us check a testimonial claim without relying on anyone's say-so.
(2) But even before deepfakes, checking what a video showed always relied on someone's say-so: the person who recorded it, the person who handled the file, the expert who authenticated it, the platform that vouched for it.
(3) So video never satisfied the condition in (1).
(4) Therefore, video never was an epistemic backstop for testimony.
(5) Deepfakes don't change that; they just make the dependence on say-so obvious, because now the possibility of fabrication is the first thing we have to rule out.
(6) Therefore, deepfakes don't introduce a new kind of epistemic vulnerability; they merely expose one we already had.

I keep thinking premise (2) is too strong, but I can't make the objection sharp. There seems to be a difference between relying on a person's honesty about what they saw and relying on a mostly physical causal chain that merely has technicians somewhere in it, and maybe (2) flattens that distinction. But I also suspect (1) is setting the bar for 'backstop' so high that the conclusion is definitional, and the move from (5) to (6) seems to need something like 'making an old dependence visible can't itself be a new vulnerability,' which he never states. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails? And if the real problem is (2), can you give the strongest reading of (2) on which it's true, and say whether the argument then still gets him to (6) or only to something weaker?
