I've been in and out of pain clinics all year for nerve pain, and every visit starts the same way: someone asks me to rate the pain from zero to ten. I say six. They write it in my chart next to my other vitals, it gets used to decide whether the medication is working, and the insurance forms treat it like a lab value. But here's what bothers me. I have no idea whether my six is your six. A woman in labor and a guy trying to tough out a kidney stone might both say seven. The nurses even act like the pain is simply whatever I say it is—and then they record the number as if we all meant the same thing by it. So the scale seems to assume I can point inward at a private sensation, privately label how strong it is, and that this inner act of pointing can somehow hook up to the same public scale everyone else uses.

Then a friend gave me a book on Wittgenstein, and if I'm following him at all, he argues that this is exactly the kind of thing that can't be done: a word can't get its meaning from a purely private act of pointing at a sensation, because there's no real difference between using a word right and merely seeming to yourself to use it right. Meaning, he seems to say, needs public rules and shared criteria. I probable have that half wrong. But if he's even half right, the whole zero-to-ten practice starts to look philosophically loaded.

So my question: what theory of pain and pain language does the zero-to-ten scale actually presuppose? Does it commit clinicians to the existence of a private inner object that can be labeled and quantified from the inside, or is there a less loaded reading where the number is just a rough behavioral signal, a way of getting a prediction out of a person in pain? Could the scale be perfectly fine medicine even if the private-language argument is right? What would the scale have to look like if someone took that argument seriously—scrapped, or kept but understood as something other than a measurement of a private object? And is the practice internally consistent, since the self-report is treated as authoritative but the number then gets written down as if it were objective data?

I want you to actually dig into the philosophical commitments here, not give me a sociology of medicine. But also tell me plainly if I've set the question up wrong, misread Wittgenstein, or if asking what a pain scale presupposes is a confused way to get at whatever I'm trying to ask. If the honest answer is that the scale presupposes nothing much, or that several incompatible theories could all license it, then say that.
