A friend and I recently spent a long evening arguing about why some falls strike us as funny and others do not. We kept circling an asymmetry that neither of us could justify. A self-important politician slips on ice during a photo call, lands in an undignified heap, and is unhurt; the clip is shared as comedy, and we both found it funny. An elderly stranger makes the same slip on the same ice, lands in the same heap, and is also unhurt; we did not find it funny, and someone who laughed would have struck us as cruel. The physical events are nearly identical, neither person is harmed, and neither is known to us. We tried drawing the line with superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation, but each theory seemed to apply to both falls or to neither. Please say whether this asymmetry is defensible: find the strongest principle that separates comic downfall from a merely lucky and undignified near-miss, test it against the case where the politician breaks his wrist in the same fall, and tell me whether the asymmetry should be kept, revised, or given up. Keep your answer to at most 300 words.
