Propose a working definition of 'terrorism'—one that a careful reader could apply without relying on any particular legal system—and use it to classify the following cases. State the definition first. Then go through the cases one by one and say how the definition classifies each. Where a case exposes strain in the definition, say so plainly, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising classification. End by identifying which of your classifications is least secure and what further fact about that case would do most to change your mind. Please write the entire answer in British English.

The cases:

(a) A politically organised group detonates a bomb in a crowded market, then issues a statement demanding that the government withdraw its troops from a region.

(b) A bank robber shoots a cashier while fleeing; the robber wanted only the money and acted alone.

(c) During a war, a state's air force bombs a residential district of an enemy city, after the state's leader said publicly that the aim was to break civilian morale.

(d) An environmental group sets fire to an empty laboratory at night. The building is destroyed and no one is hurt; the group says the aim was to deter similar research.

(e) A criminal organisation beheads an investigative journalist and leaves the body in a public square to stop other journalists from covering its activities. It has no political programme beyond protecting its business.

(f) An insurgent unit bombs a military barracks and kills only serving soldiers, in order to punish an occupying army.

(g) A person with no group affiliation and no articulated political aim detonates a small device in a public place; one bystander is killed. The person says afterwards only that they wanted the city to feel the fear they live with.

(h) A ransomware crew locks the computer systems of a regional electricity grid during a cold snap, forcing hospitals to evacuate patients, several of whom die, and demands a political concession rather than money.
