Before I evaluate any proposed solution to the combination problem for panpsychism, I want the specification on the table first: what does an adequate account of how micro-subjects (or micro-experiences) combine into a single macro-subject actually have to accomplish? Write the desiderata — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right (split-brain subjects, dreamless sleep, the unclear threshold at which a developing nervous system comes to be conscious, hypothetical group minds), and the distinctions it cannot collapse: phenomenal unity versus merely functional integration, a genuine subject versus a system that only behaves as though it had a point of view, the boundary problem of where one subject ends and the next begins, and combination versus mere co-consciousness. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps. Then flag any pairs of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the solutions currently on offer. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring individual contenders.
