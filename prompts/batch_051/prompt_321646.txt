I work as a software engineer and have only casual reading in this area, so I would appreciate a careful sanity check on a short essay I am drafting for a reading group. The essay begins from an analogy between self-hosting compilers and the kind of tangled hierarchy Hofstadter illustrates with M. C. Escher's Drawing Hands, and it tries to defend a specific claim about the inviolate level.

The central claim is that tangled hierarchies never actually dissolve foundations; they relocate them to a level the loop is not allowed to inspect. In the lithograph the two hands seem to draw each other into existence, but the image depends on a fixed sheet of paper and on the fact that the hands cannot erase that paper from inside the picture. Likewise, a self-hosting compiler can look as if it certifies itself, but the chain of compilation has to bottom out somewhere in a bootstrap that is trusted rather than read as source. The trusting-trust attack makes the point vividly: once a compiler binary has been corrupted, even recompiling it from clean source that would have removed the corruption can reproduce the corruption forever, because the source only gets its meaning through the already-compromised compiler. My tentative conclusion is that any system rich enough to refer to and revise its own rules needs at least one level that is treated as operative rather than as something reinterpretable from within, as law rather than as text; otherwise questions about the system's own rules never terminate. The essay then distinguishes principled inviolate levels, such as axioms, trust anchors in a root certificate store, or constitutional amendment procedures, from accidental ones, such as a binary nobody has source for or an inherited default nobody still understands. It argues that the interesting question is not whether such a level exists but whether we can say why this particular level deserves to be inviolate.

I would be grateful for comments on several specific points. First, is the analogy across Gödelian self-reference, pictorial self-reference, and compiler bootstrapping genuinely illuminating, or am I blending different kinds of self-reference that only superficially resemble one another? Second, does the argument collapse into the familiar point that justification has to stop somewhere, or is something more distinctive being claimed? Third, which step in the argument is weakest, and what would a convincing counterexample look like? Finally, is the essay worth developing further, and if so, what should the next section take on?
