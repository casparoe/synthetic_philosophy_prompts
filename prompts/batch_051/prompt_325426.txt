I cover artificial intelligence for a science and ideas outlet, and I am developing a column about the way content moderation is shifting from enforcing rules about words to enforcing rules about positions in embedding space. I would like your help thinking through an interpretive problem that has come up in my reporting.

A major platform's policy on generative content contains this clause: "Material is prohibited if its meaning falls within any category of prohibited content, regardless of the words, language, format, or apparent subject matter in which it is expressed." The clause was added years ago to catch euphemisms, dog whistles, and translated versions of banned material, and its drafters plainly assumed that "meaning" was something an attentive reader or moderator could recognize from what the material actually says. Since then the company has moved to embedding-based screening, in which each post is compared in vector space to known prohibited material. The case now dividing the trust and safety team is a network of accounts whose posts are on their face entirely benign—gardening calendars, tide tables, parish council notices—but whose embeddings cluster tightly with a set of removed, unequivocally prohibited posts and sit far outside the distribution of ordinary benign text. Several moderators report that the posts give them the same sense of wrongness as prohibited material, though none can point to any offending content.

I would like you to work through how each of the main interpretive approaches—the plain words of the clause, its evident purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked about embedding-space similarity, and what a reasonable reader of the policy would understand—would decide this case, and then say which approach should govern and why. I am less interested in the platform's practical outcome than in the deeper question: should "meaning" in a rule of this kind be anchored to what a text communicates to a competent reader, or to where it sits in a semantic space the platform itself has built?
