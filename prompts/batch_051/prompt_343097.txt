I dont know the right vocabulary for this, so I'll describe it plainly. In quantum mechanics, people say a helium atom contains two electrons, and that number seems to do real work: the two electrons have to take different states rather than both settling into the same one, and similar counting is supposed to explain the periodic table. But the same physicists also say the electrons are completely indistinguishable: a situation where electron A is over here and electron B over there is not treated as a different physical state from the swapped situation, and the formalism enforces that. And its not like two identical-looking marbles, where you could in principle have kept track of which marble became which; the theory seems to say there's no fact of the matter about which electron is which even in principle. So the number 'two' floats free from any property one electron has and the other lacks. Is there an established name for the view that two things can be distinct without differing in any property, or for the opposite view that such a distinction is nonsense? Is the electron case a standard counterexample to the second view, or would people who work on this say I'm using 'property' too crudely? I care less about whatever the label is than about the carving: what makes two things two, if not a difference in their properties?
