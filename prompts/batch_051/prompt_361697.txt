I help run a small forecasting outfit that produces probabilistic judgments about political risks for a foundation's grantmaking. We assign two analysts to each question on the theory that they will catch one another's mistakes, but we keep noticing a pattern: two analysts who have read the same briefing pack sometimes settle on very different numbers, and their disagreements often end not with one person updating but with both digging in. I keep thinking of Aumann's agreement theorem—roughly, that rational Bayesians with common priors cannot agree to disagree once their posteriors are common knowledge—and I am considering a mechanism that tries to operationalize the theorem rather than merely invoke it.

Here is the proposal. For each tracked question, two analysts first submit a probability in private. A dashboard then reveals both forecasts, shows each analyst the other's evidence log, and asks both to resubmit after a twenty-minute review. The system iterates until the two numbers are within 0.02 of one another or until six rounds have elapsed. Analysts are compensated for accuracy against the eventual outcome, but they also receive a small "convergence bonus" whenever the pair ends within the tolerance band, and a mildly embarrassing "polarization flag" on their team record when the deadline is reached without convergence. The hope is that the protocol will separate cases in which disagreement reflects asymmetric information—which the log-sharing should eliminate—from cases in which it reflects something else.

I would like you to analyze how this mechanism would actually behave. What would it incentivize at the level of the individual analyst, the pair, and the organization? Under what conditions would convergence track truth rather than manufactured consensus or deference to the more senior analyst? What would you expect when the analysts do not share common priors, when one holds private evidence that cannot be fully logged, or when the six-round deadline expires without convergence? And are the obvious failure modes fixable by tweaking the rule, or does the whole design mistake a theorem's assumptions for an organizational procedure?
