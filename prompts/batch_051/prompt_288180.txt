Here's a Kelly-criterion variant I haven't seen worked through. You get the standard setup: an indefinitely repeated bet on a coin that's biased in your favor — 60% heads — and you can wager any fraction of your current bankroll at even odds, winning your stake on heads and losing it on tails. The usual argument says stake 20% each time because that maximizes expected log wealth, and maximizing expected log wealth is what maximizes long-run growth. Now change one thing: the game doesn't run forever. At the start of each round there's a 2% chance the casino shuts down and the game ends permanently, and you keep whatever you have. There's no consumption out of the bankroll, and you care only about the terminal bankroll. Does the 20% rule change? If it does, which way and why; if it doesn't, explain exactly why the standard argument survives a geometrically distributed horizon. Then say whether it still survives if you take out a fixed amount for living expenses each round before betting, and at what point the usual Kelly logic genuinly breaks down. Keep the answer in American English.
