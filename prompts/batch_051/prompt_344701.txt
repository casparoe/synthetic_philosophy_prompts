Every few weeks my team gets a new feature proposal for our consumer EEG headband, and I've been asked to fix the review process. The band is non-invasive and currently does focus and sleep scores. The proposals getting serious now are: (1) a meditation app that reads your emotional state in real time and swaps the audio track to match it, (2) an optional stress badge for video calls that shows the other participant your inferred stress level if you toggle it on, and (3) a weekly attention report an employee can opt into sharing with their manager. Our one-page policy says we respect mental privacy and cognitive liberty, but nobody can turn those words into an actual yes/no on a spec. Legal talks about consent and data minimization and mostly punts. The outside neuroethicist we keep on retainer mutters about the inner domain and then says it depends.

I need a reusable decision procedure, not an essay. We're not philosophers. It has to run in a forty-five-minute review. Spell out the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to keep going, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is values rather than facts. Keep the distinctions that actually do work in the mental privacy and cognitive liberty literature, drop the ones that won't survive contact with our product team, and flag the places where you're dressing up a judgment call as a checkbox.

Then run it on the three features I listed: the meditation emotion-adapter, the video-call stress badge, and the employer attention report. At least one of those should get an answer my product team won't like, and say clearly why the proceedure forces it.

After the procedure and the three cases, give me the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields:
- "procedure": an array, each item with "step_number", "question", "evidence_needed", "proceed_if", "stop_if", and "goes_to_human_if".
- "worked_examples": an array, each with "case", "verdict", "decisive_step", and "who_has_final_call".
- "limits": an array of strings saying what the procedure cannot decide and where it will misfire.

Don't just give me the JSON; I need the prose walkthrough of the procedure first, then the three cases.
