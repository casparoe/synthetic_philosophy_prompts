Here's one I actually care about. I work mostly in typed languages, so I default to thinking of everything as having kinds, and I keep tripping over the Incarnation. The church councils' answer was that Christ is one person with two complete natures, divine and human, brought together without being mixed into some third naturey thing and without being treated as two agents sharing a body. I want you to restate that two-natures doctrine in programmer terms — types, subtypes, interfaces, composition, inheritance, unions vs. intersections, object identity, maybe an actor model for the person — and then be honest about what does not survive the translation. Which parts of the doctrine have no clean counterpart in that vocabulary? And is the leftover a sign my vocabulary is too thin, or is the doctrine itself less a positive technical theory and more a boundary marker for what you're not allowed to say? Also, whether the translation succeeds or not, what does it force you to notice about what it means to claim one person has two natures?
