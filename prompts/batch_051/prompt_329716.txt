I'm a philosopher of mind and I've been rereading Dennett and Kinsbourne's Time and the Observer for a grad seminar, and I'm struck by how completely the debate around the Stalinesque versus Orwellian revisions has stalled since the early 90s. The passage that keeps nagging me is the one where they set up the two ways the brain might handle the color phi phenomenon. On one story, the brain doesn't represent the first green flash at all; it edits the event before anything gets into consciousness, and then fills in the red flash at the second position. That's the Stalinesque revision. On the other, the brain registers both stimuli in experience, but then rewrites the memory so that the first flash gets backdated or folded into the second; so you were conscious of both but the archival record lies. That's the Orwellian revision. Dennett and Kinsbourne say there's no way, even in principle, for the subject or the experimenter to tell which happened from the inside, because any capacity or report we use as a probe can itself be located at different stages in the processing chain. I'm not sure whether to call this a deep epistemological point about the timing of experience or just a refusal to take the question seriously.

I've read a lot of the critical replies and the responses, and I have the strong impression that people just talk past each other: the critics say Dennett is denying the obvious fact that something is happening in experience at some time, and Dennett's defenders say the critics are smuggling in a Cartesian theater. The debate doesn't progress. What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is the dispute substantive or verbal? Is it a disagreement about the metaphysics of conscious experience, a disagreement about the semantics of the subject consciously experienced X at t, or a disagreement about what counts as evidence in consciousness science, or maybe all three at once? I have a hunch that the real sticking point is that the two sides have different standards for when a temporal property of experience counts as settled, and that no experiment could satisfy both. Can you test that hypothesis? And, given your diagnosis, tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress: a formal result, a new experimental protocol, a change of question, or even just a distinction that's been there but hasn't been drawn sharply enough.
