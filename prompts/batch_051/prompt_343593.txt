All right, here's the short paper prompt. In seminar on Thursday we got stuck on whether a mainstream war shooter—think storming the beach in Normandy against bots—can be criticized for glorifying war, and whether it can also be taken seriously as art about war, or whether those two options cancel eachother out. Someone in the back row said, kind of laughing, "come on, it's just a game," and a decent number of heads nodded as if that settled it. I don't think it settled it.

Your job is to take that sentence apart. "It's just a game" is doing real work, but it could be doing several different jobs. I want you to lay out the distinct precise claims it might be expressing. Candidates include: a claim about fictionality or pretense, a claim about the player's intentions, a claim about consequences being walled off from the real world, a claim that play has a special moral status, a claim that the game has no serious representational content about war, and whatever else you find. For each reading, tell me whether it's boringly true, substantive and defensible, or false (or at least far from obvious). Then the part I actually care about: which of those readings would need to be true in order for "it's just a game" to block the glorification worry, and whether that specific reading is one of the defensible ones. Also say, briefly, which reading the people nodding in the back row were probably endorsing, even if they hadn't done the work to pick it out.

No bibliography, no citations, no history of the phrase. I'm grading the distinctions, not your library card. And don't cop out with "it depends" at the end—give me a real verdict about whether that sentence can carry the weight it was being asked to carry.
