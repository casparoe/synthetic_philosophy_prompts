I am a PhD student in formal political theory with a background in statistical learning, and I want to know whether a parallel I keep drawing is deep or superficial. On one side is the Condorcet jury theorem: if each voter in a group is independently more likely than chance to answer a binary question correctly, then the probability that the majority is correct increases with group size and tends to certainty; if each voter is worse than chance, the majority becomes increasingly unreliable. On the other side is the standard informal rationale for voting ensembles in machine learning: many weak learners, each only slightly better than chance, can be combined into a strong committee, provided their errors are not too correlated. The apparent mapping is exact enough to tempt me—voters to base classifiers, individual competence to accuracy above chance, independent votes to uncorrelated errors, majority rule to the aggregate vote, and group size to ensemble size.

I am trying to determine whether this mapping is a genuine identity of structure or only a loose analogy. If it is genuine, I would expect the theorem's results to transfer, both in the positive direction and in the converse. Yet the usual formal justifications offered for ensembles are framed in terms of bias–variance trade-offs and variance reduction, not in terms of the probability that a collective decision is correct; and in practice, growing an ensemble of correlated classifiers does not drive error toward zero but levels off at a floor set by the shared error structure. The original theorem also assumes homogeneous competence and nonstrategic voting, while ensemble members vary in accuracy, are often weighted, and have no interests at all, which makes me unsure whether those assumptions are incidental or load-bearing. Are the practical departures simply the theorem's independence assumption failing, or do they indicate that the two settings are governed by different mathematics? I would like to know where the mapping holds exactly, where it breaks, and what the analogy would predict that turns out to be false.
