Okay so I just had this two-hour argument with a friend about many-worlds and I cant shake it. I'm drawn to the Everettian picture because it's so clean: you just let the wavefunction evolve, no collapse postulate, no special observer, no measurement problem. But here's where I got stuck. Suppose I'm about to measure a spin in an unequal superposition, say sqrt(0.9) up plus sqrt(0.1) down. The theory seems to say both outcomes happen: there will be a branch where I see up and a branch where I see down, and both are equally 'me' after the split. My friend kept saying that before the measurement there isn't a fact about which one I'll see, so there's no probability at all — the Born rule is just some epiphenomenal add-on, and if you naively count branches you'd get 50/50, not 90/10. But I have this really firm intuition that I should be about 90% confident I'll see up, not 50/50 and not 'no probability at all.' I'd definately pay more for a bet that pays on up than on down, and it doesn't feel like just a pragmatic policy; it feels like something about my situation before branching. So I'm torn between a theory I find metaphysically elegant and an intuition I can't get myself to drop. Walk me through what the options actually are: should I drop the intuition, amend the theory, or is there a reading of many-worlds where the Born rule is genuinely there and not bolted on? I want to know what each move costs, what would justify it, and whether 'you're just confused about personal identity across branches' is a real argument or a way of not listening to the intuition.
