Most people think it's fine that you can quit a job, leave a church, cancel a membership, even divorce a spouse you once picked. But the one association you're born into and cant opt out of is the state. Emigrating doesn't really fix it: you just swap one state for another, and the old one often still reaches after you for taxes or a draft until you do the paperwork to make it stop. Is there actually a principle that makes the state different from every other association, or is it just that states have the soldiers and we're all too polite to say that's the whole story? The sharpest test I can think of is a permanent floating city in international waters that runs its own courts, defense, and law: if the people there want to opt out of every existing state, is there a real reason to say no, or is our reaction basically just risk-aversion dressed up as principle? If there is a principled difference, defend it against that case.
