Here is the tension I keep going in circles on. The Everettian picture seems to earn its keep by dissolving the measurement problem: it takes the unitary dynamics as the whole story and never has to say which interactions count as measurements. Yet when I consider a measurement whose two possible outcomes have squared amplitudes of 0.7 and 0.3, I cannot make myself believe that the only fact worth explaining is why I should bet as if the probabilities were 0.7 and 0.3. It seems to me there is some objective sense in which I am more likely to find myself in the first branch, and that taking both branches to be real has not yet told us what the number 0.7 attaches to or why it should guide my expectations. Present the strongest version of each side, then give a negotiated verdict. Specifically: what would justify treating the demand for objective probability as a leftover of collapse thinking and dropping it? What would justify amending or abandoning the interpretation in order to recover objective probability? And is there a precise reading of probability, branch weight, or self-locating credence on which both the theory and the intuition survive without equivocation?
