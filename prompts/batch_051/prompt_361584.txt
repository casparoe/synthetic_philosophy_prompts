I keep coming back to Augustine's account of time in Confessions XI — time as a stretching-out of the mind, where the past exists only as memory, the future only as expectation, and the present as a kind of dilated attention. Its got real virtues as a theory: it explains why the past and future feel less real than the present, it makes the passage of time depend on a subject instead of some mysterious moving now, and it handles the weird phenomenology of waiting and boredom better than any physics-based story does. But I also cant shake the conviction that the universe had a real, ordered past for billions of years before there were any minds around to remember or anticipate anything. A planet accreting, sediment settling on a dead seafloor, a radioactive nucleus decaying on schedule — those seem like temporal facts that don't give a damn whether anyone attends to them.

I don't want someone to just tell me which side wins. I want to know what it would cost to debunk the intuition as a projection of the very memory-anticipation machinery Augustine is describing, what it would cost to amend the theory so it's only a claim about experienced time while physical time is left to the physicists, and whether there's a reading on which both can be kept. Is the mind-dependence claim actually doing metaphysical work, or is it a phenomenology of time perception dressed up as metaphysics? And if I keep the intuition, does Augustine just collapse into presentism, or can his view survive as a claim about lived time? Give me the strongest version of each move and tell me which one you'd actually make.
