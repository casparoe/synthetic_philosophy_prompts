In the famous passage from the Zhuangzi, the author dreams he is a butterfly and, upon waking, wonders whether he is a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. Imagine a structured variant. Suppose the transition is fully symmetrical and repeatable: each time he falls asleep in either condition he wakes into the other condition with complete, vivid memories appropriate to that condition, and each transition feels exactly like waking from a dream. There is no first-person mark distinguishing the waking state from the dreaming state, and no final awakening. Assess how this variant changes the standard philosophical analyses of dream scepticism. In particular, does the iterative symmetry make the doubt materially stronger than the one-off version, or does it simply dramatise the same problem? Does it undermine any residual Cartesian asymmetry between waking and dreaming, and if so, what would still justify treating the waking state as privileged? Or does repeating the episode reveal that the original puzzle was not really about evidence at all? Be precise about which features of the original case carry the argument.
