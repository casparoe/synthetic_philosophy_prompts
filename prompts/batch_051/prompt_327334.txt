I help run a small adult debate club, and next month I'm proposing to the club's steering committee that we add a scored competitive season. The format I have in mind is one-on-one argument matches with the topic and side assigned by lot, a judge scoring clarity, responsiveness, and fair concessions, and a standings table with promotion and relegation between divisions. My thesis is that treating argument as something you can visibly win or lose in a well-run league would improve public reasoning rather than cheapen it: the format would reward actually tracking what an opponent said and conceding what is true, and it would let spectators watch strong arguers get beaten by stronger ones instead of by whoever sounds most confident. The committee is not friendly to this. Two members are former high-school debate coaches who will say competitive debate already exists and mainly teaches speed, gamesmanship, and defending a side you were assigned rather than thinking. One is a lawyer who will say reasoning is already scored through cross-examination, and that a league would just import the vices of sports without the physical skill. The rest are adult learners who joined to get better at disagreeing with spouses and coworkers and worry the whole thing would become aggressive and performative. Prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way these members would phrase them, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, where my reply is weakest, and the objection I'm hoping nobody raises.
