I'm a software engineer at a fintech company; I work on the model that flags accounts for manual review because they may be involved in fraud or sanctions. I have no formal background in philosophy and I have not read the academic literature on interpretability or explanation; my position comes from several years of watching human analysts either override the model or rubber-stamp it, and from reading through our post-incident reviews. Next month I am presenting to our ML platform group and product leadership a proposal for the next version of the account-review model: we should train and evaluate for the legibility of outputs, so that an analyst can look at what the model presents and reconstruct why it flagged the account well enough to catch mistakes, and we should be willing to accept a small, measured drop in standalone accuracy to get that. My argument, in rough form, is this. We already hold analysts accountable for every account we restrict, which means the organisation is effectively betting on their ability to catch model errors; an analyst can only catch errors if they genuinely understand the basis of a flag; and legibility is something we can supervise and therefore train for, because experienced analysts can grade whether the rationale a model presents matches the true basis of a decision. I have been calling this property legibility, and I want the room to treat it as a first-class objective alongside accuracy rather than as documentation produced afterwards.

The room will be mostly applied machine learning engineers and data scientists, plus our product lead and two engineering managers, all of whom sign off on the quarterly metric review. What they care about, concretely, is precision and recall on our evaluation sets, false-positive rates, throughput, latency, and shipping on schedule. They already believe we have paid our explainability tax: we produce SHAP values on request and the model ships with a model card. Several of them will hear legibility as a synonym for slowing the roadmap in order to build a story generator. To be clear about the proposal, I am not suggesting we discard the current model, and I am not suggesting a post-hoc explanation layer bolted on afterwards while training is left untouched; I am suggesting that the supervised objective itself changes, and that our evaluations should include legibility measures in the same gate as accuracy.
