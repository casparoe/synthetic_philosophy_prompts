So I've got this rule I started following after the whole Santa and tooth fairy thing, and I want to know if it's defensible or if I'm just holding a grudge. When I catch an adult deliberately telling me something false "for my own good" — Santa, the tooth fairy, the elf that watches you — I don't just stop believing them about that one topic. I permanently lower my general trust in them by a fixed amount, not to zero, for everything else they tell me, and I don't fully restore it just because they admit the lie later. Last week my mom told me my uncle had lost his job, and I caught myself only half-believing her, not because I had any reason to think she was wrong, but purely because she lied to me about the tooth fairy for years when I was little. My friend says that's crazy, because a person can be totally honest about factual stuff and still do the Santa thing — it's more like a game or a cultural script than a lie. But I don't see why the two are so seperate: a lie is a lie, and now I know she's willing to lie to me when she thinks it's kind. Is my rule defensible? And if it is, how do I even pick the size of the discount — is there a principled amount, or is any fixed number just arbitrary?
