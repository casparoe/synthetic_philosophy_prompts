One of my friends is deep into this self-help stuff about treating your future self like a stranger. His line is that you should predict whether you'll go to the gym tomorrow the same way you'd predict whether a coworker will show up to a 7am meeting: base rates, track records, the works. He uses that analogy to argue you can and should assign precise probabilties to your own choices -- like, 70% I'll flake on the gym -- and that refusing to do this is just special-pleading because we're too tender about our own agency. I'm suspicious, because I've read that deliberation crowds out prediction: while you're actually deciding what to do, treating the outcome as a chancy event you're forecasting is somehow off, maybe incoherent. Audit the future-self-as-stranger analogy for me. What maps onto what (a table is fine), where the mapping breaks, which features of predicting another person it quietly imports that self-prediction lacks, and whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the conclusion doesn't survive, is there still a limited way to assign probabilities to your own future choices, and what would a better analogy be? Keep it under 800 words, American English.
