I spent thirty years doing lab work before I retired, and I've only recently started reading philosophy seriously. One idea I keep coming back to is that rationality is less like a body of facts and more like a martial art. The picture, as I understand it, is that you train on small artificial drills — Fermi problems, calibration quizes, pre-mortems, reference class forecasting, maybe probabilistic reasoning games — the way a karate student drills katas, and the hope is that the skill transfers to messy real-world decisions. I like the picture. But my lab instincts say this is a metaphor that has never been forced to specify its own testable content.

So I want you to treat it as a testable claim and design a study. Give me the treatment and control conditions in enough detail to run the thing, including an active control that controls for motivation and placebo effects, the outcome measures, and a clear statement of what result would count as real transfer rather than just improvement on the drills themselves. Then tell me what even a positive result would not settle: whether we are looking at one general rationality faculty or a bag of micro-skills, whether the kata framing earns its keep, whether far transfer is any better than ordinary practice effects, and how long the effect would have to last before the martial-art claim is really vindicated. I'd also like your honest read on whether the martial-art language is doing real work or is just a vivid way of saying practice helps.
