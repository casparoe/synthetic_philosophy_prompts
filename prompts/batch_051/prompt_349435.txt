I serve on my city's public art commission. Our most treasured work, a cast-bronze fountain by a major early twentieth-century sculptor, has corroded to the point that continued outdoor display will ruin it within a decade. A foundry has offered, at no cost to the city, to produce an exact replica from detailed moulds and scans of the original and install it on the same site; conservators agree it would be visually indistinguishable from the original. The artist's foundation objects that what the public would then see would not be the object the artist made, and that the city would be presenting a reproduction in place of the original. Alternatively, the original could be conserved and moved indoors, with a plainly labelled replica placed outdoors. Our enabling statute directs the commission to preserve and maintain the city's public art for the benefit of present and future generations. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete course of action, give the reasons, and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, and make the first item the course of action you recommend.
