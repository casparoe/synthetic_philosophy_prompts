Consider a decision under complete ignorance: an agent knows the finite set of actions available to her, the finite set of mutually exclusive and exhaustively specified states of the world, and the payoff she would receive for every action–state pair, but she has no information that warrants assigning probabilities to any of the states. For present purposes, set aside rules that respond to ignorance by stipulating probabilities, such as the principle of insufficient reason; the comparison I care about is between two rules that work directly from the payoff matrix. The decision is one-shot, the payoffs are final, and the agent cannot influence which state obtains. I would like you to build a case for the following position: in this setting, minimax regret is less rationally defensible than maximin. This is a deliberately unfashionable conclusion, because maximin's practice of rejecting an action on the strength of its single worst possible payoff is usually treated as the paradigm of fanatical caution, while the guiding thought behind minimax regret—that one should avoid choices one is likely to look back on as having left too much on the table—strikes many people as obviously sensible.

Please write a single sustained argument for the position, not a survey of decision rules. Steelman minimax regret before criticizing it. Your argument should operate at the level of what information an agent under complete ignorance is entitled to use: examine the role of the per-state best-payoff benchmark on which regret is defined, and ask whether that benchmark is the kind of thing such an agent can justifiably treat as action-guiding. Also address the sensitivity of the two rules to the menu of available actions—in particular, whether the ranking of two actions can be changed by the presence of a third action that the rule in question itself would never recommend, and what such menu-dependence would imply about regret as a guide under ignorance. Keep the focus on the abstract decision problem; you may mention maximin reasoning in Rawls's original position only where it directly illuminates the decision rule.

End by telling me what would change your mind: identify the consideration, result, or constructed example most likely to lead you to reverse the verdict and conclude that minimax regret is the better rule for this setting, and identify the step in your argument that you regard as most vulnerable.
