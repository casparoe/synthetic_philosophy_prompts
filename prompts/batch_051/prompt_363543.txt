Two people at a residents' meeting got into it over a proposal to switch our local council elections from first-past-the-post to party-list proportional representation with a 5% threshold. I was standing nearby and got roped in as referee, but I honestly couldn't sort it out. (1) Marcus said FPTP is better because each ward gets one councillor you can hold personally responsible; under a party list you can't throw the rascal out, you just shuffle your vote and the party decides who sits. (2) Ruth replied that this accountability is mostly notional: in most wards the result never changes, so incumbents only worry about a few hundered swing voters, and every vote cast for a losing candidate does nothing to the balance of the council. Under PR, she said, nearly every vote feeds into the seat count, so you can vote for the party you actually want. (3) Marcus answered that PR hands seats to small parties that couldn't win anywhere on their own, and those parties then hold coalition leverage out of proportion to their support; a threshold just sets a minimum size for the parties you'll end up courting. (4) Ruth said that leverage still depends on other parties agreeing to work with them, whereas FPTP regularly gives an outright majority on a minority of the vote, which she called more disproportionate than any threshold party. (5) Marcus then said politics is local: a councillor chosen by the central party list has little reason to fight for a new bus route or school, while a ward councillor's re-election depends on it. Ruth replied that this isn't about the voting system but about electing individuals rather than parties, and a system like STV could keep local multi-member wards with ranked ballots while fixing the proportionality. They asked me to adjudicate the whole exchange. Please go through it move by move, say who's right about what at each step, and who is more right overall. Please answer in British English.
