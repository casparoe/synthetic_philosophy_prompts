What I want is a disciplined mapping: who the players were (Leibniz answering through Clarke is fine), what exactly counts as the evidence in the bucket experiment, what the live relationist replies were in the early 1700s, and which of them the bucket actually forecloses versus merely makes weird. Then go in the other direction: does the episode actually support substantivalism, or does it mostly just show that relationism lacked the geometric machinery to say what it wanted? Finish with whether the later developments—Mach's relative-to-the-distant-matter answer, then the modern picture where absolute velocity is dropped but spacetime structure still does genuine work—should count as a win for relationism, substantivalism, or a disolution of the original question.
