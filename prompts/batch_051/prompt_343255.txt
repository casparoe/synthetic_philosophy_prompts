My advisor in experimental philosophy has been running a seminar on the folk concept of intentional action, and she has announced her own verdicts on a battery of vignettes. When a company chairman approves a profitable new program that will also damage the environment, and he says he does not care about the environmental damage because profit is his only concern, she says he intentionally damaged the environment. When the same chairman approves a profitable program that will also benefit the environment, and he says he does not care about that benefit for the same reason, she says he did not intentionally benefit the environment. When a factory manager dumps chemical waste into a protected river to avoid paying for proper disposal, foreseeing that the fish will be killed, she says he intentionally polluted the river. When a store manager's streamlined checkout happens to generate a small donation to a food bank, a side effect she neither aimed at nor opposed, she says the manager did not intentionally benefit the food bank. Finally, when a landlord illegally changes the locks to force out a tenant, knowing the tenant will end up in a shelter, she says he intentionally rendered the tenant homeless.

Next week she will offer a new vignette, and I want to predict her judgment. An engineer must install a backup generator at a hospital to satisfy the fire code; the only compliant placement will make the ambulance bay narrow enough that some arrivals will foreseeably be delayed. The engineer knows this, judges that not installing the generator would be worse and would itself violate the code, and reports that she regrets the delays. Here the side effect is a harm rather than a benefit, but the engineer is following a rule rather than violating one, and she regrets the outcome rather than accepting it indifferently. What is she most likely to say about whether the engineer intentionally caused the ambulance delays? More generally: which rule best captures her earlier verdicts — the moral valence of the side effect, whether a salient norm was violated, whether the outcome expressed the agent's values, or something else — which of those rules fit the record equally well, which earlier vignette is the closest analog, and what additional vignette would cleanly settle which rule she is actually running?
