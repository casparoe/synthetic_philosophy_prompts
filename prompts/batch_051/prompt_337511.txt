My physics teacher recently argued that the wavefunction is not a real physical thing but only a compact summary of an observer's expectations, much as a weather forecast is not itself a weather system but a summary of what one should expect the weather to do. She treats the analogy as decisive: just as it would be a kind of category mistake to ask whether this morning's forecast exists out in the atmosphere, it is likewise a mistake to ask whether the wavefunction is real. I gather that this is a live dispute between psi-epistemic views, which hold that the quantum state is information about the world rather than a thing in the world, and psi-ontic views, which hold that the quantum state is itself part of reality. I want to test how far the forecast analogy can really carry the psi-epistemic conclusion. Could you give a correspondence table — what corresponds to the weather system, the forecast, the updated forecast, and the forecast's being right or wrong — then say where the analogy breaks down, which of those breakdowns matter for my teacher's argument and which are harmless, and whether her conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table? If the analogy fails, is there a better one, and what would it license? Please also say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
