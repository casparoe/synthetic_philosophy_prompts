I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about assigning probabilities to my own choices, by running a list of cases past myself and seeing which ones feel legitimate and which feel like category mistakes. I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures my gut, and which of my calls it forces me to drop.

Here are the cases.

1. I'm about 95% sure I won't take up smoking before I'm fifty. That feels unproblematic -- it's a forecast about a person whose track record I know, and saying it doesn't change anything.

2. I've already decided to take the train tomorrow rather than drive. I can say with near certainty I'll take the train. Also fine; the choice is settled.

3. I'm outside two cafes, genuinely undecided. I don't think there's any probability of my choosing the left one that I could honestly state. Whatever number I said would be made up, and if I said 60% for left I'd have half-decided it, so it wasn't really a prediction.

4. But I can predict I won't keep to my plan to run five times a week next month -- I'd put maybe 30% on my keeping it -- and that feels fine and useful. It doesn't corrupt the plan; if anything it's the only honest way to plan. Yet that's also a prediction about my own future choices, so why is it statable when the cafe case isn't?

5. I also think it's fine to predict I'll hit snooze tomorrow morning, maybe 80%, because I know that the decision gets made half-asleep by a different version of me and isn't really being made now.

6. But on Monday a friend asked what the probability was that I'd finish my essay that night, and I found I couldn't give a number I believed. I said 40%, but it felt like a performance -- I was reporting on my energy and distractions, not on a choice I was still in the middle of.

The maddening pair is 3 and 4: same kind of thing on the surface, me making a future choice, but opposite verdicts. I can't tell wether 6 is just 3 with more social pressure.

Is there one account of first-person probability that gets all of these, or nearly all of them? Tell me which verdicts it can't accommodate and whether I should revise them. And what does the account say about the case I keep going back and forth on: I'm halfway through deciding whether to set up a monthly donation, and I ask myself how likely I am to actually follow through -- is there a real probability there, or does any number I assign just become an input to the decision rather than a reading of it?
