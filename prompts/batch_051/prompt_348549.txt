I am writing up a short statement of my views on normativity and the attitudes for a workshop, and I would like someone to audit the package for coherence rather than defend the individual pieces. Here is the set of commitments, stated as precisely as I can.

(1) The basic normative assessment of an attitude is fittingness. An attitude is fitting just when its object has the properties the attitude is answerable to: admiration is fitting toward the admirable, fear toward the fearsome, and belief toward what one's evidence supports.

(2) I hold a fitting-attitudes analysis of value: for something to be good is for it to be a fitting object of a favorable attitude, such as admiration, respect, or desire.

(3) The wrong-kind reasons problem strikes me as genuine. If a powerful being credibly threatens to destroy everything I care about unless I admire it, then there is a consideration that counts in favor of my admiring it, but that consideration does not make the being admirable.

(4) I draw the right-kind/wrong-kind distinction by grounding. Right-kind reasons for an attitude are object-given: they are grounded in features of the thing the attitude is about. Wrong-kind reasons are state-given: they are grounded in features of the attitude-state itself, or in its expected consequences.

(5) I think wrong-kind reasons are real reasons. They count in favor of taking steps to bring about the attitude, and I accept the standard reasons framework for attitudinal oughts: what one ought, all things considered, to admire, fear, or believe is fixed by the balance of the reasons relevant to that attitude. In the demon case, then, it would be rational to try to bring myself to admire the being, even though that admiration would be unfitting.

(6) For belief, however, I am an evidentialist: only evidential, truth-relevant considerations count as reasons for belief. Pragmatic considerations, such as the benefits of believing or the costs of failing to believe, are never reasons to believe, not even wrong-kind ones.

I cannot tell which of these are merely in appearance in conflict and which commit me to more than I want. Please go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only seem to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what the cheapest repair for each would be and what it would cost, and tell me whether there is a single revision that would clear up the most at once.
