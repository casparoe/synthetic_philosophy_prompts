In my graduate seminar on causation and explanation, we recently worked through the interventionist account of causation associated with James Woodward. After the session, two classmates—Elena and Marcus—fell into an extended argument about a familiar worry, namely whether the account is circular, and they ended up asking me to referee. I found I couldn't, so I am hoping for a careful adjudication.

Elena argued that the circularity is genuine and damaging. On her reading, Woodward characterizes causation in terms of what would happen under an intervention on the cause variable, but an intervention on X with respect to Y is itself specified in causal terms: it must be a cause of X, it must not influence Y along a route that bypasses X, and it must be independent of other causes of Y. The right-hand side, she said, therefore presupposes the very causal facts it is supposed to illuminate. She added that this is not a merely formal complaint, because the account's advertised strength is that it ties causation to manipulation and experiment; if manipulation is spelled out through causal relations, the account no longer tells us what causation consists in. Marcus replied that the circularity is conceded and benign. Woodward's project, he argued, is not to reduce causation to non-causal vocabulary but to give a non-reductive, systematic characterization of how causal claims connect with claims about manipulation, invariance, and control. On that reading, the causal characterization of interventions is no more fatal than a recursive definition of a formal notion. What matters, he said, is that the account imposes substantive constraints: a variable merely correlated with the effect, or one that affects the effect only along a route independent of the cause, does not count as an intervention.

Elena pushed back that even granting the non-reductive project, the epistemic problem remains. Scientists use randomized experiments precisely to learn whether X causes Y, yet a random assignment counts as an intervention only given background causal assumptions—for instance, that the assignment is not confounded with other causes and does not affect the outcome except through X. If those assumptions are themselves causal, the account either regresses or requires an independent way of identifying causal structure, which is exactly what the manipulationist story was supposed to explain. So, she argued, the account is less useful for causal discovery than its experimental rhetoric suggests. Marcus answered that she was conflating an account of what causal claims mean, or of what causation is, with an account of how causes are discovered. Woodward, he said, can grant that discovery is bootstrapped from prior causal knowledge without undermining the claim that a relationship is causal just in case it is invariant under interventions of the right sort. He added that every major theory of causation faces a version of this problem—counterfactual theories need a similarity relation that is partly causal, process theories appeal to a privileged notion of a genuine process—so the objection must show why Woodward is worse off. Elena responded that if the account retreats to a purely semantic thesis, it preserves consistency but gives up much of its payoff, since the attraction of the view is precisely that it explains why intervening is such a reliable way to learn about causes. She also suggested that the difficulty appears inside the formalism itself, because the surgical intervention defined in the graphical framework already presupposes the causal graph whose edges the account is trying to characterize. Marcus replied that the formalism is not meant to characterize causation from outside; it is a language for representing causal systems, within which interventions can be defined precisely. The circularity, he concluded, lies at the level of the concept rather than the level of the models, and a non-reductive account is entitled to illuminate causal notions in terms of one another.

Could you explain, point by point, where Elena and Marcus are each right and wrong, what their exchange as it stands is missing, and who is more right overall?
