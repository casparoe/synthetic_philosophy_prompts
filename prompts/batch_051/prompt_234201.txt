The position I keep coming back to is the standard money-pump argument: an agent with cyclic preferences — preferring A to B, B to C, and C to A — can be led through a sequence of trades that returns them to their starting point while they pay a small amount at each step, and this vulnerability shows that cyclic preferences are irrational. I find the argument genuinely compelling, perhaps too compelling, and I want to know how exposed it actually is.

Play it straight and give me the full map of what would count against the claim that vulnerability to a money pump demonstrates the irrationality of cyclic preferences. I am not looking for a bibliography, reading list, or citations; where you mention proposals such as sophisticated choice, resolute choice, incomplete preferences, parity, or time-indexed preferences, explain them in your own words. In particular, I want you to sort out a prior question the argument tends to slide over: whether its proper target is a cycle in an agent's preferences at a single time, or a sequence of preference changes over time, and how much of the argument's force depends on that difference. Be careful not to conflate a money pump with a Dutch book: a Dutch book is supposed to expose probabilistic incoherence at a time, while a money pump exploits choices made over time, and I want to know what the diachronicity is doing for the argument — including whether the argument can be restated so that it does not depend on the agent's being myopic.

It should also say what a committed defender of the money-pump argument would concede as counting against it, not just what critics allege; whether the argument has been insulated by qualifications and whether that insulation is a strength or a problem; and which escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible. I would personally be most moved by a formal result showing that some class of rational agents can hold cyclic preferences without exposing themselves to exploitable losses, but I want to know whether that should outweigh the standard argument rather than merely complicate it.
