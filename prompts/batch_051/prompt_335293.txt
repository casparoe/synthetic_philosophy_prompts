heres a rule I've been following for years whenever I have to put a number on a one-off thing—how long the kitchen reno will take, whether my buddy's bar survives its first year, how risky my dad's surgery is. I find the narrowest reference class I can specify in advance: same city, same age, same health history, same business type, whatever. If that class has even one past case, I use its rate and ignore all the broader stats; if it has none, I widen one step and repeat. My friend says this is the reference class problem and I'm solving it by falling in love with whichever class is small enough to feel specific—that a class of three is just an anecdote with extra steps, and that I'm picking the boundaries after the fact to make the story I want. is my rule defensible? how should a person trade off how narrow a class is against how many cases are in it, and is there any principled reason to ever prefer a one-case class over a hundred-case one?
