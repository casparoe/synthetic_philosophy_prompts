I host a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest is an epistemic logician known for defending awareness-style semantics (the explicit/implicit knowledge split) as the best response to the problem of logical omniscience. I don't want the usual "what is logical omniscience and why is it a problem" warm-up. Draft eight to ten questions that go straight at the view. At minimum, include: (1) a question pressing the line that putting all the idealization into "implicit knowledge" just relocates the problem instead of solving it, with a follow-up for the reply that implicit knowledge is a harmless idealization and explicit knowledge is what does the modeling work; (2) a question about awareness of unawareness—why a bounded agent can't know that she's unaware of something, and what that impossibility says about the model's realism, with a follow-up for the reply that its a feature, not a bug; (3) a question about whether "explicit knowledge" that isn't closed under modus ponens still counts as knowledge or is really a kind of belief, with a follow-up for the reply that epistemic logic is descriptive, not normative. Then add however many more you think the literature would push hardest on—for example, how awareness sets are supposed to be assigned without being ad hoc, or what would have to be true of real agents for the model to be empirically disconfirmed.
