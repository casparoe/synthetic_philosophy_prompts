I'm in a reading group on classical Chinese ethics, and the Mozi-vs-Confucian split keeps coming up, but people talk past eachother constantly. I want to see if the two camps can actually produce something together instead of another sterile "would you save your mother or a stranger" fight. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between (a) a Mohist committed to impartial care (jian ai), understood as the view that our action-guiding concern for others should not be graded by closeness, and (b) a Confucian defender of graded care, understood as the view that concern and obligation properly have degrees, with greater care owed to those in closer relationships. The statement should be a document both could put their names to.

Please give me: (1) a numbered "agreed claims" section that is non-vacuous — what the dispute is not about, what both reject, and what both can accept about human motivation and about the need for social order. Include in this section a joint claim about the difference between care as emotional warmth and care as practical priority, if you think both can sign it. (2) A disagreements table. Each row should give a point of difference phrased so each side accepts the wording of its own view; the crux that difference turns on; and a case or argument both would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Make at least one row about famine or disaster relief, one about filial piety and public office, and one about whether the Confucian's graded concern is an ideal or a training stage. (3) A short paragraph saying which parts of their dispute you think are verbal rather than substantive, and what disagreement remains once that is cleared away. Don't cite any texts or supply a bibliography — just write it as if the two participants drafted it today.
