I'm running a reading group on moral particularism and generalism this term, and I've noticed how rarely the debate pauses to say what an adequate theory of the place of principles in morality is actually supposed to deliver. Some participants demand exceptionless rules, others demand context-sensitivity at any cost, and others care mainly about whether ethical decision tools perform well in novel cases. Before we spend another few sessions comparing positions, I want the target specified first.

You are a professional moral philosopher with a background in both normative ethics and meta-ethics. Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the role of moral principles must meet, where adequacy is relative to explaining the underlying structure of reasons and how they combine in particular cases, not merely to drafting a code of professional conduct. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it and say whether it is a hard constraint or a negotiable preference of one camp. I especially want to know whether any pair or subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable and, if so, how the tension is usually managed; which single requirement does the most to exclude the familiar views; and, briefly, how a particularism in the Dancy mould, a Rossian moderate generalism, and a deflationary view on which principles are only heuristics or summaries of past decisions fare against your list.

One wrinkle I'd like you to weigh explicitly: whether a generalist can treat principles as default rules carrying exception clauses without quietly becoming a particularist, and what the particularist must then say about how reasons combine across cases. I would rather see that treated as a constraint to state than as a debate to settle. Before writing, take some time to brainstorm the demands people implicitly rely on in this literature and to separate genuine requirements from sectarian loyalties. In your answer, use British English. Begin with your bottom line in the very first sentence — the one requirement or conflict your list shows does the most work — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the task. A table is fine for the list itself, but keep the reasoning behind each requirement in prose.
