You are a professional philosopher specializing in existentialism, particularly the Sartrean tradition and the standard objections pressed against it. Think carefully, and begin by briefly setting out the strongest version of the theory and the strongest version of the intuition before you try to adjudicate or reconcile them.

I am drawn to the existentialist doctrine that existence precedes essence because of its systematic virtues: it offers a single, unified account of freedom, responsibility, authenticity, and self-deception; it refuses to ground ethics in a fixed human nature; and it captures the sense that I am always answerable for what I make of myself. But I also have a firm intuition that I am not sheer nothingness awaiting self-creation. I find myself already going on in specific, unchosen ways — a body, a temperament, a history, uneven and unearned capacities — and some possible projects would not merely be difficult but would feel like betrayals of who I am. That seems to presuppose some prior self to betray. I am also troubled by the charge of incoherence: if there is no determinate self before my choices, what exactly is the I that chooses, and what are my choices answerable to? Is the talk of consciousness as a lack or nothingness a workable theory of the subject, or a vivid metaphor doing all the work?

I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a reading on which both survive — for example, facticity (the unchosen givens of my situation) being genuinely constraining without those givens adding up to an essence. Walk me through what each move costs and what would justify making it, rather than merely recommending one.

Do not base your answer on citations, textual exegesis, or a reading list; the philosophical substance should carry it. At the end, say how confident you are in the central claims of your answer, and single out the point you are least sure of.
