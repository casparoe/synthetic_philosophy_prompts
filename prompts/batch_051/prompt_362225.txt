Six of us run a small family foundation. The trust instrument, drafted in 1974, says grants may be made only "for the relief or prevention of suffering among sentient beings." A local land trust wants money to restore a drained salt marsh: about 600 acres re-flooded, and their proposal boasts that it will be habitat for millions of insects, fish, and birds. One trustee (she's read alot about wild animal welfare) says the deed forbids the grant, and may even require the opposite—a marsh is basically a machine for making short, miserable lives that end in starvation, parasites, or being eaten, so on the plain text we should be destroying habitat rather than restoring it. The rest of us find that ridiculous but can't find wording in the instrument that blocks it. Walk me through what a literal reading, the purpose of the instrument, and what the 1974 grantors would have said each imply about whether the deed permits, forbids, or requires this grant; then say what the board should actually decide. If your view is that the deed is simply silent, say what principle should govern the decision instead, and whether we'd be following the deed or adding to it.
