I'm putting together a handout for a reading group that's mostly vision scientists plus a couple philosophers, and I want a decision tree that sorts people into what they actually think about mental imagery. The group knows the standard Kosslyn-vs-Pylyshyn story, but I don't want the tree to use labels like pictorial, propositional, or depictive until the leaf anyway, because the whole point is to get people to answer about cases and claims rather than pick their favorite camp.

Here's the angle I care about. People in this debate constantly run together three separate commitments: (a) that introspection about image-like experience is a trustworthy guide to what's going on underneath, (b) that mental images have a depictive representational format in the computational sense, and (c) that imagery actually does the causal work in tasks like mental rotation or scanning rather than just riding along. I want the tree to force these apart.

Leaves I need, with names at the end: strong depictive realism, functional depictivism (depictive format matters but the picture metaphor is loose), propositional/descriptivism, epiphenomenal imagery, and sensorimotor/enactivism. If your tree can't separate two of them, say so and say what extra question would. Also flag any combination of answers that's logically possible but empty, and give a one-line guess why no one lives there (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?).

Keep it to eight nodes max. Each node has to be a concrete case or a crisp yes/no claim, phrased so someone who has never heard of Kosslyn or Pylyshyn can answer it. And make the first split the one that actually does the most work in the literature, not the most intuitive philosophical one if those are different.

Oh and I'd rather the questions never ask people whether they think in pictures. They'll all say yes.
