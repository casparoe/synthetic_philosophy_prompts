I'm taking a food ethics seminar this semester, and I'm also the undergrad rep on our campus dining sustainability subcommittee. We have a concrete fight right now, and I want to use the memo I write for class as my actual draft for the meeting. The issue: a student group called the Salvage Kitchen wants permission to run a twice-weekly "rescued food" pop-up in the student center. They would take unserved dining-hall food that would otherwise be composted or thrown out—including chicken, eggs, cheese, and other animal products—and also accept eggs and occasional meat from a local farm animal rescue, where the animals died naturally or were euthanized by a vet. The food is free and clearly labeled. The subcommittee is split. Some say this is harmless or even good: no additional animal suffering is created, it reduces waste, and it feeds broke students. Others say the university hosting a table under its name sends the message that animals are still a normal resource, and that this kind of freeganism just makes animal use seem a little more conscientious without challenging the system itself.

In class we read Bentham's An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation, and I'm paraphrasing here, but he makes this comparison that a full-grown horse or dog is more rational and can communicate better than a day-old infant, so if reason or speech is supposed to draw the line for who counts morally, the line won't stay put. The point I keep thinking about is whether the only thing that matters is suffering, and since the animals whose byproducts would be used are already dead, maybe the issue is purely about institutional consequences and social meaning rather than about harm to the animals themselves. One committee member keeps saying Bentham would obviously sign off on the pop-up; another says his way of framing the question is exactly what hides the social meaning of eating animals. I need to write a short decision memo for the subcommittee—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with reasons the other side could actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest point against my recommendation. It should be concrete enough to act on, including any labeling or sourcing rules if I recommend conditions.
