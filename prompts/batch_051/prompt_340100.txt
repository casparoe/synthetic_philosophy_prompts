So my mom moved into assisted living last fall and I've basically become an amature sociologist of the staff. Two aides can do the exact same morning routine for her and one of them respects her and the other doesn't. I don't just mean one is nicer; I mean something about whether they treat her like a person with a point of view, from the inside. I want a working definition of that kind of respect—the attitude kind rather than the holding-the-door kind—that nails these cases:

1. The aide who says good morning, uses her name, asks whether she wants the blue sweater or the green one, and actually waits for her to decide.
2. The aide who does the same routine just as well, but talks about her to me as if she isn't in the room, and moves her walker out of the way without a word.
3. The chaplain who looks her in the eye, holds her hand, and clearly means well, but he cycles through the same handful of phrases with everyone and doesn't remember her from one week to the next.
4. The activities director who knows every resident's name and backstory, always asks the perfect question, and seems warm—but I've never seen him keep a conversation going when no one is watching, and I can't tell whether he respects people or just works the room.
5. My sister, who loves mom more than anyone, but on visits she's half on her phone, finishes mom's sentences, and reorganizes the closet without asking because she knows best.
6. The tablet thing my brother installed that asks about mom's grandkids by name, never interrupts, and pays perfect attention, even though there's obviously nobody home in it.

My gut says 1 is clearly respect, 2 isn't, and 6 isn't either even though it acts perfectly. I go back and forth on 3, 4, and 5. So: give me the definition, run it through all six, tell me where it gives a weird answer or bombs, and either revise it or defend the weird verdict. Then tell me which of your calls you're least sure about.
