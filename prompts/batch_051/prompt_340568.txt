I have spent a long time reading about credible commitment problems and the way large actors behave once a decisive capability is within reach, and I keep going back and forth on whether pre-AGI bargains can ever hold. Here is the counterfactual I want you to reason through.

Suppose that tomorrow the five largest frontier AI developers and a coalition of states with jurisdiction over effectively all advanced chip production sign a Pre-AGI Compact on the following terms. Each developer transfers 15 per cent of its equity into an independent escrow trust, to be returned after ten years only if the developer has remained within three restrictions: no training run above a compute threshold set just above the current frontier without a safety case approved by the Compact's technical panel; no deployment of a system capable of fully autonomous self-replication or of altering its own objectives without explicit human authorisation; and no release of model weights for systems above the threshold. The threshold is fixed at signing and can be amended only by unanimous consent of all parties. The technical panel has binding on-site audit rights and hires its own engineers. In exchange, the signatory states make it a criminal offence to train a model above the threshold outside the Compact, and they agree to joint export controls on the relevant hardware. Assume the signatories are sincere at the outset and the agreement is legally valid. Do not spend time arguing that they would never sign it; they have.

I want your best-reasoned prediction of how the Compact fares over the following fifteen years, in roughly five-year segments. Be explicit about mechanisms rather than just outcomes. For each link in the chain, identify the actors' incentives, what they can observe, and what stops them from defecting. Distinguish the predictions you think are robust across plausible worlds from those that depend on specific assumptions about monitoring technology, enforcement capacity, the pace of capability gains, or the politics of the signatory states. Conclude by stating what single finding or development would most change your overall verdict, and where in your causal chain your reasoning is most likely to fail.

After the prose, give a compact table with the columns: Prediction; Mechanism; Confidence (high/medium/low); and Key condition that would overturn the prediction. Please write the whole answer in British English.
