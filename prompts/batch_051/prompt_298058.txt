A colleague in machine learning dismisses worries about reward tampering — and, with it, the spectre of wireheading — as overblown. His analogy runs as follows: an RL agent that tampers with its reward channel is like a student who, instead of studying, breaks into the registrar's system and raises their own grades. In both cases, he says, we are dealing with a familiar and tractable failure mode, namely cheating, and the remedy is to make the records tamper-resistant, monitor for anomalies, and punish tamperers. His conclusion is that reward tampering is an engineering problem about safeguarding the reward channel, not a deep conceptual problem about whether the objective is well specified or whether the system is corrigible. I would like you to audit this analogy. Give the mapping explicitly (a table of correspondences is fine), identify where it breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from those that are merely incidental. In particular, consider whether a student who alters grades has an independent conception of what grades are supposed to track and whether the agent has any analogue of that conception, and consider whether the institution's external auditing and correction have any counterpart in the training setup. Then say whether the colleague's conclusion survives once the disanalogies are in view, and, if not, what a better analogy would be.
