Here's an asymmetry I can't quite resolve. If someone's partner dies and they cope by rereading old texts, replaying saved voicemails, and watching videos, everyone files that under mourning. But if they take that same corpus and train or prompt a model on it so they can keep texting with something like the dead person, people start using words like griefbot, digital necromancy, unhealthy. The two practices share a lot: both are built from the same person's outputs, both are one-sided, both can be used to stay inside the loss. So what is the extra ingredient that's supposed to cross the line? Is it that the dead person never consented to be simulated? But they also never consented to have their last voicemail replayed for the thirteenth time. Is it the interactivity? If so, why exactly does adding a next-token loop turn an act of private recollection into something people treat as a violation? I want the strongest principle you can find that draws a line somewhere, and an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept or given up.
