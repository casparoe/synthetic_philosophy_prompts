Please describe the most important implications of the distinction between gauge and physical degrees of freedom for scientific realism about gauge theories. I am working on a paper about whether the electromagnetic gauge potential and the connection in Yang–Mills theories should be treated as part of the world our best theories describe, or dismissed as redundant surplus structure because gauge-equivalent potentials represent the same physical state. In particular, I want to know whether the Aharonov–Bohm effect, in which an electron's interference pattern shifts even though the magnetic field strength vanishes along its accessible path, forces a realist to count the potential itself as physically real, or whether the effect can be accounted for entirely in terms of gauge-invariant quantities such as the phase differences around closed loops. What, if anything, does the answer imply for the broader dispute between ontic structural realism and more entity-based realism about fields? Give the most important implications rather than a survey of positions. In your answer, say how confident you are in your main claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
