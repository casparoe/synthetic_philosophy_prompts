I would like your credence on a claim about why some ideas fail to circulate, together with a full decomposition of the reasoning that produces it. I will use antimeme to mean an idea whose content itself lowers the chance that it is retained, taken seriously, or passed on: encountering the idea leaves people less likely to remember it, less able to explain it to others, or less willing to entertain it, through a route that is not simply environmental filtering such as censorship, taboo, or social penalty. I am consciously setting aside the science-fiction use of the term and asking you to treat this as a proposed explanatory category in the study of cultural transmission. The proposition I want you to assess is this: antimemes, so understood, form a real explanatory class; that is, some significant ideas are far less present in a culture than they otherwise would be, partly because their own content inhibits retention and retransmission, and this is a genuine causal mechanism rather than a catchy redescription. For determinacy: the claim would be vindicated by one or more clear cases in which content-level self-inhibition is an indispensable part of the best explanation of an idea's absence, and it would be undermined if every candidate case is better explained by environment-side filtering or by the idea being false, useless, or simply costly to entertain.

Please give me an actual number for your credence in that proposition, and then decompose it. I want at least these terms separated out: (1) your probability that the framework of ideas as transmissible units with transmission-relevant content is causally meaningful enough for the question to be well-posed, rather than loose metaphor; (2) conditional on (1), your probability that an idea's content can itself be the primary site of a self-inhibiting mechanism, as opposed to the inhibition necessarily operating through environments and audiences; and (3) conditional on (2), your probability that there are clear actual cases where this mechanism does independent explanatory work, such that the best explanation of some idea's obscurity would be incomplete without it. For each term, give a rough probability, a one-sentence justification, and a note on how confident you are in that specific estimate. Then show how you combine the terms into your final number, and say which term it is most sensitive to.

My own working credence is around 0.6, while a colleague puts it below 0.15 and maintains that antimeme is only an evocative relabeling of familiar failures of memory, motivation, and social pressure. Tell me where you think a 0.6 and a 0.15 person are most likely actually disagreeing. Finally, tell me what evidence, argument, or conceptual clarification would move your credence most, and single out the one point in your analysis you are least sure of.
