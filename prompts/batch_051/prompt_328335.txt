A friend who has been reading formal logic alongside theology tells me he can show that the doctrine of the Trinity is logically incoherent. He sets out the argument like this:

(1) The Father is God.
(2) The Son is God.
(3) If one thing is identical with a second, and a third thing is identical with that same second, then the first and third are identical with each other.
(4) In (1) and (2), the words "is God" express identity with one and the same being, God.
(5) Therefore the Father is identical with the Son.
(6) The Father and the Son are distinct persons, so the Father is not identical with the Son.
(7) Hence the doctrine that affirms (1), (2), and (6) — that three persons are one God — entails a contradiction and cannot be true.

I find (7) unacceptable: I want to keep the doctrine, and in particular I am not willing to abandon (1), (2), or the distinctness of the persons. So where does the argument go wrong, if it does? I would like a diagnosis rather than a defence of the doctrine. Name the step you would reject, explain why it looked plausible, survey the main ways of escaping the contradiction and the price each one pays, and then say which escape you judge least costly — or whether the argument simply goes through. Please write your answer in British English.
