I'm a PhD student in social philosophy and I've been reading some older, slightly dusty literature on supposed differences in folk morality between Germany and the United States — the rough idea being that one tradition treats rule-following and the maintenance of order as themselves moral achievements, while the other treats individual conscience and situated judgement as the higher authority, with rules being more like default guidelines. I'd like you to write a dialogue of maybe 900 to 1200 words between two fictional academics (say a German sociologist and an American philosopher) who try, in good faith, to work out wether either of these outlooks has the better of it.

Don't make it a national stereotypes contest — both characters should be competent, charitable, and responsive to the other's actual claims. They should press each other on concrete cases where the two outlooks pull apart: for example, an employee who breaks a safety procedure to prevent an accident, someone who refuses to break a minor rule in an emergency out of respect for the practice, or a whistleblower who follows their conscience against an organisation. They should also get into the trickier territory of whether rule-following can itself express conscience, and whether appeals to 'individual conscience' can quietly smuggle in shared rules. Let the dialogue narrow to one precise unresolved issue by the end, rather than ending in a bland 'both sides have a point.' And please write the whole thing in British English.
