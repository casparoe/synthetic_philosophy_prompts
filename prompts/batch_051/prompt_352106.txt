I am a student in a philosophy of mind course, and we have just read Daniel Dennett's "Where Am I?", the story in which he has his brain removed and kept conscious in a vat while his body, controlled over a radio link from the brain, undertakes a dangerous mission, leaving unsettled where he himself is. I would like to push the scenario well beyond the original thought experiment.

Suppose that, starting in 2035, the technology in the story becomes a real option: a person's living brain can be removed, sustained in a life-support capsule, and connected to a body — their own or one they lease — by a low-latency, high-bandwidth radio channel that carries sensory signals to the brain and motor commands back. The channel is a continuous causal link, not a copy or an upload, and there is exactly one brain controlling exactly one body at any time. The procedure is about as safe and affordable as a major elective surgery, and its use spreads first through hazardous occupations and long-distance commuting and then into lifestyle choices. Everything else about the world stays as it is.

Over the next fifty years, how do law, medicine, insurance, families, ordinary language, and the economy adapt? I am not looking for a story or a simple yes or no. For each predicted effect, give the mechanism you think produces it and assign that link a rough confidence. Distinguish predictions that you think are robust across different assumptions about human psychology from those that depend on people's intuitions about embodiment shifting quickly or slowly. In particular, I want to know what happens to the original question — where is the person when brain and body are in different places — once the practice is routine: does it get answered, split into several senses, or quietly avoided?

After the prose, append a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

"overall_trajectory": one or two sentences stating your main-line prediction;
"robust_predictions": an array of objects, each with "prediction" (a one-sentence predicted effect), "mechanism" (a one-sentence explanation of the mechanism), and "confidence" (a number from 0 to 1 for that prediction);
"fragile_predictions": an array in the same format, for predictions that depend on assumptions about how quickly intuitions or institutions adapt;
"sites_of_contention": an array of one-sentence descriptions of the places where law and ordinary language would be under the most strain;
"conditions_for_failure": an array of one-sentence conditions under which the practice would likely be banned or collapse;
"overall_confidence": a number from 0 to 1 indicating your confidence in the overall trajectory.
