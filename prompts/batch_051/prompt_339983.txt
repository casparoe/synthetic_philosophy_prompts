I teach a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and a visitor left me last week with an argument I can't shake. It concerns van Fraassen's Reflection principle, which I've always presented as a coherence condition on your relation to your own future opinions: roughly, your current credence in a proposition should equal the expected value of the credence you will later assign to it, so that you adopt now whatever opinion you are confident you will have then, since your future self is still you, only later and presumably better informed. The visitor says this proves to much, and I've been stuck arranging it as follows.

(1) Reflection says that P_now(A | P_later(A) = r) = r, for any proposition A, any later time, and any r. (2) I can be certain about some future credence of mine without its being based on any evidence. Suppose I know that tomorrow at noon a post-hypnotic suggestion will set my credence at 0.9 that a fair coin, to be flipped after noon, will land heads; the suggestion tells me nothing about the coin, it just sets the dial. (3) Since I'm certain that P_later(heads) = 0.9, Reflection gives P_now(heads | P_later(heads)=0.9) = 0.9, and therefore P_now(heads) = 0.9. (4) But that's absurd: a suggestion I haven't yet been given, which carries no information about the coin, shouldn't reach backward and settle what I ought to believe today about a coin I know to be fair. (5) So Reflection, stated that way, is false. But now the sting: if I reject Reflection, I seem to license the stance "I now take A to degree p, I can see that tomorrow I will take A to degree q, and tomorrow-me is wrong." That same stance looks available when tomorrow-me is right—say, a diagnostic lab report will arrive and will justifiably move me from p to q. Without Reflection, what marks the difference between the lab-report case and the pill case is exactly what's in question, so dogmatism starts to look rationally respectable.

The visitor says Reflection is in an impossible spot: either it forces me to defer to future selves I know are broken, or it gives no purchase at all. I'd like to keep the idea that I should defer to a future self who is better informed, and I'd also like to keep the idea that today's beliefs aren't hostage to tomorrow's pills. I can't see which step chokes. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
