I'm a PhD student in computational social science, and I keep thinking about this one. Suppose a reddit-like site replaced ordinary upvotes/downvotes with a staking system: to upvote a post you have to lock some of your own non-transferable karma, which you get back with interest if the post nets positive over the next 48 hours, and which you lose if it nets negative. Downvotes work symmetrically but are anonymous, while upvote stakes are public. Predict what happens to the quality and epistemic character of the front page after two years, relative to the current setup. I want the chain of reasoning visible: who's willing to vote early and why, how the anonymity asymmetry changes strategic behavior, what new games and coalitions people invent, whether brigading and pile-ons become easier or harder, and which of your predictions are robust versus dependent on assumptions about how fast users adapt and learn to game the thing. If you had to put a number on it, what's your credence that the front page becomes more truth-tracking overall?
