I'm the new member on my university's animal research ethics board, and I got handed the job of drafting our decision memo on a proposed rule that would key the level of ethical review to brain size — or brain-size-corrected-for-body-size (EQ, they call it), or total neuron count, depending which faction you ask. The pro side says it's at least a transparent, operationable proxy for how much an animal can suffer, and it would spare us endless case-by-case fights. The anti side says raw brain mass mostly tracks body size anyway, and the fancier corrections pull in different directions, so we'd overprotect big-brained species we don't understand all that well and underprotect small-brained animals with dense or distributed cognition — bees, corvids, octopuses. They also keep saying neuron counts are a cognitive-ecology variable, not a moral one. Draft the memo I'd circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should give a concrete recommendation (a cutoff, tiers, a moratorium, whatever) and it needs to honestly engage the strongest version of the brain-size criterion, not just knock down the dumbest version of it.
