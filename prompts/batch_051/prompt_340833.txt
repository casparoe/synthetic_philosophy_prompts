A friend and I recently spent a long evening arguing about whether chartered companies like the East India Company were ever genuinely sovereign or merely exercised powers delegated to them by the Crown. I kept insisting that the Company remained a private, profit-seeking corporation whose charter Parliament could revoke at any moment, so calling it sovereign had to be loose metaphor. My friend replied that I was importing modern categories: in early modern legal thought, the state itself was a form of corporation, and sovereignty was a bundle of powers that corporate bodies could hold. That still strikes me as a category error. If sovereignty is what separates the state from a private association, how can a chartered trading company count as sovereign without making that distinction meaningless? Please answer in American English.
