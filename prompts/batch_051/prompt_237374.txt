I keep coming back to the physiological criterion of biological individuality: an organism is a functionally integrated unit whose parts' activities are coordinated for the survival and reproduction of the whole. It feels right to me because it gives a clean, non-arbitrary test — find where the integration boundaries are, and that's the organism. But I also cant shake the intuition that a leafcutter ant colony is an organism, and not by loose analogy: there's a reproductive queen, sterile workers acting like soma, division of labor, colony-level homeostasis, even a kind of collective metabolism with the fungus garden. Same intuition for holobionts — a coral with its symbiotic algae, a lichen's fungus and alga — it looks like one integrated living thing, while the criterion I'm drawn to would call it a community of species. I don't want to be told which side wins. Give me the strongest version of the theory that excludes colonies and holobionts, and the strongest version of the intuition that includes them, then a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be debunked, what would it take for the theory to be amended, and is there a reading of 'organism' or 'biological individual' on which both come out true?
