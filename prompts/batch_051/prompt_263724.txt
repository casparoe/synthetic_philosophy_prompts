ok so im in philosophy club and we have two kids who've been having this arguement all year about whether you ever get to believe something without enough evidence. one side is basically Clifford: you should never believe anything unless the evidence is sufficient, and if it isnt you should suspend judgment, because believing before the evidence is in makes you a worse reasoner and eventually makes the world worse. the other side is basically James: for options that are live, forced, and momentous, and that the evidence genuinely cant settle, you're allowed (and sometimes you have to) let your passional nature decide, because waiting for proof is itself a risky choice — like if you refuse to trust a new friend until they prove theyre trustworthy, you might kill the friendship before it can start.

so for the end of the semester they have to turn in a joint statement for an adversarial collaboration instead of just talking past each other. write the document they would both actually sign. i want:

- a numbered list of claims they both agree on, and not weaselly generic stuff — substantive points about evidence, risk, when its ok to act vs when its ok to believe, and what belief even is here
- the exact disagreement, worded so each side would read their own view and say "yeah thats what i mean"
- the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where if one side won, the other side would have to move, and i suspect one of them is whether a belief held on hope can ever help make itself true in a way that changes the evidential math (like the friendship case or a fake-it-till-you-make-it case)
- for each crux, a concrete case or test both sides would agree actually bears on it, plus what each side predicts about that case
- and if part of their fight is actually just a verbal dispute, seperate out which part is verbal and which part is real
