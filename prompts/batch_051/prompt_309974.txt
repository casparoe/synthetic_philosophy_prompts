I've been helping a colleague edit a survey in which she classifies each system by which of Dennett's three stances—physical (predicting from laws and states), design (predicting from assumed function), or intentional (predicting by ascribing beliefs and desires)—best predicts its behavior. She has never stated a general rule, but her classifications so far are: a thermostat, physical; termite-mound ventilation, design; a traditional chess engine, design; a human chess player, intentional; a crow solving a novel puzzle, intentional; bee-colony thermoregulation, design; an octopus opening a jam jar, intentional. We disagree about the last case: a deep reinforcement-learning system trained on raw pixels to play a family of maze games, which generalizes to mazes it never saw. Which stance do you expect her to assign? More important: identify the rule or rules that fit her track record, say what each predicts for the new case, give your best guess with your reasons, and name the further case that would most sharply distinguish the remaining candidate rules. Please separate what is settled by the stances framework itself from what is a genuinely contested extrapolation, and don't present one reading as the consensus.
