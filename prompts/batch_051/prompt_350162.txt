In my community, voting is treated as a serious obligation: someone who stays home on election day is criticized as lazy or ungrateful, and the excuse that the election would have been decided anyway is not accepted. Yet the same people do not seem to care whether a person votes well: someone who votes on a whim, or because they like a candidate's voice, or because of a single campaign ad, is not blamed the way a non-voter is, and asking whether someone's vote was informed is often considered rude. This seems like an asymmetry. A single vote almost never changes an outcome, so if the point of voting is to influence the result, the non-voter and the careless voter are nearly equally irrelevant; but if the point is civic expression, habit, or not free-riding on democracy, I do not see why an uninformed vote should fulfill the obligation. What justifies treating the act of voting as a duty while treating the quality of the vote as nobody's business? Should we keep the asymmetry, or should we care less about turnout or more about how people vote?
