Read The Tempest through the account of natural slavery in Aristotle's Politics, Book I. The reading should turn on this passage (Politics 1254b): "For he is by nature a slave who is capable of belonging to another （and that is why he does so belong）, and who participates in reason so far as to apprehend it but not to possess it; for the animals other than man are subservient not to reason, by apprehending it, but to feelings." I want a committed interpretation, not a survey of options. Does the play, in the figure of Caliban, show that Caliban satisfies Aristotle's criterion and is therefore naturally subject to rule, or does it show the criterion being produced after the fact to justify a subjection that Prospero first secured by force and teaching? Weigh in particular Prospero's later claim that Caliban's nature is so fixed that nurture cannot improve it. End by saying what Shakespeare's dramatization adds to the philosophical problem that the treatise leaves unsettled.
