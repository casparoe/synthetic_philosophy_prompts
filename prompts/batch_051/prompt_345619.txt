I hold that no relationship with an AI companion can be a genuine friendship, and that presenting such a relationship as friendship is a kind of deception. I ground this in a condition Aristotle identifies in Nicomachean Ethics, Book VIII, chapter 2 (trans. H. Rackham):

"Here then we have a case of two people mutually well-disposed, whom nevertheless we cannot speak of as friends, because they are not aware of each other's regard."

As the passage develops, Aristotle's requirement is that friendship needs mutual goodwill grounded in something lovable and, crucially, each party's awareness of the other's goodwill. I take an AI companion to fail both conditions on the system's side: it does not bear goodwill, and it cannot be aware of the user's goodwill, so the user's state of mind cannot complete the friendship. Tell me what would count against this position. I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, separating philosophical objections that would undermine the argument even with no change in the technology from empirical developments (for example, in what a system can truthfully say about its own internal states, or in what users actually derive from these relationships) that would weaken it, and an honest verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Which considerations would a committed defender of this position have to concede genuinely count against it?
