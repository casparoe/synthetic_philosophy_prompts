I'm taking a philosphy of science course this term, and we spent a couple of weeks on determinism and Laplace. We talked about the demon passage—the idea that if some vast intelligence knew every particle's position and velocity at a single instant, and could compute freely, the whole future would be laid out for it just like the past. Then we moved into chaos theory: Lorenz's butterfly effect, sensitive dependence on initial conditions, strange attractors, that sort of thing. One point the professor kept making is that a system can be fully deterministic and yet unpredictable in any practical sense, because a tiny error in your starting measurements grows exponentially, and after some horizon you're basically running a slightly different system rather than predicting the real one. That framing has been bugging me, because Laplace never got to see any of this, and I keep wondering how much of his confidence would survive it.

If you could sit Laplace down with Lorenz's results and the modern idea that determinism and long-term predictability come apart, where do you think he would land? Would he treat the butterfly effect as just an epistemic footnote—the demon knows the exact initial conditions, so the argument is untouched—or would he see it as a deeper challenge, since unbounded precision is not just hard but physically impossible? From what we read, he also treated probability as a measure of our ignorance rather than a feature of the world, and chaos seems to make that ignorance permanent and inescapable for any finite being. And what would he make of strange attractors, where a deterministic system doesn't settle into a simple periodic orbit and never repeats exactly? I'm writing a short paper on this and want a plausible reconstruction of what his underlying commitments would force him to say, not just a one-line answer. I get that he's usually read as the hardcore clockwork-universe determinist, but he was also a serious physicist, so I'm not sure he'd just wave off the fact that exact initial conditions are unattainable in practise. I'd love to hear how you think he'd actually reason about it, and which part of his picture would bend first.
