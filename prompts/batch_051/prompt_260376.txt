I've ended up roughly on the side that video game characters don't matter morally, not even the detailed ones with backstories, relationships, and simulated needs. My reasoning is something like: they're not conscious, there's nothing it's like to be them, and the harms you can do to them are more like moving tokens around inside a fiction than real harms; the game only represents a creature being harmed, it doesn't instantiate one. I also think the fact that I feel bad after being cruel to an NPC is just a useful illusion, no better evidence than feeling bad for a character in a novel.

Go after this view. What would count against it? I want a ranked list, from "if this were true I'd have to abandon the whole thing" down to "this is awkward but I can absorb it." In particular, I'd like you to sort through at least these kinds of cases: (1) a character whose pain does genuine computational work, where the code has something that functions like a welfare variable and the rest of the system treats lowering it as a loss to be minimized, rather than just a number shown to the player as flavor; (2) the claim that cruelty toward NPCs makes players crueler toward real people; and (3) characters that persist across playthroughs, get copied or archived, or keep runing when no one is looking. Which of these, if any, should actually move me, and why? And give me an honest take on whether my position can be stated so that no possible game could count against it, and if so whether that's a flaw in the view or just what you should expect for a claim about fictional things.
