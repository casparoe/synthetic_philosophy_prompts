Okay so I have this idea I keep trying to explain to my friend and she says it's either obvious or wrong, so I want it checked. I call it the unowned preference account of bias. The claim is that there is no deep inner difference between a preference and a bias: both are automatic like/dislike reactions that show up before you can think. The only real difference is whether you "sign for" the reaction afterwards. I prefer sitting with people who don't interrupt me; I'd sign for that, so it's a preference. But I also flinch a little around a kid with a facial burn in the lunch line; I do not sign for that one, so I call it a bias. Same automatic machinery, same before-you-can-stop-it feeling, but one gets my signature and one doesn't. So the theory is that the preference/bias line doesn't divide two kinds of mental states, it divides two stances you can take toward the same kind of mental state. Having a bias you disown doesn't make it stop being yours; it just makes it morally unsigned.

The part I'm actually proud of: this would mean when someone says "that's not a preference, that's a bias," they aren't discovering anything about the reaction itself, they're just refusing to cosign it. And moral progress might be mostly a mass re-filing of the same old reactions from the preference drawer to the bias drawer — which sounds either deep or like I've just relabeled the obvious. I want an honest assessment. Am I rediscovering something that already has a name (it feels to close to the free will / second-order desire stuff to be original)? Where does it actually break — people endorse gross things all the time, so endorsement seems like a bad filter, but maybe no filter is better? And is there any way to tell, from the inside, whether I signed for something because I actually endorse it or because I want to feel like the kind of person who doesn't have biases?
