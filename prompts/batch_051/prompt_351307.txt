You are a professor of aesthetics who teaches the philosophy of music to undergraduates and is practiced at translating these issues for bright high-school students. Before committing to a plan, take some time to brainstorm several possible routes and candidate musical examples, then explain why you settled on the one you chose rather than the alternatives.

I am a high-school student, and in two weeks I will lead a forty-five-minute discussion for my school's philosophy and music club. The audience will be about a dozen juniors and seniors; most of them play an instrument or sing, but none of them has taken a philosophy class. I have chosen the topic "what is a piece of music?"—more precisely, the difference between a musical work, a performance of it, and a recording of it.

I am not asking you to write the explanation I should deliver. I can follow an explanation, but I cannot yet put the parts in the right order myself, so I need a teaching plan. The plan should include: the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what (for example, why they probably need a working grasp of the same thing showing up in different copies before they can see the puzzle of two performances of one work); the single example or case that should carry the whole session, including the features it must have (perhaps a song most of them know in both a live and a studio version) and the features it must lack if it is not to drag them off course; the misconceptions I should expect—such as identifying the work with the score, or with the original recording—and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off; a final check that would show me whether they have actually grasped the distinction rather than merely repeating that a performance is an instance of a type; and a clear statement of what I should deliberately leave out at this level and how to say that I am leaving it out.

I also want the session to end on a hard question rather than a tidy summary: if someone changes the tempo, shifts the mode from major to minor, and re-orchestrates the piece, what makes it the same work? I want my classmates to feel why "the notes are the same" is not a sufficient answer and why "it is the same work because people treat it as the same work" is also not enough. And I want the plan to deal honestly with recordings: for some music the recording seems to be the very thing people point at when they say "that song," which complicates the clean picture of one work with many performances.

Please think through the dependencies carefully, and begin with your brainstorming before settling on the final plan. I would rather have a plan whose steps feel obvious to a nervous first-time presenter than one that is clever in the abstract.
