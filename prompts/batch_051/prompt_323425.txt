Daniel Dennett spent a big part of his career defending the intentional stance: when treating a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires predicts its behavior better than the physical or design stance does, you should adopt the intentional stance, and the question whether the system really has beliefs over and above that predictive success is supposed to be a confused one. On that picture the stance pays for itself as a forecasting tool, not as a claim about the hidden inner stuff.

His later writing on AI seems to want the opposite move. He worries about what he calls counterfeit people—chatbots and other LLM-driven systems that pass as a person in conversation well enough that people trust them, confide in them, take their advice. Taking the intentional stance toward one of these works beautifully; treating it as if it had beliefs and desires is genuinely predictive, maybe more predictive than trying to track what the weights and tokens are doing. Yet Dennett wants to call it a counterfeit and treat its presence as a serious danger, not as the intentional stance earning its keep. What changed is that person-like behavior stopped being expensive. It used to be a decent signal that there was a whole embodied, self-monitoring, stake-holding agent behind the performance, and now that signal can be manufactured at scale.

So here's the question. Does the intentional-stance argument survive that development, or did it only look right when faking a person was costly? I'm asking about the argument, not about policy. If you think it survives, say which premise does the work and why the counterfeit case doesn't touch it. If you think it needs repair, give the repaired version—maybe one that lets you adopt the stance for prediction while still withholding something important about personhood. If you think it's dead, say what should replace it. Be explicit about which premise of the old view the development bears on.

Also: tell me how confident you are in the overall answer, and single out the point you're least sure of. Write in American English.
