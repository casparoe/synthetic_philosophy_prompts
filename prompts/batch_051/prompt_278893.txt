Bit of a weird parallel, wondering if it's real. I've done karate for years, and I've also gone through phases of trying to train my reasoning: base rates, pre-mortems, calibration, noticing when I'm confabulating a story instead of assigning a probability. Here's the thing. In karate there's an old fight about whether kata (the solo forms) transfer to actual fighting. The aliveness crowd say no: kata is scripted, there's no resisting opponent, no timing, no cost to mistakes, and the failure modes you meet in a real fight never show up on the dojo floor. The people who defend kata say you're ingraining stances and movement patterns that come out automatically under pressure. When I look at how I try to get better at reasoning, the drills look structurally identical to kata: I do a pre-mortem on a decision where nothing is at stake, or I practice assigning probabilities in a calm exercise, or I work through base rates for a problem in a book. The environment is clean, scripted, low-stakes, and there's nobody trying to exploit my sloppiness. So both domains seem to be training a messy, high-stakes, often adversarial skill almost entirely through clean, low-stakes, choreographed repetition. Is this a deep structural parallel or am I just pattern-matching? If it's deep, what would the live-sparring version of rationality training actually look like, and does anything like it exist? I realize the disanalogy might be that rationality isn't always up against an opponent, but that seems like it makes transfer harder to test, not easier.
