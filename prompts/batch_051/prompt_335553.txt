I am a retired behavioral ecologist. For most of my career I studied animal communication — alarm calls, mimicry, deceptive signaling — where the working habit is to analyze a signal by its effects on receivers rather than by what the sender can be said to have meant, and where a single signal's having different meanings for different receiver populations is an ordinary phenomenon rather than a defect. I came to philosophy late, and I have been trying to get clear on a framework that structures much of the literature on racist speech. As I understand it, the framework has three interlocking parts. (i) A statement may be racist in virtue of the speaker's intent — the animus or discriminatory aim behind it. (ii) A statement may be racist in virtue of its effects on the people it targets, whether or not intent was present. (iii) A dog whistle is a distinctive hybrid: a public formulation that is deniable in plain view while it reliably conveys a racialized message to an in-group, with its distinctive harm tied to the speaker's controlling the two hearings and the cover of deniability.

A new development has unsettled this framework for me. Text-generating systems trained on very large corpora now produce, on request, language that sits in the dog-whistle position: it reads as innocuous to a general audience while reliably evoking a racialized in-group meaning for readers who recognize the pattern. Yet there is no speaker with intent, no author who chose the phrasing to keep it deniable, and no single occasion of use — the same sequence can be regenerated in countless contexts at trivial cost. Clause (ii) seems to apply, since there is an effect, but the effect no longer traces back to any particular act of a person. Clause (iii) seems strained, because the deniability that distinguishes a dog whistle from an open slur was doing its work through the speaker's control, and there is now no one to embarrass, call out, or hold to account.

I would like an assessment of whether this framework survives the development, needs repair, or is dead on this terrain. Please identify which premise or presupposition the development bears on; say whether the framework can be restored in a form that handles speakerless generated text — including text adversarially prompted to produce deniable racist content — without collapsing into a merely lexical test or a merely effect-based account; and say what the receiver-effect habit from animal-signaling research can and cannot contribute to the repair, since that is the background I keep reaching for.

Keep this a conceptual question, not a legal or content-moderation design question, except insofar as those bear on the concepts. Since I am not a trained philosopher, reconstruct the framework charitably and flag where my compression of it is contestable.

Finally, please say how confident you are in the overall assessment, and single out the point in your answer you are least sure of.
