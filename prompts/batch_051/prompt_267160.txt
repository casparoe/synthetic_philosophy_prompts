Our land trust's founding charter says any intervention on a property must be one whose effects are 'foreseeably reversible within fifteen years by the ordinary tools of ecological restoration.' We're currently wieghing two proposals. One would release a gene drive to suppress the invasive rat population on one of our islands; the other would introduce a herd of back-bred aurochs-like cattle to a rewilded valley as a proxy for an extinct wild grazer. The drafters were fairly clearly thinking about removable structures, plantings, maybe a local species that could be rounded up or fenced out. What does the clause actually require when the intervention spreads on its own, or when the organism might establish in a way there's no realistic way to undo? I'm less interested in the practical answer than in how the different interpretive approaches would handle it: the plain wording, the purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable member reading it today takes it to mean.
