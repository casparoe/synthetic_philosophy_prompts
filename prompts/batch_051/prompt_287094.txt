I work in climate policy, and I keep running into a configuration that I do not have a precise term for. In debates over solar geoengineering, one side argues that we should not conduct even small-scale outdoor research into stratospheric aerosol injection, because the mere availability of the option, or serious research toward it, would lead governments and firms to relax their emissions cuts whether or not the option is ever actually used. The other side argues that choosing to stay ignorant of the option is itself risky, because if the technology were ever deployed and then interrupted, say by war, political breakdown, or the discovery of intolerable side effects, temperatures could rise sharply within a decade or two, and no one would know in advance whether the system could be switched off smoothly. The two sides also disagree about who would have standing to decide any of this, and that disagreement sharpens the dilemma: the more fragmented the decision procedure, the easier it is for the option's mere existence to be exploited by free riders, while the more centralized it is, the harder it is to legitimize. I am not asking for a verdict on either side but for a precise characterization of the underlying configuration. It involves a fallback technology whose mere availability changes present incentives even though it may never be deployed, a situation in which both acquiring the option and declining to study it are described as reckless, and a governance problem about who gets to choose. Does this configuration already have a name of its own, distinct from ordinary insurance-style moral hazard and from a generic risk-risk tradeoff, or is it simply those two concepts overlapping? If an established name exists, I want its exact boundaries: which cases fall inside it, which near cases fall outside it, and where the name would mislead. If no established name fits, coin one, define it, and use boundary cases to separate it from the neighbors. A bare label would not help me; it is the carving underneath that matters. Please answer in American English.
