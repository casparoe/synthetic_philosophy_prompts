Before I adopt any formal account of simplicity in induction — Solomonoff induction, Kolmogorov complexity, minimum description length — I want the specification first. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of simplicity must deliver if it is to count as a serious successor to Occam's razor. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable for any account claiming to capture Occam's razor and which are merely the preferences of one camp (say, subjective Bayesians versus algorithmic information theorists). Work through the pressure points: invariance under a change of universal Turing machine, the link between description length and prior probability, convergence on computable sequences, the non-computability of the ideal, and the worry that a gerrymandered description language can make any finite pattern look simple. Say whether any pair of these requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most to exclude the standard contenders. Exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; then, on its own line, add a confidence note: how confident you are in the list overall and the single point you are least sure of.
