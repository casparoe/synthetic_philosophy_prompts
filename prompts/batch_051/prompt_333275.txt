I'm a novelist, and I run a monthly story-and-place workshop at a small historical society: around a dozen adults, 75 minutes, no reading between sessions. Could you design an activity where the group accidently turns a story they know they invented into something that starts feeling like history, and then catches themselves doing it? The thing I want them to feel rather than hear me say is that a legend becoming history isn't mainly about checking the story against evidence—it's about what the community starts asking of the story (fixed names, dates, places, a consistent sequence) and what it stops letting itself change, and nobody has to be lying for that to happen; it can be honest tidying. Give me rules, roles or constraints, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the way people talk about the Trojan War, Arthur, or Exodus without turning into a lecture. Also tell me the predictable thing that'll go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of real historical transmission this exercise just can't reproduce.
