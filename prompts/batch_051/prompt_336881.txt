At a party, two of my cousins got into an argument about a story they'd read: a state employee had, with permission, taken home a decommissioned state flag that was headed for a shredder and sewed it into a dog bed, where no one else would see it. Sam said she'd done nothing wrong: no one was harmed, no one was insulted, and treating a strip of cloth as special is just animism—it's not like a flag has a ghost inside it. Priya replied that Sam's "who was harmed?" test is doing too much work. A woman who lawfully buys a dead stranger's wedding dress at an estate auction and uses it to soak up motor oil hasn't harmed anyone either, but most of us would still count that as a real moral failure, not just an aesthetic one. So Sam already accepts desecration-type wrongs; he just won't admit it. Sam came back that what's wrong in the dress case is the contempt the act expresses, and contempt is only a moral problem when it's aimed at a person; aimed at an object, it tells you someone is insensitive, not that she did something wrong. He added that disgust is a lousy moral detector anyway—it produces the same "sick to my stomach, something sacred has been soiled" response to mixed-race couples or menstruating women, so building morality on it is a nonstarter. Priya said, first, a fallible detector isnt a useless one—fear gives false alarms too—and, second, Sam keeps confusing the detector with the thing detected. The wrongness is not the feeling of disgust; it's a property some objects have, call it inviolability or sacredness, in virtue of their history, and contempt toward that property is wrong whether or not anyone is around to feel grossed out. She said if Sam's view can't make sense of why privately desecrating an unclaimed corpse is wrong, that's a problem for his view, not for the wrongness.

I stayed out of it and I genuinely cant tell who has the better of the argument. Can you adjudicate it move by move? For each move, say what in it is right, what's unearned or overstated, and where the burden of proof has shifted by the end. I want a scorecard, not just a winner.
