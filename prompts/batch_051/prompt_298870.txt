I keep seeing people argue for or against specific impact measures for low-impact AI one counterexample at a time — someone proposes penalizing distance from an inaction baseline, someone else shows that baseline can be gamed, a third proposes comparing how much an action shrinks attainable utility across a range of unknown goals. I want the standard fixed before I evaluate any particular proposal. Please write the desiderata that an adequate impact measure must meet, where the target is an agent that pursues a circumscribed goal — deliver the coffee, route the packets, rearrange the shelves — without a full utility function over everything a side effect could touch. If the agent had such a function, a separate side-effect penalty would be redundant; if it has nothing at all, "avoid side effects" has no content; so the interesting question is what an impact measure must do when it has only partial or proxy information about value. For each requirement, give the case, failure mode, or limitation of a naive measure that motivates it, and say what would be wrong with a measure that missed it. Distinguish the requirements that are non-negotiable — a measure that fails them is not a measure of side-effect impact at all — from properties particular research programs could reasonably trade off. Flag any pair or subset of the requirements you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding the candidate measures currently on the table; keep that part brief, using candidates only to illustrate the requirement. Do not write a full scorecard of proposals. Finally, since the list itself is a theoretical claim, tell me which requirement you are least confident belongs on it, and specify the kind of argument or example — in as much detail as you can — that would lead you to withdraw or substantially qualify it.
