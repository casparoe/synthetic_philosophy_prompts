A puzzle from my church's constitution, which was drafted in 1962. Article VII, Section 2 says:

"Christian baptism is the immersion of a believer in water, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. It is to be administered by the pastor, or by a deacon with the pastor's approval, in the presence of the gathered church, upon a credible profession of faith."

We've been meeting over Zoom since March 2020, and Maria, who made a credible profession of faith back in February 2020, still hasn't been baptized. She's on immunosuppressants after an organ transplant and genuinely cant attend an indoor gathering, even a small one. Her proposal: during the regular Sunday Zoom service she gets into her bathtub with the camera on, the pastor says the baptismal words from his kitchen, and she immerses herself once while the congregation watches on their screens. Half the deacons think she's been baptized; half think nothing happened at all.

Here's what I'd like. Argue the case three ways — from the actual wording of the clause, from its purpose, and from what the people who wrote it in 1962 would have said if they'd been asked — and tell me which of those approaches should decide here and why. Then the part I'm most stuck on: how does the answer shift depending on whether baptism is (a) only a symbol, (b) a sacrament that conveys grace, or (c) a rite that actually causes regeneration? Does the clause make most sense on one of those views, and does the Zoom bathtub case sort of force a choice between them?
