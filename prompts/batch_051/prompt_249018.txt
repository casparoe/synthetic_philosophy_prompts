I'm a journalist who covers mostly psychology and the ideas side of wellness and self-help, and I keep running into the word equanimity used in ways that don't seem compatible. Sometimes it's the Buddhist/meditation thing: you feel the anger or fear arise without letting it drive the bus. Sometimes it's the Stoic apatheia thing: you've reasoned your way out of being disturbed at all. And sometimes my readers use it to describe something that sounds more like flatness — a friend on an SSRI who says she's "equanimous" now but also can't cry at movies anymore, or a burnt-out nurse who's stopped reacting to patient deaths.

I want a working definition of equanimity that gets these cases right:

1. A parent watching a toddler have a supermarket meltdown, who stays calm and doesn't give in, even though internally she's annoyed.
2. A long-term meditator who notices anger rising during an argument and chooses not to speak from it.
3. Someone who suppresses grief after a death because "people need me to be strong," feels fine for months, then falls apart at something trivial.
4. A person on antidepressants who reports feeling emotionally blunted — less sadness but also less joy and less interest in other people.
5. A terminal cancer patient who says "it is what it is" and seems to mean it: not in denial, not numb, just not fighting it.
6. A nurse so burnt out that a patient's death doesn't move her anymore.
7. A sports fan whose team loses on a last-second mistake and shrugs "it's only a game" — and means it.
8. A Stoic who decides in advance that losing his job is an "indifferent" and then, when it happens, genuine isn't upset.

My instinct is that 1, 2, and 8 are the real thing, 3 and 4 are definitely not, 6 feels like something else entirely, and I genuinly don't know about 5 and 7.

Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it misclassifies or gives no answer. Then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. I'd also like to know whether equanimity and apatheia are the same concept or two different ones, and if they're two, whether one of them is maybe not worth wanting. Be concrete about which of your verdicts is least secure.

One request: if the whole task is confused — if "equanimity" isn't one thing and I'm actually gesturing at two or three different phenomena sharing a name — say that outright and give me the split instead of forcing a single definition. I'd rather have that than a tidy answer that papers over the mess.
