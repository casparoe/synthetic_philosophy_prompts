In my doctoral work on advance directives and the authority of an earlier self, I have been trying to think through a concrete proposal rather than argue for it in the abstract. Suppose a health system replaces ordinary advance directives with a system of depreciating authority:

- A competent adult may file advance instructions and, at filing, choose an authority half-life H of between one and ten years.
- If the patient later loses capacity and the directive conflicts with the patient's current expressed wishes, the directive is weighted by 2^(-t/H), where t is the time since the patient's last competent reaffirmation, and the current expressed wishes are weighted by the remainder, provided an independent patient advocate certifies that the current wishes are stable across at least two separate occasions and are more specific than a general gesture; otherwise the directive controls by default.
- A competent patient may reaffirm at any time, resetting t to zero, but reaffirmation requires restating the directive's two or three central instructions in the patient's own words and answering a short question about the burdens of following them; the patient may amend the directive at the same time.
- Once the patient lacks capacity, neither the surrogate, the treating team, nor the family may depart from the weighted result, even on compassionate grounds; any departure must go to an ethics committee with a lay majority, which must publish a redacted written explanation.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a substantive position about personal identity or autonomy: I want to know how it would actually behave. Which patients would file or renew, and with which H? What would it incentivize among surrogates, clinicians, and families? How would it be gamed or routinized? Where would it break first—under dementia with intermittent clarity, in emergencies, or when current wishes are valid but poorly articulated? Which failure modes are fixable within the scheme, and which would require abandoning its central idea that the authority of the earlier self should depreciate continuously rather than lapse all at once?
