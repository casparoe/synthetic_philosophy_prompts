My dad died about three months ago and I keep catching myself being him, and it's making me ask a question I don't know how to think about. We were close in the way where we shared a lot of mental machinery — the same running jokes, the same way of working through a problem, the same read on people. Since he died I've had things show up in my own head that feel like they have his signature. The clearest example: I was in an argument with a supplier and, without deciding to, I made a sarcastic redirect that was so exactly how dad would have handled it that my uncle, who was on the call, asked afterwards when I started talking like my old man.

It's making me wonder whether the thing that made him him was never entirely stored in his skull. Like, his habits and ways of sorting the world are still running in me and my uncle and maybe two or three other people he spent decades shaping, lower resolution but still going. If that's right, then what died when his body died was only the central copy; the pattern is still running, just spread across several heads. My wife says this is grief looking for a bargain, and that "he lives on in us" is a metaphor, not a claim about what a self is. I get why she says that. But is it literally coherent to say a self can be distributed across multiple brains, and that what survived his death is his self, fragmented and degraded but not gone? Or is there something about a self that requires it to run as one integrated thing in one head before it counts as surviving? I'm not looking for comfort; I want help figuring out whether the idea is philosophically defensible or wheather I'm just twisting words to avoid admitting he's simply gone.
