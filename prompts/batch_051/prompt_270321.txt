Okay so I've noticed this pattern and I'm wondering if it has a name. People seem to treat bad side effects as more 'on purpose' than good side effects. Like, a manager is told that a cost-cutting plan will boost profits and also pollute a local river. He says he doesn't care about the river, profits come first, do it. Everyone says he polluted the river basically on purpose. Now same manager, same 'I don't care,' but this time the plan happens to clean up a river as a side thing. Everyone says no, he didn't clean it on purpose, it was just a foreseeable side effect. The only thing that changed is whether the side effect is good or bad.

But here's the wrinkle i keep bumping into. When I tweak the cases so the bad side effect is totally routine and within the rules — like the plan will eliminate a couple of parking spaces, which everyone hates but the zoning rules allow — the 'on purpose' verdict gets weirdly weak. And when the good side effect breaks a rule or violates some expectation — like the cleanup involves skipping an environmental review — people suddenly call it on purpose. So it's not just good vs bad; it's more like the side effect being out of the ordinary or rule-flouting.

So what I want to know: is there a name for the basic pattern? And is the wrinkle a known correction to that basic story, or did I just make bad examples? i'm not going to be satisfied with a bare label — I want to know what the underlying phenomenon actually is, what drives it, where it stops working, and what bigger thing it's a special case of.
