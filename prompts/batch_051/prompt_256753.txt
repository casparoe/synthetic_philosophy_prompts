Here's one that's been bugging me. I keep thinking that legal fictions are just made-up constructs—like when a court says a corporation is a person, or that a child born after a father's death is treated as if the father survived him. On one level I know these are deliberate falsehoods the law uses for convenience, and calling a corporation a "person" doesn't make it one any more than calling a rock a person does. But then I read this argument that all social reality is like that: money is only money because we collectively pretend, marriage exists only because law declares it, and even my own status as a "person" before the law is a fiction layered on biological facts. If that's right, my intuition that legal fictions are unreal is itself just another legal fiction. I don't want to give up the intuition, but I'm worried it's inconsistent. Which should yield—the intuition that legal fictions are mere useful falsehoods, or the theory that there is no deep difference between legal fictions and legal facts? Give me the strongest version of each and tell me how you'd negotiate.
