An urban policy group proposes that a planned city, built on land leased from a willing host state, be constituted as a chartered corporation in the manner of an early modern trading company rather than as a municipality. Citizenship in the city is a share: each adult resident must hold at least one share, voting weight in the city assembly is proportional to shares held with no cap, shares trade freely on an open market, and the corporation pays an annual dividend from the city's net revenues — chiefly a land-value tax and fees for utilities it operates. The charter obliges the corporation to provide police, courts, roads, and sanitation, but permits the assembly to admit non-shareholding residents, who may live and work in the city without a vote or dividend. Treat this as a mechanism rather than a moral position: who would accumulate shares, and what policies would that select for; how would residents, outside investors, and the corporation's managers each respond; what gets gamed first; what second-order effects follow from the resident/shareholder split; and could the failures be fixed by charter amendments while keeping the corporate form? Throughout, say how confident you are in the specific predictions you make and identify the point you are least sure of.
