I sit on a small benefits conditionality panel that can hand out anything from a warning to a full suspension. We've heard five cases this year and I want our decision on the sixth to be consistent with our own record. The five: we did not sanction a mother who missed a mandatory appointment because her child had been sent home sick from school; we gave the maximum sanction to a man who did cash-in-hand building work for months while claiming and lied when questioned (he spent the money on nights out); we reduced but did not remove payment for a claimant with depression who missed several appointments after a GP wrote to us; we accepted a woman's refusal of a job with a 90-minute commute because she had no after-school childcare; and we sanctioned a man who missed an appointment because he was at a football match.

The new appeal is a single mother who had been doing unreported babysitting for a neighbour for a few months. She says the small earnings went on her kid's school uniform and food, she admitted it straight away when the department called her in, and her record is otherwise clean. Should we sanction her, and if so how much? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog? And if our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, say what principle we'd be committing ourselves to under each way of deciding. Also tell me what would change your mind: which argument or finding would flip you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.

Give the answer as JSON with these fields:

- "candidate_rules": array of at least two objects, each with "label" (short name), "statement" (the rule as a principle), and "prediction_for_new_case" (what that rule would do here).

- "confidence": "low", "medium", or "high", plus one sentence on the main uncertainty.
- "what_would_change_my_verdict": the argument or finding that would most likely make you flip to a different decision.
- "where_reasoning_is_fragile": the step in your reasoning you're least sure about.
- "case_that_would_distinguish": a short description of a future case whose decision would tell the candidate rules apart.

Return only the JSON.
