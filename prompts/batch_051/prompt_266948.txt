I want to test a definition against a hard case. Here is my working criterion for objectionable cultural appropriation in art: an artist's use of another culture's forms, motifs, or techniques is objectionable appropriation when (i) the artist's group stands in a relationship of dominance over the source group; (ii) the source group exercises some collective authority over the element, so that its use is not simply a matter of taking from the public domain; (iii) the work detaches the element from the practices and meanings that sustain it, or presents it as the artist's own discovery; and (iv) the artist or their audience captures the value of the element while the source group is shut out of the credit, profit, or control.

Now consider this case, which I will describe so you needn't know it in advance. A printmaker from a colonizing country spends years studying a particular Indigenous people's woodblock tradition under a master from that tradition. The master authorizes a series of prints, chooses the title, and gives a blessing; the profits from the exhibition are directed to a scholarship for emerging artists from that community. But the series incorporates a clan emblem that the master, as an individual, had no standing to authorize: the community's own rules treat the emblem as collectively owned by a specific clan and forbid its reproduction by outsiders in editions. The artist knows this and keeps the emblem, slightly altered for formal balance, because it organizes the series. The emblem's custodians object not to the artist's identity but to the reproduction as such.

Is this cultural appropriation by my four criteria? Work through each criterion and say whether it is satisfied, suspended, or contradicted by the case. Then say which criterion is doing the real work, and whether the case suggests my criterion (ii) needs to be split — between permission from individual practitioners and permission from the structure that actually owns the element. Finally, tell me whether what is wrong here is best described as appropriation at all, or as something closer to violating a community's jurisdiction over its images.
