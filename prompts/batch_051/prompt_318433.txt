I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a feature about a new setup at one of the big AI labs that's making people a little uncomfortable. The lab keeps a "mentor" model, M, that is responsible for a "student" model, S, over a very long training period. M plans S's curriculum, picks and filters S's examples, tweaks S's reward signal when it starts drifting, and decides when S is ready for harder tasks. S is initialized partly from a compressed version of M's weights, and the lab talks about a "family" of models. The PR people have started calling M S's parent, and the safety team says "guardian" is more than a metaphor. I want a dialogue between two serious researchers who disagree about this. One thinks it really is parenthood in an extended but literal sense, because the functional role is what matters. The other thinks calling it parenthood is a category mistake, because a parent has to be a particular kind of mortal, vulnerable being with its own life at stake. Make the two equally sharp — no strawmen — and have them actually answer each other, not just trade speeches. Push them into the weeds: is there a real difference between raising and training, between guardianship and parenthood, and does it matter that S can be copied or rolled back and will likely outlive M? I'd also like one of them to press the other on whether M's loss function can count as caring. End on a note where the reader genuinely isnt sure who's right.
