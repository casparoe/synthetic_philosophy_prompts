Give me a working definition of commodification in the pejorative sense—the wrong that critics of certain markets claim is done to what is bought and sold, distinct from ordinary worries about fraud, monopoly, or unequal bargaining power—and test it against these cases: a living donor selling a kidney for cash; paid plasma donation; commercial surrogacy; selling one's vote; a hospital that auctions faster access to treatment; surge pricing for bottled water during a hurricane evacuation; tradeable carbon emission permits; life insurance on a spouse; paying someone to stand in a government queue; and selling a used book. I am confident that selling a vote and selling a kidney are cases of it, and that life insurance and selling a used book are not; the other six I cannot classify with much confidence. Propose the definition, apply it to each of the ten cases, identify where it conflicts with my judgments or gives no clear verdict, and then either revise the definition or defend the unexpected conclusions.
