I keep getting dragged into the same argument at work: whether the systems we're building on now (LLMs plus the usual RAG/agents glue) already count as "transformative AI." One camp says obviously yes, look at what our customers are shipping now vs two years ago. The other says no, a productivity bump isn't transformative; transformative means something on the order of the industrial revolution, a one-time change to the growth rate of the whole economy. I dont have a philosophy background, but I write specs for a living, so before I commit to either definition I want the spec: what does an adequate definition of transformative AI have to deliver? Which cases does it have to get right (e.g., electricity, the internet, or a system that transforms things for the worse like total surveillance), which distinctions must it respect, and which of the usual requirements are non-negotiable vs. just one camp's preference? Then score the "it already happened" and "it hasn't yet" camps against that list, briefly — I mostly need it to settle how we talk about this in grant proposals and roadmap docs. Also flag where you're least confident in the list, and what argument or case would make you add, drop, or reorder a requirement.
