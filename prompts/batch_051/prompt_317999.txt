As I understand it, the standard argument says that a person with intransitive preferences—A over B, B over C, and C over A, all at the same time—can be exploited: start them with C, offer them B for C plus a small fee, then A for B plus a fee, then C for A plus a fee, and they finish where they started but poorer. At any one time their preferences are perfectly transitive; the cycle appears only across time. A trader who knows the schedule can time the offers to match the rotation and run the same pump. Does the money-pump argument survive this change? Is the person irrational in the same way, irrational in a different way, or not irrational at all?
