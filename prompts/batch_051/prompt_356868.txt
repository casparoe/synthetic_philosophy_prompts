My friend has gotten really weird about insects: he won't kill flies, won't eat honey, walks around ant trails. His argument is that for any given bug the chance it's sentient is low—maybe 1%—but there are on the order of 10^18 insects alive, so low probability times astronomical number means we should act as though enormous numbers of sentient lives are at stake. The thing is, I can't tell whether that last step is a real argument or a trick for turning "probably not, for this one" into "definitely yes, once there are enough of them." How would you test a claim like this? Give me the best design you can for the parts that are empirically testable—what you'd measure in insects and what result would favor each side—and then be explicit about which parts of the scale argument no experiment could settle, especially whether the uncertainty about each insect should be treated as independent and whether expected suffering spread across a population is even the right way to think about moral risk.
