I'm a grad student in a philosophy of biology seminar, and I've been going around in circles on the tautology objection and the propensity interpretation of fitness. I'd love an expert philosopher of biology to think this through with me carefully, because I keep flip-flopping. Here's where I've landed. The old worry that the survival of the fittest is empty — because fitness gets defined as actual reproductive success, so the fittest just are the ones that survived and reproduced — seems to me like a real problem for any outcome-based definition of fitness. And the propensity interpretation feels like the clean fix: fitness is a probabilistic disposition of an organism to survive and reproduce in a given environment, something the organism has before the coin flips land, so the claim that fitter organisms tend to leave more offspring becomes a substantive empirical claim about how those dispositions play out. I like that it makes fitness a real property of the organism, on a par with solubility or fragility, rather than a bookkeeping summary of what happened.

But I keep hitting the same wall, and I dont want to just hand-wave it away. I have a stubborn intuition that fitness has to be empirically accessible in a way the propensity view hasn't yet secured. If the only way to measure an organism's fitness is to watch how many offspring it actually leaves — or to decompose that into viability, fecundity, and so on, which are themselves just actual outcomes of components — then hasn't the propensity interpretation merely relocated the tautology? The claim that fitter organisms leave more offspring starts to sound true because we've defined fitter as whatever disposition it is that, on average, issues in more offspring. And I also have a firm old-fashioned intuition that when I call an organism fit, I'm talking about something like actual reproductive output, even though I know that's exactly the definition that caused the original trouble.

So my question is which of these should give. Present both sides at their strongest and give me a negotiated verdict: should I drop the intuition and accept that fitness is a theoretical quantity like mass or temperature, known only through its effects? Should I abandon or amend the propensity interpretation because it hasn't really solved the tautology, and if so, what's the alternative? Or is there a reading on which the propensity view and the intuition that fitness must be empirically accessible are compatible? I'm less interested in being told which side wins and more interested in what would justify each move. Maybe start by brainstorming how a propensity could be measured without collapsing into actual outcomes.
