I keep hearing a version of this idea at AI-policy panels and I want to actually think it through. Since nobody can agree on what intelligence is, the proposal is to punt on the definition entirely: a state-funded board runs a rolling battery of tests (theory-of-mind vignettes, one-shot tool-use puzzles, odd-one-out reasoning, the works), and any system that clears, say, 15 of 20 qualifies as "intelligent" for legal purposes, triggering extra liability and monitoring rules. Developers can retest as often as they like, but each attempt costs a big fee and they have to open-source the exact verison being tested. Walk me through what this does in practice. What do the labs start optimizing for, how fast does the test suite become the de facto definition of intelligence, and where does the whole thing get gamed first — by the companies, by the systems themselves, or by the board's own test-writers? Is there a fixable version of this operationalism, or does it just rebuild the semantic mess it was supposed to dodge?
