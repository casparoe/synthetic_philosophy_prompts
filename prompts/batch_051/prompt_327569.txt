I've been working through the Judy Benjamin problem and I want to step back from the technical proposals and ask what a satisfactory account of revising credences on conditional information must actually achieve. The setup I have in mind is the familiar one: Judy is dropped into a region divided into Red and Blue territories, each further subdivided into, say, First and Second sectors, so that her prior is a distribution over four possible locations. She then receives a radio message that does not tell her which territory she is in, nor which sector she is in, but instead constrains a conditional probability—for example, that if she is in Red territory, her chance of being in Second sector is some specified value. The question is how she should revise the probabilities of all four cells in a principled way.

I would like the deliverable to be a list of requirements that any adequate update rule for such conditional information must meet, with the consideration or case behind each requirement. Please make the requirements precise enough to exclude or include candidate rules. In particular, I want to know which requirements are non-negotiable—such as reducing to ordinary Bayesian conditionalization when the learned information is an ordinary event, or agreeing with Jeffrey conditionalization in the cases where it gives a determinate answer—and which are merely preferences of particular camps, such as invariance under reparametrization of the partition, commutativity of sequential updates, or minimizing a particular information-theoretic distance from the prior. If some subset of the requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, or if one requirement is doing most of the work to rule out otherwise reasonable proposals, that is the part I most want to see. Please also say how confident you are in the proposed list and single out the requirement you are least sure about.
