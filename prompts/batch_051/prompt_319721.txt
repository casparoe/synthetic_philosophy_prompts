I'm hoping you can help me think through a situation I'm in, because I can't tell whether I've wronged someone or merely made them angry. Some weeks ago I learned, in an ordinary way and with no promise of confidentiality, that a coworker is planning to leave our firm. His leaving matters practically: he is the only person who can complete a project we share, so his departure would ruin months of my work. There is nothing contractual stopping him, and what I know is true.

Yesterday I told him, only partly joking, that I knew about his plans. I said I could go to our manager and would be doing nothing dishonest if I did — but that I would keep it to myself, on the condition that he paid me a few thousand dollars. To be clear, I had no wish to report him; I would genuinely prefer to keep quiet, and I mainly wanted to be compensated for the position his departure would put me in. He became angry and said I was threatening him. I do not see it. It would have been within my rights simply to tell the manager. It would have been within my rights to ask him for money. It would have been within my rights to keep his secret for free. How can an offer that combines three permissible acts suddenly cross a line? If I did something wrong, I want to know exactly which feature of the offer makes it wrong — not merely that people would call it by an ugly name. If I did not, I want to know why his reaction still lingers with me.

Give me your bottom line first, then your reasoning. Keep the entire response under 500 words.
