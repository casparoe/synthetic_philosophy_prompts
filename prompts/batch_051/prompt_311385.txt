I have recently found myself unable to stop thinking about an argument I have seen attributed to Berkeley, because a close friend used a version of it on me after we argued about whether forests, oceans, and the carbon cycle exist when no mind is attending to them. I want to know whether the argument is sound, and if it is not, exactly where it goes wrong.

Here is the argument, which I have tried to set out as numbered steps.

(1) Suppose, for the sake of argument, that I can conceive of a tree T that exists unconceived: that is, T exists and no mind conceives or perceives T.

(2) In conceiving of T in this way, I am conceiving of T.

(3) Therefore, T is being conceived by me.

(4) So T is both unconceived (which is how my conception represents it) and conceived (by the act of conceiving). Contradiction.

(5) Therefore, I cannot conceive of a tree existing unconceived.

(6) If I cannot conceive of a tree existing unconceived, then a tree existing unconceived is impossible. (This is the premise that makes conceivability a guide to possibility; I am not sure the argument is entitled to it.)

(7) Therefore, it is impossible for a tree to exist unconceived; any tree must exist in a mind, at least in the sense of being perceived or conceived.

The conclusion is strange enough that I half-want the argument to fail, but I cannot pin down the failure. I suspect the word conceived slides between the act of conceiving and the content conceived: on the act reading, step (3) follows but only trivializes the argument, while on the content reading, step (3) does not follow at all. But I am not confident that this diagnosis is right, or that it is precise enough to be decisive.

Please assess the argument step by step. Say whether it is valid as written and, if not, exactly which step fails and why. If the act/content distinction does the real work, state precisely where it applies; if a different principle is the culprit, say what that principle is and what a defender of the argument would need to do to repair it. Keep your answer to at most 400 words.
