I'm a population geneticist by training—my working vocabulary is allele frequencies, fitness coefficients, heritability, hitchhiking, drift, and the Price equation—and I keep finding myself in cross-disciplinary conversations with cultural-evolution researchers who talk about memes as if they were alleles that replicate, mutate, and get selected. I have only a loose handle on that literature, so I want a careful translation, not a popular summary. The debate I have in mind has two sides. One side holds that cultural variants such as words, tunes, techniques, and rituals are genuinely replicating units: they are copied with enough fidelity to form lineages, they exhibit heritable variation, and changes in their frequencies over time can be explained by selection-like processes. The other side holds that this is at most a loose metaphor, because cultural transmission is usually reconstructive or transformative rather than replicative—each learner infers and re-expresses a variant rather than copying it the way DNA is copied—so there is no stable object whose change in frequency could be counted, let alone selected.

Restate each position, and the disagreement between them, in the vocabulary of population genetics. In particular, spell out what each side would have to claim about narrow-sense heritability, the genotype–phenotype map, mutation and copy error, assortative transmission, frequency-dependent selection, and the conditions under which the Price equation can be applied at all. Then give me the part I actually care about: the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly into that vocabulary, which translate only if they are distorted, and which have no expression in it at all? For each item of residue, tell me whether it looks like a limitation of the population-genetic framework or a sign that the memeticist's claim was less precise than it appeared. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in American English.
