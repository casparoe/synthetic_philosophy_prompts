A friend and I stayed up arguing about how much the Rietdijk–Putnam argument actually proves, and I am grading the following exam answer with that argument fresh in mind. The exam question read: "Reconstruct the line of thought associated with Rietdijk and Putnam, running from the relativity of simultaneity to the conclusion that the future is already real, and then assess exactly what that line of thought establishes. Be careful to separate what follows from what only appears to follow." The student wrote:

"Special relativity tells us that simultaneity is relative to an inertial frame: two spatially separated events can be simultaneous in one frame and not in another, and no frame is physically privileged. Take an event in my future, such as tomorrow's lecture. For a sufficiently distant observer moving relative to me, there will be a moment along that observer's worldline that is simultaneous with my lecture in that observer's frame. That moment is that observer's here-and-now, and it is just as real as any moment of mine. Since no frame is privileged, if my lecture is simultaneous with a real here-and-now in some frame, then my lecture must be real already. So future events are real, past, present, and future coexist, and the present has no special ontological status. The block universe picture is therefore correct. From this it also follows that the future is fixed independently of what I now decide: tomorrow's lecture will occur whether or not I now choose to prepare for it. The argument therefore establishes determinism, or at least fatalism."

Please grade the answer as an exam grader would. Give it a mark, then go through it point by point and explain that mark: identify which steps are correct, which transitions rely on hidden assumptions, and which conclusions are overstated or do not follow. Be specific about the exact inference at issue rather than giving a general impression.
