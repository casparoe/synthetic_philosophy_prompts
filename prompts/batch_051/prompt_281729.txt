There's an old line of argument that thinking about your own thinking is an unalloyed epistemic and practical good: a belief you haven't examined is just a guess, and a life that never steps back to check its own direction isn't worth much. The development that seems to complicate this is the psychological work on self-focused attention: concentrating on the mechanics of a well-learned skill (piano, a golf swing, typing) reliably degrades performance, and habitual rumination on one's own mental states tends to deepen the very moods it's trying to fix. Does the old argument survive, or is it dead? Specifically, which premise is actually under pressure — the claim that metacognition is always causally helpful, the claim that unexamined beliefs lack justification, or something in between? And what does a repaired version look like that concedes the data but still keeps the examined life worth aspiring to?
