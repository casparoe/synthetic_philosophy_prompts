I've been slowly working through Hume's first Enquiry, and I keep getting stuck in Section VII, Part I, where Hume sets up the problem of the idea of necessary connexion. Here is the passage I'm concerned with:

"There are no ideas, which occur in metaphysics, more obscure and uncertain, than those of power, force, energy, or necessary connexion, of which it is every moment necessary for us to treat in all our disquisitions. We shall, therefore, endeavour, in this section, to fix, if possible, the precise meaning of these terms, and thereby remove some part of that obscurity, which is so much complained of in this species of philosophy."

I understand the broad shape of the argument: he will look for the impression from which the idea of necessary connexion is copied, fail to find it in the objects or the causal relation itself, and then locate it in the felt transition of the mind after repeated experience. What I want help with is the status of that conclusion, especially against the background of the so-called New Hume reading.

Specifically: when Hume says he wants to fix the precise meaning of power and necessary connexion, is he doing something like conceptual analysis, in the sense that the meaning just is the impression-derived content and nothing more? Or is he making a weaker epistemological point, that if there is some objective necessary connection in nature we can have no idea of it, so the term as we use it can only pick out the mental customary transition? In other words, should I read the account as reductionist—there is nothing in the relation beyond regular succession plus mental habit—or as an agnostic/realist-skeptical position on which the term has two possible referents, one unknowable and one empirical?

I've seen people use the phrase New Hume to mean the second sort of reading, but I'm not confident I've got the boundaries right, and I'd like an account of what in the text of Section VII pushes one toward one reading over the other. Please don't just give me a survey of the secondary literature; I want the philosophical surface of the text itself, including what the copy principle commits him to and what it leaves open. Also, if there's a third live option I'm missing—something that isn't exactly reductionism or realist agnosticism—spell it out.
