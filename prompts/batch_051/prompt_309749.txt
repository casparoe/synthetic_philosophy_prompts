I spent my career in developmental biology before retiring, and I have lately been reading the metaphysics of modality and identity. The view that has come to seem right to me, though I know it has its critics, is a version of origin essentialism about persons. Stated carefully: a human person has the particular zygote from which they developed essentially, so that no person could have existed and developed from a different zygote — and in particular not from a different sperm or a different egg. I want to hold this as a substantive claim about what fixes a person's identity across possibilities, not as a verbal stipulation, and I want it to remain answerable to the biology. The cases I most want the view tested against are these: monozygotic twins, where one zygote gives rise to two distinct persons and neither seems privileged as the original; chimeras of the tetragametic sort, where a single person incorporates cells descended from two zygotes; in vitro fertilization, where a sibling embryo that was never implanted seems intuitively to be a different possible person rather than a possible version of the one who was born; and the fact that the egg contributes mitochondria and maternal cytoplasmic factors, so that the same nuclear genome placed in a different egg would not be the same biological start. I find the familiar line of thought in the metaphysics of modality persuasive in the straightforward cases, but I do not want a position that has been quietly protected from each difficult case by a new qualification.

Please treat the position I have just stated as the object of scrutiny and give me a map of what would count against it. I would like you to distinguish considerations that should be decisive from those that would merely be awkward; to include what a defender of origin essentialism would themselves concede counts against the view, not only what its critics say; to say where the view handles cases better or worse than its rivals, such as a psychological-continuity account of identity or a view on which origins are not essential at all; and to say frankly whether the view can be insulated from the difficult cases only by being qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be a problem for a metaphysical view of this kind. I would personally find most persuasive considerations that come from the biology — twinning, chimerism, embryo fusion, cryopreservation, and assisted reproduction — rather than from purely abstract modal intuitions. Please reach a definite verdict about how exposed the position actually is and defend it, rather than laying out both sides and stopping. I ask that you write this in continuous prose, in the manner of a serious letter or essay, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
