At our local rationalist reading group last weekend two of the regulars got stuck on anthropics for an hour, and I left wishing somone had kept a scorecard. They were arguing about self-sampling versus self-indication, and specifically about whether the one who liked the doomsday argument could hold it without taking on the other person's alien credences. Roughly:

(1) Maya led with self-sampling. Conditional on being a random human among all humans who will ever live, finding yourself around the hundred-billionth birth rank is much more likely if the total is only a hundred and fifty billion than if it is three trillion, so your rank pushes hard toward doom soon.

(2) Owen said that update ignores existence itself. The self-indication assumption says a hypothesis with more total observers makes finding yourself existing more probable, and in the doomsday case that factor points exactly as hard toward doom late as the rank factor points toward doom soon. Include both and the doomsday argument washes out.

(3) Maya said self-indication has its own known embarrassment, the presumptuous philosopher. Take two cosmic theories, same prior, same simplicity, same empirical support from everything we observe here. T1 says Earth's two hundred billion humans are the only intelligent observers ever. T2 says Earth's two hundred billion are joined by trillions of other civilizations, so vastly more observers total. Self-indication makes you overwhelmingly confident in T2 with no independent evidence for aliens. You get to conjure a crowded universe out of nothing.

(4) Owen said that is just a sneer disguised as an objection. If existence really is evidence, the philosopher in the story is being reasonable rather than presumptuous, and the intuition that he is out of line is doing all the work.

(5) Maya said there is an old-evidence problem anyway. I already knew I existed before T1 and T2 were ever proposed, so my existence cannot be new evidence for T2; it cannot shift a prior that was already formed conditional on being alive. Owen answered that the same goes for her birth rank, which she also already knew, so if old evidence kills self-indication it kills self-sampling too, and she cannot keep the doomsday argument while throwing away the presumptuous philosopher.

At that point they went in circles. Neither ever got to whether rank evidence and existence evidence are actually symmetric under one formalism, or whether the reference class problem for self-sampling undermines the doomsday conclusion all by itself.

Please adjudicate move by move. For each numbered move, say whether it is right, wrong, or half-right, and how much credit it deserves. Then say who is more defensible overall, and what both of them are missing.
