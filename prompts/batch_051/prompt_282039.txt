I sit on the compensation and role-design committee of a worker-owned software cooperative. Twice a year, our committee reviews every permanent role that carries formal authority over another member, and we decide whether that authority should be written into our operating agreement for the next cycle or dissolved as an unnecessary hierarchy. Our operating agreement allows a role to carry authority over others only when it reflects a real difference in the kind of work being done, not merely a difference in bargaining power or seniority. In practice, this standard is contested every quarter, and I have learned the hard way that without an explicit procedure the committee ends up ratifying whatever hierarchy was already there. I am the member most likely to demand an operational definition, which has earned me some good-natured complaints about importing what one colleague calls my "rationalist forum instincts" into board meetings. I still think the demand is correct.

As a shared point of reference, several of us have been reading Aristotle's *Politics*, Book I, Part II. The sentence we keep circling is this one:

"For that which can foresee by the exercise of mind is by nature intended to be lord and master, and that which can with its body give effect to such foresight is a subject, and by nature a slave; hence master and slave have the same interest."

I want you to write a reusable procedure our committee can follow to decide, for any proposed role, whether it is the kind of natural-ruler/subject arrangement Aristotle describes in that passage or whether it is a conventional hierarchy we should not ratify. The procedure must be usable by committee members who have not read Aristotle and who will not accept his metaphysics or his conclusions about slavery. It should specify the questions to ask, in what order, what evidence counts, and where the procedure stops being mechanical and a human judgment is required. We have limited meeting time, so the procedure must fit on one page and be usable in under fifteen minutes per role. Include two worked examples: one where the procedure approves a role, and one where it rejects a role we currently have and that I suspect I will not want it to reject. End by stating clearly what the procedure cannot detect — the forms of illegitimate hierarchy that could pass through it despite satisfying the checklist.
