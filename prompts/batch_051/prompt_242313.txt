I've been reading Fabre's account in The Hunting Wasps of how a Sphex wasp stings and paralyses a cricket, drags it to the entrance of her burrow, then leaves the cricket on the threshold while she goes inside alone to inspect the nest before bringing it in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a little away during the inspection, the wasp comes back out, drags the cricket to the entrance again, and goes inside to inspect again, repeating this sequence many times rather than simply pulling the cricket straight down the open burrow. Fabre's point is that what looks like careful provisioning is a fixed routine keyed to a sensory cue, with no real judgment when the routine is perturbed.

A philosopher of action I know has been collecting verdicts on whether various behaviors count as genuinely flexible in the sense she thinks matters for agency and free will. Her earlier cases: the Sphex routine just described, not flexible; a digger wasp that re-closes its nest cell after an experimenter breaks the top, not flexible; a honeybee performing the waggle dance to direct nestmates, not flexible; a rat learning a new escape route in a water maze over many trials, flexible; a chimpanzee using a stick to fish termites, flexible; a human tying shoelaces while carrying on a conversation, not flexible, because the ability is acquired but the execution is automatic.

Next she will consider this case: a mobile robot built on a subsumption architecture, with a higher-priority collision-avoidance layer that can interrupt a lower-priority wander-and-map layer, which in turn can interrupt the lowest-priority recharge-seeking layer. The robot is placed in a room it has never encountered. It first wanders to build a map, then moves toward a recharge station; when its path is blocked, the collision-avoidance layer takes over and redirects it, after which it resumes building the map from a different location. The designers did not precompute this path. What should I predict she will say about the robot, and what rule is she implicitly running? I can formulate at least three candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts: flexibility requires individual trial-and-error learning; flexibility requires behavior that can be interrupted and reorganised in real time around a goal; flexibility requires behavior shaped by consequences over the organism's history. Which of these respect her earlier verdicts, and what does each predict for the robot? Which earlier case is the closest analog, and what further hypothetical case would most cleanly separate the remaining candidate rules?
