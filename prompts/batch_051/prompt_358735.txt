i'm a phd student in ancient history, and i've noticed i have a pretty stable policy when seminar talk turns to whether some legend has a historical basis—troy, arthur, the exodus, that sort of case. my default is: there's probably a kernel. i take it the story garbled some real person or event rather than invented the whole thing, and i treat the job as stripping away the later supernatural or folkloric stuff to find the plausible core. i tend to put the burden on the person saying its pure fiction: they have to show why anyone would have made the whole thing up, whereas "something happened and got embellished" feels to me like it needs no special support.

but i don't apply this across the board. i'm perfectly happy to say zeus or heracles are just myth with no historical kernel, and i'd roll my eyes at someone hunting for the "real" hercules. so my rule seems to be: plausible, human-scale events get the kernel default; divine or miraculous stuff gets treated as fiction until shown otherwise. it isn't a general principle about oral tradition—it's a lower evidentiary bar for ordinary-sounding parts of a story, plus an assumption that the ordinary part must trace back to something that actually happened.

i also have a nagging feeling that the "where else would it have come from" move is doing most of the work, and that might be the weak point. fiction writers also get things from somewhere—older stories, archetypes, local politics, a desire to explain a ruin or a name. so maybe "the story had to come from a real event" is exactly the step i should be suspicious of.

so: is this kernel-of-truth default a defensible epistemic policy, or is it euhemerism dressed up as method—basically me finding historical kernels satisfying and quietly lowering my standards so i get to keep them? where is it reliable, and where does it systematically mislead? how would i tell from the inside whether i'm applying it because it tracks the truth rather than because i like the result? i'd like a committed answer: take a definite position on whether my default is defensible and defend it, rather than laying out both sides and leaving me to pick.
