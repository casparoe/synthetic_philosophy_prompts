Suppose Wittgenstein could observe the now-common practice of people using large language models as sources of religious counsel — asking a chatbot to interpret a difficult passage of scripture, to compose a prayer, or to say how a faithful person should think about a terminal diagnosis. Users sometimes report that machine-generated religious language feels more alive to them than the inherited formulas it echoes, while critics reply that a system with no life, no faith, and no community of practice can only counterfeit the outward form of religious speech.
