I'm a retired geologist and I've spent the last year or so working through the qualia literature, mostly because I got annoyed at people in my hiking group saying science will never explain the redness of red and I wanted to get clear. I've ended up with a set of commitments that feel right to me individually, but I cant tell whether they hang together. I'd like you to audit them.

Here is where I've landed:

(1) I'm a physicalist about the mind. Everything there is, including conscious experience, is made of the same stuff as everything else, just organized in certain ways.

(2) But I don't think qualia are an illusion or just a word for dispositions. There is something it is like to taste an IPA or smell dry pine needles, and that what-it-is-like is real and not reducible to what I can discriminate or report, even though it is physically realized.

(3) I find the Chase and Sanborn coffee taster example from Dennett pretty persuasive in showing that a lot of what philosophers call introspection of qualia is actually just beliefs about how things seem now, and those beliefs can go wrong in the way ordinary beliefs can. So the idea that you can just look inside and read off the intrinsic quality of your experience is not as innocent as it sounds.

(4) I also think Mary learns something when she leaves the black and white room, but I think its a new ability or a new way of thinking, not a new fact about the world. So I'm not a property dualist.

(5) I think comparisons of qualia over time and between people are partly stipulative or practical. If you and I both say a beer tastes bitter, there is no deep fact about whether your bitter matches my bitter; we are coordinating around shared responses, not matching an inner showcase. But I still think each of our token experiences has its own qualitative character.

(6) Finally, I think there is a genuine explanatory gap between first-person experience and third-person descriptions, and a theory of consciousness that just denies the gap will not satisfy me. But I do not think the gap forces dualism.

The part I cant sort out is whether (2), (3), and (5) pull against each other, and whether (4) and (6) are really compatible under (1). Go through the list and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look that way, and rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most without me having to abandon physicalism or the reality of what it is like.
