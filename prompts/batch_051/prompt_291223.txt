I keep coming back to a strange asymmetry between physical stuff and intellectual property, and I want you to help me work out what philosophical commitments are buried in it. Here's the practice I mean. If you leave a car rusting in a field for twenty years, or a bike locked to a railing for months, the law has doctrines of abandonment, lost property, and adverse possession that eventually let someone else claim the thing. But if a novel was published in 1955, the author died in 1970, the publisher went under in 1980, and the rights have ended up with heirs nobody can trace, the book is still in copyright. In most jurisdictions that's life of the author plus seventy years, automatic, with no renewal or registration required; nobody can reprint it, digitise it, or put it in an online archive without infringing, even though there is no one to grant a licence or be harmed by the use. Same with abandoned software: a program written in 1994 by a company that folded in 1999 is still protected decades later, so you can't legally patch it to run on modern hardware or preserve it in an emulator. Physical property can be abandoned; copyright apparently can't.

Now the question. If copyright were purely a utilitarian device to incentivise creation, an orphaned work would fall into the public domain automatically, because enforcing rights there can't incentivise anyone to create anything. So the practice seems to presuppose something stronger — maybe an authorial natural right, or the idea that a work is somehow an extension of its creator. But then why can you sell a copyright outright to a corporation while you're alive, and why do moral-rights protections (where they exist) cover only part of that picture? I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory of ownership this practice presupposes, carefully separating what the non-abandonability of copyright strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and saying where the practice is underdetermined between rival theories. Then tell me what would actually have to change in how we treat orphan works and abandoned software if we switched to a clean incentive-based view. Please answer in British English.
