At a reading group on distributive ethics, a friend walked me through an argument and now I can't find the exit. The argument purports to show that prioritarianism is an unstable halfway house: it cannot hang onto its signature idea — that a given benefit matters more the worse off its recipient is — without collapsing into the very egalitarian view the leveling-down objection was designed to refute. Here is the chain as I wrote it down.

(1) Prioritarianism says the moral value of a fixed-sized benefit is greater, the worse off the person who receives it. (2) So on this view the value of an outcome is not fixed by the sum of well-being alone; it also depends on how that well-being is located across persons. (3) If the value of an outcome depends on how well-being is located across persons, then the pattern of distribution is itself one of the respects in which outcomes are morally evaluated. (4) If the pattern of distribution is itself a respect of evaluation, then a pattern containing less inequality must be better in at least one respect than a pattern containing more, other things equal. (5) But then a change that reduces inequality by making a better-off person as badly off as everyone else, while making no one better off, is an improvement in at least one respect: it has a better pattern. (6) That is precisely the leveling-down conclusion that prioritarianism was supposed to avoid. (7) So prioritarianism either accepts the leveling-down conclusion, in which case it is egalitarianism in disguise, or it denies (2), in which case it gives up the priority claim and collapses into total utilitarianism. (8) Either way, there is no stable position between utilitarianism and egalitarianism.

Every step looks plausible to me on its own, but the conclusion seems too strong. I'd most like to keep the priority claim in (1) and (2), but not at the cost of saying that harming one person and helping no one can still count as making the world better. Which step should I reject, why did it look so natural, and what do I pay for rejecting it?
