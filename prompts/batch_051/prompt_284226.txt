"The sentence is odd but not false: it can be true in any situation where it's raining and I'm unaware of it, so there's no semantic contradiction. The oddness is pragmatic. Asserting p normally expresses or at least implies that the speaker believes p; by adding 'but I don't believe it', the speaker explicitly denies the state that their own assertion displays. So the assertion is self-undermining in a way that isn't captured by truth conditions. The belief case, however, is different and much simpler. To believe the conjunction, I'd have to believe p and also believe that I don't believe p. That is impossible: a single mind can't hold both p and ~Bp as beliefs, because the second belief directly contradicts the first. So Moore's paradox only really exists for assertion, and the lesson is that assertion is governed by a norm requiring belief. The 'commissive' version, where someone says 'p, but I believe not-p,' is just a person with contradictory beliefs, which is possible and therefore not paradoxical."
