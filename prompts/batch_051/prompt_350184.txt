I'm a novelist, and I've spent years writing dialouge in dialect, so I've gotten a little obsessive about what an alphabet is actually supposed to do. A linguist friend laid this argument out for me over drinks the other night, and I can't shake it: the conclusion seems crazy, but every step felt fine at the time.

(1) An alphabet is, at bottom, a theory of a language's phonology: it should have exactly one symbol per phoneme, no more and no less. If it marks a distinction that never changes meaning, it's redundant; if it misses a distinction that does change meaning, it's defective.
(2) What counts as a phoneme is defined by contrast within a single variety of the language. Two sounds are different phonemes for a speaker just in case swapping them can change the meaning of a word for that speaker.
(3) So whether something is a phoneme is relative to a speaker's variety. Whether "cot" and "caught" are a minimal pair depends on whether you have that merger, and whether "pin" and "pen" do depends on your vowels.
(4) Which means a spelling that's perfectly phonemic for one speaker is, from the standpoint of another speaker's phonology, misrepresenting the language: it either marks contrasts that aren't there for them or fails to mark ones that are.
(5) So there's no such thing as a spelling that's correct for English as a whole. Any orthography is just a phonological theory of some idiolect, and one such theory is as good as another.
(6) But that can't be right, because English spelling is plainly worse than a reformed spelling would be: people can't reliably pronounce an unfamiliar written word, and kids spend years memorizing spellings.
(7) So premise (1) must be false — an alphabet isn't really an encoding of phonemes at all.

I'd like to keep (1), since it's the only thing that makes my phonetic spelling of dialect feel defensible, and I'd also like to keep (6), because I can't bring myself to say English orthography is fine. But something has to give. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the argument is actually valid, tell me which premise is least costly to give up and what I'd be paying. And keep the answer in American English, please.
