Here is the purpose I want the theory to serve: it should be good enough to underwrite a general code of practice for controlling invertebrate pests, covering both collective decisions (area-wide mosquito abatement, crop protection, stored-grain fumigation) and private ones (bedbugs, headlice, a wasp nest at a doorway), across chemical, biological, and mechanical methods. Write the specification such a theory has to meet before I am willing to rely on it. Give each requirement together with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than an optional refinement. Where a requirement is contested, say so and identify which camp insists on it. I do not want a survey of existing theories, a scorecard, or a bibliography; the deliverable is the evaluation standard itself.

Make sure the specification does not silently settle the following by stipulation: (i) the difference between nociception, nocifensive behavior, and felt pain; (ii) the fact that 'pest' is a relational status conferred by human interests rather than a natural kind of organism; (iii) the aggregation problem posed by populations in the billions per hectare; (iv) the claim that many pest individuals would shortly die of starvation, predation, or weather anyway; and (v) the standing of collateral deaths among non-target arthropods from broadcast treatments. Treat these as stress cases the requirements must survive, not as the requirements themselves.

End with two judgments: which single requirement does the most to rule out the theories currently on offer, and which two requirements cannot be jointly satisfied as stated, with the direct argument for that conflict.
