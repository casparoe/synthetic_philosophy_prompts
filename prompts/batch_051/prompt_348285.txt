I keep going back and forth with a friend about whether I should take a bet. The bet: each round I stake my whole current balance, he flips a fair coin, and heads doubles the money while tails wipes out 60% of it. He points out the average result of one round is a 20% gain, so the bet has positive expected value and turning it down is just loss-aversion. But I've simulated it, and a single person who keeps playing round after round almost always ends up with next to nothing, even though the average across all possible histories goes up and up. He says I'm confusing the typical path with the average path, and that a rational person maximizes the average. Is he right? If not, what is the principle that should guide this kind of repeated decision, and does it amount to anything more than being risk-averse?
