After a long argument with a friend about whether quantum randomness could help free will, I found myself committed to a cluster of theses and I want them checked for coherence before I defend them in writing. My current view is this. (1) Free will is real, and it is incompatible with determinism: a free agent must have been able to do otherwise with the past and the laws fixed. (2) Yet I cannot see how an undetermined event at the moment of choice could be an exercise of agency rather than mere chance; if a decision were settled by a random microphysical event, it would be no more under my control than a determined one. (3) The best way to satisfy both commitments is to place the indeterminism earlier, in the deliberative process — in which considerations come to mind and with what force — while holding that the eventual choice is still caused by the agent's reasons and character. (4) I also hold that, to be responsible for an action, an agent must be its source in a way not reducible to a chain of undetermined happenings she did not control. (5) I therefore conclude that quantum-level indeterminism, if present in the brain, matters for free will only as a necessary enabling condition, never as the deciding factor. Which of these commitments are actually in tension, which merely appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the revision would cost.
