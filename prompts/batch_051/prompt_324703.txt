I've ended up as the only under-40 regular at my aunt's Sunday discussion group, and for the last month the conversation keeps getting derailed by the same move. Any time quantum mechanics comes up, a retired chemistry teacher there named Frank says Einstein already sorted all this out with his line about God not playing dice, then sits back like the question has been answered. Last week I finally asked what God's gambling habits have to do with whether our decisions are free, and Frank just repeated the line slower. So now I want the careful answer he clearly cant give, and I'd like to bring something in writing next week.

From what I've managed to read, Einstein's remark was aimed at early quantum mechanics: he resisted the idea that the theory's probabilities are the final story rather then a sign that the theory is incomplete. But in my group the line gets used way beyond that. My aunt, who is a very serious Catholic, treats it as evidence that the universe has an order we can trust; Frank treats it as a point about physics and determinism; and someone else last week used it to say chance isn't real, so we are free and responsible in the old-fashioned way, which struck me as almost the opposite of the determinism reading. I've also seen it said that Einstein's God was closer to Spinoza's God than to the personal God my aunt prays to, so the theological reading can't be straightforward either.

I don't want a single interpretation. I want a menu: the different precise claims the God-doesn't-play-dice slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical, and for each one a note on whether it's a claim about physics, about God, about free will, or some mix. Then say which of those readings would actually support Frank using it to close down a discussion of quantum randomness, and which would support my aunt's use of it as a point about cosmic order. Flag the readings that are almost certainly false and, as best as you can tell, the ones Einstein himself probably held. A bulleted list would be ideal, since I'm planning to read it out loud.
