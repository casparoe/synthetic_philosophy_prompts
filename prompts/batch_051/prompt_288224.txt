Had a long argument with a friend last night that's still bugging me. He's been reading about super-immersive VR and whole-brain uploads, and he keeps saying none of it matters for how we should live because such a life is just Nozick's experience machine with better graphics. In his telling, the moment a hedonic theory of well-being starts to say "sure, a maxed-out simulation could be as good a life as any," the machine is right there to kill it: almost nobody would chose to plug in, so there has to be something beyond the felt quality of experience that makes a life go well. His line was something like "if you make the simulation good enough, you have just rebuilt the tank, and the answer is still no."

Now, I know the rough setup of the original case, I think: a tank where electrodes feed you any experiences you want, indistinguishable from the real thing, while you float there cut off from actually doing and being anything and from actual other people. But how far does that analogy actually carry over to a shared, steerable, probably-leaveable simulation? I want the full audit. Give me the correspondence table—what in the VR/upload case plays the role of the machine, the electrodes, the real world outside, the friends you leave behind, the moment of plugging in. Then give me the places where it breaks. Does the new scenario preserve the features that did the real work in Nozick's case—the isolation, the irreversibility, the fact that someone else wrote the menu, the sense that you would be a particular kind of person rather than merely having particular experiences? And once those disanalogies are on the table, does my friend's conclusion survive, or was the analogy carrying the argument the whole time?

Bonus if you can tell me whether there is a better contemporary stand-in for the experience machine that keeps the force of the original point without smuggling in features the new case lacks—and what that shows about whether the real target is hedonic well-being, or just a very specific fantasy of a solo, sealed-off, pre-authored life.
