I've been turning over the old puzzle of how music can be sad at all, since a piece of music isn't the kind of thing that has moods the way people do. I'd like a working definition of "sad music" (or "a piece of music's being sad," if you prefer) that actually does some work. Below are seven cases. Propose the definition first, run it through all seven, and say where it breaks or gives a surprising answer; then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. For the cases where I say I have no view, I don't mind which way you come down as long as the answer is definite and follows from something you can state.

Cases:
1. A wordless funeral march, slow, low register, minor key, long falling phrases. Obviously sad.
2. A brisk major-key tune sung at a birthday party. Obviously not sad.
3. A pop song with an upbeat major-key melody and a bouncy rhythm, but the lyrics describe a breakup and the singer's delivery is flat and drained. I say this one is sad.
4. A minor-key techno track with a drooping synth line over a pounding beat that people dance to. I genuinely have no idea whether this is sad.
5. A slow, low, descending lullaby sung to an infant to calm them down. It sounds a little mournful to me but its meant to soothe, so no idea.
6. An aggressive protest song with a distorted minor-key riff and furious shouted lyrics. I say its not sad, its angry, but a dumb minor-key rule would call it sad.
7. An algorithmically generated "sad piano" loop from a streaming playlist, tagged and rated as sad by listeners; no human composed it and nobody felt anything while it was made. I lean toward counting it as sad, but maybe thats just me deferring to the label.

After the discussion, give me a JSON object (and nothing else after it) with these exact fields:
- "definition": your proposed working definition, one sentence.
- "case_verdicts": an array of seven objects, each with fields "case_id" (1–7), "label" (a two-to-four-word tag), "pre_theoretic_verdict" (one of: "sad", "not sad", "unsure"), "definition_verdict" (one of: "sad", "not sad", "no answer"), "matches" (true or false, or null if pre_theoretic_verdict is "unsure" or definition_verdict is "no answer"), and "note" (one sentence explaining the verdict).
- "revision": if you revised the definition after testing it, give the revised version here; if you kept the original, write "kept original" and briefly defend the mismatches.
- "least_secure": which of the seven verdicts you're least confident about and why, one sentence.
