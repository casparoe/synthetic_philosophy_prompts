My friend has a version of Strawson's Basic Argument that she says shows something absurd, and I can't find the weak step. Her steps: (1) To be genuinely responsible for what you do, you'd have to be genuinely responsible for the way you are, since your actions flow from how you are. (2) To be responsible for the way you are, you'd have to have chosen to be that way; but choosing already requires being some way you didn't choose. (3) So the demand to be responsible for yourself goes back forever, and nobody is genuinely responsible for anything they do. (4) Forming and revising beliefs is something you do, and it too flows from how you are — your evidence, your habits of attention, your prior beliefs. (5) So by the same reasoning, nobody is genuinely responsible for their beliefs either. (6) But that can't be right: we hold people responsible for their beliefs all the time, like when someone ignores evidence or engages in wishful thinking. (7) So the argument proves too much, and one of the steps has to go. I'd like to keep (6), but I dont know what I'm paying.
