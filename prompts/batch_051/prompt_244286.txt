The argument I want to stress-test is roughly this. The original frame problem was about logic: if you want an agent to reason about actions, you can't just give it an axiom like "turning on the faucet causes water to run"; you also need a zillion frame axioms saying what doesn't change — the cat stays on the mat, the walls stay the same color, your keys stay in your pocket. Dennett and Fodor turned this into a deeper argument: the frame problem isnt just an implementation headache, it shows common sense can't be captured by explicit symbolic representations plus inference rules, because relevance and non-change facts are unbounded and there's no principled way to decide which of them matter.

Now the development: large language models seem to handle exactly this kind of common-sense inference without any hand-coded frame axioms. Ask one what happens after someone turns on a faucet, or whether the cat is still on the mat after you open the window, and it does fine, because it soaked up statistical regularities from an enormous amount of human text.
