I'm a philosophy PhD student working on aesthetics, mostly around fakes and the value of originals, and I keep going back and forth on a case I made up for a chapter. I'd love a committed ruling rather than a survey of positions, since I can get the survey from the literature.

The criteria I was taught for something counting as a forgery are roughly: (1) it was made with the intention to deceive about authorship, date, or provenance; (2) it materially or stylistically imitates an existing artist or period; (3) it was actually passed off under a false attribution; and (4) its value depends at least partly on that false attribution rather than on its own merits.

Now here's the case, and the painter is invented. Voss was an early-20th-century painter whose small still lifes are now worth millions; only about forty are known. In 1964 a conservation student makes, as a thesis project, a full-size copy of one of Voss's still lifes, using period canvas, period pigments, and Voss's documented palette. On the back she writes in pencil: "copy after Voss, 1964, for study." She never sells it. After she dies, a relative clearing the studio misses the faint note and sells the canvas cheaply at a local auction, listed only as "still life, midcentury." A dealer picks it up for a few hundred dollars, runs pigment analysis (everything is consistent, since the student used period materials), and sells it to a regional museum as a newly discovered Voss. Two senior experts sign off. It hangs as "Still Life with Lemons, c. 1926" for fifteen years, until the note on the back is found during a conservation treatment. The museum now shows the same canvas as "the famous fake," and attendance has gone up.

So: is the object a forgery at any stage — when the student made it, when the museum showed it as a Voss, or now that it's shown as a fake? Go criterion by criterion, say where each criterion gives an ambiguous answer, and then make a definite call. And does the case show that a forger's intent is essential, or does it show that "forgery" is mostly about a work's later history? If the criteria need revising, say how. I dont want a both-sides hedge; tell me which way you'd rule and why.
