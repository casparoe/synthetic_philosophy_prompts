I've been trying to figure out something about the way modern governments are arranged, because the pieces seem to pull in opposite directions.

On one side, the rules around elected office treat governing as something no one should do for too long. Presidents are term-limited, reformers push to limit legislators' time in office, and the phrase career politician is used as an insult. The assumption seems to be that ordinary citizens rotating through office make better, or at least safer, decisions than people who have spent decades accumulating power and instincts of their own.

On the other side, the same systems depend on exactly the kind of long service they distrust in politicians. The civil servants who actually draft and administer policy often stay in the bureaucracy for an entire career, and judges are deliberately insulated from elections, sometimes for life, on the theory that sound judgment requires accumulated experience and freedom from short-term pressure. More recently, some countries have added a third element: selecting ordinary citizens by lottery to spend months studying a single question and produce recommendations, as if the relevant kind of political judgment is something a random cross-section of citizens already has.

So one practice treats long experience as a disqualification, another treats it as essential, and a third treats the distinction as almost beside the point, because any attentive citizen can step in and deliberate. I want to know what view of political judgment, or of what makes a government's decisions legitimate, actually holds this pattern together, or whether the pattern is simply incoherent.

Please reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can. Say which parts of these practices strictly presuppose that theory and which are merely compatible with several different theories. Then tell me what would follow if one view were applied consistently: how would term limits and citizen lotteries, taken seriously, bear on career civil servants and life-tenured judges, and what would a genuine commitment to acquired governing expertise imply about elections and term limits?
