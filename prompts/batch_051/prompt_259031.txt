I’m a philosophy professor, and I’ve been rereading William James’s Principles of Psychology, the chapter on attention, with a colleague from media studies. Our disagreement is about whether the attention economy is really just James’s old problem of voluntary attention dropped into a new environment. His line is that people have always had to struggle to keep their minds on what matters, platforms merely exploit that struggle, and so the remedy is the same one James proposed: train the faculty of effortful attention, don’t regulate the environment. I’m not so sure.

He pointed me to a passage where James says, roughly, that the faculty of bringing back a wandering attention over and over again is the root of judgment, character, and will, and that an education which improved this power would be the education par excellence. But James seems to treat attention as a capacity the individual can repeatedly redeploy, while the platforms treat it as something to be harvested before the person ever gets to the point of effort. It feels like the analogy is doing a lot of work in my colleague’s argument, and I want to know how much weight it can actually bear.

Please audit the analogy properly: what maps onto what, where it breaks, which features of James’s picture the platforms quietly import without justification, and whether the responsibility-not-regulation conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Give me a correspondence table, separate the disanalogies that matter for his argument from the ones that are just superficial, and tell me whether there’s a better analogy that would do the same work more honestly. Please write the answer in British English.
