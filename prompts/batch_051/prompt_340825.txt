write me a villanelle about the boundary problem — the problem of saying who counts as part of the demos before the demos can legitimately decide anything. make the two refrain lines carry the two halves of the issue: one should be the all-affected principle (everyone affected by a decision ought to have a say in it), and the other should be the circularity worry (you already need a demos in place to figure out who is affected). something like 'all those affected ought to have a say' and 'but someone first must draw the demos' line' — tweak the wording if you need to, as long as the rhymes still work. i mean an actual villanelle: five tercets and a closing quatrain, the refrains alternating and then meeting in the last two lines. the form has to do work, not just decorate: the refrains keep coming back and picking up new glosses each time, which is how the problem actually behaves — you answer 'the people decide' and the next breath asks 'but who are the people?' dont just name-check the issue; someone who works in democratic theory should recognize where the all-affected principle is earning its keep and where the circularity bites. no citations obviously, it's a poem.
