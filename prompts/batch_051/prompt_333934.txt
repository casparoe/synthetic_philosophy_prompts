Here is a position I keep meeting in philosophy, which I want to see restated inside my own discipline. I work as a Bayesian statistician; I am fluent in decision rules on a state space, priors and posteriors, loss functions, Bayes risk, admissibility, and dominated procedures, and I already think of probabilistic coherence the way de Finetti described it: a body of betting odds is incoherent when a bookie can arrange a finite set of bets that you regard as individually fair and that together lose money in every possible state. The philosophical position is this: an agent's preferences over acts or lotteries are rational only if they form a complete and transitive weak order, and obey an independence condition and some continuity condition; the supporting argument is that an agent whose preferences violate these axioms is incoherent, where the incoherence is supposed to show up as vulnerability to a money pump or a Dutch book; and the expected utility representation theorem is then cited to show that any preference ordering satisfying the axioms can be represented as maximizing the expectation of some utility function. The position treats that representability not as a convenient summary but as a demand of rationality. I want you to restate this position — the axioms, the supporting arguments, and above all the move from representable to required — in my native vocabulary: decision rules, loss functions, Bayes risk, and admissibility. Do it systematically, claim by claim, and do not smuggle in statistical assumptions under the cover of translating. Then report the residue: which claims carry over cleanly, which you had to distort to fit, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In particular, I want to know whether the philosopher's claim that rational agents must maximize expected utility becomes, in my terms, the much weaker claim that a decision rule should not be dominated; and if so, whether the extra normative force is something my framework is missing or something the philosophical argument never actually earned. Say how confident you are in the translation and in your diagnosis of the residue, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
