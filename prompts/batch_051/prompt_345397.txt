I keep collecting accounts of what 'the physical' is supposed to mean in physicalism, specifically moves people make in response to Hempel's dilemma (if 'physical' means current physics, physicalism is almost certainly false; if it means some future/ideal physics, the claim is either vague or trivially open-ended). So far I've got four:

1. The currentist account: 'physical' means what current physics posits, and physicalism is just a risky empirical bet that whatever future physics adds won't turn out to be fundamentally mental.
2. The futurist account: 'physical' means whatever an ideal completed physics posits, with the extra stipulation that ideal physics stays physics-like — it's vocabulary excludes mental and normative terms.
3. The contrastive account: 'physical' just means not fundamentally mental, so physicalism is the denial of fundamental mentality and doesn't need a positive characterization at all.
4. The methodological account: 'physical' applies to what is publicly measurable, quantifiable, and law-governed in the way physics investigates things, so the definition doesn't have to track each theory change.

I suspect that covers the standard space, but I'm missing a fifth. Give me one more account of the physical that is genuinely different from all four — not a rewording, special case, or combination of them — and take a definite line on whether it actually escapes Hempel's dilemma or just gets impaled on one horn in a new spot.

I want the answer as a JSON object with these fields: 'new_account' (your proposed account, one or two sentences), 'type' (which bucket it belongs in: currentist, futurist, contrastive, methodological, or a fresh type you name), 'difference' (why it isn't just one of the four above), 'horn' (whether it takes the hit from the current-physics horn, the future-physics horn, both, or neither), and 'verdict' (your committed call on whether it's a real improvement or a dodge, with the reason).
