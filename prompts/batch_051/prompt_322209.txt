I've been a reader of rationalist and effective-altruism-adjacent forums for years, and the question I keep finding least tractable is whether larger populations are better. In a recent argument, a friend defended what he calls the additive intuition: for any world in which everyone's life is well worth living, a second world containing all the same people at the same welfare levels plus one additional person whose life is also well worth living is, other things equal, impartially better than the first. I don't feel that directly, but I notice that if I accept it, I face pressure toward conclusions I find much harder to accept, such as preferring an enormous population of lives barely worth living over a smaller population of excellent lives. I'd like to break the dispute into components rather than keep trading intuitions.

Please give me your current credence in the following proposition: for any two possible worlds that differ only in that one contains exactly one additional person, and where every life in both worlds, including the added one, is well worth living, the larger world is impartially better than the smaller world. Decompose your estimate into the main sub-questions it depends on. I'd especially like numbers for at least the following: the probability that some impersonal totalist axiology, on which welfare is summed or totalized in a way that gives positive weight to each additional life worth living, correctly captures betterness in both same-number and different-number cases; the probability that, conditional on totalism, no strict positive critical level or side-constraint blocks the mere addition; and the probability that a person-affecting view is correct under which merely adding a life worth living, without affecting the welfare of anyone in the original population, cannot make the resulting world better. Give a rough number and one line of justification for each term, show how they combine to your overall credence, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would most shift it. For calibration, my own credence is around 0.35 and my friend's is around 0.85; I'd also be interested in where you think the disagreement between us most likely lives.
