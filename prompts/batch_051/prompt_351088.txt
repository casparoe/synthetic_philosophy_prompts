I've been collecting objections to the true-judge/ideal-critic solution to the paradox of taste—the Humean move where the standard is whatever sufficiently delicate, practiced, unprejudiced critics converge on. So far I have: (1) circularity, the critics are picked out by the very verdicts they're supposed to ground; (2) enculturation, delicacy and practice just train someone's taste to whatever canon they were steeped in; (3) residual divergence, two judges can satisfy all five of Hume's criteria and still disagree; (4) conservatism about the new, a critic trained on the current canon has no non-question-begging way to judge a genuinely novel work; (5) the normativity gap, a full convergence among ideal critics doesn't give me a reason to defer to their pleasure. I think thats basically the standard set. Give me one more genuine objection that isn't a variant of anything on that list, and make sure it doesn't just collapse into the circularity one, since a lot of nearby arguments do. Then tell me honestly whether you think it actually damages the account or whether a Humean could shrug it off.
