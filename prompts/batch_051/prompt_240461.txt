I'm a high school student and co-run a small philosophy-and-technology discussion group at my school. For our next meeting, I have to lead a critique of an essay we read arguing that mind uploading could be genuinely life-extending rather than merely producing a copy of you. The essay's core move is an analogy. It says a person is an informational pattern, and the brain is just the particular hardware currently running that pattern. When a laptop's hard drive begins to fail, we move the files to a new drive; afterward we treat the novel, the spreadsheet, and the photo archive as the very same files, and we do not say the originals were destroyed. Uploading, the author claims, is the same operation performed on a person, and so an uploaded person really survives; refusing upload is presented as a confusion of sentimental attachment to old hardware with care for the person's continued existence. The author adds that the transfer need not be an instantaneous copy, and could instead be done gradually while consciousness continues, which the author says closes off objections about a gap or a duplicate.

I need to lead the discussion by laying out exactly how far this analogy holds. I would like a committed verdict rather than a survey of both sides: what maps onto what between file transfer and uploading a mind; where the analogy breaks down; which features of file-copying the analogy quietly imports that a person's survival may not have; and whether the author's conclusion that the uploaded person genuinely survives is actually licensed once the disanalogies are on the table. If the conclusion requires extra claims beyond the analogy, say precisely what they are and whether we have reason to accept or reject them.
