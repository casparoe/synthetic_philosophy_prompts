I am a teaching assistant for an upper-level philosophy of language course and am grading an exam on the semantics and pragmatics of slurs. The exam question was as follows: “Explain the main theoretical options for locating the derogatory force of slurs—broadly, semantic and pragmatic accounts—and critically evaluate at least two kinds of linguistic evidence that bear on the choice between them. You may discuss, for example, how slurs behave under embedding (negation, conditionals, indirect reports) and the character of non-derogatory or reclaimed uses. Defend a conclusion about which account is best supported.”

One student submitted the following answer: “The derogatory force of a slur is not part of its ordinary descriptive content; it is a kind of contempt or hostility toward the target group. The strongest motivation for a semantic account comes from projective behavior. When a slur appears under negation, as in ‘Lauren is not a [slur],’ a speaker can still convey hostility toward the group even though the sentence, read as ordinary truth-conditional content, denies membership. The same holds in the antecedent of a conditional and in indirect reports. Because conversational implicatures are not normally preserved under such embeddings, this persistence cannot be explained by Gricean conversational mechanisms. Since the derogatory content is non-cancelable and projects through embeddings, it follows that it must be part of the truth-conditional content of the sentence. This is enough to settle the debate in favor of semantic accounts over pragmatic accounts. The only serious complication is reclamation: in-group speakers sometimes use a slur affectionately or in solidarity. But such uses are marked and context-dependent, and they merely show that speakers can pragmatically modulate or suspend a word whose default, truth-conditionally encoded pejorative content remains intact. I conclude that the semantic account is clearly correct, and that pragmatic accounts are at best a supplement for explaining nonstandard uses.”

I am looking for a calibrated assessment rather than a general impression, so please address each main claim in the answer, indicating whether it is sound and why, and flag any distinctions the student has collapsed. In particular, your comments should speak to the relationship between projective behavior, truth-conditional content, and the various kinds of pragmatic explanation.
