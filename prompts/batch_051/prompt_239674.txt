I'm taking a course on markets and morals this term, and I keep running into the word commodification. The prof uses it like everyone knows what it means, but every time I try to write down a definition it breaks on one of the cases below, and the readings never give one clean formulation. I want a working definition of commodification, not a list of examples.

- A living person sells a kidney to a stranger through a broker.
- Someone buys a bag of apples at the supermarket.
- After a hurricane, a gas station charges $40 for a case of bottled water that was $8 the week before.
- A city starts selling $25 front-of-line passes at the DMV.
- A company pays you $3 a month for your precise location data.
- A plasma center pays donors $50 a visit.
- Someone pays a stranger $40 to vote for a specific candidate.
- An airline buys carbon offsets to cover the emissions from a flight.
- A friend helps you move and you buy them pizza and beer afterward.

I'm pretty confident the kidney sale and the bought vote are commodification, and the apples and the pizza-and-beer move aren't, but the rest I can argue either way. I also can't tell whether commodification is about money changing hands, or about pricing something that shouldnt be priced, or about treating something as fungible, since the cases pull in different directions. Propose a definition, run it through all nine, and tell me where it forces you to defend a surprising verdict rather than the obvious one.
