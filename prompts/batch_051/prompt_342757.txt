I'm a board member on our regional flood-control district, and Thursday we vote on a $140 million bond to raise and extend the levee along the old industrial waterfront. I have to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting, and honestly my rationalist-forum reading is making this worse, not better: half of what I absorbed says uncertainty is the whole ballgame, the other half says inside-view model fights are a distraction and you should take the outside view and just build.

The staff report hands us two things. One is a 27-member regional climate model ensemble downscaled to our coast. The members basically all agree on warming, but they disagree hard on storminess and extreme precipitation; roughly a third say the current proposal—sized for 3 feet of sea-level rise by 2080 plus a 100-year storm—is adequate, a third say it's undersized, and the rest are scattered. The other is a consultant's decision-focused analysis that averages the ensemble into one central number and recommends the full design. A dissenting member, who wants to defer a year to commission more modeling, keeps saying that deciding under this much spread is just arbitrary. The chair wants the money committed before the bond window closes in the fall, and another member keeps saying the dissenters position is just status-quo bias.

One thing I suspect but am not confident about: the model members are calibrated to the same historical record, so theyre not really independent witnesses, which means both the "a third of them agree" argument and the "spread proves we know nothing" argument are weaker than they look. I want the memo to sort out which parts of the ensemble spread are decision-relevant (the levee height we'd build changes depending on which member you believe) and which parts are just uncertainty theater.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate, 900–1200 words. Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete course of action and a timeframe, and engage the dissenting member's position seriously rather than dismissing it. If you go with conditions, make them specific enough to vote on — for example an adaptive-design option or a monitoring trigger, if you think that's right. You're a climate-risk decision analyst who writes for a general technical audience. Think carefully, maybe sketch the considerations in scratch form before writing the memo. Write the memo itself in American English, and steelman the strongest argument for waiting.
