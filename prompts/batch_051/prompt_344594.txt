I am writing up a statement of my position on the quantum state for a reading group, and I would like an audit of whether the following commitments jointly hold together. They all seem individually right to me, but I do not trust my sense of how they interact.

(1) I lean towards an epistemic reading of the wavefunction: the wavefunction represents an agent's information, or rationally warranted expectations, rather than a physical object or field existing independently of anyone's knowledge.
(2) I accept the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem, but I take its force to be conditional: if the wavefunction is epistemic, then the ontic states — the states systems are really in, independently of what is known about them — of independently prepared systems cannot be assumed to factorise.
(3) I regard that failure of factorisation as a very high price. It would mean that two systems with no history of interaction could nonetheless share a hidden correlation in their ontic states, and I am not willing to accept hidden correlations of that kind.
(4) I think measurement outcomes are perfectly definite and observer-independent: when a detector fires, it fires.
(5) I think wavefunction collapse is not a physical process at all; it is merely an agent conditionalising on learning which definite outcome occurred.
(6) I am inclined to say that the wavefunction is complete, in the sense that, between measurements, there is nothing more to be said about the state of the system than the wavefunction says.
(7) I reject both retrocausal explanations and superdeterminism; neither seems to me a serious option.

Please go through these commitments and identify which pairs or triples are in genuine tension, which pairs merely appear to be in tension and dissolve on inspection, and how severe each real tension is. Then say whether there is a single revision that would do the most repair, and what that revision is and what it would cost.
