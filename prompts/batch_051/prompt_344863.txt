I want a working definition of bullshit, not merely a gesture at the ordinary word. The definition should state what the bullshitter is doing with respect to truth, and it should make bullshit come out distinct from both lying and honest error. I want to preserve the Frankfurt-style contrast: the liar is at least attentive enough to the truth that they try to steer their audience away from it, whereas the bullshitter is indifferent to how things actually are. But I want that contrast tested, not assumed.

Here are the cases I need the definition to classify:

(a) A corporate spokesperson asserts that a product is the most effective on the market. She has never examined the data and does not care whether the claim is true; she selected it because it tested well with focus groups and advances the company's position.

(b) A witness testifies in court that she saw the defendant at the scene at a time when she knows she saw him somewhere else, intending the jury to believe her.

(c) A factory inspector records precise chlorine-level readings in a logbook without having taken the measurements. The numbers are plausible, and it later turns out the real levels did fall within the range he wrote down, though he did not know this at the time and was not trying to track the truth.

(d) An astronomer reports a spectral measurement after careful calibration; the figure is later traced to a faulty detector and turns out to be wrong.

(e) A large language model, prompted to summarize a recent legal decision, produces a fluent and confident paragraph citing a case name and holding that do not exist. The system has no beliefs, no plans, and no intent to mislead, and it was not consulting any record; it was trained to maximize the plausibility of its continuations.

(f) A student who has done none of the reading writes an essay on the causes of a historical revolution by stringing together plausible generalities to meet a word count. Much of what he writes happens to be true, but he aimed only at sounding competent.

(g) A financial pundit announces an unverified but provocative explanation for a sudden market swing, selecting it because it will make a compelling segment. The explanation later turns out to be correct by luck, though at the time he had no evidence for it.

I take (b) to be a clear case of lying and (d) to be a clear case of honest error, and I would be surprised if (a) and (c) were not bullshit. The rest I cannot place with confidence, and the language-model case especially resists classification.

Please propose a definition, run it through each case, and say where it yields a surprising verdict or no verdict. Where it misfires, either revise it or defend the surprising classification. In the language-model case, make explicit whether your criterion turns on what the speaker believes or intends, or instead on what the output does within a larger epistemic practice, since a language model has no mental states yet its outputs can corrupt a shared factual record in something like the way bullshit does. End by telling me which of your final verdicts is the least secure.
