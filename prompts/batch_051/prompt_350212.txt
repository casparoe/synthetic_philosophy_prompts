I've been thinking about the way safety and security do very different work in different arguments. Sometimes they're treated as threshold values: safe is a yes/no property and we stop asking questions once something is safe enough. Sometimes they're treated as continuous goods you can trade against time, money, or liberty. Sometimes they're treated as side-constraints that can't be traded off at all. The same phrase safe enough might mean very different things depending on whether we're talking about nuclear power, vaccine injury, or surveillance. Suppose you were setting the agenda for a small research programme — four people, maybe three years, mostly political philosophy and decision theory, no big empirical studies — whose job is to clarify how a society should think about tolerable risk. Which two or three open questions should they prioritize, and why those? Rank them, defend the ranking, and say which assumptions you'd refuse to let them outsource. I'd rather have a committed take that's wrong than a list of options.
