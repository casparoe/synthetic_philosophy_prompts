In a governance reading group, a futarchy advocate defended the proposal with an analogy. A prediction market, he said, is just an election that never closes: traders stand in for voters, positions for ballots, prices for a running tally; participants can split their stake as finely as they like and change it the moment they learn something new, and putting money behind a view signals confidence where an ordinary vote signals only preference. From this he concluded that replacing per-policy votes with prediction markets would not abandon democracy—it would be democracy with better information, inheriting democratic legitimacy while adding the accuracy of markets. Audit this analogy. Give the correspondence table explicitly: voter, ballot, tally, franchise, campaign, accountability—what plays each role in the market? Then mark the points where the mapping breaks, separate the breaks that matter for the legitimacy conclusion from the ones that are mere differences, and say whether futarchy remains fairly described as democracy with better information or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion.
