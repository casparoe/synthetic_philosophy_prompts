Write the joint statement that a quantifier variantist and a heavyweight ontological realist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the question whether disputes over composite material objects are substantive. Provide just enough framing that the two positions are recognizable: the quantifier variantist holds that different communities can permissibly use “there exists” with different but equally legitimate meanings, so that one side’s “tables exist” and the other’s “only simples arranged tablewise exist” need not express conflicting claims; the heavyweight realist holds that some existential quantifier meanings are more natural, fundamental, or joint-carving than others, so that an existence question can have a determinate, non-verbal answer even when ordinary language underdetermines it.

The statement should have two parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides accept, including the basic linguistic data, what formal or translational moves are uncontroversial, what each side is not claiming about the other, and any methodological norms both share. Second, a disagreements table. Its rows should capture the remaining disputes in wording each side would accept for its own position, including at least: whether there is a uniquely joint-carving meaning for the existential quantifier; whether the two sides, when they appear to disagree about composite objects, assert conflicting propositions under a common meaning; and whether the quantifier variantist’s multiplicity of meanings can itself be stated without presupposing a privileged meaning of “there is a meaning.”

For each row, identify the crux—the sub-question that, if settled one way, would move at least one side—and give a concrete case, argument, or body of linguistic or theoretical evidence both sides agree bears on it, recording each side’s prediction in advance. Finally, state which parts of their original dispute both now classify as merely verbal and which they classify as genuine, and explain why.
