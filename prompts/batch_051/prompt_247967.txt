A friend of mine has a set of opions about whether it's good for someone to get excellent at something, and I'm trying to figure out the rule underneath them. So far she's said:

- Good: a painter who loved painting, got astonishingly good, but never showed anyone; the paintings turn up only after she dies.
- Not good: someone who became an expert torturer, even though it took real discipline and skill.
- Good: a runner who trained for years and ran a world-class time alone in an empty stadium; she hated the training but says it was worth it.
- Not good: a forger who got perfect at faking old masters, even though he never sold one.
- Good: me getting actually good at cello even though I only ever play alone in my apartment.
- Not good: a guy who memorized pi to a hundred thousand digits on a dare; she says that's storage, not excellence.

Next I'm going to ask her about this one: a woman spends decades becoming a master of a difficult, respected instrument, hates every minute of it, never performs for anyone, and just before she dies she destroys the only recording she ever made. My friend will know all that, including the misery. What should I expect her to say?

I'm less interested in the bare yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit her verdicts just as well, which earlier case is the closest analog, and which extra case would settle between the rules. Also: what would change your mind about the rule you attribute to her, or where is your read on her shakiest?
