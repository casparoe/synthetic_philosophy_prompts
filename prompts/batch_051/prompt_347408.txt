I'm drafting a handout for a graduate seminar on causal modeling, and next week we're working through Pearl's structural causal models and the do-calculus. I want the handout to open with an honest inventory of what a person commits to when they write down a particular SCM and treat it as a causal representation of a system rather than a merely statistical description of one, before we ascend the ladder from association to intervention to counterfactual. So far I have six items, and I can say why each is a separate commitment rather than a restatement of the others: (1) the causal graph is acyclic; (2) the joint distribution over the variables satisfies the causal Markov condition relative to that graph; (3) the exogenous noise terms are jointly independent; (4) the structural equations are modular, in the sense that each mechanism can be severed or replaced without altering the others; (5) intervention is local, in the sense that the do-operator replaces only the intervened variable's equation while leaving every other equation and the noise distribution intact; and (6) each structural equation is a deterministic function of its parents and its noise term, so that all randomness in the model lives in the exogenous variables.

I keep thinking there is at least one more commitment that students rarely notice until it fails, and that I should put on the list before discussing identification and the rules of the do-calculus. My sense is that it has something to do with how the graph and the equations are supposed to line up, such as whether every edge in the graph marks a dependence that actually shows up in the corresponding equation and whether every genuine dependence among the chosen variables is represented by an edge, or else with what kind of objects the variables themselves are assumed to be, such as their granularity or their stability when other parts of the system are altered. What additional assumption should I add as item (7)? It should be a commitment of the model itself, not a condition on estimation or sampling like positivity or no measurement error, and it must be genuinely distinct from the six items above rather than a special case, combination, or rewording of them. Then say briefly whether dropping it would break the do-calculus, or whether it is better understood as a modeling convention whose absence changes only what the model licenses you to claim.
