I have been reading a few old texts on time on my own, without much formal training, and I keep noticing a move I don't have a word for. A good example is in Book XI, Chapter XXIII of Augustine's Confessions, where he writes:

"What if the lights of heaven should cease, and a potter’s wheel run round, would there be no time by which we might measure those revolutions, and say either that it turned with equal pauses, or, if it were moved at one time more slowly, at another more quickly, that some revolutions were longer, others less so? Or while we were saying this, should we not also be speaking in time? Or should there in our words be some syllables long, others short, but because those sounded in a longer time, these in a shorter? ... Let no man then tell me that the motions of the heavenly bodies are times, because, when at the prayer of one the sun stood still in order that he might achieve his victorious battle, the sun stood still, but time went on. For in such space of time as was sufficient was that battle fought and ended. ... I see that time, then, is a certain extension."

The move I mean is not his conclusion that time is a certain extension; it is the move of getting there. He begins with the claim that time is the motion of the heavenly bodies, then shows those motions could stop or vary while time continued. But instead of concluding that time is nothing, or that the question is empty, he concludes that time is an underlying extension that such motions merely measure. I have seen the same shape of argument outside this text: someone asks what an abstract thing is, points to the concrete processes everyone uses to track it, shows that none of those processes can be the thing itself because they could vary while the thing stayed the same, and then says the thing is a kind of underlying extension that the processes measure. Does this argumentative move have an established name? If it does, how is it distinguished from a plain distinction between a thing and its measure? If it does not, what would you call it, and where are the edges of the concept?
