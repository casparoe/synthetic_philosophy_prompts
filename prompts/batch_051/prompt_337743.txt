Me and my friend argued for hours last night about transhumanism. I'm the one who thinks radical self-transformation is something we should actively want — cognitive enhancement, radical life extension, maybe uploading eventually. They're the committed bioconservative: they think there's something in our given limits, our given human nature, that we'd lose and shouldn't trade away for more capability or more life. By the end we'd found some places where we'd been talking past each other, but there's also a few where we still genuinely, precisely disagree. So we want to try the thing where the two opposing positions write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration. Could you draft that for us? We want a document both of us could actually sign. It should open with the substantive claims we both accept about the question itself — whether radical self-transformation is desirable and what would be lost from it — not mush like "this is complicated" or "both sides raise good points." Then give the exact formulation of our disagreement, phrased so each of us would say yes, that's my view stated right, not a strawman. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that if we got answers to them, at least one of us would have to move. And for each crux, the case or argument we'd both agree actually bears on it, with each side's prediction of how it comes out recorded in advance. If a disagreement is really just about words, say that and don't turn it into a deep dispute, but don't paper over the real ones either. Format: continuous prose only, like a real joint statement or a long letter. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
