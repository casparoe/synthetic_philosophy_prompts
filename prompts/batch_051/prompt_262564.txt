I am trying to adjudicate a strand of the debate over whether the Enlightenment is better characterised as a source of progress or a source of domination, and I have hit a preliminary obstacle: the central term, domination, is almost never defined, so I cannot tell whether the parties disagree about the concept or about the historical record. I would like a working definition of domination precise enough to test against cases, rather than a mere synonym.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

1. A colonial administration that governs a subject population without its consent, extracts taxes and labour, and punishes dissent.
2. An abusive spouse who controls the other's spending, movement, and contact with friends.
3. A parent enforcing a bedtime on a six-year-old.
4. A friend who talks you into seeing a film by giving you reasons.
5. A democratic state enforcing a law that a minority voted against and regards as unjust.
6. An employer directing a worker's tasks during paid hours, where the worker can quit but would lose their only income.
7. A welfare scheme that gives benefits only to claimants who accept any offered job.
8. A platform's automated recommender system that determines what news millions see, while its criteria remain undisclosed.
9. A doctor making treatment decisions for an unconscious patient without prior consent.
10. A lender who can use the courts to collect from a borrower who has no bankruptcy protection.

I am fairly confident that (1) and (2) are domination and that (3) and (4) are not; I genuinely do not know what to say about (5)–(10), though I suspect some of them will force a choice about whether domination is essentially a relation between persons or can be a structural condition that no single agent intends.

Please do the following. Propose a definition; run it through all ten cases and say for each whether it gives the verdict I expect, the opposite, or no clear verdict; then either revise the definition to fit the cases or explain why the surprising verdicts should be accepted. If you think the term does two jobs, for example one interpersonal and one structural, give me the two definitions and say which cases move between them. Close by explaining, in two or three sentences, what your definition implies someone must show in order to claim that the Enlightenment produced domination rather than progress.

Write the answer in British English. End your answer with a final line beginning 'Summary:' that restates the completed definition in a single sentence.
