In discussions of workplace monitoring and algorithmic management, it is common to reach for the Panopticon. The idea, drawn from Bentham's prison design and the use Foucault makes of it, is that the possibility of being observed at any moment—combined with the fact that the observed can never tell whether an observer is actually present—leads people to regulate their own behavior, so that power becomes internalized rather than imposed. Treat this as an analogy rather than as a label or an allusion. How far does it hold? Give the correspondence table (what in the modern workplace or platform plays the role of the cell, the central tower, the one-way visibility, the inspector, the inmates' separation from one another), then identify the disanalogies. Say which disanalogies matter for the intended conclusion and which are incidental. Finally, state whether the usual conclusion—that modern monitoring works primarily by making people watch and govern themselves even when no observer is looking—survives the disanalogies, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument.
