One thing I keep coming back to, now that I've retired from the lab and have time for this stuff, is Wikipedia. Here's a practice where a small number of unpaid editors do nearly all the work, and the huge mass of readers take the product without contributing anything, and nobody calls them free riders — if anything, editors insist that being read is the whole point. But if a company scrapes Wikipedia, wraps it in ads, and gives nothing back, the community does treat that as parasitic. So what implicit theory of what's wrong with taking without contributing does that pair of attitudes presuppose? Is it that free riding is okay when it doesn't make things worse for any contributer, that the encyclopedia is a gift once it exists, that readers contribute just by being an audience, or some mix? I'd like you to reconstruct that theory, and then be careful about something: tell me which parts the practice actually commits to and which parts are underdetermined, so that several philosophical theories could allow it. And say what would have to change if you took the hard line that any taking without contributing is a kind of parasitism. Continuous prose please — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just write it like a letter.
