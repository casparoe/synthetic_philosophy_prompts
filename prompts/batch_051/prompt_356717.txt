In my school philosophy group we keep circling back to the question whether human values are ultimately one thing or irreducibly many, and the same arguments keep being made without anyone moving. The monists hold that what looks like a conflict of values must in the end be settled by a single underlying standard—pleasure, preference satisfaction, or something of that kind—because otherwise hard choices would be arbitrary. The pluralists reply that values such as justice, love, curiosity, loyalty, and beauty can genuinely collide, and that any single scale would flatten what is actually at stake in such choices. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are not really in a substantive factual dispute: the monist is looking for a standard that would make all values commensurable in principle, while the pluralist is trying to describe the texture of actual deliberation, and the word "value" is being used for two different projects. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a real conceptual or even empirical disagreement underneath? And on your diagnosis, what would count as progress—a distinction, a kind of evidence, a sharper formulation of the question—or should we expect the impasse itself to be the lesson?
