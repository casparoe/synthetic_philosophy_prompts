I've realized I use the word 'commodification' constantly and have no actual defintion of it, just vibes. I don't mean 'wrong to buy or sell.' Buying a stolen painting is wrong, but nobody would call the problem 'commodification.' I want a definition of commodification itself — the thing that's supposedly happening in kidney sales or vote selling or the other stuff that worries people. And don't just tell me it's putting a price on something, because then buying bread counts and I'm pretty sure it shouldn't.

Here's the test set. Run your definition through all of them:

1. buying a loaf of bread at the grocery store
2. paying the neighbor kid to mow your lawn
3. selling your kidney to a stranger who needs it
4. a gas station charging $14 for bottled water two days after a hurricane
5. a plasma 'donation' center that gives you $50 per visit
6. a couple paying a surrogate to carry their baby
7. paying someone $30 an hour to be your platonic friend
8. selling your vote in a presidential election
9. a city selling the naming rights to its central library to a bank
10. a company paying employees $500 to get its logo tattooed on their arm

I'm confident the first two are not commodification and vote-selling is. Kidney selling I'd also call commodification, though I can't articulate why it differs from selling plasma or surrogacy. The rest I honestly can't sort. Propose the definition, run it through all ten, and tell me where your definition gives a verdict you'd want to defend anyway, and where it forces you to redraw the boundary somewhere surprising. If it helps: I keep flip-flopping on wether price gouging is commodification or just a separate complaint about unfair pricing, so a definition that cleanly separates those would be a bonus.
