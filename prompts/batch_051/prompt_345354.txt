Her track record, as best I remember:

- The single-player shooter that turns on the player — starts as a standard hero-shooter, then the white phosphorus scene makes you realize you killed a bunch of civilians, and the game starts treating the player as the problem (Spec Ops: The Line-ish): good, actually. But the framing is unmistakably condemning: civilians are named and shown with family photos, the ending is hollow, nobody comes off as a hero, and the devs worked with historians and survivors. No glory, no sanitizing, no triumphalism.
