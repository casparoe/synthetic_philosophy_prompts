I'm a philosophy of physics grad student, and next month I'm giving a work-in-progress talk to our department's philsci group. I walk into Q&A cold all the time, so I want the questions mapped out in advance. My thesis: the hole argument only refutes what I'll call rigid substantivalism — the view that spacetime points have primitive identity across distinct models of a theory — and after that it runs out of ammunition; it doesn't settle the live fight between relationalism and sophisticated substantivalism (where points are individuated by the overall pattern of metric and matter fields, so the two diffeomorphism-related models are one possible world described twice). I'll grant that relationalism and sophisticated substantivalism are empirically equivalent, but I'll insist they're different theories and that you can't get from the hole argument to picking either one. The quick version of the argument I'll give: take a generally covariant model (manifold, metric, matter fields), choose a hole region, and apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside the hole but not inside; because the field equations are diffeomorphism-invariant, you get two apparently distinct solutions that agree outside the hole but differ over which points have which fields, so determinism seems to fail — at least if you insist on individuating points across models.

Who'll be there: my advisor, a determinism-is-gauge-invariant person who thinks I'm showing up ten years late to a fight everyone left; a postdoc who works on this stuff and basically holds the line that substantivalism is the problem and should go; a grad student who's a hard relationalist and will say sophisticated substantivalism is relationalism with extra steps; and a cosmology postdoc who thinks the whole thing is a verbal dispute and will ask what observation could possibly turn on my conclusion. At least half the room hasn't worked through the diffeomorphism details in years, so the obvious "walk me through the setup" question is also coming.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll actually be raised, phrased as those people would phrase them, each with what I should concede, what I should hold, and a short reply that fits a Q&A. Also flag the objection I'm hoping nobody is sharp enough to ask, and tell me honestly where my own position is weakest so I dont bluff through it.
