ok here's where I've ended up on vagueness after messing with the sorites for a while, and I'd like a consistency check before I put this in a paper.

(1) I'm a supervaluationist: a sentence like 'Bob is bald' is true iff it comes out true on every acceptable way of making 'bald' precise, false iff it comes out false on every such sharpening, and neither true nor false when the sharpenings disagree.

(2) I still think classical logic is basically fine. In particular I accept excluded middle, so 'either Bob is bald or he isn't' is true even in borderline cases where the disjuncts are neither true nor false.

(3) I'm not an epistemicist. There's no hidden sharp boundary that we're simply ignorant of—no precise number of hairs separating bald from not-bald that the world or our language has already settled.

(4) I also think higher-order vagueness is real. 'Clearly bald' is itself vague; there's no sharp line between the clear cases and the borderline cases, and this doesn't stop at any finite level.

(5) At the same time, I want to say of a forced-march sorites from a clear case to a clear non-case that the sentence 'there is a last bald man in the series' is true, because every acceptable sharpening has to draw the line somewhere. But I can't point to any particuar guy in the series as the one where the boundary is.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. If I'm being sloppy in a way that matters, call that out before adjudicating.
