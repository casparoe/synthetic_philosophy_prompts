I'm in a philosophy of action course and we keep throwing around "acting together" like its obvious, but the minute I try to define it I either rule out things that are clearly joint or let in things that clearly aren't. I want a working definition of acting together — joint action, doing something as a group, whatever the best label is — that I could actually apply to new cases.

Here are the cases to run it through. Two friends carrying a couch up a flight of stairs. A string quartet playing through a piece from sheet music. Two strangers who happen to fall into step on a sidewalk and walk side by side for three blocks without acknowledging each other. A flash mob where strangers who have never met coordinate online to freeze in place in a train station at 3pm for exactly two minutes; some show up for the joke, some just to be part of something big, and a few only freeze because their friends freeze. A drug company hikes a price after executives, lawyers, and accountants each do their assigned task, even though no single person intended the final number and some thought it was a bad idea but did their piece anyway. And two people carrying a table up a walkway where one believes he's just helping a friend move and the other believes they are co-owners moving it into their shared apartment.

I think the first two are clearly joint and the sidewalk case clearly isn't; the rest I don't have a confident verdict on. Propose the definition, run it through the cases, and say where it gives a verdict you'd want to revise or defend as a suprise.
