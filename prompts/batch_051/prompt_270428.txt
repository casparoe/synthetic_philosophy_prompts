I keep going in circles on this game theory point and I want someone to make me pick a side, so here's the setup. I choose a row, you choose a column, and these are my payoffs:

Left: A pays 3, B pays 0, C pays 1.
Right: A pays 0, B pays 3, C pays 1.

Notice C is the only row that never leaves me with 0 — it guarentees 1 whichever column you pick. A and B each make 3 on their good side and 0 on their bad side.

The standard iterated-deletion story, once you allow mixed strategies, says C is strictly dominated and therefor irrational. The argument: if I flip a fair coin between A and B, then against Left my expected payoff is the average of 3 and 0, i.e. 1.5, and against Right it's also 1.5. So the coin flip pays 1.5 against both columns, which strictly beats C's 1 against both columns. No pure row beats C in both columns, but a mixture does, so C gets deleted. (Iteration doesn't run further after that — A and B each look best against one of the columns — so the whole dispute is in that first deletion.)

I can't find a hole in the arithmetic, and I do like that dominance gives me a decision rule that doesn't depend on guessing your column. But my gut won't budge: the coin flip only averages to 1.5. In a one-shot game the coin can land the wrong way and I actually walk away with 0. C is the one thing I can choose that rules out 0. Comparing C to a probability distribution over two other acts feels like comparing an action I can perform to something I can only half-perform, and it treats the mixture's expected payoff as if the mixture itself delivered that payoff. If I refuse to randomize — or if what matters to me is the worst realized outcome rather than the expectation — then C looks like the sanest row available.

So which side should give way? Is the "C is the safe row" intuition just a confusion that expected-utility reasoning is right to steamroll, or does mixed-strategy dominance have a substantive assumption baked in about mixtures being chosen acts with their expectations as payoffs? Is there a reading where both are correct, just about different senses of "doing better no matter what the column is"? Give me the best version of each side and then a verdict on which parts to keep and which to drop.
