My friend and I just spent two hours arguing about how much risk a society should tolerate, and he kept saying its inconsistent: I'm fine forcing people to wear seatbelts but not with a speed limit that would save way more lives. He might be right, but I want to see whether there's actually a principle behind my gut verdicts, or at least a small set of candidate principles.

My verdicts, as of tonight:
- Seatbelt laws: fine, even though I wouldn't ban cars.
- Motorcycle helmet laws: fine too, even though the risk is the rider's own.
- A universal 20-mph speed limit on highways: I don't support it, even though it would likely save more lives than seatbelt laws do.
- A kid's toy with a one-in-a-million chance of killing the kid who plays with it: I'd ban it without much hesitation.
- A vaccine with a one-in-a-million serious reaction, where the disease kills one in a thousand: I'd probably require it for school.
- Stop-and-frisk or bulk surveillance, even if the numbers showed they'd prevent some terrorist attacks: not fine.

Is there a single principle (or a few candidates) that gets all of these, or most of them, and what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on — banning peanuts in schools to protect allergic kids? If some verdict has to go, which one, and why?
