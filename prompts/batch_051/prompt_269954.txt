I keep coming back to an asymmetry in how we treat unproven treatments. A doctor can put a patient on an off-label drug because she has a hunch, a mechanism story, a couple of case reports—nobody demands that the expert community be genuinely split about whether it works. But if the same doctor wants to randomize patients between that drug and standard care to actually find out whether her hunch is right, the ethics committee will bring up clinical equipoise and may block the trial precisely because the professional community hasn't reached honest disagreement. So it's acceptable to give all your patients the unproven thing on your own say-so, but not acceptable to enroll them in a study where half might get standard care instead. Is there a principle that makes those two situations genuinely different, or have we just made trials carry all the ethical anxiety that we should also be feeling in clinic? I want the strongest justification you can give for treating the trial as the problem, and an honest verdict on whether you'd keep the asymmetry.
