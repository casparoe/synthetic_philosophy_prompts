my brother got super into longtermism recently and now we've been arguing about the doomsday argument for like a month, and it turned out the whole dispute comes down to whether to accept SIA (the self-indication assumption). I've been collecting the standard objections to SIA so I can win the next family dinner. So far I have four, and I'm told this is basicly the complete standard list: (1) the presumptuous philosopher — SIA makes you nearly certain there are vastly more observers somewhere than our telescopes have any evidence for, just from your own existence, which seems flat-out crazy; (2) the reference-class problem — SIA only gives you a number once you decide which beings count as observers, and you can gerrymander that class until you get whatever answer you want; (3) the infinite-populations problem — if two rival theories each say there are infinitely many observers, the weighting blows up and SIA stops giving any usable answer; (4) the duplication objection — SIA rewards theories that make lots of copies or near-copies of your current state (branches, uploads, Boltzmann-ish duplicates), even though those copies don't predict anything new about your experiences. Give me one more objection to SIA that isn't on this list, and isn't a restatement, special case, or combination of any of them — something with a different structure from all four. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good, or whether I'd embarass myself bringing it up at dinner.
