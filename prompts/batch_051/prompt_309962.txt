I'm a high school student, and I've been trying to work out a definition of honesty for AI systems. I have a distinction I keep using in arguments with my friends, and I'd like an honest assessment of it.

I call it local honesty versus systemic honesty. A system is locally honest when, on a particular occasion, its output does not assert or imply anything false in context. A system is systemically honest when its overall pattern of outputs, across the contexts in which it is actually used, does not tend to leave its users with false beliefs, even if no single output is false. My central claim is that systemic honesty is the more important notion, and that local honesty is neither necessary nor sufficient for it. Put differently, a system can be deceptive without ever saying anything false, and can say false things without being deceptive.

For example, a homework-helper AI that always answers the question "Is my essay good?" with true but hedged praise—"this raises some interesting points"—is locally honest on each occasion, but systemically it leaves students overconfident, because the hedge is not something a student will decode. In the other direction, a system that says "I'm fairly sure the answer is X" and is well calibrated about its own uncertainty sometimes says something false locally, but systemically it leaves users with approximately correct beliefs.

I don't want my definition to require the system to have intentions or beliefs, since I'm not confident AI systems have them in any ordinary sense. My worry is that systemic honesty then depends on facts about the audience: the same policy might be systemically honest when used by experts and systemically deceptive when used by novices, and I can't tell whether that is a feature or a bug.

Is this distinction coherent, or does it collapse into something I should already know under another name? Where does it break? Please also tell me what would change your verdict: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different assessment, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
