The problem: a chunk of users' answers look internally inconsistent in a specific way my PM calls "the Allais thing," and we don't know whether to trust those users, re-ask them, or block them from auto-allocation.

Here's the exact battery we run. Each user sees two choices:

Pair 1: a sure $1,000, or a gamble that pays an 89% chance of $1,000, a 10% chance of $5,000, and a 1% chance of $0.
Pair 2: a gamble that pays an 11% chance of $1,000 (and an 89% chance of $0), or a 10% chance of $5,000 (and a 90% chance of $0).

The classic pattern is that people take the sure thing in Pair 1, but in Pair 2 they take the 10% shot at 5k. Apparently that's a violation of the independence axiom, because Pair 2 is just Pair 1 with the shared 89% chance of $1,000 removed. That mismatch actually matters for us: if it's a real preference (someone who weights certainty differently than a plain expected-value model), we shouldn't quietly overwrite it; if it's a mis-click or boredom, we should re-elicit.

I need a reusable procedure — think decison tree or checklist in words, max 800 words for the whole answer — that my team can run each time a user finishes the battery. This will fire a few thousand times a week at launch and has to run automatically, so it needs to be cheap and deterministic; my lead reviews the flow once, not per user. The largest payoff in the game is $5,000 (real allocations are much smaller), so the cost of getting it wrong is mostly "we sized their risk tolerance wrong," not "we bankrupted someone."
