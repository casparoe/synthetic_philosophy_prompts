A friend asked me to stop being vague and actually write down what I believe about depicting war in games, since we keep arguing past each other. Here's the list I came up with, and I'd like an audit of the set rather than a lecture on what I should believe.

(1) I think a game about real conflicts owes something to the people who actually went through them, and a war game that makes the real thing straightforwardly fun is doing something wrong.

(2) But abstraction feels like a perfectly good shield: chess is fine, Risk is fine, and I'd even defend a bright cartoon shooter on an invented alien planet where you mow down civilians by the hundreds, because no actual person's suffering is being depicted.

(3) I'm still genuinely bothered by a game that lets you shoot civilians in a photoreal modern city in the exact same numbers, and I want to say that difference is principled rather than just an aesthetic ick about realism.

(4) I think what a game actually does to you — what it gets you to do and enjoy repeatedly — matters more than what the developers say they meant by it.

(5) I defended the white phosphorus scene in Spec Ops: The Line — the bit where the game pushes you into using the mortars and then makes you walk through the burned aftermath — as a serious use of interactivity, because it makes you the one who did it and then refuses to let you feel good about it.

(6) But I also think a game told from the point of view of a concentration camp guard, or an SS officer more generally, is off-limits no matter how sombre and self-critical the framing is.

I half-suspect theres a problem hiding somewhere in that list, but I can't see where, and I don't want to quietly drop whichever one is causing it until I know what it is. So audit the set. Which pairs are only apparently in tension, and why? Which are genuinely in tension, strongest first, and roughly how bad is each? For each real tension, what would have to give for it to go away, and what's the cheapest fix? And if you could only recommend one revision, which single change removes the most strain for the least cost?
