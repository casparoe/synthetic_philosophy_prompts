I'm in a philosophy of biology class and we've spent the last two weeks going around in circles about what an organism is. Every defintion we try either rules out something that feels obviously alive-and-one or lets in stuff that feels like parts, not organisms. I want a working definition of "organism" — not the dictionary kind, but something I can actually sort cases with.

Here's my list. Clear cases: a single E. coli, a cat, an oak tree. Clearly not: a red blood cell, a ribosome, a protein. Hard cases: a Portuguese man o' war (one siphonophore made of specialized zooids, each of which is a modified individual animal); a honeybee hive when people call the colony a superorganism; a lichen (a fungus plus algae or cyanobacteria living as a single package); a person plus their gut microbiome, the holobiont; an aspen grove that is genetically one individual all connected at the roots; a cellular slime mold like Dictyostelium, which spends part of its life as lone amoebas and then aggregates into a crawling slug; a coral colony whose polyps are all clones; and a virus. Give me a definition, run it through all the cases, say where it gets the wrong answer or just goes silent, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Then tell me which of your verdicts you'd be least willing to bet on.
