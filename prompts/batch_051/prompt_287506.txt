Constraint: four stanzas, one per pramāṇa, around 24–32 lines total, in which each stanza's verse form enacts that pramāṇa. So tight end-stopped couplets for perception; a crossing or heavily enjambed pattern for inference's move from sign to signified; a stanza built around a simile for upamāna; and for śabda something that only works because a line is repeated or accepted on another's authority. End with a couplet that makes the reader notice the poem itself is śabda. The philosophy should be right enough that someone who knows this stuff won't roll their eyes, and the poem should still be good for someone who doesn't.
