My friend and I spent the evening arguing over a clause in our community's covenant and reached no agreement. The community was founded in 1972 on principles of voluntary simplicity, and the clause reads: "A member shall not keep any material thing that serves neither present need, present work, nor the common life; whatever is kept only for a future that may never come, or only because it once served, shall be given away, save that a member may keep one small box of letters and photographs." One member has uploaded forty years of photographs to a cloud archive that she almost never opens but says is her life; another keeps his late mother's furniture in a storage unit he has not visited in three years and says the furniture is his grief. The drafters plainly had closets and attics in mind, not cloud archives or storage units. Does the clause, read as its drafters would have read it, reach either of them? Read as a general principle about possession and attachment, does it? If the text, its purpose, and what the drafters would likely have said if asked come apart here, which should govern, and what would an amended clause have to look like to cover these cases without forbidding the shoebox of letters and photographs the covenant deliberately allows?
