I'm a policy adviser to a state legislator who chairs a committee on technology and civil liberties. We expect to introduce a first-generation bill on consumer neurotechnology and early brain-computer interfaces, but we can realistically secure support for at most three distinct provisions. The drafters have floated five candidate provisions:

A. Neural data protection: treat raw neural signals and inferred mental states as sensitive personal data, requiring opt-in consent for collection, sharing, or sale and giving users rights of access and deletion.
B. Evidentiary protection: prohibit compelling a person to submit to neural monitoring and bar the use of brain-derived evidence against a defendant, on the model of the privilege against self-incrimination.
C. Anti-manipulation rules: prohibit covert use of neural monitoring or stimulation to alter a person's choices, beliefs, or emotional states, and require transparency when a device is designed to influence mental states.
D. Cognitive liberty provisions: establish that competent adults have a right to use neurotechnology to modify their own cognition or mood, and a right to refuse such modification in employment, education, and correctional settings.
E. Identity-continuity safeguards: require review, before deployment, of devices that directly modulate brain activity for effects on personality, memory, and agency, and create a legal claim for unexpected changes to one's sense of self.

Please give me a defended prioritization, not a survey of the options. Which two or three provisions would you actually fight to include, and which would you explicitly deprioritize or omit? For each judgment, explain what you are assuming about the near-term trajectory of the technology, how probable and severe the harm is, how draftable and enforceable the provision would be, and which provisions are upstream of others. Also address a framing dispute the committee keeps relitigating: should the bill be built around privacy (control over information about one's inner states), around cognitive liberty (control over one's own mental life), or around mental integrity (protection of the self from outside intrusion)? Indicate which framework your top-ranked package commits us to and what downstream problems that framework risks.

At the very end, on its own line, state your ranking with ties marked by "=", ordered from most to least worth including, using the labels above, in the form \answer{A > C > D > B > E}.
