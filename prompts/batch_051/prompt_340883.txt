I'd like you to write the mock Terms of Service for one of these platforms. Deadpan legalese, 550-650 words, for a product called something like Verity or Echo-Check that promises "testimonial justice as a service" and is, from clause one, automating the exact thing it claims to fix.

The humor has to come from getting the philosophy right, not from generic startup snark. The document should run on the actual machinery of epistemic injustice: the split between testimonial injustice (a credibility deficit owed to prejudice against the speaker) and hermeneutical injustice (a gap in the shared interpretive resources, where the speaker's experience doesn't fit the concepts lying around); the point that the injustice lives mainly in the hearer's snap credibility judgment, so any real fix has to change hearers rather than just run speakers through a procedure; the further point that credibility excess is not a harmless mirror image of a deficit — it does its own kind of epistemic damage; and the circularity problem in scoring credibility from past speech, because the historical record is already shaped by who got to speak and be believed to begin with. The satire should land those objections without naming them outright. Include at least one clause that reifies the very thing it claims to dismantle (say, a "pre-emptive credibility hold" on users below some threshold), and at least one clause where the "hermeneutical inclusion module" is just a managed list of approved terms that the company updates by subscription tier — shared conceptual resources as a licensed add-on. A "Definitions" section where key words are defined in ways that already rig the outcome would be excellent.
