I have been reading Locke's Essay Concerning Human Understanding on my own, and I keep coming back to the passage in Book II, Chapter IX, section 8, where he reports a question that William Molyneux put to him in a letter. The question, as I understand it, runs like this: imagine a man born blind who has learned by touch alone to tell apart a cube and a sphere made of the same metal; now suppose his sight is suddenly restored and the two objects are set before him; could he tell by sight which is the cube and which is the sphere before he touches them? Locke reports that Molyneux answered no, and he adds that he agrees.

What puzzles me is that the debate over this question has not gone away after more than three hundred years, and even studies of people who gained sight later in life have not settled it. One side holds that the man would immediately see which is which, because the shape he knows through touch is the same shape he now sees, just presented through a different sense. Another side holds that he could not, because what he learned through touch is not the sort of thing that sight alone can give him, and he would need to connect the two senses by experience. A third view says the question is confused, because the words cube and sphere would not yet mean what they mean for someone who has both senses, or because so much depends on how exactly the man had been trained and how his sight was restored.

My own hypothesis is that the impasse is not really about what the man would do, but about what the question is trying to ask. Some people seem to be asking a factual question about how the brain links the senses; others are asking whether the idea of shape is present in the same form across touch and sight; still others are asking whether the man would have the concept of a cube at all before he had correlated the two senses. If that is right, the debate has stalled because the two sides are answering different questions under one heading, and no single experiment could end it. But I am not sure, and I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about perception, a verbal disagreement about what words like recognize and shape mean here, or a disagreement about which imagined details matter? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress, or is the question ill-posed?
