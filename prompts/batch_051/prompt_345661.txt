I've been going down a rabbit hole about pain asymbolia—people who can still tell you exactly where a pain is and whether it's sharp or burning or cold, but they have none of the normal urge to get away from it, and they genuinely dont seem bothered by it. It's made me weirdly uncertain about what pain even is: maybe the badness is just part of the sensation itself, or maybe the sensation is more like information and the badness is a separate thing the brain does with it. I'd like you to write me a short story about a character who is like this from birth. Don't make it a superhero story and don't make it a horror story. I want the story to actually live inside what their experience is like—how they learn that other people's pain is different, how it shapes their relationships, their work, how they react when someone they love is suffering. Give the story a philosophical edge: let it put real pressure on the idea that the badness of pain is in the raw feeling itself, but also have at least one character defend that position in a way that isn't stupid. You can set it in the present day, or in a near future where parents can select this trait for their children and it's become a quiet moral controversy.
