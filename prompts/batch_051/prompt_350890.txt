I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a committed maximizing act-utilitarian and a deontologist who thinks the separateness of persons objection is decisive (roughly, that utilitarianism wrongly sums welfare across distinct lives, as though persons were just buckets for good experiences) could both sign after a year of arguing, without either feeling misrepresented. Structure it like this: first a numbered list of claims they both accept, including claims about what their disagreement is not (they agree the transplant case where one healthy person is carved up to save five pulls hard, they agree that how a burden is distributed matters, etc.); then a disagreements table where each row states a claim one party accepts and the other rejects, worded so the accepting side would say yes, thats my view; then the real cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether aggregating within a single life over time is importantly different from aggregating across lives, and another is whether making the numbers large enough matters when one unlucky person absorbs the whole cost. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, plus what each predicts about it. End by saying which parts of the remaining dispute you think are mostly verbal. No bibliography, no footnotes, just the document theyd both sign.
