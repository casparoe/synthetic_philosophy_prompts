I am a PhD student in cognitive science working on computational models of semantic representation, and I have been stuck on a disagreement that keeps resurfacing in our reading group. The question is where the meaning of an utterance, a sign, or a neural representation is located. One camp argues that meaning is essentially in the symbolic structure itself—the syntactic object, the token, the pattern of activation—because any competent decoder with the right rules will recover the same interpretation. Another camp argues that meaning is in the interpreter: without a decoder that assigns significance, the marks or signals are just physical events. A third camp argues that meaning is in the interaction between sign, interpreter, and environment—the shared practices, causal history, or stabilizing context of use. Everyone agrees that all these ingredients matter somehow, but they flatly disagree about where the meaning is, and the debate has not moved in months.

I am not asking for a survey of theories of meaning or a verdict on which camp is right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. My working hypothesis is that Hofstadter's trichotomy of frame message, outer message, and inner message names three different questions that our group keeps conflating: one about how something is recognized as a message at all, one about what information is physically or formally carried, and one about what the message means for someone. Is that diagnosis right? Or is the impasse really an empirical disagreement about generalization in neural systems, a normative disagreement about what we want an explanation of representation to do, a verbal disagreement about the word 'meaning,' or something else? Ground your diagnosis in what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis—a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question—or argue that no progress should be expected and why. Please keep the response under 900 words.
