I teach an upper-level MSc seminar called 'Persuasion, Debate and Machine Reasoners'. I am setting the final essay, and I want one 90-minute question on the debate proposal in AI safety and the challenge raised by obfuscated arguments. It should be unanswerable by a summary of how debate is meant to keep superhuman reasoners aligned with human judgement: full marks should require a candidate to locate exactly which premise obfuscation threatens, and to argue whether the threat is principled or merely practical.

Please draft:

(1) The essay question itself, worded so that a student who had not done the reading could still attempt it from the information supplied in the question. It should ask the candidate to reconstruct the asymmetry on which the debate proposal rests, to characterise an obfuscated argument precisely and give a concrete illustrative example of their own devising, to identify which step in the proposal's argument is endangered, and to reach a defended verdict on whether obfuscation reveals a principled limit or only a limitation of unaided human judges.

(2) A model answer of roughly 700 words, in British English, that would be worth full marks.

(3) A marking scheme out of 20, organised into five must-hit points. For each point, state the marks it carries, the specific move a top answer must contain, and a one-line note on how weaker candidates typically go wrong. The scheme should distinguish a candidate who merely says that obfuscated arguments are very complicated from one who sees that obfuscation attacks the verifiability of individual moves rather than the soundness of the whole case, and that the intended asymmetry may invert when hiding a flaw is cheaper than finding one.

Keep the question and answer self-contained, and do not include a bibliography or reading list.
