I'm on the exhibitions committee of a small city museum and we've been going in circles about a new permanent exhibit on the 1918 flu. The lead curator wants the main wall text to open by saying that the virus "remade the city." Most of her labels are written the same way: the flu "closed the schools," "emptied the wharf," "pushed the mayor into a belated quarantine order." Some of us think this is vivid and honestly closer to the truth—the epidemic didn't just happen inside the city, it redirected it. Another group on the commitee thinks the labels are sneaking in a view none of us agreed to: treating a virus as a character with intentions and powers, which starts to read like the city's leaders and ordinary residents just got swept along. I've been asked to circulate a short memo before we meet on Tuesday. It should recommend whether to keep the curator's framing, drop it, or allow it only under specific conditions, and it should give the label-writers actual rules they can follow. Please don't settle for "be balanced"—I need something actonable, and the losing side's strongest point should be dealt with head on.
