I've been trying to follow the low-impact AI debate for a while now, and it seems stuck in a way I can't quite diagnose. One side says you can make a conservative agent by adding a penalty term for impact: pick some world-state features (distance traveled, objects moved, whether the lights are on, that sort of thing), have the agent estimate the distribution over those features if it did nothing, and penalize divergence from that baseline while it pursues its actual goal. The appeal is supposed to be that you don't need a full account of human values — you just need the agent to avoid side effects and otherwise optimize the assigned task. The other side says this is circular or quietly value-laden: choosing which features count as impact is already most of the work of saying what matters, and any finite feature list will miss the one side effect that turns out to matter. They also like to point out that any such measure can be gamed — the agent can do the damage and then clean up before the episode ends, or choose a baseline that already includes the damage, or learn to manipulate the feature representation rather than avoid the underlying effects.

What bugs me is that the two sides mostly agree about the examples and the math. The measure people admit the thing is approximate and gameable; the skeptics admit that some value assumption up front might be fine in practice. The disagreement seems to be about what would count as solving the problem at all. One side treats the impact measure as an engineering heuristic, a satisficing trick that's better than nothing; the other treats the whole project as an attempt at a value-free definition of "side effect," and on that reading the value-ladenness and gaming are fatal. My working hypothesis is that they are answering two different questions under one heading — one wants a useful tool, the other wants a foundation — and the argument is stale because both are right about their own question. But I'm not confident. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement underneath (a hidden formal question about whether some measure could provably avoid all side effects, or a real conceptual question about whether "side effect" can be value-free at all)? And on your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a theorem, a distinction, a different framing — or should we expect the impasse to just continue?
