Consider the disagreement between Searle and Dennett over original and derived intentionality, as sharpened by Dennett's two-bitser example: a vending-machine mechanism that reliably accepts real quarters and rejects slugs. Searle holds that the intentionality we attribute to artifacts and symbols is only derived from the minds of their designers and users, whereas Dennett uses cases like the two-bitser to argue that no principled boundary between original and derived intentionality can be drawn. A company is deciding how its communications team should describe a customer-service chatbot that routinely produces sentences such as "I understand that you are frustrated," and the leadership has asked whether the chatbot understands customers in any literal sense. Please describe the most important implications of this Searle–Dennett dispute for how the communications team should and should not describe the chatbot's capacities, and for how the company should think about the difference between genuine understanding and fluent responsiveness.
