Although I trained as a physicist, I have only recently begun reading the philosophical side of Bell's theorem, and I am unsure what half a century of Bell-type experiments has actually established. Those experiments violate the Bell inequalities, and the usual moral drawn is that one must give up at least one of three assumptions: locality; realism, understood as measurement outcomes being fixed by pre-existing hidden variables; or measurement independence, understood as the measurement settings being statistically independent of those hidden variables. The difficulty is that the three are only ever tested as a package. I would like to know whether the conceptual claim 'nature is local' can be tested on its own. Please design the best experiment you can for that isolated claim, state which result would count in favour of locality and which against it, and then say precisely what the test would and would not settle — in particular, whether any data, however clean, could distinguish a failure of locality from a failure of realism or of measurement independence.
