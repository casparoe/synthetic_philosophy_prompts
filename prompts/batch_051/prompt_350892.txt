Here is the original case. An urn contains 90 balls: 30 are red, and the remaining 60 are black or yellow in an unknown proportion. A person is offered two bets. Bet R pays £100 if a red ball is drawn. Bet B pays £100 if a black ball is drawn. Most people choose R over B. The same person, offered a bet that pays £100 if the ball is red or yellow (bet RY) and one that pays £100 if the ball is black or yellow (bet BY), tends to choose BY over RY. This pair of choices is hard to reconcile with standard expected utility.

Now vary the case in just one respect: keep everything the same, but let bet B pay £X instead of £100, where X is a positive amount the chooser can be told. The red bet still pays £100.

Please work through two analyses of the varied case. First, what would a standard subjective expected-utility agent with a single prior have to believe about the probability of drawing a black ball in order to prefer R over B when X is £100, and how does that requirement change as X is raised? Second, what would a maxmin expected-utility agent, who treats imprecise probabilities as a set of priors, choose in this varied case? In particular, does the value of X make any difference to the maxmin agent's choice between R and B, and if so, at what values? Conclude by saying whether you think this variation strengthens, weakens, or simply changes the argument for modelling the original choices with imprecise probabilities.
