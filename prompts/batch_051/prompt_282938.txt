i keep coming back to a parallel between deterministic chaos and pseudorandom number generators and i can't tell wether it's deep or just an analogy. a chaotic system is deterministic, but because you can't measure the initial conditions with infinite precision, the trajectory beyond the Lyapunov horizon (the timescale on which small errors blow up) is treated as unpredictable. a pseudorandom generator is also deterministic: if you don't know the seed, the output looks random, but the moment you have the seed the whole stream is reproducible. in both cases the unpredictability seems to be located in the input state, not in the rule that evolves it. is chaotic unpredictability a kind of pseudorandomness, or is pseudorandomness a different and stronger notion? what would have to be true for results to transfer between the two, and where does the parallel actually break?
