I have been turning an argument over on my own after a long conversation about whether a heaven that went on forever could actually be something to look forward to. I am not trained in philosophy, so I would like you to check my reasoning rather than supply a theory. The argument, as I have made it exact, runs:

(1) Suppose a person has an unending life in which every moment is perfectly blissful.
(2) Perfect bliss means the person's happiness is already as high as it can possibly be at every moment; there is no better state a moment could reach.
(3) If every moment is already as good as any moment could be, then no moment can be better or worse than another; and if no moment is better or worse than another, the moments must also be the same in what they feel like.
(4) An unending life whose moments all feel the same would eventually turn dull and then unbearable for a human-like mind, however pleasant each moment is at the outset.
(5) Therefore an unending life of perfect bliss would eventually cease to be blissful, which contradicts (1); so the very idea of an endless heaven is incoherent.

Please tell me whether the argument is valid, and if it is not, exactly which step fails. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one for each step, each one or two sentences saying whether the step is sound or is the place where the argument breaks, with no introduction or conclusion.
