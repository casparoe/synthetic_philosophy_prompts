Write the document that an adversarial collaboration between two researchers would produce after a year of arguing about whether trained machine learning models are capable of genuine novelty. One side — call her the recombinationist — holds that a model's outputs are always recompositions or interpolations of patterns already present in its training distribution, and that apparent novelty is an artifact of the distribution's size and dimensionality. The other side — call him the compositional extrapolationist — holds that a model can combine separately learned constraints, for example the rules of a formal system together with a novel auxiliary condition, into configurations that lie outside the support of the training distribution and that at least some such configurations count as genuine novelty even though no source in the training set contains them.

The document should contain: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about what novelty requires, about what the training distribution does and does not literally contain, and about what would count as evidence; a disagreements table whose entries give each side's view in wording that side would accept; a list of cruxes, meaning sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and, for each crux, a concrete test, case, or experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. In drafting, keep the agreed claims neither vacuous nor slanted, and state explicitly where any part of the dispute looks verbal rather than substantive, for example a disagreement about what "novelty" or "beyond the training distribution" means.

End with a short note saying how confident you are in what you have written, and single out the point you are least sure of.
