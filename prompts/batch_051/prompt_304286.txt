Construct a thought experiment with the structure of Quinn's self-torturer, but with the incremental trap built from social or professional choices rather than money and physical pain. The protagonist faces a long sequence of discrete, individually optional steps; at each step, the one-step move is locally rational by the protagonist's own fixed values — judged purely as a move from the current position, it offers a genuine gain in an ordinary good (status, belonging, security, or advancement) at a cost too small on its own to outweigh that gain — yet after many steps the protagonist stands in a state they would have refused before the first step, and which they now regard as not worth the path that led there. Constraints: no change in the protagonist's values, no adaptation, no self-deception, no imperfect information, no coercion or manipulation by others, and no penalty for stopping at any point. After presenting the scenario, show (i) step by step that each move is rational in the specified one-step sense, (ii) the protagonist's ex ante and ex post verdicts on the whole sequence, and (iii) which structural principle of rational choice the case puts under pressure — transitivity of rational preference, dominance, or something else — and why it is that principle rather than a rival. End by explaining how a defender of the principle you named would attack the case, and which single feature of the scenario would have to be removed for that attack to succeed. Please write the answer in British English.
