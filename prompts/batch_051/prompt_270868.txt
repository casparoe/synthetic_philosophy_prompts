I have been trying to get clear on what an analogy is, because I keep encountering a strong claim in cognitive science—associated especially with Hofstadter and Sander—that analogy-making is not one specialized tool of thought among others but the basic operation underlying nearly all cognition: categorization, pattern recognition, scientific modeling, the learning of words. I cannot tell whether this claim is profound or whether it just stretches the word "analogy" until it covers everything and excludes nothing.

I want a working definition of "analogy" that lets me test the claim against cases. Please propose the definition, run it through each case below, and say explicitly where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. Then either revise the definition or defend the verdicts that come out feeling wrong.

1. A textbook explains the flow of electric current by comparing it to water running through a pipe.
2. A two-year-old who has never seen the moon at night points at a crescent moon and says "banana."
3. A judge deciding whether a drone flying over a neighbor's land counts as trespass reasons by comparing it to an older case involving a horse that strayed onto a neighbor's property.
4. A thermostat closes the furnace circuit when the room temperature drops below its set point.
5. A physicist models highway traffic with equations originally derived for fluid dynamics.
6. A person recites the alphabet from memory.
7. A chess master glances at an unfamiliar mid-game position and immediately sees it as the same kind of bind as a game he studied years earlier.
8. A doctor looks at a new patient's rash and says, "This looks like the Lyme case I saw last month," and orders the test.
9. A pigeon trained to peck at photographs of trees pecks at a photograph of a tree species it has never seen before.

I am confident that (1) is an analogy and that (4) and (6) are not; the rest I cannot settle. After you have run the definition through all nine, identify which resulting verdict is the least secure and say what, if anything, the definition implies about whether the Hofstadter and Sander claim is a substantive empirical thesis or a definitional maneuver.
