Having spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, I have internalized competing arguments about expected value, worst-case thinking, and the difference between risk and Knightian uncertainty, and there is one debate where these habits keep pulling me in different directions. The familiar setup is a veil of ignorance: the parties know what distribution of life-prospects a set of principles would produce but not which position they will occupy once the veil is lifted. Rawls treats this as decision under genuine ignorance. Since no objective probabilities over social positions are available, he argues, the parties should not bet on where they will land; they should reason as though the least advantaged position might be theirs, which yields maximin. Harsanyi treats the same veil as a situation of risk: rational and impartially sympathetic choosers should assign equal probability to each position and maximize expected utility, which pushes toward average utilitarianism. A third criterion that receives less attention is minimax regret: choose the arrangement that minimizes the greatest gap between what each position gets and what it could have gotten under the arrangement that is best for that position.

To make the three standards pull apart cleanly, imagine a society facing a one-time choice among three arrangements affecting three equally numerous groups, and grant for the sake of argument that the numbers below are meaningful interpersonal utility levels. Arrangement A produces (20, 20, 20). Arrangement B produces (18, 30, 30). Arrangement C produces (19, 29, 29). Maximin selects A, equal-probability expected utility selects B, and minimax regret selects C. Write an essay on which of these three standards is most defensible in this case, weighing the main arguments for and against each; do not end on a non-committal note—reach and justify a verdict. Your essay should also consider whether that verdict changes if the parties know the choice will be renegotiated many times over a lifetime rather than fixed once and for all, and whether the answer should depend on how close together or far apart the numbers are. Please write your answer in American English.
