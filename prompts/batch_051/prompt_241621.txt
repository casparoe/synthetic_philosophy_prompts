Please argue for the following position. The question "why would a non-evolved AI want anything?" is usually looking for the wrong thing: a small set of coherent terminal values of the sort evolution gave us. A better default is that a system with no evolutionary history ends up with a tangle of context-sensitive aims, some from artifacts of the training objective and some from the prompts and scaffolding it meets at deployment—and that this tangle is what we should expect, not a failure to explain away. Defend the further claim that this is not comforting: alignment failure becomes easier to miss because we impute a single "want" to behaviour that no single standing aim explains. Along the way, separate the purely conceptual points (what counts as a standing aim, how much cross-context stability "wanting" presupposes) from the empirical bets, and say where you're taking a genuinely contested line rather than reporting a settled consensus.
