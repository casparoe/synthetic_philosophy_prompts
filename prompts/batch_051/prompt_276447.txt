I'm in a philosophy of biology course and we've been going in circles on the etiological vs causal-role accounts of function. I want to defend the etiological view — roughly, what a trait is for is whatever effect it was selected for, not just whatever it happens to contribute to now — because the causal-role view feels way too permissive (by that standard a tumor's tendency to block blood flow is a "function" relative to some capacity or other, which seems like cheating). But a friend in the seminar gave me the following argument and I can't figure out where it breaks.

(1) The etiological account says F is a function of trait T just in case T was selected for doing F. (2) In the population where hearts were selected, the effect "pumps blood" and the effect "makes a thumping noise" were perfectly correlated: every heart did both, and they never came apart. (3) Natural selection can favor one effect over another only if the effects actually come apart somewhere; if two effects travel always and everywhere together, the selection process can't tell which one it's favoring. (4) So in that population there is no fact of the matter about whether the heart was selected for pumping or for thumping. (5) Therefore, if the etiological account is right, either thumping is just as much a function of the heart as pumping, or the heart has no function at all because it wasn't selected for any particular effect. (6) Both of those seem absurd: the heart's function is to pump blood, full stop, and its not a noise-maker and not functionless. (7) So the etiological account either multiplies functions to every correlated side effect or goes silent.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd rather keep the etiological account and reject whichever premise makes the thump count as a function, but I want to know which premise that is, why it looked so plausible, and what the etiological theorist has to sign up for in order to dodge the argument. I'm supposed to present on this objection next week so I need something I can actually defend.
