Having spent my working life in the physical sciences, I still cannot take a measurement seriously unless I can point to an independent check on the standard or instrument used to make it. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy, and I keep returning to a parallel that I would like you to examine rather than merely ratify. I have in mind the private language argument, understood roughly as follows. A person resolves to use a sign, call it S, only for a certain private sensation, and writes S in a diary whenever the sensation recurs. The difficulty is supposed to be that nothing could settle whether S is used correctly on a later occasion: the diarist can appeal only to memory, and memory of the meaning is exactly what is in question. If seeming right is the only standard, then the distinction between following the rule and merely believing one is following it seems to collapse.

Now consider a metrological problem from my own former discipline. A single physical artifact cannot certify itself. If I define the unit of length as the length of one particular metal bar under specified conditions, and later wonder whether the bar has changed, comparing the bar to itself tells me nothing. I need a second, independent realization of the quantity, or an independent physical process, against which to check it. Metrology handles this through traceability: chains of comparisons leading to standards that can be realized independently and publicly, so that no standard is ever validated only by consulting itself. The private diarist appears to be in the position of a metrologist who possesses exactly one artifact and no external comparator.

I cannot decide whether this is an identity of structure or only a loose analogy. Please do three things in your answer. First, state the mapping as precisely as you can: what corresponds to the standard, the act of comparison, and the criterion of correctness in each case. Second, say what would have to be true of both sides for a result to transfer from one domain to the other. Third, identify at least one thing the parallel appears to predict that is false or dubious, and say whether that false prediction is fatal to the analogy or merely limits it. In particular, address the apparent disanalogy that physical standards can be shared and stipulated publicly, whereas the private diarist insists the sensation is inaccessible to anyone else. I am not looking for a survey of the scholarly literature; I want the structure tested. Keep the response to at most 800 words.
