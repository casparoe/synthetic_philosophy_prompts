Last night a friend and I spent the entire evening arguing about Frege. I had mentioned that I was going back through his essays on sense and reference after several years away, and my friend brought up the political diary Frege kept near the end of his life. In the parts he described, and in what I have since skimmed, Frege comes across as deeply antisemitic and hostile to parliamentary democracy. My friend's claim was that this changes how we should read the philosophical work: he argued that we should treat the whole apparatus — the attack on psychologism, the distinction between Sinn and Bedeutung, the doctrine that thoughts belong to a third realm distinct from both the mental and the physical — as a project with that politics already baked into it, rather than as a neutral account of the structure of language and logic. I resisted at first and said that an argument stands or falls on its own, but by the end of the evening that phrase had begun to sound like a way of pretending ideas have no authors.

I am left genuinely unsure what to do with this. Does the diary actually bear on Frege's philosophy of language and logic, and if it does, how exactly should it change the way I read him? I would rather you commit to a definite position and argue for it, even if the truth turns out to be messier than either my friend or I wanted it to be.
