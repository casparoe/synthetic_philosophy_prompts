I am of two minds about hedonism about well-being. The view has the systematic virtues I want in a theory: a single currency of value, definite answers to trade-off questions, and a straightforward decision procedure—promote pleasure. But I also have a firm intuition that the items the theory treats as commensurable are not ultimately the same sort of thing: the pleasure of a joke landing, of an itch being scratched, of finally seeing how a proof goes, of being loved, of a narcotic haze. These seem to differ not merely in degree or in cause, but in kind; it does not feel as though there is a single felt something-or-other that makes each of them good. I am aware of the standard replies, such as treating pleasure as a determinable with heterogeneous determinates, moving to attitudinal pleasure, or saying my intuition is just a failure of abstraction, but I cannot tell whether these replies explain what pleasures have in common or merely relabel the problem while preserving unification on paper. Walk me through what it costs to keep the theory and debunk the intuition, to abandon the theory and keep the intuition, and to amend the theory so both can be kept, and then say which of these you find most defensible and why.
