I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I keep finding popular explainers of Sleeping Beauty that I'm pretty sure are smuggling in one side's premises. There's a chapter in a paradox book I was reading (I won't name it) that summarizes the debate like this:

"Sleeping Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is flipped. If heads, she is woken once, on Monday; if tails, she is woken Monday and Tuesday, with an amnesia drug between wakings so Tuesday feels like the first waking. Each time she wakes she is asked her credence that the coin landed heads. Halfers say 1/2: the coin is fair, and waking is no news, since she was certain in advance that she would wake at least once either way, so by the Principal Principle her credence should still be the chance of heads. Thirders say 1/3: on tails there are two indistinguishable wakings, so the three wakings she might now be occupying are equally likely. The book's favored derivation is the standard one: if Beauty learned it was Monday, she would be 1/2 on heads, and if she learned the coin landed tails, she would be 50-50 between Monday and Tuesday; those two facts force 1/3 before either update. The chapter then says both sides accept the Monday intuition and the fight is only about whether waking itself is evidence. It closes by saying halfers think thirders confuse a fair coin with a fair lottery over wakings, and that the thirder update abandons conditionalization."

Something smells off to me here. I thought Lewis—the most careful halfer—actually denies the Monday intuition: doesn't he have Beauty go to 2/3 on heads when she learns it's Monday, not 1/2? And I thought the thirder argument doesn't just assume the three wakings are equally likely; it argues for the equal split from the Monday and Tails conditional claims, which is different. So is this summary fair to both sides, or is it building the thirder's assumptions into the "neutral" setup? I'd like an accuracy audit: separate whats right, whats a fair simplification, and what's just wrong or question-begging. The thing I most want to know is whether the "both sides accept the Monday intuition" line is a subtle strawman of the halfer, and whether a halfer who hangs everything on the Principal Principle can really say waking is no news while still ordering her self-locating credences in a principled way.
