Banging my head against something in a philosophy of linguistics reading group and I want a straight answer out of someone, not a survey. We're arguing about whether any grammatical categories carve language at the joints, or whether they're all labels Greek and Roman grammarians handed us and we never cleaned up. Take these eight: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, subject, object, nominative case. Rank them from most natural-kind-ish to least. By natural-kind-ish I mean: the category picks out a class whose members share a bunch of independent properties — morphological, syntactic, semantic — rather than one stipulated feature or just a tradition. (And yes, I know subject and object are normally called grammatical relations, not categories; that's part of what I want you to work through, not dodge.) Give me a full ordering, no ties, no "it depends on the language" without then taking a stand anyway. Then tell me which adjacent pair in your ranking has the steepest drop in kindhood, and give your reason for putting the bottom item essentially in the "convenient label" bin.
