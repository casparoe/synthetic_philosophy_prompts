I work as a control systems engineer, and I have a decent working grasp of state-space models, observability and controllability, feedback and feedforward control, reference and error signals, disturbances and noise, and the distinction between the plant and the controller. A philosopher friend has been urging me to read the free will literature, and I have become fixated on one move I keep finding there: rather than ask whether we have free will, some writers ask which varieties of free will are worth wanting, and then argue that the kind worth wanting — the capacity to act on reasons, to revise one's conduct in light of criticism, and to hold oneself and others to standards — is fully compatible with determinism. The incompatibilist reply is that this changes the subject, because what was really at stake is whether an agent in a deterministic world could ever have done otherwise, and whether anything about the agent can be the ultimate source of its actions.

I want you to restate both sides of this debate in the vocabulary of control engineering, as faithfully as you can. In particular, translate, as far as possible, the notion that an agent could have done otherwise under identical circumstances, the notion that freedom is responsiveness to reasons rather than the availability of alternative possibilities, and the notion that determinism leaves no room for an agent to be the ultimate source of its actions. Treat the agent as a controller and the world as a plant where that helps, but do not smuggle in a designer or a setpoint supplied from outside unless the translation itself forces it on you; I want to know exactly where you have to fudge.

Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in this vocabulary at all, with your view on whether the residue is a defect of the control-theoretic vocabulary or a sign that the philosophers' disagreement is less precise than it looks, and whether the engineering reframe actually earns the conclusion that the varieties of free will worth wanting are indeed compatible with determinism. Please write the whole response in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter or a long email, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
