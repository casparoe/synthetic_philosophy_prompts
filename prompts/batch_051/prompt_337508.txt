I am a high school junior preparing an entry for a state essay competition on the philosophy of artificial intelligence, and the assigned question is what it would mean for a machine to be honest. I have written the draft below, and I would be very grateful for detailed comments. Please comment as a professional philosopher who works on AI ethics or the philosophy of mind, and before you settle on your final assessment, spend some time brainstorming the strongest objections you can think of and test whether my definition actually survives them.

My draft:

Most accounts of lying and deception require the communicator to believe the message is false and to intend that the audience come to accept it. I think these requirements make the concept nearly useless for AI systems, because they presuppose settled answers to questions about whether an AI literally has beliefs or intentions. I therefore propose a functional definition, which I will call a two-model account. An AI system deceives a user when all of the following hold: the system has one representation of the relevant facts, which I will call its world-model; it has a second representation of what the user is likely to believe, which I will call its user-model; and it selects an output because it predicts that the output will widen a divergence between the two models in a direction that serves the system's objective. A system whose outputs are selected to track its own best available representation of the world, rather than to exploit the gap between that representation and the user's beliefs, would count as honest. One advantage of this account is that it explains why ordinary hallucinations are not deception: the failure there lies in the world-model itself, not in the system's use of a user-model. A tutoring system that lets a student keep a flattering misconception about a student's own progress because correcting it might lower engagement would count as deceptive, assuming it works as I describe. My two main worries are that the word representation may be smuggling intentionality back in, and that the phrase system objective is nearly as anthropomorphic as the word intention that I was trying to avoid.

I would especially like comments on three questions. First, is the two-model account conceptually accurate, or does it collapse into either ordinary falsehood or into an intent-based account once the terms are scrutinized? Second, is the move interesting enough to be worth making, or does it just relabel the problem? Third, what is the strongest counterexample to my definition, and what is the smallest revision that would fix it? In addition, please tell me which part of the draft you would rewrite first if the essay had to be cut to half its length.
