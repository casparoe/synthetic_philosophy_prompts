I've been trying to get clear on what a grammatical rule even is, and my own verdicts keep pulling in different directions. Here's where I land on a bunch of cases. For 'go/went' I'd say 'went' is just a stored replacement: the past-tense rule would give 'goed', and 'went' blocks it, but it isn't the rule's output. Same for 'ox/oxen'—stored irregularity, not a rule. For 'a branch'/'an apple' I'd say there's a real rule picking one of a/an, no hedging. For 'index', 'indices' is something I learned, not something my grammar produces; 'indexes' is the rule. Now the ones that bother me. 'Sing/sang' doesn't feel like rote memory, because if a new verb 'spling' showed up I suspect I'd produce 'splang' without having heard it—but 'sang' also isn't the default the way -ed is. And 'leaf/leaves': the f-to-v change feels like a genuine rule, just one with a messy reach, since I'd say 'roofs' and not treat that as a mistake. Is there a single account of what a rule is that delivers all of these verdicts? If not, which one should I give up? Please state the account precisely, and say what it predicts for the past tense of a made-up verb like 'fring' and the plural of a made-up noun like 'bloose'.
