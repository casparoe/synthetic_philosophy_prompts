The Letter of James says: 'You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone' (James 2:24, NRSVUE). Paul's Letter to the Romans says: 'For we hold that a person is justified by faith apart from works prescribed by the law' (Romans 3:28, NRSVUE). On their face, the two sentences appear to contradict one another about the relationship between justification, faith, and works.

Set out the strongest case for reading them as a genuine contradiction, then work through the candidate reconciliations and assess them honestly. In particular, address the following:

1. Distinguish the possible senses in which each writer may be using justification, faith, and works. Which distinctions are actually supported by the immediate argument of each passage, and which are imported merely to make the two texts agree?
2. Test the standard reconciliation: that James is speaking of the kind of faith that is alive only when it issues in deeds, while Paul is speaking of the ground on which a person is accepted by God. Does this fit what each passage says, or does it quietly change the subject of one of them?
3. Consider the less tidy possibility that the two writers are answering different questions under pressure from different pastoral circumstances, and that a single doctrine of justification is being stretched to cover both. What would each author have to concede for this to work?
4. Give a verdict: which reading requires the least special pleading, and what does it cost? Say plainly where you think the line falls between a distinction the texts themselves make and a distinction a reader manufactures to avoid a conflict.
