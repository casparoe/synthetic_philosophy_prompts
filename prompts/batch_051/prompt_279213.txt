I keep finding that I use the phrase "a meaningful life" with perfect confidence in easy cases and almost no confidence in the adjacent ones. I would like you to propose a working definition of what it is for a human life to be meaningful, then test that definition against the cases below. Please state the definition first, apply it to each case in order, and say explicitly where it gives a verdict that seems wrong or gives no verdict at all; then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.

The cases:

1. A nurse who works for decades under grueling conditions and saves many lives, but who hates the work, takes no satisfaction or pride in it, and would leave tomorrow if she could.
2. A retired person who spends thirty years comfortably watching television, largely indifferent to what is on, with no projects and no close relationships. I am confident this life is not meaningful.
3. Sisyphus, condemned forever to roll a boulder up a hill only to watch it roll back down, who has come to embrace the task for its own sake and no longer wishes to be anywhere else. I have no settled view about this case.
4. A mathematician who spends his career proving a beautiful conjecture; after his death the proof is shown to rest on a fatal error, and his manuscripts are discarded. I cannot make up my mind about this one.
5. A woman who spends three decades building and tending a garden that is genuinely beautiful but almost never seen by anyone else, and whose satisfaction in it is real but modest. I lean toward calling this meaningful, but I could not defend the lean.
6. A man who devotes his life to counting and recounting the fibers in a single carpet, keeps meticulous ledgers, and feels deeply absorbed and purposeful while doing it. I am confident this is not meaningful.
7. An activist who spends his life organizing for a political change he regards as just. The cause fails while he lives, and he dies believing he accomplished nothing; years later the change comes partly through groundwork he laid, though nobody remembers him. I am confident this life is meaningful.
8. A brilliant forger who spends his life perfecting fakes to sell as originals; he finds the work absorbing and deeply satisfying, and he regards the deception itself as his art. I find my intuitions about this case shifting.

I am especially interested in whether the definition can let Sisyphus come out as meaningful without letting the carpet-counter come out as meaningful, and in how it handles the contrast between the first and last cases. I do not want a survey of how different theories would classify the cases; I want the best single working definition you can assemble and defend.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the definition you propose, and identify the single point about which you are least sure.
