I have been developing a position I call forfeiture utility, and I would like an honest assessment before I pursue it further. Standard revealed-preference theory runs from choice to utility: what you choose from a menu reveals where your preferences stand, and the utility function is whatever would rationalize the choices. But that picture has an awkward asymmetry. When you choose an option, you are also declining every other option in the menu, and standard theory treats what you decline as mere noise against the chosen object. I propose to reverse the emphasis. On my view, the utility of an outcome for an agent is not a magnitude attached to the outcome itself but a measure of the opportunity the agent is willing to forgo for it: the size, diversity, or value of the set of alternatives the agent would give up in order to secure that outcome. If one person would sacrifice only a single alternative for x while another would sacrifice an entire rich menu for x, the second counts, by my lights, as valuing x more, even if their binary choice behavior is identical. Choice reveals a preference only derivatively, by showing how much of the option space someone is prepared to foreclose. I think this repairs the singleton-menu problem, since choosing from a menu of one reveals preferences of zero depth, because nothing is forgone; it also gives utility a natural cross-agent scale whenever menus can be measured, and it explains why choices among many good options feel more revealing than choices among few.

I am aware this is adjacent to opportunity cost, willingness-to-pay, and the literature on menu-dependence and preference for flexibility, but I want to know whether the differences do real work. Give me the three strongest objections to forfeiture utility, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely awkward. Where does the view break or fail to be an account of utility at all, as opposed to an account of something looser, like the costliness of decisiveness? And am I rediscovering an existing position under another name?
