I'm a backend engineer on a team building a natural-language query interface for our company's internal data. We have to parse user questions into something we can run against a database, and the worst part is phrases that start with "the". I started reading Bertrand Russell's essay On Denoting, and this passage seems like it might be the key:

"Now the, when it is strictly used, involves uniqueness; we do, it is true, speak of 'the son of So-and-so' even when So-and-so has several sons, but it would be more correct to say 'a son of So-and-so'."

So my rough takeaway is: in careful use, "the F" implies there's exactly one F, and then "the F is G" should unpack into three claims — something is F, nothing else is F, and that thing is G. But the passage also says people say "the son of So-and-so" when there are several sons, so there's a looser use too. I need a decision procedure my team can actually follow, not just the theory. None of us have formal philosophy background.

Could you write a step-by-step procedure for converting a simple English sentence containing a singular definite description into a first-order-logic-style form, or flagging that it shouldn't be converted? It has to be something a junior engineer can run in code review without understanding Russell. It should handle: the strict case ("the F is G" → existence, uniqueness, predication), the non-strict case from the passage ("the son of So-and-so" when there might be multiple sons), empty descriptions ("the report that John never wrote"), plural descriptions ("the employees who submitted reports"), embedded descriptions ("the friend of the manager who left" — ambiguous), and cases where "the" is part of a proper name or idiom. For each step, say what counts as a sufficient answer and where the procedure exits with "not handled by this procedure, needs human."

Then work through three examples: "The employee who submitted the expense report is on vacation", "The son of the director got the job", and "The committee made a decision". For each, show exactly what the procedure outputs and where a human has to override. Finally, tell me what the procedure can't do, because I don't want my team to pretend this is a mechanical seperation of logic from natural language when it isn't.
