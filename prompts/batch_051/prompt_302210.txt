My team is going back and forth on machine ethics, and the view that won the room is basically "it's a test suite problem." The analogy they lean on: the target behavior is the spec, the model is the code, RLHF/evals/red-teaming are the test cases, and "state-of-the-art on the eval harness" is CI going green. So their conclusion is that making a model more ethical is just standard eng practice—more coverage, more fuzzing, more integration tests, ship when it passes. I get the intution but I don't trust it. Can you play this analogy out properly: give me the correspondence table (what's the spec, the test, the code, the production environment, the regression), then hit the places where it actually breaks for a learned model that is stochastic, can drift after deploy, can game the eval, and can hit inputs nobody wrote a test for. The thing I care about most: does "better eval coverage = more ethical model" survive the disanalogies, or was the testing analogy carrying the whole conclusion? And if it breaks, is there a better analogy for what we're really doing?
