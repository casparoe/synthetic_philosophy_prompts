Give me a number: your actual credence that epistemic two-dimensionalism is true, where true means the doctrine does real semantic and epistemic explanatory work, not merely that two-dimensional notation is occasionally handy. Let me fix the target doctrine. On the view I have in mind, a substantial class of expressions — at least proper names, natural-kind terms, and indexicals — have two intensions. The secondary intension is a function from metaphysically possible worlds to extensions with the actual world held fixed; it accounts for the expression's modal profile. The primary intension is a function from epistemic scenarios, understood as maximally specific ways the actual world could turn out for all a subject knows a priori and centered on a subject and time, to extensions; it is meant to capture what a speaker can know a priori and what the expression contributes to cognitive significance. The doctrine holds that these two intensions are two aspects of one semantic value and that their interaction explains the canonical combinations: necessary a posteriori truths such as water is H2O and Hesperus is Phosphorus turn out to have contingent primary intensions and necessary secondary intensions, while a priori contingent truths exhibit the reverse pattern. Treat the doctrine as true if some consistent version of this view gives a non-gerrymandered, correct account of its target class.

Now decompose your credence. Give each of the following components a probability and a sentence of justification, and add components you rely on if I have left them out.

1. The target is real: there is a genuine phenomenon of cognitive significance that a one-dimensional possible-worlds semantics fails to capture; two expressions can agree in extension and in possible-worlds intension and still differ in what they contribute to thought.

2. Primary intensions are well defined: for the relevant names, kind terms, and indexicals, there is a determinate function from epistemic scenarios to extensions, and it is not an artifact of whichever descriptions a theorist happens to choose.

3. The epistemic space is grounded: a complete qualitative description of a scenario plus indexical information — the scrutability base — suffices, without circularity, to determine the extensions of the target expressions.

4. The epistemology lines up: the framework returns the right a priori status for the canonical cases, so that necessary a posteriori truths have contingent primary intensions and necessary secondary intensions, a priori contingent truths have the reverse pattern, and this is why the surface data about truth and knowability have their shape.

5. The framework is nontrivial: primary and secondary intension are two genuine aspects of one semantic value rather than two separate pieces of information placed side by side; the view is not reducible to Kaplan-style character plus content for indexicals, and not a notational variant of ordinary modal semantics.

6. The strong-necessity threat is survivable: there are no, or only harmless, a posteriori necessities that are wholly invisible to the a priori, so primary possibility tracks metaphysical possibility closely enough for the explanatory claims to go through.

7. The context and indeterminacy problems are manageable: primary intensions are stable enough across speakers, contexts, and descriptive choices to support shared content and genuine explanation, rather than becoming objects whose identity depends on arbitrary features of an occasion of use.

These components are not independent, and I do not want seven independent guesses averaged. State explicitly which probabilities are conditional on which, give the relevant conditional probabilities, and show how they combine into your final number.
