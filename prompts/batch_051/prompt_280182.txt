Okay, so I've read Politics and the English Language a few times and I still can't pin down what Orwell actually thought the causal story was between language and politics. In the essay he seems to be saying that junk prose—the stale metaphors, the inflated Latin words, the ready-made slogans—doesn't just look ugly, it actually corrupts political thought, and that cleaning up your own prose is a way of staying honest. But then in 1984 the Party is running what sounds like the same program with the sign flipped: shrink the vocabulary, make dissenting thoughts unsayable, and people eventually stop being able to think them. That's the nightmare ending. So is the real difference just who gets to do the language engineering, and whether its voluntary? Did Orwell think language reform can fix political thought, such that a rationalist forum that actually enforced his six rules on commenters would be doing the good version at small scale, while Newspeak is the bad version at state scale? I'm stuck on the causal arrow: if fuzzy language causes fuzzy politics, then why shouldn't I go around defuzzifying everyone else's prose, and if it doesn't, what was the essay even for?
