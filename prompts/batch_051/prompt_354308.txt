Okay here's where I'm stuck. On reflection I find the regularity view of causation basically unavoidable: all I ever observe is events following events, and I never catch any extra ingredient — no glue running from one to the other. And the view is so economical. It handles causal talk, explanation, prediction, and even our feeling that some outcomes are necessary, by saying that necessity is a habit of expectation I project onto the world after enough repetitions. That's a lot of mileage out of a metaphysics of nothing but regularities, and I like that it doesn't ask me to posit occult powers.

But I also can't shake the intuition, watching one billiard ball hit another, that the first ball makes the second one move. Not merely that the second has always moved after such impacts and I've learned to expect it, but that the effect is produced, that it had to happen given the setup. The regularity story says that "had to" is something my mind adds and it doesn't describe anything out there. And that feels less like an explanation of the intuition than a polite way of setting it aside. If the world is just one constant conjunction after another, why does it present itself with this compulsory feel?

I know the New Hume reading tries to split the difference: constant conjunction gives us no knowledge of hidden powers or real connections, but that's an epistemic point, and it doesn't rule out that such things exist behind the scenes, doing the actual binding, forever beyond what we can observe. That view honestly sounds built for people like me, but I also worry it's a face-saver, because the hidden connection it allows is exactly as invisible and exactly as idle as the regularity view said.

So I don't want a winner. I want to know what the world would have to be like for each move to be the right one: (1) the intuition is a byproduct of how a regularity-learning animal works and should be dropped; (2) the regularity theory is leaving something out and should be amended or replaced; (3) the New Hume compromise is a genuine third position and not a dodge. How would I tell, and which move is most honest about the costs it asks me to pay? And please answer in British English — spellings and all.
