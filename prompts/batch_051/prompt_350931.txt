Enactivism is usually presented as the view that cognition does not consist in recovering or manipulating internal representations of a pre-given world, but in the enactment of a meaningful world through a history of structural coupling between an autonomous organism and its environment. If that is genuinely the view, I do not see why it is not simply behaviorism under new terminology: the cognitive system is specified by its history of interactions with environmental contingencies, with sense-making and structural coupling doing the work that stimulus, response, and reinforcement once did. What does enactivism assert that behaviorism denies, and where would the two accounts part company in explaining, for instance, an infant's mastery of object permanence or a rat's learning of a maze?
