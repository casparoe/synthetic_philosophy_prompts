I have an argument that hedonism about well-being — the view that an episode is good for a person just when, and because, it gives that person pleasure — is either false or empty, and the reason is not the familiar worry that some pleasures are base. The reason is that 'pleasure' may fail to pick out a single thing at all. I would like to know whether the argument goes through and, if not, exactly where it breaks.

(1) If hedonism is a substantive theory, then the term 'pleasure' must denote a unified kind: there must be some property that all and only pleasures possess, and in virtue of which they count as pleasures.

(2) The only plausible candidate for such a property is a common felt quality — one introspectible character present in every pleasure regardless of its cause, object, or circumstances.

(3) But pleasures have no common felt quality. The feeling of warm water on cold hands, the relief when a month of uncertainty ends, the satisfaction of seeing a proof come together, and malicious pleasure at a rival's setback do not feel the same; they form a kind only by courtesy.

(4) So hedonism faces a dilemma. If it treats 'pleasure' as a unified felt kind, then (3) makes it false. If instead it stipulates that 'pleasure' just means whatever the subject finds positively pleasant, then the theory says only that well-being consists in whatever is positively pleasant — a restatement rather than a substantive claim.

I suspect the trouble is in (3), because one could insist that my four examples all share some residual quality of pleasantness; but that insistence can look like it is simply refusing to let the question be settled by introspection. Which step actually fails? If more than one step is weak, rank them; and for the weakest step, give the strongest reading on which it is true and say whether the argument still goes through under that reading.

I would also like you to say which single argument or consideration would most likely change your verdict about this argument, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
