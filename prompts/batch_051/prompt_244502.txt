Consider the following cluster of working rules in contemporary music licensing, and treat them as the practice to be analyzed.

A single song made available on a streaming service generates two distinct royalty streams. One stream is owed for the musical work (the composition), administered for songwriters and publishers; the other is owed for the sound recording (the master), administered for artists and labels. The same composition is registered across studio, live, remastered, and cover recordings, which may differ in key, tempo, instrumentation, and arrangement; each counts as an instance of the same work provided it reproduces the melody and, where the song has them, the lyrics, no matter how much else is altered. A live performance of the song by a different band is likewise treated as the same work for performance-licensing purposes, although it shares no physical part with the recording. Someone who records the song from scratch must obtain a license in the composition and, in doing so, creates a new and separate recording: the composition owner takes no interest in the new master, and the master owner takes no interest in the composition. At the same time, when a producer samples a brief, recognizable melodic fragment from an existing recording and places it inside a new track, the clearing practice requires licenses in both the sampled master and the underlying composition, and the new track is treated as containing the earlier work even though only a fragment of it was taken.

What theory of the nature of musical works does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct its implicit ontology in type/token terms: are musical works being treated as types rather than particulars, and, if so, as what sort of type — an abstract sound structure, a structure brought into being by a particular act of composition, a recipe or specification for performance, or something else? Distinguish carefully what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined because several theories of musical works would license the same rules. Finish by stating one concrete revision to the rules that would follow if a musical work were instead held to be nothing over and above a particular performance or recording event.
