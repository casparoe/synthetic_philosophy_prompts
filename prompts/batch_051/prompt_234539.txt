ok so ive been trying to actually live out stoic ideas for a few years now — mostly the modern revival flavor (daily-stoic type stuff, cbt-adjacent) — and i want an audit of my current position set before i send it to a friend whose actually done philosophy. i think i hold all of the following, but i get the feeling something in the list is pulling against something else:

(1) the dichotomy of control is basically right: the only things properly up to me are my judgments, choices, and where i put my attention. my health, my reputation, other people, election results — not up to me.

(2) i also think this doesn't make me passive. organizing, donating, voting, canvassing are choices, so they're inside the sphere; caring about the outcome is fine because what i control is the trying, not the result.

(3) i buy the axiology too, at least in practice: virtue is the only good, vice the only bad, and everything else — wealth, health, reputation, political outcomes — is indifferent, even if some of it is strongly preferred or dispreferred.

(4) but i can't actually say a child dying is a dispreferred indifferent and mean it. some things in the externals bucket are just bad, and grief or anger about them is appropriate, not a faulty judgment to be corrected.

(5) at the same time i think the passions are judgments — or at least ride on judgments — and that you loosen anger and anxiety by finding and testing the underlying judgment. this is the part that has worked best for me in practice.

(6) yet i also think emotions aren't fully voluntary. you can't withdraw assent from heartbreak the way you withdraw it from a suspicious email, and the idea that you could seems like a control fantasy rather than self-mastery.

so: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad are the real ones? rank the tensions by severity and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs me.
