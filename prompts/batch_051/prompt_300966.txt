I hold the view that corrigibility should be pursued by making an agent uncertain about its objective rather than by encoding shutdown behavior directly into the goal: the agent treats being shut down as evidence about what it should be doing, and is thereby disposed not to resist or disable the shutdown mechanism. Tell me what would count against this approach to the shutdown problem. I want the defeaters ranked from decisive to merely awkward: agent behaviors that would show the design has failed rather than just been badly trained, cases the approach cannot handle even in principle, what the view predicts that rival approaches would predict differently, and arguments or formal results that should move me. Also say what defenders of this approach would themselves concede counts against it, and give an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
