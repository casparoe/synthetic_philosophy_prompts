Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether it is legitimate to treat variables as independent when one has no specific evidence of dependence. His view was that, when all we know are the marginal distributions, the least opinionated assignment of a joint distribution is the product of the marginals — the independent one — and that this is what the maximum-entropy distribution already gives us, so defaulting to independence is simply refusing to smuggle in hidden assumptions. I replied that maximum entropy is itself a substantive commitment rather than a neutral absence of assumptions, and that when dependence is concentrated in the tails, this default is exactly what makes forecasters and risk models systematically overconfident.

I would like to move past arguments at the level of principle and ask what evidence could actually bear on this. Design the best test you can of the claim that independence is the appropriate default for inference under incomplete information — in the sense that it leads to better-calibrated or more decision-relevant judgments than a default that imputes at least some dependence among the variables.
