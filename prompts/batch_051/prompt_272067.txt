One of my seminars has drifted onto decision theory, and I keep being told I need to take "evidential cooperation in large worlds" seriously. As I understand it, the thought is this: in a big enough universe there are likely to be many agents whose reasoning and circumstances are correlated with mine, maybe even near-copies of me. So my deciding to cooperate (or free-ride) carries information about what those agents will decide too, and that evidential link can matter even when there is no causal connection between our choices. I'm a causal decision theorist by instinct, so I don't know how much of this is real and how much is double counting. If the idea is roughly right, I'd like you to spell out its most important implications for a few ordinary decisions: voting, donating to charity when I'll never see the result, and keeping an inconvenient promise that no one will find out about. Please keep it to the implications rather than a literature survey. Say how confident you are in each implication, and single out the one you are least sure about. And could you write the whole thing in British English, please?
