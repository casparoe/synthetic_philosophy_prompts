I run a small reading group on AI safety, and after a recent session on scalable oversight I ended up volunteering to interview a researcher who defends the debate protocol as the central route to evaluating arguments too sophisticated for any single judge to assess. The group's discussion kept snagging on obfuscated arguments: cases where a false or harmful conclusion is supported by reasoning that has been deliberately arranged to look exactly like the work of a careful, candid expert. I came away convinced this is the place to put real pressure on the position, and I would like help drafting the interview.

Please write eight to ten questions that go beyond having him restate the protocol or rehearse its motivation. At minimum, include a question pressing on the asymmetry between constructing a misleadingly persuasive argument and checking one (why think finding the flaw is easier than planting it?); a question on why the incentive structure of debate would not simply reward whichever debater is better at obfuscating; a question on what protects the verdict when the human judge is systematically weaker than both debaters at the relevant reasoning; and a question on what happens when the two debaters share a false background assumption that neither has an incentive to expose. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer a defender of debate is most likely to give — for instance, if he retreats to the claim that the better-supported position wins in expectation, push on what a claim about expected performance is supposed to license in a single high-stakes deployment where there is no averaging over many cases. The aim is a set of questions that makes the interview a genuine test of the view rather than a friendly restatement.
