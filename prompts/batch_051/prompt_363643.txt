I was reading the Book of Leviticus in the King James Version, and these two verses keep messing with my head:

21: And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness:

22: And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.

The text plainly says the iniquities get "put upon the head of the goat" and the goat "shall bear" them. So here's what I don't get: if the ritual really does transfer guilt like that, then once Aaron finishes, isn't the goat now the single most blameworthy creature around—the one stuck with every murder, theft, and betrayal committed by the whole community? Not just unclean or unlucky, but actually guilty. And if so, how is shoving it out into the desert anything other than dumping the community's punishment on an innocent animal? I can't tell whether I'm missing something basic about what "bearing iniquity" meant to whoever wrote this, or whether the institution is just built on a category mistake.
