I am a high school student who has spent the last few years writing tabletop game systems and moderating a small Minecraft server, so I think most naturally in a vocabulary of written rules versus moderator rulings, patch notes, secret versus published rules, vague rules that get enforced selectively, rules that no player could actually comply with, admins who are not bound by their own rules, and the distinction between a server that is merely well administered and one that is fair to its players. I have also been reading about the rule of law as an ideal, and I can follow the rough positions of Fuller, Raz, and Waldron.

I would like you to restate the debate among those three in my game-design and server-moderation vocabulary, and then tell me what did not survive the translation.

Here is the debate as I understand it. Fuller argues that law which systematically violates certain procedural criteria — rules made in advance and applied generally rather than ad hoc decisions, rules made public, no retroactive punishment, rules expressed clearly, rules that do not contradict one another, rules that do not demand impossible conduct, rules that remain stable enough for people to plan around, and officials actually acting according to the announced rules — does not just fail as good law; it fails as law in some important sense, and these criteria amount to an internal morality of law rather than mere efficiency conditions. Raz argues the opposite: the rule of law is a negative virtue, a virtue of the instrument rather than of the purposes it serves, the way a well-made knife is good whether it cuts bread or inflicts harm; it does not by itself give law moral content. Waldron shifts attention to process: the core of the rule of law is that those who exercise power over others must answer for it, give reasons, and allow those subject to the rules to argue about how they are applied.

Take each of these three positions and restate it as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Then report the residue. Which position translates cleanly, which translates only with distortion, and which has no real counterpart in the way I have learned to think about games and servers? And tell me your view of the residue: does it reveal a limitation in my vocabulary, something the philosophers are tracking that game administration does not naturally capture, or does it expose a place where the original philosophical claim is less clear than it looks? Keep the translation honest; do not help yourself to assumptions from game administration, such as that rules exist to make the activity fun or that players can always leave, and then count those as conclusions.
