After forty years as a physical chemist, I have retired and begun reading philosophy of biology for pleasure. I recently read a long essay in a popular technology magazine that set out a view I would like help evaluating. In essence, the essay claimed that natural selection is best understood as a hill-climbing algorithm: it nudges populations up a fitness landscape toward whichever local peak happens to be nearest, never sacrifices short-term fitness for long-term advantage, and for that reason tends to get marooned on local optima — which, the essay said, explains why organisms are clumsy, good-enough compromises rather than optimal designs. It then contrasted selection with modern gradient descent, which the author credited with computing exact gradients, escaping shallow local minima through momentum and restarts, and optimizing a single well-defined objective; on these grounds the essay called gradient descent a strictly better optimizer than natural selection. It closed with a general moral for machine learning: since gradient descent, like selection, can only ever make local improvements, we should not expect it to find globally optimal solutions.

Something about this felt too neat when I read it, but I cannot locate the error, if there is one. My own suspicion is that the trouble lies somewhere in the idea of a fixed landscape, but I would not trust that hunch without analysis. Treat me as a scientifically literate outsider, not as a grader. I want an accuracy audit of the essay's summary: which claims are defensible, which are subtly misleading or conflate things that should be kept apart, what a defender of the selection-as-optimizer picture would object to, and whether the closing moral about gradient descent survives correction.

Answer as an expert in evolutionary theory and the philosophy of optimization. Before writing, brainstorm the candidate objections and select the most important ones. Present your final answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
