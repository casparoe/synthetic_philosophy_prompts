I keep tripping over a failure pattern in long-running automated systems (pricing, recommendations, dynamic bidding) that I don't have a name for, and the names people offer me all feel like they're for a different animal. A system starts with a noisy model of the real-world target: what users actually want, or what the business actually cares about. Early on, the model gets checked against the real target fairly often, so it stays honest. Later, small mismatches get fixed by tweaking the model. Each tweak is small, locally sensible, and improves the weekly numbers, so nobody objects. After enough iterations, something strange has happened: the system is mostly tracking its own previous model rather than the outside target. It isn't exploiting a fixed metric, and it isn't trying to protect itself or accumulate influence. The map just quietly became the territory. The real target drifted off somewhere else, but no single change was ever big enough to trip an alarm, and any one update, audited alone, looks like a reasonable correction. I saw the same shape once in a non-AI setting: a policy manual revised every quarter, each revision fixing one felt misalignment with the previous version; six years later it plainly contradicted the orginal goals, even though every individual edit looked fine.

I'm not after the cinematic failure where the system goes power-hungry or resists being shut down. What I want to know is whether the slow migration of the thing-being-optimized has an established name, and whether whatever name exists actually covers the self-anchoring part—the map-becomes-territory part—or only a broader class. People say it's Goodhart's law, or reward hacking, but those seem to me to be about gaming a measure that stays fixed while behavior gets gamed; here the measure is being quietly rewritten by the system's own history. Is this a recognized concept, is it just a species of Goodhart, or is it genuinely distinct, and how would you draw the boundary: how self-referential does the drift have to be before it stops being ordinary model updating and becomes this thing?
