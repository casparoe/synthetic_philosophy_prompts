I’m a high school student writing an essay for a philosophy and literature class, and I’m trying to work through Tolstoy’s theory of art as it appears in *What Is Art?*. My essay builds around this passage from chapter 5 of the Maude translation:

“Art is not, as the metaphysicians say, the manifestation of some mysterious Idea of beauty or God; it is not, as the æsthetic physiologists say, a game in which man lets off his surplus stored-up energy; it is not the expression of man's emotions by external signs; it is not the production of pleasing objects; and, above all, it is not pleasure; but it is a means of union among men, joining them together in the same feelings, and indispensable for the life and progress toward well-being of individuals and of humanity.”

In my essay, I treat this passage as Tolstoy’s central definition of art, and I say it commits him to an infection or contagion view: real art happens when an artist has felt something and succeeds in transmitting that feeling to an audience. I then apply this to three cases. First, a documentary photograph of refugees that visibly transmits the photographer’s grief to viewers. Second, a pop song written from a breakup that leaves many listeners feeling the same sadness. Third, a technically complex atonal concert piece that most audience members find baffling and emotionally cold. My current claim is that Tolstoy would accept the first two as art, and would call the third bad art because it fails to transmit feeling. But I’m worried I may be conflating Tolstoy’s definition of art with his standard for good art. Does he think a failed transmission is still art, just unsuccessful art, or does failure to infect the audience mean the object is not art at all? I’d appreciate comments on whether my reading of this passage is defensible, whether my application to the three cases is faithful to Tolstoy, and where my essay most needs to distinguish what Tolstoy actually says from what I’m reading into him. Please also separate what is settled about Tolstoy’s view from what is genuinely contested among scholars, and avoid presenting a contested interpretation as the consensus.
