The old reductionist argument in philosophy of physics goes roughly like this: there is a bottom level of physics, and every higher-level fact (materials, chemistry, biology, you name it) is in principle fixed by how the fundamental stuff is arranged; higher-level theories are just compressed approximations, and the real explanation always runs upward from the micro level. Since the 1970s work on the Wilsonian renormalisation group and effective field theories, though, people have argued for a kind of autonomy of scales: a low-energy theory (say, hydrodynamics or BCS superconductivity) can be formulated and used on its own, is largely insensitive to the details of whatever UV completion sits underneath, and often many different UV completions give the same low-energy behaviour, so low-energy physics is underdetermined by any particular fundamental story.
