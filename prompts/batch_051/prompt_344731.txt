Consider Bernard Williams's Makropulos case and the dilemma he derives from it. On the first horn, an endless life in which the person's character and categorical desires remain fixed must eventually settle into tedium: any fixed repertoire of fundamental projects can be pursued and re-pursued only so many times before nothing engages the will any longer, which is the state of the centuries-old Makropulos when she finally lets herself die. On the second horn, if the person's character and categorical desires change enough to keep generating new reasons to live, then the later agent is no longer, in the relevant sense, the same person who wanted more life, so the earlier person does not receive the endless life they desired. Endless life is therefore either tedious or not the life of the one who asked for it.

Now vary the case in one respect. The immortal has a single unchanging categorical desire of second order: the desire that, whenever their present first-order categorical desires are close to exhaustion, new first-order categorical desires arise whose objects differ from the old ones and take their place. The second-order desire itself never changes, and memory, skill, and ordinary psychological continuity are preserved throughout. Nothing else is changed: health is perfect, external obstacles are absent, and time extends indefinitely forward.

Give the precise analysis of how Williams's dilemma applies to this self-renewing immortal. Does the case fall on the tedium horn, because satisfying even a second-order desire for fresh projects would eventually become a repetition of the same higher-order satisfaction? Does it fall on the identity horn, because the later first-order projects are not the earlier self's projects in the sense Williams requires? Or does the variant genuinely escape the dilemma, and if so, which premise of the original argument does it refute? State whether the self-renewing immortal has reason to welcome endless life, and whether this variant vindicates the coherence of heavenly bliss understood as unending life animated by a fixed desire for perpetual renewal. Do not merely note that Williams did not discuss this variant: construct the strongest Williams-style response the case permits and say whether it succeeds.
