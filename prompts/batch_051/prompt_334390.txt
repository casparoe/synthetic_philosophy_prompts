so my friend and i just spent two hours arguing about otto neurath and whether his isotype picture language was actually a language or just a set of standardized icons. his line was basically: pictures can only show particular things, they can't assert, negate, or argue, so calling it a language is a methaphor and a misleading one. i kept trying to explain that neurath and his collaborators were explicit that the pictograms are conventional signs, not little portraits, and that they get combined by rules into charts that do make claims — things like "more workers in this industry died at this time than at that time" — and that the whole point was a neutral visual way of stating social and scientific information for people who weren't trained readers and for public education. we never settled it because we kept talking past each other, and afterwards i realized the fault was probably mine: i had never thought through what a person has to understand before the claim "pictures can be a language" even becomes something you can argue about rather than a slogan.
