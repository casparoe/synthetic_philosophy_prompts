I teach an upper-level undergraduate course on rational choice and the foundations of decision theory, and I am drafting the final exam. We have covered the von Neumann–Morgenstern and Savage axiomatizations of expected utility, their representation theorems, Dutch book and money pump arguments, and the Allais paradox, so the students know the standard machinery well. I need one 75-minute essay question, worth 25 points, that cannot be answered by reciting the axioms or summarizing the readings. The question should force students to think through what coherence arguments for expected-utility maximization actually establish. In particular, it should make them separate three things: (1) the formal claim that conditions such as completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence guarantee that preferences can be represented by a utility function; (2) the normative claim that preferences violating these conditions are incoherent and therefore rationally criticizable; and (3) the support that exploitability arguments, such as Dutch books and money pumps, actually provide for (2). A strong question will require students to confront the gap between (1) and (2) and to assess whether (3) closes it, including at least one familiar objection: for example, that a sophisticated chooser can decline sequential trades and avoid being exploited, or that vulnerability to a clever bookie is not the same thing as irrationality.

Please write the exam question itself, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer. The marking scheme should name the specific moves an A answer must contain, roughly five or six of them, and should also identify the specific misunderstandings that keep an answer at B level, such as treating the representation theorem as if it were already a normative argument, listing the axioms without putting them to work, or presenting the Dutch book as a knockdown proof that non-expected-utility preferences are irrational without considering any replies. The question should be self-contained for students with the course background described above and should not require outside reading or citations. Please write the question, model answer, and marking scheme in American English.
