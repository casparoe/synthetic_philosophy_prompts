Two people on my course spent an entire seminar arguing about this and I still don't know who was right. Sam was defending closure: he said if I know I have hands, and I know that having hands entails that I'm not a brain in a vat, then I'm in a position to know I'm not a brain in a vat. He said abandoning that is worse than scepticism, because it leaves you stuck asserting that you know you have hands but you don't know you're not a brain in a vat.

Priya said that's exactly the right result. She was pushing Nozick's sensitivity condition: you know p only if, had p been false, you wouldn't have believed p. For 'I have hands' the nearest world in which I don't have hands is one where I lost them, not one where I'm a brain in a vat, and there I wouldnt believe I have hands. So I do know I have hands. But for 'I am not a brain in a vat', the nearest world in which I am one is a world where I'd still believe I'm not, because the stimulation is that good. So I don't know that. Closure is the thing that has to go, she said.

Sam came back with the lottery. He said on the sensitivity condition you also can't know, just from the odds, that your ticket will lose, because in the nearest world where it wins you'd still have believed it would lose before the draw. He thought that was absurd enough to reject the condition. Priya said that's a feature: you don't know a lottery outcome from the odds alone.

Then Sam said well why not keep the tracking idea but use safety instead: you know p only if, in nearby worlds where you believe p, p is true. Safety lets you know you aren't a brain in a vat, keeps ordinary knowledge, and doesn't force you to give up closure, so sensitivity was never the only game in town. Priya said safety isn't tracking at all — it just rules the vat world out as 'not nearby'; it doesn't require your belief to be connected to the fact in the way sensitivity does.

I never worked out where each step goes right or wrong. Go through their exchange and adjudicate it: which claims should each side concede, and which position, on balance, has the stronger case? Please write the analysis in British English.
