Before Friday I have to present my views on complicity, and it's dawned on me that the committments have piled up one at a time and I've never actualy made them face each other. Could you do an audit? The position, as it currently stands, goes like this.

I think complicity is about participating in someone else's wrong, not about whether your part made a causal difference. The lookout who falls asleep before the robbery and never warns anyone is still complicit for me, because he signed on to the plan.

I also think complicity requires actual knowledge. If you merely suspected something, or you didn't investigate when maybe you should have, that's some other kind of failure—recklessness or negligence—but not complicity.

But I don't think you need to share the wrongdoer's aim. A lab tech who figures out the safety data is being faked and quietly keeps assembling the spreadsheets because it's her job and she needs the paycheck is complicit, even though she wishes they'd stop.

On omissions: staying silent can make you complicit, but only if you'd already taken on a role or a duty toward the victim or the situation. A random bystander who could have reported it and didn't is just a failed bystander, not complicit.

Then I hold that benefiting from a wrong, all by itself, never makes you complicit. You have to actually do one of the complicity-ish things—provide the means, coordinate, cover up, encourage. So an investor who learns the company is concealing a safety defect but keeps the shares for the dividend is a passive beneficiary, not complicit.

And two more case verdicts I'd defend. A supplier sells ordinary off-the-shelf laptops to a company he knows is running a boiler-room fraud; he'd sell the same laptops to anyone, and I don't call him complicit. An executive has a gut feeling that the expense reports in his division are fraudulent but deliberately never looks into them, and I do call him complicit in whatever gets hidden on his watch.

Which of these pull against each other, ranked by how severe, with what would have to give and what it costs? And which pairs merely look inconsistent until you draw the right distinction?
