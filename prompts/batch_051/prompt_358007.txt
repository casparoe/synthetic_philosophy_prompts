I keep going back and forth on original vs derived intentionality. On reflection the Dennett side seems right: every bit of content we can point to is there because we treat something as if it had it, and the idea of a special intrinsic aboutness in human heads starts to look like an unearned assumption. But then I sit with my own thoughts and I cannot make myself believe they are only derived in that way. Searle's point about the Chinese room keeps pulling at me, and I find Dennett's two-bitser charge a bit too quick: maybe Searle is pointing at something real that we haven't explained yet, not something we should define away. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would actually cost to give up the intuition that some intentionality is original, what it would cost to amend the all-derived view so it can mark a difference between me and a pocket calculator, and whether there's a reading of original on which both are true. Also, is Dennett's two-bitser move a real argument or just rhetoric? Give me the strongest version of each side and then say which you would give up.
