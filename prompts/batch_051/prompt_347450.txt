I am a high school student working on a history-and-economics project about why some technologies and rules persist, and I keep meeting a claim I do not know how to evaluate: that history is 'locked in.' As I understand it, there is a weaker version and a stronger version. The weaker version says an early, causally small accident — the stock example is the QWERTY keyboard, though I have seen even that case disputed — can push later choices into a groove that persists. The stronger version says the groove persists even when a better alternative is available, and that if history were rerun with tiny differences, our outcome would not have arrived anyway.

I want a way to test that stronger claim rather than just repeat it. Two problems stand in the way: we cannot rerun history, and we mostly hear about the cases that stayed stuck, while cases that corrected themselves would be invisible.

Please design the strongest possible test of the lock-in claim. Be concrete about what comparison, measurement, or simulation you would use; what pattern of results would favor the lock-in reading and what pattern would favor the alternative (that the outcome reflects actual superiority, or would have arrived anyway); and how the design copes with the rerun problem and the survivor problem. End by saying exactly what a positive result would and would not settle.
