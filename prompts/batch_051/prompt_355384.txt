I keep coming back to this claim that a brain interface could someday give someone a genuinely new colour — not just a shade between two existing ones, but a colour no one has ever seen, the way reddish-green is supposed to be forbidden by opponent-process theory because red and green are mutually inhibitory. There's already a small literature of people trying to get subjects to see forbidden colours by stabilizing the image on the retina, with mixed results. Now suppose a lab claims they've got an implant that, when they stimulate a certain spot in visual cortex, makes a subject reliably report 'a colour I've never seen and can't describe.' How would you test whether that's a genuinely novel quale, as opposed to a weird composite of existing colours, or a run-of-the-mill forbidden colour like reddish-green, or just the subject confabulating a report because the experimenter wants one? Design the best experiment you can — be concrete about tasks, stimuli, and which results count for which reading. You'll need to say up front how you're operationalizing 'genuinely novel,' and where that definition is doing real work. Then tell me what a positive result would actually show, and what no experiment could settle even in principle.
