I keep hearing philosophers and some scientists defend what they call a presumption of independence: when you're doing a back-of-the-envelope estimate and you multiply together a bunch of separately estimated factors (fraction of stars with planets, times fraction of those where life emerges, times...), you're entitled to treat the factors as independent unless you have specific evidence they're coupled. The idea being that independence is the neutral, no-information default.

I gather some people push back hard on this and say independence isn't the absence of an assumption at all — its an extremely strong, almost-never-exactly-true structural claim, and being ignorant of a coupling between factors doesn't license setting that coupling to zero. And maybe there's a subtler back-and-forth under that: if you fix the marginals, multiplying them gives you the maximum-entropy joint, which sounds like a principled "least additional information" choice, but you don't actually know the marginals, and max-entropy can still make surprisingly sharp predictions about functions of the factors.
