I need a working definition of “moral progress” that can handle the following cases without collapsing into mere change in moral beliefs or mere improvement in material welfare. State the definition, then apply it to each case, indicating whether it counts as progress, regression, both, or neither, and flag which of your verdicts is least secure.

The cases:

- The British Empire abolishes the slave trade in 1807 and slavery itself in 1833, after decades of antislavery argument and in the face of strong commercial resistance.
- Tokugawa Japan suppresses junshi, the practice of a retainer following his lord in death, reclassifying what had been admired as loyalty as a legal offense.
- Switzerland adds a constitutional provision declaring that animals are not things, while industrial animal farming continues to expand within the same legal order.
- A kinship-based society replaces cyclical blood feuds with an impartial court system that reduces vendetta killings but sometimes punishes the innocent and concentrates power in a distant state.
- A revolutionary government defines a whole class of people as enemies outside the moral community, and ordinary citizens denounce former neighbors, leading to mass imprisonment and execution.
- A religious community strengthens care for its own members while treating ex-members as spiritually defiled and refusing ordinary social contact with them.

Then answer two further questions. Does your definition imply that moral progress is necessarily an expansion of the circle of moral concern — and if so, how does it handle the second, fifth, and sixth cases? And is there any case on the list for which your definition yields the verdict “progress” while your own judgment hesitates, or vice versa?
