I'm putting together a unit for a graduate seminar on comparing welfare across very different kinds of minds — adult humans, human infants, fish, insects, and hypothetical artificial systems — and I keep running into a prior question about a single experience before we ever get to comparing two lives or two populations. Call the two features that seem to matter "height" and "valence": height is how vivid, wakeful, or high-resolution the experience is (a rough stand-in for degree of consciousness or sentience), and valence is how pleasant or unpleasant it feels, abstracting away from height. I don't want to get tangled in measurement for now; I just want the underlying axiological shape sorted.

I'd like you to rank the following candidate combination rules, treating them as rival accounts of how the value-for-the-being of an experience is fixed by its height and valence. The candidates are: (a) additive — height and valence each contribute directly with independent weight, so one can partly compensate for the other; (b) multiplicative — value is the product of the two, so a zero on either score yields zero, and gains in one dimension amplify gains in the other; (c) height-lexical — any difference in height outweighs any difference in valence, with valence serving only as a tiebreaker within fixed height; (d) valence-lexical — the reverse; (e) threshold — valence below a minimum height counts at zero or is steeply discounted, and above that minimum only valence matters, so height is a gate rather than a weight; (f) valence-only — height has no axiological role at all, though it remains evidentially useful for deciding whether valence is present or genuine.

Please give me a three-tier ranking (top tier: the account or accounts you'd actually defend; middle tier: defensible but clearly worse; bottom tier: should be rejected), with a one-sentence justification for each candidate's placement, and make at least three of those justifications lean on concrete comparison cases — say, a minimally responsive but comfortable patient, a vividly conscious being in mild discomfort, and a high-capacity mind in ecstasy. Then close with a short paragraph telling me which argument or consideration would most likely make you move the top tier down or a middle-tier account into the top tier, and where your ranking is least stable or most an artifact of the cases you chose.
