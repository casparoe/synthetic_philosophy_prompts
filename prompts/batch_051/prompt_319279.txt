HR departments run generational sensitivity trainings, marketing teams plan whole campaigns around Boomers vs. Millennials vs. Gen Z, and pollsters report things like "Gen Z now believes X." What sort of theory of what a generation is does this practice presuppose? Does it presuppose that cohorts are real kinds—stable, explanatory groupings like biological species—or is it compatible with thinking theyre rough statistical categories or just made-up labels? I keep going back and forth because the same firms that treat these cohorts as deeply different will also admit the boundaries are arbitrary and that the differences are small compared to variation within a cohort. Reconstruct the implicit ontology here, say whether the practice actually needs it or whether several different views could underwrite the practice, and tell me what would have to change in how these companies talk and act if we replaced it with a deflationary view on which generations are just convenient fictions.
