I'm an engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I'd like help pinning down a problem I keep running into when designing systems that reason under uncertainty. I want a system to hold graded confidence in mathematical statements and update those confidences as new information arrives, more or less the way a Bayesian updates on evidence. But the numbers keep coming out inconsistent with logic. The system can be 0.9 confident in each of two lemmas, 0.7 confident in their conjunction, and 0.4 confident in a theorem that the conjunction demonstrably entails, even though the theorem cannot be less probable than the conjunction. The only remedy I know is to have the system actually run the derivation, but then it becomes simply certain of the theorem, and the graded uncertainty I wanted for guiding search disappears. I keep half-recognizing a name for this problem in things I've read, but I can't tell whether those discussions are about the same thing or something adjacent. Does this situation have an established name, and how is it usually carved up? I'm not satisfied with a bare label; I want to know what the nearest established concepts are, how they differ from one another, and which of them actually matches the thing I'm describing. If none fits cleanly, I'd like to know what you would call it and where its boundaries lie.
