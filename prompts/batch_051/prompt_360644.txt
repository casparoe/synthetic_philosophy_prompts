Ok so in my world history class we spent a day on Han Feizi and legalism, and my teacher framed it as a fight between Confucians (rule through virtue, ritual, setting a good example) and Han Feizi (people are basically self-interested, nobody is reliably good, so you need published laws plus the "two handles" of reward and punishment). The stuff that stuck with me: the ruler should hide his preferences so advisors can't just tell him what he wants to hear, there's this thing called xing-ming where you compare someone's stated job or title to their actual output and punish the mismatch, and the punishments are supposed to be scary enough that people actually fear them.

So I got put on the student council committee redesigning our peer tutoring program, which currently runs on guilt and "please be a good person" emails, and everybody flakes. I want to propose basically a Legalist version, as a thought experiment. Here's how I imagine it: no more appeals to empathy. There's a public spreadsheet. Each session is a posted slot with a specific topic and learning goal. The tutee fills out a 1-5 form after each session; no form equals zero points for the tutor. Tutors with a high enough average after ten sessions get something they actually want (priority parking, two extra prom tickets, whatever), one no-show per semester is fine but a second costs you prom tickets, and the council chair (me, maybe) never praises or scolds anyone in person, never meets tutors privately about their performance, and automatically ignores any "but I was trying" appeal because only the posted numbers count. If a tutee no-shows but rates you 1 anyway, you have 48 hours to file a dispute or the rating stands.

Walk me through how this actually plays out over a semester. What have tutors and tutees figured out by week three, where does it get gamed first (fake sessions, bribing tutees with snacks for a 5, only taking easy tutees), does tutoring get better, or does everyone just learn to farm points? And the part I keep going back to: Han Feizi seems to assume the ruler has genuinely scary punishments like death or exile. Does the system still do what he wants when the worst possible penalty is loosing prom tickets, or does it only work because the stakes are high enough that people don't get cute about it?
