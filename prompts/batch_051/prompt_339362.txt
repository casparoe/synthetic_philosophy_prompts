Okay, I'm on the rules committee of a local civic league and we have a live dispute about a clause I think is about to get interpreted well past what the people who wrote it had in mind. The clause is in our rules of debate, and it got added after a period where a couple of members kept trying to settle every housing and policing question by citing scripture. It reads:

"In debate, no member may offer as a reason for or against a resolution any claim that depends for its truth on the authority of a particular sacred text, on a doctrine of a particular faith, or on an event known only through private revelation."

The case: a member moved that the league endorse a county restorative-justice diversion ballot measure. In her floor remarks she used only policy stuff — recidivism numbers, cost per participant, results elsewhere. Then she asked that a letter signed by forty-one clergy, almost all from two denominations, be entered into the minutes as part of her statement. The letter ends by calling the measure "a faithful response to the God who commands repair," but most of it just repeats her policy points in churchier language. Two members filed a challenge: entering the letter is offering it as a reason, and the religious framing is doing real justificatory work regardless of who signed it. She responds that she is not asking anyone to accept the theological claim; the letter is evidence that support for the measure crosses religious lines, and a letter from clergy is about the only way to show that publicly. The rule, she says, was written to keep sectarian arguments off the floor, not to stop members from putting community support into the record just because the support comes in a religious idiom.

So I want you to rule on it, or at least tell me what the best ruling is. Go through it three ways: the text alone, the purpose of the clause (and what the clause is actually protecting), and what the people who wrote it would have said if we could ask them. Then tell me which of those should control when they pull apart, and whether there is a version of the clause that would keep out scripture-quoting without also excluding letters like this. I want a definite answer, not a tour of possible readings — if the right answer is that the challenge fails, say that and defend it, and if the right answer is that the letter has to come out of the minutes, same. And do it in American English, please.
