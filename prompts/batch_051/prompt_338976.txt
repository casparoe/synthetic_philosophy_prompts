I'm a retired entomologist who got into ethics after I stopped working, and the two now rub against eachother constantly. At a township meeting, two neighbors argued about whether to keep our summer mosquito-fogging program—no disease outbreak, just comfort. They both sounded right to me at points, so I'd like a real verdict.

(1) Ellen said the program kills billions of insects for a bit of comfort, and since some insects show pain-like behavior and learned avoidance, even a modest chance they suffer should stop us at that scale. Mark said insects are reflex bundles with nobody home; writhing is just nociception, like a thermostat.

(2) Ellen said that's the exact line people used about fish, and with numbers this big uncertainty should carry a lot of weight. Mark said even if an insect feels a tiny blip, its interests are near zero; one mosquito is worth a fraction of an itch-free evening.

(3) Ellen said that's bad aggregation: a billion paper cuts aren't fine because each is small; if insects count at all, he needs a reason their interests don't add up. Mark said the harm is just what nature does anyway—dragonflies shred mosquitoes—so if the sum mattered, every meadow would be a moral emergency, which is absurd.

(4) Ellen said 'too demanding' is about comfort, not truth, and our deciding to blanket-spray differs from a dragonfly's killing. Mark said if suffering matters, the killer's identity shouldn't; she's just squeamish about human agency, and itch-free yards are a real good.

(5) Ellen said agency matters because we can deliberate and scale back, comfort is weak against massive possible harm, and traps and screens get most of the benefit with a fraction of the killing. Mark said that slides from 'killing is wrong' to 'kill fewer if convenient,' which isn't a principle—just a compromise.

Please give the adjudication as exactly five numbered items, one per move, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion; for each move say who's right about what, what's wrong or missing, and how it bears on the overall question.
