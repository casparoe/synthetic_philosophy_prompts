A friend of mine who thinks blackmail laws are incoherent keeps saying blackmail is just selling silence: in a case where the threatened disclosure would itself be legal, the secret-holder is allowed to stay quiet, allowed to spill, and allowed to ask for money, so the law is punishing him for selling a thing he'd otherwise be free to give away. Audit that way of putting it. Give me the correspondence table first — what's supposed to be the commodity, the seller, the buyer, the threat, the price — and then the places where it breaks. Which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion my friend wants to draw, and which are just incidental? Does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy carrying the whole argument?
