The original Pascal's mugging, as I understand it: a mugger says that unless you hand over $5, he will torture 10^10^10 or some comparably huge number of simulated people. If you assign even a tiny nonzero probability to his claim, and your utility is unbounded, expected utility says pay, because the stakes swamp the improbability. The standard saves are to bound the utility function, to put a complexity penalty on hypotheses so the mugger's claim ends up with effectively zero probability, or to notice that the same logic lets infinitely many hypothetical muggers empty your wallet, so you need a standing policy of not paying.
