The standard debate about fakes and forgeries assumes there is an original whose distinctive value lies in its unique history. Walter Benjamin took a related thought further: mechanical reproduction, he argued, drains an artwork of the aura connected to its unique existence at a particular time and place. Generative AI now produces images that are not reproductions of any particular original — there is no single originating moment, location, or authorial act whose uniqueness could be lost. What do you think Benjamin would have made of this, if he had lived to see it? Without just repeating the aura slogan, reconstruct from the actual commitments in his account of originals, copies, and the changing function of art where he would likely have come down. Would he have treated AI-generated imagery as one more stage in the destruction of aura, or as a break that dissolved the original/copy distinction his framework depends on? Reason through the answer.
