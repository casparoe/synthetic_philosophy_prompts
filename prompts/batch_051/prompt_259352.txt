Next week I present to my department's philosophy of probability reading group, and the paper argues that the maximum entropy principle solves the problem of choosing priors when you have only partial background information. My line is this: if all you know is, for example, the mean of a nonnegative quantity, then among all the distributions consistent with that constraint, the rational one is the maximum-entropy one, because any other distribution is smuggling in information you don't actually have. I extend the argument to continuous unknowns via the usual limiting procedure, and I claim the standard reparameterization objection is not decisive, because reparameterizing also changes what the constraint says, so the two priors aren't really answers to the same question.

The audience is not friendly. There's Morton, a de Finetti-style subjective Bayesian who thinks rationality is just coherence and any coherent prior is permissible; he'll say my whole project is an attempt to get uniqueness out of axioms that are themselves optional. There's Priya, a statistician who cares about frequentist performance and will ask whether max-ent priors actually produce good coverage or just look philosophically tidy. There are two people sympathetic to objective Bayesianism who are less sure about the maximum entropy part: they'll press on how I justify the constraints themselves, and whether the principle just launders information through an entropy functional. And I wouldn't be suprised if a grad student asks about Bertrand's paradox and the choice of limiting procedure.

What I want is a prep sheet, not a summary of my talk. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way those people would actually phrase them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my own argument I should prepare to defend instead of hoping nobody notices. Also tell me the single reply that has to land for the talk to survive. And if the whole position as I've described it is confused or the task doesn't make sense, say so plainly rather than humoring me.
