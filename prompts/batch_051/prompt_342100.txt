Suppose a statute imposes an enhanced penalty on anyone who transports a controlled substance within three hundred meters of a school. A defendant stops his car just outside the zone and then pushes it through the zone with the engine off and the drugs in the trunk. A textualist judge asks what the statutory term transports would ordinarily mean in this context; a purposivist judge asks what rule would best serve the statute's evident aim of keeping drugs away from schools. My confusion is this: to determine which sense of transports is the ordinary one in context, the textualist must already decide whether pushing the car counts as the relevant kind of conveyance, and that decision seems indistinguishable from asking what the statute is for. In a case like this, is the disagreement between textualism and purposivism a genuine disagreement about what makes an interpretation correct, or only a disagreement about which moves a judge may openly acknowledge in an opinion?
