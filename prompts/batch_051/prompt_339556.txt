I teach a small grad seminar on decision theory — 12 students, one 75-minute block. I want a game that makes them feel the pull of updateless decision theory without me naming it, or Newcomb's problem, untill the debrief. The thing I want them to run into on their own is this: when your decision procedure is correlated with an observation you've already made, the action with the highest expected value can be the one you'd have committed to from a prior perspective, and updating on the observation can make that action look like a mistake. I don't want a lecture upfront and I don't want a box-game rehashing Newcomb — they've all seen Newcomb. Design something with real small stakes (points or candy, no money), say what each student observes and when they observe it, the payoff scheme, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief. The debrief should map what happened back onto the distinction between choosing a policy under your prior and choosing an act after updating, and it should be honest about which part of UDT the activity can't reproduce no matter how the rules are set up. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it.
