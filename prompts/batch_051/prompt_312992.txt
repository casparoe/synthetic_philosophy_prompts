Compare open sets and groups as cases where mathematicians picked a fairly abstract definition over the concrete thing they originally wanted to talk about. For open sets: why should continuity in metric spaces be re-expressed as preimages of open sets being open, and why do the axioms allow arbitrary unions but only finite intersections—why not countable intersections, or closed sets as the primitive notion? For groups: why should symmetry be captured by an associative operation with an identity and inverses, rather than by a concrete bunch of permutations closed under composition? Is the payoff the same in both cases, or is one definition successful mainly because it isolates a shared structure while the other is successful because it deliberately forgets structure? If a student complained that both definitions feel like the axioms were rigged so the theorems come out right, how would you answer differently in the two cases?
