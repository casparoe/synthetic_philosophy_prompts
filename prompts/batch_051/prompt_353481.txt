I've been trying to think through the handicap principle on my own, and I keep arriving at an argument that seems to show it is empty. I half-suspect I'm making a mistake, but I can't locate it. Here is the argument.

(1) A signal is honest only if a low-quality individual would lose more by sending it than they could gain, while a high-quality individual would gain more than they lose.
(2) So an honest signal works by imposing a cost that low-quality individuals cannot profitably bear.
(3) But the handicap principle also says the high-quality individual pays a real cost for the same display; the signal is a handicap, not a free badge.
(4) Any cost that a high-quality individual and a low-quality faker would both pay in full cannot be the feature that tells them apart, since both of them bear it.
(5) Therefore only the difference in cost between high- and low-quality individuals can do the sorting.
(6) But if only a difference in cost does the sorting, then the costly display itself is doing none of the work; any trait that happened to impose the same cost difference would do just as well.
(7) So the handicap principle collapses into the trivial point that honest signals must be correlated with quality, and the display's expense turns out to be pure waste.

Tell me which step, if any, is wrong, and whether the conclusion follows. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Use American English.
