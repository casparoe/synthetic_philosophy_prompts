Here's a proposal for a restaurant rating app that wants to kill the pattern where the first handful of reviews basically decides a place's score, because everyone after them sees the average and piles on. The rule: for a newly opened restaurant, the first 40 reviews are collected into a hidden pool. Whenever one of those 40 reviewers rates, they are shown exactly three earlier reviews from the pool, chosen uniformly at random, and they are never shown the overall average or the number of reviews so far. After the 40th review, the pool goes public and the site becomes a normal average-and-count rating page. Walk me through what this actually does. Do those first 40 reviews stay genuinely independent and informative, or do the random three just produce a fuzzier, slower version of the same cascade? Who ends up in the early reviewing pool once people understand the system, and how do restaurants game it (planting friendly early reviews, trying to identify or compensate the first 40 reviewers, maybe delaying opening until the pool is seeded)? Does the choice of 40 and 3 matter a lot, and what happens as you push either number toward an extreme? Where does this scheme fail first, and is that failure patchable or would a patch just move the herding one step earlier?
