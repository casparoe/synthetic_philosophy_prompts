So I'm in this political ethics seminar and we've been doing a unit on continuity of government — like the actual practice, not the show — where before a big event with the whole line of succession in one room, one eligible person is quietly send to an undisclosed location so that if everyone else dies, the state still has a head. What our prof kept stressing is that this isn't just boring administrative design; it's this weird moral trade: the survivor is singled out for protection, but they're also being preserved like a spare part, and the officials sitting in the chamber are all being left with the risk on the theory that continuity of the state matters more than any particular one of them. And none of them — the survivor included — gets to consent to which role they're assigned; somebody else makes that call in advance, usually in secret.

I'd like you to write a short story about someone who is that designated survivor. But don't do the standard lone-leader-emerges-after-tragedy arc. I want the story to actually live inside these questions: Is pulling one person out of the room respecting everyone equally, or treating public officials as interchangeable instruments of the state? Is the survivor protected, rewarded, excluded, or fenced off like livestock — and how would they tell the difference? The main character should oscillate on this and never fully land. Please don't name-drop philosophers or turn it into a debate; just dramatize it. End on a small but loaded decision the survivor has to make that sharpens the question without resolving it.
