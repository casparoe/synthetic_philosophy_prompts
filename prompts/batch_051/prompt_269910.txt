I'm building a list of experimental results that have been used to argue that conscious experience is postdictively assembled — that later events can alter the content of an earlier experience, its subjectively felt timing, or whether it reaches awareness at all — and thereby to suggest that the distinction between Stalinesque revision before awareness and Orwellian revision of memory after the fact may be empirically underdetermined. So far I have five standard phenomena:

1. The color-phi phenomenon: a red dot flashed at one location followed by a green dot at another is seen as a single dot moving across the gap and turning green at the midpoint, before the second dot is physically present.
2. Libet's backward referral: a skin or cortical stimulus is felt as occurring at the moment of stimulation, even though the neural processing on which awareness depends takes several hundred milliseconds.
3. The cutaneous rabbit: brief taps delivered rapidly at two or three sites on the forearm are felt at evenly spaced intermediate sites, so later taps shift the felt location of earlier ones.
4. Metacontrast masking: a briefly flashed target followed tens of milliseconds later by a non-overlapping surrounding contour is not consciously seen, so a later event determines whether an earlier one becomes conscious.
5. The stopped-clock illusion (chronostasis): on first glancing at a clock with a ticking second hand, the hand appears frozen for longer than a second, as if the post-saccadic image is retained and backdated across the interval of the saccade.

I am fairly sure I am forgetting one more standard phenomenon of exactly the same kind. Give me the sixth. It must be genuinely new relative to this list — not a rewording, special case, or merely multimodal version of any of the five, and in particular not another masking effect and not another apparent-motion effect. Describe it precisely enough to distinguish it from all five; state which item on the list it comes closest to and why it is nonetheless a distinct phenomenon; and explain what it contributes to the postdiction debate that the other five do not.
