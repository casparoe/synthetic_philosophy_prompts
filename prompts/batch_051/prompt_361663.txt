I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or classics, and in my spare time I read a lot of mythology. I've noticed that I follow a consistent rule when a story credits a named figure with divine powers, miracles, or supernatural ancestry: I assume that a real person or a real event lies somewhere behind the story, and that later retellings inflated it into something superhuman. I turn to symbolic, cosmological, or psychological readings only after the human-origin reading has failed in several independent ways. For example, when I read that a local ruler could control storms, my first move is to wonder which actual ruler and which actual storm got fused together. Is this a defensible rule of thumb, or is it a bias toward the most human-shaped explanation dressed up as a principle? Under what conditions does a human-origin default track the truth, and where does it systematically mislead? Could the rule be restated to keep its advantages without its blind spots?
