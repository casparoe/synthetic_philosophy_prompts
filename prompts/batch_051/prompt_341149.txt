I've been gnawing on this one for a while and I want someone to structure it for me rather than pick a side. In my philosophy of language class I keep running the Latin-to-Italian case, and I catch myself giving the continuity story: there was never a day when Latin-speaking parents failed to understand their Italian-speaking children; each generation understood the last well enough; 'Latin' and 'Italian' are just labels we stick on the two ends of a long chain of overlapping idiolects. I find that story genuinely convincing. But I also cant shake the intuition that something real changed in there, not just our labeling. It isnt vocabulary piling up: the case system fell apart, word order took over its jobs, and somewhere along the chain the grammar stopped being a variant of the old system and became a new one. If every change was gradual, how could that happen? And if it couldnt, why does the intuition keep surviving every time I try to kill it? I don't want to be told which side wins. Tell me what it would take for the intuition to be the thing I give up, what it would take for the continuity story to be the thing I amend, and whether there's a reading of 'became' on which both survive. Maybe the real question is what kind of fact 'it's still Latin' could be.
