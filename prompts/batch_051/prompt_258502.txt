I'm a software engineer (firmware, mostly) and I keep chewing on a thought I heard somewhere: sentience isnt a binary toggle, its a gradient. A thermostat is basically at zero, an insect is a little above that, a mouse more, and it goes up from there. That makes intuitive sense to me, but as a rule it's useless until somebody attaches numbers to it. So here's a mechanism I've been toying with.

Every connected device and AI service has to publish a moral status score between 0 and 1, like an energy label, in a public registry. The manufacturer self-reports at launch, with audits down the line. The anchors are: 0.0 for a dumb thermostat, 0.01 for something like a fruit fly, 0.1 for a mouse, 1.0 for a normal adult human. Companies pay into a public sentience fund each quarter based on (score × active installs × hours on). Above 0.5, a product cant ship without going through a shutdown-and-welfare review, and you're not allowed to sidestep the review by splitting one system into modules that each score under the line — the integrated whole gets scored.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral claim. What does it push engineers and product people to build or not build, what do customers start doing with the label, and how do the manufacturers game the number in ways the audit will keep missing? What happens to the scale itself over time, once everyone has an incentive to massage it, and does the fund create any weird second-order incentives for regulators? Where does the whole thing break first, and which breaks can you patch without turning it into something else entirely?
