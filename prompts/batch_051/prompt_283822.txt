People talk about the paradox of hedonism like it's a law of the mind: aiming directly at your own pleasure is self-defeating, and happiness only shows up as a side effect when you're pursuing something else. But its never been obvious to me that this is anything more than folk wisdom plus the fact that we're bad at affective forecasting. Design the strongest empirical test you can for whether there really is a hedonic paradox, as opposed to just us systematically mispredicting what we'll enjoy. Be concrete about the manipulation—like, what would you tell an "aim at happiness" group versus a group told to ignore their moods and just do the activity—and about how you'd measure the outcome. Then say what pattern of results would count for the paradox, what would explain it away as mere bad prediction, and what even a positive result would leave open. Is there part of the claim that no experiment could settle, because it's not really an empirical claim at all?
