Having come to philosophy late, after a career in the natural sciences, I am struck by how readily the claim that art is propaganda is dismissed by readers who would never dismiss a measurement because it had been commissioned by an engineer. I have been reading about W. E. B. Du Bois's position that art is never politically innocent: every work, by what it depicts and what it omits, either reinforces or undermines some arrangement of power, and an artist who refuses a political brief has simply chosen an unacknowledged brief. The opposing camp denies this. It holds that a work of art is answerable primarily to standards internal to the work, and that weighting art by its political effects confuses art with advocacy.

Draft the document that would emerge from an adversarial collaboration between (1) a Du Boisian instrumentalist, who holds that art is unavoidably propaganda and that the artist's proper task is to make it good propaganda, and (2) an aesthetic autonomist, who holds that political efficacy is neither necessary nor sufficient for a work's value as art and that subordinating art to politics is a category mistake. The document should be one both could sign.

It must include a numbered list of claims both accept — about what individual artworks can causally do, about artists' intentions, about the presence of moral and political content in much of the art both sides admire, and about the cases where their judgments diverge. It must then include a disagreement table: in neutral wording, the exact points of residual disagreement, with each side's own position phrased so that that side would sign it. Finally, it must name the cruxes — the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side — and, for each crux, a concrete case or test that both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's prediction about what the test would show.

I want you to separate the contribution to the dispute that is genuinely philosophical from the part that is only about the word *propaganda*. State where a Du Boisian should concede that not all art functions as a wartime poster does, and where an autonomist should concede that the choice of subject and the refusal to choose are both politically consequential. Give exact wording, not vacuous formulas.
