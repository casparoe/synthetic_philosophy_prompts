I'm hosting a small working group on institutional trust, and two of the participants have been arguing for months about whether Scandinavian-style trust or American-style adversarial suspicion is the more reasonable default attitude toward government. They're treating these as ideal types, not national characters. One says high-trust institutions with real discretion are self-fulfilling, and that dense monitoring crowds out the civic motivation it's trying to secure. The other says trust without standing scrutiny is just unsecured credit, and that discretion quietly drifts toward capture no matter how well-intentioned the staff start out. They've agreed to try an adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement they could both sign. I'd like it to include: (1) a numbered list of claims both can endorse, stated precisely enough that neither is just nodding along to a slogan; (2) a disagreements table where each camp's position is worded in a way that camp would accept, with empirical and normative claims kept seperate; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions that would actually move at least one of them if resolved; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test or case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Don't resolve the debate; the piece works only if the residual disagreement comes out sharper rather than smaller.
