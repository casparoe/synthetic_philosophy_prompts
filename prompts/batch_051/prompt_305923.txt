I've been chewing on an argument about Dennett's two-bitser and Searle's original-vs-derived intentionality distinction, and I can't tell whether it goes through or just feels like it does. The two-bitser is the dumb little coin gizmo that goes two bits! when a US quarter drops through. Searle would put it on the derived side: whatever aboutness it has, it has only because somebody with real, original intentionality built it to respond to quarters. Fine. But then this argument started bugging me.

(1) The two-bitser goes off when, and only when, a quarter drops through. Its quarter-detecting state is about quarters.
(2) That aboutness is derived from the designers' intentions: they meant that inner state to stand for a quarter.
(3) But the designers' own contentful states — like the intention to build a quarter-detector — are themselves caused by evolution, enculturation, and training. No little homunculus sat inside them and assigned their states meanings.
(4) So the designers' content is derived from evolutionary and social processes in just the same way the gadget's content is derived from the designers: it's borrowed from something upstream that gave it a job.
(5) If that's right, there's no original intentionality anywhere. Every bit of aboutness is derived from something further up the chain, and the original/derived distinction just marks which rung of the chain you happen to be standing on.

I keep going back and forth on this. Is the argument valid? Is each premise true? I half-suspect "derived" is doing two different jobs around step (4), but I can't make it stick. If some step is the culprit, give the strongest reading of that premise on which its true, and then tell me whether the argument goes through on that reading or breaks there.
