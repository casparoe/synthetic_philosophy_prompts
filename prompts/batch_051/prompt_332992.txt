My uncle grows vegetables and sells them at a market stall. My cousin trades foreign exchange online. At a family dinner last week they got into it, and I've been going back and forth about it since. My cousin's position was that the two jobs are basically the same: both of them buy something when it's cheaper and sell it when it's more expensive, and the point in either case is to end up with more money than you started with. My uncle said that's exactly the problem - he grows actual food that people eat, whereas my cousin just moves numbers around and produces nothing. My cousin laughed and said that's just an old-fashioned superstition about "real work."

I get why my cousin says that, but I can't shake the feeling that my uncle is pointing at something real. There seems to be a difference between making money by supplying something people can use, and making money by trading money itself, even if both look like exchanges from the outside. So what I want to know is: is there a principled way to draw that distinction, or is my uncle just sentimental? If there is a distinction, what exactly does it consist in, and could my cousin's job ever be on the right side of it?
