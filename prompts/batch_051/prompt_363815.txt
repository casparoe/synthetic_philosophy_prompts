For a graduate seminar on epistemic justification, I've been asked to circulate a one-page "theoretical profile" of my commitments on the structure of reasons, and I'd like an advance audit of whether the set hangs together before I defend it in front of the group. I find myself pulled in the following directions:

(1) Justification cannot rest on an arbitrary stopping point. If a belief is justified, something must support it; a bare assumption that is simply taken as basic, with no further backing, is no better than a dogma, even if other parts of my view depend on it.

(2) I am therefore suspicious of classical foundationalism. Even fallibilist foundationalism, which allows basic beliefs to be defeasible rather than infallible, seems to me to purchase modesty at the price of arbitrariness: why should those particular beliefs get to halt the regress?

(3) I am equally suspicious of coherentism. A set of beliefs that hang together and support one another may be internally elegant, but circular support strikes me as just another way of stopping the regress, not a way of grounding it. Coherence cannot manufacture justification where there is none.

(4) What remains attractive to me is infinitism: a belief is justified when there is an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons available for it. No step is ever the final, unsupported one; the justificatory demand is never exhausted, so arbitrariness never sets in.

(5) But I also think justification is supposed to be something a believer has and can use. We are finite thinkers; we cannot survey or complete an infinite chain, and it seems strange to say a person is justified by reasons most of which she will never have entertained.

(6) I do not want to conclude that ordinary perceptual beliefs, memory beliefs, or simple inferences are unjustified. I retain the conviction that I am justified right now in believing, say, that the seminar met last Tuesday.

(7) Finally, I find it very plausible that inquiry requires default entitlements: things we are entitled to assume without evidence — for example, that our senses are roughly reliable, or that future inquiry will not make all our current methods worthless — and that these are not ordinary justified beliefs but something more like presuppositions we rationally treat as beyond question for current purposes.

I don't have a settled diagnosis. Could you go through these commitments and say which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and how bad each tension is? For each genuine tension, what would have to give, and what is the single least costly revision that would leave the overall position coherent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than saying everything is equally problematic.
