In my decision theory class we've been going over Kelly betting and I keep flip-flopping. The arguement for geometric rationality seems almost airtight for repeated bets: maximizing expected log wealth makes your bankroll grow fastest over the long run, other fixed fractions eventually fall behind, and you never go bankrupt. But I also can't shake the intuition that for a one-shot bet with positive expected value — say I get 2-to-1 on a fair coin and will never see this bet again — the rational thing is just to maximize expected value, not log, and log feels like an arbitrary curvature smuggled in. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what geometric rationality is actually claiming when it says to bet Kelly on a single, non-repeatable gamble: is it a claim about what a long-run agent would do, or about what I have reason to do right now? What do I give up if I drop EV-maximization as my default for one-shot decisions, and what do I give up if I confine Kelly to repeated multiplicative settings? Is there a reading on which both survive, or is my intuition just an ergodicity confusion — and how would I tell? Give me the strongest version of each side, then tell me which you'd give up and what would have to be true for that to be a mistake. Please write the answer in American English.
