I've been thinking about whether a person can really do something wrong by being cruel to a character in a video game, and I've come to think the usual alternatives miss the point. On one side, people say the character matters only as a stand-in for the real designer, other players, or spectators who might be affected; on the other, people treat the character as if it were a sentient creature with its own interests. I want an argument for a third view: that the failure is in what the player does to the interaction itself, not to any bearer of interests. Please argue that gratuitously tormenting a non-sentient NPC can be a genuine moral failing even when the NPC is procedurally generated, nobody else ever learns of it, and the act has no influence on the player's conduct outside the game. The argument should not appeal to the NPC's having interests, to consequences for real people, or to any corruption of real-world habits. Instead it should explain how the player, by treating the character's displayed vulnerability not as part of a rule-governed challenge to be engaged with but only as material for domination, fails at the very thing that makes it a game at all. Make the strong version of this case, and end by saying whether and in what sense it allows that one can wrong someone without there being a wronged party.
