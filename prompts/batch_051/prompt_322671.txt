I keep seeing people argue past each other about whether Dennett's 'user illusion' view of consciousness is obviously wrong or obviously right, so I actually sat down and read a long explainer article (I can't remember which, it was linked in a reddit thread). The article summarized the view like this: "Consciousness is what the brain shows itself in order to keep a massively parallel machine running smoothly — a useful simplification, the way desktop icons hide the actual machine code. All the real work is done in parallel by unconscious modules; consciousness is just the narrow-bandwidth, serialized feed that the brain's front office gets, and the self is the story that feed tells. Since this feed is an illusion, there is no fact of the matter about what it's like to be someone, and qualia are not real. In fact, anything you say about your own experience could be wrong even by your own lights, so the only way to find out what's going on in a mind is from the third-person stance." The last move feels off to me, since I thought Dennett was careful to say he dosen't deny that consciousness exists, only a certain picture of it. I also have a nagging feeling the 'user illusion' phrase isn't even his. Could you go through that summary and seperate what's fair, what's a standard pop simplification, and what a careful defender of the view would reject? In particular, does the conclusion that there's no fact of the matter about what it's like actually follow from the desktop-icon idea, or is the article sneaking that in?
