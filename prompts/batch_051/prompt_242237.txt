I have a rule for how much to trust someone's word. When they swear an oath, I ignore the swearing and ask only what actually happens to them if they break it. So a courtroom oath counts for me, because perjury has consequences; a deathbed promise to a friend counts, because the guilt would eat at them. But "I swear on my life" or "I swear on the Styx" sounds like pure theater, and I treat it as a small mark against the person's honesty. My partner says I'm being too cynical: the act of swearing can itself give someone a new reason they didn't have before, even without a penalty.
