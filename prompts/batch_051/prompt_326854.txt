Okay so I had a long argument with my friend last night about whether beauty is objective; he kept saying that everyone agrees on the vocabulary so there must be a shared standard, and I kept pointing out that the agreement evaporates once you get to actual cases. At one point I showed him this bit from David Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste":

"There are certain terms in every language, which import blame, and others praise; and all men, who use the same tongue, must agree in their application of them. Every voice is united in applauding elegance, propriety, simplicity, spirit in writing; and in blaming fustian, affectation, coldness, and a false brilliant: But when critics come to particulars, this seeming unanimity vanishes; and it is found, that they had affixed a very different meaning to their expressions."

Afterwards I realized his move is structurally the same as something I used to see in programming: an interface or type signature is like the thin layer everyone agrees on, and the implementation is where the actual behavior diverges. Two functions with the same signature can do totally different things; the fact that they both typecheck doesn't mean they're the same program. In Hume's language, "elegance" and "fustian" are the type signature, and what a given critic calls elegant or fustian is the implementation. Agreement at the type level is real but it doesn't settle anything about the particular judgments, just like agreement on a method signature doesn't settle what the code does.

My friend says this is just a cute analogy and does no work. I partly beleive him, but I don't want to decide purely on whether the analogy feels clever. Is there a genuine structural identity here, or is it just pattern-matching on the word "agreement"? If it's genuine, does anything transfer from type theory or programming-language semantics to aesthetics here—for example, would Hume's "true judges" be trying to supply something like a stronger behavioral spec or contract beyond the thin public type? And if it's not genuine, what's the specific point where the two sides come apart? What would the parallel have to predict for the transfer to work, and what would it predict that's false?
