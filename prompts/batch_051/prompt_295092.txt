A colleague who works on governance through prediction markets has taken to repeating the slogan "vote on values, bet on beliefs" as if it settled the conversation. Last week I objected that prediction markets can be manipulated by traders with deep pockets or private information, and they replied that this was precisely the point of the slogan: betting is the proper instrument for beliefs, while voting should fix the ends. I want to understand what that move could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct precise theses that "vote on values, bet on beliefs" could be expressing, ordered from near-tautological to substantive and radical, and indicate which of those readings are defensible, which are false or misleading, and which ones would actually support deflecting manipulation worries in the way my colleague did. In particular, say where the slogan presupposes a clean separation between values and beliefs, whether that separation is itself part of the claim, and how much work it is doing in each reading. Please write the answer in British English.
