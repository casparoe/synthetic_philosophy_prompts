I've just been co-opted as finance person on the committee of my local swimming club, after the last one resigned over a hole in the accounts. My first real vote is next week, and its one I'd never have noticed from outside: whether to authorise another £30,000 to finish the spectator stand extension that's already swallowed £85,000 against a budget of £60,000. The contractor got the groundworks wrong, and now if we stop we're left with a half-built stand and a lot of money gone; if we pay the extra we finish, but the stand won't bring in much extra income (no cafe, just seats) and the £30,000 would clean out this year's reserves. The diggers have been sitting behind the main building for weeks, so every member can see the mess.

I've read back through the previous committee's minutes to try to be consistent. Their calls were:

- They paid an extra £7,200 to finish resurfacing the car park after the original quote proved an underestimate, on the reasoning that stopping then would mean throwing away the £22,000 already spent.
- They refused to fund the club gala dinner for a third year after it lost money twice, with the chair saying what's gone is gone and each year has to pay its own way.
- They approved taking an employment dispute to a second round of tribunal even though the fees had already passed any likely payout, because backing down would have looked like admitting fault.
- They refused a third sealed bid for a cafe lease after losing two rounds to a rival club, even though the rival would have 'got one over on us'; the chair said the odds weren't there and the money was better set aside for something else.
- They approved a mid-season top-up for the junior swimming programme after it ran over budget, because they'd promised parents a full season and cancelling would let the kids down.

The record's all over the shop, and I can see a few principles that each fit a good chunk of it: never walk away once you've sunk money in, always compare future cost to future benefit and ignore what's spent, never look weak or admit fault in public, and never break a promise to members. But I can't tell which of those we'd actually be following if I voted one way or the other on the stand.

Concretely, how should I vote if I want the club to be consistent with its own track record? Don't just lay out the options and leave me to pick. Tell me your decision, say which old decision is the closest analog, explain which principle you're weighing most and why, and be honest about what we'd be locking in whichever way we go. Also name one further case, not too contrived, that would really separate the candidate principles if it came up later this year.
