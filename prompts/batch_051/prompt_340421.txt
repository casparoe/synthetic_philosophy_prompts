I run a small philosophy blog and want a "Which account of incorruptibility do you actually hold?" quiz. Please write it. It should have eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete vignette with two or three answer options, followed by a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto at least five views: (a) incorruptibility as strength of will, (b) as the absence of temptation, (c) as possession of a "sacred" value that cannot be traded against ordinary inducements, (d) as a structural or situational feature of offices and incentive environments rather than a trait of persons, and (e) as a normative verdict about the quality of what is refused, so that refusing the right inducements counts as incorruptible while refusing to be moved by any consideration is mere rigidity. Make sure the questions sort readers by their responses to cases rather than by which definition they prefer, and include at least one vignette in which someone's incorruptibility serves an evil cause; the scoring key should not assume that unbribability is automatically good. Flag any pair of positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate and say what further question would be needed to separate them.
