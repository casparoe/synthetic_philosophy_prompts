I've been reading adversarial-collaboration write-ups for years, and it strikes me that the debate over parental licensing is exactly the sort of dispute that would benefit from one: the two sides keep talking past each other because one reasons from the child's prospective interests and the other from institutional incentives and the risk of capture. I'd like you to write the document such a collaboration should have produced. Treat a defender of parental licensing and an opponent of parental licensing as the two parties. The defender holds that a well-ordered state should require prospective parents to complete a programme of preparation and pass a screening process before they are permitted to raise a child—not because parenting is a privilege the state grants, but because an ex ante check would protect children's interest in a minimally open future better than after-the-fact removal and rehabilitation can. The opponent holds that parental licensing is either illegitimate in principle or at least predictably harmful in practice: it would encode a contested conception of adequate parenting, invite moralistic and class-based judgement, and close more futures than it opens.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. It should contain: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, going beyond truisms and covering what both sides accept about the child's right to an open future, the harms of child maltreatment, the limits of analogies to licensing drivers or doctors, and what existing screening regimes can and cannot show; (2) a disagreements table giving, for each residual issue, the defender's own wording of the defender's view and the opponent's own wording of the opponent's view, each phrased so that the relevant side would accept it; (3) a list of cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or body of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of the disagreement is merely verbal, say which part and why. I suspect the central cruxes are whether any workable ex ante screening criteria can remain thin enough not to smuggle in a thick conception of the good life, and whether a licensing regime would in practice reduce net harm and future closure or instead be captured and misapplied; but add any further cruxes the collaboration would settle on.

Write the whole response in British English. I do not want citations, a reading list, or any dependence on naming particular authors; I want the conceptual structure itself. After the document, add a short note stating how confident you are in the formulations, and singling out the single point you are least sure about.
