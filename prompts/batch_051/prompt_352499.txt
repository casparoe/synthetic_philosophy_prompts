I've been developing an idea I call the independence budget of a climate model ensemble. The core claim is that model agreement should be weighed not by how many models or simulations agree, but by how many effectively independent model lineages the ensemble actually contains. When twenty models inherit the same convection scheme, land-surface code, or calibration target, their agreement on a projection is not twenty pieces of confirmation but one piece of evidence wearing twenty different hats. I also suspect the budget is decision-relative: two models share evidential weight only with respect to a quantity the decision actually depends on, so a projection can be robust for one decision and nearly unconfirmed for another — global mean temperature for a mitigation target versus regional precipitation for an infrastructure decision. I know this can sound like the platitude that models share assumptions, but I think the idea has teeth because it tells you how much confidence to take from a multi-model ensemble and when large model agreement should impress no one. Is this a coherent idea or a dressed-up platitude? Does it already exist in the climate-modeling or philosophy-of-science literature under another name? And where does it break — are there cases in which shared structure between models should add evidential weight rather than discount it?
