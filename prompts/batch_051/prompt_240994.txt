Having retired from a career in physical chemistry, I have lately been reading my way through the philosophy of religion, and the cosmological fine-tuning argument has become something of a private project. I am trying to catalogue the distinct moves one can make against the claim that the apparent delicacy of the physical constants — and, more controversially, of the low-entropy early universe — is evidence for a cosmic designer. My working list so far has six entries. The first is the multiverse reply: if some mechanism generates a vast number of universes with differing constants, then selection effects make it unsurprising that at least one universe is life-permitting, and we should not be surprised to find ourselves in it. The second is the bare anthropic reply, which says a life-permitting universe is the only kind we could observe, so the observation gives no evidence one way or the other even without positing a multiverse. The third is the measure problem: the improbability claim requires a probability distribution over possible values of the constants, and without a physically motivated measure the claim that our values are unlikely is not well defined. The fourth is the regress objection: a designer finely tuned enough to set the constants would itself be a highly specific, complex, low-entropy system, so the explanation threatens to relocate the puzzle rather than dissolve it. The fifth is the necessity reply: a deeper unified theory may fix the constants, in which case there is no real alternative against which the actual values can be said to be tuned. The sixth is the target-selection complaint: life is only one of many quantities one might have chosen as the target of the tuning, and selecting it after the fact weakens the inference.

I should like to add a seventh. I am looking for an objection that is genuinely distinct — not a rewording of any of these, not a special case of the anthropic or measure objections, and not a mere combination of them. In particular, if possible, I would prefer an objection that grants, for the sake of argument, that a coherent improbability statement about the constants can be made and that something does need explaining, yet still resists the move to a designer. If my six already exhaust what is standard, then say so plainly and offer the best nonstandard objection you can construct; in either case, explain exactly where it differs from my list and whether you think it succeeds.
