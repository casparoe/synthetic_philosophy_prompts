Exam question:
In Amartya Sen's Tanner Lecture "Equality of What?", Sen argues that the appropriate informational focus for egalitarian assessment is capability rather than utility or Rawlsian primary goods. He proposes attending to what people are actually able to do and be, understood as the alternative combinations of functionings available to a person. In your own words: (a) explain what Sen means by a capability set; (b) explain why he thinks neither utility nor primary goods captures the relevant dimension of advantage; and (c) show how the capability view would treat a person with a physical disability that makes it more costly for them to achieve the same functionings as others.

Student answer:
Sen argues that equality should be assessed in the space of capabilities. A capability is the set of functionings a person actually performs, such as being adequately nourished or participating in the life of the community. Sen criticizes utilitarianism because utility is a subjective mental state, so it cannot be compared between people. He criticizes Rawls because primary goods are just resources, and resources matter only insofar as they produce utility. The advantage of the capability view is that it looks at achieved well-being rather than means to well-being or feelings. In the disability case, Sen would say we should give the disabled person extra resources until their achieved functionings match those of others. Since each capability is one of the functionings a person actually performs, Sen can compare people by counting the number of functionings each person achieves.
