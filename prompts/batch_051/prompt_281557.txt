In my philosophy of law course, we have been working through how a clause should be applied when the world has changed underneath it, and I am trying to settle a case that strikes me as the Ship of Theseus run through a will. The will in question, from 1929, contains this clause:

"I give my cutter, the Storm Petrel, to the Harbour Trust, upon trust that the Storm Petrel herself shall be kept in seaworthy condition, maintained on public view, and put under sail around the harbour once each year."

The Storm Petrel was built in 1875. In the Trust's care, she has never been declared a total loss or taken out of commission, but keeping a wooden cutter seaworthy in salt water has required continuous restoration. One by one, over the decades, every original part has been replaced—hull planks, keel, decks, spars, and fittings—each replacement made during an ordinary refit and following the original lines. By last year, no material that formed part of the 1929 vessel remained, though the Trust's records trace an unbroken chain of custody and maintenance, and the annual harbour sail has occurred every year.

A great-niece of the testator now argues that the Trust has breached the condition: on her reading, the clause requires the very vessel to remain, and what the Trust now sails is a replica assembled from new materials, so the gift should revert to the family. The Trust replies that a ship is not a heap of planks but a functioning craft whose identity survives gradual repair; that the clause's own seaworthiness requirement made exactly this restoration obligatory; and that a reading under which compliance with one part of the clause destroys the object the other part protects is self-defeating. The will says nothing about replacement.

I would like you to work out what the clause requires on each of the standard approaches—the plain text, the purpose of the condition, what the testator would have said if asked in 1929 about gradual total replacement, and what a reasonable reader would take the clause to mean—and then tell me which approach should control and why.
