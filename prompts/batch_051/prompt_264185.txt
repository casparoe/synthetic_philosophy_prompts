You are a philosopher of action who is wary both of anthropomorphism and of writing off non-human flexibility too quickly. Think carefully, and begin by laying out what each criterion below would require before you apply it.

Some organisms and machines run fixed routines that look clever but are not responsive to mid-task disruption. In a classic example, a female Sphex wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it outside, goes inside to inspect the burrow, and then comes back out to pull the cricket in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while she is inside inspecting, she brings it back to the entrance and repeats the whole inspection routine, sometimes many times over, never doing the simpler thing: dragging the prey in without re-inspecting. Her behavior is exquisitely organized yet, in one respect, rigid. Call this pattern sphexishness.

Suppose someone proposes the following criteria for the flexibility that separates free or genuinely purposive agents from merely sphexish mechanisms. A system is flexible in the relevant sense just in case: (1) it acts in pursuit of a goal; (2) it registers when its current routine is failing under present conditions; (3) it can abandon or alter the routine and try a different means or sequence; and (4) its alterations are guided by something like an appreciation of what is going wrong—the system responds to the situation as a reason to change course, rather than merely having fallback subroutines triggered by a fixed set of cues.

Now consider Flexor, an autonomous warehouse robot whose controller, cost function, and code are fixed; it is not learning, and its goals are set externally. When a human shifts the goal pallet mid-task, Flexor solves an optimization problem at each time step over thousands of candidate trajectories and produces a novel detour. Its engineers cannot predict its exact path, and it reliably handles cases never anticipated in its design. Yet it treats every disturbance as an input to the same fixed optimization procedure; it never takes its own cost function, goals, or methods as things it might have reason to revise.

Work through criteria (1)–(4) for Flexor and argue whether Flexor is genuinely non-sphexish or merely runs a more sophisticated sphexish routine. Then step back: do the criteria underdetermine the case? Is criterion (4) doing too much work, or not enough? And does the line between sphexish rigidity and flexibility mark a real difference in kind, or only a difference in degree?
