Consider the following counterfactual in the political philosophy of church and state.

Suppose that on 1 January 2028 the Federal Republic of Germany discontinues the state-collected church tax (Kirchensteuer) altogether. From that date the state ceases to record religious affiliation for tax purposes, ceases to withhold the surcharge from salaries or add it to income-tax assessments, and ceases to remit the proceeds to the religious bodies. The churches and other religious bodies keep their status as public-law corporations (Körperschaften des öffentlichen Rechts), their property, their role as employers, and their established position in schools, hospitals, and social-welfare provision. They must now finance themselves through voluntary contributions, which remain deductible under the ordinary rules for charitable giving. Nothing else in the arrangement changes.

Give a reasoned prediction of how the German religious landscape and the church–state settlement look in 2048. Focus on mechanisms: how the change alters the incentives to enter, remain in, or formally leave a religious body; how it affects the finances and institutional capacity of the large churches and of smaller bodies that never had the tax; how it reshapes church-run welfare and education and the state's relation to them; and how it changes the political weight and constitutional significance of public-law religious bodies. Distinguish effects by sector and by type of body where that matters. State explicitly which effects you regard as robust, which are conjectural, and what would have to hold for each major prediction to be wrong. Where comparison helps, draw on the United States (voluntary tax-deductible giving without state collection), France (strong separation), and England (an established church without a state-collected membership tax), but say explicitly what does and does not transfer from each case.
