I am a novelist, and I want to use a real publishing episode to pressure-test the debate over authorial intention and the meaning of a text. The episode is the 2015 release of Harper Lee's Go Set a Watchman. The salient facts as I understand them: the book was marketed as a newly discovered novel, but it was in fact an early draft of To Kill a Mockingbird, written before that book; it presents an adult Jean Louise returning to Maycomb and an Atticus who expresses segregationist views; and its release was accompanied by public doubt about whether Lee, then elderly and in declining health, had authorized publication. I want a case analysis that runs in both directions. Map the episode onto the main positions in the debate — actual intentionalism, which fixes meaning by what the author intended; anti-intentionalism, which fixes meaning by the text itself; and hypothetical intentionalism, which fixes meaning by what an appropriately informed reader would take the author to intend. For each position, say what it treats as the work to be interpreted, whose intentions or what textual features fix the meaning of its Atticus, and whether the draft's abandoned and possibly unauthorized status should affect interpretation. Then say where each position handles the episode well and where each is forced into something implausible, and finish by telling me what the episode reveals about the theory — in particular, whether the usual categories of author, text, and reader are sufficient here, or whether something like an authorized work is doing unacknowledged interpretive work.
