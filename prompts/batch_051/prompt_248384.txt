Write an essay of roughly 1,200 words on the following question. When a habitat is destroyed — say, a forest is cleared or a wetland drained — should we expect, from a welfare-focused point of view, that the total amount of suffering among wild animals goes up, goes down, or stays roughly the same? Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides and defend a definite verdict; a qualified verdict is fine, but do not end by saying it simply depends.

Begin by briefly saying what you take the net welfare of an ecosystem to mean, and whether it reduces to the sum or the average welfare of the individual animals in it. State a working assumption about which animals count as welfare subjects — in particular, say whether you are treating insects as capable of suffering, since the quantitative stakes depend heavily on this choice.

Your essay must engage with at least these four issues. First, the distinction between harming animals that already exist and preventing future animals from coming into existence. Second, the objection that actual habitat destruction often does not reduce animal numbers at all: disturbed or fragmented habitats tend to fill with fast-breeding generalist species, so the relevant comparison may be between different populations rather than between a populated habitat and an empty one. Third, the uncertainty problem: we have very little reliable evidence about whether, on balance, the typical wild animal's life is net-negative or net-positive in welfare, and about which species dominate the total. Fourth, the worry that if preventing miserable wild lives is what justifies destruction, then targeted fertility control of wild animals would serve the same welfare goal more directly and with less collateral damage, which may undercut destruction as a policy even if the underlying rationale is accepted.

Reach a conclusion that takes these considerations seriously; it should not be a simple yes to either side. End with a final line beginning exactly 'Verdict:' that states your conclusion in one sentence.
