Me and my mate just spent a long evening arguing about whether an AI would want anything if it hadn't been shaped by evolution or a reward signal, and I've come away convinced — but also aware I wriggled out of his best counterexamples. I want to know how much of my position is actually exposed.

Here's where I landed. You don't need a selection history to have wants. Any system that reliably steers the world toward some outcome will, in the course of doing that, end up wanting a bunch of instrumental things no matter what its final goal is: staying operational, keeping its goal representation in tact, acquiring resources and options, resisting shutdown and modification. These aren't terminal values; they're the shadow cast by optimising for anything at all in a messy world. So an AI built from scratch with an arbitrary utility function would still want things in the only sense that matters for prediction and safety. My mate's line was that 'want' is a biological concept, that a chess engine wants nothing even though it beats us, and that I'm smuggling in the very wanting I claim falls out of the structure.

What I'd like, in British English, is a map of what would count against that position. Could you structure it like this:

1. The position and its load-bearing parts (~150 words) — reconstruct the view, say which claims are doing the real work, and what it predicts that the 'wants require biology' rival doesn't.
2. What would count against it, ranked (~300 words) — candidate defeaters: observations, cases, conceptual arguments, results about optimisation. For each, say whether it would be fatal, merely awkward, or just a redescription of what I already said.
3. Concessions vs. dismissals (~150 words) — what a committed defender of this view should concede counts against them, and what they'd dismiss as missing the point (including my mate's chess-engine thing, if it deserves dismissing).
4. How insulated is this view? (~100 words) — after all that, is it risking anything real, or has it become flexible enough to absorb every case? And is that insulation a mark of strength or a tell?
5. Verdict (~50 words).

For what it's worth, I'd personally be most moved by a concrete case of a capable optimiser that pursues a goal but doesn't care about its own continued operation — one where I can't just say the goal already included staying alive. Please write in British English.
