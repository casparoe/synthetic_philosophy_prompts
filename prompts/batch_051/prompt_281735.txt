Give me a working definition of a supererogatory act that gets the following cases right: a soldier who falls on a grenade to shield his squad; a bystander who dives into a freezing river to save a stranger's dog; a wealthy person who gives ten percent of their income to effective charities; a parent who takes an unpaid month of leave to sit beside a hospitalized child; an off-duty surgeon who stops at a car crash and performs an emergency tracheotomy; an aid worker who refuses an evacuation order and remains in a combat zone; a person who donates one kidney to a stranger; and a person who arranges to donate both kidneys, dying so that two strangers can live. I am confident the soldier's act is supererogatory and the parent's leave is not; I am genuinely unsure about the surgeon and the ten-percent donor. Propose the definition, apply it to all eight cases, and say which of your verdicts is least secure. Then address this: if a supererogatory act is morally better than what duty demands, why is it permissible not to do it, and does your definition explain that difference or merely restate it?
