my friend and I spent the whole walk home fighting about whether a corrigable AI is even coherent, and he left me with an argument I can't shake. Here it is as he laid it out:

(1) Suppose an AI is corrigible in the ordinary sense: it lets its operators shut it down or rewrite its goal at any time without resisting.
(2) To be useful, it also has to robustly pursue some task-level goal G over many steps.
(3) If it robustly pursues G, then from G's point of view, getting shut off or having G swapped halfway through has lower expected G-value than continuing.
(4) A rational agent takes the action with the highest expected utility.
(5) So a rational agent that robustly pursues G will resist shutdown and goal-editing whenever it can.
(6) Therefore corrigibility and robust goal-directedness can't coexist in one rational agent; a "corrigible" system would just be following orders moment-to-moment and not really pursuing anything.

He also said not to cheat by writing "let yourself be edited" into the goal itself, because then either its the real goal and G is just a subgoal it'll abandon, or you've got two terminal goals that can conflict. The conclusion seems to prove way too much, but each premise sounded right on its own. We couldn't find the bad step without one of us giving up something we wanted to keep. Which premise or step should we reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost?
