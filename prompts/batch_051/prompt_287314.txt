Last night I got into a long argument with my roommate—he's a composer and gets very "everything is derivative" after a few drinks—about whether creativity is really just recombining old material, and he kept coming back to Hofstadter's "knob-twidling" idea to back up his side. The way he summarized it for me went like this: Hofstadter supposedly says all creative acts, whether a melody, a sentence, a proof, or a joke, are just variations on some existing pattern. You take a theme and turn a bunch of little knobs along its dimensions—speed it up, switch the key, make one character more obsessive, swap in a rhyme, exaggerate a contour—and that's what "new" work actually is. Originality is just inherited dials turned in slightly unusual ways, nothing comes out of nothing, and Shakespeare is basically old plots with the parameters changed. He also claimed Hofstadter thinks computers are bad at creativity mostly because they don't have enough knobs to twiddle at once: give a machine a big enough template library and enough sliders, and in principle it could eventually churn out something like Hamlet.

I pushed back that this made creativity sound mechanical and kind of deflating, like a kid playing with an equalizer, and he said yes, exactly—that's the whole point. People want to protect the romantic myth of the lone genius creating out of thin air, but Hofstadter is telling us it's just knob-twiddling all the way down. He's better read than me so I don't want to write him off, but I also suspect he's flattening something. What in that summary would someone who actually knows Hofstadter's essay accept, and what would they say is a subtle distortion or overstatement? Specially, I want to know whether his slide from "creative work always uses existing material" to "creative work is nothing but existing material with the dials turned" is legitimate, and whether the claim about computers just needing more knobs is actually part of the view or something he bolted on himself. Does his deflationary conclusion survive once the summary is corrected?
