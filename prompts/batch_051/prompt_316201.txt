I am drawn to the No-Free-Lunch theorems because they seem to expose a deep problem about induction. If every learning rule's advantage on some possible worlds is exactly cancelled by its disadvantage on others, then, averaged over all possible worlds, no rule of generalisation is better than any other, and induction's superiority over anti-induction can come only from the prior one places on the worlds. Yet I also hold the firm conviction that induction is not merely one bias among many: a learner who expects observed regularities to continue is tracking something that a learner who expects them to reverse is missing, and its success is not just a matter of suiting our interests or our corner of modal space. Does the theorem actually contradict that conviction, or only a philosophical gloss on it? I want the strongest statement of each move: what would justify debunking the intuition as adaptive prejudice, what would justify dismissing the theorem's bearing on induction as an artefact of an impossible uniform averaging, and whether there is a reading on which both can be kept.
