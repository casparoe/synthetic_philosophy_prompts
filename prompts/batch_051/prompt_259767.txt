I started fact-checking the "Animal Facts" trivia cards for a kids app a few weeks ago, and the style guide is tying me in knots. The rule says a card can be a bare generalization like "Ducks lay eggs" as long as it's "true as a rule", with exceptions left implicit. Fair enough. But the same editor who lets "Ducks lay eggs" through will cut "Ducks are female" as false, even though something like half of all ducks are female while only some ducks (the hens, in season) ever lay an egg. So the sentence that's true of fewer ducks gets published and the sentence that's true of more ducks gets pulled. That seems like an asymmetry worth either explaining or admitting is just vibes.

There's a second one. We print "Mosquitoes carry malaria" even though only a tiny minority of actual mosquitoes carry the parasite, but we'd never print "Books are paperbacks" even though a huge share of books are paperbacks. So a rare but scary property passes and a common but boring property fails.

I can almost feel a rule hiding in there, but I dont trust my gut. I'm the one who has to sign off on the next card drop, so I need something I can defend when the editor asks. I'd like the reply as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `asymmetry`: a one-paragraph restatement of the puzzle, covering both cases.
- `best_rule`: your best attempt at a single general principle that predicts which bare generics get printed and which get cut.
- `counterexamples`: an array of 2 to 4 cases that strain your `best_rule`, each an object with `sentence` and `problem` (the problem in one sentence).
- `rulings`: an array of objects, one per sentence on my test list, each with `sentence`, `verdict` (one of "print", "rewrite", or "cut"), and `reason` (one sentence applying the rule).
- `bottom_line`: one sentence on whether the asymmetry should be kept as a defensible rule, revised, or abandoned as a quirk.

Here's the test list: "Ducks lay eggs", "Ducks are female", "Ducks have webbed feet", "Lions have manes", "Lions are male", "Kangaroos have pouches", "Kangaroos are female", "Humans are right-handed", "Humans are female", "Mosquitoes carry malaria", "Mosquitoes are infected with malaria", "Books are paperbacks", "Tigers have stripes", "Spiders have eight legs".
