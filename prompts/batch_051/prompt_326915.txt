I am drafting the research agenda for a small program at the intersection of AI governance and technical safety, and I need to decide which open question about the alignment tax and the competitiveness constraint deserves a two-year, two-person project. By alignment tax I mean the extra cost of producing a system that is safe and aligned rather than merely capable; by competitiveness constraint I mean the pressure on labs not to spend more on safety than their rivals do. The candidate projects are: (a) whether the tax is best measured at the frontier margin, as the capability loss from safety interventions on the most advanced systems, or as an industry-level fixed cost of safety processes, auditing, and slower release norms; (b) whether competitive pressure on safety is best modeled as a strategic game among a few labs or as a selection effect over a larger population of labs, funders, and teams with heterogeneous willingness to pay; (c) whether the binding constraint is that even safety-conscious labs lack the technique to align the most capable systems, or that the techniques exist but the market will not pay for them; (d) whether the tax should be expected to grow or shrink with scale; and (e) how much of the competitiveness story depends on safety effort and capability progress being substitutes rather than complements at the margin. Rank these by how much settling them would change what labs or regulators should do, and defend both the ordering and the criteria behind it. I want a dependency-aware judgment: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable enough to resolve within the project's horizon, and which should be deprioritized because their answers would alter little downstream; please also tell me what empirical finding would most change your ranking.
