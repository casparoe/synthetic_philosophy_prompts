I do backend work for a payments company, but I've been volunteering with the local historical society, helping scan and index a collection they basically inherited when the town hall basement flooded. It's got parish registers from the 1800s, letters, minutes from a union local during a strike in the 30s, and a few taped interviews with people who were there. The board's operating principle is pretty simple: digitize everything and make it as open as possible, because a gap in the archive is a failure of memory and its not our job to decide what people are allowed to know. As an engineer I'm kind of drawn to that—it treats a body of material like a dataset where missing values are just bugs, and telling people what they can't see feels paternalistic, like I'm overridding the user. But while scanning I keep having a reaction I can't quite justify. Some of these items feel like they were never meant to become permanently searchable: a woman describing in a taped interview something she plainly thought would never leave that room; a ledger that seems to have been used to keep track of which families were "from somewhere else"; letters where people are airing old community disputes. And at the same time, I notice the collection is already massively skewed—mostly the people who could write, the official minutes, the winners' notes—so "the complete archive" is kind of a myth to begin with. The board's reply is that we should preserve what survived and not reproduce the old gatekeeping by adding our own. My gut says a gap isn't always damage: sometimes the silence is part of what happened, and sometimes the silence is doing someone a favor.

So I'm stuck between two things I both believe. On one side, the principle: an archive should be as complete, open, and neutral as possible, and any selection is just somebody's power sneaking back in through the back door. On the other side, the intuition: some absences should be left alone, and "record everything and publish it" is itself an exercise of power, especially when the original records were made under unequal conditions. I don't have any training in this stuff, so maybe this is a solved problem, but I'd want both sides stated as strong as they can be, and then a considered judgment about which should yield: should I debunk the intuition as a technologist's over-caution (and maybe its own subtle paternalism), should the completeness principle be amended, or is there a reading of "open archive" where both can survive? And if the principle should bend, I want to know what actually justifies drawing the line in a given case, not just "it depends."
