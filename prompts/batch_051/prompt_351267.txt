So my school's astronomy club has basically become a Fermi paradox club, and after a year of arguing about the Great Filter we've split into two camps. The "behind us" camp says the nearly impossible step was early: somewhere between plain non-living chemistry and a self-replicating cell, or maybe between simple bacteria-like cells and the more complex cells that eventually made animals possible. The "ahead of us" camp says simple life is probably all over the place and the real choke point is later — that either tool-using intelligence is a freak accident or that technological civilizations burn out or never expand, which would mean the silence out there is mostly evidence that we're in trouble too.

We've been going in circles for months, so I want the thing we should have written instead of re-litigating the same points. Can you draft a joint adversarial-collaboration statement that both camps could actually sign? It should have: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept about the step ladder from chemistry to a galaxy-spanning civilization and about what our one example of life and our null result do and don't tell us; (2) the exact disagreements, worded neutrally so each side can sign off on how its own view is stated; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions that would actually of moved at least one side if we could answer them — and for each crux, a concrete observation or kind of evidence both camps agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction filed in advance. Don't pick a winner. I just want the disagreement made exact enough that we'd know what evidence would settle it.
