I run a staged-interview series for a philosophy department, where a living host plays devil's advocate against a dead thinker and tries to crack their position. Next one is Simone Weil on political parties. Write me ten questions and follow-ups for that episode. The questions have to bite into the machinery of her view, not summarize it: her claim that a party is already totalitarian in embryo just by being a party, that its real end is its own growth and survival and not truth, and that party membership substitutes collective mechanical opinion for individual judgment. Cover at least these pressure points: (1) does the critique really apply only to parties, or does it swallow trade unions, churches, civic associations—any organized group? (2) is she making a conceptual claim (no party can put truth first without ceasing to be a party) or an empirical one, and what would she count as a genuine counterexample? (3) her positive proposal—politics after parties are abolished—still needs candidates, campaigns, and legislative majorities; ask whether the party just gets smuggled back in as an 'association' or 'coalition.' (4) from the voter's side, parties are a cheap coordination device, a bundle of policies, a brand you can hold accountable—what replaces that? (5) as a seperate question, ask whether her later ethics of attention and affliction connects to the party essay, or whether that's just a retrospective reading. For each question include a follow-up that assumes she gives the reply defenders of her usually give, then pushes one step further. Number the questions 1–10, put each follow-up on the next line starting 'If she says...' Keep the whole response under 900 words.
