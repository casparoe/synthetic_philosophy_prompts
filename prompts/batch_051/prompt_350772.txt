I have two convictions about mandatory arbitration that I cannot yet reconcile. The first is that consent should matter: if two parties may settle a dispute any way they like, then they should also be allowed to agree in advance to use a private arbitrator rather than a public court, and a rule forbidding that would be paternalism dressed up as consumer protection. The second is that a corporation which buries an arbitration clause in a take-it-or-leave-it form contract has not really secured anyone's consent; it has routed a mass of people into a confidential, privately funded process that looks disturbingly like a purchased substitute for justice. I am not asking whether arbitration clauses are good or bad on balance. I am asking which of these convictions should yield: what it would cost to give up the consent principle, what it would cost to give up the intuition, and whether there is a version of mandatory arbitration under which both survive. Behind this is a further question I keep circling: is the problem simply that consent in these contracts is defective, or is it that adjudication is a public function that cannot be privatized even by genuine consent?
