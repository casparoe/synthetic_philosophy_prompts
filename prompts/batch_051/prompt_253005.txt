Over the past two decades the exchange over the combination problem in the philosophy of panpsychism has seemed to me to be stalled, and I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a verdict. The critics of constitutive micropsychism argue that no account has been given of how many micro-subjects could constitute a single unified human subject: adding subjects together, however elaborately, appears to yield a collection of subjects rather than one subject, and physical part–whole composition does not obviously carry over to phenomenal unity. Panpsychists reply that this demand is set too high, since physicalism faces a parallel binding problem and every theory of consciousness leaves some explanatory gap; some propose primitive phenomenal bonding relations, while others switch to emergent or cosmopsychist forms of panpsychism precisely to avoid the problem. Both sides appear to accept the same facts about introspection and microphysics, which is why the persistence of the disagreement puzzles me. My hypothesis is that the real disagreement is methodological—about what counts as an acceptable explanation of the existence of a macro-subject—rather than straightforwardly factual. What kind of disagreement is this, really: a clash of standards of explanation, a substantive difference about whether macro-subjects need a constitutive ground, an equivocation in the word "combination" among composition, fusion, and co-consciousness, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress, or should none be expected? Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
