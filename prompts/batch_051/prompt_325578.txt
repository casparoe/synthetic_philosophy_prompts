i've got a way of reading old math and science that i'm starting to suspect is too easy. whenever a text says something weird I just find the closest modern concept, translate it, and then ask whether they got the modern concept right. so when Aristotle talks about heavy things having a natural place, I read it as gravity and grade him on whether he got gravity. when the presocratics talk about uncuttable bits, I read them as atoms. and when a greek math proof works with ratios of magnitudes, I rewrite it as algebra with fractions and check if the algebra works. this gives me fast answers about who was ahead of their time and who was wrong. but my teacher says this is presentism and that sometimes the old concept wasnt pointing at my modern target at all. is my shortcut defensible? under what conditions is translating old science or math into modern terms okay, and when does it just change the subject? if I wanted to keep the speed but not the distortion, how should I restate the policy? and is there a way to tell from inside when im doing it badly, because it always feels like the same operation to me.
