Last night a friend and I argued for hours about a decision I have been putting off, and the dispute kept circling back to the inside view versus the outside view. By the end I was persuaded that epistemic modesty means taking the outside view: one should begin with the base rate for cases of the same kind and treat the feeling that one's own case is special as a bias to be discounted. But when I sat down to do this, I found that the outside view does not by itself specify the comparison class. Whether I take "cases of the same kind" to be all people with my background, all people attempting this specific sort of project, or only those in a narrower situation I regard as relevant will yield very different base rates. The choice among these descriptions seems to require exactly the first-person judgment about which features of my situation matter that the outside view was meant to keep from doing the work. So my question is this: how should one choose the reference class for an outside-view estimate without letting the inside view in through the back door? And if no such choice can be made from a genuinely outside standpoint, what makes the outside view the epistemically modest option here, rather than simply a delayed expression of the inside judgment it was supposed to correct?
