i keep getting pulled toward the view that there's just no fact of the matter about comparing how bad two different people feel. The technical version is almost embarrassingly simple: my utility numbers are only pinned down up to positive affine transformations — you can rescale the zero point and the unit however you want and the numbers still represent exactly the same preferences — so my "8" and your "8" are readings on two rulers with no common origin. Any interpersonal comparison has to smuggle in an extra assumption, and its hard to see what fact in the world that assumption could be answerable to. It's clean and it explains why all our cross-person comparisons feel so floaty. But then the person next to me is mid-migraine and I know, as solidly as I know anything, that she's worse off than I am with my mild headache. A moral theory that can't say that seems to have missed the thing morality is for.

What I want is not for you to pick a side and move on. Give me the strongest case that the intuition is the thing I should abandon — what's the best debunking story for why comparisons feel so compelling even if they're not truth-tracking, and does that story leave any usable residue. Then the strongest case that the theory is the thing I should abandon — what exactly has to be true for "A is worse off than B" to be a real claim, and what sort of evidence would count for it. Then tell me whether there's a reading on which both survive, something like: comparisons are commitments we make for doing ethics together rather than measurements we take of hidden magnitudes — and if so, whether that reading does real work or just renames the problem. Give me your actual verdict on which move you'd make. And tell me how confident you are in that verdict, and single out the point in the argument you're least sure of.
