Write an essay on whether the martial virtues — physical courage, valour, toughness in the face of death or serious injury — have any real place in a peaceful, liberal society, or whether they are just leftovers from a way of life we are right to have left behind. The essay should weigh the arguments on both sides. On one side: these virtues seem to track something genuinely admirable about how a person faces fear and risk, and a society that lets them wither may be losing something important. On the other side: their value seems tied to the needs and self-image of a warrior culture, so wanting them back in peacetime might be a bit like missing the virtues of a good slave-owner. Reach a conclusion and justify it; don't end on a fence. A good answer will probably need to seperate physical courage from moral courage, and to take some view about whether courage and valour are one underlying trait or just a family of traits that happen to be elicited by danger. It should also say whether valour can survive being detached from actual war without collapsing into something like extreme-sports risk-taking or mere grim endurance.
