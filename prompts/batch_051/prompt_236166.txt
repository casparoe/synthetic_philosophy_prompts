Posting this here on the off chance someone here writes verse: I'm putting together a zine for a philosophy of time reading group (mixed audience: a few physics grad students plus the usual blog-reading autodidacts), and I want to close it with a villanelle about Gödel's 1949 rotating universe. Here's what the poem has to get right, and I want it accurate enough that a philosopher of physics wouldn't wince: Gödel found an exact solution to Einstein's field equations in which the whole universe rotates, and far enough from the rotation axis the light cones tip over, so a timelike path can loop back into its own past. His upshot was that in such a universe there's no objective global lapse of time and no one correct set of simultaneous nows; becoming is at most local, and a privledged present is exactly what has to go. I want the villanelle's form to do that work: the two refrains should be the closed timelike curves, so the reader comes back to the same line and it means something slightly different each time because the preceding stanzas rotated the context. One refrain should carry the tipping cones and the looping worldline; the other should carry the unreal now. Don't versify a lecture, and no time-travel clichés (nothing about entropy running backwards). Please start the response with the poem itself, no preface or commentary; if you need to add notes, put them after the poem, but the first line I read should be the first line of the poem.
