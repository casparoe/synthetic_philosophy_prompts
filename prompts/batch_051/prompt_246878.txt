Right, here's what I want. I'd like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing for years about whether religion makes people better and whether secular societies can keep the trust and decency that (maybe) religion used to supply. Call the two positions the social-infrastructure view and the functional-equivalence view.

The social-infrastructure view: participation in an organised religious community does real causal work in making people more honest, cooperative and trusting of strangers — through supernatural monitoring, costly commitment, moral community, ritual, accountability — and these are mechanisms that secular institutions only partially copy. They think long-run secularisation may be drawing down moral capital unless something is deliberately built to replace it.

The functional-equivalence view: the apparent prosocial effects of religion are mostly selection (already-cooperative people join and stay), confounding (religion tracks age, community involvement, social control), or effects of generic social infrastructure that secular institutions can supply just as well. They think highly secular societies can be just as trusting and decent if their legal, educational and civic institutions are strong, and that Scandinavia is a decent existence proof.

Neither side is allowed to be a strawman. Both accept that religious attendance correlates with things like volunteering and charitable giving; both accept that correlation isn't causation; both accept that "more moral" needs unpacking before the arguement can even start.

Now the deliverable. Please write the document itself, in this format:

1. Agreed claims (about 400 words). A numbered list of 8–12 substantive claims both sides can sign. These shouldn't be vacuous ("the question is hard") — they should pin down the descriptive facts both accept, the distinctions they agree matter (belief vs participation, in-group vs stranger, self-report vs costly behaviour), and the standards of evidence they'll both respect.

2. The disagreement, stated exactly (about 250 words). Write each side's position in the first person, in wording its own side would accept as neither slanted nor softened. Then say in one sentance where the deepest fault line is.

3. Crux table (about 350 words). A table with columns: crux / social-infrastructure prediction / functional-equivalence prediction / evidence both accept would bear on it. I want 4 or 5 cruxes, and they should be genuinely load-bearing. Make at least one about selection vs causation within persons over time, one about whether a demanding secular group can fully substitute for a congregation, and one about the high-trust secular countries. For each row, both predictions have to be written down in advance, so that a result could embarrass at least one side.

4. What would move each side (about 150 words). Each side says, in advance and in its own voice, what finding or case would make it revise its view — not "nothing would".

5. Is any of it verbal? (about 100 words). Tell me which parts of the dispute are really about the meaning of "more moral" or "religion" rather than about the world, and which parts survive once the words are fixed.

Don't give me a summary of what such a document would say; write the actual document. And please don't invent citations or named studies — I only want reasoning and clearly-labelled types of evidence, since I know you can't look anything up.
