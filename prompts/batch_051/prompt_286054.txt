I've been going in circles on the time's-arrow debates — the one that's been running from Boltzmann down to the modern past-hypothesis people without anyone really conceding — and I'd kill for a diagnosis of the impasse rather than another survey of positions. The setup everyone seems to share is this: the underlying microphysical laws look time-symmetric, yet entropy rises toward the future everywhere in our experience. The fight is over what, if anything, explains that asymmetry.

One camp says it's statistics plus boundary conditions. From any macrostate below equilibrium, there are overwhelmingly many more microstates that lead to higher entropy later than lower, so increase is just an overwhelming probability — but only once you stipulate that the universe began in an absurdly low-entropy macrostate. People call that stipulation the past hypothesis, and it does the real work: without it, the same statistical reasoning run backwards says entropy should also have been higher in the past, which is flatly not what records and memory show.

The other camp says this isnt an explanation, just a relabeling. Pointing at a special initial condition, they say, leaves the puzzle exactly where it was — why was the past like that? A real solution would either show that typical cosmological histories start low (some typicality or dynamical story), or show that the question is ill-posed: maybe the entropy of the past is an artifact of how we coarse-grain and which variables we conditionalize on, not something out there waiting for an explanation.

Then there's the older Boltzmann-fluctuation idea, mostly treated as a nonstarter now because it makes us absurdly atypical observers, but it hangs around as a sign of how hard it is to avoid specialness somewhere.

My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about physics at all. It's about what an explanation is supposed to do — whether a statistical or typicality account can explain an asymmetry, or whether explaining requires deriving it from a time-asymmetric mechanism — and about whether the psychological arrow (we remember the past, not the future) is part of the data a theory has to explain or just another symptom to be dissolved. But I may be wrong: there could be a genuine empirical question about the early universe, or a hidden formal question about whether any time-symmetric dynamics plus a special boundary condition can ever count as explaining an asymmetry. I want your diagnosis, with reasons, of what kind of disagreement this really is. Then tell me what would count as progress given the diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, a cosmological observation, a better-posed question — or whether we should expect none because the sides are essentially disputing taste. And be honest about how confident you are in the diagnosis, and which piece of it you're least sure of.
