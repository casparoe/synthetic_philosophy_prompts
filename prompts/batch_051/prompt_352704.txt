I'm drafting the opening chapter of a longform essay on the paradox of voting — the turnout puzzle specifically: the chance any single vote is decisive in a mass election is minuscule, so on standard expected-utility grounds voting looks irrational, and yet turnout is persistently high. I've got maybe a semester of time and would rather not get dragged into formal modeling I cant pull off, so I want a defended agenda, not a survey.

Here are the questions I keep circling. Tell me which three or four are actually worth the semester, in what order, and which are rabbit wholes. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria — importance, tractability for someone without serious game-theory chops, neglectedness, how much else depends on the answer — and say what would make you reshuffle the ranking.

- whether the paradox just dissolves once you allow a taste for civic duty or doing your part (the old D-term repair), or whether that's overfitting the model to the very behavior it was meant to explain;
- the altruistic/ethical voting line: a tiny pivot probability multiplied by the welfare of everyone affected by the result can make one vote a decent low-cost gamble, even if the self-interested payoff is roughly zero;
- whether the paradox even gets off the ground for rule-rational or Kantian voters, who ask what would happen if everyone like them stayed home;
- what the paradox implies for compulsory voting — is low turnout a policy failure, or the predictable result of people being roughly rational about an act that's instrumentally almost pointless;
- expressive accounts, where a vote is more like speech or identity-display than a tool for changing outcomes, and whether they explain why turnout varies so much across offices and elections;
- whether near-zero decisiveness is the wrong baseline because people vote in nested, repeated elections and for parties over decades, not in isolated one-off referenda;
- whether voters' own estimates of their chance of being decisive — whatever they turn out to be — undercut or strengthen the paradox's normative force.

Some of these are probably mergeable and some are dead ends, so I'd rather you throw one out than be polite about it.
