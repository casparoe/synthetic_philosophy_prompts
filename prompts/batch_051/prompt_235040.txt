Ok here's the tension I've been stuck on for weeks. I find the Advaita Vedanta line genuinely compelling when I actually run the argument. You can't be an object of your own experience: anything you notice is something you're aware of, so it can't be the thing doing the noticing. The I that I take myself to be — this body, these thoughts, this biography — is all contents of awareness, so the real self has to be the awareness in which all that shows up, a kind of witness that is never itself an object. Then the three states seem to confirm it. In waking there's the subject-object split; in dreaming the mind generates a whole apparent world; in deep sleep there's no object at all but (they say) awareness is still there, just objectless. If that's right, the subject-object structure of the world is something superimposed on a single undivided awareness, and māyā is just the name for why it looks otherwise. That gives a clean, simple picture: the individual self and Brahman aren't two things, the boundaries between persons are appearances not ultimate facts, and it even seems to dissolve the mind-body problem rather than solve it.

But I also have a completely firm intuition that I am this particular, bounded, embodied center of experience, and that the world is not an appearance. When I stub my toe the pain is mine and it's real; other people are not the same one awareness wearing different masks; and the claim that I am Brahman feels like something I can say but not something I can believe in my bones.

So which side should give? Specifically: can Advaita debunk my separate-self intuition without circularity, given that the intuition is exactly the thing māyā is invoked to explain? Or does it have to grant that the seperate self is real at the conventional level and unreal only from the ultimate standpoint — and is that a genuine compatibility or just using real two different ways? Give me the strongest version of the Advaita argument and the strongest version of the separate-self intuition, then a negotiated verdict: what would justify dropping the intuition, amending the theory, or treating them as compatible.
