Give me a working definition of an 'absurd result' in statutory interpretation — the defect that licenses a court to depart from a statute's plain text under the absurdity doctrine — that gets the following seven cases right. I'm confident that (1) and (2) are absurd in the relevant sense, that (7) is not, and I have no settled view about the rest. The hard part is drawing the line between a result the legislature could not have meant and one it probably did mean but that a judge finds harsh or unwise; your definition should draw that line rather than blur it.

(1) A federal statute makes it a crime to obstruct the mail. A county sheriff, acting on a warrant charging murder, arrests a mail carrier while the carrier is on his delivery route.

(2) A probate code provides that a testator's property passes to the beneficiary named in a valid will. A grandson murders his grandfather to prevent a new will, then claims the estate under the old one.

(3) A federal statute forbids prepaying a foreigner's passage to the United States to take up work of any kind. A congregation prepays the passage of an English clergyman coming to serve as its pastor.

(4) A park ordinance bars operating a vehicle in the park. An ambulance enters to reach a cardiac arrest.

(5) The same ordinance is used to ticket a person using a motorized wheelchair on a park path.

(6) A newly enacted revenue measure sets a tax rate at 5 percent in the session laws, while every earlier draft, the committee report, and the revenue estimate say 15 percent.

(7) A workers' compensation statute gives a worker one year from the injury to file a claim, with no exception for incapacity. A worker injured in an accident spends eighteen months in a coma and files one month after waking.

Start with the definition in one sentence, run it through all seven cases, and tell me which of your classifications is least secure. Also say whether any case is better handled by something other than the absurdity doctrine, such as correcting a scrivener's error or reading the disputed word in its ordinary sense. Keep the answer to no more than 700 words, and write in American English.
