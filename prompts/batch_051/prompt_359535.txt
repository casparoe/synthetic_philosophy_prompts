Here's a parallel I can't decide about. Dennett's ladder of generate-and-test creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — maps suspiciously well onto how modern LLMs get built: pretraining as Darwinian (variation and selection by an external optimizer, with no within-lifetime learning), RLHF as Skinnerian (emitted outputs shaped by reward), chain-of-thought and deliberative search as Popperian (candidate thoughts die inside the model before reaching the world), and tool use, retrieval, and prompt libraries as Gregorian (leaning on external mind-tools, language included). Is this a real structural identity or just pattern-matching off the names? Where does the mapping break under load, and does anything genuine transfer — for instance, do the limitations each rung is supposed to overcome show up as real failure modes of the corresponding LLM stage?
