I have come to accept the interventionist account of causation associated with James Woodward. In the version I hold, X is a cause of Y just in case there is a possible surgical intervention on X—one that sets the value of X independently of the other causes of Y and influences Y, if at all, only through X—that would change the value of Y or its probability distribution, against a specified background. I know the stock objection: an intervention so characterised already trades in causal notions, so the account looks circular. I accept Woodward's reply that the project is not reductive in intent, but I do not treat that reply as closing the matter.

I want an honest map of the view's exposure. What would have to be true for interventionism about causation to be false, or to collapse into one of its rivals, or to be true only within a much narrower domain than its defenders claim? In particular: (a) Is the circularity worry a merely presentational problem that any family of causal concepts must tolerate, or could it deepen into a demonstration that interventionism cannot get a grip without a prior notion of causal structure that it was meant to illuminate? (b) Which cases are genuine pressure points—preemption and overdetermination, omissions and absences, causes that cannot be manipulated even in principle, causal cycles and simultaneous causation—and which are only apparent counterexamples that a careful choice of variables dissolves? (c) Does the freedom to choose variables, and to decide their fineness of grain, make the account too permissive, too restrictive, or both in different places? (d) Is the invariance criterion strong enough to distinguish causal relations from non-causal regularities that happen to be stable under the interventions we can actually perform or describe?

Sort these into what would be a decisive refutation, what would force a major amendment, and what would merely be awkward. Say where defenders of the view would concede ground as opposed to where they would insist that the objection demands a different project from the one interventionism claims to be. And end with a judgement: has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and, if so, is that a defect in a theory of this kind, or the proper price of a non-reductive account?
