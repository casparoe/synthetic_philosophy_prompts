A few years ago my sister converted to a church that teaches eternal conscious torment, and I've been trying to work out whether a finite life of ordinary wrongs could ever proportionally deserve a punishment that never ends. If hell's intensity tapered off geometrically—say each year's suffering is half the previous year's—then an everlasting punishment could add up to a perfectly finite amount of pain (1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ... = 2), and in principle we could tune that finite sum to match the finite wrong. You'd keep the word "eternal" without any infinite disvalue. Is this actually a coherent position? Does it solve the proportionality objection, or does it just collapse into annihilationism (the later suffering becomes negligible) or universalism (the punishment effectively ends)? Would a defender of eternal torment be right or wrong to adopt it? I honestly can't tell if its original or just a math trick.
