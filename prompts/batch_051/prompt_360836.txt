I have a policy about my own grief and sadness that I'd like someone to stress-test as an epistemic rule rather than a stance about how to live. I treat the felt intensity of a negative emotion—grief at a loss, regret after a decision, the low-grade sadness of a fading friendship—as evidence about how much the thing mattered to me, and I deliberately refuse to blunt those feelings, chemically or through rituals of "moving on," until they have finished their evidential work. The rationale: my attachments are mostly opaque to introspection, and the pain is the only readout I have; if I dampen it early, I am left with a biased sample of my own values. I know this is also the sort of thing people say to romanticize suffering, so I want the policy checked. What would have to be true for it to be reliable? When is it just a story I tell to avoid doing anything about the sadness or to avoid moving on? And is there a version that keeps the information while discarding the misery, or is the misery itself doing the epistemic work—such that a pill that removed the felt pain but preserved the information would still leave me worse at knowing what mattered?
