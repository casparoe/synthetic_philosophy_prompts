The consumer agreements I keep reviewing in my work all contain binding arbitration clauses, and I keep encountering the claim that mandatory arbitration privatizes justice. I want to know whether the charge is doing any real work. Here is an argument that it is not. (1) What justice in a particular dispute requires is that the governing law be applied correctly to the facts by an impartial decision-maker, after each side has had a fair opportunity to present evidence and argument. (2) Consumer arbitration supplies all of that: the arbitrator is supposed to apply the same substantive law a court would apply, the parties may present evidence and argument, and the resulting award is binding. (3) So a case sent to arbitration receives what justice requires in that case, in the same sense in which a court provides it. (4) Therefore moving a dispute from a court to binding arbitration does not take away anything that constitutes justice; it only moves the hearing to another venue. I am suspicious of step 2, since arbitrators generally need not issue public decisions and their legal errors are rarely reviewed on the merits, but I worry that I am confusing accountability mechanisms with ingredients of justice. Tell me exactly which step, if any, fails, and what the strongest corrected version of that step would be. If the argument is valid as stated, say what follows from it and what does not.
