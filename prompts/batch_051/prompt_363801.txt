In the course of an argument about climate obligations, I told a friend that he was in no position to criticise other people's driving because he takes several long-haul flights a year. He replied that, on that principle, no one could criticise anyone about anything, since none of us is morally faultless, and he closed the matter with the proverb 'let him who is without sin cast the first stone'. I am fairly sure this conclusion is too strong, but I cannot articulate exactly what the proverb is claiming or where the reasoning goes astray.

Please disambiguate that proverb as it functions in debates about hypocrisy and the standing to blame. Lay out the distinct precise principles it could be used to express — for instance, about who is permitted to blame, who is permitted to punish, whether a true accusation loses its force when the accuser is a hypocrite, and whether the disqualifying defect is complete faultlessness or simply committing the very wrong one condemns. For each reading, state whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false, and note what difference it makes in practice. Then say which reading or readings, if any, would actually support the conclusion that no one may blame anyone because no one is blameless, and which readings would leave my original criticism of him intact. Present the readings as a menu rather than as a defence of a single interpretation, and please write the answer in British English.
