I have been trying to write out my views on how proper names get their referents, and I keep finding that the difference between given names and family names is doing more work than the standard debates acknowledge. Before I take the summary to a reading group, I would like an outside consistency check.

Here is roughly where I have landed.

1. I am a Millian about given names as used for particular individuals: a given name's only semantic contribution is its bearer, not a description or a cluster of properties associated with the bearer.

2. I accept a causal-historical account of how such a name acquires and keeps its bearer. The initial assignment fixes the bearer, later uses preserve reference by deferring to earlier uses, and the descriptive beliefs of later users are semantically inert.

3. I do not think a family name is a name of an individual in the same sense. A surname such as Kennedy is more like a predicate: it denotes membership in a lineage or descent group, and it can be true of many people at once.

4. But when a speaker uses Kennedy to refer to one particular member of the family, I want to say the expression has been resolved to an individual name. The same surface string can therefore be a surname-predicate in one use and an individual name in another.

5. It follows, on my view, that a string such as Kim or Morgan is not one ambiguous name when it functions as a given name in one community and as a family name in another. It is two different names that happen to share a surface form.

6. I also accept Madagascar-style reference shift: a name can change bearers along a chain when later users systematically misidentify the intended bearer. I think this has happened for some family names as well, where a surname now tracks a lineage different from the one it originally tracked.

7. I find the Paderewski puzzle a genuine pressure on treating names as transparent tags for their bearers. A competent, logically acute speaker can have two separate internal files for a single name and rationally disbelieve that the two files concern the same person, even when they do concern one person.

What I want is an audit rather than a re-teaching of the standard positions. For each pair of commitments you judge to be in genuine tension, say where the tension comes from, how severe it is, and what would have to give to resolve it. Also flag any pair that looks tense but dissolves once it is stated carefully. Then order the genuine tensions by severity and tell me which single revision to my list would remove the most strain, and at what cost.
