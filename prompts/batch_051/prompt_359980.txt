At a dinner party last weekend two friends got into an argument that I've been turning over ever since. Lena had been watching a nature video about a digger wasp. The wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the mouth of its burrow, leaves it on the threshold, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out, and then pulls the cricket in. But if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp comes out, drags it back to the threshold, and then goes inside to check all over again instead of just dragging it in. It will repeat this indefinitely. Lena said the wasp is basically a little machine running a subroutine: all the language we use about it 'deciding' or 'preparing' is projection, and it has about as much free will as a screen door. Omar said that's too fast. The wasp is doing something enormously complicated and adaptive, and one loop an experimenter can exploit doesn't erase that it hunts, digs, navigates, and stings with precision. Humans get caught in loops too, he said, like a person checking a locked door or rereading the same email, and nobody concludes the person is a machine. Lena said that's exactly the difference: a person can notice the loop and step back, while the wasp apparently can't, and having a representation of what you're doing that lets you veto the routine is what makes us free. Omar replied that this is just adding a bigger loop with a self-monitoring subroutine, and there's no reason to call that freedom rather than more mechanism. Lena said it's the only thing 'freedom' could usefully mean: when I know what I'm doing, I can do otherwise. Omar said the wasp 'knows' what it's doing at each step too; it just doesn't know the whole sequence is being puppeted by an experimenter. I honestly coudn't tell where they started talking past each other.
