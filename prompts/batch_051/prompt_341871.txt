Thomson's lamp seems like a total nonissue if you've ever debugged a race condition. You flip a switch infinitely many times, each flip taking half as long as the one before it, so by t=60s the whole sequence is done. The question everyone asks — is the lamp on or off at the end? — looks to me like reading an uninitialized variable: the process simply never assigns a value to the state at the limit, so of course it's undefined. In code your'd fix this with a default case, like setting the state to NULL at the limit, or by specifying in advance what the switch does at exactly one minute. Then there's nothing left to puzzle over. So why do philosophers treat this as a serious argument that you can't complete infinitely many tasks in finite time, rather than as a spec that just forgot to define the final state? Is there an actual contradiction in the lamp I'm missing, or is the whole paradox just undefined behavior?
