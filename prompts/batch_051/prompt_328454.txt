For my local adult education group (around 18 people, 2.5 hours), design a structured negotiation game on secession and self-determination. Use Scotland/UK, Catalonia/Spain, or a fictional state with similar features—your pick, but then commit to it. The participants should actually run into the problem that the “self” in self-determination has no pre-political boundary: whoever gets to draw the voting map can decide the outcome. So include at least one moment where the group has to decide the voting unit before deciding the substantive question. The activity needs concrete role cards, a sequence of timed rounds, and simple rules about what counts as agreement. No lecture at the start, and don’t name the doctrine until the debrief. In the debrief, map each moment in the game onto a specific move in the philosophical debate (e.g., plebiscite, partition, remedial secession, recognition by outside states), and be honest about which aspects of real secession conflicts the excercise cannot simulate. Give me one full runnable version, not a menu of options, and defend the choices you made.
