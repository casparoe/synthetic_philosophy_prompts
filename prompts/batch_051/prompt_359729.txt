I'm a novelist working on a book about a legal aid lawyer, and my protagonist has a steady stream of clients who got burned by take-it-or-leave-it contracts they clicked through without reading—phone plans that ban class actions, cloud storage that deletes accounts after a late payment, warranties that disappear behind a checkbox. I want her to have a real decision procedure, not authorial hand-waving, and I want it to track the actual line between assent and mere notice—between terms that were genuinely communicated and terms that were just dumped in a scrollbox. Can you give me a procedure she can run in about fifteen minutes per intake, with no legal database and maybe one quick question to the grumpy senior attorney down the hall, to sort a clicked I agree into probably binding or probably challengeable? The questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where she stops and treats it as binding, where she stops and challenges, and where she hands it off because it's a judgment call not a rule. Then run it on three clients: one who had to scroll through a wall of terms and check a box before buying a phone; one who just used a site with a footer saying continued use counts as acceptance; and one who clicked I agree on a 12-screen form where clause 9 says they wave the right to sue. Flag at least one case where the procedure comes out binding even though the client feels cheated, and say plainly what the method can't do for her.
