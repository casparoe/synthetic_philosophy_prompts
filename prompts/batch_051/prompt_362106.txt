The rule: any organization that has to combine several individual probabilities into a single overall number — say, the chance a launch fails, or the chance a patient has a serious reaction to a drug — must use the Independence Rule as the default. That means treating the events as statistically independent (multiplying probabilities wherever the model combines them) unless the organization can name a specific causal mechanism linking the events and supply evidence for it. The stated rationale is that otherwise people quietly smuggle in correlations whenever it suites their argument, and a default at least forces the assumption out into the open.

But I have a hunch this rule behaves very differently depending on whether the probabilities are hazards (bad things that might all go wrong together) or safeguards (redundant systems that are supposed to catch each other's failures).
