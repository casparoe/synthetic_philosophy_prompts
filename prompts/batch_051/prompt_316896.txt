You are a moral philosopher specializing in manipulation, persuasion, and the ethics of influence. You are constructing a case for a paper whose thesis is that an influence attempt can be manipulative even when it is fully open about what the influencer is doing. Work through the task in stages: begin by brainstorming at least two candidate scenarios and briefly note why each one succeeds or fails against the specifications. Then settle on the strongest candidate and develop it carefully. Do not jump straight to a final answer.

Two accounts of manipulation are on the table. Account A, the bypassing account, holds that an influence is manipulative when it intentionally brings about the target's decision by engaging processes other than the target's capacity to assess reasons — for example, cognitive biases, visceral or affective reactions, or automatic heuristics — rather than by offering considerations the target can evaluate. Account B, the disclosure account, holds that an influence is manipulative only if it is covert or deceptive in a way that prevents the target from seeing that she is being influenced, or from seeing how she is being influenced. On this view, influence whose intent, means, and expected effects are all openly declared is not manipulation, however emotionally wrought or hard-pressed the target may be.

Your task is to design a realistic scenario — at most one paragraph, involving only ordinary competent adults and everyday means of influence — in which one person influences another in a way that Account A classifies as manipulation while Account B classifies as legitimate persuasion. The target's decision must remain recognizably her own. The case must not turn into coercion through threats that make refusal unreasonably costly, must not rely on deception that violates the disclosure constraint, and must not exploit severe cognitive impairment or a special power relation. The point is to isolate the variable of openness: the influence should be transparent about what is being done and why, while still working through a channel that does not run through rational assessment.

Produce the following six components in order. First, state the scenario clearly, no longer than one paragraph. Second, explain why Account A classifies it as manipulation: identify the exact non-rational process being engaged, show that the influencer intentionally relies on it, and explain why merely informing the target about the process is not enough, on this account, to restore rational control. Third, explain why Account B classifies the same interaction as legitimate persuasion: identify the disclosure conditions that are satisfied and why they defeat the charge of manipulation on this view. Fourth, identify the structural feature that does the work — the element whose presence produces the divergence — and run a robustness check: if that feature were removed, would the case collapse into a more standard case of non-manipulative persuasion or a more standard case of manipulation? Explain. Fifth, give the strongest attack a defender of Account B would make on your case — for example, that the case is really coercion, that the target cannot genuinely use the disclosure, or that the scenario cheats by weakening the target's autonomy in some other way — and give the best reply available to a defender of Account A. Sixth, end with one sentence stating what the case, if it succeeds, establishes about the relationship between openness and manipulation.
