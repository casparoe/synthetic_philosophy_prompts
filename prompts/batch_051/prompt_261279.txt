Here is roughly where I have landed on oaths, and I would like an audit of whether it hangs together before I put it in writing:

(1) An oath binds not by changing the moral facts about the promised act but by the swearer's act of self-binding: in swearing, one calls a penalty down on one's own future breach.

(2) Therefore an oath has force only if some sanction the swearer genuinely cares about actually attaches; a purely private vow with no possible consequence does not bind.

(3) An oath taken under duress does not bind, because the act of self-binding must be voluntary.

(4) Swearing by the Styx is a paradigm binding oath, even though the Greek gods do not exist; its force lives in the unconditional form of the self-curse, not in anyone actually listening.

(5) Perjury is a distinct and graver wrong than ordinary promise-breaking would have been, and it remains wrong even when no penalty follows.

(6) One cannot release oneself from a solemn oath; only the person to whom it was sworn, or the authority that sanctions it, can release the swearer.

Where are the real tensions, where do they only look like tensions, and which single revision would clean up the most? Please reply with exactly five numbered findings, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
