Here are the criteria I was taught for something counting as a forest: it has to be a contiguous area of at least one hectare that is dominated by trees, with canopy cover above 30 percent, an interior microclimate that measurably differs from the surrounding non-forest land, and it has to support at least some species that can't live in the open. Now take this case: a logged-over plot that was fenced off at exactly one hectare and left to regenerate for twenty years. The last few canopy surveys came back 29.9%, 30.1%, 29.8%, 30.2%—the flucuation is inside the measurement error. On hot still afternoons the middle of the plot is noticeably cooler and more humid than the pasture around it, but the effect disappears on windy days. A pair of forest-interior birds have been nesting in the center for three summers, though the nearest other forest is miles away and the surrounding pasture offers nothing else. Is this place a forest? Go criterion by criterion, and say honestly where the criteria just don't settle it. Then tell me whether this is a sorites case: does there have to be a single branch's worth of leaf cover that, if added or removed, would flip the answer one way or the other? And is the right lesson that 'forest' is vague—no fact of the matter, or a hidden sharp fact we can't quite measure—or that my criteria are just bad?
