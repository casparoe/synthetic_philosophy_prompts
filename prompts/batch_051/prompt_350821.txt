I run a reading group of advanced undergraduates who have had a little decision theory—they can state the transitivity axiom and expected utility—but have never met Quinn's self-torturer. Next week I have ninety minutes to get them from their default picture of rational preference as a series of pairwise comparisons to seeing precisely what the self-torturer does and does not show about whether rational preferences must be transitive. Do not explain the puzzle to me; plan how I should explain it.

The case I will use is roughly Quinn's. A person controls a device with a long chain of settings, from painless at one end to excruciating at the other; each step upward adds a pain increment too small to notice and pays a large fixed sum. The person would refuse to move all the way in a single step, yet at every adjacent pair the step seems worth taking. I want the session to make that conflict feel forced rather than like a trick.

Give me the order in which the component ideas must be introduced and what depends on what—at minimum pairwise choice versus global evaluation, indiscriminability versus indifference, and the difference between a preference relation that is genuinely intransitive and an indifference relation that is merely nontransitive. Identify the one smaller example or simplified case that should carry the whole session before I present the full device, and say what it must have and must lack; also say why a tempting alternative such as temperature increments or color chips would set up the wrong lesson. Map the misconceptions the students predictably bring or form—I expect several, including treating "too small to matter" as literal indifference, inferring from the rationality of each step to the rationality of the endpoint, and concluding that the puzzle simply proves preferences may be intransitive—and specify where in the sequence each should be headed off. End with one question or short scenario whose answer would show me they understand what Quinn's case actually establishes, and tell me what you would leave out at this level and how you would say so.
