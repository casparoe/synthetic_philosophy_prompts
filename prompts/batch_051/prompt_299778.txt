I'm a few weeks into reporting a longread on anticorruption rules and I've hit a wall. The NGOs and multinational compliance departments I talk to are absolutist about one thing — you can't hand anything of value to a public official, no cash, no expensive dinners, no gifts for there family; that's corruption or the doorway to it. But a lot of those same institutions are completely comfortable with American-style lobbying and campaign finance, where a company buys a table at a fundraiser or its PAC writes a check and gets access, and with the foreign-bribery carve-out that lets firms pay small grease payments to speed up routine government paperwork while banning payments meant to influence policy or win a contract. Meanwhile, when a politician in a patronage-heavy system routes a housing slot or a public-works job to a loyal supporter in exchange for votes, the anticorruption crowd calls that clientelism and treats it as corrupt — even though the supporter is, in effect, trading political support for favorable treatment, which sounds a lot like what the donor is doing.

So here's what I'm trying to sort out. What theory of corruption does this whole setup presuppose? It seems to assume there's a principled line between a quid pro quo tied to a specific official act (corrupt) and a more diffuse exchange of money for access or goodwill (legitimate), and maybe between particularistic exchanges and the impersonal administration of public office. But is that line doing real philosophical work, or is it just a Western, post-Weberian way of drawing boundaries that labels one society's particularism as corruption while blessing another's? Reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can, note which rival views of political influence it quietly rules out, say where the practice strictly presupposes it rather than merely fitting with it, and tell me what would have to change in the anticorruption regime if the presupposed theory turned out to be parochial rather than universal. I'd also like to know which parts of the practice are underdetermined by that theory, so that more than one theory of corruption could license them.
