I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I'm stuck on a scene. Two characters are at an airport bar during an overnight layover: a retired computational neuroscientist and a philosopher of biology who keeps saying the brain is a computer only if you've already decided what counts as a computer. They're a little drunk and a lot tired, and they keep going in circles. I want the dialogue to be genuinely even-handed—neither one should be a strawman, and each should respond to the actual thing the other just said, not just wait their turn. At some point I want them to get into the weeds about whether a system has to represent its own rules to count as computing, or whether any reliable physical state-transition mapping is enough. Maybe have one of them propose that the real fight is whether computation is observer-relative or intrinsic, and the other trys to show that this distinction is doing too much work. It'd be nice if by the end they both concede something real without the scene turning into a debate-club tie. Could you write the dialogue?
