I help run a reading group on distributive justice at my university, and for weeks we have been stuck on the question of what, exactly, an egalitarian should be trying to equalize. One faction in the room, call them the resourcists, says the relevant unit is the external means people command: income, wealth, and generalized opportunities understood as bundles of resources, with individuals themselves responsible for how they convert those means into a life they value. The other faction, the capabilitarians, says this stops too early: resources matter only instrumentally, and what we should compare is people's real capability to be and do the things they have reason to value, with particular attention to how different people convert the same resources into different functionings. The question stopped being abstract for me when I joined a university committee charged with setting the principles behind a new student support fund; we are now arguing about whether the fund should assess financial need alone, or also account for differences in what students require to convert money into genuine educational capability, such as disability-related costs or caregiving responsibilities.

I would like to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps, of the sort the two sides could sign and circulate as a shared map of their dispute. Please produce the document itself. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, about the phenomenon both camps are trying to capture, the shortcomings of purely subjective-welfare metrics, and the constraints any adequate account must respect; (2) a disagreements table in which the exact remaining differences are stated so that a representative of each camp would accept the wording attributed to their own side; (3) the cruxes, meaning the specific sub-questions such that settling them one way or the other would move at least one camp, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) a short note on any part of the dispute you regard as merely verbal, if you think some of it is.

The group will use this as a working document, so it should avoid survey talk and avoid citations. Please keep the whole thing to at most 750 words; the table counts toward the limit.
