Consider the following counterfactual. In 2030, a religiously diverse constitutional democracy of roughly fifty million people, with a codified constitution and a tradition of judicial review, amends its constitution to require that every enacted law be accompanied by a published Public Justification Statement. The amendment provides that the statement must show how the law's central provisions can be supported by reasons that all reasonable citizens could accept irrespective of the particular religious or philosophical doctrines they hold. Religious or other comprehensive rationales may be included only insofar as they are translated into such generally acceptable reasons; the statement may not simply recite the teachings of a particular tradition as the ground of the law. A constitutional court is empowered to invalidate a law when its accompanying statement relies on an untranslated comprehensive doctrine, is implausibly strained, or fails to address what the law actually does.

Predict, with your reasoning made visible, how this polity's law and politics look fifty years later. I want the chain of mechanisms and incentives, not a narrative. In particular: Which domains of law become easier or harder to legislate, and why — for example, laws touching on bioethics, family structure, education, or economic regulation? How do religious citizens, parties, and advocacy groups adapt — do they learn to translate their convictions sincerely, use the statement instrumentally to launder sectarian policy, or partially defect from the system? How does the behavior of legislators and the constitutional court change once justification itself becomes a litigable question? And does the rule actually deliver the mutual respect and civic peace its designers presumably sought, or does it professionalize insincerity and drive religious argument out of public view while leaving the underlying disagreements untouched? For each major prediction, indicate whether you regard it as robust, as a plausible guess, or as contingent on further facts about the society, and state what would have to be different for your prediction to fail.
