I'm retired now — spent most of my career in statistical physics — and I've been using the time to read philosophy. Lately Dennett, specifically the three stances (physical, design, intentional) and his claim that patterns are real when they let you predict and compress. And I can't shake the feeling that I already know this from my old field.

Here's the parallel. In statistical physics you almost never model the microstate. You coarse-grain: a gas becomes a few macroscopic variables, temperature and pressure, and you get thermodynamics, which is predictive and real even though it's just a summary of 10^23 molecules. Go up in scale and you get a tower of effective descriptions — effective field theories, hydrodynamics, whatever — each with its own variables and couplings, none of them fundamental, each predictively useful in its own regime, and nobody thinks they're fake. Dennett's ladder from physical stance to design stance to intentional stance looks to me like the same move repeated: the intentional stance is the effective theory of a person, with beliefs and desires as the effective variables, and the criterion for taking them seriously is the same criterion we use for temperature or pressure — they buy you prediction and compression. His real-patterns idea seems like it is just saying that higher-level effective descriptions are real for exactly the reason a physicist says a thermodynamic variable is real.

But I'm old enough to know that a seductive analogy can be doing no work. So what I want to know is wether this is deep or superficial. Be specific: (a) state the mapping cleanly — what corresponds to what on the two sides; (b) say where it genuinely holds; (c) say where it breaks; (d) tell me what would have to be true of the intentional stance for the physics results to transfer — in particular, effective theories are supposed to be derivable in principle from the microtheory and to come with a systematic way of computing corrections, and I don't see the equivalent for beliefs and desires; (e) give me something the parallel would predict that seems false — maybe that there should be a continuum of intermediate stances between design and intentional, or that the intentional stance should be systematically improvable, or something else.
