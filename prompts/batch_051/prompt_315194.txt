I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep noticing a structural parallel that I cannot decide about. In Dedekind's 1888 monograph on the foundations of arithmetic, as I understand it, an infinite set is defined by its ability to be put into one-to-one correspondence with one of its proper subsets: the whole can be mapped without omission or repetition onto a part of itself. That looks intimately close to the way a lazy stream in a functional language can be defined recursively, for example defining the infinite stream of ones as being one consed onto that same stream: the whole object occurs again as its own tail, so there is an isomorphism between the entire stream and a proper part of itself, just as in Dedekind's criterion.

I can see two ways to read this. On one reading, this is a genuine shared structure: a finite definition generating an actually infinite object whose totality is nevertheless available to local operations such as mapping or shifting, and perhaps Hilbert's hotel is just a story about performing a shift operation over such a stream. On the other, I suspect I am pattern-matching on the word part, since a lazy stream is a computational object with evaluation rules, and Dedekind's sets are collections that exist independently of any procedure for producing them. What I want is a precise diagnosis of whether the analogy is deep or superficial, and if there is a real transfer of results, an example of something new that it would predict about either side; if not, an exact statement of where the two structures come apart.
