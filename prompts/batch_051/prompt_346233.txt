I have been going back and forth with a colleague about whether a sufficiently sophisticated reinforcement learning agent could ever be said to suffer, and we kept getting stuck because we were arguing about what a scalar reward signal even is from the agent’s own point of view. So I want to step back and ask what the practice of building agents around reward signals presupposes. I was rereading Sutton and Barto’s Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, and in the section on goals and rewards they present the reward signal as the designer’s way of telling the agent what to achieve, not how to achieve it; the agent is built to maximise cumulative reward. I am paraphrasing rather than quoting, because I do not have the exact wording in front of me, but that is the passage I want the answer to turn on. The passage struck me as saying something philosophically modest, namely that reward is just a communication channel from the designer to the agent, while the surrounding practice of the field seems to say something far less modest. People speak of negative reward as punishment, of reward shaping as making a task easier or harder to like, of reward hacking as a failure in what the agent treats as worth wanting, and of agents trying to get reward in ways that attach intentional and affective language to a number. What philosophical theory, if any, is presupposed by treating the reward signal as both a formal objective and a bearer of normative or affective valence?

I would like you to reconstruct the implied view. Specifically, does this practice presuppose that valence is scalar, that pleasures and pains can be traded off by summing, that negative and positive rewards differ only in sign, and that the designer’s intention to mark some outcomes as good or bad is sufficient for them to be good or bad for the agent? Or is the practice philosophically underdetermined, so that several rival views of valence and welfare are compatible with the formalism and the surrounding talk is merely convenient shorthand? And what would have to change in how the field talks and designs reward functions if we replaced the presupposed view with, say, the view that valence is multidimensional, or that no scalar objective can capture the distinction between what is pursued and what is felt? If the question is confused, or if I am misreading what the practice actually presupposes, I would rather you say that plainly than construct a neat answer that misses the disagreement.
