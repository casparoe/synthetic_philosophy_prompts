I've been asked to give the opening talk at our platform's trust and safety offsite, and I keep getting stuck on the same problem, so I want the speech to be about it. Half the room are the applied ML people who build the classifiers, half are policy and enforcement. The problem: when someone posts a piece of propaganda in order to debunk it -- a hate group's video, a fake 'immigrants bring scabies' infographic, a mass shooter's manifesto clip -- the debunking depends on reproducing the thing, but the reproduced thing is itself the harm. And there's a less-discussed version of the same trade-off inside our own stack: the detection and labelling pipelines duplicate and circulate that material at a scale orders of magnitude bigger than any user post they exist to catch. Please write the speech, about 12 minutes. It should make the engineers see that reproducing something for labelled data or evaluation is a distribution decision, not a neutral technical step, and make the policy people see why a blanket 'never reproduce propaganda' rule also bans the debunking we keep asking users to do. Don't turn it into a free-speech lecture, and don't end with a soft line about exposure doing the work; end on the actual question the offsite needs to answer. One mild joke at my expense is fine, and please write in British English.
