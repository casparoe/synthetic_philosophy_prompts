Im putting together questions for a Q&A next month with a climate scientist who defends a pretty specific view: that the thing policy should hang on is not the output of any single climate model, even a good one, but the robustness of a result across a structurally diverse ensemble of models. She has gotten very good at deflecting the familiar objection that no model is literally true by saying robustness does the epistemic work that validation cannot do before the future shows up. Draft me nine interview questions that make that deflection harder to use each time—not the usual "so why should the public trust models" stuff.

Make sure there is at least one question on each of the following, and for each one give the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give:

- Many of the models in the big ensembles share code, parameterizations, or tuning targets, so their agreement can be redundancy rather than independent evidence.
- Calling an ensemble diverse is always relative to some axes and not others, and choosing those axes is itself a modeling decision.
- Robust agreement can come from a common structural error rather than from tracking the real system.
- The relevant decisions are fat-tailed and irreversible, and the ensemble often has nothing robust to say about exactly the dangerous top end of the distribution.

End with a question about what would actually count as evidence that an ensemble is diverse in the epistemically right way, and not just varying the parameters the field happened to care about.
