Phil-of-bio PhD student here. The animal consciousness debates are starting to feel like people drawing lines on a map with no agreement about what a good map even is. One paper says octopuses are in because they've got X; another says bees are probably out because they lack Y. I want to stop reading those until I have the spec written down first.

Here's the assignment: give me a list of demands that any account of how consciousness is distributed across the animal kingdom has to meet before I take it seriously. Not the account itself — the demands. For each demand, tell me what makes it binding: is it a case the account has to get right, a distinction it isn't allowed to blur, or a biological or computational finding it can't flatly ignore? And say which demands are non-negotiable and which are just the aesthetic of one research program.

Some things I definitely want covered: humans in deep non-REM sleep, dogs, octopuses, bees, whatever a nematode has going on, maybe organoids, plus one deliberately constructed borderline case where a feature I'd normally trust comes apart from the thing it's supposed to track. Also the distinctions people quietly fudge — being conscious, being sentient in the suffering sense, having valenced states, having a self, being responsive, being integrated. I want those kept apart, and I want to know which fudges are disqualifying and which are tolerable shorthand.

Then the part I actually need help with: which single demand, if I insist on it, does the most damage to the standard menu (global workspace, integrated information, higher-order, welfare-first, whatever you'd put on the list)? And is there a pair of demands that can't be satisfied at once, or at least pull hard in opposite directions? I'd rather find that out now than after building the theory. A table's fine if that's cleaner. Keep the report card on the theories brief — the demands are the whole point for me.

Last one: should I also require an account to include an explicit bridge from whatever makes a creature conscious to the signs we actually use to apply the account in practice, or is it acceptable to just stipulate the distribution? That seems like a real demand but Ive never seen it written down as one.
