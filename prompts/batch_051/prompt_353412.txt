I'm a software engineer, so I tend to see language as a very old protocol with surprisingly lax collision handling. English routinely gives unrelated meanings to the same string: "pitch" names a throw, a tar-like substance, and an auditory frequency property; "bank" names a financial institution and the edge of a river. In an engineered system those would be flagged as collisions and one of them would be renamed, but in natural language they seem to cause few failures because context, frequency, and register usually select the intended sense before the collision becomes live. What I am trying to name is not ambiguity itself, not the process of resolving a particular instance, and not a trait of individual speakers who tolerate unclear messages; it is the system-level property that lets a language absorb many such collisions without breaking down, apparently by tolerating them mainly where the surrounding signal is strong enough to resolve them. Is there an established term for that property, and where does it part ways with the closest related concepts?
