A visiting historian, Professor Alix Hart, works on the attribution of responsibility for the outbreak of wars and other great-power confrontations. She maintains that the question of who started a conflict is usually answerable, but insists that the answer is not simply whoever fired first. She says her case verdicts are applications of a consistent method, though she has never stated it in full generality, preferring to let the accumulated cases speak.

In her seminar she has given the following verdicts:

– The 1931 Japanese seizure of Manchuria: Japan, because it manufactured a railway incident at Mukden and used it as a pretext to occupy the region while deflecting League of Nations mediation.
– The 1935 Italian invasion of Ethiopia: Italy, because it converted the Walwal border clash into an ultimatum and invaded while arbitration was still on the table.
– The 1939 German invasion of Poland: Germany, because, having repudiated the Versailles order, it made demands it knew Poland could not accept and then crossed the frontier.
– The 1939–40 Soviet–Finnish War: the Soviet Union, because it broke off negotiations and attacked after Finland refused the Soviet demand for territory and a naval base.
– The 1990–91 Gulf War: Iraq, because it declared Kuwait a stolen province, made annexationist demands, and invaded after negotiations it never treated as genuine.

In each case she denies that her verdict implies the other side was blameless; she says the other side often shared in the underlying tensions, but that is not the same as starting the conflict.

Next week she turns to the opening of the Cold War, and she has not announced a verdict. The background she has distributed runs as follows. The wartime Allies had agreed to cooperate over liberated Europe and to administer occupied Germany as one economic unit under four-power control. After 1945, the Soviet Union installed allied regimes across Eastern Europe, while the Western powers consolidated their occupation zones into a Western bloc. In March 1947 the United States announced the Truman Doctrine, committing Washington to resisting Soviet political expansion wherever it appeared; in June 1947 it launched the Marshall Plan for European recovery, which Moscow rejected and which the East European states were prevented from joining. During 1948 the Western Allies moved to create a separate West German state, and in June 1948 they introduced a new currency in their occupation zones without Soviet agreement; the Soviet Union responded by blockading the road, rail, and canal routes into West Berlin.

Predict Professor Hart's verdict on who started the Cold War, and comment on whether her method, as exhibited so far, permits a single verdict for a decades-long, largely non-military rivalry. Identify the candidate principles that are jointly consistent with her earlier verdicts, say what each candidate principle implies about the Cold War case, and state which prediction best coheres with her precedents and what principle that prediction commits her to. Finally, name the further case, real or constructed, that would do the most to separate the surviving candidates.
