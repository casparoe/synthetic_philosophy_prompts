I am a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on the philosophy of mind, and I have been turning over a result about the timing of conscious experience that was mentioned only in passing. I would like to know whether this phenomenon has an established name, and, more importantly, whether the two ways of describing it are genuinely distinct hypotheses.

The experiment is as follows. A small red dot is flashed briefly at one position; after a short blank interval, a green dot is flashed at a second position. With the right timing, observers do not report two separate dots. Instead, they report a single dot that appears to move continuously across the gap and to turn from red to green somewhere around the midpoint of its path. What puzzles me is that the reported change point falls before the green dot was physically presented. It seems as though information arriving later reaches back and determines how the earlier portion of the sequence was experienced. As far as I can tell, this is a standard, repeatable experimental effect rather than careless reporting.

I have encountered two descriptions of what might be going on. On the first, the subject initially experiences a moving dot of one color, and then, once the green flash arrives, the brain quietly edits the record of the experience; the unedited version is not preserved, so the subject cannot catch the edit. On the second, there is no editing at all: the experience of the whole sequence is withheld until both flashes are available, and only then is the moving, color-changing dot made conscious. The two accounts feel importantly different—one is a story of revision, the other of delay—yet I cannot see how the subject, or an experimenter relying on the subject's reports, could ever tell them apart.

Is there an established term for the experimental phenomenon itself, and an established term for the contrast between the two descriptions? I am not looking for a bare label. I want to know whether the two accounts are genuine rivals about the time at which an experience occurs, or whether they are two equivalent redescriptions of the same data, and what evidence could in principle separate them. If the standard terms apply only imperfectly, I would like to know what the nearest concepts are and where the boundaries between them lie.
