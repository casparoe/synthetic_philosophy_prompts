I sit on the board of a small charity. After examining the accounts myself, I have become fairly confident that the treasurer has been siphoning off funds: the figures only reconcile if money was moved between accounts in a pattern that looks deliberate. I have also just come across a rigorous study showing that people with my level of accounting experience, when presented with exactly this kind of financial evidence, make confident judgements of wrongdoing that later turn out to be wrong in a large majority of cases. I cannot find any flaw in my own reasoning—but of course I would not be able to if I were one of the mistaken majority. Should I lower my confidence that the treasurer is guilty? And if I keep believing he is guilty while accepting the study's claim about people like me, am I being irrational? Please say how confident you are in your answer and which point you are least sure of.
