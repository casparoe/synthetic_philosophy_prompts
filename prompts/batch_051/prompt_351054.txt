I’m a journalist covering science and ideas, and I’m preparing a piece on why people laugh at slightly malfunctioning technology: voice assistants that misunderstand, delivery robots that bump into curbs, autocorrect that produces surreal errors. I want the piece to include a short interactive sidebar: a “which theory of humor do you actually hold?” sorting quiz for readers.

The piece’s intellectual anchor is Herbert Spencer’s essay The Physiology of Laughter. Spencer argues there that laughter is a vent for surplus nervous energy that, finding no more serious outlet, takes the path of least resistance through the muscles of the face, voice, and breathing. On this picture, laughter is not essentially a judgment about the person or thing laughed at; it is a release of accumulated arousal or blocked expectation. I want the quiz to test whether readers’ concrete responses actually commit them to that kind of relief theory, or to one of the other standard positions: superiority theory, incongruity theory, or benign violation theory.

Please construct the quiz and its scoring key. Use eight to ten questions. Each question should present a concrete case—drawn from everyday life, comedy, or the technology-failure examples above—with two or three answer options. The options should describe reactions or reasons, not labels, and no question should ask readers whether they identify with a named theory. The quiz should sort readers into four leaves: superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation.

Build the instrument from the diagnostic fault lines first rather than from the names of theories. In particular, work out which questions separate the following possibilities:

- whether the laugher is evaluating the object or person laughed at,
- whether laughter requires a prior expectation that is then violated,
- whether a norm violation is necessary for humor,
- whether the violation must also be perceived as safe or benign,
- whether laughter is essentially a release of physiological or psychological tension,
- whether the laugher’s felt superiority is doing the explanatory work,
- and whether laughter can occur without any object at all.

Use those questions to generate answer patterns, then locate the four named positions within that logical space. Flag any answer pattern that no standard position occupies, and briefly diagnose whether that combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Also flag any two positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate, rather than forcing a distinction the questions do not actually support. Present the final instrument in a form suitable for a magazine sidebar: the questions, the answer options, the scoring key, and a short note on what the quiz can and cannot reveal.

Please write the response in American English.

If I am leaning on Spencer in a way that misreads his argument, or if the four-theory taxonomy collapses under the diagnostic questions, tell me plainly; don’t smooth over the problem just to produce a quiz. If the quiz cannot do what I want, say so and propose the nearest instrument that would actually work.
