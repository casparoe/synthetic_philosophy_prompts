Before I settle on an account of gratitude I want the checklist, not the readings. Write the desiderata: what does any adequate theory of gratitude have to deliver? Go through the cases it has to get right: the person who tries to help and botches it; the benefit that reaches you purely through luck or through someone who didn't mean to help; the help you never asked for and even resented at the time; whether gratitude can be required of you and still be the real thing; the dead benefactor or the past generation that built what you now rely on; and the difference between being grateful and just being glad or relieved. For each case, say what it forces a theory to say. Then tell me which of these desiderata are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's tastes (I take it some people insist gratitude has to track good will, others that it tracks actual benefit, others that it tracks the cost to the benefactor). Flag any pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied, and I suspect there's at least one: gratitude seems to need a someone to address, but people also seem to feel it toward luck and the impersonal past, so either the addressee requirement goes or those feelings get called something else. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases, not on a scoreboard of the existing theories, though a line or two on how the usual views fare against the list is ok.
