After a long argument with a friend last night over infinite regress arguments, I wrote down the commitments I ended up defending, and I would like an audit of whether they hang together. The commitments are as follows. (1) A regress is vicious exactly when it is a dependency regress that perpetually defers the very explanatory task or set of conditions it was introduced to supply; a regress that merely generates an endless series of items, with each item's work discharged at its own stage, is benign. (2) The Third Man argument against the theory of Forms is benign by this test: even if a Form of Largeness requires a further Form without end, that does not refute the theory. (3) Bradley's regress is vicious: the unity of a fact is never achieved if each attempt to relate a relation to its terms is met by positing a further relation. (4) I lean toward infinitism about epistemic justification: a non-repeating endless chain of reasons can justify a belief even with no foundational belief. (5) The truth regress, in which each true proposition p yields the further true proposition that p is true, is benign.

Please assess whether this package is jointly tenable. Identify which commitments pull against which, order any genuine tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest consistent revision would cost, and flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection. Please write the answer in American English.
