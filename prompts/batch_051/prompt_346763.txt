Imagine two of the most discussed challenges to physicalism about consciousness. The first is the knowledge argument: Mary, a color scientist raised in a monochrome environment, is said to know every physical fact about color vision, yet she seems to learn something new on first seeing red. The second is the explanatory gap: however complete a physical and functional description of an experience becomes, it seems to leave unexplained why that experience feels the way it does. Compare these two arguments as carefully as you can. Are they best understood as the same underlying challenge expressed in two different vocabularies — one about facts and knowledge, the other about explanation and intelligibility — or do they place different demands on a physicalist? In particular, consider how they interact with the ability hypothesis: if Mary does not gain a new fact but only a new ability, has the explanatory gap also been closed? And if the explanatory gap is dismissed as a merely conceptual artifact rather than an ontological gap, does that also defuse Mary's apparent discovery? Take a definite view about whether these arguments stand or fall together, and about which poses the more serious problem for physicalism. Write in continuous prose, as in an essay or a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
