I have no academic training, but I have been reading on my own about the older debates in artificial intelligence, and I keep coming back to the frame problem. As I understand it, the problem began with logic-based planners that had to say, for every action, both what changes and what stays the same. Once a system was expected to do this completely, it seemed to need an unmanageable number of frame axioms, and if it tried to work out relevance by general inference instead, the search blew up. The lesson many people drew was that a finite reasoner cannot afford to treat the whole world as a list of facts plus rules, because relevance cannot be computed cheaply from a complete world model.

Since then, much of AI has shifted toward systems trained on enormous amounts of text and other data rather than built from hand-written symbolism. Large language models and learned world models can often produce sensible plans without ever being told that, say, clearing a table does not repaint the wall. So the question I want answered is whether the old frame-problem argument survives this development, needs repair, or is best pronounced dead.

Please write a careful piece that does the following. State which premise the old argument needs in order to bite, and say whether that premise is about explicit symbolic representation or about something deeper, such as the need for a prior over relevance. Say whether a learned statistical system has actually solved the relevance problem or has merely relocated it into its training data, its prompt, its context window, or its evaluation set. Describe concretely what a residual frame problem would look like in such a system if it has not been solved, and consider whether it is best described as a new frame problem about attention, context windows, or distribution shift rather than the old one. Offer a verdict, and if the argument survives in some form, give the most defensible rebuilt version aimed at statistical predictors rather than logical planners.

Please do not turn this into a literature survey or add citations; the argument should stand or fall on its own. Where there is genuine disagreement among people who know the topic, say so and distinguish what is settled from what is contested rather than presenting one side as the consensus. Write in British English.
