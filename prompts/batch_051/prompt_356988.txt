I'm drafting a position statement for an edited volume on forecasting and epistemic modesty, and I would like a check of its internal coherence before I submit it. The commitments I would defend are as follows. (1) When a usable base rate exists, the outside view should normally outweigh my inside-view judgment, including when the two conflict. (2) A base rate is only as good as its reference class, and choosing a reference class is a substantive, contestable judgment rather than something forced on me by the data. (3) Epistemic modesty works as a second-order commitment: I should be most suspicious of my inside-view narratives exactly where I have an outside view available. (4) In genuinely novel, high-stakes cases where no reference class fits, the inside view is not merely a fallback; it can be the best instrument available, and modesty does not require giving it up. (5) Anyone offering an outside-view estimate ought to state the reference class used and report how sensitive the conclusion is to defensible alternative reference classes. I do not have a settled view about which of these, if any, is the problem. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be.
