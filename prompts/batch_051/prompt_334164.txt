Suppose a town brings in a bylaw: any group can register as a 'religion' and get the tax breaks and zoning exceptions, but it has to score 3 out of 5 on an official checklist: (1) regular communal ritual, (2) a narrative or teaching about what's ultimate or unseen, (3) a moral code members are expected to follow, (4) some places/times/objects treated as specially set apart, (5) standing mutual care among members. Supernatural beings, worship, clergy, and shared creed aren't required, and the board can't interrogate members' private beliefs, it just scores the application. I think of this as a way to test whether 'religion' is a family resemblance category rather than a crisp one, but before anyone builds the thing I want to know how it would actualy run. Walk me through the first year: who applies first, what do they stretch, where does the gaming show up. Then the ten-year version: what does the register look like, and what happens to actual Buddhist meditation groups, a small Methodist congregation, a soccer supporters' club with chants and ritual scarves, a twelve-step group? Does the category inflate until the exemptions get scraped, or does it settle into something stable? And when the dust settles, what has this shown about whether 'religion' has a real core or is just a word regulators can't make behave? If the setup itself begs the question or the task as posed doesn't make sense, say so instead of humoring me.
