You are a historian of early twentieth-century African American intellectual life and a philosopher of art. Think carefully, and begin by reconstructing the relevant theoretical position in your own words before turning to the case; do not simply assert conclusions.

Analyze Ryan Coogler’s 2018 film Black Panther as a test case for W. E. B. Du Bois’s account of art and propaganda, as developed in his 1926 essay Criteria of Negro Art. I want the analysis to run in both directions: use Du Bois to say something precise about the film, and use the film to test the limits of Du Bois’s position.

First, reconstruct Du Bois’s argument carefully. Your reconstruction should address at least these points: his claim that all art is propaganda in the broad sense and that the only real question is what cause the propaganda serves; his insistence that Black artists cannot stand outside the political situation of their audience; his criteria of beauty, truth, and uplift as appropriate standards for Black art; and his worry about the distorting pressure of white patronage and the white gaze.

Then apply that framework to Black Panther. Treat the film as a real cultural event, not as an obvious theoretical success. Consider questions such as: On Du Bois’s analysis, what propaganda does the film actually carry, and how coherent is that propaganda? Would Du Bois’s criteria endorse the film’s depiction of Black power, Black technology, and Black nobility, or would he see it as a compromised fantasy shaped by corporate and predominantly white institutional gatekeeping? Does the tension between T’Challa and Killmonger map onto any tension within Du Bois’s own view about what Black art should do?

Finally, reverse direction. What does this case reveal about Du Bois’s account? Does a commercially spectacular, widely distributed Black-led superhero film show that art can serve emancipatory ends even within mass entertainment, or does it expose an ambiguity in Du Bois’s willingness to instrumentalize art? For example, does the difference between representing Black dignity and actually building collective agency create a problem for Du Bois’s evaluative criteria? Say explicitly where the theory is silent, where it misdescribes the case, and where it remains illuminating.
