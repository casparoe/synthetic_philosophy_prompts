I work in product analytics. My company routinely segments users into generational cohorts — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z — and acts on those segments: our personas assert that Gen Z users will abandon nested navigation, we run feature experiments separately by generation, and HR briefs managers on how Gen Z employees supposedly respond to feedback. Yet when I ask whether Gen Z is a real category, the same colleagues say the boundaries are arbitrary marketing fictions and that we should not reify them. That tension runs through the whole practice. What view of generational categories does this practice presuppose? Does using these buckets to make predictions commit us to real shared features among people born in those years, or only to some weaker sort of conventional grouping that happens to be useful? Please reconstruct the implicit theory of the practice, distinguish what it strictly presupposes from what it merely finds convenient, and say whether several different views of what generations are could each license it — and what would have to change if generations were purely conventional labels with no underlying regularity.
