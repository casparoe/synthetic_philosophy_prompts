I've been trying to nail down what "alive" means for me, and I keep ending up with a list of intuitions that dont obviously hang together. Here are my gut verdicts:

- A mule: alive, obviously, even though it can't reproduce.
- A sterile worker honeybee: alive, same reason — reproduction isnt doing a lot of work for me.
- The influenza virus on a doorknob: not alive, or at best a weird edge case, even though it has nucleic acid and can evolve.
- A bacterial endospore sitting in dry soil for decades: alive, just dormant. No metabolism, no growth, but I'd still call it alive.
- A tardigrade in its dried-up tun state: alive, definitely.
- Fire: not alive. It metabolizes, grows, spreads, but no.
- A prion that converts normal proteins into more prions: not alive, somehow even further from alive than the virus.
- A brain-dead human body on a ventilator, cells still respiring, wounds maybe even healing: not alive. The person is gone.
- A lichen, which is a fungus and an alga living as one thing: alive, but I'm genuinely unsure whether it's one organism or two cooperating, and I don't know if that matters for the question.
- A crystal growing in a saturated solution: not alive.

The pattern I can almost see is something about being a self-maintaining organized system, but the endospore and the brain-dead body pull in opposite directions — one is alive with no current activity, the other is dead with plenty of current activity. And the mule and the virus make me unsure whether reproduction belongs in the definition at all.

Is there a principle (or a small set of candidate principles) that gets all these verdicts, or close? Where does it force me to revise, and which of these gut calls would it overturn? Also, what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — a synthetic cell built from off-the-shelf molecules that does nothing but keep a membrane intact and copy a loop of DNA, and about a computer program that replicates across machines and rewrites bits of its own code? I'm not sure those two should get the same verdict.
