Please assign a number to the following claim and show me the reasoning that produces it, term by term.

The claim: The gender-equality paradox—the apparent tendency for countries scored higher on gender equality to show larger, not smaller, sex differences in occupational and educational choices—is a robust cross-national pattern, and its best explanation is that more gender-equal environments primarily lower the costs of acting on stable, partly endogenous gender-typical occupational preferences; those preferences are authentic (informed and stable under reflection, not adaptive preferences or internalized stereotypes); and such preferences account for at least half of the remaining occupational gender segregation in those countries.

Do not answer "it depends on whether the paradox is real." Treat the question whether the paradox is real as one of the terms. Decompose your credence into at least these four terms, give each a number, and justify each in a sentence or two:

1. Robustness: the probability that the cross-national association is not an artifact. State briefly how you are operationalizing both sides of the association, then estimate whether it survives non-circular measurement, controls for national wealth and welfare-state generosity, and replication across outcome measures.

2. Mechanism, conditional on robustness: the probability that the causal story runs through constraint reduction allowing preference expression, rather than through greater material security enabling women to act on preferences that are themselves still socially shaped, or through stereotype and identity effects that operate differently in more egalitarian contexts.

3. Authenticity, conditional on the mechanism: the probability that the expressed preferences are authentic—they would survive increased information and reflection—rather than adaptive preferences formed under residual inequality.

4. Sufficiency, conditional on authenticity: the probability that these preferences account for at least half of the observed segregation once employer discrimination, pipeline effects, and household bargaining are held constant.

Then show how you combine the terms, and state which single term your final credence is most sensitive to: where a small change in that term shifts the full number the most. Finally, tell me what evidence—realistically describable even if not yet collected—would move that term the most.

One further diagnostic: if one person puts their credence in the full claim at 0.3 and another puts theirs at 0.7, on which of the four terms do they most likely diverge?
