Act as an expert on the Kelly criterion and geometric rationality who also knows a fair bit about poetic form. I teach a philosophy course on decision theory and practical rationality, and this week we finished the Kelly criterion for repeated bets with a positive edge. For a binary bet with net odds b and win probability p, the bettor who maximizes expected log wealth — equivalently long-run growth — stakes the fraction f* = (bp − q)/b of her bankroll, where q = 1 − p. The arithmetic-mean intuition says any +EV bet is worth taking and the bigger the better, but because wealth compounds multiplicatively, overbetting, say roughly doubling Kelly, turns a winning game into a losing one in the long run, even though each individual bet is +EV.

I'd like to close the seminar with a Shakespearean sonnet — 14 lines of iambic pentameter, three quatrains and a couplet — that captures this idea. Have the first two quatrains build the arithmetic-mean picture: a positive expectation feels like free money, and if a little is good, more is better. Put the volta at line 9, where the poem turns on the fact that wealth compounds, so the arithmetic mean overweights the rare lucky paths and underweights the typical path; variance drags the geometric mean down. Use the third quatrain to build the log-wealth / growth-rate picture, and let the closing couplet say, precisely, the lesson: one should maximize expected log wealth or stake the Kelly fraction, not maximize expected wealth. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows Kelly would recognize the mathematics — the overbetting and ruin points in particular have to be right, and the formula can be hinted at rather than spelled out — and good enough as a poem that someone who doesn't know Kelly still feels the vertigo of a bet thats too good not to take and too big to survive.

Before you draft, brainstorm briefly: list the key ideas (edge, compounding, variance drag, growth rate, overbetting, ruin, the log) and assign each to a location in the 14 lines. Then draft, then revise once for meter and once for conceptual accuracy. Dont just name-check the criterion; make the volta do the work.
