I need a working definition of phantasia — Aristotle's term, the one translators keep rendering as 'imagination' even though it clearly isnt the same as my capacity to conjure up mental pictures. I'll give you eight cases; some are obviously in, some obviously out, and a few I genuinely can't sort. (1) looking at a ripe tomato in good light and seeing it as red; (2) closing my eyes after staring at a bright window and still 'seeing' the window's shape hanging there; (3) dreaming that I'm arguing with my brother who lives in another city; (4) remembering the exact shape of the scar above my eyebrow; (5) doing geometry and deliberatly forming a picture of a triangle in my head; (6) at dusk the sun looking about a foot across even though I believe it's enormous; (7) a dog that watched a rabbit disappear behind a boulder and keeps scrabbling around the boulder as if the rabbit were still there; (8) me thinking about justice, which doesn't come with an image, but I'm definitely thinking about something. I'm confident that dreaming and the triangle count as phantasia, and that plain veridical seeing doesn't. The afterimage, the sun, the dog, and justice I have no idea about. Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it surprises you or forces you to revise. Then make it do the job Aristotle gives it: say how it's involved in memory, dreams, and the way an animal can keep after something that isn't present, and whether thought really can require it even when the object has no body.
