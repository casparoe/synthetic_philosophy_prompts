I keep seeing, in arguments about biblical interpretation, the claim that fundamentalists are allegorists in denial — that their "plain sense" is just one more interpretive scheme. I tried to steelman that as a formal argument, and I ended up with something that seems to prove the literal/allegorical distinction is empty, which I do not actually believe: Origen reading the Song of Songs as about Christ and the Church is not doing the same thing as a fundamentalist insisting that the days of Genesis are ordinary twenty-four-hour days. The steelman runs like this. (1) A reading is literal when it assigns a passage the meaning its words carry under the linguistic and genre conventions in force for the author and intended audience; a reading is allegorical when it assigns a meaning imported from outside those conventions. (2) Which conventions were in force is not itself written into the words — it has to be supplied by the interpreter, whether by historical reconstruction or by a theological picture of what the text must be doing. (3) So the same string of words can be literal relative to one supplied set of conventions and allegorical relative to another. (4) Therefore whether a reading counts as literal or allegorical is not a fact about the text but a fact about which conventions the interpreter chooses to attribute. (5) The fundamentalist's plain sense is one such attribution — usually the conventions of a modern translation read with modern background assumptions — so it has no better claim to literalness than Origen's spiritual reading does. (6) Since "allegorical" is defined by contrast with "literal," the contrast ends up with no work to do, and the distinction collapses. I would most like to keep the idea that there is a real difference between reading a text in its own terms and reading it against or beyond them; if that requires giving up (1) as stated, or giving up some later step, I want to know exactly what the choice costs. Where does the argument go wrong? Please say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
