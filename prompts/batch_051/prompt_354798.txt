Before I retired I spent my career in population genetics, working mostly on small, inbred populations; since then I have been working my way through the philosophy of biology and the adjacent cultural evolution literature, including the older debate about memes. An old reflex from that career keeps firing, and I cannot decide whether it has caught something real or is just an occupational tic. In genetics, non-random association between alleles at different loci—linkage disequilibrium—has at least two quite different sources. One is physical linkage: loci that sit close together on a chromosome are inherited together because recombination rarely cuts between them. The other is epistasis: when the fitness of an allele at one locus depends on which allele sits at another locus, selection itself can build associations between unlinked loci. Both produce the same snapshot statistic, but they differ in their dynamics and in what dissolves the association. Cultural traits exhibit the snapshot statistic constantly: a dietary rule travels with a cosmology, a metrical form with a theology, a political identification with a package of aesthetic defaults, often with no obvious functional link among them. The memeticist's gloss is that these are memes riding on the same cultural chromosome. But culture has no chromosome, and no physical strand ties the taboo to the myth. I genuinely cannot tell whether I have found a structural identity—one that would let the machinery of replicators, recombination, and epistasis do real explanatory work on culture—or whether two very different systems can produce statistical association for unrelated reasons, in which case the population-genetic vocabulary would be a metaphor with misleading joints.

My question is therefore whether this parallel is deep or superficial, and I would like the reasoning made explicit rather than settled by fiat. In particular: under what conditions does treating co-occurring cultural variants as being in linkage disequilibrium require particulate, faithfully copied replicators and a genuine cultural analogue of a chromosome, and under what conditions could the same clustering instead be produced by selection on whole persons for psychological coherence, by selection on entire packages of variants, or by differences in transmission fidelity alone? Where exactly does the analogy break, and what in-principle evidence from the cultural record—transmission chains, recombination across communities, the fate of hybrid packages when communities mix—would distinguish true cultural linkage from mere associative clustering among ideas? Finally, if cultural variants turn out to replicate only loosely, partially, and with heavy blending, does the word "meme" survive as anything more than a metaphor, or does the population-genetic translation end up borrowing the prestige of a mature science without importing the conditions that made its formalism meaningful?
