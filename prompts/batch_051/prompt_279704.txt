I've just spent about three hours arguing with a mate about whether expecting something can change what you actually see or hear, and we got absolutely nowhere. He's on the predictive processing / free energy side: the brain's always churning out top-down guesses about its input, perception basically is the winning guess, so a strong enough expectation should genuinely change the percept itself. I was defending the older bottom-up story, where the stimulus gets assembled into a percept from low-level features upwards, and expectations only shape what you do with the percept afterwards — what you attend to, how you report it, how surprised you are. He kept accusing me of explaining away every example by saying I'd only changed what I said I saw, not what I actually saw.

So now I want a thought experiment built specifically to pull those two apart. Make it an ordinary everyday situation — no labs, no scanners, no lesions, no drugs — and keep the scenario to one paragraph. There should be a person with a strong expectation about an ambiguous or degraded stimulus (listening for a name in static, spotting someone in a crowd, whatever). Then add a twist that forces the two views to give different predictions about something the person says or does, not just about how vivid or confident they feel. The contrast I'm after: the predictive-processing view should predict a change in the person's actual discrimination of the stimulus — they genuinely pick out the expected thing better — while the bottom-up view should predict only a shift in the responses they're inclined to give: same underlying signal, biased threshold. And please don't make the twist settle things by introspection.

After the scenario, walk through what each view says and why, and identify the exact feature that does the seperating. Then tell me: if I remove that feature, does the case collapse back into one where both views predict the same behaviour? And how would a defender of whichever side comes out badly attack the case — what would they say is doing the smuggled work, and is there a tweak that blocks that objection?
