For a small forecasting and decision-making meetup, I wanna put together a decision tree that sorts people into positions about when epistemic modesty actually requires deferring to the outside view and when an inside view gets to override a base rate. The participants are mostly engineers, doctors, and founders who have to make singular predictions under real money and career risk, and they keep overtrusting their private stories. What I want is a classification instrument, not an essay: a tree whose branch points are concrete questions about the case, phrased so someone can answer them without knowing which position theyre being sorted toward. It should start from a generic situation: a person has a detailed causal model of the specific case, and there is a well-sourced base rate from some reference class that points the other way. Each branch should be a question about that situation, not about whether they say they believe in base rates.

The tree should include at least the following branch questions somewhere:

- Is the reference class one you would still defend after the project failed?
- Is that reference class actually homogeneous enough that a base rate means something specific?
- Can you say in advance what mechanism makes your case a right-tail exception, rather than just telling a plausible story?
- Does your claimed private information also require making the same exception for lots of other members of the class?
- If the situation were anonymized so you didnt know it was yours, would you still take the inside-view bet?
- If you are wrong, are the costs roughly symmetric, or does one direction ruin you?
- Does the base rate already include people who had the same kind of private information, where you only know your own side of it?
- If this were a lottery ticket with the same implied edge, would you buy a large stake?

The leaves should include, at minimum: hard outside view, base-rate-disciplined inside view, reference-class pluralism, calibration-trumps-story, exception-claiming, and asymmetric prudence. You can rename these or add others if the logic forces it. Mark any empty combinations of answers and say briefly whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cant really separate.

Use these running examples, not as new branches but as cases to show the tree in action: a founder who believes her startup will beat the 90% first-startup failure rate; a student at a top school who predicts he will finish in the top tenth of his class when the base rate for any given student is 10%; an engineer who predicts her novel launch vehicle will hit its schedule even though no comparable vehicle has; and a doctor who predicts a particular patient will beat a 15% five-year survival rate. The instrument should sort people by what they would actually bet on in those cases, not by how they describe themselves.

Please write the answer in American English.
