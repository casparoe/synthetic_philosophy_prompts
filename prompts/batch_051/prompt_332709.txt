I've been reading the chapters of Darwin's Descent of Man in which he tries to naturalize the moral sense that earlier writers had treated as a divinely implanted monitor, and at the same time working through some material on reinforcement learning, and the two have produced a parallel I cannot decide is a real structural identity or just two stories that happen to use similar words. Darwin's account of conscience, as I understand it, has a distinctive temporal shape. Some motives — the social instincts, sympathy, the desire for the good opinion of others — are persistent and comparatively steady features of the organism's standing orientation toward its group. Other motives — hunger, lust, sudden fear, anger — are episodic: at the moment of action they are far stronger than the social instincts, and then they subside. The result is that conscience is typically not what restrains the impulse in advance. The impulsive motive wins at the moment, and only afterward, once it has cooled and the enduring social instincts reassert themselves, does the agent compare the two states in memory and feel remorse. On this picture conscience is largely a post hoc verdict delivered by a slower, more durable drive against a faster, transient one, rather than a separate faculty that intervenes before the fact.

In reinforcement learning there is a division of labor that seems to me to have the same shape. A critic learns to estimate the long-run value of states and actions from many past trajectories and changes only gradually, while an actor selects actions rapidly in response to the current state. The actor can repeatedly take actions that the critic evaluates badly, and the mismatch shows up afterward as an error signal or a correction rather than as an ex ante veto. So the mapping I am tempted by is this: Darwin's enduring social instincts correspond to the critic, the episodic appetites correspond to the actor, and remorse corresponds to the delayed error signal. The underlying idea would be that any control system combining durable evaluation with rapid reactive selection will inevitably develop something conscience-like, a slow judge that catches up with fast action only after the fact.

Is this a genuine common structure, or does the analogy break down because Darwin's social instincts are themselves plastic, acquired partly through habit, language, and reflection, and because a critic neither reflects on itself nor suffers? If I wanted to test whether the two really are cases of one underlying structure, what exactly would the mapping have to preserve, and what is one concrete thing the parallel predicts about one side that turns out false — and what would that false prediction tell us about where the analogy stops being illuminating?
