Here's a parallel I've been chewing on and can't tell if its deep or just a pun on the word "shortcut." In computational irreducibility, there are systems where the only way to find out the state at step n is to actually run the system through every step; no compressed description of the system lets you leap ahead, because the fastest description just is the evolution itself. That sounds to me exactly like the line you get about consciousness: no amount of physical or functional knowledge lets you know what an experience is like — you have to be the system doing the experiencing, not just hold a description of it. Both seem to say that a certain kind of information can't be gotten by any shorter path than instantiating it. So my question: is this a real identity of structure, or am I pattern-matching? If it's real, does Mary-the-color-scientist's missing knowledge have the same status as my missing knowledge of what some cellular automaton will do at step a million before I run it? And if it breaks, where exactly? My worry is that in the computational case the output is public and checkable once produced, while in the consciousness case what's produced isn't a public output but a private "what it's like," and that might be a different kind of limit entirely — but I can't make that worry precise. Is the parallel one where results could transfer in either direction, or should I drop it?
