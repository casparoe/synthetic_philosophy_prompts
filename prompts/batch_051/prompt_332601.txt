I got into an argument with a friend who works in a genocide archive, and she said something that's been bugging me: our legal systems seem to have a full-blown memory policy, but nobody can say what principle it reflects. On one side there's a huge apparatus of remembering — state-funded memorials and museums for atrocities, school curricula built around teaching the history of the Holocaust and other massacres, national remembrance days, laws criminalizing genocide denial, and occasional prosecutions or truth commissions for crimes committed decades earlier. On the other side the same societies keep deliberately building in forgetting: statutes of limitations that make old wrongs unprosecutable, amnesties after political transitions, sealed or expunged criminal records, and data-protection rules that grant people a right to have old information about them erased. Its not just a pragmatic compromise with scarce resources; the forgetting looks intentional and is treated as a matter of dignity.

I want to know what implicit theory of memory and obligation to the past this whole pattern presupposes. Does it presuppose that we owe the past something — a duty to remember at least some events — while also presupposing that forgetting can be permissible or even required under some principle? If so, what principle could draw the line: the scale or moral character of the wrong, whether the victims' dignity is still at stake, whether the memory is still doing social work? Or does the pattern only make sense on a consequentialist view where both remembering and forgetting are tools for present social health, with no independent duty to the dead at all? And which rival views does the practice quietly rule out — a pure duty-to-forget view, a pure duty-to-remember view, or something else?

Please act as a professional philosopher working on the ethics of memory and transitional justice. Start by brainstorming several candidate principles, then settle on the one that best explains the whole pattern, and be clear about what the practices strictly presuppose versus what they merely happen to fit. Write your answer in American English.
