I've been sitting in on an intro logic course as a TA, and we're closing the unit on truth this week. I keep coming back to the liar sentence — "this sentence is false" — and the way any answer immediately flips into the other answer: argue it's true and it must be false; argue it's false and it must be true. I want to end the last section with a poem that makes people feel that loop instead of just hearing it described.

So: could you write a villanelle where the two refrains are the two verdicts, something like "it must be true" and "it must be false" (tweak the wording for meter if you need), so the form itself keeps reversing exactly the way the reasoning does? The middle tercets could gesture at the standard moves — the strengthened liar, the idea that the sentence fails to say anything at all, the dialetheist thought that it's both true and false, and the contextualist thought that it's true at one level and false at another — and each time one refrain should hand things over to the other. The final two lines should set the refrains next to eachother without resolving them, since the whole point is that the loop doesn't bottom out. I'd like it to be accurate enough that a logician would recognize the moves, and good enough that someone who's never heard of the liar just feels the vertigo. Keep it an actual villanelle, not free verse with a few repeated phrases.
