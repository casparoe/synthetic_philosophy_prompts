Here's an asymmetry I keep noticing. If someone gets their politics entirely from a few hyper-partisan podcasts and treats mainstream outlets as compromised, we say they're in an echo chamber, and maybe that they've failed epistemically. But if someone gets their science entirely from a few mainstream journals and treats, say, creationist or anti-vax sources as not worth reading, we call them well-informed. Both people are filtering out sources on the basis of a standing judgment about which ones are trustworthy. Is there actually a principled epistemic difference between these cases—something about the filter's reliability, or how it reacts to new evidence—or is the asymmetry mostly a matter of whose filters we happen to share? I want the strongest justification you can give for treating the two differently, and an honest verdict on whether it survives contact with harder cases, like a person who filters based on a view you think is false but they arrived at carefully.
