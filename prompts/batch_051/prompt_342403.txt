I am a distributed-systems engineer, and a conversation with a friend about grief — he described occasionally taking his late wife's side in a small argument by anticipating exactly how she would have responded — has pushed me toward a view I used to find sentimental. I now think a human self is not a single indivisible thing locked to one brain but a pattern, a kind of process, that can run at reduced resolution on other substrates, including other people's brains; roughly the line of thought people associate with Douglas Hofstadter. In my own terms: my "I" is like a program whose execution is ordinarily bound to one physical machine but whose state and control flow can be partially replicated elsewhere. When someone knows me well, they run a coarse-grained emulator of some of my characteristic loops, and after I die something of my point of view keeps executing inside them, at various fidelities. Survival, on this view, is a matter of degree and is not anchored to any single hardware instance.

I want to stress-test this before I lean on it further. What would count against it? I do not mean only the obvious reply that a copy is not the original; I want the full map of exposure. Which possible cases, introspective or neuroscientific observations, or results from elsewhere would a committed proponent of this position have to concede count against it, and which of those would be fatal rather than merely awkward? What does the view predict that its rivals do not — about what it is like to lose someone, about the sense in which someone who knew me well partly survives me, about what a high-fidelity recording or a future simulation of my mental state would and would not be — and which of those predictions, if they failed, would bring the whole picture down? I would also like a frank assessment of whether the view, as I have stated it, has already been insulated into unfalsifiability by the elasticity of the words "pattern," "fidelity," and "partially," and whether that would be a defect in a view of this kind or an acceptable price for a graded notion of survival. I personally would be most moved by a demonstration that the difference between a copy of me and a mere model of me is either doing real causal work or is arbitrary.
