I just came from a long argument with a friend about whether predictive processing is a theory or a piece of motivated mathematics. He kept saying the free energy principle is compatible with anything and therefore explains nothing; I kept insisting it makes real commitments about neural implementation. I want your number, not a it-depends. Please give me your credence that the following claim is true: perception, action, and learning in biological brains are substantially implemented as approximate Bayesian inference—perception as inverting a hierarchical generative model, action as active inference, learning as updating the model—and that this is an empirically contentful, falsifiable account rather than a flexible redescription. Decompose the number: at least, the probability that perception is fundamentally inferential rather than, say, feedforward feature detection or a dynamical-systems story; the probability, conditional on that, that the brain implements something like hierarchical predictive coding rather than an alternative Bayesian scheme such as neural sampling or direct discriminative inference; the probability, conditional on those, that action is active inference rather than a separate control process; and the probability that the framework has enough empirical constraints to be testable at all. Give a rough probability with one line of justification for each term, then show how you combine them. Finally, tell me which term is doing most of the work in your result, what evidence or argument would move it, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
