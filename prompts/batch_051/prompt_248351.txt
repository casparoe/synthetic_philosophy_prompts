hey — i'm a novelist, and my current book has a blackmail subplot. Three characters end up on different sides of it: one thinks the blackmailer should be prosecuted, one thinks blackmail should basically be legal, and a third thinks the law's inconsistency is the real issue but still wants the blackmailer punished. I keep writing them as just vibes — "it feels extortionate," "it's two rights making a wrong" — and I want each of them to actually hold a distinct, worked-out position on the paradox of blackmail (the puzzle where threatening to reveal a true secret you learned lawfully, and demanding money for silence, can each be permissible on their own, but combining them is a crime). so what I'd like is a decision-tree-ish taxonomy of the real positions on why blackmail is wrong (or not), built for a non-philosopher who has to turn these positions into believable dialogue.

make the tree branch on claims, not on labels. the questions i at least care about are: whether the wrong lives in the threatened disclosure, in the demanded payment, or in the conditional link between them; whether the victim has something like an ownership right over their own secrecy; whether the blackmailer owes an independent duty of confidentiality (or even a duty to disclose, when the secret is a serious crime); whether a threat to do something perfectly permissible can still count as coercion. each question should be answerable by someone who just read the case, without already knowing which position it leads to. every leaf should get a descriptive name (real academic label if there is one, otherwise just make one up, or say "unnamed" if nobody defends it) and one sentence on the most awkward commitment the position has to live with.

please also mark the combinations of answers that nobody actually occupies, and say briefly whether each one is empty because its incoherent, unattractive, or just never explored. and flag any pairs of positions the tree can't really separate rather than forcing a clean branch.

to keep it grounded, run at least these three cases through the resulting tree: (1) the standard case — someone threatens to tell a spouse about an affair unless paid; (2) the secret was learned under a professional confidence, say the blackmailer used to be the victim's therapist or a lawyer's clerk; (3) the threatened disclosure is going to the police with a truthful account of a serious crime the victim committed. Then end by telling me which leaf each of my three characters probably lands on from the little sketches i gave, and if the tree leaves one of them ambiguous, say what single follow-up question would settle it. keep the whole thing something i could print and tape next to my desk — two pages max.
