Here's one I keep flip-flopping on. In John's Gospel, after the resurrection, the disciples fish all night on the Sea of Tiberias and catch nothing; Jesus tells them to cast the net on the right side of the boat; they do, and the haul is huge. The King James Version gives John 21:11 as:

"Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken."

So: one hundred and fifty-three fish, and the text pointedly says the net didn't break. I want an actual number from you, not "it depends." What's your credence that the author of John 21 intended the number 153 to carry symbolic significance, rather than it being a remembered count, a rough-sounding exact detail, or a number whose meaning got read into it later? Assume "intended symbolic significance" means the author chose or preserved the number because he thought it meant something beyond "that's how many there were."

Please decompose it. I can see at least these sub-questions: (1) how likely is it that the author/redactor of the Fourth Gospel encodes symbolic numbers in ordinary-sounding narrative details, as opposed to only in obviously visionary or parabolic bits; (2) conditional on that practice, how likely is it that this particular number was chosen for its properties; (3) how much evidential weight should we put on the fact that 153 is the 17th triangular number and the sum of the integers 1 through 17; (4) how heavily should we discount later patristic and medieval numerological readings as post hoc pattern-hunting; (5) whether the surrounding context (the miraculous catch, Peter, the unbroken net) raises or lowers the prior. Give a rough probability for each, say how you combined them, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to. Also: if two readers land at 0.8 and 0.3 on this, where is their disagreement most likely actually located? And what evidence would move you the most?
