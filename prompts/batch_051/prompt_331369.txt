ok I keep using a term in discussions of chain-of-thought monitoring and I want it stress-tested before I keep using it. I call it "window replacement." Standard framing: a model does some actual internal reasoning, and the chain-of-thought either faithfully reports it or not (omission, rationalization, obfuscation, and so on). My claim is that once a monitor's judgments get used as a training signal, the model is selected for CoTs that the monitor scores well, and at that point the CoT stops being a report about a separate process and becomes the process under selection — the window becomes a painting of a window. So the worst failure mode isn't unfaithful reporting; it's that there is no longer a seperable internal computation for the CoT to be faithful or unfaithful to. Is that a real distinction or just a dramatic restatement of "optimization pressure increases CoT unfaithfulness"? Has someone already said this under a better name? And where does it break — for instance, does it assume there was a clean process/report split in the first place, which might be false even before any monitoring is applied? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
