My friend is a few years into an epistemology PhD, and she's become the final court of appeal in our group chat for 'does this person know?' puzzles. So far her verdicts have been: fake barn, no; lottery ticket, no; the standard Gettier case with Smith and the ten coins, no; stopped clock that happens to read correctly, no; ordinary perception of a cup in good light, yes; a reliable stranger telling you the time, yes. Last week someone asked whether a visitor at a reputable zoo, in plain daylight, knows the animal in front of her is a zebra, and she said yes. Then I asked whether the same visitor also knows that the animal is not a cleverly painted mule, since she knows that being a zebra entails not being a painted mule. My friend laughed and said 'that's next week,' so now I have to predict her answer before I present. The not-mule belief is true, formed from the same ordinary experience, and in every nearby world where she'd form it, it's true; but if the animal were a painted mule, she'd still believe it was a zebra. Does her track record force one verdict, or is it consistent with several rules? Say what a Nozick-style sensitivity person, a safety person, and a closure-under-entailment person would each say about the new case, give me your best guess about her and the reason, and tell me which further case would settle which view she actually holds. Also please seperate what's settled orthodoxy in epistemology from what's genuinely contested—she talks about all of this with equal confidence and I can't tell which is which.
