I want a scenario engineered so that two views of time give opposite verdicts on the same persistence claim, in the direction that makes the Dōgen-style view sound unreasonable at first and then hard to dismiss.

Here are the two views. The container view says time is a background medium things move through, so one and the same object can exist at two times and remain itself; "the dough I mixed is the bread I'm eating" is literally true. Dōgen's seeing-time (uji) says time is not a container, and each moment is a dharma-position: firewood is firewood in its moment, ash is ash in its moment, each has before and after, but the before and after are cut off, so nothing single travels from firewood to ash. Persistence on that view is a usefull convention, not a fact about some substratum underneath the moments.
