I'm working on a piece for the ideas section about how rational people should change their minds when the evidence doesn't come wrapped in a clean yes-or-no observation. I've got a rough set of notes for the essay and I'd love a gut-check before I pitch it.

The setup is Bayesian: if you learn E for certain, your new credence in H should just be your old conditional credence P(H|E). Richard Jeffrey's whole move was to handle uncertain evidence, where an experience only shifts your probabilities across some partition — say a glimpse in bad light moves your confidence that the thing is a rose from 0.3 to 0.7 without your becoming certain of any precise description. His rule says: keep your old conditional probabilities given each cell of the partition fixed, then reweight everything by your new probabilities for the cells. What I want to build the essay around is that this makes the update weirdly partition-dependent. Same experience, same new probabilities on the cells you consciously noticed, but if the space is carved up more finely — rose vs. tulip vs. daffodil vs. something-weedlike — the posterior for a downstream claim depends on old conditionals inside the finer cells that the experience never touched. Two reasonable partitions can nudge the same headline credence in opposite directions, and the rule itself doesn't say which partition is the real input. My argument is that this pushes uncertain evidence back toward the very thing Jeffrey was trying to escape: the update only behaves properly when the experience comes with enough content to single out a canonical partition, and that starts to look like a proposition you learn for sure. In other words, either Jeffrey conditionalization is incomplete as a rule, or it's certain evidence in disguise.

I'd appreciate comments on a couple of things. First, is my summary of the formalism actually right, or am I overstating how bad the partiton problem is — is there a standard constraint or theorem that closes the gap? Second, does the dilemma feel fair, or is it a strawman of what Jeffrey and radical probabilists actually wanted (maybe they'd be fine saying the partition is supplied by experience itself and isn't something a rule should fix in advance)? And third, for a general reader, which part is the hook and what everyday example would make it land without burying them in algebra in the first few hundred words?
