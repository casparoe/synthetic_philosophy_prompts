I'm trying to sort out when something counts as a performance of a musical work, and where recordings fit, because my case judgments are pulling in different directions. My verdicts: (1) an orchestra playing Beethoven's Fifth from the score, mistakes and all, is a performance of the Fifth; (2) a MIDI file of the Fifth rendered through a synthesizer is not a performance, even though it presents the right notes in the right order; (3) a jazz quartet's reharmonized, heavily improvised take on "Autumn Leaves" is a performance of that song; (4) a singer who keeps only the words of "Hallelujah" and sets them to a new tune is still, barely, performing that song; (5) two realizations of a sparse graphic score that share almost no sonic content are both performances of the same work; (6) a pop record assembled from dozens of studio takes, never played straight through by a human being, is itself the work, and later live versions are performances of it. I doubt one principle gets all six: if following the score is what matters, the MIDI case should count; if sonic similarity is what matters, the graphic-score case shouldn't. What principle or small set of principles best captures these verdicts? Which of my verdicts, if any, would it overturn, and what would it say about a case I cannot settle: a DJ who plays someone else's finished record to a room, is that a performance of the work? Please also tell me what would most likely change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different overall account, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
