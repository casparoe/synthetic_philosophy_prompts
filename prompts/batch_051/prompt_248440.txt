Write a sorting quiz for a first-year epistemology tutorial, titled "Do practical stakes change whether someone knows?" Its purpose is to sort respondents into three positions — classical invariantism, subject-sensitive invariantism (the pragmatic encroachment view), and attributor contextualism — purely from their verdicts on concrete cases, without ever asking which doctrine they endorse. Use six to eight short scenarios. In each, the subject's evidence is exactly as strong as in the others, and the only things that vary are (i) whether the subject of the knowledge claim faces high or low practical stakes and (ii) whether the claim is made by that subject in the first person or by an outsider. Each scenario should ask only whether a sentence of the form "S knows that p" is true or false. Vary the two stake factors independently, and include at least two third-person cases where the outsider's stakes diverge from the subject's, since first-person cases alone cannot separate contextualism from subject-sensitive invariantism. Provide a scoring key that maps each response pattern onto a position. Before assigning patterns, lay out the logical space generated by the two factors, state which answer combinations the three positions occupy, note any plausible combinations no live position occupies and briefly say whether that is because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate.
