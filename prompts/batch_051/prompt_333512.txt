You are a professional philosopher of law with expertise in analytic jurisprudence and the Hart–Fuller debate. I want you to audit the following set of my own commitments for internal consistency. Begin by brainstorming possible tensions, work through the commitments pairwise, and separate genuine tensions from merely apparent ones; do not lecture me on background.

My commitments:

1. I am a Hartian positivist: the ultimate criteria of legal validity in any legal system are fixed by a conventional rule of recognition, understood as a social practice among officials.
2. I accept the separation thesis: there is no necessary conceptual connection between law and morality, and a norm can be legally valid even when it is grossly unjust.
3. I am an inclusive positivist: a rule of recognition may itself include moral criteria of validity, for instance a constitutional clause that makes statutes violating human dignity void.
4. The existence of a rule of recognition requires officials to adopt an internal point of view toward it, but that attitude may be a detached professional acceptance rather than a moral endorsement.
5. I find Fuller persuasive that genuine legality depends on the eight desiderata—generality, promulgation, prospectivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility of obedience, constancy over time, and congruence between official action and declared rule—and that these amount to an internal morality of law, so that a regime systematically flouting them is not running a legal system.
6. I also think Hart was right about wicked legal systems: a statute's radical injustice is a strong reason to condemn or disobey it, but it is not by itself a reason to deny that the statute was legally valid.

For each pair of commitments, say whether it is a genuine tension, a merely apparent tension, or no tension at all. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one state what would have to be revised and at what philosophical cost. Finally, identify the single revision that would resolve the most while sacrificing the least.
