Recently a friend who works in policy summarized the debate over how states should regulate prostitution like this:

'The Nordic model starts from the fact that prostitution is a form of violence against women. The buyer's demand creates the market, so the state criminalizes buying while decriminalizing selling, and funds exit services. That makes it more coherent than legalization, which tells women that selling access to their bodies is just another job and licenses brothels. Decriminalization sounds gentler, but it is effectively legalization under another name: it normalizes the industry and lets third parties profit from other people's prostitution while the state pretends to step back.'

I think the description of the Nordic model is roughly right, though its opening "fact" is really one contested premise in the debate rather than a neutral observation, and I know New Zealand's decriminalization model is not simply legalization renamed. I would like a careful accuracy audit. Please work through the summary claim by claim and take a definite position on each: accurate, a fair-but-partial simplification, or a misrepresentation. In particular, separate legalization from decriminalization as distinct legal-institutional options, and separate a model's coherence with its own stated values from the empirical question of what it produces. Then say outright whether the summary's concluding comparison of the Nordic model with legalization survives once the conflation between decriminalization and legalization is corrected, and where a defender of New Zealand-style decriminalization would object. I am asking for a committed verdict with reasons, not a survey of ways one might look at it.
