Singapore regulates coexistence across racial and religious lines through two conspicuous kinds of policy. Public-housing quotas cap how many flats any one ethnic group may occupy in a given block or neighbourhood, so that no district becomes an ethnic enclave; criminal law punishes speech that wounds racial or religious feelings. Both are officially justified by the need to preserve racial and religious harmony. Yet the same state readily accepts large inequalities in market outcomes and treats economic life as a sphere of competition. What political value does this pattern presuppose? Does it presuppose that harmony or order is a primary good in its own right, in the manner of Confucian or conservative thought, or would a liberal pluralist license some or all of the same policies as instrumental protections of equal citizenship and public order? Reconstruct the implicit theory, separating what the policies strictly presuppose from what they merely fit well with, and say whether the housing quotas, the speech restrictions, and the economic competition presuppose one consistent idea of harmony or several. Then state what would have to change in practice if the presupposed value were replaced by liberal pluralism rather than harmony. Finally, say which consideration would most likely lead you to revise your verdict about what the pattern really presupposes, or where your reasoning is least confident.
