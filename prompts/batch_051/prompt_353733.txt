I'm writing a novel and I need your help with a piece of conceptual scaffolding before I draft the next chapter. My protagonist is a doctor who develops something called pain asymbolia after a minor stroke: she can still say where a pain is and how strong it is, but it doesn't bother her anymore. No wincing, no pulling away, no dread of the next twinge. She knows her hand is on a hot pan because it hurts, but she watches it burn the way you'd watch a weather report. The condition is real, but I'll explain it in the book so the reader doesn't need to know beforehand. Here's what I want from you.

I have three characters who each need a different, thinkable position on what makes pain bad: the doctor I just described, a pain researcher who ran studies on her, and a philosopher she meets in a support group. I don't want the two stock positions everyone reaches for — pain as a mere signal versus pain as intrinsically awful. I want the real logical space between and around those, including maybe a position nobody actually defends, since the philosopher might be just odd enough to hold it.

What I need is a taxonomy in the form of a decision tree, built from the questions first rather than from a list of labels. Each branch should be a concrete question a person could answer without knowing philosophy, and each leaf should be a named or clearly described position with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Aim for six to eight leaves, but don't pad the space to hit that number. Work the actual positions into the space, but flag any leaves your tree can't seperate from each other. Also tell me about unoccupied combinations: is nobody there because the position is incoherent, too ugly to hold, or just never been explored? The cases you should use as the diagnostic material are pain asymbolia, a morphine patient who says the pain is still there but no longer unpleasant, and a person born unable to feel pain at all. The distinctions I especially care about are whether pain's badness is inside the sensation or in our response to it, and whether something can be bad for a person without her disliking it. Positions that should show up somewhere include something like: the sensation itself is the badness; the badness is a separate evaluative response; pain is a command to avoid; pain is only bad if judged bad; and some deflationary view that says the whole question is badly posed.

Ordinarily I'd accept a branching diagram or a table for this, but I keep my novel notes in a single running document, so please write it as continuous prose, like a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just walk me down the tree in sentences.
