I sit on the advisory board of an aquaculture certification scheme and we're about to rule on a new application. We don't have a written general welfare policy, so we're supposed to stay consistent with our own past decisions. The simplified track record: we approved, with conditions, electrical stunning of farmed Atlantic salmon before slaughter; we rejected routine eyestalk ablation of broodstock shrimp despite the big fecundity gains, calling it needless mutilation without pain relief; we allowed seine-net crowding of rainbow trout at harvest only if killing was by percussive stun rather than asphyxiation on ice; and we rejected CO2 stunning of salmon as aversive and unreliable. The new application is from a shrimp processor that wants to kill whiteleg shrimp by dropping them directly into an ice slurry, no prior electrical stunning; they say this is standard practice in the industry. What should we do if we're being consistent with ourselves? Tell me which old decision is the closest analog, what principle a yes or a no would commit us to, and whether the track record really settles it. Also tell me how confident you are in your answer and which part you're least sure about.
