Here's an asymmetry that keeps bugging me. We treat remembering as a duty—memorial days, "never forget," people being furious when you forget a promise or an anniversary—and forgetting as a moral failure. But we also treat forgetting as a mercy or a kind of health: we say "I wish I could forget," we tell people stuck on old wounds to move on, we even praise someone for finally putting a terrible memory down. Nietzsche, in On the Use and Abuse of History for Life, writes: "He wonders also about himself, that he cannot learn to forget, but hangs on the past: however far or fast he run, that chain runs with him." If he's right that the past is a chain and that forgetting is something like a capacity we need, then forgetting shouldn't just be blameworthy. But we do blame it all the time. Is there a defensible line between the forgetting we condemn and the forgetting we recommend, or is this just an inconsistency we live with? Give me the strongest principled line you can, then say what would change your mind about it and where the argument is most likely to fail.
