I keep going back and forth on semantic externalism and I want you to referee it. The theory strikes me as right on reflection: what my words pick out isnt settled just by what's in my skull — facts about my environment, what I've causally bumped into, and which experts I'd defer to all do part of the fixing. The standard Twin Earth story makes this pretty hard to deny. A molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me on a planet where the watery-looking stuff is XYZ rather than H2O has exactly the same neural goings-on when he says "water," but his word seems to track the XYZ stuff, not H2O, because that's what he's been swimming in and drinking his whole life.

But then there's the intuition I can't shake. Picture my duplicate right now, believing in his head, in exactly the brain state I'm in, that water is refreshing. It feels undeniable that we believe the same thing and mean the same thing by "water." The idea that our minds could be identical down to the last molecule and yet our thoughts have different contents strikes me as almost spooky. Content shouldn't float outside the skin like that.

So I'm torn. Is the intuition just an artifact of how I'm imagining the case — I picture his mind from my own standpoint and automatically import my own stuff into it — and should it be debunked and dropped? Or is the theory overreaching, sliding from a claim about how we'd describe his word from the outside, or what he'd rationally defer to if he found out, to a claim about what's literally in his head? Or is the honest move to split content into two kinds — whatever is shared between us and whatever isn't — and say the two sides are just talking past each other? Give me the strongest version of each side, then tell me which you'd actually give up, and what would justify each of the moves rather than just picking a side.
