A friend runs an online comunity of a few thousand people who do lots of anonymous one-off deals with each other that have the usual prisoner's-dilemma structure: mutual cooperation is best jointly, but in a one-shot each person does best by defecting no matter what the other does. Right now they use a public blacklist, which helps a bit but not much. The new proposal is a voucher-bond system. Before you can enter a deal as a stranger, an established member has to vouch for you by putting a bond in escrow, sized at twice the one-shot temptation payoff. If you cooperate, the bond is returned and the voucher gets a small fee plus a reliability point from the community fund. If you defect against a cooperator, the bond goes to the victim, the voucher loses a reliability point, and you get blacklisted; if you both defect, the bond goes to the fund. A voucher who has two vouchees defect within a rolling year loses the right to vouch for two years. Vouching is voluntary, the voucher can charge the vouchee whatever the market will bear, and people nobody will vouch for can still transact with each other but are flagged as unvouched and the other side can refuse. Treat it as a mechanism, not as a moral proposal: walk me through who gets vouched and at what price, what this does to cooperation compared to the blacklist, where it gets gamed first (collusion? extortion? voucher capture? something weirder?), what happens to the unvouched, and whether the failure modes can be patched without killing the scheme. Wherever you land, say how confident you are and flag the single point you're least sure about.
