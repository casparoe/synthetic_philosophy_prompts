Write a parody of a corporation's internal Ethics Help Desk FAQ, about 600 words, for a firm whose employee handbook is rigorously deontological. The handbook categorically forbids certain act-types — lying, breaking a promise, falsifying a record, using any person as a mere means — and instructs employees never to perform such an act even to prevent several other people from performing that same type of act. The jokes must come from accurate moral philosophy rather than mere silliness: build them from agent-relative constraints, the doing/allowing distinction, and the system's refusal to aggregate rule-violations into one bad to be minimized. Include a worked exchange in which an employee could stop five colleagues from falsifying safety records only by falsifying one record herself, and have the Help Desk answer with a straight face, arguing only from the code's own rules and the deontological resources named above. The piece should make the reader feel the paradox of deontology — why forbid one violation when that prohibition leaves several identical violations unstoppable? — while never using the term. Keep the voice deadpan corporate legalese throughout.
