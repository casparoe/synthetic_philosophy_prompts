In the Definitions of Isaac Newton's *Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy* (Motte's translation), Newton writes: "A stone, whirled about in a sling, endeavours to recede from the hand that turns it; and by that endeavour, distends the sling, and that with so much the greater force, as it is revolved with the greater velocity, and as soon as ever it is let go, flies away." After a long argument with a friend about whether this passage shows that rotation is absolute, I want to know whether my verdicts on the following cases hang together, and what principle, if any, captures them.

My verdicts are these. In the sling case, I accept that the stone's distension is a real effect and that it shows the stone is genuinely rotating, not merely changing its relation to my hand. In the bucket case, I also accept that the water's concavity is a real effect, and I do not think it is caused by the water's motion relative to the bucket, since the water is flat when there is relative motion between water and bucket, and concave when there is none. For two globes joined by a cord in an otherwise empty universe, revolving about their common centre, I find myself saying the cord would be taut, and that the tension shows they really rotate; but when my friend pressed me on what this "really" amounts to if there is nothing else, I could not say. For a single body in an otherwise empty universe, I think there is no fact about whether it is moving uniformly or at rest. For the hypothesis that the entire material universe is rotating, I am unsure: my first instinct is that there would be nothing for it to be rotating relative to, and therefore no fact, but I also suspect a bucket inside that universe would still show the same concavity. I want a principle that tells me which of these two thoughts wins.

Is there a coherent account of rotation and space behind these verdicts, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Answer in British English.
