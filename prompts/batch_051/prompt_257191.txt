I'm a software engineer, not a lawyer or a philosopher, and I sit on the platform policy committee at a company that runs a cloud API. We have to decide a case that has split the committee down the middle, and I've been asked to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting.

The facts are these. A three-person startup has run a production service on our API for three years under our standard developer agreement. Eight months ago we added a rule prohibiting clients from caching our API responses and serving them downstream without a separate distribution license. The change was posted in our public changelog and inserted into section 14.2 of the developer agreement, but we did not require developers to re-acknowledge the terms, and the change was never surfaced in the developer dashboard. The changelog is treated internally as an informational channel, not as legal notice. The startup's founder signed up under the old terms and has not revisited the legal pages since; they do not follow the changelog. Our automated compliance system flagged their service last week for exactly the prohibited caching. The default penalty is a 90-day suspension of their API key, which would take down their product and cut off their own customers.

The founder's argument is that they had no realistic way to know the rule existed: the terms they actually agreed to did not contain it, and nothing in the product told them the terms had changed. Half the committee says that is not a defense — users are responsible for the rules they operate under, and the changelog was public, so they could have checked. The other half says it is unfair to bind someone to a rule they could not realistically have learned, especially when the penalty is severe and imposed automatically.

I want the memo to recommend a concrete course of action — enforce the rule as written, waive this instance, or enforce with conditions — and to include a time frame. Please make sure it genuinely engages the strongest argument on the losing side rather than just restating the terms, and that it addresses the deeper question this keeps raising for me: under what conditions is it fair to hold a user to a rule they had no practical opportunity to learn? If the memo proposes a policy change to prevent this from recurring, it should say what the change is and when it should take effect.

Write the memo in American English.
