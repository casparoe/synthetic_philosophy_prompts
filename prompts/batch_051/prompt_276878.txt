For a graduate seminar on whether large language models understand language, I want a specification before we take sides. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of understanding must satisfy if it is to adjudicate the dispute between those who regard such systems as stochastic parrots and those who credit them with genuine understanding. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement; the list should at least cover the distinction between producing a correct answer and grasping why it is correct, the difference between passing a benchmark and using an ability beyond the conditions under which it was trained, and the possibility that understanding comes in degrees and kinds. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which merely reflect one side's commitments: a demand for phenomenal consciousness, biological embodiment, or a particular architecture may be contestable in a way that, say, distinguishing a model that generalises from a lookup table is not. Flag any subset of the desiderata that cannot plausibly be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does most to exclude either the claim that present systems understand or the claim that no artificial system could. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring particular systems. The answer should be written in British English.
