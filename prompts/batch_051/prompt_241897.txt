Here's the working definition I've been using for what it is to be incorruptible: (1) you don't change a decision for money, gifts, or favors; (2) you don't cave to threats aimed at your comfort, safety, or reputation; (3) you stick to your stated commitments and principles even when it hurts to do so. Now consider a fixer for a violent political faction who is sincere, fearless, and completely reliable, but whose two principles are just "obey the boss" and "never break a promise." He can't be bought, can't be blackmailed, and won't soften a decision out of pity, because he happens not to care much about money, his own safety, or other people. Does he count as incorruptible, or as having integrity? Work through the three criteria; where he satisfies them, say whether that's enough, and if it isn't, say whether the problem is in my definion or in the case. Then say whether this shows that incorruptibility is closer to a machine's reliability than to a virtue, or whether we should just refuse the label because his commitments are terrible.
