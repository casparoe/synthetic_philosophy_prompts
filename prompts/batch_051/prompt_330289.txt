I have recently started working on a neuromorphic computing project, and rereading the late writings in which von Neumann compared the brain to computing machinery has made me wonder how he would judge where the field has gone since his death. According to him, the nervous system is a computational system of a different design from the digital machines then being built: neural signals are all-or-none in one respect but embedded in continuous chemical and humoral processes; reliability comes from massive parallelism and redundancy rather than from precise components; it is very slow per element but fast through enormous parallelism; and its organization is statistical rather than step-by-step and mathematical. Suppose von Neumann could survey today's landscape: deep learning on ordinary stored-program hardware, analog and spiking neuromorphic chips, and whole-brain emulation projects that model neurons in detail. Which of these, if any, would he likely have seen as capturing the features of the brain he thought mattered most, and where would he likely have said the field ignored or misunderstood his point? Argue from the commitments he expressed rather than from his general reputation, and flag the places where the prediction is genuinely shaky.
