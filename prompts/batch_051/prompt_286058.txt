For a movie club I'm running, the theme is Habermas's ideal speech situation and the screening is 12 Angry Men. The theory says genuine agreement needs a coercion-free exchange where anyone can challenge any claim and the only thing that moves people is the better argument. The film looks like a perfect fit: twelve jurors talk until they are unanimous. But watching the actual deliberation, much of what shifts people is bullying, appeals to personal experience, fatigue, and a couple of holdouts just getting worn down. So the thing I want to chew on: which moments in the film would a Habermasian count as proper discourse, and which are exactly what the ideal is supposed to rule out? Would Habermas have to say the final unanimous verdict is not rationally achieved? And does the film work better as proof that the ideal speech situation is still useful as a critical standard, or as evidence that its just not the right model for how real deliberation produces agreement?
