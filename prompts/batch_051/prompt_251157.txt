Write a deadpan parody of a productivity-guru listicle, exactly five numbered rules, about satisficing and bounded rationality. The voice should be a life coach who has just discovered Herbert Simon and is now selling radical satisficing as a five-step system, without ever noticing how much optimizing he's doing to sell it. Each rule is one or two sentences; no intro and no outro. The jokes should come from getting the theory right rather than from the word itself: search costs, aspiration levels, the stopping-rule regress, why maximize-everything is not even a well-defined instruction for a bounded agent, and the difference between satisficing and settling for whatever garbage shows up first. The target is the self-optimization genre's assumption that maximizing is the only sane norm, but keep it fair—dont pretend satisficing is just an excuse for laziness. At least one rule should quietly reintroduce an optimization problem (like how to set your aspiration level), and the piece should show that satisficing is rational instead of ever announcing it.
