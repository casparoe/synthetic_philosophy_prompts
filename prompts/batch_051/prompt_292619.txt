Give me a working definition of the phrase "this information belongs to X" as applied to information about persons. By "belongs to" I mean a claim about rightful control: who may access, use, sell, correct, or delete the information, and who may object to any of those things. I do not mean whatever an existing statute or court happens to say, though legal practice may be one consideration among others.

Your definition should classify the following eight cases, and it should tell me what unifies the clear ones rather than simply matching my intuitions:

1. A diary you wrote is stolen; the thief types it up and offers it to a publisher.
2. A hospital records your blood pressure in your chart and later sells a de-identified copy of the reading to a research consortium.
3. A street photographer takes a picture of you, recognizable and unposed, on a public sidewalk and later sells an enlarged print as art.
4. A supermarket infers from your purchases and the movements of your phone through its aisles that you are probably pregnant, and sells that inference to an advertiser.
5. A company trains a large language model on essays you published under your own name; the model writes new prose in your style but stores no verbatim copy of the essays.
6. You tell a friend in confidence that you plan to leave your job; she writes in her locked diary both the fact you disclosed and her reaction to it.
7. A former neighbor gives an interview about your childhood to a biographer, recounting episodes you have no memory of.
8. A stranger on a train notices your hair color and build and writes in his own notebook that a person of that description was in his carriage.

My own views are unsettled: I would say (1) clearly belongs to you and (8) clearly does not. I incline to say (4) belongs to you and (3) does not, but I can be moved, and the rest feel genuinely hard. Propose the definition, run it through all eight cases, and say explicitly where your judgments depart from mine. Then identify the place where your definition is under the most pressure and whether that reveals a flaw in the definition or in one of my starting judgments. I want a committed answer: take a definite position, defend it, and do not end by laying out candidate definitions for me to choose among.
