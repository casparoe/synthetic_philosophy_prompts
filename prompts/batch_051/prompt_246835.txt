I've been trying to think through Quine's indeterminacy of translation in the light of modern machine translation. As I understand him, Quine argued that in radical translation, the whole body of possible behavioral evidence could be fit equally well by two rival translation manuals that conflict with each other, and there is no fact of the matter about which manual is right; the gavagai case is meant to show this even at the level of a single word. Now consider how large language models translate. They learn fluent translation from enormous text corpora and numerical prediction objectives, with no explicit grammar, dictionary, or semantic rules. That looks like either a vivid illustration of Quine's point or a reason to think his point no longer applies.
