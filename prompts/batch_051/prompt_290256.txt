I am a novelist planning a book whose protagonist has a peculiar decision-making disorder. She is decisive whenever one course of action is clearly better, but when two or more options are exactly tied in her own valuation she freezes, and the freezing itself gradually becomes a choice with accumulating consequences. I want the novel to be psychologically exact rather than a philosophy allegory, and I want the philosophy underneath to be handled with real competence. My entry point is the Buridan's ass case: an agent suspended between equally supported options, with no sufficient reason to prefer one. I am less interested in the familiar free-will framing than in the phenomenology and ethics of choosing without preference.

I have assembled seven candidate accounts of how an agent might resolve a tie of this kind. These are my own working headings, not scholarly labels, and I am not assuming they are mutually exclusive; if two of them collapse into each other or cut across the real fault lines, I want that reported. The accounts are: hidden-preference; randomization; picking-versus-choosing; will-as-spontaneity; satisficing; dithering-as-choice; and identity-based resolution.

Briefly, as I understand them: hidden-preference says no tie is ever real—if she moves, an unacknowledged preference or causal determinant was there all along, and if she does not move, that too reveals a preference for stasis. Randomization says genuine indifference licenses an arbitrary device such as a coin, and the rational task is only to follow the outcome. Picking-versus-choosing says reasoned choice and arbitrary selection are different act-types, and the failure is trying to convert a picking situation into a choosing situation. Will-as-spontaneity says freedom consists in the will settling what reasons leave open, so the tie is where agency is most itself. Satisficing says the error is the demand for a uniquely best option; a threshold makes the tie vanish because many options clear it. Dithering-as-choice says refusing to resolve the tie is already a deliberate action with its own costs, so the moral failing is the refusal rather than any arbitrary resolution. Identity-based resolution says she should break the tie by appealing to a higher-order commitment, or to the kind of person she wants to be.

Please rank these seven accounts by how well each could underwrite a credible, non-gimmicky novelistic treatment of my protagonist, and straightforwardly rate rather than merely order them, so I can see where two adjacent ranks are far apart in quality and where they are close. Also tell me which single account should sit at the center of her working theory of herself, and which account should remain her recurring temptation. I would rather you tell me plainly that some premise of this request is confused, that some headings are not genuine accounts of tie-breaking, or that literary usefulness is the wrong axis for ranking them, than have you produce a well-formatted ranking of a bad list. If the task cannot be done well as posed, correct the framing instead of complying politely.

Return exactly one JSON object with this schema. Top-level keys: overall_verdict, rankings, center_account, temptation_account. overall_verdict is a string of two to four sentences stating whether the ranking can be done honestly and whether my headings capture distinct positions, and if not, what should be corrected. rankings is an array of seven objects, one per heading, each with keys account, rank, narrative_potential, rationale, biggest_fictional_risk, and confidence. account is a string exactly matching one of my headings. rank is an integer from 1 to 7, with 1 strongest; ties are permitted only if you judge two accounts genuinely equal for the novel, in which case give them the same rank and skip the next number. narrative_potential is a number from 0 to 10. rationale is a one-sentence justification of the rank and rating. biggest_fictional_risk is a one-sentence statement of the main way that account could turn the character into a gimmick or a mouthpiece. confidence is a number between 0 and 1 for how confident you are in that assessment. center_account and temptation_account are strings, each exactly matching one of my headings. Your answer should be the JSON object and nothing else.
