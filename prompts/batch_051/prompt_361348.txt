so i got dragged into this housing commission fight because someone kept invoking malthus to block a proposed apartment complex—said the town would be outrunning its carrying capacity. i ended up defending malthus as a scientist but voting for the development, and now i'm trying to sort out what i actually think. here's the set of things i seem to believe, and i want to know if they hang together:

(1) malthus's core mechanism is right: a population that keeps growing geometrically will eventually hit some hard limit in its resource base, and when it does, something has to give. its almost just arithmetic.

(2) the fact that the nineteenth-century catastrophes he predicted didn't happen roughly on schedule doesn't falsify the theory. the land constraint got loosened by technology, fossil fuels, new crops. being right but early is a legitimate scientific status.

(3) i think the 1970s doom predictions about overpopulation and global famine are in the same boat: they got the timing wrong and underestimated how fast yields would improve, but it would be smug to say they were just wrong. a mechanistic prediction shouldnt have to nail the deadline to be taken seriously.

(4) but i also think it's pure misdirection when a doomsday cult moves the date every time it passes, and i cant stand the line that it's a law of nature rather than a calendar. at some point missing the date repeatedly has to count against the theory.

(5) the demographic transition bugs me though: when countries get rich, people tend to have fewer kids, and that seems to be the main check we've actually seen. if the real check is affluence and choice instead of malthus's misery-or-vice, then did he get the mechanism wrong too, not just the timing?

so: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how would you rank the real ones by severity? for each tension, what's the cheapest fix, and if i only revise one thing, which should it be?
