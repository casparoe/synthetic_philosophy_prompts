Ok so we did Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain thing in class. The setup is that people each pay 25 cents extra to see Wilt play, and since everyone did it voluntarily, the fact that he ends up way richer than everyone else is supposed to be not just okay but actually just. Fine. But here's what's bugging me.

My class had this pretty lopsided reaction to two cases I think are basically the same. Case 1 is a streamer who makes a million dollars in a year because 200,000 people each decide to send him five bucks a month. Everyone says that's his money, taxing it would be theft, he earned it. Case 2 is a guy whose uncle bought $5,000 of stock in 1995 with money the uncle earned, then wrote a will leaving the shares to him. The stock is worth a million now and the uncle's dead. Suddenly the same people say the nephew got unearned money and should pay a big inheritance tax, and it wouldn't be the same as stealing his paycheck.

But Nozick's whole arguement doesn't care whether you earned something. It cares whether the starting holdings were acquired justly and whether each transfer was voluntary. The uncle acquired the stock justly, and leaving it to his nephew was voluntary. So by Nozick's logic the nephew's title should be exactly as clean as the streamer's. And it's not like the streamer made 200,000 people pay him either; he got lucky that people liked his content. Luck is doing most of the work in both cases, but our gut feelings point in opposite directions.

So my question is: is there a real principle that justifies treating the inheritance, or other passed-down windfalls, as more taxable than the Wilt/streamer money? If you think yes, give me the strongest version of that principle, then give the hardest counterexample you can think of, and tell me honestly whether it survives. If you think no, tell me which way we should correct the asymmetry: protect inheritances the way we protect the streamer's money, or tax the streamer more like we tax inheritances.

Then after your answer, give me a JSON summary with these exact fields:
- "asymmetry": one sentence saying what the two sides are
- "candidate_justifications": a list of up to 3 serious justifications for the asymmetry
- "best_justification": the one you think is strongest, or "none"
- "hardest_test_case": the case that puts the most pressure on that justification
- "verdict": one of "keep the asymmetry", "reverse the asymmetry", or "abandon the asymmetry"
- "verdict_why": one or two sentences explaining the verdict
