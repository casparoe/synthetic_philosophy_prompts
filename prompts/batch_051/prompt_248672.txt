I am a philosophy professor scheduled to give six-minute opening remarks at the kickoff of a city's year-long experiment with a standing citizens' assembly that has real control over a portion of the capital budget. The room will include democratic theorists, municipal officials, and neighborhood organizers. Please write the remarks around Dewey's claim, from The Public and Its Problems, that the state should be treated as an experiment rather than as something fixed once and for all — and that a public is formed through inquiry into the indirect consequences of associated action, not found ready-made. This idea must be load-bearing: it should determine how the speech handles the officials' worry that experiments are unreliable and the organizers' worry that more inquiry is just delay. Do not offer reassurance that the experiment will work; treat any outcome as information for the next trial. Begin with the speech itself, with no preface or commentary.
