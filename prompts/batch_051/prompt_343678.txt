I am preparing a recorded interview for a cognitive science podcast with a philosopher who has recently defended an extension of Dennett's tower of generate-and-test, the ladder running from Darwinian through Skinnerian and Popperian to Gregorian creatures. Her added fifth kind, which she calls library-native creatures, is meant to capture systems whose candidate behavioral options come overwhelmingly from a pre-existing human linguistic and cultural archive rather than from the four older mechanisms. She explicitly claims this is not just a more efficient Gregorian creature, because a Gregorian creature still has to internalize and re-deploy cultural tools, whereas a library-native system can offload even the search through candidate behaviors to statistical patterns in the archive itself.

I want the interview to be substantive and philosophically adversarial, not a restatement. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. At least one question each should press on: whether library-nativeness is a new kind or only a difference of degree within the Gregorian level; whether the proposed fifth level has a generate-and-test mechanism of its own or merely a different source of candidate behaviors; whether the tower is best understood as a historical progression, a nested hierarchy of capacities, or a ladder of increasingly flexible learning; and whether a system with no world model separate from its linguistic training corpus can count as a Popperian creature in any relevant sense. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.

In particular, her standard response to the objection that she has merely described advanced Gregorian tool use is that the distinction is architectural rather than substrate-based: what matters is where the search through candidates occurs, not what the system is made of. Please include a sequence of two or three questions that makes that deflection progressively harder to maintain, ending with a question about what behavioral signature or intervention would show that a library-native creature is not already doing something covered by the Gregorian level.

As you draft the questions, please keep clearly separate what is settled exegetically about the earlier four levels from what is genuinely contested in her extension, and do not present the proposed fifth level as if the field had accepted it.
