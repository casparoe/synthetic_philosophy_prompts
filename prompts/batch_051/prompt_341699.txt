I've just spent an evening arguing with a friend about whether a finite mind could in principle exhaust the space of possible experience—whether a bounded creature could ever reach a point where it stops having genuinely new experiences and is only rereading what it has already lived through. Going by my reactions to particular cases, I'm no longer sure what view I actually hold.

Here are my verdicts. A person with flawless episodic memory who lives long enough to have visited every describable kind of landscape has still not exhausted the experience of landscapes: weather, attention, mood, and the accumulating history of past visits make the next visit a new experience. A person confined to a fixed, simple room whose memory of each day is wiped overnight does not have a new experience each morning, even though each day feels entirely fresh from the inside. A person who has played out every possible game of a simple finite game has exhausted that game-space, yet their hundredth replay of a particular game is still a new experience in some weaker sense. A person who has experienced every qualia type on a finite list produced by some hypothetical stimulator has exhausted that specific kind of novelty, but I resist saying they have exhausted experience altogether. Reading the same poem at twenty and again at sixty is a new experience each time because the reader has changed. Composers working with a small fixed vocabulary never seem to me to stop producing novelties, or at least I cannot bring myself to treat the later works as mere repeats.

Is there a single principle about what it is for a finite mind to have a new experience that captures these verdicts, or nearly all of them? If such a principle forces me to drop or revise one or two of my verdicts, which ones should go and why? And what does the principle say about the case I kept going back and forth on: a person who has seen every possible arrangement of a small set of simple shapes, and is then shown one of those arrangements scaled to twice its usual size—is that a new experience in the same sense as the others, or a different kind of thing? Please write the response in American English.
