For a lecture I am preparing on distributive justice, I have been reading an otherwise careful survey that gives the following paragraph summarizing the Rawls–Harsanyi debate. I would like you to audit this paragraph for fairness and accuracy.

“The classic dispute between Rawls and Harsanyi is usually presented as a disagreement about what rational people would choose behind Rawls’s veil of ignorance. Rawls argued that because the parties do not know who they will be, and cannot assign probabilities to the various positions in society, the rational rule for them is maximin: rank the possible arrangements by the position of the worst-off person, and choose the arrangement whose worst-off position is highest. This is what leads the parties to select the difference principle, which permits inequalities only to the extent that they make the worst-off better off. Harsanyi showed that this conclusion is not forced by rationality. If the parties instead assign an equal probability to being any member of society and then maximize expected utility, they will choose average utilitarianism, not the difference principle. The entire debate therefore reduces to a single question: under complete ignorance, is it rational to use maximin or to use expected utility with an equal-probability assumption? And since maximin is a wildly overcautious rule for ordinary decisions, many have concluded that Rawls’s whole construction is an elaborate way of reaching an implausible result.”

I suspect this is a mostly competent popularization, but I am wary of at least two of its moves: the claim that the whole debate reduces to the single question stated near the end, and the closing dismissal. I would like a careful, sentence-by-sentence audit. Which parts would Rawls accept, which would he say misdescribe or overstate his position, and which would Harsanyi accept? In particular, how much work is being done by the phrase ‘under complete ignorance,’ and does the final sentence follow from anything that is accurately reported? I am less interested in the label for each error than in exactly what is true, what is simplified, and what is wrong.
