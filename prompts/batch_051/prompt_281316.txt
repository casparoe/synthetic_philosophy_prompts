ok ive been chewing on an argument against massive modularity and i can't tell if it's actually sound or if i've smuggled something in. by massive modularity i mean the evolutionary-psychology-ish claim that the whole mind is built out of modules, not just perception. here's the argument:

(1) For a cognitive system to count as a module, it has to be informationally encapsulated: while its running, it gets access only to a proprietary database for its own domain, not whatever else the mind happens to know.
(2) Massive modularity says the mind is wholly made of modules.
(3) So if massive modularity is true, belief fixation — deciding what to believe in light of what you already accept — is itself a module.
(4) But belief fixation is exactly the kind of process that draws promiscuously on everything: perception, memory, other people's testimony, your grasp of probabilities, the lot.
(5) A process that draws promiscuously on everything cannot be informationally encapsulated in the sense of step 1.
(6) So belief fixation isn't a module, which contradicts (3), so massive modularity is false.

i keep feeling like 'module' is doing two different jobs somewhere in here, but i can't say exactly where a modularity person would legitimately dig in without just changing the subject. is the argument valid? if not, which step fails and why? and what's the strongest restatement of massive modularity that my argument would miss?
