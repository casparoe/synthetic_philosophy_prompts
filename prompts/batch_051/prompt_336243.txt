Here is a disagreement in the philosophy of love. On the appraisal view, love is best understood as a response to value: when you love someone, you are perceiving and being moved by real and valuable properties the person has, and love can therefore be assessed for whether it fits its object. On the bestowal view, the direction runs the other way: love does not discover value that is already there; the lover's care, attention, and commitment themselves create a value the beloved would otherwise lack. A colleague maintains that this is, at bottom, an empirical dispute — that the right study of how lovers form and revise their evaluations would show whether love tracks pre-existing value or manufactures it. I suspect the philosophical disagreement outruns anything a study could show, but I want to know exactly how far evidence can reach. Design the strongest concrete study you can of the claim that love bestows rather than appraises value. Specify what you would measure and compare, say what pattern of results would favor bestowal and what would favor appraisal, and then identify which element of the philosophical dispute — for instance, claims about reasons versus claims about causal psychology — would remain unsettled even by a clean outcome, and why.
