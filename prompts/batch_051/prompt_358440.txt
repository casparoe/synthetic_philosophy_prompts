For a one-page handout in a graduate value-theory seminar, write a decision tree that sorts someone from their reactions to a spectrum argument into a named position on whether all-things-considered betterness is transitive. Anchor it in one concrete spectrum: a sequence of a thousand alternatives beginning with one person undergoing two years of excruciating pain, each small step slightly shortening that person's pain while spreading an incrementally tiny amount of additional discomfort across a fixed population of one million, and ending with those million people each briefly noticing one extra second of mild irritation. The branch questions should be answerable by someone who has never seen the position labels and should separate positions along lines such as these: whether some step in the sequence marks a genuine drop in value even though no one could say which step it is; whether the relation expressed by 'not worse than' may fail to chain across a thousand steps; whether the claim that one adjacent alternative is worse than the next is neither true nor false rather than determinately false; whether the first step changes the scale in a way no later step does; and whether comparing whole outcomes step by step presupposes a single underlying scale at all. The leaves should include at least an epistemic-cutoff view, a vagueness view, a nontransitivist view, a lexical-priority view, and a parity or incomparability view; flag any pairs of leaves the tree cannot separate, and briefly diagnose any unoccupied combination as unattractive rather than merely unfilled. End with a short paragraph stating which split you are least confident the positions genuinely fall across and what consideration would lead you to redraw that branch.
