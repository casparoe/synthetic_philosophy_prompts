I spent most of my working life as a physical chemist, and since retiring I've been reading my way through philosophy in a haphazard way. The thing that's got its hooks in me is the Moloch/multipolar trap idea: a situation where several agents each get a short-term edge from defecting on a shared norm, and each loses badly if it cooperates while the others defect, so they all drive toward an outcome none of them actually wants. It reminds me of nothing so much as a runaway reaction or a feedback loop in an ecosystem, so even though the vocabulary is new to me, the shape of the thing feels familiar.

Concretely: suppose the leading AI labs set up an escrow fund to brake the race to deploy ever more capable models. Each member lab deposits, say, $2 billion (or some percentage of its annual compute spend). A lab that unilaterally decides to slow down — to run extra safety evals for six months before releasing something it could have shipped sooner — can claim compensation from the fund for the market share it plausibly lost by waiting. If a lab ships a model that fails an agreed audit (capability tests, alignment exercises, that sort of thing), it forfeits its deposit, which is distributed among the members currently slowed down. The audits are run by an independent body paid out of the fund, and there's a defection bounty: anyone who credibly exposes hidden deployment or sandbagging gets ten percent of the forfeited bond.

What actually happens if this ran? Treat it as a mechanism, not as a moral argument: what does it incentivize among the labs, among the auditors, and among third parties? How is it gamed — fake slowdowns to collect compensation, sandbagged evaluations so a dangerous model looks safe, strategic timing to drain the fund, collusive defection claims against a rival, the trustee or auditors quietly becoming a soft regulator? Does it brake the race or just move the race up a level, with the labs now competing to game the fund? Could the failure modes be patched without gutting the thing, or is that the whole story? And be straight with me: if I've got Moloch wrong, or if this fund is just the same trap wearing a different mask, say so plainly rather than humoring the setup — I'd like to know if its well-founded, not politely validated.
