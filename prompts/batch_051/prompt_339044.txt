In discussions of presupposition accommodation, a common move is to say that conversation is like a game with a scoreboard: a speaker's utterance is a move, the common ground is the score, and a presupposition that isn't yet in the common ground can be added to it by a move, the way a scoreboard is updated, provided the move isn't challenged; the score then determines which later moves are admissible. The analogy is used to argue that accommodation isn't a repair process but a normal rule-governed update. Audit this analogy. Give the correspondence table: what in conversation plays the role of the scoreboard, the rules, the players, a legal move, a foul, and the referee. Separate the disanalogies that matter for that conclusion from the ones that don't. In particular, consider that a game score exists independently of the players' beliefs about it, while what can be presupposed depends on what speakers take to be common ground, and that a game move can be challenged and the score corrected, whereas an accommodated presupposition often cannot simply be withdrawn by later objection. Then say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
