Me and my friend have been going back and forth about uploading and teleportation for months, and she has a surprisingly fixed set of verdicts. For a Star Trek style transporter — the kind that scans you down to the molecule, destroys the original, and assembles an exact replica somewhere else — she says you survive, no hesitation. For destructive whole-brain emulation, where the scan itself destroys the biological tissue and a perfect emulation is then booted up in silico, she also says you survive. But for a non-destructive scan, where the emulation wakes up in a virtual environment while your original biological brain stays wide awake and unharmed, she says the biological original is you and the emulation is not, at least so long as the original is still around. And she says the same about gradual replacement as long as its gradual: swap your neurons one at a time for functionally identical artificial ones until no biological neurons remain, with your behavior and memories untouched the whole way, and it's still you.

Here's the case I actually want a prediction about. Suppose we do the non-destructive scan and wake up the emulation, and then, while both exist, the biological original has a sudden aneurysm and dies, with the emulation carrying on. Does my friend say you survived as the emulation, or that you died with the original? I'm less interested in your own verdict than in what her rule really is. What other rules fit all four earlier verdicts just as well, and what does each one predict about this new case? Give me your best guess about which rule she's running and why, and tell me the further case you'd run by her to settle it.
