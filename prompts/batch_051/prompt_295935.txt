I am drafting a short essay on the debate about love's reasons and keep getting stuck in the same place. The dispute is usually framed as a standoff between appraisal views — on which love is, or should be, a response to value that antecedently exists in the beloved — and bestowal views, on which love confers value on the beloved or creates the lover's reasons instead of tracking value already there. But as I read the actual positions, the camps refuse to line up cleanly. Velleman's appraisal story locates the relevant value in something thin and shared by all persons, namely a capacity for rationality or autonomy; Frankfurt appears to deny that love must answer to any evaluative judgment about the beloved at all; and Kolodny locates the justification in the history and normative shape of the relationship rather than in the beloved's properties. Before I take a side or try to reconcile them, I want the full specification of what a theory in this area actually has to deliver.

Write the desiderata: what must an adequate account of love's reasons — why a person loves this particular person, and when that love is justified, apt, or intelligible — be able to do? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it binding, and separate the requirements into those any view must satisfy, those that are local to one camp, and those that are negotiable. In particular, address at least these: the difference between reasons for coming to love, for continuing to love (including loving someone after the qualities that first attracted you have faded), and reasons love itself creates for the lover; whether a theory must explain partiality and irreplaceability, so that the reasons are not simply transferable to someone else with the same properties; whether it must explain how love can go wrong, be inappropriate, or properly end; and whether it must respect the fact that love is not usually something one chooses or arrives at by inference. Flag any pairs of desiderata that might be in tension, and identify the single requirement that does the most to separate the main views. Then, briefly, score the appraisal, bestowal, and relationship-history pictures against your list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria, not the scorecard. I do not need page numbers or a bibliography. Keep the whole thing under 1,000 words; a table for the desiderata is welcome.
