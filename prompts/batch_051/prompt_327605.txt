I'm a novelist, working on a book about a couple in their forties who've been monogamous for twenty years and then one of them asks to open the relationship. As research I've read a stack of philosophy and relationship ethics, and the more I read the more it feels like the debate about whether monogamy is a moral requirement or just a social convention has been stuck for a long time. The arguments have become totally predictable. The anticoventionalist side says: nothing consenting adults do is wrong, so sexual exclusivity can't be a moral requirement, at most a preference, and probably a leftover from property-based marriage norms. The other side says: that just assumes consent is the whole of morality; fidelity can be a real obligation inside a committed relationship, and breaking it isn't changing a preference, it's betraying a person. Each side thinks the other is rationalizing or begging the question, and nobody moves.

What I actually want, for the book and for my own sanity, is a diagnosis of why this debate is stuck. Is it a substantive moral disagreement, a verbal one about what "commitment" or "fidelity" means, or are the two sides answering different questions—one about whether non-monogamy is permissible in general, the other about whether exclusivity can be a requirement within a particular relationship? My working theory is that the hidden question is about the metaphysics of commitments: whether the content of a commitment is exhausted by what the parties explicitly agree to, or whether some commitments (marriage, long-term partnership) carry a social meaning that fixes obligations partly independent of what the parties say. If that's right, the ethics debate is secretly a debate about social kinds, and it won't move until that's surfaced. But I don't trust my own theory.

So: what kind of disagreement is this really? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a conceptual distinction, a class of edge cases (a couple who agree in advance that sex with others is fine and one of them still experiences it as betrayal; emotional but not sexual infidelity), or is the honest conclusion that there's a bedrock difference in intuitions here and no argument will move it? I'd rather get a clear diagnosis of the stall than another take on who's right.
