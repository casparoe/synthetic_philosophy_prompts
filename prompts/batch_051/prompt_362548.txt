Lately I've been poking around in multiobjective RL, and I cant stop noticing a structural echo with Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason. In Sidgwick you've got two independently rational aims—your own good and the good of everyone—each passes the test of rational intuition, but they can issue contradictory verdicts and there's no higher principle to settle which one wins. In multiobjective optimization you've got a vector-valued objective (say, one reward channel for self-interest and one for aggregate welfare): neither dominates the other, you get a Pareto frontier, and every point on it is "optimal" only relative to an arbitrary scalarization. Maximization doesn't single out a policy once the objectives conflict. That feels like Sidgwick's situation, restated as an engineering problem. But maybe I'm just pattern-matching.

So: is this a genuine identity of structure, or a loose analogy? If it's real, state the mapping precisely, and say what would have to be true of practical reason for results about Pareto optimality, scalarization, and incomplete orderings to transfer to the dualism. Then, to stress-test it: name one thing the multiobjective picture predicts about Sidgwick's view that you think is false—for instance, does it turn his contradiction into a merely underdetermined choice of weights, and if so, is that a defect of the analogy or a genuine criticism of Sidgwick?
