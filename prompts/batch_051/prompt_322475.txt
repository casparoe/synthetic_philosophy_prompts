Me and a friend spent the whole bus ride arguing about what actually happens when a constitution just runs out—not when someone breaks it, but when the text has nothing at all to say. Our toy case was a president dying and the constitution being weirdly silent on succession, or a parliament whose term expires and then a three-day gap before the new one can be sworn in. The thing that struck both of us is that we were comfortable letting quite a lot happen in the gap: someone acts "provisionally," civil servants keep paying salaries, courts keep issuing routine orders, and we shrugged and said sure, someone has to keep the lights on. But the second the gap was used to do something new—sign a treaty, rewrite an election rule, sell off state land—we both got sqeamish and said no, you can't read that out of silence. So we were treating the same hole in the rules under two different principles: silence can justify staying the course, but silence can't justify actually governing.
