i have a rule about historical explanation: the cleaner the story, the less I trust it. if a book says the 2008 crash came down to a handful of greedy bankers, or a podcast says the first world war was basically inevitable once the archduke was shot, i knock my estimate way down. i trust the messier versions that pile up several partial causes, bad luck, and institutional detail, and that make the outcome look close or avoidable. my friend says this is just a preference for clutter—some events really do have one dominant cause. is my rule defensible? how strongly should neatness count as evidence against a narrative, and is there a version that keeps the hindsight-bias protection without treating "tidy" as a reliable sign of "false"?
