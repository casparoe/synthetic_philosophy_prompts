I'm giving the eulogy at the funeral of my old philosophy mentor, who was a serious and, as far as I can tell, completely sincere Spinozist. She held that there is one substance, that everything that happens follows necessarily from its nature and could not have been otherwise, and that the best a human life can do is the intellectual love of God — understanding things under the aspect of eternity. She was also clear that none of this allows for a personal afterlife, so I can't say she's watching over us now or that she's in a better place, and I can't praise her for choices she freely made, since on her view nobody makes those. Half the room is Spinoza scholars who will catch every slip; the other half is her kids and grandkids, who mostly aren't philosophers. Write the eulogy for me: it has to genuinely comfort that mixed room, land as affection rather than as a lecture on the Ethics, and not smuggle in any free-will or afterlife talk she would have rolled her eyes at. Max 500 words.
