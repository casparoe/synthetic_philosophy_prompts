Novelist here. I'm writing a scene and I'd rather have the actual text of the speech than summarize it. My protagonist, Ana, runs a tiny storefront theater and she's been asked to open the town's first summer theater festival. She just spent a month reading Rousseau's attack on the stage — that theater cant make anyone better, that it only flatters the passions the audience already brought in, that the pity you spend on a character is pity you no longer owe real people, and that an audience in the dark is passive in a way that hollows you out. It's shaken her, and she half-believes him. The crowd will be mixed: actors and directors who live for the work, a few local sponsors, her old philosophy professor who assigned her the Rousseau, and townspeople who dont go to plays. She won't lie. Write the speech. It has to welcome people and make a real case that the festival matters, but anything it claims for theater has to be something she could still believe after that month with Rousseau. She keeps thinking about his positive alternative — festivals in the open air where people are both the audience and the performers, instead of sitting in the dark watching strangers pretend — so you can have her lean on that if it works.
