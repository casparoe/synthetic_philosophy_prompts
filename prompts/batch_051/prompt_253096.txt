im teaching Slaughterhouse-Five in an intro philosophy unit and I want to use the Tralfamadorians as a way into presentism vs eternalism. The way I read it, the aliens see all of time laid out at once, every moment equally real, and Billy's time-jumping is basically what it would feel like to live in a block universe. But the book also seems to slide from that into a kind of fatalism -- nothing can be changed, so the only decent response to death and atrocity is a shrug. I'm worried I'm letting the novel smuggle in a conclusion the metaphysics doesn't actually support. Can you give me a reading that separates what the book gets right about eternalism from what it overreaches: where the block universe picture really does work in the story, where the novel confuses eternalism with determinism or fatalism, and whether an actual eternalist would have to accept the book's attitude toward death, or whether that's an extra step? I care less about whether Vonnegut intended the philosophy and more about whether the novel can be read as a serious thought experiment.
