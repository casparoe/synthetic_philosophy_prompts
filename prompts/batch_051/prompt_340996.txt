My friend Dana has spent a weird amount of time thinking about when it's okay to impose a risk on someone without asking them, and I've been keeping a running list of her verdicts:

- A drunk driver heading home at .09 BAC on a basically empty rural road at 2 a.m.: wrong, no caveats, even after I pointed out the actual chance of hitting anyone was tiny.
- A commercial airliner on a normal approach over a residential neighborhood: fine, even though the people below never consented to the crash risk.
- A factory that emits a pollutant that raises lifetime cancer risk for the surrounding town by around one in 100,000, fully inside its permits: fine.
- Someone with measles who rides a crowded subway car during an outbreak: obviously wrong.
- A guy with a mild cold who goes into his open-plan office: fine, even though his coworkers didn't consent to the germs.
- Someone who glances at a text while merging onto a highway: wrong, even though the added risk is tiny.
- A homeowner burning leaves on a dry, windy day in a fire-prone area: wrong.

Now a delivery-drone company wants to run small drones along a corridor about ninety feet over a residential neighborhood. The people underneath get noise and a tiny, not-entirely-characterized risk of a drone failing and hitting someone or a house. The packages are going between two commercial districts on the other side of town, so the people under the flight path get no direct benefit and were never asked. The company has all the required permits, and the risk estimate is actually lower than the airline crash risk she already called fine.

What does Dana say about the drone corridor, if she's being consistent? I'm less interested in the verdict than the rule: what rules fit her record, which of them diverge on the drone case, which one is she probly running, and what further case would cleanly settle which rule she's actually using? Also tell me if you think some of her earlier verdicts only look like they fit and she's actually got an unstated exception for something like ordinary background risks versus optional extra risks.
