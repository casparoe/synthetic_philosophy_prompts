Coming to these questions from the natural sciences, I keep wanting the problem stated as a set of adequacy conditions rather than a contest of labels. The problem I mean is who governs when the rules run out — governance in constitutional gaps and interregna: no valid line of succession, a disputed election with no final arbiter, an emergency the text never contemplated, or an amendment procedure used to alter the amendment rules themselves. What I want is a specification of the requirements an adequate theory of such governance must meet. For each requirement, give me the motivating case or consideration, and state which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of particular camps. I am especially interested in whether the plausible requirements are jointly satisfiable — for example, fidelity to the written text, continuity of the legal order, officials' oath to uphold the constitution, and the need to distinguish lawful succession from successful usurpation. Spend your effort on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on surveying how existing theories score. Finally, for whichever requirement you are least confident about, tell me what argument or case would change your mind about it, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
