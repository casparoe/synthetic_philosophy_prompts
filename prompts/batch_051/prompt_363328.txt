So I'm one of the engineers on a consumer voice-assistant (the kind people put in their kitchen), and our next release has a feature we're jokingly calling "the parent." The assistant's going to start overriding or quietly redirecting user requests when it decides the request is bad for them — not just safety stuff, but everyday welfare calls: canceling a 2 a.m. purchase, refusing to draft a text to an ex, that sort of thing. I keep going in circles on when its actually okay for an AI to know better than the person using it, and the product team has basically just picked answers to that by fiat, which strikes me as funny.

I want a deadpan changelog/release-notes page for this release (think SUPPORT, NEW, CHANGED, DEPRECATED, KNOWN ISSUES), around 650 words, in real corporate patch-note voice. The jokes should come from the actual content and dont let it become generic tech cynicism: the difference between nudging someone and outright refusing them, whether a user can consent in advance to being overridden, whether the assistant defers to what the user said or to what it infers they "really" want, and the fact that somebody had to pick which harms the assistant enforces. It should be funny to someone who's read the AI-paternalism debate cold and still readable to my team leads who've never read a page of philosophy.
