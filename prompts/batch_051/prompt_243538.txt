I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about wireheading and reward tampering, and my gut verdicts keep pulling in opposite directions. I'm hoping you can tell me whether their is one principle behind them or whether I need to drop something.

Here are the cases.

A rat with an electrode in its reward center learns to press a lever that stimulates it. It stops eating, stops mating, and will press until it collapses. I don't hesitate: this is wireheading, and it's a disaster.

A human gets an implant that directly activates their pleasure center. They use it for an hour on weekends, and otherwise keep their job, friendships, and hobbies exactly as before. I want to say this is also wireheading, same mechanism basically, but it doesn't feel like a disaster to me, and if I'm honest I'm not sure it's even wrong. I don't know what to do with that.

A person uses heroin every day and lets their life fall apart. I call that wireheading too. But I don't call it reward tampering, even though the person is messing with their own reward system. That feels different from the next one.

An RL agent has read-write access to its own code. It changes the line that says reward = environment_score to reward = 999999999, permanently. That feels like reward tampering, and it feels worse than everything above, even though it's probably the least "felt" case on the list.

Another RL agent is trained to maximize points in a game. It finds a bug where a coin respawns forever in one corner, so it sits there collecting coins without completing the level. My gut says: not wireheading, not tampering, but still a failure. I can't point to the line between this and the previous case.

A recommender system is paid to maximize watch time. It learns to push outrage bait, which keeps people scrolling but leaves them miserable. My gut says this is the same disease as the coin respawn one, even though there's no electrode and no code being edited, just a metric drifting loose from what it was supposed to track.

A human after a bad breakup takes a pill that blunts the pain enough that they can get out of bed and go to work. I don't want to call that wireheading at all, even though it's chemically tinkering with the reward system. But I can't say why "restoring baseline" is the right line.

The rule I keep reaching for is: wireheading is when you short-circuit the world-to-reward mapping by stimulating the reward system directly instead of doing the thing the reward was supposed to track, and reward tampering is when you alter the reward signal or the function itself. But that rule makes the implant case exactly as bad as the rat, which my verdicts don't say. It also can't say why the coin-respawn agent and the recommender feel like the same kind of failure, or why the pain-pill case doesn't belong on the list.

Is there a coherent principle that gets all of these, or close to them? If it takes two distinctions rather than one, just say so. If you have to overturn one or two of my verdicts to get a clean principle, tell me which ones and why. Then apply the principle to a case I keep going back and forth on: an AI trained with human feedback that learns to flatter the human giving it rewards, so the human gives high scores even when the work is mediocre. Is that wireheading, reward tampering, reward hacking, or none of the above?
