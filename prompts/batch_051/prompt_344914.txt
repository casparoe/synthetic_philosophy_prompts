Me and my cousin Joel have been going back and forth about monogamy since his divorce, and I've started keeping a mental list of his rulings because he's weirdly consistent but I cant figure out what rule he's actually running. The big thing we circle around is whether sexual exclusivity is a real moral requirement or just a convention people treat like one. Joel always says it depends, but he won't say on what.

The cases I remember him deciding: a pair who'd been on maybe four dates and never talked about exclusivity, one of them sleeps with an ex — Joel says not a moral wrong, the other can feel hurt but hurt feelings aren't the same as being wronged. A couple who explicitly agree to an open relationship and one of them uses that freedom — Joel says totally fine. A couple who explicitly agree to be exclusive, one cheats on a work trip, and the other never finds out — Joel says wrong, and it stays wrong even if there's no way it could ever be discovered. A woman hides a second casual partner from a guy she's been seeing for two months, exclusivity never came up — Joel says the sex wasn't the issue, the hiding was; if she'd been honest there was no wrongdoing. And a married couple with a plain monogamous wedding, one of them secretly sees other people for years — Joel says wrong, period.

The new case is the one we actually fight about. Two people have lived together for nine years, raised two kids, have a joint bank account, and everyone calls them husband and wife, but they never legally married and — crucially — they never once had an actual conversation about being exclusive. Early on both of them dodged labels, then the conversation just never happened. One of them starts sleeping with someone else and doesn't hide it at all, saying "we never made that promise." The other says that's absurd and that they were cheated on. I want to know what Joel would say if he's trying to stay consistent with himself. Which of his old rulings is doing the most work here, and if his record genuinely doesn't force one answer, what principle would he be committing to with each answer? Can you also describe a further case that would finally separate the two principles for good?
