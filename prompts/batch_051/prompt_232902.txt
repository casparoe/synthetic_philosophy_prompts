So I'm on the committee for a small business grant fund in our town, and we're stuck on one application and I've been made the tiebreaker. A woman wants to open a specialty bakery. Taken purely on its own merits, her application is the best I've seen in eight years on this committee: six years running a home baking business, a regular customer list, a real budget, a signed lease, supplier letters, the works. If all we did was read her plan, we'd approve it without much argument. The problem is the track record. The fund has backed something like thirty food businesses, and only two of the last eleven were still running two years later; from what I could find, new bakeries in general fail at about the same clip. And every one of those earlier failures came to us with a polished plan too. Half the committee says we should judge this application on its own merits and not punish her for other people's bad luck or bad decisions, especially since her plan really is stronger than the ones that failed. The other half says the plan is just the inside view — our excited read of one particular story — and we've been wrong before while feeling exactly this sure, so the outside view, the plain track record for businesses of that kind, is the only honest thing we know, and it says no. I have to write the decision memo before next weeks meeting. It needs a clear recommendation — fund, don't fund, or fund with conditions — plus the reasoning, and it has to take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously instead of waving it away. Don't include citations or a reading list. And please keep it continuous prose, the way you'd write an email to the rest of the committee: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
