I'm setting out my considered view of indicative conditionals for a discussion group, and I want a consistency audit of the package rather than a survey of the terrain. My commitments are these:

(1) Adams's thesis: for any rational agent, the credence in an indicative conditional 'if A then C' equals the conditional credence P(C | A), whenever P(A) > 0.

(2) Indicative conditionals express propositions: each has truth conditions, and they embed freely under negation, conjunction, and as antecedents or consequents of further conditionals.

(3) The Ramsey test is the right account of how that credence is fixed: one evaluates 'if A then C' by hypothetically adding A to one's beliefs with minimal collateral change and then gauging C.

(4) Import–export holds: 'if A and B, then C' is equivalent to 'if A, then if B then C.'

(5) Rational learning is exhausted by Bayesian conditionalization on propositions.

(6) Modus ponens is valid for indicatives: from A and 'if A then C' one may infer C.

(7) As a concrete anchor: for a fair six-sided die, my credence in 'if it lands even, it lands two' is 1/3, and my credence in 'if it lands even, then if it lands two, it lands six' is 0.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and whether the set is jointly tenable. I want the genuine tensions ranked by severity, each with a statement of what would have to give and what that revision costs, and then a recommendation as to which single change would do the most to restore coherence.
