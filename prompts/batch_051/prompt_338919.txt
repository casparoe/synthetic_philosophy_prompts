i'm putting together a little philosophy and poetry night and i want a poem that does parmenides' argument instead of just paraphrasing it. could you write a villanelle set inside the goddess's speech from the way of truth, nineteen lines in proper form, where the two refrains are the two halves of the single eleatic claim — something like, one refrain says what-is is, whole and one and now, and the other says the is-not route is no road for a mind to take, or your own versions, but they have to be present-tense. and here's the constraint that matters to me: the entire poem, refrains included, may use only present-tense finite verbs — no was, no will be, no came to be, no verbs of motion or generation except when negated, like cannot come to be. the idea is that the poem's grammar should be unable to say anything is born or passes away, so the only thing it can do is re-assert the one thing parmenides keeps saying; the villanelle's refrains do the structural work of an arguement that keeps circling back to the same thought and never lets anything new enter the poem. it'd be good if it touches the usual way-of-truth beats without sounding like a lecture: the ban on the is-not route, the point that coming-to-be would have to start from nothing or from something that already is (and so never gets started either way), being as ungenerated and imperishable and complete, and the image of the well rounded sphere. someone who knows their parmenides should be able to recogonise the moves from fragment 8 in there, but a room full of people who've never read him should still get a good poem rather than a position paper.
