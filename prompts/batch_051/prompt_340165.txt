I'm a PhD student working at the philosophy/decision-theory boundary, and for a reading group I've been asked to write out my philosophical profile on transformative experience so the others can poke holes in it. I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher who works on rational choice, and to think carefully about this—start by brainstorming candidate tensions in your head, then whittle them down rather than flagging everything. Here is what I currently believe.

One, I'm a decision theorist at heart: an action is rational to the extent that it maximises expected utility relative to one's preferences at the time of choice.

Two, I accept the core transformative-experience claim, at least for big cases like becoming a parent or taking religious vows: you cannot know in advance what your post-choice evaluative standpoint would be like, because the choice changes the standpoint itself, not merely your information.

Three, but I don't think such choices are arational. I want to say plenty of people rationally decide to become parents, plenty rationally decide not to, and neither makes a mistake merely in virtue of that decision.

Four, I think testimony from people who have crossed the divide is genuinely useful. It can sometimes rule a choice out—if everyone who has done it reports it as miserable, that's a defeater—but it can never, by itself, establish that it was rational for me to go ahead.

Five, if someone crosses over and later would not undo it, I don't count that retrospective endorsement as evidence that the original choice was rational; the endorsement comes from a changed evaluator and I don't see why the earlier self should defer to it.

Six, I'm also drawn to an asymmetry: stepping into a transformative state raises a problem of rational justification that staying where I am does not.

I honestly can't see where the serious strain is, though I suspect their is one. Go through these and tell me, roughly ordered by severity, which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly, which pairs only look incompatible and why the appearance is misleading, and for each genuine tension what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would do the most work overall. Please write the answer in British English.
