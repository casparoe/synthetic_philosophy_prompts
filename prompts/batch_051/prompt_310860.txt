In his Ethics (Scito te ipsum), Peter Abelard defends the view that the moral quality of a human action is settled by the agent’s intention and consent, not by the outward act or its results. Consider a familiar feature of criminal law: a person who tries to kill someone but fails is normally convicted of attempted murder and punished less severely than a person who succeeds, even when the two offenders had the same intention and engaged in the same conduct up to the point where chance intervened. What theory of wrongdoing and moral luck does this sentencing practice presuppose? Reconstruct that implicit theory, compare it with Abelard’s position, and say what would have to change in the law if it consistently accepted Abelard’s view that sin lies in consent rather than in the deed. Please answer in British English.
