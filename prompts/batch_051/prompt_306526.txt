I'm preparing a one-hour section for second-year undergraduates in a course on practical rationality; the students are split between economics and philosophy, and the economics students arrive with revealed-preference material fresh in their heads. I want the session built around a distinction I plan to introduce them to: choosing as selecting on the basis of a reason or ranking, versus picking as selecting among tied or unranked alternatives where no such reason is available. Buridan's ass is the obvious carrying example, but I am not sure it actually carries the weight, and I would like you to plan the session rather than explain the material to me.

What I need from you is a teaching sequence, not a lecture.
