I keep having the feeling that the fight over whether neural networks "really have features" is the same fight biologists already had about genes, and I want to know whether the analogy is actually deep or just the same words doing different work.

The gene version: for a long time a gene was a neat physical thing, a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein. Then came alternative splicing, overlapping genes, regulatory DNA, genes within genes, etc., and now a lot of philosophers of biology think "gene" has no single physical essence—it's a pragmatic way of talking about a region that does something useful in some context. Biologists still use the word all the time and it's still useful.

The interpretability version: you find a direction in activation space, maybe with a probe or a sparse autoencoder, and it seems to track a concept, and when you steer along it the model behaves as if that concept matters. But different methods find different directions, training seeds change things, one direction can be a mix of many concepts, and one concept can be spread across many directions. So people argue about whether these directions are real features or artifacts of the method.

The structural mapping I have in mind is: DNA locus ↔ activation direction, knockout/knockdown ↔ ablation or steering, pleiotropy/polygenicity ↔ polysemanticity/superposition, "the gene for X" ↔ "the feature for X." That all lines up suspiciously well.

My question: is this a real isomorphism of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it's real, the lesson from the gene concept seems to be that "is it really a representation?" was the wrong question, and the right question is "when does feature talk earn its keep?" But maybe there's a disanalogy: a network is an artifact trained to minimize a loss, while an organism isn't made for anything. I can't tell whether that's the thing that breaks the analogy or just a different way of phrasing the same point. Where exactly does the mapping fail, and what does that tell us about realism in interpretability?
