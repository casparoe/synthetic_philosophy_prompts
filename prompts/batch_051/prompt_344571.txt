Consider the trolley problem, but with the locus of decision moved from a human at the switch to an autonomous weapon system acting under rules approved hours earlier. A loitering munition has two pre-authorized targets: Target A, a command vehicle, and Target B, a logistics truck. Its targeting policy, approved by a human commander, authorizes the system to select between them in real time using its own sensor classifiers and a programmed objective function; there is no human in the loop for this particular engagement. The system initially selects A. Just before release, its model indicates with high confidence that striking A will kill five civilians near the vehicle, and that striking B instead will kill one civilian, while B remains a valid military objective. The system re-selects B, strikes, and the one civilian is killed. The commander never reviewed this particular choice of targets; the only human act was the earlier approval of the general policy, which instructs the system, other things equal, to choose the attack with the lower number of expected civilian casualties.

The canonical case is normally analyzed through the doing/allowing distinction, the intended/foreseen distinction, and the aggregative question whether one may redirect a lethal threat from five people to one. Work through whether and how those distinctions retain their force in this variant. In the original, the threat is already under way and the agent redirects an existing lethal trajectory; here the system is itself the source of the lethal trajectory in both options, so is there any doing/allowing contrast left, or only a choice about which deaths to cause? Can a system whose objective function treats civilian deaths as comparative costs have anything analogous to the intended/foreseen distinction, or is the only intention that exists located in the commander's earlier approval of the policy? And does that ex ante approval of a general rule constitute meaningful human control over the death of the one civilian, or does meaningful human control require a human to be appropriately related to the particular decision? Finally, say which of the standard answers to the trolley problem is most undermined by this relocation of agency, and which best survives it.
