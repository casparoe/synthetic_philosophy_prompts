I teach an upper-level undergrad seminar on collective intentionality and joint action — about 14 students, one 90-minute session. We've read the standard stuff (Bratman, Gilbert, Searle), so they can all recite the difference between shared intention and coincidentally aligned individual intentions, but I don't think they've internalized it. I want a structured in-class activity or game that makes them feel the difference between doing something together and doing the same thing merely in parallel. Ideally the distinction should show up in what happens during the excercise, before I name any theory. Rules should be simple enough to explain in under five minutes, and the stakes should be real but small — no money, maybe points, social embarrassment, loss of a privilege, whatever works. Please give me: the setup and materials, the rules, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto concepts like mutual responsiveness, commitment to the joint activity, and commitment to mutual support, or onto Gilbert-style joint commitment and the standing to rebuke and demand repair. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of shared agency the exercise can't reproduce and I'll have to supply in discussion.
