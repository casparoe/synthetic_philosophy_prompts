I just spent an hour arguing with a friend about whether sentience is a matter of degree, from insects down to thermostats, and I still can't tell who had the better of it. I wrote the whole exchange out afterwards as best I could. I won't say which side was mine — call the two of us A and B — because I want a straight adjudication rather than a defence of my own line.

(1) A said: look at the continuum — person, dog, fish, bee, flatworm, thermostat. Each step down loses a bit of responsiveness, learning, integration. Theres no point where the lights suddenly go off, so moral status must be graded too, and it never snaps to zero.

(2) B replied: that's a story about hardware. The moral question is whether there's a subject of experience. There is something it's like to be a dog; there is nothing it's like to be a thermostat. That's binary, not a matter of degree.

(3) A: but evolution doesn't do binary. There was no first ancestor that woke up. Whatever having an experience is, it must have come in by degrees, so you're just insisting on a line you can't draw.

(4) B: continuity in the underlying stuff doesn't mean continuity in the property. Water warms continuously and then at 0°C it freezes — liquid to solid is a phase change. Sentience could be a threshold property like that.

(5) A: freezing is a measurable phase transition; you can point to the crystal structure. With sentience there's no measurable switch, so any threshold you pick just tracks how much something resembles us. That's chauvinism, not biology.

(6) B: we can know there's a threshold without knowing exactly where it sits. And it needn't be anthropocentric: the line is between systems with interests — ones that can be benefited or harmed — and systems that can only be damaged, like a thermostat. Interests are the issue; complexity is a red herring.

(7) A: but interests and valenced experience come in degrees too. A bee's experience is dimmer and less unified than a dog's. If it's graded above the line, why think it drops straight to zero at the last sentient thing rather than trailing off? A thermostat holding a set point is doing something a bit like pursuing a goal — why deny it a tiny sliver of standing?

(8) B: because a dim light is still on; it doesn't become slightly off. The question is whether there's any light at all. A thermostat's 'goal' is only metaphorical — there's no way for it to be better or worse off, so there's nothing to owe it. That's zero, not just less.

(9) A: fine, then tell me about a sea anemone, or a single neuron. Is there something it's like to be either? If you say no, you're drawing a line through the middle of a nervous-system continuum with no criterion. If you say yes, you're halfway to the thermostat.

(10) B: I can live with not knowing where the line is. What I can't live with is pretending there's no line — because if nothing is at zero, then unplugging a router or deleting a file becomes morally loaded, and the difference between harming and merely damaging collapses. That's a bigger cost than an arbitrary-looking threshold.

We never settled it. Can you adjudicate the exchange move by move — rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly say why, identify who's right about which specific point, where each of us goes wrong, and what we're both missing? And I'd definately like to know whether either position can be patched up to survive the other's best objection.
