I am the clinical ethicist on the reproductive medicine review board of a teaching hospital. The board meets twice a week to adjudicate requests for IVF, preimplantation genetic diagnosis, and surrogacy where there is foreseeable risk that the future child will have a serious, painful, or severely impairing condition. We currently decide case by case with no shared rule, and the inconsistency bothers me. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Benatar's asymmetry between pleasure and pain in procreation, that the board can actually run: the other members are clinicians with no training in moral philosophy, decisions must be reached within days, and the board's chair signs off. For clarity, the asymmetry is this: the presence of pain is bad, and the presence of pleasure is good; the absence of pain is good even when no one exists to experience it, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there is no one to be deprived. Please turn that into an operational checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it must hand the decision to a person because the next step is a value judgment rather than an inference from the asymmetry. Say which distinctions in the asymmetry survive the translation and which are flattened, and what the procedure cannot settle. Then run it on three cases: (1) a couple carrying a mutation for a condition that causes constant pain and death by age five, seeking IVF with embryo selection; (2) a couple with no known genetic risk who want IVF simply to have a child; and (3) a couple with a 25 percent chance of transmitting a condition that causes moderate hearing loss and some social disadvantage but little physical pain. I want the procedure, and I want to know in advance where it will return an answer the board will resist.
