I'm a novelist, so I'm supposed to care about the felt texture of a character's inner life, but I've never managed to get a grip on what 'qualia' actually are. Give me a working definition of a quale that handles these cases: staring at a ripe tomato in full daylight; the coffee-taster case where one guy insists the taste is unchanged and only his standards have risen while the other says the taste itself went sour (Dennett's Chase and Sanborn, I think); a pain you hadn't noticed until someone says you're limping; blindsight; a shape flashed too quickly to report; and the difference between remembering a toothache and having one. I'd count the tomato as a clear yes, blindsight and the subliminal flash as clear no's, and the coffee case is the one I most want the definition to sort out. Propose the definition, run it through all six, tell me where it breaks or gives a verdict you'd have to bite the bullet on, and say whether the defintion survives the quining worry that we can't tell from the inside whether our qualia have changed or just our judgments about them.
