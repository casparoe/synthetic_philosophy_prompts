I'm on the teaching-elders committee of a small congregation, and we've spent the last three meetings going in circles about head coverings. Several members read the passage in 1 Corinthians 11 where Paul tells women to cover their heads while praying or prophesying and want us to make that a standing practice again. Others point out that we already quietly set aside a lot of instructions from the same stretch of the New Testament — the bit about men not having long hair, the rules about meat offered to idols, the stuff addressed to slaves — and they say our line between binding commands and local customs is basically just vibes. The elders have asked me to draft a short policy memo that settles what we should do about head coverings and, more importantly, lays out the principle by which we'd decide whether any contested command is culturally local or binding across time and place.

The memo should give a clear recommendation, a set of criteria we can actually apply to other disputed commands, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the position you don't recommend. We're not going to publish it, so it can be direct. Please keep it under 450 words.
