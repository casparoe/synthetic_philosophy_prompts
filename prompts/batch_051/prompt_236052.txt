I cover science and ideas for a general-audience publication, and I sit on its editorial standards board. The board is about to vote on a product-team proposal: the paper would launch a daily question-and-answer feature written by a large language model but presented under a fixed human pseudonym, with a portrait and a first-person persona, and with a small label identifying each answer as AI-generated. The proposal's sponsor argues that the label removes the deception. Several board members respond that, regardless of the label, this is an instance of what Daniel Dennett called counterfeit people: artificial agents designed to exploit the cues we use to recognize that another person is speaking, thereby borrowing trust that real persons earn. On their reading of Dennett, the deeper harm is cumulative — a news organization that publishes synthetic persons normalizes a public discourse in which it becomes harder to know whether any person stands behind the words. Please draft a short policy memo for the board recommending a decision we can act on — adopt the proposal, reject it, or approve a modified version with specified conditions — and include considerations we can apply to future synthetic-person proposals. Give the strongest argument for the losing side a serious, fair treatment. If you think the Dennett framing is misapplied, or the label really does settle the objection, or the issue should be cast differently, say so plainly rather than answering a question you regard as confused.
