I've been trying to pin down what the low-impact AI agenda is actually committing itself to. The usual design goes something like this: you train an agent to do some job, but you also penalise it for making the world differ from the 'agent never existed' baseline, where 'differ' is measured over a finite set of variables the designers choose in advanced — positions of objects, energy use, money in bank accounts, that sort of thing. Changes to variables not on the list count as free. My question is what theory of value that design presupposes. Does it presuppose that a side effect can be picked out without a full specification of what matters, or only that a partial list is a workable approximation? Could a value pluralist sign up to the design without contradicting themselves? And what happens if the same change is a side effect on one list and the whole point on another, or if value turns out not to decompose into a neutral set of measurable state variables at all? I'm less interested in whether the engineering works and more in what philosophical picture someone has to accept, even quietly, to think the objective is well formed. Answer in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just as if you were writing me an email.
