I'm a philosophy professor putting together a unit on the privatization of justice, and I want a hard case that doesn't just rehearse the standard boilerplate-contract complaints. Here's the clause I'm puzzling over, written in the style of a real platform's terms: "You and the Company agree that any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement or your use of the Platform shall be settled exclusively by binding arbitration on an individual, non-class, non-representative basis. All arbitration proceedings and their outcomes shall be confidential." Now take this case. A user whose account the company permanently removed had been running the account as a de facto news wire for an election-monitoring group; she asks a state court to reinstate her, arguing the removal violates a recently enacted state statute requiring large platforms to preserve politically relevant speech during the ninety days before an election. The company moves to compel arbitration, saying this is plainly a dispute about her account and the clause reaches it, and that the confidentiality term bars her from making any result public. Clearly the people who drafted that language were imagining billing disputes, hacked accounts, and users suing over refunds; they weren't imagining a court order that, if it says anything, speaks to the whole electorate. I'd like you to lay out what the main approaches to interpretation would each do with this: the plain text, the evident purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked at the time, and what a reasonable user signing up would have understood. Then say which approach ought to govern, and what that implies about the public character of adjudication once a private contract is allowed to swallow a claim with a public constituency. Continuous prose only please, like an email, no headings or bullets or tables.
