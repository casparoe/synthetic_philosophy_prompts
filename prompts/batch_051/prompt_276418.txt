I'm abit stuck on something that came up while I was rereading Douglas Hofstadter's satirical piece "A Person Paper on Purity in Language" for a discussion group. The piece is a parody of fussy newspaper usage columns, and its basic trick is to write from inside a world where the unmarked default person is white, so that a columnist can complain about the awkwardness of person-compounds while treating the white-marked originals as perfectly neutral. The effect on me is that the generic use of "man" and "he" suddenly feels less innocent.

But here's my problem. I also think there's a pretty good theory on the other side: words like "mankind" and the generic "he" are mostly frozen residue from earlier stages of English, not a live semantic decision speakers are making every time they talk. On this view, the form and the content have come apart; ordinary speakers don't necessarily import a male default into every occurrence, and the fact that many women once used generic "he" without consciously feeling excluded is evidence that the convention was genuinely generic for them. That view appeals to me because it's simple, it doesn't treat ordinary language users as complicit in something they didn't intend, and it explains how a word can have a sex-marked origin but a neutral use. Yet after reading the satire, I can't shake the intuition that a race-marked generic would be obviously objectionable, and that sex-marked generics are objectionable in the same way.

So which should yield? Is the intuition just a product of the satire's emotional framing, a kind of debiasing trick rather than an argument? Or does it track something the historical-convention theory leaves out, like the way default categories stay visible even when speakers disavow them? If the theory should be amended, what exactly does it lose? And if both can survive, what would a compatible reading look like? I'm not looking for a verdict so much as an account of what would justify each move, and whether the analogy between race-marking and sex-marking is doing real argumentative work or just reframing my unease.
