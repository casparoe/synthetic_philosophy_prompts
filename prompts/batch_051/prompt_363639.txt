Here are my criteria for a democratic legislature: political equality—every adult citizen has an equal chance to influence lawmaking; authorization—lawmakers can plausibly claim to act with the people's ongoing authorization; accountability—citizens can punish or replace lawmakers for decisions they judge poor; deliberative quality—decisions issue from informed, structured reasoning among diverse perspectives rather than from bargaining or raw preference aggregation. Now consider a unicameral legislature selected by random, stratified sampling of the adult citizenry: members serve a single two-year term, may not be reselected, are paid and given expert briefings, and deliberate under professional moderators, and its enactments are binding unless overturned by referendum. Is this lottocratic body democratic by my four criteria? Go through the criteria one by one, note where they underdetermine the answer, and then say whether any apparent shortfall belongs to the institution, to the criteria themselves, or to the background assumption that democracy requires elections.
