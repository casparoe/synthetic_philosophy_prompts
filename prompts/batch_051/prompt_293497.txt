I recently had a long argument with a friend about Gödel's argument that time is not objectively real. As I understand the argument, Gödel noticed that certain rotating solutions to Einstein's field equations contain closed timelike curves: worldlines along which a traveler could return to an earlier point in their own history. He took the physical possibility of such worlds to show that the intuitive picture of time as a flowing, objective dimension cannot be right, and he drew idealistic conclusions from this. My friend insisted that later developments have defused the argument. Specifically, he pointed to Hawking's chronology protection conjecture, which suggests quantum effects would prevent closed timelike curves from forming, and to the fact that our actual universe does not appear to have the global rotation Gödel's solution requires. I want to know whether Gödel's argument survives this development. Does the fact that general relativity permits such worlds still put pressure on the reality of time even if a fuller quantum theory excludes them? Or does the argument collapse once we accept that the actual world is not one of the rotating solutions? Please identify which premise of Gödel's argument is under pressure, say whether it can be repaired, and state what, if anything, remains of his idealist conclusion.
