For a philosophy of criminal law post on a rationalist-adjacent blog, I want a sorting quiz titled something like Which theory of punishment without fault do you actually hold? The quiz should have eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case: a manufacturer that followed every feasible safety protocol but still produced one defective unit; a defendant who reasonably but wrongly believed a sexual partner was above the age of consent; a person found carrying a prohibited item they did not know they had; a corporation fined for a rogue employee's unauthorized act; a small regulatory fine with no stigma; a core criminal offence carrying imprisonment and lasting stigma; a negligence case where the agent had the capacity to know but failed to attend; a case where strict liability would predictably reduce the relevant harm; and a high fine that a firm can treat as a cost of doing business. Each question should force a choice about the case, such as punish or not, blame or not, or how much to punish, without using any label for the theory the answer points toward.

The scoring key should sort answers into the logical space formed by three splits: whether punishment is justified backward-looking or forward-looking; whether inadvertence or negligence can itself count as fault; and whether public-welfare or regulatory offences should be handled differently from core criminal offences. Work out the eight combinations first, then locate the positions in that space. Require these leaves: a strict fault-essentialist retributivism, a strong deterrence or consequentialist view, a two-track view that permits strict liability only for regulatory or public-welfare offences, a negligence-inclusive view, and a capacity-based view under which punishing the faultless is a category mistake. For each empty combination, say whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of positions the questions cannot separate and explain why. The point is to sort readers by their case judgments rather than by which label they like, so no question should ask readers to identify with a named theory.
