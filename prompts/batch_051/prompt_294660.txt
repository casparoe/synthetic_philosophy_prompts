I'm taking a seminar on archives and power this term, and my housemate volunteers at a community archive, so we keep rehashing what's worth keeping and what isn't. She walked me through three calls she made on mixed donations recently, and they all feel consistent but I can't pin down the actual rule shes running. The three:

- A full run of the city's daily paper from the 1980s, which the public library already has on microfilm, versus a near-complete set of flyers and matchbooks from a lesbian bar that closed in '92. She said scan a few front pages for context and let the rest go, but take every flyer.
- A carton of wage ledgers from a textile mill, with carbon copies already sitting in the county historical society, versus a single handwritten notebook where three generations of women in the same family logged their piece rates, the fines the foreman charged them, and the days they got sent home. She kept the notebook and didn't fight for the ledgers.
- A stack of studio portraits of former mayors, all already scanned and hanging in city hall, versus a crate of VHS tapes of a community-access show about the neighborhood from the mid-90s, no other copies and starting to smell vinegary. She took the tapes immediately.

My working theory was that she's running something like "fill the silences: keep the stuff about people the official record skipped" — factory women, the gay bar scene, neighborhood TV callers. But then a new decision came up and now I'm not sure. The archive can only take one of these:

A set of letters from a 19th-century domestic worker to her sister, already transcribed, digitized, and online — a historian published an edition years ago, and the originals are in perfectly fine acid-free boxes. Or a failing external hard drive from an estate sale, the only copy of a local real-estate developer's complete emails from about 2003 to 2011, the drive probably months from dying and nothing backed up.

Which do you think she'd take, and why? I care less about the answer than about how you'd reconstruct the rule from her track record. What are the candidate rules that fit all three earlier calls? What does each of them say she should do with the letters-versus-hard-drive choice? Which do you think she's actually running, and what single extra case — real or invented — would best settle which rule it is? My TA keeps saying it's more like "an archive should only spend shelf space on what would otherwise disappear," which sounds right for the tapes but i cant shake the feeling it misses something about the whole point of her choices.
