I have been arguing with a colleague about whether the limitations of word embeddings undermine the idea that their geometry is semantically meaningful. The passage I keep coming back to is from Mikolov et al., *Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality*:

“An inherent limitation of word representations is their indifference to word order and their inability to represent idiomatic phrases. For example, the meanings of “Canada” and “Air” cannot be easily combined to obtain “Air Canada”.”

Here is the argument I want to put forward, but I am not sure it is valid:

(1) The vectors for “Canada” and “Air” are learned from distributions of words, not from symbolic definitions.
(2) If these vectors encoded the full lexical meanings of the words, then there should be some compositional operation on the two vectors that yields the vector for the phrase “Air Canada,” since the phrase is just those words in a fixed order.
(3) The quoted result shows that ordinary vector composition does not yield the phrase vector; indeed, the paper treats “Air Canada” as an idiomatic unit that must be represented by its own vector.
(4) Therefore, either the word vectors do not encode full lexical meaning, or no general geometric operation corresponds to phrase composition.
(5) But if either of those is true, then the celebrated analogical regularities in embedding spaces (e.g., country–capital offsets) are probably not evidence of genuine semantic structure; they are just a distributional byproduct.
(6) So the vector-space approach to meaning faces a dilemma: either it gives up full lexical meanings, or it gives up the claim that vector arithmetic tracks semantic composition.

Is this argument valid? I suspect step (5) is too strong, but maybe the real failure is earlier. Go through the steps one by one: identify the exact step where the argument overreaches, explain what the quoted limitation actually shows, and say what, if anything, one can still legitimately infer about semantic structure in embedding spaces from the analogy results.
