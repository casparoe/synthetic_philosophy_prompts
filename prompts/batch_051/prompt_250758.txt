I am a high school student who competes in debate and also spends time reading online argument communities, and there is a recurring move in those settings that I have never seen given a precise name. The move looks like this. Someone challenges a current usage of a word by appealing to what the word used to mean, and they treat the older meaning as the word's real or correct meaning when it supports their conclusion. For example, they might say that decimate cannot properly mean "devastate" because it originally referred to killing one soldier in ten. So far this is just a familiar line of argument. But the pattern I am trying to isolate is the asymmetry that often comes with it: minutes later, the same person will use nice, silly, or awful in their modern senses without a thought for their older ones, even though those words have drifted at least as far. If challenged, they would probably insist that they never committed themselves to a general rule about all words; the earlier meaning mattered in the one case, they would say, because it happened to support what they were arguing.

What I want to know is whether this particular configuration has a name and how it should be carved up. It does not seem to be plain inconsistency, because the person is not claiming that every word should be used in its original sense, and it does not seem to be exactly the well-known mistake of thinking a word's history tells you what it now means, because this speaker applies that principle only when it is convenient. The older meaning is being treated like a witness called for one side rather than as a neutral rule about language. I am looking for an account of where the boundary lies between the simple historical fallacy, selective or opportunistic use of it, and special pleading — and whether the opportunistic version is best described as a species of one of these or as a distinct pattern that deserves its own term. A bare label will not help me much; what I am after is a precise definition of the asymmetry and the cases at its edges. Please also say how confident you are in your answer, and single out the point you are least sure about.
