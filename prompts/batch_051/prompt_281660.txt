Define 'bias' in a way that seperates it from ordinary preferences and tastes, but still captures what feels wrong in the cases where we reach for the word. Run your definition through all seven cases and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer.

Here are the cases:

1. A bathroom scale that always reads three pounds heavy.
2. A hiring manager who rates two otherwise identical resumes higher when the name sounds like her own background, and can't point to any other difference.
3. Someone who only gets their news from their own political side, and treats the other side's reporting as corrupt before actually looking at it.
4. A person who prefers tea to coffee and, when asked why, just shrugs.
5. A lending algorithm that uses zip code as a variable because it's predictive of repayment, even though zip code is also correlated with race; the model never sees race itself.
6. A person who only wants to date within their own religion, not out of any hostility to outsiders but because a shared way of life matters to them.
7. A referee whose individual calls are all defensible, but who across a season calls measurably more fouls on the visiting team when the home crowd is loud.

I suspect the word is doing at least two jobs—one about systematically missing some truth, and one about unfair partiality—and that no single definition gets all seven right. For the record: I think the scale is biased but not a preference, the hiring manager is biased, the tea-drinker isn't biased, and I genuinely don't know about the loan algorithm, the dater, and the referee. Propose the definition, run it through the cases, and say where you'd revise it, abandon a verdict, or defend a surprising one.
