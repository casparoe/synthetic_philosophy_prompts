I run a small cricket-farming operation—we raise crickets for protein powder and snacks. We're about to upgrade the processing line, and my operations lead (biologist by training) is pushing us to spend a real chunk of the budget on making harvest less awful for the crickets: slower gas knock-down, lower density in the crates, dimmer lighting, that sort of thing. Her line is that the crickets can suffer, and that if you can reduce suffering with a bit of a margin hit, not doing it is a moral failure. Our main investor thinks she's being sentimental. He says a cricket has about as much going on upstairs as the thermostat that regulates our grow room, and we don't stay up nights worrying about the thermostat's "interests." He'd maybe grant that pigs are a different story, but bugs and simple machines are, for him, basically the same category once the nervous system is simple enough.

I'm the one writting the check, and I find both positions too quick. I'm not convinced crickets are exactly like pigs, but I also don't think a cricket is literally a thermostat. Part of me thinks the whole issue is a matter of degree and we're all talking as if it's binary: either you count or you don't. Is there a defensible middle path? What would it actually be to treat insects and simple devices as different points on a scale rather than two bins, and how should that scale affect a decision like this? And is my operations lead right that "can suffer" is the relevant test, or is that too narrow?
