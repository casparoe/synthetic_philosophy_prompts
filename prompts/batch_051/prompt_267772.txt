I have been writing as though the phrase incommensurable values picks out a clear phenomenon, and I want to stop doing that until I have a definition that earns its keep. Give me a working definition of value incommensurability, one precise enough to sort a set of cases, rather than a survey of the literature. To keep the definition honest, here are eight choice situations with my current hunches about them.

1. Choosing between a $100 gift card and a $50 gift card to the same store. Clearly not incommensurable.
2. Choosing between two job offers identical in location, role, and hours except that one pays more. Clearly not incommensurable.
3. In a rescue, choosing which of two equally needy strangers to save when you can save only one. I would not call this incommensurable, but I want the definition to explain why it is not.
4. Choosing between a career as a research scientist and a career as a high school teacher, when both would make for about equally good lives though in different ways. I take this to be a paradigm case of incommensurable values.
5. Deciding whether to donate a sizable windfall to a global health charity or to a program that preserves threatened languages. I am genuinely unsure whether this is incommensurability or just uncertainty about how to weigh different goods.
6. Choosing between a cheaper apartment with a longer commute and a more expensive apartment with a short commute, where you have no stable trade-off rate between money and time. Maybe incommensurable, maybe merely multidimensional.
7. Choosing between punishing a genuinely remorseful offender and extending mercy, when mercy risks eroding deterrence. Unsure.
8. Choosing between two flavors of ice cream you enjoy equally. Clearly not incommensurable, but I want the definition to say why rather than stipulate it away.

Your definition should also tell me how value incommensurability relates to, and differs from, each of the following: incomparability, understood as neither alternative being better than, worse than, or exactly equal in value to the other; equality or being tied; being on a par, in roughly the sense from the hard choices literature, where two alternatives are in the same league but neither is better and they are not exactly equal; ordinary uncertainty about which option is better; and incompleteness in one's preferences. Then run the definition against all eight cases above, say where it gives a verdict at odds with my hunch, where it gives no verdict at all, and where one of the neighboring notions is doing the work instead. If the definition needs revision after running it through the cases, revise it rather than forcing the cases to fit.

Finally, I need to know which parts of this terrain are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on it. Do not present a contested view, such as the parity account of hard choices, as if it were the consensus. Please answer in American English.
