I'm a novelist, and I keep noticing a parralel between asceticism as a moral ideal and the minimalist streak in literary style — the whole cult of cutting. The ascetic says: deny the needless appetite, strip away what binds you, and something truer emerges underneath. The workshop version says: cut adjectives, delete the line you're proudest of, starve the prose of ornament until only the essential remains. Both treat subtraction as a kind of purification, and both assume that whatever survives the cuts is more you, or more the story, than what was there before. But I cant tell whether this is a real identity of structure or just two practices sharing a vocabulary of denial and restraint. Is the justification for ascetic self-denial the same kind of justification as the one for spare prose? Does anything transfer from one side to the other — say, the ascetic worry that self-denial can curdle into pride, or the stylistic worry that minimalism is just another style rather than a closer grip on the truth? Or does the analogy break because asceticism aims at transforming the self, while prose style aims at an effect on a reader? I'd like to know whether the parallel is deep or superficial, and where exactly it comes apart.
