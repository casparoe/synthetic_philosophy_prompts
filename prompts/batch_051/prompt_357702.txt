I'm tired of consent being used as a rubber stamp. People say it was consensual so it's fine, or she signed the form so it's fine, or he said yes when the alternative was terrible so it's on him. Before I read another theory of what makes consent valid, I want the spec itself: what any adequate account of valid consent has to deliver, restricted to the big three inputs — deception, coercion, competence. Don't survey existing positions and don't settle the substantive disputes; I want the requirements, the tensions betweeen them, and the edge cases that break simple rules.

Make each requirement earn its place with a micro-case, and make sure you cover at least these knots: hiding vs. actively lying, and within lying, a lie about the world vs. a lie about your own feelings; whether consent is to the act or to the act as described; threats vs. offers vs. merely bad background options; competence that fluctuates or is sortof present; and consent that can be withdrawn midway, and what that does to the idea that consent is a one-shot event. If some pairs of requirements can't be jointly satisfied, that's the part I most want spelled out. Then say which single requirement does the most work cutting down candidate accounts, without naming or scoring them.

Format: four sections in this order with rough lengths. "Fixed points" (short): the cases no theory may get wrong. "Desiderata" (long; numbered, each with its motivating case). "Tensions" (medium): pairs that look jointly unsatisfiable. "One-line payoffs" (short): which requirement excludes the most and why. Nothing outside these four.
