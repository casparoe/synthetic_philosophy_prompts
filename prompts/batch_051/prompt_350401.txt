For years I've been reading rationalist-adjacent takes on philosophy of physics, and the Humean/anti-Humean fight about laws of nature is one of those debates I keep checking back on and finding that nobody has moved. Both sides accept the same physics: nobody is arguing about the equations or the experiments. As I understand them, the Humeans say laws are just the best systematisation of the mosaic of local particular facts — the simplest, strongest summary of what actually happens, full stop. Anything beyond that, like primitive nomic necessity or 'governing,' is machinery you shouldn't pay for unless the data force you to. The anti-Humeans say a law isn't just a summary that happens to be true; it's what makes things happen the way they do, constrains what can happen, and supports counterfactuals in a way a mere true generalisation doesn't. On their view the regularities are explained by the laws, not constitutive of them, and they're fine paying for a primitive modal ingredient to get that.

I'm not asking who's right. I'm asking why this thing is stuck, and what kind of disagreement it actually is. My hypothesis is that it's really seperate disagreements stapled together: one about what explanation has to do, one about whether primitive modality is a cost or a feature, and maybe one that's partly verbal about the word 'law' — when I try to taboo the word, the argument moves a bit, but not much. I could also see it being a hidden question about which cases count as data: fundamental physics, special-science laws with exceptions, ceteris paribus generalisations, counterfactual intuitions, or scientific practice. Or maybe the question is just ill-posed.

So: diagnose the disagreement. Is it substantive, verbal, a difference in bedrock methodological taste, two groups answering different questions under one heading, or a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? And once you've diagnosed it, say what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a case from scientific practice, a change of question — or whether we should expect it to stay stuck and why. Please write in British English, and keep a clean line between what's actually settled and what's genuinely contested amont people who work on this; don't present a contested view as though it were the consensus.
