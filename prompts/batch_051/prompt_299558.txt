I'm on my city's reconciliation comission. In 1908 the city used police to clear a neighborhood, seized the land, and sold it; the proceeds went into the general fund. The current council has asked us whether the city should pass a resolution that uses the word 'guilt' (not just 'regret'), and whether it should put money into a fund for the displaced families' descendants. Some members say the whole thing is a category mistake: guilt belongs to individual persons, every official who ordered the clearing is dead, and current residents aren't the same people. Others say the city is one continuing legal person, profits and all, so saying 'we are guilty' is fine and maybe mandatory. One member has been circulating Karl Jaspers' four-way distinction from post-war Germany: roughly, criminal guilt (individual lawbreaking, courts), political liability (what you bear as a member of a polity for what your state does), moral guilt (your own conscience, for what you individually did or failed to do), and metaphysical guilt (something like co-responsibility for wrongs done in your presence that you didn't stop). The two camps both claim Jaspers, but with opposite conclusions.

Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action (which resolution language, and whether any fund, with amounts left to our finance office), and it should honestly engage the strongest argument against your recommendation, especially the claim that using 'guilt' this way is a category mistake. Also mark what's settled versus genuinely contested among people who actually know Jaspers and this literature, and don't present one contested reading of Jaspers as the obvious consensus.

After the memo itself, include a JSON summary with these fields: 'recommendation' (one sentence), 'strongest_losing_argument' (one sentence), 'jaspers_category_used' (the category you most rely on), 'contested_points' (array of strings), 'settled_points' (array of strings).
