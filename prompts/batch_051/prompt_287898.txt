Ok, here's one I'd love you to take a crack at. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've been arguing about coincidences for a year: a hard-nosed probabilist who thinks striking coincidences are almost always the birthday paradox plus selection effects, and a synchronicity-type who thinks at least some are too improbable and too well-timed to be chance, and point to some non-causal meaningful structure (they don't have to be Jungian about it). Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Be spesific about the birthday-style reasoning: what both sides can sign about why "somebody somewhere wins the coincidence lottery" is cheap, the difference between the probability of some coincidence happening to someone and the probability of this coincidence happening to you, and how each side would set the reference class for a single anecdote (meeting a stranger who shares your exact date of birth in a smallish town; dreaming of a friend seconds before they ring). Then make the disagreement exact: where exactly do the probabilist's selection-effect account and the synchronicity person's meaningful-structure account part ways, in wording each side would accept. I suspect two cruxes are whether "specified in advance" can ever be salvaged for a retrospective story, and whether the felt meaningfulness of a coincidence is itself evidence; you can add one more if you think it matters. For each crux, give a test, argument, or case both sides would accept as bearing on it, and write down each side's prediction ahead of time. Don't let either side hand-wave: the probabilist's claims need real denominators, and the synchronicity side's claims need to be about actual structure rather than just "we can't explain it." Please write the whole thing in British English.
