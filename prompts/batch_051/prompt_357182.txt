I am a retired natural scientist who has recently taken up philosophy, and I convene a reading group of thoughtful amateurs, mostly from the sciences. We are about to spend several sessions on the ethics of humanitarian intervention and regime change, using Kosovo, Iraq, and Libya as our recurring cases. Could you produce a one-page decision tree that a participant could work through at the start, of the sort that sorts people into a position on the basis of how they answer concrete questions rather than which label they prefer? I would like six named leaves: strict legal positivism about the UN Charter, just-war traditionalism, cosmopolitan liberal interventionism, sovereignty-first realism, anti-imperialist critique, and pacifism. The root should ask whether armed force may ever be used inside another state's territory, without that state's consent and without Security Council authorisation, to halt mass atrocities. The branch nodes should turn on distinctions on which these positions genuinely come apart: where legitimate authority resides; whether right intention must be pure or merely dominant; whether the Security Council is an acceptable arbiter or itself compromised; whether sovereignty is a fundamental value or merely an instrument; whether the permissible objective may extend beyond protecting civilians to replacing the regime; and whether national interest may ever form part of the justification. Please use the three cases at whichever nodes they illuminate, and after the tree briefly note any answer combinations that no leaf occupies, saying whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Also flag any pairs of leaves the tree cannot separate, with the further question that would separate them. Aim for roughly eight to twelve nodes, and write the whole thing in British English.
