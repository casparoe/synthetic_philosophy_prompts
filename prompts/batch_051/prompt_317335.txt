People I keep arguing with say internet memes and stuff like slang, fashion trends, and political slogans are literally replicators: variants crop up, get copied with modification, and the ones that spread are the ones that "fit," so culture evolves by natural selection in a strict Darwinian sense, not just as a loose metaphor. They use that to claim we can build a proper science of cultural evolution with memes as the units, and that "meme" isn't just a catchy word. I want an audit of how far the gene-meme analogy actually carries. Start with the correspondence table (what maps to gene, mutation, enviroment, fitness), then walk through the places where cultural transmission looks least gene-like — fidelity of copying, blending versus particulate inheritance, hosts tinkering with memes rather than copying them, and whether culture can even be carved into discrete units at all — and tell me whether the literal-replicator conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing the Darwinian work all along.
