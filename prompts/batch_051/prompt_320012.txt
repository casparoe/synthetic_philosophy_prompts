An AI lab decides its safety team has been coasting, so it sets up this scheme. Before each big training run, the safety team publishes a list of capabilities the model is not supposed to have — things like "writes working exploit code for up-to-date software" or "improves on a textbook recipe for a dangerous compound." After standard eval, the team gets a bonus for every listed capability the evals say is missing. If later red-teaming finds the model had a listed capability, they pay back the bonus plus a penalty. If red-teaming finds a dangerous capability that was never listed, the team gets fined, and the fine comes out of the list-writing subgroup's budget. The model team is paid flat for benchmark progress. Walk through what gets listed first, how the safety team spends its time, how the model team reacts, and where the whole thing breaks. Can any fix survive the same incentives?
