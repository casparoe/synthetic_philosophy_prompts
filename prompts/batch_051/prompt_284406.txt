You are a law clerk with deep expertise in criminal law and due-process-based vagueness doctrine. I am the judge you clerk for, and I need a bench memo analyzing a pending motion to dismiss on void-for-vagueness grounds.

The defendant runs a neighborhood watch social media account. After a series of package thefts, he posted security footage of a neighbor, identified the neighbor by name and home address, and wrote that the neighbor "should expect a knock." The neighbor, it turns out, was not involved in the thefts. The state charged the defendant under a newly enacted statute:

"Any person who knowingly and with intent to harass, annoy, or alarm another person publicly disseminates an image of that person's residence or motor vehicle together with information identifying the person's home address commits a misdemeanor; however, this section does not apply to dissemination made for a legitimate purpose."

The defendant moves to dismiss, arguing that the phrases "harass, annoy, or alarm" and "legitimate purpose" are so indefinite that an ordinary person cannot tell which postings are criminal, and that the language invites arbitrary and discriminatory enforcement against unpopular speakers. The prosecution responds that these are familiar legislative terms that gain content from context and from how a reasonable person would understand them. Apply the usual due-process standard: a penal statute is void for vagueness if it fails to give fair notice of what it prohibits or if it authorizes or encourages arbitrary and discriminatory enforcement.

Before drafting, work through the strongest arguments on each side and test the statute against a few clear and borderline cases. Then draft the short bench memo I will use to rule. Recommend granting the motion, denying it, or denying it while adopting a narrowing construction, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side. Focus on the vagueness question; you may note, but need not resolve, any separate overbreadth problem.
