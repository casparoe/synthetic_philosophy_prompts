I keep noticing a structural analogy between the way moral thought sizes up groups of people and a distinction from thermodynamics, and I cannot tell whether the analogy is deep or merely verbal. In thermodynamics the distinction between intensive and extensive quantities is exact. If you double a body in equilibrium, its temperature remains the same while its energy and entropy double. Intensive quantities do not scale with the size of the system; extensive ones do. Under composition the extensive quantity of the combined body adds, while the intensive quantity does not add: when two samples are mixed, the resulting temperature lies between the two — a weighted mean in the simple ideal case, not a sum.

I see something very like this in ordinary moral thought about population size. Sometimes we evaluate a family, a city, or a species by asking how well off its members are on average; sometimes we evaluate it by asking how much well-being or suffering there is altogether. The two come apart exactly when size changes. A city can add many moderately flourishing lives so that total well-being rises while average quality of life falls, and ordinary thought will say both that the city now contains more good and that it has, in another sense, worsened. When two cities merge, the total well-being of the combined city appears to be the sum of the two totals, while the average appears to be the population-weighted mean of the two averages. Average welfare behaves as if it were intensive; total welfare behaves as if it were extensive.

I suspect this is more than a pun, because both distinctions mark something about whether a quantity attaches to a local state of the whole or only to the whole as an aggregate. But I also suspect the analogy breaks, because well-being is not conserved the way energy is, people are not interchangeable molecules, and moral evaluation may have nothing corresponding to the conservation laws and equilibrium constraints that make thermodynamic talk well-defined. I want a diagnosis rather than an endorsement or a dismissal. State the mapping precisely; say what would have to be true on both sides for anything in the thermodynamic treatment of intensive and extensive quantities to transfer to moral evaluation; and name at least one thing the parallel would predict that turns out false.
