I am a doctoral student in philosophical theology, and I want to draft a working document for a seminar on the ethics and coherence of doctrines of hell. Draft an adversarial-collaboration summary between two camps: (1) a defender of eternal conscious torment, who holds that the traditional doctrine is justified by a principle of retributive proportionality, and (2) a universalist, who holds that divine punishment is ultimately restorative and that all persons will be saved. Treat annihilationism as a live third option that may pressure either side but is not itself one of the collaborating parties.

The document should contain four parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: claims about the moral gravity of sin, the character of divine justice and love, what a proportionate punishment would need to do, and what scripture and tradition settle or leave open. Second, a disagreements table: each row states one point of disagreement in wording acceptable to both sides, with each side's position set out in its own column. Third, a short list of cruxes, understood as sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. Fourth, for each crux, an argument, test, or thought experiment that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction registered in advance.

Focus especially on three narrow issues: whether an offense's severity tracks the dignity of the offended party, so that any sin against an infinite God carries infinite desert; whether a person in hell can continue to sin and thereby acquire new desert, so that ongoing punishment answers to ongoing sin rather than to the original finite sin; and whether annihilation could ever satisfy retributive proportionality in a way both sides could respect. Where the residual disagreement turns out to rest on different senses of justice or love, or on merely verbal differences, say exactly where and why.
