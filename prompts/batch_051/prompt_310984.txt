I'm a PhD student in neuroethics, and in our journal club every time brain-computer interfaces come up somebody reaches for the phrase "mind reading" — a decoder that lets a person spell by imagining handwriting, or reconstructs the gist of a sentence they're silently thinking, is literally reading the mind. The analogy is doing real argumentative work in the field: the standard move is that because the device extracts mental content, neural data deserves a special category of protection, closer to involunary self-incrimination than to ordinary biometric data, and that this is what justifies a distinct right to mental privacy / cognitive liberty rather than just stricter data-protection law. I'm sympathetic to the conclusion but I worry the "reading" picture is carrying more than it looks like.

So I want an audit of the mind-reading analogy. Give me a correspondence table first: in the BCI decoding case, what plays the role of the text, the reader's eyes, the reader's knowledge of the language, and the act of reading itself? Then separate the places where the mapping actually holds from the places it breaks, and be concrete about how the tech works when it matters — decoders are trained on the individual user, they output probabilistic reconstructions, they often recover semantic gist rather than verbatim language, and they rely on the person cooperating with the imagery task. Which of those disanalogies are load-bearing for the mental-privacy conclusion, and which are superficial? What does the analogy quietly import that the BCI case lacks, and which of those imports are doing the work? Finally: does the conclusion survive the disanalogies — that neural data is special in kind, not just in degree — or was the mind-reading image propping it up the whole time? If there's a better analogy, I'd like that too.

Please separate what's settled (empirically about the tech, and about the current shape of the neuroethics debate) from what's genuinely contested among people who know the area, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
