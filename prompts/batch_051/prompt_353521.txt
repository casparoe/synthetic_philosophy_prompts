I've been going in circles on this because it almost became my life a few weeks ago. I found out my former employer did something that a regulator would definitely care about, and I left on bad terms and was broke. It crossed my mind to ask for a bigger severance in exchange for staying quiet. A lawyer friend told me that would be blackmail. Fine. But here's what I can't shake: it would've been perfectly legal to just post the whole story, and perfectly legal to ask for more money. Seperately, either one is fine. Put them together—"pay me or I go public"—and it's a crime. Two rights add up to a wrong.

And the comparisons make it worse. "Settle or I'll sue" is a normal sentence; suing is legal and asking for money is legal, and the combination is fine. "Meet our terms or we strike" is legal too, even though the strike hurts the other side. So why is "pay me or I reveal your secret" different from "pay me or I sue"? Both are demands backed by a threat to do something you're allowed to do. In one case the threatened act is litigation, in the other it's speech—the more protected thing, theoretically.

I want your best attempt at a principle that separates blackmail from those other threats, and I want to know whether it's defensible morally, not just legally. Test it against at least one case where someone threatens to reveal something true but trivial (an embarrassing photo) and one where they threaten to reveal something true and serious (a safety defect), then say whether you'd keep the current line or draw it somewhere else. If your principle says the "pay me or I sue" demand should also be blackmail, own that.
