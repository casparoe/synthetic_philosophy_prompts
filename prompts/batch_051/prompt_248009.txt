I am putting together a unit for an advanced undergraduate philosophy course on pragmatism and realism, and I want a worked example I can walk through with students. The unit contrasts two positions. The first is representationalism: our best beliefs and theories succeed when they represent the world as it is apart from us, and the aim of inquiry is to close in on that representation. The second is Rortyan antirepresentationalism: vocabularies are not answerable to the world in that mirroring sense, and a community's shift from one vocabulary to another is better understood as a change in its practical purposes and self-description than as a gain in representational accuracy. The usual caricature, which my students already reach for, is that the representationalist has a fact of the matter to settle any dispute, while the Rortyan leaves every shift of vocabulary to convention or solidarity.

Construct a thought experiment that reverses that caricature. I want a realistic scenario — a hospital revising diagnostic categories, a school board replacing the language it uses for student misconduct, or a small firm rewriting its job descriptions — no exotic science, no perfect predictors, and no more than one paragraph. In the scenario, the two views must be brought to bear on a choice between two rival vocabularies, and it must come out that the representationalist, not the Rortyan, is the one forced to say the choice is rationally underdetermined or arbitrary, while the Rortyan can say that, by the community's existing purposes and standards, one vocabulary is plainly the one to adopt. The verdicts should be generated from the two positions as characterized above, not stipulated by the setup.

After the scenario, do the work. Spell out the reasoning each side uses to reach its verdict. Identify the single feature of the case that produces the reversal, and say what would happen if that feature were removed — does the case collapse back into the standard picture? Finish with the strongest objection a realist critic of Rorty would press, especially whether the Rortyan's confident verdict quietly borrows a notion of correctness that antirepresentationalism has no right to claim. Do not give citations or a reading list; the case should stand on the commitments I stated. Please write the entire answer in American English.
