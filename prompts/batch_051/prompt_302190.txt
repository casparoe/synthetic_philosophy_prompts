I keep circling around this bit from the Anattalakkhana Sutta (SN 22.59, Ñanamoli translation on Access to Insight):

Bhikkhus, form is not-self. Were form self, then this form would not lead to affliction, and one could have it of form: 'Let my form be thus, let my form be not thus.' And since form is not-self, so it leads to affliction, and none can have it of form: 'Let my form be thus, let my form be not thus.'

The argument seems to be that a real self would be in charge of the aggregates, and because form and the rest don't obey, they aren't a self. The objections I already have are: it quietly defines self as controller so it's no discovery; it only targets one historically specific idea of the self; it proves too much because nothing in any philosophy would pass that control test; and it leaves room for a non-controlling witness-self. I want one more objection to this passage's argument that is not on my list and not just a variant or mix of the ones on it, and tell me honestly whether it is any good.
