I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, so I'm at home with the standard decision-theoretic toolkit there: expected value, Kelly betting, log utility, ergodicity arguments, and the cluster of ideas people have started calling geometric rationality. Separately, I have a working knowledge of evolutionary theory—fitness, long-run lineage growth, geometric-mean reproductive success, bet-hedging, variance discounting in stochastic environments, and the distinction between what selection maximizes in expectation versus what actually survives over time. I can handle the mathematics, but I'm not a professional researcher in either decision theory or evolutionary biology.

I want you to translate the central claims of the Kelly/geometric-rationality/time-average camp, as they circulate in rationalist discussion, into the vocabulary of evolutionary theory. The claims I have in mind are roughly these:

1. Under multiplicative dynamics, the long-run growth rate of a portfolio, strategy, or population is given by the geometric mean of per-period growth factors, so maximizing expected per-period value can produce near-certain long-run decline through variance.
2. A rational agent facing a repeated positive-expected-value bet should not simply maximize expected wealth each period, but should choose the position that maximizes long-run growth rate, even at the cost of lower expected wealth.
3. Risk aversion and utility curvature are better understood as derived consequences of growth-rate maximization over different timescales than as primitive psychological attitudes toward risk.
4. Many apparently sound expected-value arguments fail in non-ergodic settings because ensemble averages over parallel possibilities diverge from the time average experienced by a single trajectory, and the time average is what matters for any one agent.

Translate each of these into evolutionary language as faithfully as you can: fitness as a growth rate, geometric mean reproductive success across generations, bet-hedging across variable environments, variance discounting, drift versus selection, lineages rather than individual organisms. I want the translation to be honest, not a rebranding.

Then give me the residue report. For each claim, tell me what carries over cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in the evolutionary vocabulary. Finally, take a position on what the residue shows: does it reveal something the evolutionary framework cannot say but that a decision theory needs to say, or does it reveal that some of the geometric-rationality claims are doing less work than their mathematical presentation suggests? I'm particularly interested in whether "maximize long-run growth rate" comes out looking like a theorem about what selection favors, like a normative rule for how one ought to bet, or like two different claims that have been run together.
