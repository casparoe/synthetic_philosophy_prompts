I am compiling a list of serious objections to Searle's Chinese Room argument, and I want a new one. So far I have four. (1) The systems reply: the man inside the room does not understand Chinese, but the whole system—man, instruction book, and database of symbols—might. (2) The robot reply: place the program inside a robot with genuine sensory input and behavioral output, so the symbols are causally connected to the world in a way the room's symbols are not. (3) The brain-simulator reply: have the program simulate the actual neural firings of a Chinese speaker's brain; if the intuition survives that, Searle's argument may prove too much. (4) Dennett's two-bitser argument: Searle dismisses the room's states as merely derived intentionality, but Dennett's two-bitser—a coin-slot mechanism whose states are about quarters only in a derived way—is meant to press the question of what non-question-begging difference makes a human brain original rather than just a more complex derived system.

I want an objection that is genuinely different from these four, not a restatement, special case, or combination of them. It can be an objection that argues the room does understand, or one that rejects the terms of the original/derived intentionality debate altogether, as long as it is not one of the four. Tell me what the objection is, how it works, and whether it is any good. Also tell me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to conclude that the new objection is weaker than I think, or where your reasoning about it is most likely to fail.
