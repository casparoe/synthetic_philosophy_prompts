After a long career in systematic biology, I have recently been reading philosophy of mind and language, and I keep returning to a parallel I cannot decide is profound or merely superficial.

One side is a puzzle about letterforms. We recognize the letter a across an enormous range of forms: the double-story a of a serif typeface, the single-story a of a child's handwriting, blackletter, italic, sign-painting, degraded photocopies. Yet there appears to be no fixed set of features shared by all and only a's. Any candidate feature—a closed bowl, an ascender, a particular stroke order, a particular proportion—can be dropped or distorted while the glyph remains an a, and some non-a glyphs share several of those features. In practice the category is robust; in analysis it resists definition by necessary and sufficient conditions.

The other side is a puzzle from my old discipline. The pre-Darwinian essentialist assumed that a species is defined by a checklist of characters, but modern systematics largely abandoned that view: species are variable, historically connected populations, and no intrinsic trait is necessary or sufficient for membership. We still identify species reliably, but not by applying a fixed definition of their visible features.

Both cases look like categories that function well in practice while lacking a classical essence, and in both, variation seems not to be noise around a norm but part of what the category is. I see obvious disanalogies too: species are linked by descent and reproductive continuity, whereas letterforms are cultural artifacts linked by imitation, convention, and design; and species are often treated as individuals rather than kinds, which I am not sure makes sense for a letterform. I cannot tell whether these disanalogies are fatal or whether they point toward a more general account of categories that covers both.

Please assess this parallel. Is it a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? State the mapping explicitly, say what would have to be true of both sides for conclusions to transfer from one domain to the other, and identify at least one thing the parallel would predict that turns out false.
