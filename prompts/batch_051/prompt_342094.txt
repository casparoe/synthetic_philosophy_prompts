I keep seeing people claim that a referendum result expresses "the will of the people," and I genuinly can't tell whether its a testable claim or just a high-sounding way of saying the winning side won. Suppose I wanted to find out. What's the strongest empirical test (or set of tests) you can design to check whether an actual vote — a California ballot proposition, a Swiss initiative, Brexit, whatever — is revealing a settled popular preference rather than an artifact of how the question was worded, who happened to turn out, the order in which issues reached voters, or campaign spending? Be concrete about the data you'd gather and what results would favor each reading. Then say what even a clean positive result would leave unsettled — e.g., whether a stable majority counts as "the will of the people" in any morally weighty sense, or whether "will" is just the wrong word. I want you to commit to a verdict on how far the claim can be tested, not list possible approaches.
