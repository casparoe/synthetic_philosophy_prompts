I chair my university's student activities fee committee. We've got a petition from the debating society asking to be moved from "academic club" to "club sport" status so they can draw on the athletics fund for travel, coaching, and tournament entry. Their pitch is that competitive debating is already a rule-governed, bracketed, judge-scored activity with a season and a national championship, and that the line between it and recognized sports is mostly institutional inertia; they also point out that much of the format is switch-side, so the activity is plainly about arguing well rather than about advancing some cherished belief. The director of student life is against it. Her worry is that argumentation is supposed to serve the person with the better case winning, and that if you write the check from the athletics fund and call it a sport, you will accelerate a trend where debaters optimize for judges and ballot wins instead of for actually getting a view right, which would make the activity worse, not better, at the thing that made it worth funding in the first place. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend one concrete action (reclassify, refuse, or some intermediate arrangement), take the director's worry seriously rather than just brushing it off, and say whether there's a coherent sense in which competitive argumentation is or isn't a sport. Also, please seperate what's fairly settled from what's genuinely contested among people who think about argumentation or sport, and don't dress a contestable position up as the consensus.
