I'm putting together a set of arguments for a discussion of Dennett's critique of Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality. I want to understand the case for thinking the distinction is not a principled one. So far I have collected these arguments:

(1) The two-bitser argument: Dennett points to a coin-detecting mechanism (a two-bitser) whose intentionality everyone agrees is derived from its designers, and then asks what principled difference there is between this device and any other intentional system. Since the same sort of external-selection story can be told about brains (via natural selection), the distinction seems to mark no real division in nature.

(2) The intentional stance argument: intentionality is not an intrinsic property of systems but something we attribute to them when we adopt the intentional stance. Since the predictive success of that stance does not require any original intentionality in the system itself, original intentionality is dispensable.

(3) The evolutionary argument: human minds have evolved through natural selection, which is a mindless process. Therefore the intentionality of human minds is itself derived from a process that does not presuppose original intentionality, which undermines the idea that biological minds are the unique locus of original intentionality.

(4) The explanatory redundancy argument: postulating original intentionality does no explanatory work that cannot be done by derived or instrumental intentionality plus the intentional stance. The distinction is therefore idle.

I would like one more argument for the same conclusion — that the original/derived intentionality distinction is not a principled one — that is genuinely different from these four, not a restatement, special case, or combination of them. I am particularly interested in an argument that does not simply appeal to the intentional stance as a general philosophical move. After giving the argument, I would like you to say honestly whether it is any good, and in what ways it differs from or goes beyond the arguments already on my list.

Please write your answer in British English.
