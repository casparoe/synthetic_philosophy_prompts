So I spent most of last night having a massive argument with a friend about origin essentialism — whether I could only exist because one particular sperm met one particular egg, or whether that would just have been a sibling wearing my name. I came away with a bunch of gut verdicts but no principle holding them together, and its doing my head in. Here they are:

1. If a different sperm had won, or if my parents had conceived a month later, the resulting child is a sibling, not me. I'm certain of it.
2. If the exact same sperm and egg had met in a lab dish rather than in my mum, it's still me. The location doesnt matter.
3. If the exact same sperm and egg had been frozen for a decade before being used, still me. Delay doesn't change who you are.
4. If the same sperm and egg had made a zygote which then split into twins, I don't think either twin is me — even though both came from exactly the cells that made me. That one keeps me up, because it means same-sperm-and-egg isn't the whole story.
5. If the same sperm and egg had made a zygote and someone snipped out one disease gene before it divided, still me. I wasn't swapped for a different person by a single edit. My friend insists this clashes with 4, and maybe with 1.
6. If a different egg from the same cycle, but the same sperm, had been fertilised instead, that's not me either. A fraternal twin who might have happened instead of me — not me.

Is there a principle that captures all six, or near enough? If not, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does the surviving view say about a case we couldnt resolve: if two early embryos had fused into a chimera, and one of them was "mine" (made from the usual sperm and egg), would the resulting person be me, and why?
