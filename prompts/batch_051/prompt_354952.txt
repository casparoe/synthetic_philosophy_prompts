I'm a backend engineer and I've been going down a rabbit hole on global workspace theory -- the idea that a mental state becomes conscious when its content gets broadcast globally to a bunch of otherwise-specialized subsystems and becomes available for report, planning, memory, and so on. The thing is, it sounds exactly like the pub/sub systems I build at work: a conscious thought is just the message that wins access to the shared bus, and everything downstream can then read from it and act on it. That's honestly why it feels right to me.

A friend of mine who actually reads philosophy keeps insisting this only explains the access side -- why some information is reportable, usable by other modules, etc. -- and not the phenomenal side, the raw what-it's-like. She distinguishes access consciousness from phenomenal consciousness and says the workspace account says nothing about the second one. I sort of want to say the second thing isn't a real extra, or at least that once you've fully specified the broadcast architecture plus the right downstream disspositions, nothing is left out. She puts her credence around 0.2 that phenomenal consciousness just is global availability in that sense; I'm around 0.65.

Give me your own number for this claim: phenomenal consciousness (the what-it's-like aspect) is fully identical to, or reducible to, the functional property of being globally available in something like the workspace sense -- no extra ingredient once the architecture and dispositions are fixed. Put a rough probability on each, show how you combine them into the final credence, tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to, which one my friend and I most likely disagree about, and what evidence or argument would move your number the most.
