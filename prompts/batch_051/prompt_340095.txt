I'm bothered by a double standard in physics. When a new observation disagrees with theory, nobody treats that as a place to stop—it's a puzzle to solve. But ask someone why fundamental laws or constants are the way they are, and the same person will often say explanation has to stop somewhere; maybe the laws just are. So unexplained phenomena count as problems, while unexplained laws count as acceptable endpoints. Is there really a difference between these two situations, or is that an inconsistency we've gotten comfortable with? And tied to that: if a final theory is allowed to have brute elements, how would we ever know we'd found the final theory rather than just run out of ideas?
