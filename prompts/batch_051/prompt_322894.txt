I've been going back and forth on what it means to say the function of a heart is to pump blood. I keep ending up with two ways of assigning functions — I think people call them the etiological account and the causal-role account, though I might have the labels wrong. One way says the function is whatever the part was selected for in the past, so a heart that never actually pumps is still for pumping, just broken. The other says the function is whatever the part actually does now, or would reliably do, so if an animal's malformed heart doesn't pump but thumps loudly enough to warn the herd about predators, then warning the herd is one of its functions. Compare these two ways of thinking, and work through the hard cases: a part that did one thing in the past but now does something else, a part that does something useful now but was never selected for it, and an organ that does nothing at all. Which view handles them better, or are they just answering different questions? Please answer in American English.
