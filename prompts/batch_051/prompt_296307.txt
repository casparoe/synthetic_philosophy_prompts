A colleague who works on model-based reinforcement learning makes the following case. Dennett's four 'kinds of creatures' — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — are usually read as a ladder of increasingly sophisticated minds. He claims this is the wrong picture: they are not four kinds of mind but one algorithm, generate-and-test, run in four different venues. In Darwinian creatures, natural selection generates and tests whole organisms; in Skinnerian creatures, behavior is varied and the environment selects through reinforcement; in Popperian creatures, hypotheses are varied and an internal world model selects by simulating their outcomes; in Gregorian creatures, words, tools, and memes are varied and culture selects. From this he concludes that thinking is natural selection moved indoors, so a system that learns an internal model and tests candidate plans in simulation is already a Popperian mind in the full sense, and what separates it from us is only how much of the tower it has climbed. Audit this 'selection all the way down' analogy. Give the correspondence table: what plays the role of variation, heredity, replicator, and selection environment at each level. Then identify where the mapping breaks: which features of Darwinian natural selection the analogy quietly imports into the other levels and whether those levels actually possess them. Separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the ones that do not. Finally, say whether the conclusion — that a simulation-testing learner is thereby a mind of the relevant kind — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and offer a better characterization of any level the analogy distorts.
