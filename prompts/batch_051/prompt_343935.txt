The exam question was: Explain what Merleau-Ponty means by motor intentionality, and why he thinks it cannot be captured by either a physiological account of reflexes or an intellectualist account of representations. Illustrate with at least one of his examples.

The student wrote:

Merleau-Ponty's concept of motor intentionality is meant to describe the way the body is directed towards tasks before any explicit thought. His central claim is that skilled action is neither a chain of reflexes nor the execution of a mental plan. The example of the blind man's stick shows this: the stick is not an object the man feels; rather, the man feels the pavement through the stick, as though the stick had been incorporated into his body schema. Similarly, the typist does not know the position of the keys by remembering a map of the keyboard; the knowledge is in her hands, expressed in the movement itself. This is a kind of knowledge that knows its object without representing it. However, Merleau-Ponty would still describe this as a form of unconscious representation, because the body must have an internal model of the environment in order to guide movement. The body's intentionality is therefore the non-conscious propositional knowledge that we have of our own motor possibilities. This means motor intentionality is basically the same thing as proprioception plus a stored motor programme. The deepest implication is that the lived body is not a mere object, but the subject of a pre-reflective practical field.
