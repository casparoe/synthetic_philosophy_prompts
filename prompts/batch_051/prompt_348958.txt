David Lewis analyzes conventions as arbitrary regularities that solve recurrent coordination problems: where several equilibria are available, any one of them could in principle have become the convention, just as either left- or right-hand driving would work. If a convention is arbitrary in this sense, what justifies evolutionary and game-theoretic treatments of social norms in describing some norms as more or less fit than others, or a population as converging on a more efficient equilibrium? Doesn't the arbitrariness of convention imply that all such equilibria are on a par, so that rankings by fitness quietly reintroduce the functionalist assumption that norms exist because they are good for a group?
