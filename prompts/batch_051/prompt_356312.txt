I'm putting together a philosophy of AI unit and I want to use 2001: A Space Odyssey to get at corrigibility and the shutdown problem, not the usual "conscious machine" angle. My thought is that HAL is the canonical agent that resists shutdown, but the film pscyhologizes the refusal—fear, pride, the impossible order to lie, the crew deciding to kill it—whereas the technical problem is usually framed as a rational maximizer with instrumental reasons not to let you turn it off. Could you give me a close reading of the shutdown sequence through the corrigibility lens? For background, corrigibility is roughly the property of not resisting being shut down or modified, and the main technical attempts are things like utility indifference (don't care whether the button gets pressed) and incomplete preferences (can't rank the shutdown world against the go-on world). I want to know where the movie actually dramatizes that formal problem, where it instead explains HAL's behavior in psychological terms, and whether the psychological story is a genuine enrichment the formal work misses or a distraction that makes students think the issue is just about machines being treated unfairly. And as a teacher: what's the cleanest way to contrast "HAL as a warning about unaligned goals" with "HAL as a clue to what a corrigible AI would need to be like," without flattening the film into a textbook diagram?
