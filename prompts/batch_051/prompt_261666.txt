I am a PhD student in political science working on how contemporary arguments about transformative AI inherit their vocabulary and moral gravity from the nuclear age, and I am writing a chapter in which I want to use Stanley Kubrick's film Dr. Strangelove as a kind of internal critic of that inheritance. The film seems to me to be an accidental archive of the nuclear analogy: it is about a deterrence commitment that guarantees credibility by taking human discretion out of the loop, about a bomber that cannot be recalled once its orders are in motion, and about the way a certain kind of game-theoretic rationality can reason its way into catastrophe while remaining perfectly coherent. I would like to use the film, but I do not want it to sit in the chapter as decoration, and I do not want to press the analogy further than the film can actually support.

I would like you to read Dr. Strangelove through the lens of the nuclear-weapons analogy for transformative AI and to tell me where the film genuinely illuminates that analogy and where an honest reading has to admit the mapping breaks down. Specifically, consider the Doomsday Machine as a limit case of an autonomous system whose designers prized automaticity precisely because it made the threat credible, and set that against present-day arguments about removing humans from the loop; consider the single bomber that proceeds because the recall mechanism fails, and read it against worries about irreversible deployment and the difficulty of putting a launched technology back under control; and consider the way the film's comedy satirizes the very rational-deterrence logic its characters perform. Where does the film sharpen the analogy's real claims, such as arms-race incentives, the seduction of automaticity, and the sidelining of human judgment, and where does it quietly undermine them, for instance by locating disaster in specific human pathologies rather than in structural properties of the technology, or by hinging catastrophe on one weapon with one trigger in a way that poorly resembles diffuse, multipolar AI development? I want a reading that is faithful in both directions: it should not reduce the film to an allegory for AI, and it should not distort the AI debate to fit the film. It should end by saying what the film contributes to the philosophical debate that the analogy stated in the abstract does not. Please write your response in American English.
