Write a dialogue between two friends who disagree about the moral weight of small promises and punctuality. One holds that small commitments—showing up on time, calling when one said one would, finishing a promised favour by an agreed day—create genuine obligations, and that breaking them for mere convenience is already a defect of reliability, independently of how much is at stake. The other holds that the force of such commitments is proportional to what reasonably depends on them: what matters is mostly not the non-performance but the consideration or disregard it expresses, so that a prompt, honest cancellation normally discharges whatever one owes. Have them argue through concrete cases: a last-minute cancellation with full notice, a friend who is habitually twenty minutes late, a favour one promised but now finds genuinely costly, and the question whether a person can be fully trustworthy while treating small promises as loose defaults. Ensure each character offers real replies to the other's strongest objections rather than trading slogans.
