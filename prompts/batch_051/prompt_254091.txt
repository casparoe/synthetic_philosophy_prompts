I am a student in an upper-level epistemology seminar this term devoted to the norms of assertion. We have spent several weeks working through the competing accounts — that assertion is governed by a knowledge norm, a truth norm, a belief norm, or a reasonable-belief norm — and through the problem cases usually pressed against them: lottery propositions, Moorean assertions of the form "p but I do not know that p," Gettier cases, and assertions made on merely statistical grounds. For our final session the instructor has asked us to bring something in a different register, and I would like to submit a satirical piece.

Please write a parody of a university Academic Integrity and Speech Code — formatted as a formal policy document with numbered clauses and a short preamble — for a philosophy department that has officially adopted the knowledge norm of assertion as its sole rule governing all public statements by students and faculty. The piece should run about 700 words, read like a plausible administrative document, and stay deadpan throughout.

The humor should come from getting the epistemology right, not from misdescribing the view. In particular, the policy should: define a "prohibited assertion" as any utterance of a proposition the speaker does not know; specify that implicature, presupposition, and questioning fall outside the rule even when they convey the same information; include a clause on lottery propositions under which a speaker may not assert "my ticket lost" however strong the probabilistic grounds; include a clause on retraction once the speaker's grounds are defeated; forbid Moorean assertions because the speaker cannot know the conjunction; and establish an enforcement committee whose task is to determine, not whether what was said was true, but whether the speaker knew it. Work in at least one unstated absurdity, such as the difficulty the committee itself faces in issuing a verdict it does not know on the policy's own standard.

The satire should be fair: a specialist should recognize the real consequences of the knowledge account being exaggerated, and a reader who has never studied the debate should still be able to follow the document and tell what is being made fun of.

After the piece, provide a brief structured summary as a single JSON object with the following fields:

- "target_view": one or two sentences identifying the position satirized.
- "form": the genre being parodied and the main ironic device.
- "exaggerated_feature": the actual commitment of the target view that the piece pushes to an extreme.
- "embedded_objection": the standard objection to the knowledge norm that the satire implies without stating it.
- "fidelity_check": one sentence assessing whether the joke misrepresents the position, and where a specialist might object that the satire has become unfair.
