I'm giving the commencement speech at a small liberal arts college. I'm a novelist, and the conviction behind everything I've written is that the self is a center of narrative gravity: there's no fixed true self waiting to be found underneath it all, just a character that gets written and revised and partly written by other people. The audience is graduating students, their parents, and the faculty. I need a ten-minute speech that does the job advice like "go find your true self" normally does at these things — something for them to leave holding — but I can't say that or any close cousin of it, because I think it's false and the philosophers in the crowd will catch every time I quietly smuggle an inner self back in. So the speech has to offer whatever the narrative-gravity view offers instead: authorship, revision, borrowed plots, the parts of your story that get written by other people whether you like it or not. It should be a real novelists speech, warm and concrete, not a lecture; don't mention Dennett by name, let the idea do the work under the surface. A little technicality is fine, but the parents should leave moved, not educated.
