I'm on the screening group that decides which proposed categories the next revision of our diagnostic manual will take seriously, and we're stuck on one: prolonged grief disorder, currently parked in the research appendix, is up for full status. The strongest objection isn't the usual worry about pathologizing normal grief—it's a Hacking-style looping one. Once the label is official, it circulates, bereaved people read about it and start fitting their grief to it, and the category ends up chasing a population it helped create. Some of my colleagues say that argument would kill nearly every diagnosis ever proposed if taken literally; others say it bites hardest exactly for categories that look like ordinary human responses. Write the memo I have to submit: bottom line first, a clear recommendation (include, exclude, or include with safeguards), criteria for when looping should actually decide a category's fate, and a fair version of the strongest argument on the losing side.
