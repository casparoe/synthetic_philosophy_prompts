You are a philosopher of psychiatry serving as an outside consultant to a working group that is revising a major diagnostic manual. The group is split over a proposed new category, and your job is to apply its own standing criteria honestly, not to settle nosology by fiat. Work in this mode: first do slow, criterion-by-criterion reasoning; state the strongest argument on each side before saying whether that criterion settles anything; then confront the looping-effect problem explicitly.

The working group uses four criteria, in simplified form, for whether a condition belongs in the manual as a mental disorder:

1. Clinical significance: the condition is associated with clinically significant distress or with impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning, or with a substantially elevated risk of those.
2. Internal dysfunction: it reflects a dysfunction in the individual's psychological, biological, or developmental processes, rather than a mere mismatch between the person and the environment.
3. Non-expectability and non-sanctioning: it is not an expectable response to a common stressor or loss, and it is not a pattern of behaviour that the person's culture or subculture values or approves.
4. Delineability: it is a discrete, recurring syndrome whose symptoms hang together with acceptable reliability and validity, are distinguishable from neighbouring conditions, and have a defensible boundary with ordinary variation.

Now consider the contested proposal. A coalition of clinicians and a large online patient community asks the working group to recognize a new diagnosis tentatively called self-narrative instability disorder, or SNID. The core clinical picture is persistent and distressing destabilization of a person's sense of who they are: their values, ambitions, and biography feel unmoored; they repeatedly and urgently rewrite their life story; they can recognize themselves only through the imagined or quantified reactions of an audience; and they describe their real self as inaccessible. The symptoms demonstrably interfere with intimacy and with career decisions in the people reporting them. Strikingly, the syndrome was first assembled around an informal social-media label, and the community developed a shared symptom vocabulary before clinicians formalized it. After the label and its draft criteria began circulating, people who had previously described themselves as merely indecisive, inauthentic, or a bit lost began to describe themselves in the new vocabulary and to report more of the listed symptoms. Some say the label made their experience finally cohere and reduced their self-blame; others say that identifying with the label intensified their symptoms. Clinicians disagree about whether this history shows the label is helping to produce the syndrome or merely making an already existing syndrome visible. The group is explicitly worried about the phenomenon Ian Hacking called a looping effect, in which classifying people changes the people classified, which then changes the classification itself.

Work through the four criteria one by one for SNID. For each criterion: give the strongest case that SNID satisfies it, the strongest case that it fails it, and a frank verdict on whether the criterion actually settles the question or leaves it underdetermined. Where a criterion underdetermines the verdict, say whether that is a defect in the criterion or a feature of a genuinely mixed case. Do not treat the social history of the label as automatically disqualifying, since many well-established diagnoses also have looping histories; but do not ignore the possibility that the looping here is unusually strong.

Then address the deeper question. Does the fact that SNID loops — that people change in response to the classification, and the classification changes as it circulates — show that one of the four criteria should be revised, or that a further criterion, such as stability of the category over time, is needed? Or does it show that a psychiatric category can be real and valid even when it is partly constituted by the act of classifying it? Finish with your considered verdict: should SNID be admitted, and what, if anything, should change about the working group's criteria?
