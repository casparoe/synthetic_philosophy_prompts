In my philosophy of probability course we have been working through Bertrand's chord paradox and the challenge it poses to the principle of indifference. I want to defend a specific diagnosis rather than survey alternatives. Please argue for the following position, as strongly and committedly as possible: the paradox does not show that the principle of indifference is hopeless; it shows that the principle is well defined only relative to a privileged partition of equipossible outcomes or a symmetry-derived measure, and that the rival uniform distributions over different parametrizations answer different tacitly specified questions rather than one question in conflicting ways. In exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, present the argument. Include at least one point that responds to the objection that choosing a privileged partition or symmetry group simply reintroduces the arbitrariness the principle was supposed to eliminate.
