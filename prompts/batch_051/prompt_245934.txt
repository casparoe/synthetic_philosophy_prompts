I sit on my university's student-organisation appeals board, and I need to circulate a short decision memo before thursday's meeting. The case is this. A wine-tasting society (I'll call it the Cork and Candle Society) was found responsible last spring for damaging a dining hall at an unregistered function, was fined 600 pounds, and placed on probation. Over the summer every single one of its officers and active members graduated or left the university, and in September a completely new group of students re-registered the society under the same name and constitution. Under our rules a registered student group is treated as a continuing entity seperate from its current members: it can hold a bank account, sign contracts, and carry sanctions across years. The new students are appealing the fine on exactly that point. They say the continuity is a legal fiction, that the society exists only on paper, and that upholding the fine would mean punishing a fresh cohort for something none of its current members did. The outgoing members say the fine should die with the old cohort. The panel is split, and our one law professor keeps insisting this is a clean test of whether a corporate-style identity is a fiction with real practical force or a fiction we should be willing to see through when it produces injustice.

I'd like you to act as an expert on the ontology of legal fictions who also knows how to write workable committee decisions. Think it through carefully, and before you write the final memo, spend a paragraph or two brainstorming: what is the strongest sense in which the society is still the same entity despite the turnover, what is the strongest sense in which it is not, and what each answer would mean for the appeal. Then draft the decision memo I'll circulate. It should have a clear bottom line (deny the appeal, grant it, or grant it with conditions), a concrete course of action with a timeframe, and an honest engagement with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than just restating the rules. Keep it around 700 words.
