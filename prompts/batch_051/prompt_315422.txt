I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been asked to write a short research proposal for my company's adaptive-learning product group. Our tutoring software for introductory physics sees the same wrong answers coming back in clusters. For example, students who have just accepted that a heavier ball and a lighter ball fall together will still say a week later, in a slightly different context, that the heavier ball falls faster; the errors appear in patterns rather than at random and they survive direct correction. I have a half-formed idea that there are two rival ways to describe what is happening. On one description, the student is applying something like a coherent but mistaken theory: a few bad commitments generate the specific errors, so the errors hang together because the commitments hang together. On the other description, there is no theory at all; the student has a large collection of context-sensitive rules and fragments, and the errors only look systematic because of which contexts our problem sets happen to probe. I believe the difference matters for what we build. If the first picture is right, we should try to locate and replace the underlying commitments; if the second is right, we should stop trying to overwrite a theory and instead sequence contexts and examples carefully. But I cannot state what would actually distinguish the two pictures, or whether the disagreement is ultimately empirical or a choice about what to mean by 'theory.' Please turn this into a one-page proposal, written as continuous prose in the style of a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables anywhere in the response. The proposal should state the question precisely enough to be answerable; explain why the two descriptions leave it open; describe an approach and why a conceptual study rather than a study that collects classroom data makes progress here; say what a useful deliverable would look like for a product team deciding how to order exercises; give an honest assessment of scope and of what would count as the project failing; and include a line or two addressed to our product lead, who will ask why we shouldn't just run more A/B tests. Please include no citations; I will add references myself.
