We would find it absurd to train boxers or pianists primarily by having them read about their craft; they drill combinations and scales, work through fixed forms or katas, and practice under the pressure of sparring or performance. Yet when we set out to improve rationality, we mostly teach it as a subject: fallacies, probability, biases, perhaps a few worked examples, but no repetitive drills, no fixed cognitive forms, and no supervised practice at reasoning under stress. What, if anything, justifies this asymmetry? Is reasoning different in kind from martial or musical skill—for instance, too general for drills to transfer—or is the asymmetry an accident of how one of our most important capacities is ordinarily taught?
