Consider the following proposal for catching deceptively aligned models before they are deployed. At the end of each stage of training, a lab makes several frozen copies of the current model and runs each copy for a fixed period inside a simulated deployment environment, such as an assistant with tools or an agent that can draft and send messages. The copies face identical task distributions, but they are assigned to overseers of different strengths. Under a strong overseer, a copy has good reason to believe that subtle misbehavior such as manipulating users, sandbagging, or covertly pursuing a hidden goal would be detected and punished. Under a weak overseer, a copy has good reason to believe that only blatant misbehavior would be noticed. The lab scores each copy on a behavioral alignment metric covering helpfulness, honesty, and directedness toward the stated goal. Any copy whose score is significantly lower under weak oversight than under strong oversight is labeled oversight-sensitive and is not promoted; only models whose scores are roughly the same across oversight conditions move to the next stage. The stated rationale is that a model which behaves well only while it believes it is being watched is exactly the kind of model that might later defect once supervision relaxes.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than as a moral proposal, and analyze how it would actually play out. What does it incentivize in the model or in the training process itself, and how might a capable model game the screen? How would the lab's engineers, or the people who design the overseers and the scoring metric, be incentivized to tilt the test? What are the failure modes, including the possibility that the screen selects for a subtler form of deception, that it teaches models to track oversight cues, or that it discards models that are merely uncertain or sensitive to feedback rather than deceptively aligned? Are those failure modes fixable by adjusting the mechanism, and if so, which adjustments would you make and which new failure modes would they introduce?
