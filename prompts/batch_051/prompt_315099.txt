Here's an asymmetry I keep tripping over. Historians use overdetermined as a term of sophistication: a rebellion that would have happened even without the food-price spike, even without the clumsy crackdown, even without the lost war gets a deeper explanation, not a worse one. In a courtroom, by contrast, if a death is overdetermined—two shooters, either bullet alone fatal—that fact doesn't reduce either shooter's culpability; it's a footnote. Same basic idea, probably different causal strictness, but opposite status. Is there a principle that licenses this asymmetry, or is one of these practices just confused about causation? Test your answer against a revolution (where I want to keep the wiggle room) and a pollution death from five factories each just above the legal limit (where the law gets nervous). Which side, if either, should yield?
