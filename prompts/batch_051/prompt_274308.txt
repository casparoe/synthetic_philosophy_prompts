I've been reading around the long-running dispute over whether Confucianism is a religion, and what strikes me is that it has not moved in decades even though the contending parties seem to agree on the main facts. All sides can agree that Confucianism involves ancestor rites, a conception of Heaven, canonical texts, and programs of moral self-cultivation, and that it lacks a church, a generally enforced creed, a sharp natural/supernatural boundary, and a founder comparable to a prophet. Yet one group concludes that by any reasonable cross-cultural criterion Confucianism is plainly a religion; a second concludes that it is plainly an ethical and political tradition, and that calling it a religion is a category mistake; and a third says the question is badly posed because "religion" is itself a modern Western category whose implicit contrasts between sacred and profane, belief and practice, and the religious and the secular were projected onto East Asia. The same stalemate reappears with Buddhism, which routinely gets carved into "Buddhist philosophy" and "Buddhist religion," and with so-called secular religions such as nationalism or revolutionary Communism, which are said to function like religions while repudiating the name.

My working hypothesis is that the impasse is not a hidden factual disagreement about East Asian history but a collision of three different projects: one side is trying to build a descriptive category that tracks some cross-cultural feature of human life, another is doing genealogy of the term and its political uses, and a third is arguing about what deserves the social and legal standing that comes with the label. I do not want a survey of the literature, and I do not want a verdict on whether Confucianism is or is not a religion. I want a committed diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is and what, if anything, would count as progress. Do not hedge by laying out the options and leaving me to pick: state which diagnosis you find most defensible, give its reasons, and where the evidence is genuinely equivocal, say which way you would nonetheless lean and why.

Format the answer as a single JSON object with no surrounding prose, with exactly these fields, each value a string:

- "substantive_component": any part that could in principle be settled by historical or social-scientific evidence, and why it has not in fact settled the debate.
- "normative_or_political_component": the part that concerns what should count as religion for purposes such as legal recognition, state regulation, or public respect, and whose interests hang on that classification.
- "diagnosis": a definite classification of the impasse, such as mostly verbal, mostly substantive, mostly normative, or a specified mixture, with one or two sentences of justification.
- "what_would_count_as_progress": the distinction, evidence, change of question, or institutional development that would actually move the debate, or an argument that no such progress should be expected.
- "least_confident": the component of the diagnosis you are least sure about and why.
