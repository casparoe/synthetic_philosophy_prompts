In Science and Method, Henri Poincaré argues that a cause too small for us to detect can determine an effect large enough to be unmistakable, and that we then call the result chance—not because there was no cause, but because it escaped our notice. Compare this idea with the modern concept of sensitive dependence on initial conditions in chaos theory. In particular, distinguish the epistemic claim that chance is ignorance of small causes from the dynamical claim that arbitrarily small differences in initial conditions grow to produce divergent trajectories, and assess whether Poincaré's account should be read as an early statement of the butterfly effect or as a substantially different view about predictability and determinism.
