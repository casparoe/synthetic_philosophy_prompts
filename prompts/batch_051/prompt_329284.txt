I've been fiddling with the absent-minded driver problem for an undergrad decision theory seminar, and I want to give them a version that isn't in the textbooks. In case the framing helps: a driver has to take the second of two identical exits to get home. Taking the first exit lands him in a bad part of town (payoff 0), taking the second gets him home (payoff 4), and driving past both forces him to double back (payoff 1). He's absent-minded enough that he can't tell the exits apart or remember what he did earlier, so the night before he commits to exiting with probability p at whatever exit he finds himself at; the standard ex ante optimum is p = 1/3. The puzzle is that once he's at an exit, conditional reasoning about which exit he's likely at seems to pull against the ex ante plan, and people who work on this genuinely disagree about how to model that.

Here's my variant: each exit now has a sign (Exit 1 or Exit 2), and the sign is legible with probability 1−ε and iledgible with probability ε, independently at each exit, with ε known to the driver in advance. The absent-mindedness is otherwise unchanged—by the time he reaches the next exit he doesn't recall what he saw or did at the previous one. If he can read the sign he just does the obvious thing (continue at Exit 1, exit at Exit 2); if he can't read it, he's in the old fog and has to use some single probability q of exiting at any unreadable sign, since an unreadable Exit 1 and an unreadable Exit 2 look exactly the same to him. I want someone to work through how the two standard lines of argument change. What is the ex ante optimal q as a function of ε? Once he's at an unreadable sign, what does conditional reasoning say about whether exiting or continuing is better, and how does that compare with the ex ante plan? In particular I'd like to know whether the familiar tension just smoothly interpolates between the original puzzle (ε = 1) and the fully-signposted case (ε → 0), or whether there's a threshold where one side of the argument stops applying. I keep getting a piecewise-looking answer and can't tell if I've botched the conditional probabilities.
