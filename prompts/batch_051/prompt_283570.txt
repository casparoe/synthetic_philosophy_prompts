I keep running into "poverty of the stimulus" being used as a trump card in rationalist-adjacent arguments about AI and language. Recently someone on a forum dismissed a whole line of LLM results by saying "that doesn't touch the poverty of the stimulus" — the idea being that a child picks up language from far less and far messier input than GPT's corpus, so the fact that a model learns grammar from terabytes of text says nothing about whether humans need innate structure. But when I press people on what exactly is supposed to be too poor, the phrase seems to do different work each time: sometimes its about the sheer size of the data, sometimes about the absence of negative evidence, sometimes about the child never hearing certain crucial constructions, sometimes about the difference between the input that's out there and what the child can actually use. I want to know what "poverty of the stimulus" can actually be made to mean. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that phrase might stand for — what is supposed to be too poor, relative to what, and in service of what conclusion — and say for each whether it's trivially true, substantive and defensible, or just false? And which reading, if any, would actually let someone use it to brush off LLM evidence the way that commenter did?
