I lead a small reading group of working programmers; most are pretty convinced that thinking is just talking to yourself, so coding, being more precise, is basically thinking made visible. Next session I want to ask whether programming is a language of thought in the strong sense: the medium thought actually runs in is a compositional symbol system with syntax and semantics, like a programming language, not merely a tool for expressing thought. Plan the explanatory arc, not the explanation itself: the pieces that have to go in and what depends on what before the strong claim is even statable, the one carrying example and waht it must have and lack, the misconceptions to expect ('syntax proves thought is compositional,' 'LLMs write code so they think in code,' 'language of thought is just inner speech in formal notation') and where in the sequence to defuse each, a check for understanding that would show they can separate the strong claim from the weak one, and what I should openly leave out at this level.
