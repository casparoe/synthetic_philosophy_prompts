I am preparing to moderate a department colloquium Q&A with a visiting metaphysician who defends a Gödel-style modal ontological argument: she holds that a small set of axioms about positive properties, together with the modal system S5, entails the necessary existence of a being exemplifying every positive property. I have just come from a long argument with a friend about this proof, and the conversation kept circling two moves — his insistence that the positivity axioms presuppose the conclusion, and my own difficulty saying what the proof is even for if it cannot rationally compel an atheist. Draft eight to ten colloquium questions that test the argument at its real pressure points rather than asking her to restate it, omitting purely biographical or scene-setting questions. I want at least one question, with a follow-up anticipating the reply she is most likely to give, on each of the following: (1) whether positivity can be given a non-circular interpretation once necessary existence is declared positive, and why existence-involving properties belong on the same scale as omniscience or omnipotence; (2) the charge that the derivation of the possibility of a God-like being does all the work and that the axioms quietly encode its necessity; (3) the modal-collapse worry that the proof combined with S5 collapses modal distinctions, so that every true proposition comes out necessary; and (4) the dialectical aim of the proof — demonstrative proof, consistency result, or something else — and what sort of objection would count as a genuine defeater, for instance a counterexample to an axiom or a pair of intuitively positive properties that cannot be co-instantiated. For each of these four areas, write the follow-up that puts the most pressure on the reply the guest is most likely to give.
