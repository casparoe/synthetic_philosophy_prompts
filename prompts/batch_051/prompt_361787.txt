The required leaves are:

- Heterophenomenology in roughly Dennett's sense: subjects' reports are treated as data about what subjects say and believe about their experience, while the science remains neutral about whether there is a phenomenal fact behind the report.
- Illusionism or eliminativism about phenomenal properties: sincere reports are widespread, systematically false or misleading posits, and the explanatory job is to explain the reports away rather than merely to bracket them.
- First-person realism with privileged access: sincere reports are authoritative, or at least strongly privileged, about the experiences themselves.
- A fallible-but-trainable-instrument view: reports are genuine data, can be systematically wrong, and can be improved through disciplined first-person methods without thereby becoming incorrigible.

The angle that matters for our purposes is experimental design: we need to decide how much weight a verbal report gets relative to task performance, neural measures, and behavioral dissociations, and whether the report is the target of explanation, a measurement, or a confabulation to be explained away. Keep the instrument to six to eight questions and at most seven leaves.
