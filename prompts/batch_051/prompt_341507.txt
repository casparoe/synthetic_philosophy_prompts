Most adults would say that deceiving another adult for months or years about something the other person cares about is a serious wrong, yet many of those same adults regard it as not merely acceptable but good parenting to maintain an elaborate, years-long deception of a child about Santa Claus or the tooth fairy — and they do this while punishing the child for lying. Is there a principled distinction that justifies this asymmetry, or is it only a sentimentalized convention? The strongest candidates are something about the child's developmental stage, the difference between a literal lie and a shared fiction or game, or the benevolent aim of the deceit. Formulate the strongest version of the best candidate and test it against nearby cases, such as a parent who benevolently lies to a child about a grandparent's death or a serious illness. Give an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept, narrowed, or abandoned.
