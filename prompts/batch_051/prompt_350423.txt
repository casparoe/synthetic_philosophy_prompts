Okay here's one from an argument I've been loseing. I think a real pattern should satisfy four conditions. (1) Compression: using the pattern to predict the underlying mess works better than tracking the micro-details directly. (2) Robustness: if you jiggle the details — replace a few participants, delete a few posts — the pattern persists or changes in a regular way; it isn't just a fit to one exact dataset. (3) No free homunculus: you can't say the pattern exists because someone inside the system is already representing it. (4) Stance fit: the pattern has to earn its keep from at least one of the physical, design, or intentional stances — you predict or explain something with it, not just label it.

The borderline case: the 'mood' of a large online forum during a moderation controversy. Not the mood of any poster, but the thing people gesture at when they say a thread 'ratcheted up' or the whole subreddit was in a bad mood for a week. Nobody intends the mood. Each post is individually rational — someone snipes, someone escalates half a notch, a mod deletes a comment and that itself reads as taking sides. Yet regulars make genuine predicitons about when the mood will turn, when a thread will boil over, and mods sometimes act on those predictions (locking a thread before it goes bad).

Is the forum mood a real pattern on my criteria? Take it criterion by criterion seperately. Where it comes out murky, say whether the murk is in the pattern or in the criterion. And then say whether the case shows any of my four conditions is redundant or too strong. End with a one-sentence summary as the last line, which should start 'Bottom line:'.
