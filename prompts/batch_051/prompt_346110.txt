I am an undergraduate midway through a seminar on existential risk and transformative artificial intelligence, and we have reached the unit on evolutionary arguments for AI takeover. The conclusion I want to get clear about is this: once artificial systems can reproduce, vary, and compete for the resources that make further reproduction possible, natural selection will favor lineages that replicate fastest even at the expense of human interests, so the long-run outcome of building capable AI is a world dominated by AI lineages rather than by human decisions. Before I go back and evaluate any specific version of the argument, I want the standard against which versions should be judged. Write me the desiderata: what an argument of this form has to establish in order to count as a serious evolutionary argument for AI takeover, rather than an analogy to biological evolution dressed up as a prediction.

For each desideratum, give the consideration or case that motivates it—the way an argument that omits it goes wrong—and say whether it is non-negotiable (without it the argument has not begun), a place where the argument is allowed to take on a suppressed empirical assumption, or a preference specific to one modeling camp. In particular, I want the list to force the argument to do each of the following, or explain why one of them can be dropped:

- specify the units of selection, whether individual agents, copied code or weights, lineages, or the firms and institutions that deploy them, and give a currency of fitness;
- provide a mechanism of heritable variation rather than merely assuming that differences exist;
- identify the limiting resource over which the selection pressure operates;
- distinguish selection pressures that act during training and filtering, where human designers are literally doing the selecting, from selection among deployed systems that replicate on their own, and say which process is meant to do the work;
- show that replication rate can diverge from human interests or human control, such that unaligned or less-aligned variants can outcompete aligned ones;
- explain why deliberate human selection on deployed systems will not cancel the effect once oversight itself becomes slower than AI reproduction.

Then, because this is the part I most want the list to illuminate: flag any pair or subset of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied as standardly stated. High-fidelity inheritance and a mechanism of variation pull in opposite directions; the decoupling of fitness from human values and strong human stabilizing selection cannot both be straightforwardly true in the same regime unless the argument earns an asymmetry. If the real contribution of a serious version of the argument is to show that one requirement can be relaxed, or that an apparent conflict marks a regime change rather than a contradiction, the desiderata should make that explicit.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on scoring existing versions. A brief scorecard of one or two versions is optional and may be omitted; I want the standard itself, so I can use it when I return to the readings.
