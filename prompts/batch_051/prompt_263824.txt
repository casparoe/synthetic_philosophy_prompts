I am a novelist, and for the past year I have been researching a novel whose central character is a professional gambler turned investor. In the course of that research I keep encountering the Kelly criterion, and a friend who knows the project recently sent me a long explainer that presents the idea and its philosophical upshot with great confidence. Its gist, as I understand it, runs as follows. Kelly proved that when you face a stream of favorable wagers you should maximize the expected logarithm of your bankroll rather than its expected value, because only the logarithm compounds correctly across repeated bets; the resulting fraction, f* = (bp - q)/b, is therefore the unique rational position size, and any smaller bet is a failure to take compounding seriously—an emotional concession to risk aversion. The piece then generalizes. Drawing on work in ergodicity economics, it claims that the arithmetic ensemble average of a multiplicative process is a statistical fiction, that the time average is what a decision-maker actually lives through, and that log utility is therefore not one risk preference among many but the only way of valuing outcomes that is consistent with being rational under uncertainty. It closes with the thought that anyone who half-Kellys their way through life is quietly conceding that they do not understand why compounding matters.

I had planned to make my protagonist someone who understands the mathematics but deliberately underbets in order to survive bad years, so I need to know whether that portrait is psychologically plausible or merely mathematically confused. Is the explainer's summary fair to what the Kelly criterion and the geometric-rationality arguments actually establish? I would like it audited as close to sentence by sentence as it permits: what is right, what overstates the result, what conflates a growth theorem with a norm of rationality, and whether the concluding dismissiveness about half-Kelly betting follows from the accurate parts. Also, because I will be turning this into fiction, tell me where artistic license would most distort the ideas rather than merely simplify them.
