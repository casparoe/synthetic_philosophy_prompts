This exercise is for a unit on causal discovery, and its point is to build a single concrete example showing that a graph can satisfy the Causal Markov Condition and Minimality while still failing Faithfulness.

Work with the following definitions. For a DAG G and a joint distribution P over its variables:

- Markov: P satisfies the Causal Markov Condition relative to G iff each variable in G is independent of its non-descendants conditional on its parents (equivalently, every d-separation in G corresponds to a conditional independence in P).
- Faithfulness: P is faithful to G iff every conditional independence that holds in P corresponds to a d-separation in G, so that the only independences in P are the ones the Causal Markov Condition entails.
- Minimality: G is minimal relative to P iff P satisfies the Causal Markov Condition relative to G, but P violates it relative to every proper subgraph of G (obtained by deleting at least one edge).

Construct such a case under the following constraints: exactly three binary variables; all eight joint configurations have positive probability, with no deterministic or logical relations among the variables; and the failure of faithfulness must arise from an extra conditional or unconditional independence, not from a degenerate distribution.

Then provide the following.

1. The DAG and the distribution, written as conditional probability tables that fully determine the joint distribution.
2. The Markov check: state the independence relations the DAG entails and confirm that the corresponding independences actually hold in P.
3. The faithfulness failure: identify a conditional or unconditional independence in P that the DAG does not entail, and explain why the DAG does not entail it.
4. The minimality check: for each proper subgraph, give one independence relation that subgraph would entail and show that P violates it, so that no edge can be removed.
5. Diagnosis: identify the numerical feature of the distribution that does the work — the cancellation, mixing, or interaction that produces the extra independence — and say what would happen if that feature were removed. Then give the strongest objection a defender of the Faithfulness assumption could press against treating this kind of case as a serious counterexample, and say how much force you think that objection has for the purpose of the example.
