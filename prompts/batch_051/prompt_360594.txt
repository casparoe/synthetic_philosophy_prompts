Take the euhemerist thesis that at least some of the gods began as human beings, and put pressure on the form of the thesis rather than on the label. I want to distinguish, at minimum, an ascending claim (a particular deity was once a mortal and only later became an object of worship), a memory claim (the narrative about a deity is a distorted residue of real persons and events), and a descending claim (later interpreters retroactively turn gods into humans, so the rationalizing reading is itself the phenomenon to be explained rather than evidence about origins). Treat the ascending claim as the strongest testable core, with the memory claim hanging on it.

Design the best test you can of the ascending claim using a natural contrast: deifications of documented mortals, such as rulers or founders worshipped within living memory or a few generations of death, against deities for whom no historical substrate is attested. Specify exactly which features of the two corpora should be compared — for instance, biographical specificity, the age of the figure at the moment divine status attaches, the retention of moral flaws, whether cosmic or natural functions accumulate after deification, and how the cult explains its own origin — and state what pattern across those features would favor the hypothesis that the two corpora differ in origin, and what pattern would favor the skeptical reply that they differ only in how much documentation happened to survive. Include a check against the descending claim: how would you tell a genuinely remembered mortal-turned-god from a god who acquired a mortal biography later because that was the available idiom of commemoration?

Conclude by saying precisely what a positive result would and would not show. Suppose the documented-deification corpus displays the predicted signature. What follows about any particular god with no attested human substrate, and what follows for the unrestricted thesis that the gods were once people? Identify the residue that no comparative test of this kind could eliminate even in principle, and say whether that residue is a limitation of the evidence or a defect in the thesis itself.
