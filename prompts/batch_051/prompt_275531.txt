I spent most of my twenties in a political party and left this winter after a perfectly ordinary housing policy fight curdled into a loyalty contest. Since then I've been chewing on Simone Weil's claim, from the piece on doing away with political parties, that a party is totalitarian by nature rather than by the accident of bad leadership: it exists to convert collective pressure into uniform opinion, and its own growth becomes its first goal even when the platform started out decent. I agreed to run a two-hour political-education session for my old branch — akward, I know — about twenty people, most still members, and I want the structural claim to land instead of sounding like my personal breakup speech.

Plan the session, don't write it. I need the dependency order: what has to be in place before the structural claim even makes sense, and what builds on it. I need the one carrying example, and please don't reach for fascist or Stalinist parties — it has to bite for a normal, well-meaning center-left branch. I need the misconceptions this crowd will form in the order they'll form them, with the exact point in the sequence to head each one off. And I need a closing question whose answer would show me whether someone got it, plus the thing I should openly say I'm not covering.

Give me exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro, no conclusion. Item 1: the idea that has to come first and why. Item 2: the carrying example and why it works. Items 3 and 4: the two misconceptions you'd defuse and where in the sequence. Item 5: the closing check and what to explicitly skip.
