I keep a vegetable garden, and over the years I have moved from thinking of insects simply as pests to thinking of them as creatures about which I am genuinely uncertain. I read somewhere that at any moment there are on the order of a billion billion insects alive, a number so large it is hard to hold in the mind. That led me to an uncomfortable line of thought. For any single insect, I do not know whether it can feel anything; it may be a little biological machine that detects damage and responds without any experience of pain. But if I allow even a modest chance that it does feel something — say, five percent — then across a billion billion individuals the expected amount of unnoticed or unaddressed suffering becomes enormous. It ends up feeling less like a settled fact about bugs and more like a bet with vast stakes, placed on a question I do not know how to answer.

So I want to think about whether the claim "insects are sentient" is the kind of claim that can be tested, at least in part, and what a strong test would look like. One could imagine measuring whether an injured insect grooms or protects the damaged part; whether it will pay some cost to avoid a place where it was harmed; whether it keeps doing a harmful thing when its simpler responses are blocked; whether drugs that ease pain in humans change insect behavior in a parallel way; whether harm disrupts the other things an insect is trying to do rather than merely setting off a fixed reflex. My confusion is that I cannot tell which of these would count as genuine evidence of felt experience and which are just more elaborate reflexes, consistent with no inner life at all.

Set out the strongest concrete test or combination of tests you can, and say what each possible result would show for the claim. Then address the part I find hardest. Suppose the test comes out positive: does that settle that an individual insect has conscious experience, or only that it has some of the machinery and behavior we associate with pain? And suppose the test comes out negative or ambiguous: how should that interact with the sheer-numbers argument — is acting on a small probability across a vast number of individuals still a claim about what insects are, or is it really a claim about how careful we should be under uncertainty? Please be explicit about what no experiment could ever settle here.
