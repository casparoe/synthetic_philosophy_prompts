I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been reading philosophy of language on my own because my team is building an NLP system that has to handle figurative language — sarcasm, idiom, metaphor — and our current rules keep either missing metaphors or treating ordinary literal statements as figurative. A blog post aimed at NLP practitioners summarized Davidson's view of metaphor and contrasted it with conceptual metaphor theory, and I'd like to know how much of the summary I can trust. The post's account went roughly as follows. Davidson's position is that a metaphor means exactly what its words mean literally and nothing more: taken literally, a line such as the one in which Romeo calls Juliet the sun says only that Juliet is the star at the center of our solar system, which is false, and for Davidson that is the end of the story semantically. The familiar sense that a good metaphor tells us something more is, on this picture, a psychological aftereffect — the words cause us to notice a resemblance the way a picture or a sudden shock might, but the association is not part of what the sentence means. The post then said this pits Davidson directly against conceptual metaphor theory, according to which a phrase like ARGUMENT IS WAR is the surface expression of a systematic mapping in thought, so that metaphors do carry content beyond the literal. It concluded that Davidson's argument settles the matter: if metaphors had content over and above the literal, that content ought to be statable in literal terms, but no literal paraphrase captures an ARGUMENT IS WAR mapping, so the conceptual metaphor program is chasing a kind of meaning that cannot exist.

This struck me as probably too quick, because the post seemed to slide between meaning, content, and what a sentence says as if they were the same thing, and I suspect the two camps may be answering different questions rather than contradicting each other. But I don't have the background to adjudicate, and I'd rather not build our metaphor-handling code on a caricature of either view. Could you give a sentence-by-sentence accuracy audit of that summary? In particular: which attributions to Davidson are accurate and which are overstatements, what Davidson would say a metaphor does if it doesn't carry special semantic content, whether the argument about literal paraphrasability actually refutes the conceptual metaphor program, and whether the post's either/or choice is a real forced choice.
