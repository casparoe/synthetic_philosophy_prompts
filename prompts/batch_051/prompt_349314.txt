I've spent two years studying the actual painters and workshops, and I credit my sources in my artist statement, but my critique panel is split. Half of them say the work is cultural appropriation, full stop; the other half say that without theft, mockery, or cluelessness its not appropriation at all. I realized I can't articulate what the line actually is.

So could you give me a working definition of cultural appropriation specifically in art? Please write it so it sorts the following cases. I'm fairly confident the fashion house case is appropriation and the calligrapher case isnt, but the rest I keep flipping on: (1) a white American guitarist who learned the blues from old records and now plays that idiom for mostly white audiences; (2) a European fashion house printing Amazigh (Berber) tattoo motifs on a dress line without credit or compensation to the community; (3) a non-Native painter making abstract work inspired by Australian Aboriginal dot painting, after years living and working alongside the community and with their permission; (4) a Korean ceramicist who trained in Japan and now makes tea bowls in a style closely tied to a Japanese master she never met; (5) a museum displaying religious objects taken under colonial rule, over the originating community's objections; (6) a non-Native performance artist wearing a feathered war bonnet in a piece critiquing toxic masculinity; (7) a novelist from the majority culture writing a whole book from the point of view of a marginalized character about displacement; (8) a Japanese calligrapher continuing the calligraphic tradition she grew up in.

Then run your definition through each case. If it gives a verdict you think is wrong, revise the definition or defend the verdict, and say which of the eight verdicts you're least confident about and why.
