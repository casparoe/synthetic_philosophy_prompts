I run the grantmaking side of a small foundation that mostly funds animal-welfare work (laying hens, carp, some wild-insect stuff) plus a little human global health. Every quarter we score about sixty proposals on two 1–10 scales. Scale A is "moral weight," our rough number for how much the affected being's experience counts: humans and some mammals a 9 or 10, pigs maybe a 6, carp a 3, a fruit-fly larva a 1. Scale B is "per-individual welfare gain," how much each affected individual is expected to improve, with 10 being roughly saved-from-torture and 1 a mildly positive nudge. We move a couple million a year, and while the board signs off on the final slate, staff ranking drives almost everything.

The team can't agree on how to combine the two. Half of them add. Half multiply, partly because multiplying feels right for the consciousness-times-happiness idea: a barely sentient fly shouldn't have its big welfare-gain score count as an independent benefit, it should be discounted by how little its experience matters. A third faction wants a floor on A, then rank by B. The board likes additive because its easy to explain.

I need a one-and-a-half-page procedure my non-philosophical staff can use next quarter, twenty minutes per proposal, that tells them when to add, when to multiply, and when to treat one scale as lexically prior. Start from what actually makes two scores add, multiply, or stack lexically, and translate that into a checklist with no theory words in it. Then work it through on at least these three docket items: a farmed-fish intervention (A=3, B=9), a human surgical-pain program (A=9, B=2), and a fruit-fly-larvae replacement scheme (A=1, B=7). I also keep hearing that "treat A as a hard gate, then rank by B" is not the same as "treat A as lexically prior"—my team uses them interchangeably, and I need the procedure to say when the difference actually flips a decision; give a fourth case where the two come apart. Flag the residual judgment calls the checklist can't settle, because those are the ones I'll have to take to the board.
