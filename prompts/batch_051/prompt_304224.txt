For some years I have been writing fiction that depends on branching narrative structures, and I keep noticing a feature of point of view for which I have no name, though I suspect it has been articulated somewhere.

When a focal character meets a fatal outcome in one strand of a branching story, the narrative never follows that outcome through from the inside. It shifts to the survivors, or to another strand, or it turns to the second person; but a second-person sentence announcing the reader's death is still addressed to a reader who is plainly alive and reading. The same pattern governs ordinary first-person fiction. The bare fact that a story is narrated in the first person implies that the narrator survived long enough to tell it, and in the unusual cases of a dead narrator the narration continues past death, which means the point of view has been relocated rather than extinguished. If a first-person narration stops at the moment of death, it stops exactly at the boundary; it never reports the far side. A point of view, it seems, cannot narrate its own disappearance. Every narrative perspective selects, as a matter of its own grammar, the continuation in which it persists.

I can see that this is adjacent to the commonplace observation that only those who survive get to tell the story. I also half-remember being told of a thought experiment in physics in which a conscious observer should never find himself in a branch of the wave function in which he has died, since there would be no one in that branch to make the finding; but I do not know how seriously that is meant, and I am not certain it is the same idea as mine. What I am looking for is the general concept, literary or philosophical, for the necessity that a presented perspective always inhabit its own continuation.

Does this have an established name? If it does, I want to know how it is defined, and how the literary version differs from the physical or metaphysical one. If the nearest concepts only imperfectly match, I want the boundaries drawn: which of them this most resembles, where the fit fails, and where the phenomenon itself stops applying. I care less about the label than about knowing precisely what this thing is and is not.
