ok so i was reading about evolutionary debunking arguments for the first time and im kind of freaked out, because I tried to extend one and now it seems to show we dont know anything, which cant be right. heres the argument i ended up with:

(1) pretty much everyone believes that hurting a kid just for fun is wrong.
(2) there's a good evolutionary story for why we believe that: groups where people had empathy, guilt, and anger at cheaters did better, so selection gave us those gut moral reactions. and notice this explanation only talks about what helped genes spread; it never says any of those acts actually are wrong.
(3) if the best explanation for why someone holds a belief doesn't involve the belief being true, then that belief is unjustified.
(4) so our belief that hurting a kid for fun is wrong is unjustified.

but then the exact same move seems to go through everywhere:
(5) evolution also explains why we believe 2+2=4 (a number sense helped track food, predators, etc.), why we trust induction (assuming past patterns keep holding is super useful for survival), and why our ordinary perception is roughly accurate (organisms that got their environment wrong died). in all these cases the explanation is about fitness, not about mathematical facts, the real structure of the world, or whatever being true.
(6) so by (3), math, induction, and ordinary perception are unjustified too.
(7) but that's nuts. nobody can actually give up 2+2=4, or that theres a tree in front of them, just because of a story about natural selection.
(8) so one of the steps has to be wrong.

i'd rather keep (7) than reject it, but i also see why someone might think the moral case and the math case are different without being able to say exactly how. which premise or step do i reject, and what does rejecting it cost? like if i give up (3) i feel like i lose the whole debunking argument, but if i keep (3) i might have to give up way more than i want. what exactly is the difference between moral beliefs and math/perception, if there even is one, and why does it matter for the debunking?
