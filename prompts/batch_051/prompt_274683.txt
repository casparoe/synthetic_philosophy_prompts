Suppose a small jurisdiction adopts the following rule for inheritance, debts, and criminal liability: Y is counted as the same person as X just in case Y can give a sworn account of at least one episode from X’s life as remembered from the inside, and then same person is extended transitively across chains of such memory-links. So if the old general remembers leading the charge, and the officer remembers being flogged as a schoolboy, the general is also the schoolboy even if he has no memory of the flogging. Treat the rule as a mechanism: what incentives does it create for heirs, creditors, prosecutors, and the people whose identity is being litigated? How would people game it or try to shape the record of their own memories? What happens when a claimant has memory-links to two incompatible past persons, or a past person has several would-be present continuants? At what point does the rule stop working like Locke’s memory criterion and start becoming something else?
