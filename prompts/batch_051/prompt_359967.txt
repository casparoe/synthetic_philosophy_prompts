I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am drafting a long essay in which I set out my own ethics of eating rather than merely surveying positions. Before I publish, I want a consistency check on the set of commitments the essay ends up endorsing, because I suspect my current draft stitches together claims whose implications I have not fully traced. Here are the commitments as I would state them now:

(a) What matters morally is suffering: it matters wherever it occurs, in whatever body, and species membership, charisma, and how near the suffering is to me are irrelevant to how much it counts.

(b) Comparing foods should be a matter of expected suffering: the number of animals affected, weighted by how intensely and for how long each is affected—not just a count of animals killed.

(c) This is part of why I am less uneasy about beef than about chicken. A single beef animal yields food for a person for many months, while a chicken yields about a day of food, so eating chicken kills vastly more individual animals per calorie than eating beef, and broiler lives are particularly grim.

(d) I also count the animals killed in crop cultivation—mice, voles, and insects killed by harvesting and pest control—and I reject the idea that a plant-based diet is automatically clean.

(e) The same reasoning extends to wild animals. The suffering of wild animals from predation, disease, and starvation must be counted, and the fact that it is natural does not remove it from consideration.

(f) At the same time, I support rewilding and habitat protection, and I mark down foods with high greenhouse gas and land footprints. I treat emissions and land use as further entries in the moral tally for food, alongside direct animal deaths.

Which of these commitments actually pull against one another, and which pairs only appear to conflict? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give: which commitment is the least costly to revise, and what is lost by revising it. I am asking for an audit of my position, not an overview of animal ethics, and I would rather learn that my commitments force me to a conclusion I have not yet accepted than be told they all fit together.
