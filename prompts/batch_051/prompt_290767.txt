I'm in the middle of a disagreement about quantified modal logic and the metaphysics of existence, and I would rather not get another answer that says it depends on your semantics. I want a number with the reasoning opened up.

The claim to which I would like your credence is necessitism in the standard modal sense: the unrestricted quantifier ranges over the same domain in every possible world, so that everything there is exists necessarily, and there could not have existed anything beyond what there actually is. More technically, letting □ be metaphysical necessity and ∀ the unrestricted quantifier, necessitism is the claim that both the Barcan formula and the converse Barcan formula are correct principles of the logic of metaphysical modality: (BF) ∀x□φ → □∀xφ, and (CBF) □∀xφ → ∀x□φ. In variable-domain Kripke semantics, BF is valid exactly when an accessible world never contains an object missing from the world you started in, while CBF is valid exactly when no object drops out as you move to accessible worlds; accepting both forces constant domains. So the formal question and the metaphysical question are the same question by another name.
