I am compiling a list of objections to deflationary theories of truth for a graduate seminar handout, and I want to make sure I have not missed a structurally distinct kind of objection. The list so far is as follows.

1. The generalization problem: a finite thinker can accept general claims like every theorem of Peano arithmetic is true, or everything a certain source said yesterday is true, while the available deflationary resources — an infinite conjunction of T-sentences — are not available to a finite believer, and the standard repairs seem to smuggle in more structure than deflationism is entitled to.
2. The normativity objection: truth functions as a norm of assertion and of inquiry — one ought not to assert what is untrue, and an inquiry that misses the truth is to that extent defective — but a purely logical or disquotational predicate cannot explain this prescriptive profile.
3. The explanatory-role objection: statements such as our theories are successful because they are approximately true are plausibly genuine explanations, and a deflationary reading either empties them of content or leaves the success of science a standing coincidence.
4. The truth-conditional semantics objection: influential programs in semantics explain meaning in terms of substantive truth conditions, and deflationary truth cannot underwrite meaning because its T-sentences presuppose that one already understands the sentence used on the right-hand side.
5. The descriptive objection: the ordinary concept of truth seems to present truth as agreement with reality, a relating of thought to world, so the deflationist owes an error theory about why the concept seems relational if in fact it is not.
6. The paradox objection: the naive equivalence schema, applied to liar-like or Curry-like sentences, yields contradiction, and the standard blocking moves appear to require a hierarchy, a groundedness story, or a non-classical logic that deflationism is not obviously entitled to.

I already know the familiar deflationist replies to each of these, and I am not asking for them. What I want is a further objection that is not one of the above and not a rewording, special case, or combination of any of them. It should not appeal to truth's normative role, its explanatory role, or the common-sense feeling that truth is relational, and it should be an objection to deflationary theories in general, not merely to disquotational theories tied to a single language. Then say candidly whether the new objection is any good, how it differs structurally from the six above, and what a committed deflationist would have to give up in order to meet it.
