I'm a novelist working on a book of speculative political fiction, and I'd like you to take the role of a political theorist specializing in statehood, recognition, and international institutions. Think carefully rather than reaching for a one-line answer. The scenario I need to reason through is this: in the 2040s, after a prolonged regional war, a peace settlement establishes a clearly bounded coastal territory of a few million people with an effective monopoly on force, working courts, a tax system, and a government that most residents obey — but the treaty permanently bars every other state from recognizing it diplomatically, and all parties comply. There are no embassies, no treaties, no membership in international organizations, no entry into the ordinary system of recognized states. I am not asking for plot ideas. Give me your best reasoned prediction of what happens to this entity over the next fifty years, tracing the causal chain through internal legitimacy, trade and finance, border control and travel, and the character of its domestic politics. For each major prediction, say whether it is robust or a guess, identify the mechanism behind it, and state what would have to hold for the prediction to fail. Before you predict, lay out what work you take recognition normally to do for a state, so I can see exactly which functions this scenario removes. And finally, address whether the exercise suggests recognition is partly constitutive of statehood or mainly a convenience that can be worked around, marking where your own reasoning rests on contestable assumptions.
