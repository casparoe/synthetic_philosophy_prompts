Give me a working definition of "a wasted vote" — not the dictionary definition, the one you'd actually defend — and run it through the cases below. I have strong intuitions about two of them and no idea about the rest.

- A vote for a candidate polling around 1% in a safe seat, cast mainly to signal support for the platform.
- A vote for the lesser of two evils in a swing state, where you think both major candidates are bad but one is a lot worse.
- Spoiling your ballot in a race where only one candidate is on the ballot.
- Staying home even though you strongly prefer one candidate, because your state isn't competitive.
- A vote for an extreme challenger in a primary who can't win, but whose strong showing might move the winner's platform.
- A vote in a proportional representation system for a party that ends just below the threshold, so it gets no seats.
- A vote for the overwhelming favorite you support, in a race everyone knows won't be close.

I'm pretty sure the first one is wasted and the last one isn't. I want you to propose the definition, run it through all seven, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise it or bite the bullet on the surprising verdicts. Also flag which feature of the cases is doing the work: is a wasted vote about what your vote causally changes, what it expresses, or what you could have done instead?
