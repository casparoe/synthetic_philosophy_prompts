Just had a long argument with a friend about whether thinking about your own thinking actually makes you decide better or just gives you fancier rationalisations. He kept saying it's pure performance. So now I want to stress-test a concrete version of the idea. Say a midsize company adopts a rule that any decision above about $50k or affecting more then ten people has to include a short meta-memo from the decision-maker, written before the meeting, naming what kind of reasoning they're using, what evidence would change their mind, and which pressure (deadline, bonus, looking dumb) is pushing them hardest. The memo gets read aloud at the start, but nobody can vote on it or ask follow-ups. What does this actually do to decisions? Who gets good at writing the memos while staying bad at deciding? Where does it get gamed first, and can it be patched without turning the whole thing into box-ticking?
