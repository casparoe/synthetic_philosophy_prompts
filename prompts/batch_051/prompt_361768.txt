I am a retired theoretical physicist who has come to philosophy only recently, and I would like a short research proposal written for a foundation that funds conceptual work in the philosophy of physics. The topic is the philosophical significance of Gödel's rotating-universe solution to the Einstein field equations. That solution is a cosmological model in which matter rotates and the spacetime admits closed timelike curves; Gödel took it to show that time, understood as the objective passage of events, cannot be fully real. The question the proposal should define and address is whether the existence of such a solution actually warrants that conclusion, or whether it establishes only the weaker claim that general relativity permits causal structures more exotic than our own universe appears to have. Please have the proposal state the question precisely, explain why Gödel's own remarks and the standard appeal to global hyperbolicity (the demand that spacetime permit a consistent global time) do not already settle it, sketch a philosophical approach that separates features of the mathematical solution from conclusions about time itself, and specify what a successful result would look like and what would show the question to be badly posed. It should be sized for one investigator working for six months and should not call for experimental or observational work.
