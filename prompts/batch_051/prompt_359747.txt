Exam question:
Explain the distinction between the inside view and the outside view in forecasting. Use an example to illustrate both approaches, explain why the outside view often produces more accurate forecasts, and state at least one condition under which it would be a mistake to rely on the outside view.

Student answer:
The inside view focuses on the particular features of the case at hand: the steps in the plan, the people involved, the known obstacles, and how long each component should take if things go reasonably well. The outside view sets those details aside and asks what actually happened in a distribution of similar cases, then forecasts the present case from that distribution rather than by adding up task estimates.

For example, suppose a team is estimating the release date for a software product. The inside view would decompose the remaining work into features and multiply developers by weeks, arriving at around eight weeks. The outside view would instead collect past software projects of comparable size and complexity and observe that similar projects have historically shipped in two to three times the time of their original internal estimates. The team should then predict at least sixteen weeks rather than eight.

The outside view tends to be more accurate because it corrects for the planning fallacy and for the optimistic bias that infects within-case reasoning. When people reason from plan details, they give too much weight to how they intend things to go and too little weight to base rates of delay, failure, and cost overrun in comparable efforts. The outside view replaces the agent's self-serving simulation of the case with an empirically observed distribution. Because that distribution is objective, the outside-view forecast is the correct one once an appropriate reference class has been selected. At that point the analyst should resist bringing in further case-specific details, since reintroducing details is exactly how optimism creeps back into the estimate.

The only circumstance in which the outside view would be a mistake is when no suitable reference class exists. If a project is genuinely novel, there are no comparable cases to average over, so an outside-view forecast cannot be formed and one must fall back on the inside view. In every other case the outside view is the most reliable predictor available.
