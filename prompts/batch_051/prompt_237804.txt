Everyone agrees corruption is bad, but the moment I try to define it the boundaries smear. I need a working definition of political corruption that classifies the cases below correctly—or, where it can't, that tells me clearly that I should change my mind about the case.

Background: this definition has to travel across quite different political cultures, so it can't just be "whatever a rule-bound Western bureaucrat finds distasteful," but it also can't slide into localism where every envelope of cash gets called a sacred gift.

Cases:
1. A minister awards a construction contract to her brother's firm. The work is competent and the price is the going rate.
2. A mayor paves the streets of a neighborhood that voted heavily for him, using the public road budget, two months before an election.
3. A candidate hands out small banknotes to people at a campaign rally.
4. A businesswoman pays the school fees of a customs officer's daughter. Shortly afterward her container clears faster than everyone else's.
5. A legislator votes for a bill she genuinely believes is good, and the bill's industry backers had funded her last campaign—funding they would not have given a reliable opponent.
6. At a wedding, a politician gives the groom's family an envelope of cash, and everyone in the room understands it as a debt to be repaid in influence.
7. A constituent gives her alderman a jar of homemade jam while mentioning that her streetlamp has been broken for a month.
8. A foreign diplomat gives a general an expensive watch; the general later shares intelligence he would not otherwise have shared.
9. A minister's cousin applies through the ordinary civil-service exam and tops the list on merit; the minister has recused herself from the panel.

Tell me which of these your definition gets right, which it gets wrong or leaves unsettled, and whether it can be fixed without tacking on a special clause for each case. If your definition forces a surprising verdict somewhere, defend it. Then tell me which of your verdicts you'd least want to have to defend in a seminar, and whether that wobble is in the definition or in the category itself.
