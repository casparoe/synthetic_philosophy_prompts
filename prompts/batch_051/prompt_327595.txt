For a colloquium I'm expected to present a coherent position on whether the world is becoming less violent, and I'd like a consistency check on the package of commitments I currently hold before I circulate it. They are:

(1) The long-run historical record shows a dramatic decline in rates of interpersonal violence, with homicide data carrying most of the evidential weight.
(2) I also think the concept of violence is partly normative: whether a harm counts as violence rather than as misfortune or system output depends on judgments about agency, intention, and responsibility.
(3) Nevertheless, I hold that there is an objective fact of the matter about whether the world is becoming more or less violent — a fact that is not simply reducible to a choice among equally arbitrary metrics.
(4) For the comparative question, I take per-capita trends, not absolute counts, to be the correct standard, because a trend must be assessed relative to the population exposed to it.
(5) I count certain structural harms as violence — for example, famine deaths produced by preventable institutional decisions, or occupational deaths allowed because regulators treat a workforce as expendable.
(6) At the same time, I'm drawn to a pluralist view of measurement: reasonable evaluators may weight war deaths, ordinary homicide, and structural harms differently, with no single weighting uniquely correct.

I'd like to know which of (1)–(6) are in genuine tension with which, and which are only apparently in tension. For the genuine tensions, please rank them by severity, say what each tension makes jointly untenable, and identify the least costly revision in each case. I'm looking for a targeted audit of this specific package rather than a general overview.
