I'm putting together a handout for my epistemology seminar and I'd like a consistency check on a package I've been defending, because the students keep poking at different pieces of it and I want to know wether they're onto something or just not seeing how the parts fit together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Process reliabilism about justification: a belief is justified just in case it's produced by a cognitive process that is in fact reliable, full stop — no requirement that the subject know, or have independent reason to think, that the process is reliable.

(2) Closure under known entailment: if I know p and I know that p entails q, then I'm in a position to know q by deducing it from p.

(3) Bootstrapping is junk. If I form a bunch of color beliefs by using my color vision, keep track of how often those judgments turn out correct, and then — using that same color vision as my only check — conclude that my color vision is reliable, that conclusion gets no justification from the procedure. The circularity is vicious, not harmless.

(4) Right now, looking at the red cup on my desk gives me knowledge that the cup is red. I don't need independent evidence that the lighting is normal or that my color vision is working well for this to count as knowledge.

(5) Since I know the cup is red, and I know that its being red entails that it's not a white cup under red lighting, I can deduce and thereby know that it's not a white cup under red lighting. I think that's completely fine, even though I've done nothing to check the lighting independently.

(6) But I can't know just by looking that my color vision is reliable on this occasion. For that I'd need some independent check — another sense, a second person, something like that.

Does this set hang together? Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only look like they're in tension, and how severe each real tension is. If the cheapest fix involves revising one commitment rather than another, say which revision you'd make and what it costs. And if you had to keep five of the six and give one up, which one would you drop?
