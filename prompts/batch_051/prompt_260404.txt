Dropping a prompt from a long-running argument with my roommate. He half-jokingly insists everyone else is basically an NPC: he can't shake the feeling that only his own experience is "on," and that strangers, coworkers, even his girlfriend are just very elaborate background entities. I told him solipsism is unfalsifiable but basically unliveable, and he shot back: okay, then tell me what makes the woman across from me on the bus a "someone" in the same way I am, when I'd never say a literal video-game NPC or a chatbot is. I realized I don't actually have a clean answer.

Give me a working definition of "someone" (as opposed to a "something") that sorts the following list in a way I can defend. Clear cases, at least for me: a random stranger on the bus is a someone; my best friend is a someone; a rock is not; a literal video-game NPC is not. Then the hard ones: a dog; a current LLM running a long, coherent exchange; a human in dreamless sleep; a late-stage Alzheimer's patient; a character in a novel I care about; a person I only meet inside a dream; a person in a persistant vegetative state. And I'd love it if the definition also explained why the "everyone else is an NPC" move keeps feeling compelling even though almost nobody actually believes it. Tell me, at the end, which verdict on the list you're least secure about.
