I teach the Sleeping Beauty problem every couple of years in my formal epistemology course, and I've started to dread it — not because the students are confused, but because the literature is. The setup is airtight: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if it lands heads she's woken Monday only, while if it lands tails she's woken Monday and Tuesday with her memory wiped in between, so she can't tell which day it is. Each time she's woken, she's asked for her credence that the coin came up heads. Halfers say 1/2, on the grounds that she learns nothing new when she wakes: she knew in advance she'd be woken at least once either way, so there's no new evidence, so her Sunday credence survives unchanged. Thirders say 1/3, on the grounds that of the three possible wakings she might currently be in, only one is a heads-waking, and across repeated trials a third of all wakings are heads-wakings. Both sides have more careful versions of these arguments, and each side thinks the other is making a move that's transparantly illegitimate — halfers think thirders are treating awakenings as if they'd been randomly sampled from some reference class; thirders think halfers are ignoring the centered information that she's awake now.

What gets me is that this has been going since the early 2000s and nobody moves. All the non-centered facts are shared, the protocol is fully specified, and there's no experiment to run, so the impasse isnt empirical in any ordinary sense. My working theory is that the real disagreement is about what a credence is even supposed to track here — the probability of the coin outcome across complete runs, or how Beauty should distribute uncertainty over self-locating possibilities like which awakening this is. Which is to say, maybe it's really a dispute about the right reference class, and maybe the word 'credence' is quietly doing different work for the two sides.

Diagnose this one for me. What kind of disagreement is it, really — substantive but about epistemic principles rather than facts, verbal, or two groups answering different questions under the same label? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress from here: a formal result, a new distinction, a variant of the protocol that isolates one principle — or should I conclude there's no reason to expect any, and why?
