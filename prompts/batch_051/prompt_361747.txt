For a seminar on philosophy of religion I have been reading Hilary Putnam's essays on religion, and I have ended up defending a cluster of views whose internal consistency I can no longer judge on my own. Before I present the position, I would like an audit of whether it hangs together. My commitments, roughly as I have been defending them, are these. (1) God is not one more thing in the world, however powerful; the question whether God exists is not a hypothesis to be confirmed or disconfirmed as scientific claims are, so demands for evidence for or against God misfire from the outset. (2) The primary work of religious language is not to describe a supernatural realm but to orient a life: it shapes practices of gratitude, lament, hope and care, and it is held in place by those practices rather than by theoretical belief. (3) Fundamentalists and New Atheists make the same underlying mistake, since both treat religious utterances as if they were proto-scientific descriptions of an invisible object. (4) But I am not a relativist, and I do not want to drift into a fideism that treats each tradition as a sealed language-game: I think some religious outlooks are genuinely better or worse than others, and that traditions are answerable to ethical criticism, including criticism offered by people outside the tradition being criticised. (5) I also think there is no neutral, tradition-independent standpoint from which religions can be assessed; criticism always starts from somewhere, though this does not make it merely internal to the tradition under discussion. (6) I continue to pray and to take part in religious ritual, even though I do not believe in a supernatural person who hears the prayers, and I do not regard this participation as make-believe or as a mere technique of self-management.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say in each case what would have to give in order to restore consistency and how costly that revision would be, and then say whether any single revision would resolve the most serious problems at the lowest cost.
