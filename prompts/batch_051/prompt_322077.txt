I'm working on a novel set in the south Pacific where a small island language is down to its last few hundred elderly speakers and the kids mostly get by in English and a trading creole. A linguist friend keeps calling the situation an extinction, and she talks about language ecologies and says linguistic diversity is like biodiversity. A conservationist character I'm sketching uses the same idea to argue that letting the language die is the same kind of loss as losing an endemic bird, and that we have the same reasons to preserve languages that we do species.

I want the novel to be smarter than my characters, so help me figure out how far this analogy actually goes before I put it in somebody's mouth.

Lay out the correspondences for me. If a language is a species, what plays the role of the genes, the breeding population, the habitat, the invasive competitor, the extinction event? Is grammar the genome and words the phenotype, or is that already wobbly? Then tell me where the mapping breaks. I'm especially suspicious of a few spots: languages borrow and merge all the time, while species mostly don't; a language can in principle be revived from recordings and notebooks in a way a dead species can't; speakers' own wishes about thier language don't map onto anything biological; and there's a whole ecosystem level in conservation, where people protect wetlands or reefs, and I'm not sure language has an equivalent.

Then the part that actually matters for the book: once the disanalogies are laid out, does the preservationist conclusion survive? Is the species analogy doing real work here, or is it just dressing up an independent reason to care about linguistic diversity? If it mostly collapses, tell me what a better analogy would be for what's lost when a small language stops being spoken — a craft, a song tradition, a library, something else I haven't thought of. I'd rather have my novel argue the strongest version of the preservationist position, not a version that falls apart when somebody notices that languages aren't animals.
