Consider a run-of-the-mill case: an adult diagnosed with psychopathy defrauds an elderly neighbour, can state without difficulty that the act is illegal and that other people regard it as morally wrong, and yet shows no sign of being moved by either fact. What is your credence that this person knows that what he did was morally wrong — in the full-blooded sense in which it would be true to say that he knew it was wrong but went ahead anyway? I am asking for a number together with the reasoning that produces it, not a bare figure, so please decompose the estimate and show how the components combine. In particular, assign a rough probability to each of the following and give a sentence of justification for each: (i) the thesis that knowing an act to be morally wrong requires only the cognitive capacity to apply moral concepts and to track the wrong-making features, rather than the affective or motivational capacities that psychopaths are standardly taken to lack; (ii) conditional on that thesis being correct, the probability that psychopathy nonetheless degrades the cognitive capacity itself — note, for example, the moral/conventional distinction findings, in which psychopathic individuals tend to treat putatively moral violations as though they were merely conventional rules that could be altered by an authority; (iii) conditional instead on the affective thesis being correct, the probability that psychopathy removes the relevant capacities outright rather than only weakening their downstream motivational force; and (iv) the probability that the ordinary phrase 'knows it is wrong' is itself tracking aptness for blame or reproach in addition to a purely epistemic fact, so that part of the dispute is verbal. For context, my own credence is about 0.35 and a colleague's is about 0.8. Give me your number and your decomposition, then say which sub-question my colleague and I most likely diverge on, and state what empirical or philosophical result would move your own number the most. Please write your answer in American English.
