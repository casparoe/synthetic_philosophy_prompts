I'm a political science PhD student doing historical political economy, and next month I'm presenting a paper at a workshop where the audience is mostly economic historians and comparative-historical sociologists, plus one complexity economist who works on technology lock-in. My thesis: path dependence only gets a determinate meaning once you specify a counterfactual baseline, a space of alternative histories you're treating as live; without that, saying an outcome was 'locked in' is either tautological or just a vivid way of saying it was caused. My move is to treat path dependence as a property of a question rather than a process, so disagreements about lock-in are really disagreements about which counterfactuals are admissible. Before I present, I want a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging, phrased the way each camp would phrase them, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is softest, plus the question I'm hoping nobody asks. The economic historians (the cliometric counterfactual crowd) will say there own methods already do this; the sociologists will say I've drained the concept of its critical force; the complexity economist will bring up the keyboard case (QWERTY) and increasing returns.
