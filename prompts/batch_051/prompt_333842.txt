In my seminar on Rorty a grad student said—paraphrasing the usual one-liner, not quoting—that truth is what your peers will let you get away with saying. I have watched people use that line to end arguments about whether scientific inquiry progressively represents the world, as if the line itself settles antirepresentationalism. I want you to pull that slogan apart. What are the distinct precise claims it could be making? Lay them out as a menu, and for each one say whether it is a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or just false. Then say which reading is actually being invoked when someone wields the line against representationalism, and which readings are compatible with Rorty's actual antirepresentationalism and which are caricatures. Oh, and write the whole answer in American English.
