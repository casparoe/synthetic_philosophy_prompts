I have been trying to map the debate between moral particularism and moral generalism, and I would like help filling in one corner of the map. By particularism I mean the view that there are no true, non-vacuous moral principles: whether a feature counts for or against an action depends irreducibly on the case, and competent moral judgment is a kind of trained perception rather than the application of a rule. By generalism I mean the view that sound moral thought requires at least some genuine principles linking non-moral features to moral verdicts.

I am collecting arguments that have been or could be offered for generalism against particularism, and my list so far is this: (1) the consistency argument, that without principles an agent cannot show why treating the same feature differently in two cases is not arbitrary; (2) the explanation argument, that particular features make actions right or wrong, and the natural explanation is that those features fall under general right-making or wrong-making relations; (3) the moral education and advice argument, that we teach children and advise one another in general terms such as that lying is wrong, and particularism cannot make sense of this; (4) the reply to holism, that the fact that a feature can change valence between cases is compatible with principles stating default or contributory reasons, so holism of reasons does not by itself refute generalism; (5) the reliability argument, that our confidence in judging novel cases is best explained by our carrying general principles across situations; and (6) the supervenience argument, that since moral facts supervene on descriptive facts, the moral facts are in principle entailed by descriptive facts, which suggests that some disjunctive or indefinitely complex set of principles is available. I take this list to cover the main moves in the literature, so please do not simply restate one of them.

I want an argument for generalism that is not on this list and is not a variant, special case, or combination of items already on it — something a generalist could actually deploy, even if it is still undeveloped. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good: does it succeed in putting pressure on particularism, and does it force a particularist to concede anything beyond what they already concede in answering the six arguments above?
