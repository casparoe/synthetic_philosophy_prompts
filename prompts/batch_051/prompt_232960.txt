Okay so I've been going back and forth on inference to the best explanation (IBE) for ages—partly from reading LessWrong-adjacent stuff about whether 'explanation' is doing real epistemic work or just smuggling in priors through the back door. I keep flip-flopping, and I'd like to watch two smart people actually argue it out instead of talking past each other.

You're a professional philosopher working in epistemology and philosophy of science. Before you write anything, take a step back and brainstorm the strongest version of each side, including the objections you'd be most annoyed to see ignored. Think carefully about where the real crux is, not just the usual talking points.

Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated characters:

- An explanationist who thinks IBE is an indispensible and fundamental form of ampliative inference, and that explanatory virtues like simplicity, unification and consilience are genuinely truth-conducive, not merely pragmatic.
- A Bayesian sceptic who thinks IBE is at best a handy heuristic that collapses into prior and likelihood assignments once you spell it out, and at worst unsound. This character presses something like the bad lot objection and worries that the loveliest explanation need not be the likeliest.

The dialogue should get properly into the weeds. I want actual back-and-forth, where each person responds to the other's specific points rather than monologuing. Somewhere in there, have them tussle over at least these questions: whether simplicity can be cashed out in Bayesian terms or is just an aesthetic or cognitive bias; whether unifying apparently independent lines of evidence is evidence for a theory or merely a compression trick; and whether the explanationist can avoid claiming that the best available candidate is probably true. If one character lands a decent punch, let the other try to absorb it and recover rather than changing the subject.

Have the dialogue end with the two of them naming what they take to be their deepest crux, even if neither conceeds.
