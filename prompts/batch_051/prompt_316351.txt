A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about whether a fact that was already known when a theory was proposed can genuinely count as evidence for that theory. My friend belongs to a small scientific society whose evaluation bylaw, adopted in 1911, reads as follows: "Section 14(c). In assessing the empirical support of a hypothesis, an observation that was already known to the relevant scientific community at the time the hypothesis was first formulated shall not be counted as evidence in favor of that hypothesis; this rule is intended to prevent post hoc accommodation of known data." Our argument kept returning to a case its drafters would not have had in view: general relativity and the perihelion of Mercury. The anomalous precession had been measured and discussed since the mid-nineteenth century, and Einstein knew of it while he was completing the theory in 1915; the theory then yielded the observed precession without fine-tuning. The plain words of Section 14(c) seem to rule Mercury out as evidence, but the clause's purpose seems aimed at curve-fitting and post hoc adjustment, not at a long-standing anomaly that functions like a novel prediction. My friend and I could not agree on what the clause, read well, actually says about this case.

I would like you to argue the question the way a careful reader of a bylaw would: first from the wording, then from the purpose of the clause, then from what its drafters would have said had they been asked in 1915, and then from what a reasonable member of the society would take the clause to require. Then say which approach ought to govern, whether Section 14(c) on its best reading reaches the use of Mercury as evidence for general relativity, and what this reveals about the underlying problem of old evidence in Bayesian epistemology. I know this example sits inside a genuine philosophical dispute; please keep clear which parts of your answer are settled and which are contested among people who work on these questions, and do not present a contested position as if it were the consensus.
