My sister has been working as an ICU nurse, and she keeps bringing home stories about their ethics consults. Here's the thing I can't stop thinking about: when a family and the ICU team are at loggerheads over whether to withdraw life support, the hospital sends in a clinical ethicist. The ethicist reviews the chart, talks to everybody, and then writes a note that gives a recommendation. But the whole institution treats that recommendation as a contribution to the family's and team's deliberation, not as a determination. Nobody says the ethicist determined that the right course of action is extubation, so we'll extubate; instead they say the ethicist helped clarify our thinking. Yet in that same hallway, everyone defers to the neurologist about brain death and to the risk-management lawyer about what the hospital can legally do. Same people, same afternoon: expertise is authoritative for fact and law, but for ethics it's only advisory.

I'm trying to figure out what theory of moral judgment or moral testimony this asymmetry presupposes. Is the implicit view that there simply are no moral experts; or that there might be, but it's wrong or corrupting or self-alienating for a competent adult to take a moral conclusion on someone else's say-so; or that a moral judgment has to be arrived at from the inside to actually be yours and to move you? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology of moral deference that would make the practice coherent, say explicitly what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it merely fits comfortably with, and tell me what would have to change in how a hospital handles these consults if we started treating the ethicist's recommendation the way we treat a radiologist's read: something a layperson can permissibly just adopt.

The part that keeps tripping me up is that people seem most resistant to moral deference exactly when the moral stakes are highest, which is backwards from how one normally behaves with experts (you'd take the cardiologist's word precisely becuase the stakes are high). That inversion feels like it must be doing some real philosophical work.
