For a research paper in my high school biology class on the units-of-selection debate, I want the full specification before I read any more arguments. Write the desiderata: what must an adequate theory of the level or levels at which natural selection acts get right? Include the phenomena it has to handle — such as altruistic behavior, sterile worker castes in eusocial insects, meiotic drive, and transposable elements — the distinctions it must not collapse (for instance, between a replicator, a vehicle, and an interactor, and between selection acting at a level and adaptation existing for the benefit of that level), and the results from population genetics it must not contradict. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only commitments of one camp. Then flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and briefly say how a strict gene's-eye view and a multilevel-selection view fare against the list, including which requirement does the most to separate them.
