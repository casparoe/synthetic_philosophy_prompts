I'm a philosophy professor who teaches a course that spills over into social choice theory, and there's a tension I've never managed to resolve to my satisfaction. As I understand it, the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem says that once there are at least three alternatives and any strict ranking of them is allowed, no social choice function can be both non-dictatorial and strategy-proof: somebody can sometimes do better by misreporting their preferences. Yet the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism in auction theory is routinely described as strategy-proof and efficient, and it isn't dictatorial. I know VCG involves transfer payments and assumes quasi-linear utility, but I'd always taken the theorem to be a fully general impossibility result for preference aggregation, side payments or not. An auction with three possible allocations is still a social choice problem with at least three outcomes, so shouldn't the theorem bit there too? Is one of these results being stated with a hidden qualification, or does 'strategy-proof' mean something slightly different in the two settings? Please act as an expert in social choice theory and mechanism design, start by laying out the exact assumptions of each result, and then show me which step in my reasoning is the wrong one rather than just answering the surface question.
