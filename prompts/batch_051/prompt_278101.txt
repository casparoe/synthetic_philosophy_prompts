I'm an archaeologist with a mix of field and lab work behind me, and I keep noticing that my own inferences about past beliefs and institutions don't follow any rule I can state. Here's where my gut lands, and I want someone to tell me wether there's a coherent principle behind it or whether I need to drop some of these.

A shaft grave in a settlement: an adult buried with twenty intact pots, sheep bones, and a mirror placed over the face. I'm fine saying people provisioned the dead, and that this probably means they thought the dead continued in some way. I am not fine saying it points to a specific afterlife, or that mirrors were soul-guides.

A pit inside a building I'd already call a shrine: hundreds of deliberately smashed high-status pots sorted by part, rims with rims, bases with bases, and a complete dog skeleton laid on top. Ritual deposition? Fine, call it that. A sacrifice to a chthonic deity? No, stop.

The midden outside that same building has the same smashed pot type, some rim-down. I call that trash. But part of me thinks this is cicular: the only reason the first is "ritual" and the second is "trash" is that I already labeled one building a shrine.

A house corner with a small female figurine under a door socket. I'm comfortable saying "foundation deposit, apotropaic." I get jumpy if someone says "domestic goddess worship."

A block of identical small rooms with no hearths but with clay sealings and a few tokens. I'll happily say centralized storage, maybe administration. But if the sealings and tokens weren't there I'd only say "non-domestic, some communal function." So the tiny objects are doing a lot of work.

A child's grave with a miniature bow, arrows, and a spindle whorl. I'm fine saying the community gave the child a social role in death. I resist saying they believed children go to an afterlife. And I notice this sits badly with the adult grave, where I happily inferred something about the dead continuing.

The case I keep flipping on: a female figurine snapped cleanly in half, one half in the house and the other half buried at the doorway. I'm sure something deliberate is going on. I can't say whether it's a curse, a contract, a rite of separation, or just two pieces of the same broken object thrown out in different places.

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these? If so, state it precisely, tell me what it says about the split figurine, and which of my verdicts the principle forces me to drop. If there isn't a single principle, give me the smallest set that works and show me where the real fault lines are.
