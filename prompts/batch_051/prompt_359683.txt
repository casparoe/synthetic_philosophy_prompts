I am a novelist writing a legal thriller about a woman who discovers, after a medical device injures her, that the purchase contract contains a pre-dispute arbitration clause. Her claim will be decided by a private provider behind closed doors, and judicial review of the award will be narrow. In the background reading I am doing for the book, I keep encountering a strong version of the usual criticism of such arrangements: that what gets privatized is not merely the forum but justice itself. The industry response is also familiar: arbitration is still adjudication, it applies the same substantive law, and it is simply a faster and cheaper forum. I cannot tell whether this disagreement is empirical, conceptual, or partly both, and I would like to know whether any observation could actually settle the stronger charge.

Please treat the claim that mandatory arbitration amounts to the privatization of justice as a hypothesis about institutions rather than only a slogan. Design the strongest feasible test, or set of observations, that could distinguish the privatization of justice from the more modest privatization of the forum. Be concrete about what kinds of disputes, parties, or jurisdictions you would compare; what outcomes or structural features you would measure; and what pattern of results would speak for or against the strong claim. You may weigh candidates such as comparative outcomes for repeat-playing firms, the production of public precedent, deterrence effects, or the quality and publicity of reasoned decisions, but do not treat them as given; choose the most probative and justify the choice. Then mark the boundary of the test. What exactly would a confirmatory result establish, and what would it still leave open? Which part of the phrase privatization of justice can be operationalized, and which part is a normative or conceptual residue no test can reach? I want to know whether my protagonist's situation is best described as a loss of a public forum, a loss of public justice, or both, and whether the difference between those descriptions is observable or only rhetorical.
