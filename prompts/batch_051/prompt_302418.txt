I'm TAing a philosophy of mind course and the unit on the temporal structure of experience is always a mess. Students come in confidently saying they're "Stalinesque" or "Orwellian" about the color phi phenomenon, but when you press them on what exactly would settle the difference they just repeat the labels. I want to build a "which account of the timing of experience do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts by answers to cases, not by which name they like. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case: the color phi setup (two alternating colored dots seen as one moving dot that changes color mid-flight), the flash-lag effect, the cutaneous rabbit, chronostasis after a saccade, and a masking case where a target is never consciously seen at all. Each question needs two or three answer options that force a real decision about when in the processing stream the experience gets its content, whether there is a single "moment of consciousness," and whether the timing is genuinely edited or just misreported after the fact.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least: Orwellian (content is fixed before awareness, and memory later misdescribes it), Stalinesque (awareness itself is delayed, so the editing happens before the show starts), a multiple drafts/no-observer view, and a view that treats apparent timing as mostly a post hoc reconstruction. Include the unoccupied or weird answer combinations and say briefly for each whether it's incoherent, merely unappealing, or just underexplored. And no question should use the words Orwellian, Stalinesque, or multiple drafts: those only show up in the key. Also flag which pairs of views the quiz genuinely can't separate with these cases, however you slice the scoring.
