I am a second-year undergraduate taking a course on cultural evolution, and I am writing an assessed essay about what digital platforms implicitly assume regarding how culture moves and changes. I want to press on a concrete practice. Streaming services and social-media platforms run automated content-identification systems that treat a song, a video clip, or an online meme as the same item across an open-ended series of uploads: re-encoded versions, remixes, edits, slowed-down and sped-up variants. At the same time, those platforms openly celebrate remix culture and transformative editing, which suggests that cultural items are continuously reworked rather than copied as stable units. My question is: what philosophical commitments about the replication and identity of cultural variants does this content-identification practice presuppose? Does it strictly presuppose that cultural variants are replicators with stable identities, in something like the way genes are copied, or is it equally compatible with the view that cultural transmission is mostly transformative reconstruction? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say where it is philosophically underdetermined, and explain what a platform or copyright regime built on a reconstructionist view of cultural transmission would have to change. My tutor would like the answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. Please write in British English.
