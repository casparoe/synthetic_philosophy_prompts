I have to lead a two-hour session on costly signaling and the handicap principle for a reading group of philosophy postgraduates and evolutionary-biology postgraduates, each knowing the other's field only at a survey level. Don't explain the idea to me; produce the teaching plan. The plan must build toward a precise statement of the handicap principle: that a signal can honestly advertise quality when it is differentially costly — costlier for a low-quality signaller to produce at a given level, or unaffordable for the signaller a receiver would want to screen out — and that the informative part is this differential cost, not the cost's being large in some absolute sense. It must also separate handicap signals from physically unfakeable indices, whose honesty comes from production constraints rather than strategic cost.

Return the plan as a JSON object with these fields:
