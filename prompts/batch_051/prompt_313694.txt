I've been re-reading W.E.B. Du Bois's "Criteria of Negro Art" and keep coming back to the bit where he says: "The thing we are talking about tonight is part of the great fight we are carrying on and it represents a forward and an upward look -- a pushing onward." I'm on the grants committee for a small Black artist collective, and someone has proposed we take this literally as a decision rule. Every application has to include a short "struggle statement" saying concretely what material effect the work will have on our people, and the committee scores applications 50/50 on aesthetic merit and on how credible that statement is; if the struggle score is below a low bar, the application is rejected no matter how good the art is. The struggle statement also gets shown next to the work at shows, so audiences read it before engaging the piece.

Walk through what this does to who applies, what gets made, how the committee's taste drifts, and how artists game it. Where does it break first, and are the failure modes fixable without abandoning the rule? Is this actually more faithful to Du Bois than the usual compromise where we say "all art is propaganda" but then don't evaluate the propaganda directly?
