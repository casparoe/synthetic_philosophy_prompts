Take the standard bootstrapping case — Roxanne and the gas gauge, or your own version of it: she reads a gauge, forms beliefs about the tank on the basis of the readings, then over many occasions notices the gauge said F when the tank was full, and concludes the gauge is reliable. The two camps: one says this can't yield knowledge of the gauge's reliability, because the only support for each 'the tank was full' premise is the gauge itself, so either unrestricted closure or simple reliabilism has to go. The other says the argument isn't premise-circular, only rule-circular, and that rule-circular track-record reasoning is fine, so there's no easy-knowledge problem at all. Draft the joint statement these two camps would sign in an adversarial collaboration. Include: (i) a numbered list of claims both can sign, about the case and about what the argument is and isn't; (ii) a disagreements table with the residual claims worded so each side accepts it's own wording; (iii) the cruxes — sub-questions where a settled answer would move at least one camp — with each side's prediction recorded in advance, and any test or case both agree would bear on those cruxes. Make the wording tight, not vacuous, and sort out which bits of the dispute are substantive and which are just different uses of 'circular'. Commit to exact formulations instead of offering options. Start with the statement itself, no preamble, and write in British English.
