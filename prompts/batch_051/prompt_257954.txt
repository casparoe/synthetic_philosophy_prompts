I'm a PhD student in comparative memory studies (mainly Germany and the American South, with a bit of Turkey and Japan) and somehow ended up the graduate rep on my university's naming commission. We're in the American South. The building under review is a 1920s biology building named for a former governor of the state who was also the main architect of the state's eugenic sterilization law — a law that, depending on which historian on the commission you ask, was cited approvingly by eugenicists abroad. A student coalition wants the name removed before the semester ends. Some alumni and the history department want to keep the name but add a plaque about the sterilization program and a small permanent exhibit in the lobby. The wrinkle is that in 1963 the steps of this same building were the site of the first desegregation sit-in on campus, and a lot of alumni and even the local civil-rights tour now associate the building's name with the sit-in rather than with the governor. So the keep-with-context camp keeps arguing that removing the name would erase the one meaning the community actually uses.

The chair asked me to bring in the comparative angle because of my field: what Germany, Japan, and Turkey did with tainted public names and monuments, and the South's own recent removals — but only where it actually does work for our decision, not as a four-country tour. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It needs a concrete recommendation (rename, keep with context, or a third option) with a short timeline; an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side rather than writing it off as nostalgia; and a paragraph on what our choice would be doing that a plaque alone would not. Keep it under about 900 words and dont let it turn into an encyclopedia entry.
