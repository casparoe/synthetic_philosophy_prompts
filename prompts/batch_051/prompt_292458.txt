I'm retired from physical chemistry and I've recently got into the causal inference literature, which I never really learned as a working scientist. In the lab, randomization and experimental design mostly handled these questions for me, so the first rung of Pearl's ladder—association, conditional probability, just seeing—feels like home. My stats backround was always "condition on everything and hope." But the second rung, the do-calculus, keeps tripping me up.

When I look at an expression like P(Y | do(X = x)), my instinct is to say: draw the DAG with an extra node F standing for "intervention on X," make F a parent of X, and condition on F = "set X to x." Then you're back to ordinary conditioning, and the do-calculus is just conditional probability with extra bookkeeping. So why does Pearl treat "doing" as a seperate rung of the ladder? Is there something that conditioning on an intervention node gets wrong, or is the distinction mostly philosophical rather than mathematical? I'd like to know exactly where my reformulation breaks down, if it does.
