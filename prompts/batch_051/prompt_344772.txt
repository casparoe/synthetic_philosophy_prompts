Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in climate modeling. Camp A, the equal-weighters: when you have a multi-model ensemble — say the handful-to-dozens of global climate models run for a big assessment — take the simple mean and the across-model spread at face value, one model one vote. Camp B, the weighters: models differ a lot in skill and in independence, and some share enough code, parameterizations, or developers that they're near-clones, so you should downweight worse and redundant models rather than let one cluster dominate the average.

Write the actual document these two groups would co-sign. Start with a numbered list of claims both camps can sign: about what an ensemble is, what the spread does and doesn't quantify, why models disagree, where shared errors come from, and what decision-makers are and aren't entitled to conclude from the inter-model range. Then a disagreements table, where each row gives the issue, the equal-weight position phrased so that camp would actually sign it, and the weighting position phrased so that camp would actually sign it. Mark each row substantive or mostly verbal; for the verbal ones, say what the two sides would need to rephrase to stop talking past each other. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, spell out a test or analysis both camps agree would bear on it — hindcasts over held-out periods, a perfect-model experiment where one model plays the real world, out-of-sample projection skill, or something else — and record each side's prediction in advance, precisely enough that a failure is recognizable.

Threads worth working in, but only where both camps would accept the wording: the distinction between reliability (matching the observed record) and relevance (representing a future state that record doesn't sample), and why weighting optimizes reliability under a stationarity assumption nobody fully accepts; the circularity worry that models are tuned to parts of the observational record, so hindcast skill is partly rigged in their favor; the case where one high-skill model disagrees with a cluster of mediocre but mutually independent models; and whether the "model democracy" analogy is right at all, since models aren't voters with interests but tools with correlated error. Dont force all of these in if the compromise document ends up elsewhere — the point is non-vacuous agreed claims and a sharp residual disagreement.
