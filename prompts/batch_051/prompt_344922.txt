Compiling my notes on path dependence and I've hit the point where people on here mean at least four different things by it, and the usual move is to swap in whichever definition wins the current argument — 'locked in' and 'path dependent' get used interchangeably, which I'm pretty sure swallows a real distinction. Before I read another thread that takes QWERTY or VHS vs Betamax as settled folk history, I want the spec. Write the desiderata an adequate theory of path dependence has to meet, for the purpose of sorting claims like 'this early decision functionally committed us to that outcome' from claims that are just 'history matters, plus a plausible-looking story.' Include the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it has to get right (including the ones where the standard example probably isn't what people say it is), the distinctions it cant collapse, and any empirical regularities it has to be consistent with, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's taste (increasing-returns people vs. the critics who think everything is just high switching costs), and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Keep the scoring of existing views to a short paragraph at the end — I care about the criteria, not a review essay. Also, if 'the same outcome would have arrived anyway' is underdetermined until you specify a counterfactual framework, put that in the list rather than leaving it to do hidden work in the background.
