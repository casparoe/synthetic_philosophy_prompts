I'm on our university's conduct board, and this week we have to decide a case that's splitting us down the middle. A student learned from a friend that a married professor was having an affair, and emailed the professor: pay me $2,000 or I tell your spouse. The professor reported it, and the student's defense is that this shouldn't count as blackmail under our code, since the code doesn't prohibit either threatening to disclose a true private fact or asking someone for money on its own, and a pair of permitted acts can't combine into a prohibited one. The chair has asked me to draft a short memo for the board with a clear recommendation on how we should rule, plus the reasoning, and she wants the strongest version of the student's argument given its due, because several members find it weirdly compelling. I cant quite say where the defense goes wrong, if it does. Write the memo.
