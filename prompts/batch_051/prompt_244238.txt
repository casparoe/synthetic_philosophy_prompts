Over the last few months I've been collecting criticisms of effective altruism that actually feel like they bite, mostly from old EA Forum threads and arguments with friends at my local meetup, because the conversation keeps going in circles and I want to know whether there's anything left that isn't just a remix of the same few moves. The list I've converged on so far:

- Measurability bias: EA overweights interventions with clean, legible metrics (bednets, deworming, cash transfers) and underweights messy things like institution-building or culture change, not because they're worse but because you can't put a number on them, so the portfolio ends up biased toward the measurable rather than the important.
- The institutional critique: EA mostly treats the rules of the game as fixed and tries to optimize within them — donate, pick careers, start charities — rather than trying to change the rules themselves, which makes it quietly conservative and maybe even launders the legitimacy of the institutions doing the actual harm.
- The earning-to-give critique: telling talented people to take high-paying jobs and donate the surplus is at best a self-serving rationalization and at worst actively harmful, because it discounts direct work, leans on shaky counterfactuals about who would have taken the job otherwise, and selects for people who are comfortable with high-salary jobs in the first place.
- Cluelessness: for longtermist causes the downstream effects branch so fast and so far that any expected-value number is dominated by arbitrary modeling choices, so the apparent rigor is fake and the honest answer is that we have no idea.
- The neglectedness reflexivity point: EA's advice to go where things are neglected assumes the rest of the world stays put while you move; if enough people follow the advice, the neglected areas stop being neglected and the counterfactual reasoning quietly breaks.

I have a nagging suspicion that this list is still missing something, even though it looks like it covers all the standard ones. So here's what I want: one more critique of effective altruism that is genuinely different from everything above — not a rewording, not a special case, not a conjunction of two of them. If you cant find one that's actually good, say so instead of manufacturing one. And if you do find one, tell me honestly wether it survives contact with what EA orgs actually do, or whether it's the kind of thing that sounds devastating in a blog comment but falls apart when you look at the details.
