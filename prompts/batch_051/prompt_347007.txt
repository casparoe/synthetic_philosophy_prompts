Goodman's new riddle of induction is standardly posed with a time-indexed predicate: 'grue' applies to an object just in case it is green when first observed before some future time t, and blue when first observed at or after t. Since every emerald observed so far has been green and has been observed before t, the evidence appears to confirm 'All emeralds are green' and 'All emeralds are grue' equally well, even though the two hypotheses assign different colors to an emerald first observed after t; the standard Goodmanian response is that green is better entrenched than grue. Now construct a spatial analogue. Let 'geogrue' apply to an object just in case it is green when first observed in the Northern Hemisphere, and blue when first observed in the Southern Hemisphere. Suppose every emerald observed so far has come from Northern Hemisphere mines and has been green. Does the riddle survive the substitution of a spatial index for a temporal one? Do the two general hypotheses remain equally well confirmed, does the entrenchment response separate them exactly as it separates green from grue, and if anything is lost in the spatial version, what precisely is doing the work in the original temporal case?
