Im trying to settle a running argument. One friend says queueing is just a coordination device, like driving on the left: break it and you're a nuisance, but not the kind of wrong a visitor with different habits has to take seriously. Another says a place in a queue is a little property-like holding, so jumping the line is closer to a small taking than to bad manners. I want less introspection and more evidence. How would you test which picture better captures the norm? Design an experiment or set of observations that would pull the two readings apart — what result pattern would count as mere coordination convention and what would count as moralized holding? Then lay out what even a clean positive result would still leave open about whether a visitor who knows the local rules but finds them pointless is actually obligated to queue. Along the way, seperate what's settled from what's genuinely disputed among people who study this sort of thing, and don't present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
