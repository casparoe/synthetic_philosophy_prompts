I am a PhD student working on analogy and conceptual representation, and I have been thinking inside a deliberately small formal world: lowercase letter strings, with analogy problems of the form "source -> target; probe -> ?". The task is to supply the completion that best preserves the transformation that took source to target. There are no official answers, and that is why the domain interests me: my judgments about these completions seem to be doing real conceptual work. I am particularly interested in conceptual slippage—the way an operative concept such as "successor", "last", or "letter" slides into a nearby concept (say, "predecessor", "first", or "block") when the context pushes on it. I want to know whether a single principle captures my verdicts, or which of them I should give up.

My judgments are these. (1) abc -> abd; ijk -> ? I accept ijl and only ijl: the rightmost letter is replaced by its alphabetic successor. (2) abc -> abd; kji -> ? My inclination is kjh, not kjj. Because the probe runs backward, "successor" seems to slip to "predecessor"; moving one further step along the probe's own direction feels more faithful than applying the alphabetic operation literally, yet nothing in the rule I would state for (1) licenses that slip. (3) abc -> abd; cab -> ? Here my inclination flips: I want to change the actual letter c, since c is the letter that changed in the source, so I answer dab. I feel the pull of cac—changing the rightmost letter, as in (1)—but it feels worse, and I cannot say why. (4) abc -> abd; iijjkk -> ? I answer iijjll: the relevant units are the repeated-letter blocks ii, jj, kk, and the last block is replaced by its successor block. The letter-level answer iijjkl strikes me as wooden, even though it follows the same rule that gave me ijl in (1).

Taken together these verdicts look unstable: (2) is positional and direction-sensitive, (3) abandons position and tracks letter identity, and (4) changes the unit of analysis from letters to blocks. Is there one precise principle—one account of conceptual slippage in this microdomain—that delivers all four verdicts? What does it say about the further case abc -> abd; mrrjjj -> ? If no single principle can deliver all four, which verdict should be overturned, and what is the simplest principle that captures the rest?
