I'm in a philosophy of religion seminar and we just did the Incarnation, and I've wound up with an argument that feels like it proves to much—like if it works, the whole Chalcedonian formula is just words. I don't want it to work, but I can't find the exit. Here's the argument as I wrote it out:

(1) Jesus is one person: the same subject who is the Son of God is the man from Nazareth, and there aren't two of him. (2) That one person has two complete natures, divine and human, and each nature carries the capacities proper to it. So the divine nature includes omniscience, and the human nature includes an ordinary human mind that learns gradually, reasons from what it has picked up, and can be ignorant of some things. (3) A self-conscious mind with its own first-person outlook is the kind of thing you only get in a person; if a being has two distinct minds like that, each with its own point of view and no shared awareness, you do not have one person with two minds, you have two persons sharing a body or something. (4) The Gospels describe Jesus as really exercising that human mind: as a kid he's in the temple asking and answering questions and increasing in wisdom, and later he says there are things he does not know. But an omniscient being cannot learn, and cannot be ignorant of something. (5) So we are stuck: either the two natures have two distinct minds, which by (3) is two persons; or the human nature's mind is omniscient, which contradicts (4); or there is one mind that is both omniscient and not omniscient, which is a flat contradiction. (6) Therefore the one-person-two-natures view is not coherent as stated: once you look at the minds, you either split the person or collapse the natures.

I'd most like to keep (1) and (2), and I find (4) hard to deny because that's just how Jesus is described. So my question: is there a bad step here, or is the argument valid and I just have to decide which premise is least costly to reject? Either way, which step goes, and what does it cost someone who wants to stay Chalcedonian to give it up?
