i keep ending up on Hart's side of the Hart–Devlin debate because the harm principle is the only view there with a stopping point: you may criminalize conduct because it harms someone, and once you allow 'immoral' or 'revolting' as free-standing grounds, the line dissolves and the majority is policing bedrooms again. But i can't shake the intuition that some conduct which harms no assignable person is still properly the criminal law's business. Take someone who leaves notarized instructions that after he dies his body may be desecrated in private, by willing participants, with no family to grieve and no one else ever knowing. I'd be lying if I said it was obviously wrong for the law to prohibit that, and it doesn't feel like mere disgust: the law isn't only a harm-prevention machine, it marks the boundaries of a form of common life. But that's exactly the legal-moralist move I rejected. Which should yield? i dont want a verdict; I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the harm principle to be amended or abandoned, and whether there's a reading on which both survive. Is a moralized or expressive notion of 'harm' a real fix, or is it just disgust wearing a theory?
