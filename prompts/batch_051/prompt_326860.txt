Two researchers have spent a year arguing about whether cultural variants really replicate, or whether the term meme is a loose metaphor. The first holds a replicator view: many cultural variants are copied with enough fidelity to form lineages, variations are inherited, and Darwinian selection acts on those variants, so talk of replicating memes earns its keep. The second holds an attractor view: most cultural transmission is reconstruction, in which a learner infers an underlying idea or task and regenerates a performance, and most apparent stability is convergence on shared cognitive attractors; discrete replicating variants exist only as a special case, not as the general mechanism of cultural change.

Draft the adversarial-collaboration document they could both sign. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive rather than vacuous: for example, they may agree that selection-like population dynamics can be described at the level of cultural variants under some conditions, that craft traditions show descent with modification, that some items are copied far more faithfully than others, and that a genuine replicator would have to meet specified conditions. For each residual disagreement, phrase that side's commitment in wording the side itself would accept. Then identify the cruxes, and for each crux describe a concrete transmission-chain experiment or natural case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The cruxes I suspect matter are: whether copying fidelity is high enough outside restricted domains to sustain open-ended cumulative lineages, whether transmission is better modeled as copying a variant or triggering an internally specified form, and whether Darwinian selection requires discrete particulate variants rather than graded reconstructions. Make clear which parts of the dispute are empirical, which are terminological, and which a sharper definition would dissolve; if you conclude a crux is purely verbal, say why and repair the wording.
