Here are notes for a short essay I keep meaning to write; I'd like comments on whether the central distinction is real, and on the two or three specific questions at the end. Working title: Kelly's theorem, Kelly's utility, and the missing bet. Setup: a bettor with a bankroll repeatedly wagers some fixed fraction f of current wealth on an independent gamble with known odds and a positive edge. The standard growth-optimality result is that the f* maximizing expected log wealth will, with probability approaching 1, outperform any other fixed fraction as the number of trials grows. People then move to practical advice: bet full Kelly, because it maximizes long-run growth. My first claim is that this inference only goes through if you've already decided that log terminal wealth is the thing to care about. The theorem itself is a dominance claim about long-run outcomes; it says nothing about utility. An expected-utility maximizer with a different utility function can rationally pick a different f. Second claim: fractional Kelly is usually described as Kelly with a safety margin, but if the theorem is what's doing the work then half-Kelly isn't a cautious version of the theorem's conclusion; it's just a different strategy, and in fact the same asymptotic result may say full Kelly beats half Kelly in the long run. So the safety-margin story has to be imported from elsewhere: parameter uncertainty, ambiguity, drawdown aversion, non-log utility, etc. Questions: (1) am I drawing a line the standard story already draws, or does the standard story really run theorem and utility together? I can't tell wether I'm being unfair. (2) Is there a clean growth-rate argument under parameter uncertainty that singles out some fractional Kelly as optimal, rather than just as a heuristic? I have a nagging memory that unknown drift in log-normal returns does something like this but I don't trust it. (3) Should the essay end by generalizing to geometric rationality more broadly, and is the move from multiplicative outcomes being compared by their geometric mean to the prescription maximize expected log wealth as obviously valid as its usually made to sound?
