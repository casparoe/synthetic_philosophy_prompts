Act as a professional philosopher of metaethics with a serious interest in narrative theory. I am a working novelist: I think about fiction every day in terms of point of view, free indirect style, showing versus telling, evaluative description, character motivation, reader inference, and the way a novel's events and its final moral or emotional judgment are only loosely coupled. I have only a layperson's grasp of the philosophical positions, so define technical terms as they come up, but do not condescend to me about craft; use my vocabulary precisely.

I would like you to restate the is–ought gap in its classical Humean form and the naturalistic fallacy in Moore's Open Question sense, together with any standard objections you find clarifying, in my vocabulary as a novelist. In particular, map the distinction between descriptive facts and normative or evaluative claims onto things like: what narration shows versus what it tells; what a plot event establishes versus what the reader is invited to feel or conclude from it; the difference between a character's motivation being intelligible and its being justified; the way evaluative adjectives do work that neutral description cannot; and the way an ending can fail to follow from everything the novel has established.

For each mapping, give me three things: what translates cleanly; what you had to bend or distort to make it fit; and what has no counterpart in my craft vocabulary at all. Then give me the residue report: where no counterpart exists, is it because my vocabulary is missing a distinction the philosophers need, or because the philosophical claim is less determinate than its technical vocabulary makes it look? In particular, I want to know whether a novelist's working intuition that no accumulation of fictional facts can force a moral judgment on the reader is simply the is–ought gap wearing ordinary clothes, or whether the translation quietly changes the subject.

Start by brainstorming several possible mappings between the philosophical distinctions and specific craft mechanisms, and discard the ones that paper over a mismatch. Keep the translation honest: if a craft term only half-carries a philosophical idea, say which half and leave the rest as residue. End with the three or four mappings you trust most and the one place where the translation does the most damage.
