My friend thinks about this stuff an unhealthy amount and she's got pretty settled views on what's real in online games. She says a rare sword in an MMO isn't a real object—it's just a row in a database. But she also says that if someone hacks your account and deletes your inventory, that's a real wrong, and the hours you put in make it a real loss. She reckons the people she raids with are real friends, even though they've never met face to face, and that being abused over voice chat is real harm. But she's adamant that losing a duel and dropping your gear isn't real harm, because that's just the game working the way its meant to. And she says spending actual money on a cosmetic skin is a real financial transaction, but the skin itself isn't a real object you own, not like a bike.

The new case we're stuck on: a player builds a house in a game, someone else pays real money for it, and then a server rollback deletes it. Would she say the house was real property, and would she say deleting it was a real loss or just something that happened in a game? And a second one, if it helps: two people hold a wedding inside a game and honestly treat themselves as married afterwards—would she call that a real marriage? I'm not that fussed about the exact answers; I'm trying to work out the rule she's running, what other rules would fit her past verdicts just as well, what each of them says about the house, and which of the two new cases would do most to settle which rule she's actually running.
