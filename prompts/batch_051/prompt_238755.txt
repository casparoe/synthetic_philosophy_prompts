For a small philosophy reading group, draft a joke that turns on Tolstoy’s theory of art in *What Is Art?*. The joke should presuppose the actual content of the theory — for instance, the view that a work is genuine art only insofar as the artist has felt an emotion and transmits it to the audience through infection, with sincerity as a central criterion — but it should be in the weeds enough that its humour depends on a real feature of the view rather than on a generic caricature of Tolstoy as a moralising anti-aesthete. For example, the punchline could turn on the tension between Tolstoy’s theory and his own evaluative judgements, on the self-undermining worry that the essay must itself infect readers with Tolstoy’s feeling if it is to count as art, or on the distinction between genuine art and counterfeit art. The joke should be intelligible and genuinely amusing to someone who has read the essay carefully. After the joke, add a short note taking a definite position on which parts of the joke are philosophically faithful to Tolstoy and which parts, if any, lean on a contested or simplified reading of his position. Please write in British English.
