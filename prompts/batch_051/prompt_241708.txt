My mother recently inherited a small painting that has been in the family for a long time. When she had it valued, the appraiser said it would probably fetch about £3,000 at auction, but she insists she would never pay £3,000 for a painting like it; she doesn't much care for it and says she would rather put the money towards a holiday or the grandchildren. Yet when a dealer offered her £4,500 shortly after the valuation, she turned him down without hesitation, saying that parting with it would feel like losing a piece of the family. This seems inconsistent to me: if the painting is not worth £3,000 to her as a buyer, how can it be worth at least £4,500 to her as a seller? Is she being irrational, or is there a defensible sense in which owning something changes what it is genuinely worth? I can't tell whether I should keep trying to reason her out of this or accept that she knows something about her own values that I'm missing.
