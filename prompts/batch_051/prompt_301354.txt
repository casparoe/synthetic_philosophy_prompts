I'm a student in a course on the meaning of work, and I've been working toward a position I want pressure-tested rather than merely defended. The position is this: the contribution that paid work makes to a flourishing life is real but almost wholly instrumental. Work matters because of what it brings about — income and security, temporal structure, social contact, a setting in which one can exercise and be recognized for competence, and a place in a narrative in which one's efforts count. The activity of working itself, the institution of the job, and the social role of being a worker are not valuable in their own right; they are simply convenient vehicles for those underlying goods. If that is right, then a future in which automation removes most jobs should be seen, from the standpoint of human flourishing, as an opportunity to be welcomed rather than a loss to be mourned — provided we reproduce the underlying goods through other means.

Which would a committed defender of my view concede as counting against it, and which would they dismiss as confusions or as problems of bad implementation rather than problems with the position itself?
