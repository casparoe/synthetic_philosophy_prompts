ok so I've been reading Nozick's stuff on animals in Anarchy State and Utopia and I have a little theory I want pressure-tested. He draws a sharp line where people get side-constraints (rights you can't violate even for the greater good) but animals just get utilitarian treatment (you can use or harm them if the benefits are big enough). I'm trying to replace that binary with what I call 'capacity-weighted side-constraints': every conscious being gets a side-constraint whose strength is a function of its degree of rational/moral agency, from zero for something like a clam up to full for a normal adult human. So for a dog, maybe the side-constraint is weaker but not zero: you can't feed it to the utility monster unless the benefit is really massive, whereas for a person the side-constraint is absolute. My idea is that this solves the marginal-cases problem for Nozick — severely cognitively disabled humans still have full side-constraints because of their species membership or potential, or maybe because their capacity is still above some threshold — and it also makes his animal view less weird, because he doesn't actually give a principled reason why sentient nonhumans are just utilitarian resources. Does this already exsist under another name? And does it actually work, or does it just smuggle in a threshold anyway and collapse back into Nozick's binary? I want honest but charitable, like I'm not sure if this is novel or just a confused version of something obvious.
