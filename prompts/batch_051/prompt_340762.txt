One strand of the free will debate concerns what the ordinary, pretheoretical concept of free will actually is. On one side is the claim that the folk concept is incompatibilist: that laypeople, when not in the grip of an explicit theory, judge that an agent in a deterministic universe does not act freely and is not morally responsible. On the other side is the claim that the folk concept is compatibilist, and that apparently incompatibilist lay responses are an artifact of how deterministic vignettes are written. Treat this as a hypothesis about a measurable cognitive disposition rather than a piece of linguistic legislation, and design a test that could genuinely move the dispute.

Specify the experiment concretely: the vignettes, the dimensions you would cross (for example, deterministic versus indeterministic universe, and whether the agent’s own reasons and deliberations actually guide the action versus being causally bypassed), the control conditions, the probe questions (judgments about acting freely, being morally responsible, deserving blame or praise, and being able to do otherwise), and the exact pattern of responses that should count as evidence for each side. Consider the possibility that the two factors interact, and say what a specific interaction pattern would indicate about whether lay incompatibilist intuitions are really tracking determinism itself or something else, such as the perceived loss of control or the bypassing of the agent’s mental states.

Then say what the test would and would not settle. In particular, address how each side could reasonably explain away a result that initially favors the other: the compatibilist could diagnose an apparently incompatibilist result as a performance error, a conflation of determinism with constraint or psychological compulsion, or an artifact of abstract versus concrete descriptions; the incompatibilist could diagnose an apparently compatibilist result as a failure of subjects to grasp what determinism entails, a manipulation that was too weak, or a fact about judgments of blame rather than judgments of free action. Finally, identify the part of the compatibilism versus incompatibilism debate that would remain standing no matter how clean the data are, and say whether the experiment is best understood as empirical arbitration or as a device that forces the two positions to state themselves precisely enough that their residual disagreement becomes visible.
