The usual relativity-of-simultaneity argument for the block universe goes roughly like this. Two people pass each other on a street; one is walking toward Andromeda, the other away. Because their "now" hyperplanes are tilted differently, at that moment the toward-Andromeda person's now-slice cuts through Andromeda after the war council has already voted to launch its fleet, while the away-walker's now-slice cuts through Andromeda before the council has even begun deliberating. Since no inertial frame's now is privileged, the future must already be real, and presentism/growing-block are in trouble.

Now vary it. Suppose the council's vote is settled by a genuinely chancy device: a single radioactive atom in a box on the table; if it decays before the council's one-hour recess ends, they launch, and otherwise they don't. The atom's half-life is chosen so that, in the council's proper time, the probability of a decay before the recess is exactly 50%. All inertial observers agree on that probability, and all agree later on whether the decay actually happened. But at the moment the walkers pass each other, the toward-Andromeda hyperplane cuts through the chamber after the atom has decayed and the launch order is signed, while the away-walker's hyperplane cuts through before the recess has begun. So one says the launch is already real; the other says it isn't yet real.

I've heard people say the original argument secretly leans on determinism, and that adding objective chance would keep the future "open" and let a presentist or growing-block view escape.
