A few friends and I were up late and two of them got into an argument about whether special relativity forces the block universe, and I ended up unable to referee.

Omar opened with the standard simultaneity point. You take an event far enough away that it's spacelike separated from you — say a supernova in Andromeda that, in my frame, happens next Thursday. For someone whipping past me at a large fraction of c, that supernova is simultaneous with them here, now. Since special relativity says neither frame is privileged, there's no fact of the matter about whether the explosion is "already real" or "not yet real." Presentism says only what is now is real; but what's now is frame-relative; so what's real ends up frame-relative, and that's not a picture of reality at all. So the block universe wins.

Dana said that's too fast. "Simultaneous with" is just a coordinate relation. She compared it to "here": there's no frame-independent "here" either, but nobody takes that to show that all places are somehow "here" or that "here" is metaphysically deep. Omar replied that the two aren't analogous, because "here" doesn't come with a claim to exclusive reality, whereas "now" does. Dana retorted that the argument only works if you've already adopted a tenseless way of reading the word "real" — you picture the supernova sitting there in spacetime, ready to get cross-sectioned by some moving observer's simultaneity plane — so the argument presupposes the eternalist picture instead of showing it.

Omar then said the only surviving presentism is one with a privileged foliation that relativity itself can't detect, which to him is just adding invisible structure to save a metaphysical intuition. Dana answered that "undetectable by clock synchronization" doesn't mean "false," and that the parsimony argument cuts both ways: the block universe reading itself imports the assumption that the criterion for existence is frame-relative simultaneity.

They couldn't settle it and I don't trust my own read. I can't tell how much of this is physics doing real work and how much is a vocabulary fight.
