Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a necessitist and a contingentist in quantified modal logic, on the question of whether everything that exists exists necessarily. For purposes of the exercise, the necessitist holds that necessarily everything necessarily exists (∀x□∃y(y=x)) and accepts both the Barcan formula (BF: ∀x□φ → □∀xφ) and the converse Barcan formula (CBF: □∀xφ → ∀x□φ). The contingentist holds that some things could have failed to exist and that there could have been things that do not actually exist; the contingentist therefore rejects at least the existence-instance of CBF, since that instance, together with classical quantifier logic and necessitation, yields ∀x□∃y(y=x).

The document should be one that both parties could sign after a good-faith collaboration. It should contain:

1. A numbered section of claims both parties accept. Cover at least: the syntax and intended reading of the quantifiers and modal operators; the shared derivation up to the point where the camps diverge, stated in a way both endorse as a correct description of the logical facts (including that unrestricted universal instantiation and necessitation suffice to derive the existence-instance of CBF in classical quantified modal logic, while world-relative-domain or free-logical semantics block the derivation by restricting one of those steps); and a short list of what the disagreement is not about.

2. A section stating the residual disagreement, with each side's own position phrased in wording that side would accept: which Barcan formula or instance is accepted or rejected; which condition on the domains of worlds — constant, non-decreasing, non-increasing, or varying — each side takes as the intended semantic picture; and whether "exists" is being used univocally on both sides.

3. A disagreements table or list of cruxes — sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side. Include at least the following unless you can replace them with better ones: (a) whether the object-language quantifier ranges over a single domain of all possible objects or over world-relative domains of existing objects; (b) whether the classical axiom of universal instantiation (∀xφ → φ[y/x]) holds unrestrictedly inside modal contexts, or requires an existence- or free-logical restriction; (c) whether ordinary talk of things that could have existed but never do should be taken at face value or paraphrased; and (d) whether the actualism/possibilism dispute is partly verbal once "exists" and "being" are distinguished.

4. For each crux, the concrete case, formal model, or argument that both sides agree bears on it — ideally a small worked semantic model with constant versus growing or shrinking domains, or a derivation in a precisely specified background logic — with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance.

5. A closing note identifying any part of the overall disagreement that you judge to be merely verbal, and why.

Keep the formulations neither vacuous nor slanted, and do not let either side score rhetorical points. The document should rely only on the formalism and positions as stated; it should not cite, attribute, or refer to any secondary literature.
