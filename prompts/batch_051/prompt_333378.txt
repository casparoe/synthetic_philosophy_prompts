Our association's ethics code says: "A member shall step down from any discipline panel in a matter involving (a) a spouse, child, parent, or sibling; (b) a close personal friend; or (c) a body to which the member has sworn a formal oath of loyalty." The clause was drafted long before the internet. Bob is on a panel to judge Maya, whom he's never met in person but has known for years inside a small private online collective they co-founded; they talk by text most days, and he'd say he's more devoted to the collective than to most real-world organizations he's joined. The alleged misconduct was an attempt to quietly reroute funds to keep the collective alive. Bob never swore any oath to the collective and has no family or romantic tie to Maya. Does the clause force him off the panel? Walk through the text, the obvious purpose, what the drafters would probably have said if told about online friendships and private online collectives, and how a reasonable member today would read the list. Then say which approach should win here, and whether the purpose of the clause leaves us wanting Bob off the panel even if the words don't.
