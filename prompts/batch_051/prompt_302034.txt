I'm a retired chemist who got into philosophy of mind over the last few years, and I've been asked to give a short seminar to the local university philosophy department reading group. My paper will defend the claim that Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality doesnt do any real work: I want to say all intentionality is derived in the only sense that matters, and that calling human intentionality original just names the spot where our current explanation gives out. My main move is the old Dennett two-bitser argument: you can organise a system (a machine, or a person shaped by evolution and culture) so that its behaviour is only intelligible if you treat it as understanding what it's doing, and Searle's reply that this is merely as-if intentionality just assumes the very distinction he is trying to draw. My worry is the audience. There is one Searle loyalist who thinks the Chinese Room basically settles the matter, two embodied-cognition people who are sympathetic to Searle but think intentionality requires a living body and a history of interaction with an environment, and a logician who will pounce on any slide from information to meaning. Prepare me for the question period: which objections will I actually get, in roughly the order they will come, phrased the way those people would phrase them, which ones have real force, what should I concede up front, and what short reply should I have ready with a note on where it is soft? Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Please answer in British English.
