Okay so my friend Priya runs a few mid-sized discussion forums (like 20k to 150k users), and she's the one who actually decides what karma and reputation system gets built. She's made a pretty consistant set of calls over the years and I want to pitch her something, but I'd like to hear your prediction before I do.

Her track record:

- She approved a standard up/down vote system where posts show only the net score, and you can't vote until your account is 30 days old and has 25 karma.
- She rejected a proposal to let users spend their own karma to boost a thread's place in the hot ranking. Her take was it would turn reputation into an auction for attention.
- She rejected a marketplace-style mutual feedback system where the two sides of any exchange rate each other and both ratings are public. She was worried about retaliatory reviews.
- She approved, with the condition that the reason goes only to moderators, a rule making downvotes cost 1 karma. Her argument was that it cuts drive-by pile-ons without silencing anyone.
- She approved a trust-level system where accounts gradually unlock content powers (like editing the wiki) based on account age and a clean report history, not karma.
- She rejected a karma-inheritance idea where a new account vouched for by two high-karma users would start with 10% of their average karma. She said it would just create a market for vouches.

The feature I want to pitch is weighted community polls. It would keep the same 30-day/25-karma eligibility rule for voting, but in official policy votes each user's vote would count as the square root of their karma, capped at 20 (so 100 karma gets you 10 votes, 400+ gets you the full 20). No one spends or transfers karma; high-karma users just get a louder vote in governance.

What I want to know is not just approve/reject. Tell me the rules that fit all six of her past calls, whether they come apart on the weighted poll idea, and what each rule would predict for it. Give me your best guess about how she'll actually react, and say which of the older decisions is the closest analog. If her track record doesn't settle it, tell me what principle she'd be adopting by rejecting it versus approving it (with or without conditions). Finally, name the further case you'd bring to her to cleanly seperate whichever rules survive.
