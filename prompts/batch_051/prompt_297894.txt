my brother and I have been arguing about this since nan got her bone scan back: she was told her bone density is in the 'osteopenia' range, not yet osteoporosis, so it isn't a disease and treatment can wait. He's the naturalist about disease; I'm the normativist. He says the GP is right once you get the biology clear: disease is just a part of the body failing to do what it is biologically for, measured against species-typical functioning, and even if we have to put a number on the boundary, that doesn't make the underlying distinction any less real. I say that is sleight of hand: the boundary gets put where it is because of judgements about fracture risk, cost, and what kinds of life are worth worrying about, and no amount of bone scans will tell you where 'disease' begins. We've talked past each other for a year.

Write the joint statement we should have produced. It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims about health, disease, and biology that both of us will sign; (2) a table of the exact remaining disagreements, each worded so each of us accepts the wording of our own view; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or test we'd both agree bears on it, plus what each of us predicts about it. Also flag the places where we probably only disagreed because we were using 'health' or 'disease' differently. Don't let us get away with a vague 'health is complicated' list; each agreed claim should be one a reasonable person could have denied. And don't pick a winner—produce the statement we could both put our names to.
