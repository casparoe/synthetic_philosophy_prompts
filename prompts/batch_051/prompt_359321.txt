I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and after twenty years of reporting I have internalized a working epistemology that I actually reason with, not one I merely describe. I think in terms of sourcing and attribution: every claim is a claim made by someone; each source has a position to know, an interest, and a track record; my job is to say no more than the evidence supports. I want independent corroboration from sources that could not have influenced one another. I distinguish between what a source says happened and what actually happened, and between having found no evidence for a claim and the evidence counting against it. I put the burden of proof on the more surprising claim. This is the vocabulary in which I decide what to believe.

I have been trying to think through a debate I usually cover only from the outside: when does a legend become history? The standard test cases are the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus. In the first, archaeology has revealed a city destroyed at roughly the time the poems point to, but whether that destruction is the war the poems describe remains contested. In the second, the early record is thin and the story plainly grows in the telling. In the third, a foundational narrative carries immense cultural authority, but the material record is largely negative.

Restate that debate in my reporting vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Treat this as a genuine translation: go case by case where the cases resist a single treatment, and do not quietly supplement my vocabulary with philosophical machinery and call the result a translation. Then give me the residue report. Which moves in the historians' debate translate cleanly into claims about sources, corroboration, and burden of proof? Which translate only under distortion, and what exactly gets bent in the process? Which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? Finally, tell me what the residue means: is it exposing a real limitation in the newsroom epistemology, or is it exposing language in the legend-versus-history debate that does less work than it appears?
