I've got three years of funding for a PhD student to work in formal epistemology, specifically on the Judy Benjamin problem and the broader question of how to update on conditional information. I keep going back and forth about whether the standard relative-entropy (KL-minimization) answer is right: it satisfies the constraint P(A|B)=q, but it also moves the probability of the condition B itself, and how much it moves B depends on your old conditional probability P(A|B), not just on P(B). A lot of people think that's a bug, on the grounds that learning something about A given B shouldn't tell you anything about B. But Im not convinced the obvious alternative of just leaving P(B) alone is coherent once you have to iterate the update, so I want an actual research agenda, not a survey or a reading list.

Give me a defended, dependancy-aware prioritization of the open questions in this area. Here are the ones I'm currently weighing (add your own if you think I'm missing something important):

1. Should a rational update on a conditional-probability constraint leave P(B) fixed? If yes, what rule does that, and what does it sacrifice, e.g. iterability or path-independence?
2. Is the input ill-posed? If conditionals are not propositions in your algebra, maybe P(A|B)=q is not the kind of thing you learn outright, and the real question is what the object of learning is and how it constrains a Jeffrey-style revision.
3. Iterated and multiple constraints: if you learn two conditional-probability constraints in sequence, which rules are order-independent? Is path-independence a genuine rationality requirement here or just aesthetically nice?
4. Partial conditional information: how should you update on an inequality like P(A|B) ≥ q, or on a comparative claim like A-given-B being more likely than C-given-D? Do the standard rules generalize, or do we need different machinery?
5. Why minimize KL divergence at all? What axiomatic characterization singles out the right divergence, or family of divergences, for conditional constraints, and does the choice change the answer in a way that matters?
6. The imaging angle: Lewis-style imaging is another way to revise on conditional-ish input. Is there a unified story about when conditional information calls for KL-minimization and when it calls for imaging?
