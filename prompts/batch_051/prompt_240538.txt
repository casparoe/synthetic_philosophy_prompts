A friend of mine chairs the committee at her local council that decides whether risky goings-on can go ahead when they'll affect people who haven't signed up for them. I've been noting her rulings because she seems to be following some principle, but I can't quite work out what it is.

Her record so far:

- She let a large fireworks display go ahead in the park, even though people in the flats around the park faced a small risk of injury without being consulted. Most of them could see the display from their own windows, though none had been asked.
- She approved a bypass around the edge of town despite the slightly raised pollution risk to the houses nearest to it. Those residents had been the loudest campaigners for the bypass, because the town-centre traffic had been making their roads miserable.
- She rejected a chemical plant upwind of a housing estate. It would have added a one-in-a-million lifetime cancer risk to the people downwind, none of whom would have got any of the jobs, and fitting filters would have removed the risk cheaply.
- She approved occasional blasting at a quarry next to a row of cottages, on condition the quarry paid into a fund that would fully cover any cracked windows or plaster. The residents hadn't been consulted beforehand, and none of them worked for the quarry.
- She rejected a plan to send night-time lorries down a residential street as a cut-through. The added accident risk to residents was small, but the only people who stood to gain were the haulage company and a few shops on the far side, and the alternative route added less than a minute to each journey.

Now for the hard one. The regional transport body wants to run high-speed trains through a village. There will be no station in the village, so the residents get no quicker journeys themselves, and no compensation has been offered. They will face a small extra risk from noise and air pollution, well within the legal limits but not zero, while passengers between two nearby cities would save tens of thousands of hours a year.

What should she decide if she wants to be consistent with her earlier calls? Which principle is she implicitly running, which other principle or principles fit her record about as well, and what further case would best show which principle is really doing the work?
