I'm an algebra postdoc, and this term I got stuck teaching a first course in set theory, which means I've been arguing about encodings of the natural numbers more than I ever wanted to. At a holiday party, a philosopher friend insisted that 2 is literally an element of 4, and he meant it set-theoretically: on the von Neumann encoding, 4 = {0,1,2,3}, so 2 is one of its members. He knows the difference between membership and subset; he meant membership. But on Zermelo's encoding, where each number is the singleton of its predecessor, 2 isn't in 4 at all. Since then I've noticed that I run a standing policy in these arguments, and I want to know whether its actually defensible. The policy: when a mathematical claim flips its truth value depending only on which set-theoretic coding is chosen, and nobody is claiming one coding is the correct one, I treat the claim as not being about the mathematical objects themselves and discount it to zero. So "2 ∈ 4" isn't a fact about the numbers 2 and 4; it's a fact about one implementation of the naturals. I use the same rule for "is 0 an element of 3" and for whether the ordered pair (a,b) really is {{a},{a,b}} rather than some other set.

I realize this is structuralism-flavored, but I don't want a reading list or a general defense of structuralism. I want to know whether the rule itself is a good epistemic policy for a working mathematician like me. Sometimes it feels like it saves me from pseudo-questions; other times it feels like a shield, since once you fix a coding, "2 ∈ 4" is a perfectly determinate theorem or falsehood, and there are real contexts where the coding matters. Where's the line between "not a question about arithmetic" and "a genuine question relative to a coding"? Under what conditions would my habit lead me astray, and is there a restated version that keeps the protection without making me the kind of person who dismisses every foundational question as unreal? And how much is my own situation — I'm an algebraist, and I only care about encodings when I have to teach them — doing the work in making the rule feel safe? Please answer in American English.
