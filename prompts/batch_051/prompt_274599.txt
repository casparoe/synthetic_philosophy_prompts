i run a small blog that does the 'which position do you actually hold?' style quizzes, and I want one on how much rule-following a society should demand. It has to sort people by their answers to concrete cases, never by whether they like the word 'tight' or 'loose'. Heres the angle that matters to me: most debates about tight vs loose cultures run together three different grounds for thinking a rule should be followed — (1) the violation does real harm, (2) the rule solves a coordination problem and lets strangers predict each other, and (3) the community has invested social meaning in the rule and feels wounded when its broken. Give me eight questions, each a small ordinary case (parking on the sidewalk, cutting a queue, a school uniform rule, a mask rule, a no-pets clause in a lease, insulting a flag, loud music in a shared courtyard, recycling rules), with two or three answer choices per question that capture different verdicts in plain language. Then build a scoring key: first lay out the space of answer combinations the eight questions allow, then map those patterns onto five positions — (a) tight communitarian, groups may demand conformity for its own sake even with no harm and no coordination value; (b) harm-tracker, a rule is only as binding as the harm it prevents; (c) coordinationist, rules bind because and when they smooth interaction between strangers; (d) social-meaning tracker, a community's felt investment gives a rule its authority; (e) autonomy-first, any rule that doesn't track real harm is presumptively illegitimate. At least three of the questions should be set up so these three grounds clearly pull in different directions. Then flag which answer patterns the key can't reach, or where it leaves someone ambiguous between two positions, and for each empty pattern say whether nobody is there because its incoherent, just unattractive, or your questions never separated it. It should read like a breezy blog quiz, and a reader who has never heard the phrase 'tight vs loose cultures' should still leave with a named position and a one-sentence description.
