I'm building a hobby sim, and I've been trying to score whole world-states by one number, where the only things that count are, for each mind, (a) how conscious it is and (b) how good its current experience feels. Let c be consciousness on a rough 0–100 scale (human 100, dog 20, chicken 5, carp 1, rock 0) and h be felt-goodness on -100..100, from worst agony to best possible. I want you to reverse-engineer what rule my own gut is using, because every formula I try gets one of my verdicts wrong.

My confident verdicts:

1. A rock with h=100 is worth zero. The h reading only matters when there's some consciousness there. So it's not additive, or at least h can't just be added on its own.

2. A dog at h=95 vs a human at h=5: I save the human, not even close. My multiplication guess (c × h) says the dog wins—20 × 95 = 1900 vs 100 × 5 = 500—and I don't buy it. So consciousness isn't just a linear multiplier either, apparently.

3. A billion carp at h=100 do not beat one human at h=5. I don't care how many I stack. This feels like the same instinct as (2): once there's a high-enough consciousness in the picture, it just dominates lower ones, like lexicographic sorting where the first character wins no matter what comes later.

4. But one carp at h=100 beats an empty world. So a carp's happiness is worth something. I can't say its zero. So I don't have a clean cutoff below which h stops counting; I have something more like "it counts within its band, but the bands don't trade against each other." Is that even coherent?

5. Suffering seems to break the lexicographic picture. A carp at h=-95 vs a human at h=-2 (stubbed toe): I'd fix the carp first. So for pain the human doesn't automatically dominate, which is the opposite of what my positive-side lexicographic rule says. Maybe pain aggregates more like magnitude-of-h dominates, or maybe I need an asymmetric rule.

6. And I have no idea where the bands go. Dog is obviously in the human's band; carp obviously isnt. Chicken? Alligator? A severely mentally impaired human? I go back and forth.

So: is there one principle (or a small set of candidates) that captures all of 1–5, stated precisely enough that I could implement it as a scoring function? For each clause of your principle, tell me which verdict motivates it. Then tell me what it says about some cases I didn't list—especially the chicken and the severely impaired human—and which of my verdicts, if any, I have to give up to keep the principle from being an ugly pile of patches.
