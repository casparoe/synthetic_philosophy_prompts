I'm a TA for an intro New Testament class, and I just got out of a long argument with a friend about weither James 2 actually contradicts Paul on justification. My friend kept insisting it's a flat contradiction; I kept saying the two authors can be read together. I have to grade a short-answer question on the passage below, and I want to make sure the grade isn't just my side of that argument in disguise. So I'm handing it to you.

Could you act as an expert on New Testament theology and the Paul–James debate? Think carefully, and before you settle on a grade, do a quick brainstorm: list the main ways interpreters have tried to reconcile (or refused to reconcile) James and Paul, and note what each reading would treat as the decisive evidence in James 2:21–24. Question:
Consider James 2:21–24 (KJV):
"Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar?
Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?
And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.
Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only."
Explain whether James is here contradicting Paul's teaching that a person is justified by faith apart from works of the law. If they are not contradicting each other, explain as precisely as you can what James means when he says Abraham was justified by works, and what the relation between faith and works is in this passage.

Student answer:
James is answering a distorted version of Paul, not Paul himself. Paul's polemic was against works of the law, meaning the boundary markers like circumcision and purity rules, not against loving obedience; James is concerned with ordinary acts of mercy and obedience. So there is no contradiction. The Abraham example is exactly the proof: Abraham was justified by faith in Genesis 15:6, and then the offering of Isaac in Genesis 22 is where that earlier declaration was fulfilled, meaning the initial justification was incomplete until the work completed it. So justification has two stages, faith first and works finishing it, and that is precisely what James means when he says by works faith was made perfect. The works don't create faith, but they complete it into the kind that saves. Because of this, James actually agrees with Paul that final justification at the judgment is based on works; Paul says in Romans 2:13 that it is the doers of the law, not the hearers, who will be justified. The only real difference is that Paul writes about the first stage, initial justification by faith, and James writes about the second stage, final justification by works. They aren't teaching opposite things; therefor they are describing two parts of one process.
