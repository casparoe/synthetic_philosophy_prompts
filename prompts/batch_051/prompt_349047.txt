I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size company, and last week I got dragged into yet another fight about whether two services "do the same thing." We bought a smaller company a while back and now we have two notification systems sitting next to each other: one fans out through Kafka, the other just wakes up on a cron schedule, polls a database table and sends the emails itself. Product want to declare them duplicates and kill one; one of our architects says they're different features because they fail differently (a lagging consumer group is not the same outage as a missed cron run), and another says that's exactly the implementation detail we're supposed to ignore. It struck me that this is the same old argument in philosophy of mind about whether a mental state is defined by its causal role or by the stuff that realises it — functionalism and multiple realizability, or at least what I half-remember of them from a podcast. I've got no formal philosophy, so I'm probably paraphrasing badly, but the analogy seems too neat to be a coincidence: feature = role, implementation = realiser.

I'd like you to turn that into a procedure my team could actually run. We do these consolidation/deprecation calls roughly once a fortnight, and the outcome decides what gets sunset and whether customers have to migrate, so the stakes are real but nobody has a day to spend on it. The person running it will usually be an engineer with zero philosophy background, and whatever we adopt has to be honest about where it's making a judgment call dressed up as a rule. Give me a checklist or a fixed sequence of tests — answer in British English, please — for deciding when two implementations count as "the same feature" for these decisions. Then apply it to two or three worked cases: one should be the Kafka-vs-cron fan-out thing, and at least one should be a case where the procedure comes back with an answer product doesn't like. And flag the steps where no amount of legwork settles it.
