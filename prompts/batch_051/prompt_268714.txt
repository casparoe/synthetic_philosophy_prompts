You are an expert in philosophy of cognitive science and conceptual analysis. I am a philosophy professor preparing a unit on modularity of mind, from Fodor's original proposals through massive-modularity views, and I need a working definition of a cognitive module that I can actually teach and apply. Please think carefully before settling on an answer: start by brainstorming what a module is supposed to do or explain, and which criteria are in tension with one another. Then propose a definition that gets the following straightforward cases right. It should count early visual processing, which is rapid, mandatory, and unaffected by what the perceiver believes or wants, as modular. It should count syntactic parsing in language comprehension, which assigns grammatical structure even to nonsense sentences or to sentences the hearer knows are false, as modular. And it should count slow, voluntary, domain-general processes such as deciding whether to change careers, or working through an unfamiliar logic puzzle, as non-modular. Now run your definition against the following harder cases and return a definite verdict, modular or not, for each: (a) face recognition, which is fast and automatic and can be selectively lost after brain damage, yet is sensitive to context and to what one knows about the person; (b) mindreading, the attribution of beliefs and desires to others, which competent adults do quickly and spontaneously even when not trying, though its outputs are revisable by explicit deliberation; (c) skilled reading, a recently acquired and culture-specific ability that becomes automatic and can be selectively lost after brain damage, but is plainly learned and built from older capacities; and (d) subitizing, the immediate exact apprehension of how many items are in a small array, before any counting. Where your definition yields the wrong verdict or no verdict, revise it or defend the surprising result. Finally, say whether you think modularity is best treated as all-or-nothing or as coming in degrees, and whether a single definition can cover both narrowly Fodorian input systems and the broader uses of "module" in massive-modularity debates.
