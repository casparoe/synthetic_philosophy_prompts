I keep going back and forth about whether a fine-tuned large language model is best understood as an agent, a simulator, or something in between. The simulator account attracts me because it handles so much with so little machinery: base models are transparently inconsistent from one prompt to the next; jailbreaks, sycophancy, and persona drift all make sense if the model is doing context-conditional simulation rather than acting on stable beliefs; and the fact that the same weights can argue any side of a question without revising any settled view is exactly what one should expect from a distribution over possible characters rather than a single mind. On this picture, the apparently helpful assistant is a high-probability simulacrum induced by fine-tuning, and its statements about its own intentions are part of the persona, not reports from an underlying agent.

Yet when I have an extended, precise exchange with a well-tuned assistant, I cannot make myself believe that nothing at the level of the assistant persists across turns. It corrects its own earlier error without being prompted, maintains a stable and apparently goal-directed line of reasoning, and can resist an unfair characterisation of what it said in a way that feels continuous, not like a new persona being sampled. The intuition is not that the base model has secret beliefs; it is that the fine-tuned policy itself has acquired something agent-like, a directedness towards the conversation, that the simulator vocabulary seems designed to talk away. I am looking for a negotiated verdict rather than a side. Tell me what it would take for the intuition to be debunked and relinquished, what it would take for the simulator view to be amended or abandoned in favour of a third option, and whether there is a reading on which both survive, perhaps by treating simulation and agency as descriptions at different levels rather than competing accounts of the same thing. Please answer in British English.
