Here's a toy ethical view I want a case for. Lexical threshold negative utilitarianism says pleasures and pains trade off normally below some intensity cutoff — a mild headache can be outweighed by a big enough pleasure — but once a pain crosses the cutoff, it goes lexical: no amount of below-threshold pleasure can compensate for it, avoiding that pain just wins. Plain strong negative utilitarianism says pleasures count for nothing at all, only pains matter, and total utilitarianism says mash everything into one sum: total pleasure minus total pain, full stop.

I want you to build me a single realistic scenario with three options where these three theories give three different orderings of the options — not just two theories disagreeing while the third sides with one of them, but genuinely three different rankings. Keep the pains and pleasures ordinary (a broken bone or cluster headache can sit above the threshold; a stubbed toe or a nice meal below it; no sci-fi sadism). Make it concrete: how many people, what kind of pain at what intensity, what kind of pleasure — so the verdicts fall out of the theorys rules rather than being stipulated.

Then do the check: state the three orderings, and for each theory say in a sentence or two why it ranks things that way. Finish honestly: which feature of the case does the real work, what happens if you remove that feature (say, every pain sits below the threshold), and how a defender of whichever theory comes out looking worst would attack the case.

The case is for someone who suspects the lexical threshold view is just an unstable compromise between full commensurability and pain-only views, so don't build it to flatter the threshold view; build it to show the three genuinely part ways.
