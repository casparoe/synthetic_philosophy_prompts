A consciousness-transfer clinic proposes the following continuity rule for its gradual whole-brain emulation procedure. Patients are awake while one small brain region at a time is replaced by a functionally equivalent prosthetic module. A region is replaced only if, throughout and after the replacement, the patient reports no change in any conscious experience, and a blinded panel cannot distinguish the patient's answers to a fixed battery of cognitive and autobiographical questions from the patient's own baseline answers. Walk through how this rule would actually behave as a test of whether the emulation is the same mind: what the test genuinely checks, what changes could pass it undetected, what incentives it creates for the clinic, the patient, and the panel, where it gets gamed first, and which failure modes could be fixed without gutting the procedure. Also say which consideration would most change your verdict about whether the rule is a good test, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
