In discussions of deepfakes and the epistemic backstop, a familiar analogy is monetary. Before cheap synthetic media arrived, the argument runs, ordinary testimonial claims were like paper currency: convenient, circulating everywhere, but only as trustworthy as their issuers. Photographs, audio recordings, and video were the reserve that backed that currency — the hard thing a disputed claim could be redeemed for, credible without anyone's say-so. Deepfakes, on this picture, are comparable to large-scale counterfeiting, or to leaving the gold standard without any credible replacement: once the redemption medium itself can be fabricated cheaply at scale, the testimonial economy is left with unbacked paper, and the old backstop no longer anchors it. Proponents of the analogy usually draw the conclusion that the right response is to build a new technical backstop — cryptographic provenance, signed capture, chain-of-custody records, and similar authentication infrastructure — just as a monetary system requires either a credible anchor or institutions strong enough to operate a fiat currency. Please press on this analogy and tell me how far it actually holds. Give an explicit correspondence table: what in the epistemic setting plays the role of the currency, the gold reserve, redemption, counterfeit, the mint, the note-holder, and the bank. Then identify the places where the mapping breaks. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that are merely cosmetic. Pay particular attention to what the monetary framing quietly imports: for example, that testimonial trust is the kind of thing that can be backed by a stock of hard items, that consulting a recording is like redeeming a note, and that trust is chiefly sustained by redeemability rather than by ongoing practices and relationships. Finally, say whether the conclusion — that the proper response to deepfakes is to construct a new technical backstop — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and offer a better analogy if you think there is one.
