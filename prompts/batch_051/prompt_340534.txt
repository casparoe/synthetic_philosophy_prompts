I teach a first-year interdisciplinary seminar called How We Got Here: about twenty students and one eighty-minute session. The next topic is the shift from foraging to farming, and the students arrive more or less committed to the view that farming was obvious progress — more food, more security, more of what makes a life good. I want an activity that makes them feel the force of the opposite thought: that the transition can be read as a collective trap whose short-term winners ended up with more work, poorer health, and new forms of inequality, and that hunter-gatherer life may have been a kind of affluence rather than a squalid struggle for survival — and I want them to feel this before it is named, not after I lecture it at them. Design an activity in which the participants themselves generate the dynamic and only notice where they have been heading when it is named in the debrief.

The activity should fit one eighty-minute session, use no real money and minimal materials, and may be a multi-generation simulation, a role-play across groups, or a set of staged choices under uncertainty. Give me all of the following: (1) the complete rules or roles; (2) a scoring or payoff scheme that makes different subsistence strategies genuinely tempting for different reasons, so the designed conclusion is not handed to the students; (3) a timeline for the session; (4) a debrief that maps particular moments in the activity onto the core ideas, including how affluence might be measured by wants satisfied rather than sheer output, how a locally rational choice can become collectively irreversible, and how storable surplus creates the conditions for durable inequality rather than automatically producing it. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — groups that try to game the scoring, participants who claim the outcome was rigged against their choices, a design move that makes the trap visible too early — and how I should handle each without giving away the ending. Finally, say which feature of the real historical debate the activity cannot reproduce in the room, and where I will need to supply that as a caveat rather than as a felt experience.
