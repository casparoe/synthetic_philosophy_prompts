I've been chewing on a thought experiment I half remember and it's got me stuck. Someone imagined a universe containing nothing but two iron spheres, exactly the same size, same metal, same temperature, floating a couple miles apart. The line of thought was: if those two spheres share absolutly every property, then there can't really be two of them — if two things were alike in every single way, they'd have to be the same thing. But then I watched a science video saying two electrons in an atom are 'indistinguishable' — the equations come out the same if you swap them, and in some sense nature keeps no record of which is which. So now I'm wondering: how would you actually test whether two things can be perfectly alike and still be two seperate things? I don't want the usual back-and-forth about definitions; I want the closest thing to an actual experiment or observation, what result would count for each side, and what the test would still leave unresolved no matter how it came out. Also: when physicists say electrons are indistinguishable, are they even talking about the same thing as the two sphere idea, or is that a red herring?
