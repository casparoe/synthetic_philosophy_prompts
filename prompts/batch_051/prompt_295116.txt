People in my AI governance group keep waving off the idea of AI legal personhood by saying it is just the corporate personhood move all over again: corporations are already persons for legal purposes, and nobody thinks they are humans, so giving an AI legal personhood is a boring legal convenience rather than a deep claim about consciousness or moral status. I want to know how far that analogy actually holds. They sometimes back it with Marshall's line in Dartmouth College about a corporation being an artificial being that exists only in the mind of the law. Give me the correspondence table: what in the corporate case plays the role of the AI's developers, users, training data, system prompt, assets, liability shield, and what would count as piercing the corporate veil for an AI? Then tell me the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion. I am especially suspicious that the corporate form has natural persons as its ultimate supports — shareholders, directors, creditors, beneficiaries — so legal personhood is just a vessel for aggregating their interests. For an AI, what is being aggregated, and whose interests are actually behind the entity? Does the relaxed 'it is just another legal fiction' conclusion survive once that disanalogy is on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work?
