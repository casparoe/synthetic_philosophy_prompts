I've been rereading the lordship/bondage section of Hegel's Phenomenology of Mind (Baillie translation), and one sentence stopped me: "the phase of inherent existence (Ansichsein) or thinghood, which received its shape and form through labour, is no other substance than consciousness." The surrounding passage in section 197 says the bondsman reaches genuine self-consciousness not through being recognized by the master, but through formative labor on a thing: the worked-on thing becomes his object, so he sees his own activity reflected back and becomes a thinking, free self-consciousness. The master, who just consumes, never gets this.

Now here's the parallel I can't decide about. In reinforcement learning and embodied cognition there's a familiar-sounding claim that an agent builds a better world model by acting on its environment than by passively observing it, because the action's effects on the state make the observed state partly the agent's own product. I keep feeling like Hegel is making the same structural point, but about self-consciousness rather than world models. The master, who only consumes, stays dependent; the agent that only watches never gets a grip on the environment. Is this a real identity of structure, or is it just a loose analogy built on the phrase "seeing yourself in what you've shaped"? If it's real, does anything transfer from Hegel to RL—for example, a reason to think online interaction yields not merely better predictions but a different kind of self-model? If it's superficial, where exactly does the analogy break?
