I'm on the school board for a public district where about a third of families are first- or second-generation immigrants. We vote in three weeks on a new staff conduct policy, and the one clause dividing everyone is about religious dress: should teachers and other staff be barred from wearing visible religious symbols—headscarves, crosses, kippahs, turbans, the lot—while on duty? The committee that drafted it says a public school has to present a neutral face to children, and that staff act as representatives of the institution, so their individual religious identities stay at the door (that's the French laïcité logic). The dissenting members say this would in practice screen observant Muslim women, Sikh men, and others out of a public profession, and that the school should be modeling accommodation of difference rather than flattening it (the Anglo-American multiculturalist line). A third group floated an amendment banning only conspicuous symbols, but nobody can say what counts as conspicuous — same headscarf, different meeting? — and I suspect that's where the real fight is.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate to my fellow board members before the vote. It should recommend one concrete decision (adopt the full ban, delete the clause, or adopt the conspicuous-only version with a workable definition of 'conspicuous'), lead with the bottom line, and then give the reasoning. Engage the strongest argument for whichever option you're telling us to reject — not a strawman — and don't punt to a task force. Max 600 words. Seperately, if you go conspicuous-only, the definition has to be something a substitute teacher could apply on a Tuesday morning.
