A philosophical society's constitution includes the following two provisions:

"3.1 Eligibility. A system shall be admitted to the Register of Recognised Reasoners if, in the Qualifying Examination, it performs to the standard of a competent human examinee on every section specified in Schedule B.
3.2 Substrate-blindness. In applying clause 3.1, the Board shall disregard the medium in which a candidate's capacities are realized, and no candidate shall be refused admission solely on the ground that it is realized in silicon rather than carbon, or in any other substrate."

The clauses were adopted after a long campaign to ensure that sophisticated machine intelligences would be assessed on their capacities rather than their ancestry; the drafters had in mind electronic computers that reason much as humans do, only in different hardware.

A consortium now submits a candidate that passes every section of the examination at superhuman speed. It is not, in any ordinary sense, reasoning. The consortium engineers enumerated every possible input string the examination could produce, up to the examination's maximum length of 80,000 characters, and paired each with a correct response computed in advance by human mathematicians. The candidate is a finite lookup device: it represents nothing as a problem, draws no inference, and would fail any question reworded even trivially outside the enumerated set. The consortium argues that clause 3.2 was written precisely to stop the Board from rejecting unusual realizers on intuitive grounds, and that the device is just another physical system whose input–output function matches the examination profile. A Board member replies that clause 3.1 was meant to identify a capacity present in the candidate, not to license the submission of a printed record of reasoning done elsewhere, by the engineers.

Does this device qualify under clause 3.1? Work through what the text requires, what the clauses were for, what the drafters would most plausibly have said if this case had been put to them, and what a reasonable reader would take the constitution to mean. Then say whether there is a coherent reading that admits a genuinely reasoning silicon system while excluding this device, without falling back on an unargued prejudice about substrate. If the cleanest readings cannot do this, explain what that shows about the functionalist principle clause 3.2 appears to state.
