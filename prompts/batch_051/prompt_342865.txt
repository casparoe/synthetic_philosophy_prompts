Two graduate students in my philosophy of physics seminar argued about whether Landauer's principle is what actually rescues the second law from Maxwell's demon. Here is how it went.

Rhea: The demon has to detect molecular speeds, and measurement is a physical interaction. To learn which side of a partition a molecule is on, the demon must couple to the gas, and that coupling dissipates enough entropy to compensate for the subsequent sorting. So the second law is never really threatened.

Sam: That is the old mistake. Detection can in principle be done reversibly; the older analyses treated measurement as necessarily dissipative, but they were really smuggling in the cost of resetting the instrument. The demon can record the outcome in a memory bit at no thermodynamic cost. The bill comes only when the memory is erased: Landauer's principle says erasing one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of heat. Erasure, not measurement, is what pays the entropy bill.

Rhea: Then a demon with an unbounded memory could write each result to a fresh register and never erase. It would sort indefinitely, lowering the gas's entropy with no compensating entropy increase anywhere. If erasure is the only cost, we still have a violation; you have just postponed it.

Sam: Unbounded memory is not a physical operating cycle. Any real demon is finite and eventually has to reset its memory to keep working, and at that point the Landauer cost kicks in. An infinite blank tape is a never-ending thermodynamic resource, so that case lies outside the ordinary scope of the second law rather than being a counterexample to it.

Rhea: But now the second law is only being saved by declaring infinite resources out of bounds. If Maxwell's problem is resolved by a boundary condition on the demon's memory rather than by a local physical mechanism, that looks less like an explanation and more like changing the rules.

They asked me to referee, but I could not sort out the moves. Please adjudicate the exchange stage by stage: for each of Rhea's and Sam's claims, say who is right about what, whether the reply meets the objection, and what both are missing. End by saying who is more right overall.
