Please give me a precise credence for the following proposition:

Co-nationality itself grounds genuine special claims. That is, in a forced choice between aiding one of two strangers who are identical in every relevant respect except that one shares your nationality, the shared nationality supplies a real, non-instrumental, pro tanto moral reason to aid that person rather than the other. By non-instrumental I mean that the reason is not merely a useful heuristic for impartial beneficence, not a stand-in for some other relationship such as friendship or family, and not just an excusable bias; the claim is that co-nationality is itself morally weighty.

Decompose the probability into at least the following four terms, and adjust or rename them if you think I have carved the question at the wrong joints:

(a) The probability that associative duties in general, such as those owed to friends or family, are genuine and not fully reducible to impartial duties plus convenience.

(b) Conditional on some associative duties being genuine, the probability that co-nationality is the kind of relation that can ground such a duty, rather than being a morally arbitrary shared label, an imagined community, or too thin and diffuse a relation to do the grounding work.

(c) The probability that the firm intuition of special obligation to compatriots survives debunking explanations that trace it to kin-altruism heuristics, in-group/out-group psychology, or parochial self-interest; that is, that the intuition is best explained by the truth of the proposition rather than by a cognitive misfiring we should discount.

(d) Conditional on a special claim surviving, the probability that it is a fundamental moral relation rather than something whose force is fully explained by the instrumental goods of shared political self-determination, a functioning common legal order, or the need for bounded trust and solidarity in large-scale collective action.

For each term, give a number and a one-line justification. Then show explicitly how you combine the terms into your bottom-line credence, noting where a term functions as a condition, a multiplier, or a near-tautological restatement. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in, and specify the single argument, piece of evidence, or hypothetical that would move that term the most.
