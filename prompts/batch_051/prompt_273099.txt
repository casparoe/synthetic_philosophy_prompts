A friend and I keep circling back, in a reading group on population ethics, to a dispute about what is sometimes called lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the view that suffering above a certain intensity has lexical priority over everything below that threshold, so that no amount of mild or moderate happiness, aggregated across any number of people, can outweigh or compensate for a single instance of above-threshold suffering, while below the threshold ordinary trade-offs between pleasure and pain remain. I want your actual credence in the following claim, stated precisely enough to be true or false. There exists a threshold of suffering intensity such that (i) in axiological terms, no amount of below-threshold positive well-being, no matter how widely distributed, can outweigh one instance of above-threshold suffering, and (ii) in deontic terms, preventing such above-threshold suffering takes strict priority over producing below-threshold positive well-being whenever the two conflict. Please give a number rather than a survey of positions or an "it depends." My own credence is roughly 0.6; my friend's is roughly 0.2.

Show your work by decomposing the claim. I would like rough probabilities for at least the following sub-questions: whether pleasure and pain are commensurable on a single evaluative scale at all; whether value must be continuous and Archimedean or can admit genuine discontinuities of the kind a lexical threshold requires; whether there is a principled way to locate the threshold and whether actual cases of extreme suffering (unremitting severe pain, certain end-of-life states, torture) clearly fall above it; whether a finite but overwhelmingly large weight on extreme suffering could reproduce every practical verdict of a lexical view, and whether that distinction matters; whether aggregating many small benefits across many people could ever legitimately outweigh one person's concentrated extreme suffering; whether the axiological claim, even if true, carries over to a strict obligation to prioritize; and how far the intuitions driving the threshold view can be trusted rather than being artifacts of scope insensitivity or an asymmetry bias. For each, say how strongly your final number depends on it and how the pieces combine. Then tell me which single sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, what kind of argument or evidence would move that sub-estimate the most, and—given that I sit near 0.6 and my friend near 0.2—where you suspect the real disagreement between us lives.
