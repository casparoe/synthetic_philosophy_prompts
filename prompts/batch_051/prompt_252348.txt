For a value theory seminar I have been asked to write down my considered position on spectrum arguments, and I would like a consistency check before I circulate it. My commitments are as follows.

(1) All-things-considered betterness is transitive. I treat this as a constraint on any acceptable view, and I would give up a great deal elsewhere before abandoning it.

(2) In a spectrum case — a sequence of alternatives in which each adjacent pair is separated by a change too small to register as a loss — I endorse every adjacent comparison: each later item is not worse than the one immediately before it.

(3) I also endorse the comparison of the endpoints: the first item in the series is clearly better than the last.

(4) I reject parity and incomparability. For any two items in such a series, exactly one of "better than," "worse than," and "equally good" holds.

(5) I take "not worse than" to mean "better than or equally good," and I assume that this relation inherits its transitivity from the transitivity of betterness together with the transitivity of equality.

(6) Finally, I think the relevant comparisons are vague: there is no sharp fact about where in the series the first worsening occurs.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which pairs only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, identify the cheapest revision that would resolve each, and say which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent — and what that revision would leave unexplained.
