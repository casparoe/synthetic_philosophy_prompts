I am a PhD student in evolutionary biology, and I have persuaded two colleagues to attempt a good-faith adversarial collaboration. One is an adaptationist population geneticist who holds that natural selection is, in the respects that matter, an optimization process—noisy, slow, and local, but optimization nonetheless—and that machine learning is right to mine it for principles. The other is a learning theorist who holds that natural selection is at best a stupid optimizer: a gradient-free, myopic sieve whose apparent success does not license optimizer vocabulary, and whose example gradient-based methods should treat as a warning rather than a template. Both accept that their disagreement runs through a single passage from Charles Darwin, On the Origin of Species, first edition, Chapter IV:

"It may be said that natural selection is daily and hourly scrutinising, throughout the world, every variation, even the slightest; rejecting that which is bad, preserving and adding up all that is good; silently and insensibly working, whenever and wherever opportunity offers, at the improvement of each organic being in relation to its organic and inorganic conditions of life."

Draft the joint statement these two could sign. It should open with a numbered list of claims they agree on, including a shared account of what this passage does and does not establish. It should then present a disagreements table whose rows give, for each residual disagreement, the live question phrased so both accept it, each side's position in wording that side would accept as its own, the crux whose resolution would move at least one of them, and the case, simulation, or argument both agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The agreed claims should not be vacuous, and the statements of the positions should not be slanted. Please write the response in American English.
