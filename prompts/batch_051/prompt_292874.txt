In a chapter of my dissertation I am cataloguing the available responses to the identification problem for the natural numbers—the fact that the truth of 2 ∈ 4 flips depending on which standard set-theoretic reduction one adopts. My list so far has four entries. (1) Indeterminacy: the sentence 2 ∈ 4 has no determinate truth-value outside an antecedently chosen reduction. (2) Structuralism: natural numbers are only positions in the natural-number structure, so asking whether one of them is an element of another is a category mistake rather than an open question. (3) Privileged reduction: one reduction may be preferred on broad theoretical grounds; on the von Neumann ordinals, for instance, membership among the finite ordinals coincides with the less-than relation, so 2 ∈ 4 comes out true for a reason rather than by bare stipulation. (4) Fictionalism: arithmetic has no objects at all, so 2 ∈ 4 has no literal answer, though it can be answered inside the fiction. Give me a fifth response that is genuinely distinct from these—not a restatement, special case, or recombination of any of them—and say what it explains about the status of arithmetic that the other four do not.
