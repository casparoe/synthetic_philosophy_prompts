After a career in the natural sciences, I have recently been reading philosophy of technology, and I have become stuck on a dispute about persuasive design. One proponent argues that attention-holding design is presumptively legitimate when it neither deceives nor coerces, because adults' continued engagement reveals their actual preferences and overriding it would be paternalistic. The other argues that such design can manipulate even in the absence of deception and coercion, because it predictably exploits variable rewards, loss aversion, and ordinary precommitment failures, producing engagement that does not reflect the user's settled ends. Please write a dialogue between these two equally competent proponents, addressing whether non-deceptive influence over attention can undermine autonomy rather than reducing the disagreement to deception or force. Each must respond to the other's actual arguments; neither side should be a strawman. Give me exactly five numbered exchanges, alternating speakers, each exchange two or three sentences, with no framing introduction, stage directions, or concluding paragraph.
