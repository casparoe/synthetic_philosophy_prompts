Ever time I read about futarchy the proposal gets summarized like this: first we vote on a metric for how well the country is doing, then for each proposed policy we open a prediction market on what that metric will be if the policy goes into effect, and finally we pass whichever policy has the highest market price. But a prediction market only means something because it eventually gets settled against what actually happened. If we're choosing between two policies A and B, only one of them can actually go into effect, so the other policy's market never has a real outcome to settle against. Doesn't that mean futarchy is comparing one number that will eventually get checked against reality with another number that never will? And if the market on the losing policy just gets voided, what incentive did traders ever have to keep that price honest, instaed of just trading on the policy they want to pass? I feel like I'm missing the piece that turns a bunch of counterfactual markets into an actual decision rule.
