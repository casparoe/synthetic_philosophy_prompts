For the last year or so I've been going back and forth in a forum argument about why languages tolerate homonymy, and it has stalled so completely that I cant tell anymore whether anyone is actually disagreeing about the world. The two camps, as I'd steelman them: one says homonymy is efficient coding — short, high-frequency words get reused because context and prior expectations do the disambiguation for free, so a language optimising for information rate under production constraints will keep a fair bit of ambiguity on purpose. The other says homonymy is just tolerated noise — sound change, borrowing, and drift keep collapsing distinct forms into the same shell, and the cost per token is so small that selection never gets a grip, so it survives despite being a defect, not because it earns its keep.

We all agree on bank and pitch and the usual examples, and at this point we agree on the broad descriptive facts as well. The efficiency camp keeps pointing out that homonyms skew short and high-frequency, which is what you'd expect if a channel is packing information under a length budget; the noise camp says that's exactly what drift predicts too, because frequent short forms simply have more chances to collide and less pressure to be repaired. So the one bit of data both sides cite gets explained twice, and nobody moves.

My working theory is that the whole impasse is about explanatory standards rather than an empirical claim: one side is asking what maintains the equilibrium (why the system keeps the ambiguity around), the other is asking what produced it historically (why it got a foothold at all), and 'why does a language tolerate X' quietly slides between those two readings. If my theory is right, the argument is stuck because we're answering different questions under one heading, not because we need more data.

Diagnose it instead of picking a side. Is my working theory right, or is the disagreement verbal, or is there actually an empirical crux about how much ambiguity a language can carry before the costs show up? And what would count as progress under your diagnosis — a corpus result, a formal model of the channel, tabooing the word efficiency, or a reason to expect no progress at all?
