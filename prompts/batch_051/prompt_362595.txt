i've been reading the cross-cultural moral psychology stuff — the WEIRD problem, all that — and i'm now stuck in a way that feels like it matters more than the usual textbook argument from disagreement. the view i'm drawn to is this: if folk moral judgments vary as much as the surveys suggest — not just in the surface rules but in what counts as harm, who's in the moral circle, whether third-party punishment even makes sense — then the best explanation is that morality is a set of culturally local norms, not something that tracks objective moral facts. that view is tidy: one mechanism explains a huge amount of variation, and it gives a clear instruction, namely treat your own convictions as local products rather than discoveries. but i have one conviction that won't budge: that cutting into a child's genitals without medical reason and without her consent is not merely wrong by my culture's lights, full stop — and a society that treats it as a harmless local custom is making a mistake, not just being different. i know the objectivist's usual reply is that the disagreement dissolves once you control for differing factual beliefs and incentives, and that move works for some cases, but reaching for it here feels like special pleading for the one case i care about. so which should yield? do the cross-cultural data give me reason to debunk this intuition as my own parochial psychology in a universalist costume, or does the intuition give me reason to amend or abandon the tidy anti-objectivist story? or is there a reading on which they're compatible — and what would license each of those moves, rather than just asserting one of them?
