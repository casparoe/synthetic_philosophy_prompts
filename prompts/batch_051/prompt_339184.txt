I'm taking a course on the philosophy of the social sciences, and I keep circling back to what it could mean for a long-run prediction to be falsified when its proponents can always say the time scale was just too short. The case I have in mind is Malthus. His famous argument was that population tends to increase geometrically while the means of subsistence increase only arithmetically, so that unless some check intervenes, population must eventually press against the food supply and be cut back by misery or vice. Two centuries later, the global catastrophe he forecast for a fairly near horizon has not arrived in the form he described: world population has grown several times over, yet agricultural output has grown with it, and famine has remained regional and episodic rather than the universal check his argument seemed to predict. Some people defend Malthus by saying the theory was right but early—that the underlying resource constraints are real, and that fossil fuels, industrial nitrogen fixation, and the Green Revolution merely postponed the reckoning rather than refuting the mechanism. Others reply that this is unfalsifiable rescue: if every failed forecast can be redescribed as a postponement, then "right but early" does no empirical work.

I want you to design a test of the claim that Malthus was right but early rather than simply wrong or rescued ad hoc. The test should be one a researcher could specify in advance: what quantity would be measured, over what span, what trend or event would count for and against the mechanism, and how a genuine postponement would be distinguished from a failed model. Be concrete enough that two scientists could agree in advance about what result would count. Then say exactly what a positive result would and would not settle—does "right but early" survive a positive result, or would all we have shown be something weaker about a single mechanism, such as diminishing returns or carrying capacity? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
