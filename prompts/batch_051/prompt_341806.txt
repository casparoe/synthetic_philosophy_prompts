In a seminar on consciousness, a classmate shut down a discussion of whether a split-brain patient's silent right hemisphere has its own point of view by announcing that "consciousness is an illusion." His move seemed to be that there is no fact of the matter to look for, because the "point of view" itself does not exist. I have also heard the same sentence used to dismiss questions about machine sentience, and, oddly, to mean something much narrower about the self being a constructed narrative. Before I push back, I want to know what the sentence could actually be asserting. Separate the distinct precise claims that "consciousness is an illusion" might be making, from the least to the most radical. For each reading, say (a) whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible philosophical thesis, or likely false or self-undermining, and (b) what it would imply about the hard problem of consciousness—does this reading solve it, dissolve it, deny its subject matter, or leave it untouched? Finally, identify which reading or readings would actually support using the slogan to dismiss the split-brain question, and which readings would merely change the subject.
