I spent most of my career as an experimental physicist and only got serious about philosophy after I retired, so bare with me if I frame this like an experimental design problem. I've been reading the mental imagery debate — Kosslyn on one side saying images are depictive, that there is a real spatial array in the head, and Pylyshyn on the other saying the picture talk is a seductive illusion and the underlying code is propositional, with the experimental effects explained by subjects' tacit knowledge and task demands. Here's the hunch I'd like turned into a proposal. In physics and engineering we constantly work with representations that are spatially organized without being pictures of anything: topographic maps, graded fields, phase arrays, that kind of thing. It seems to me both camps treat the choice as exhaustive — either picture-like or language-like — and because of that "depictive" and "spatially organized" get run together. I suspect a good part of the behavioral evidence (scanning, rotation, interference) settles the spatial-organization question but not the depictive-versus-propositional one, because the experiments where designed against the wrong pair of alternatives.

I'm applying for a one-year research fellowship, my first in philosophy, and I need a short proposal, around two pages. Turn this hunch into one. State the question precisely — what exactly would have been shown if the project succeeds. Say why the existing positions leave it open rather than settle it. Then lay out what I'd actually do month by month, and why this sort of conceptual work is the right next step rather than just running more experiments. I'd also like a section on what a useful deliverable looks like for people doing empirical work on imagery, and a section on what counts as the project failing. Keep the scope realistic for one year, and don't include a bibliography or citations — I'll fill those in myself.
