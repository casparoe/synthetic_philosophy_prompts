The criteria I mean by a role (my own paraphrase of Confucian role ethics, not something you need to check against any text): (1) constitutive—the role partly constitutes who the parties are, so you couldn't give a full account of yourself without it. (2) Thick and particular—its obligations are tuned to the specific person and shared history, not fungible or derivable from some general duty like be courteous to clients. (3) Found, not just chosen—you're partly thrown into it, and its demands aren't reducible to whatever was agreed or contracted, even if it started with a contract. (4) Cultivated through ritual and propriety—there's a repertoire of small everyday forms, courtesies, and habits that express and deepen the relation. (5) Whole-person excellence—success is a matter of becoming good in a role-specific way, like being a caring son or a dependable friend, not just hitting performance indicators. (6) Normative priority—its demands can legitimately override or reshape your independent plans rather than weighing as one preference among others.

The case: I've been a paid home health aide for an elderly woman for eleven years. I was hired by her daughter through an agency, but at this point I know her late husband's stories by heart, I cook the dishes she grew up with, and I know what she means when she refers to people I have never met. The daughter lives abroad, and I'm the one at the hospital when there's a scare. I'm paid for weekday hours, but I show up on Sundays off the books because the small rituals of those visits matter to both of us. When my sister asked me to move across the country for a better job, I turned it down partly because I couldn't imagine being the person who leaves her. But when the daughter visits, I'm introduced as the caregiver, and the old woman will sometimes say, in front of others, that I do too much for someone who is paid—which stings in a way that tells me the relation has already become more than work. A colleague says it's just a job that's gotten very familiar; I experience it as a son's role, even though I'm not her son and I'm paid.

Using the six criteria, is this a role in the Confucian sense? Work criterion by criterion, say honestly where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether any of the six criteria needs amending or dropping in light of the case.
