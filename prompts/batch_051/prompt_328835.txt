A working group on astrobiology and anthropic reasoning that I belong to has asked each member to circulate a short statement of where they stand on the Great Filter, so the rest can pressure-test the set of commitments before a workshop. This is the statement I plan to send. I would like you to do the pressure-testing in advance: find where these commitments pull against one another, distinguish the real tensions from the merely apparent ones, and tell me the least costly repair. My commitments, stated as flatly as I can:

1. Abiogenesis is not the main bottleneck. The step from prebiotic chemistry to the first self-replicating or proto-metabolic system was probably quick and robust on the early Earth, and simple life may be common wherever there is liquid water, energy, and time.

2. The real Great Filter lies somewhere between simple life and technological civilization, most plausibly at the emergence of complex eukaryotic cells or at the later emergence of intelligence and cumulative technology.

3. I assign a low probability to the filter lying ahead of us. I do not think technologically capable species reliably extinguish or permanently stall themselves.

4. The observed silence, no detected technological civilizations or their artifacts, is genuine evidence that some strong filter operates, but I take it to be neutral between a behind-us filter and an ahead-of-us filter until it is combined with what our own existence implies.

5. I hold a Copernican or mediocrity principle: we should treat ourselves as typical, not special, within the relevant reference class unless the data force otherwise.

6. I also hold that anthropic reasoning in its conditional form is inescapable: we can only find ourselves in a history that contains observers, so our own existence is a selection effect to be conditioned on, not a datum that can surprise us.

7. If we found an independent origin of simple life elsewhere in the solar system, fossilized or extant, I would take it as strong evidence that the filter sits after simple life. And since we have already crossed the threshold to technological civilization, I would take it as strong evidence that the filter is behind us rather than ahead of us, substantially lowering my already-low estimate of a future filter.

I do not want a survey of the Great Filter literature or a neutral summary of the debate. I want an audit of this exact set of commitments. First, identify the pairs in real tension and rank them by how severe the conflict is. Second, for each real tension, say exactly what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Third, identify any pair that merely appears to be in tension and say which distinction dissolves it. Fourth, tell me which single revision would do the most to leave the whole set coherent.
