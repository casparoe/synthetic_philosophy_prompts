Construct a thought experiment in which an object comes out as art under Dickie's institutional definition but not under Levinson's historical definition. This is the direction people usually find harder to construct: Levinson's definition was built to be permissive enough to absorb readymades and other avant-garde work, so it is not obvious where it will refuse something the institutional theory admits. Work with Dickie's later institutional definition (an artifact of a kind created to be presented to an artworld public) and Levinson's historical definition (an object is art just in case its maker non-passingly intends it to be regarded in one or more of the ways in which earlier artworks are or were correctly regarded). Constraints: the object must be a physical artifact and the scenario psychologically realistic; and do not simply stipulate that the maker's intention fails Levinson's test — specify the actual mode of regard she intends, and argue that this mode is not among the ways earlier artworks are or were correctly regarded. After presenting the case, run both definitions over it explicitly, identify the structural feature that produces the divergence, say what would happen if that feature were removed, and explain how a defender of the historical definition would most plausibly try to bring the case back under the definition.
