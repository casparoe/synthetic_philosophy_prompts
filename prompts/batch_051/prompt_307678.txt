So I'm a retired physicist, and in the last few years I've been reading a lot of philosophy of mathematics, and one debate has started to drive me a little nuts. Benacerraf's dilemma is usually put as: if numbers are mind-independent abstract objects outside space and time and causally inert, then how could creatures like us ever come to know them, when knowledge normally requires some kind of causal or reliable access? Fair enough as a challenge. But then the debate just seems to idle. Platonists reply that not all knowledge is causal, or that we have some faculty or intuition that's reliable about the abstract. Nominalists and structuralists reply that this is just special pleading, and so the dilemma stands as a reason to give up platonism. Rinse and repeat for decades, and nobody seems to move. My suspicion is that this isnt really a disagreement about mathematics at all, but about what should count as an epistemological explanation: the two camps have different standards, and each side's response looks question-begging to the other. Is that diagnosis right? If not, what kind of disagreement is it--a hidden empirical question, a formal issue, a verbal dispute about 'knowledge', or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress?
