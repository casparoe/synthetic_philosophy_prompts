I read an online essay recently that tried to settle a debate about artificial intelligence by appealing to definitions, and I would like help deciding how much of it to trust before I write about the topic for school. The author's position can be summarized as follows. Honesty and dishonesty are matters of whether a speaker's statements match the speaker's beliefs, and deception requires intending to produce a false belief in an audience. Language models generate text without having beliefs or intentions, so a model's outputs cannot be honest or dishonest in a literal sense, and calling a model a liar is a category mistake born of anthropomorphism. The author also says that training a model through reinforcement learning does not change this, because reinforcement learning merely alters which text is produced in response to which input and does not install goals in the model; since the model never aims to make users believe anything, its outputs may be false or harmful but are never deceptive. The essay concludes that we would be more precise to abandon the vocabulary of AI deception, AI lying, and AI honesty altogether, and to speak only about the designers' and users' intentions when chatbots produce false or misleading information.

I am inclined to think this conclusion is too quick, because chatbots sometimes present false information as though it were true and people are actually misled; but I am not sure whether my objection confuses truthfulness with honesty or misses the author's point. Could you go through the summary claim by claim and tell me which parts a careful researcher on AI and truthfulness would accept, which parts they would find overstated or subtly confused, and whether the final conclusion follows from the accurate parts? In particular, are there defensible uses of the word deception, or of the idea that an AI system misleads people, that do not require the system to have beliefs or intentions?
