Near the end of book IV of The Gay Science, section 341, Nietzsche has a demon tell someone that this life, exactly as it has been lived, will be repeated endlessly, with no detail changed, and then asks how he would respond: as a curse or as an affirmation. I am trying to decide how much weight to give the cosmological reading of that passage—the claim that the universe literally recurs in exact cycles—and how much to give the practical reading, on which it is a test of whether one can affirm one's life. Give me your own credence for the proposition: the doctrine of eternal recurrence, understood as a literal claim about the universe, is true and carries the existential weight Nietzsche attaches to it. Decompose this into at least four sub-questions: (1) whether Nietzsche himself took the doctrine to be a cosmological thesis, an existential thought experiment, or somehow both; (2) whether exact recurrence is physically plausible given what we understand about entropy, expansion, and the possible number of microstates; (3) whether an exact repetition of my life without any memory of an earlier cycle would count as my life recurring, or whether the idea of recurrence silently presupposes a persisting observer; and (4) whether the demand to live as if each moment would recur forever can have normative force even if the cosmology is false. Assign a rough probability to each, show how you combine them into your final credence, and tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, say what specific evidence or argument would most change that sub-question.
