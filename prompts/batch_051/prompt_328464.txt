(3) But a recording, unlike a live performance, is itself repeatable: the file on my laptop and the CD in your car are two instances of the same recording. (4) So a recording turns out to be both a token of the work and a type with its own tokens. (5) But nothing can be both a token and a type. (6) Therefore the type/token picture of musical works, which seems right for live performances, breaks down as soon as recordings are in the picture. I keep suspecting the problem is either that token and type are roles rather than intrinsic kinds, so (5) is false, or that the word recording is sliding between a sound-event and a stored track across (2) and (3).
