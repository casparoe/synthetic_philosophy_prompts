I just had a long argument with a friend about the paradox of tragedy. She thinks it isnt really a paradox because we dont enjoy the suffering — we enjoy the craftsmanship, or the relief that it isnt us, or the expanded emotional range. I was defending the idea, from Humes essay Of Tragedy, that the pain of the represented passion gets converted into pleasure by the eloquence and skill of the artist, and that this second pleasure outweighs the first. By the end we were both just pointing at cases. So I want the full spec instead.

What would a theory of why we seek out sad/tragic artworks actually have to get right? Give me the list of requirements — the phenomena it has to explain and the distinctions it cant collapse — with the reasoning behind each. Include cases like: Oedipus Rex, a documentary about a real disaster, a sad song with no narrative, a horror film that is mostly dread, a tearjerker you rewatch precisely because it makes you cry, and someone who finds tragedy merely painful and avoids it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp. And tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude Hume-style conversion theories, because thats the hill I was dying on.
