I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the unilateralist's curse: when every member of a group can act on their own, the group often ends up doing what only the most optimistic member wanted, because that person just goes ahead. I want to use a concrete case to show how hard it is to draft a rule that actually stops this.

Here's the clause, from the charter of a large open-science AI consortium adopted in 2021:

"Section 9(c). No Member may, acting alone, release outside the Consortium any model, model weights, training code, or fine-tuned checkpoint produced using Consortium compute or data, except as authorized in advance by a two-thirds vote of the Board."

Now a Member has published, on her personal blog, a set of two hundred natural-language prompts plus a scoring rubric that together reliably get several widely available public models to produce step-by-step instructions for a dangerous chemical synthesis. She wrote the prompts on her own time and never released any model, weights, code, or checkpoint; she did use consortium GPUs for a few hours to score and rank the prompts, which she says is incidental and not what "produced" means.

Does the clause reach her? Give me the textual reading, the purpose reading, what the people who drafted it in 2021 would probably say if asked, and what a reasonable member in her position would understand. Then tell me what the case shows about whether you can draft your way out of a unilateralist's curse, or whether the curse just moves into the gaps in the words.
