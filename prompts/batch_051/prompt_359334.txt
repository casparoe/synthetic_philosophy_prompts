I've been reading about the problem of the criterion for a school philosophy project, and its got the same shape as the cold start problem in recommender systems, and I can't tell if that's a real parallel or just pattern matching. In the skeptical version, you can't justify a method for telling truth from falsity without either a criterion (whose justification needs a method) or a list of clear cases (which already presumes some method). In the recommendation version, you can't learn what to recommend without data from old recommendations, but you can't make old recommendations without knowing what people like. Both look like a bootstrap trap. Yet recommenders escape all the time with random starts, content-based guesses, and outside data. So is the parallel deep or superficial? And if deep, does the fact that recommenders escape mean the skeptic's circle isn't as airtight as it looks, or is the epistemic case different because there's no external measure of success to check against?
