But he also says it requires prior perception and that without phantasia there is no thinking. I want you to write an essay that takes a definite stand on what phantasia actually is for Aristotle—is it a kind of image store, a capacity for having appearances, a degraded or weak perception, or something else entirely? Weigh at least two rival interpretations against each other, don't just summarize the chapter, and defend a conclusion. The essay should directly confront the tension I just described: how can phantasia be neither perceiving nor thinking but still be necessary for thinking? Don't end on a fence-sitting note.
