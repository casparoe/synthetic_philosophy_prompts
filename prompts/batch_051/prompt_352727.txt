I am a surgeon at a public hospital, and over the last year I have kept running into a puzzle in my own moral thinking that I later learned has a name: the paradox of deontology. I find myself committed to constraints of a strongly agent-relative kind. It may not be me who kills one patient even when doing so would save five others, just as I may not falsify a ward record even if doing so would stop several colleagues from falsifying records more often. Yet the very rule that forbids me from harming one person for the sake of preventing several harms seems to prohibit an act that would leave the world with fewer violations of that same rule. If killing is terrible, why should I honor a constraint that would leave more killings in the world, committed by other hands? I can see several candidate knots inside this puzzle. One is whether the constraint is really a rule about my own conduct rather than a policy for minimizing the bad things that conduct involves. Another is whether the constraint is grounded in what I owe the particular person in front of me, so that counting up violations across different agents misunderstands it from the start. Another is whether there is some threshold of disproportion at which the numbers become too lopsided for the constraint to retain its force. And another is whether the paradox is not deontology-specific at all, but simply what it feels like to reason with any agent-relative consideration.

I have about six months and one evening a week to think, not to read widely, and I want to spend that time on the questions that are genuinely load-bearing for how I act when I am called to make triage or ward decisions. Which two or three of these knots, or others I have not listed, would you have me think through first, and in what order? For each, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone who can think carefully but is not a professional philosopher, and what would concretely change in my decisions depending on the answer. I am asking for a defended ordering, not a survey of the literature. Please reply in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a letter.
