In The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, Max Weber writes:

“The real, moral objection is to relaxation in the security of possession, the enjoyment of wealth with the consequence of idleness and the temptations of the flesh, above all of distraction from the pursuit of a righteous life. In fact, it is only because possession involves this danger of relaxation that it is objectionable at all. For the saints’ everlasting rest is in the next world; on earth man must, to be certain of his state of grace, ‘do the works of Him who sent him, as long as it is yet day.’ Not leisure and enjoyment, but only activity serves to increase the glory of God, according to the definite manifestations of His will.”

I want to set aside the broad thesis usually attached to this book—that ascetic Protestantism caused the rise of modern capitalism—and concentrate on the narrower, local causal claim expressed in this passage. Weber appears to be arguing that the decisive moral teaching was not an objection to wealth itself but an objection to what wealth makes possible: idleness, enjoyment, and a relaxed sense of security in one’s possessions. The behavioral consequence, on his account, is a redirection of the believer’s energies into continuous, methodical labor in a worldly calling as the only conduct that glorifies God. That is a psychological and behavioral claim about how a system of values altered everyday economic conduct.

I want an account of how one could bring evidence to bear on that specific claim, given that it concerns early-modern populations. Design the strongest feasible test or quasi-test you can: what comparison group or natural variation would you exploit, what observations or measurements would you collect, and what pattern of results would favor the mechanism just described rather than the rival interpretation that the ethic was merely a post hoc rationalization for conduct people would have engaged in anyway? Then be precise about what a positive result would still not settle—in particular, what it would leave open about whether the values were a genuine cause or only a marker of something else, and whether the word “cause” is being used here in a sense that any observational test could capture at all.
