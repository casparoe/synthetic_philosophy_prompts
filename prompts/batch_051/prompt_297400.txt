When I said that just collapses into 'survivors survive' unless fitness is defined seperately, I got told I was being pedantic. I want to get this straight, not score points. Take 'survival of the fittest' and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making. For each reading, say whether it's a tautology, a stipulative definition, or a substantive empirical claim, and what would have to be true for it to do genuine explanatory work rather than bookkeeping. In particular, work through whether the propensity interpretation of fitness — fitness as expected or probabilistic reproductive success, not actual survival — dissolves the tautology objection, or just moves the problem into how you measure the propensities without using actual survival as a crutch. And tell me which reading, if any, the moth explanation actually needed. I don't need the history of the phrase. American English please.
