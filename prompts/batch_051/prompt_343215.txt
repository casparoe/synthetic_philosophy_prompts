Im prepping to interview a philosopher for my small podcast. Their recent book defends a version of G.E.M. Anscombe's argument in *Mr Truman's Degree* — the pamphlet she wrote against Oxford giving Truman an honorary degree after he'd ordered the bomb. The book's core claim is that the atomic bombings weren't just horrible consequences of war but murder, because the US chose to kill civilians as a means to get Japan to surrender, and Anscombe's line in the pamphlet is that choosing to kill the innocent as a means to your ends is always murder. I dont want to just ask them to restate that. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the hard parts and force them beyond the slogan "murder is murder." At least one question should be on the double-effect reply — the reply that the civilian deaths weren't intended as a means but were a foreseen side-effect of bombing military targets. Include a follow-up keyed to the answer they're most likely to give. At least one should be on the combatant/noncombatant line, especially the second bomb after the first had already destroyed a city. And at least one should be on the counterfactual about invasion casualties: how does their position handle the fact that Truman and his advisors believed, however imperfectly, that the bombs would end the war and save lives? For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates the standard response and pushes past it.
