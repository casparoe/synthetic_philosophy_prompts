In metaphysics courses I've heard composition reduced to three tests: the parts must touch; the parts must be fastened so that the whole moves as one when any part is moved; and the parts must act as one, so that the whole's behavior is not just the sum of what each part does on its own. Now take a bait ball: thousands of sardines packed so densely that most are touching, none fastened, shifting as a single rotating formation to confuse predators, while fish at the edge continuously leave and rejoin. Each sardine is a separate animal with its own senses and behavior. Is the bait ball one material object in the way a single sardine is, or the way a brick wall is, or is it merely a collection? Run the three tests one at a time and say what each implies. Where the tests conflict, or where it becomes unclear which fish are part of the ball, say whether the tests need revision or the question itself needs to be recast.
