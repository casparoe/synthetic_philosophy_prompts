I read a magazine essay the other day on whether there's a duty to be legible to other people, and I dont know how much of the version of the view it attacks I should trust. The author summarized the position she was criticizing as: there is a moral duty to be transparent, because sincerity really just is transparency — telling people what you believe and making your actual reasons available to them. On this picture, whenever you keep your reasoning to yourself, or talk in ways others can't easily parse, you are shading toward dishonesty. She then says the ideal is obviously self-defeating, since a true duty of transparency would force everyone to broadcast every stray thought and private motive, which nobody actually wants. Is this a fair account of the duty-of-legibility position? I'd like a sentence-level audit: which bits would a defender accept, which would they quietly correct, and does the author's it-collapses-on-itself conclusion survive once the view is stated more carefully?
