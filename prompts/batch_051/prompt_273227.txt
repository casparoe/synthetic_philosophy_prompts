In my school's philosophy club we have been reading about Robert Brandom's inferentialism: the view that the meaning of an expression is its inferential role — what a speaker is committed to in using it, what follows from it, and what it is incompatible with — rather than primarily what it denotes. A development seems to me to put pressure on this position. Large language models produce fluent, apparently meaningful text by learning statistical patterns over human language and predicting likely next tokens, without tracking or undertaking inferential commitments, entitlements, or incompatibilities in Brandom's sense. Does Brandom's argument survive this development? Does it now imply something surprising about whether such models mean anything at all? Or does the development undermine one of the argument's premises — and if so, can the view be repaired? I am asking about the philosophical argument, not AI policy.
