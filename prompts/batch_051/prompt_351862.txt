I've spent enough years reading rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums that I've seen the dust-specks-versus-torture debate resurface more times than I can count, and I've come to think the real disagreements aren't between people who call themselves classical utilitarians and people who call themselves negative utilitarians. They're between people who give the same answers to two or three nested questions about trade-offs, thresholds, and aggregation, and only afterward discover they've been assigned different labels — or no label at all. I'd rather sort people by what they'd actually choose, not by which ism they like.

I'm putting together a 'map of the territory' handout for a reading group on suffering-focused ethics, and I'd like you to write it as a taxonomy of positions on the commensurability of pleasure and pain, in the form of a decision tree, with lexical threshold negative utilitarianism as one of the leaves. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the branch questions, then the combinations of answers they allow, then the named positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations noted and briefly diagnosed as incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. The branch questions should be concrete cases or claims a person can answer without already knowing which label the answer leads to. The leaves should include at least classical Benthamite utilitarianism, straightforward negative utilitarianism, lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, prioritarianism, and whatever neighboring positions the space makes natural; each leaf should carry a name (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Angle and constraints: the tree should be usable by a smart reader with no formal philosophy background but a taste for thought experiments, and it should run to something like six to ten leaves. Make sure it can route someone through the dust-specks-versus-torture trade-off, and through the question of whether the lexical threshold attaches to an individual experience or to an aggregate of mild pains. Where two positions genuinely cannot be separated by any question in the tree — for example, two formulations of what 'lexical' amounts to that make all the same choices — flag that explicitly rather than papering over it.
