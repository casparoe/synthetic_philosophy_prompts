I've been trying to sort out which things in the world genuinely represent or mean something, as opposed to just tracking stuff or setting off a reflex. I have gut calls on a bunch of cases and I want to know if there is one principle behind them.

A frog snaps at small dark moving things. I think whatever fires in the frog means fly (or food), even though it also snaps at a BB. When it snaps at a BB, that's a mistake about a fly, not evidence that it meant fly-or-BB the whole time.

A honeybee does a waggle dance and other bees use it to find the nectar. I think the dance means nectar is that far away in that direction, even in the case where you move the dish after the returning bee last saw it and the dance is now false.

A car's gas gauge gets stuck on full. I think it still says the tank is full, so it's misrepresenting, not changing its meaning to full-or-empty.

Some bacteria have little magnetic crystals inside them that line up with the earth's field, and the bacteria swim along the field lines, which carries them down toward low-oxygen water. I don't think the crystals or the swimming response represent north, or down, or low-oxygen, or anything. It's just a very reliable reflex.

An antibody latches onto one particular shape on the surface of a flu virus. I don't think the antibody means flu virus, for all its specificity. It's a lock and key.

A guy I know trained a parrot to squawk polly wants a cracker whenever it's hungry. I keep going back and forth on this one. Sometimes it seems like the squawk means the parrot wants a cracker, or at least means give me a cracker. Other times it seems like just a noisier version of the bacteria.

The pair that bothers me is the frog and the thermostat. The bimetallic strip in a thermostat bends as the temperature changes and closes a circuit that turns the furnace on. I want to say the strip doesn't represent the temperature, it just responds to it. But I can't see why I don't say the same thing about the frog, which is also a mechanism built to respond to a sign. Is there a difference that matters, or do I have to give up one of those calls?

Is their a single principle that gets most of these, what does it say about the thermostat, the parrot, and about a robot vacuum's cliff sensor that stops it from tumbling down the stairs, and which of my gut calls does it force me to drop?
