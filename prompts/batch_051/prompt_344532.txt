so in my philosophy of science class we're doing these adversarial collaboration assignments, and the pair i got assigned is a Popperian falsificationist and a Lakatosian (the research programmes person). i'm supposed to write the actual joint statement they could both sign, not just a compare/contrast. the format the teacher wants is: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, (2) a table of the remaining disagreements where each side's view is worded in a way that side would actually accept, no strawmen, (3) a list of cruxes, meaning sub-questions where settling them would actually move one side, and if possible (4) a concrete historical episode both sides can agree to use as a test, with each side writing down in advance what their view predicts.

the Popperian in the readings keeps saying that a real theory sticks its neck out, and if one risky prediction fails the theory is falsified and you have to abandon it. the Lakatosian keeps saying no single experiment ever kills a programme, because the hard core is protected by a belt of auxiliary assumptions, and programmes only get abandoned when a rival programme explains more and is more progressive. i know the point of the assignment is to get past those talking-past-each-other slogans and find wording that is neither vacuous nor slanted.

also the teacher wants us to seperate disagreements that are genuine from ones that are just about what counts as a "falsification" or a "programme", and to say which of their differences would actually be changed by evidence and which are more like methodological preferences. could you draft it so both sides would definately put their names to it? try to be specific about what makes a theory adjustment legitimate rather than ad hoc, and about whether a programme can be called degenerating in advance or only in hindsight, because those are the two places where i keep seeing them talk past each other.
