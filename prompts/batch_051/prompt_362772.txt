I keep noticing a specific move in disputes about justification, and I do not have a word for it. Here are two examples. In a discussion of whether a dating method is reliable, one person asks why the reading should be trusted, and the answer is that it was calibrated against a standard; when asked why the standard should be trusted, the answer is that it was checked against another standard; and then, rather than offering a third-order claim, the person says that at some point measurement is just a shared practice, not the kind of thing that stands in need of propositional justification. In an argument about a disputed inference, someone asks why a step in a proof is valid, and the answer is that to call that rule into question is already to rely on it, so the challenge is unanswerable but also not a legitimate challenge. In both cases, the regress is closed by switching from beliefs or propositions to something else — a rule, a practice, a precondition of inquiry — rather than by producing a self-evident or incorrigible belief. I know foundationalism stops the chain in a belief that somehow needs no further support; this move stops the chain outside beliefs altogether. Does it have an established name, and should it be filed under one of the standard responses to the regress problem (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism) or treated as something distinct? I am less interested in the label than in the carving: when is such a category shift a legitimate end of the regress, and when is it merely changing the subject, and what principle separates the two?
