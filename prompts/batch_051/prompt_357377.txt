I recently completed a risk-tolerance questionnaire while reallocating my retirement account. It presented two separate decisions. In the first, I chose a guaranteed $100,000 over an 89% chance of $100,000, a 10% chance of $500,000, and a 1% chance of nothing. In the second, I chose a 10% chance of $500,000 over an 11% chance of $100,000; in each option of that second decision, the remaining probability was simply that I would receive nothing. A colleague with some training in economics tells me these two preferences cannot coherently coexist, because the second pair of options is just the first pair with a common 89% chance of $100,000 replaced by a common 89% chance of nothing. I can follow that arithmetic, but the two decisions do not feel alike to me: the first felt like a choice between certainty and a small risk of ruin, while the second felt like choosing between two comparable long shots. Is my colleague right that I am being inconsistent, and what, if anything, should that imply about how I ought to make decisions under risk?
