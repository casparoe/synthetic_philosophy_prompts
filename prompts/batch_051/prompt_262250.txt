I'm trying to reconstruct the argument W. E. B. Du Bois makes in Criteria of Negro Art as a formal argument, and I want to know if it works. Here's my version, from memory, so I might be flattening him: (1) All art is propaganda, in the sense that any artwork takes a side, whether or not the artist admits it. (2) The situation of Black Americans is one of active struggle for survival and recognition. (3) Given (1), Black art is already taking a side in that struggle; the only real choice is whether it does so deliberately or by default. (4) Making that side explicit doesn't destroy beauty or truth; it gives them a job to do and can make them more serious. (5) Therefore the Black artist ought to make art that explicitly serves the cause of Black freedom and dignity, and the artist who claims to be apolitical is just serving another side without saying so. I think the weak point is the jump from "all art is propaganda" to "you ought to make your art propaganda for my side," but I can't tell if that's a gap in Du Bois or just in my reconstruction. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails and why? And is he using "propaganda" in two different senses—first a descriptive one, then a normative one? Please say how confident you are in your reading of Du Bois here, and single out the point your least sure about.
