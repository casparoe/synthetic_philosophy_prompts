I am an undergraduate in a course on Kierkegaard’s pseudonymous writings, and next week I lead a fifty-minute section for first-year students on the idea of the teleological suspension of the ethical in Fear and Trembling. The students will have read the opening Attunement and Problema I in translation, but no secondary literature, and I do not want them to leave thinking the book is either a defense of fanaticism or a vague celebration of “faith.” Do not explain the concept to me; plan how I should explain it to them. I need the sequence of ideas and what depends on what: in particular, how much has to be in place about the ethical as the universal and about the difference between the tragic hero and the knight of faith before “suspension of the ethical” can mean anything other than “a permitted exception.” Then give me the one carrying example, or the one contrasting pair of examples, that should anchor the entire session, and say why it serves this audience better than the alternatives they are likely to bring in on their own. I also want a misconception map: the predictable readings they will form—that “teleological” means the end justifies the means, that Abraham is being offered as a model to imitate, that the religious stage is just the ethical stage with stronger obligations, that faith is a private feeling of certainty, or that Johannes de Silentio is simply Kierkegaard endorsing all of this—and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off rather than waiting until the end. Tell me what should be left deliberately unresolved at this level and how to say that explicitly without making the concept sound like mysticism. Finally, propose one question or small exercise for the last five minutes that would show whether they have understood the structural distinction rather than merely learned the phrase.
