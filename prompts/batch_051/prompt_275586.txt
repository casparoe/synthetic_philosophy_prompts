Act as a professional philosopher working in social philosophy and value theory, with a focus on membership, exit, and the goods of community. I am preparing a short position statement for a workshop on "Community and belonging as goods: what is lost when communities dissolve?" and I want a pre-circulation consistency audit.

Here are the six commitments I currently endorse. I am genuinely unsure where the cracks are, so do not assume my ordering or my own sense of which claims are central is correct. Audit the set itself, not the way I present it.

(1) Belonging to a community is a non-instrumental, final good. A human life with no stable community membership is missing something valuable in itself, even if the person's material welfare, health, and felt happiness were unchanged.

(2) This good is relational, not merely experiential. It requires actually being recognized, trusted, and answerable to particular others with whom one shares a history and some continuing practices. A mere feeling of belonging toward a group that does not in fact recognize one as a member is not the same good.

(3) Some of the most formative forms of belonging are unchosen at the start: family, place, first language, and a religious or class community one was raised in. These unchosen attachments can generate genuine special obligations even when the person has performed no voluntary act of joining.

(4) Exit is a real permission. A person may permissibly leave the community that formed them, or quietly let their involvement lapse, for the sake of work, love, or escape from a stifling community, even when this predictably hastens the community's dissolution. It would be wrong to require someone to sacrifice major life plans to keep a community alive.

(5) When a community dissolves through the uncoordinated free choices of its members, the dissolution itself is not a distinct loss. Once the last member has chosen to leave, no further value remains lost and no one is wronged by the mere disappearance of the community; the only losses are the members' individual welfare losses, such as loneliness or lost networks.

(6) Public institutions should not try to preserve particular communities or ways of life. The state should be neutral among communities, protecting the rights of association and exit but not subsidizing or otherwise promoting any community's survival.

Please audit this set as follows. First, work through the fifteen pairs of commitments and judge, for each, whether it is compatible, compatible only with qualification, or in genuine tension; you may summarize rather than list all fifteen, but I want to see that the pairwise reasoning drove your conclusions. Second, rank the genuine tensions by severity, say exactly what each tension is a tension between, and state what would have to give to resolve it. Third, identify the single most important revision that would make the package most coherent while preserving as much of it as possible. Do not give a general lecture on communitarianism or liberalism; work only from what the six commitments themselves assert or commit me to.

Think carefully before you answer, and at the very end give your bottom line in the following fixed form:

\answer{<short phrase naming the single most important revision>}
