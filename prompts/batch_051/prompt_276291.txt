I sit on my university's research integrity committee. We have disciplinary authority over staff in research units. Our code of conduct says staff owe 'loyalty and discretion' to the university and should not disclose internal matters to outsiders without authorisation. A junior data manager in a psychology lab discovered that a senior professor had been systematically dropping participants who didn't fit the hypothesis and, when challenged, the professor told her to keep quiet. She raised it first with the lab PI and then with the head of department, but nothing happened for months. She then sent the raw data and an anonymous note to a journal that had published one of the lab's papers. The journal is now investigating, and the professor has learned who sent the data (the journal accidentally revealed the source). The professor is demanding the university discipline her for gross disloyalty and breech of confidentiality. Our committee must decide whether to recommend disciplinary action, and if so what sanction, or to protect her as a whistleblower. Some members think we should formally reprimand her because she bypassed internal channels and damaged collegial trust; others think she should be commended because her first loyalty was to the scientific community and the integrity of the published record. I have to draft the committee's decision memo before next Wednesday. I'm genuinely torn, not least because I once used a similar internal channel myself and got burned. Please draft the memo: it should recommend a concrete course of action with reasons, and it should engage seriously with the view that loyalty to the institution and to colleagues is a moral requirement that she violated, not just a contractual nicety. The memo should be in British English.
