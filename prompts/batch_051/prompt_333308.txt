I have been trying to pin down what a refugee is, and the edge cases keep slipping out from under the familiar descriptions. Give me a working definition of "refugee" — one I could actually apply to a new case. To fix the two endpoints: a family that flees relentless shelling of its city and crosses into a neighboring country clearly counts, and a software engineer who emigrates from a safe, functioning country for a much higher salary clearly does not. The cases I cannot settle are these:

1. A farmer leaves his country because years of drought have made his land unable to feed his family.
2. A teenager flees a neighborhood ruled by a gang, in a country whose police have effectively stopped entering it.
3. A young woman goes abroad to escape a forced marriage and violence from relatives, which the local police treat as a private family matter.
4. A man leaves to avoid conscription into a war he considers unjust.
5. A family leaves a country in the middle of an economic collapse: nobody is persecuting them, but food and medicine are gone.
6. A family flees armed violence to a safer province but remains inside the same country and never crosses a border.

The last case feels as though it should not count, but I cannot see what the principled difference is. Give me the definition, run it through all eight cases, and say where it yields a verdict that surprises you or yields no verdict at all; then tell me whether you would revise the definition or stand by that verdict. Finally, in a sentence or two, indicate whether deciding who counts as a refugee settles the question of who must take them in, or whether those are two separate questions.
