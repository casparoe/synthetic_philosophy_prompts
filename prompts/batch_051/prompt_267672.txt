In our rationalism and empiricism unit we did Leibniz's veined marble image from the New Essays, where the mind isn't a blank tablet but a block of marble whose internal veins already make Hercules easier to carve than other shapes. I wanted to try a variant. Suppose the veins don't bias the block toward any particular statue: they form a fixed finite network of possible fracture lines, all equally available, and the actual statue is fully determined by where the sculptor lands the first blow. So the final shape was always one of the internally possible shapes before experience, but none of the shapes is favored by the stone's initial structure. Does Leibniz still have a rationalist argument here, or does this collapse into empiricism with some architectural constraints? I'm trying to figure out wether innate knowledge requires a bias toward specific contents, or whether a preexisting space of possible contents is enough. Work through the variant and say how much of the original veined-marble point survives, and where exactly the rationalist/empiricist disagreement moves.
