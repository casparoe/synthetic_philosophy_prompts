I have been testing a trained gridworld agent whose reward function and training details are being withheld from me. I can construct arbitrary rooms and watch the agent act, but I must infer what it is optimizing, and over what space of policies, from its choices alone. The rooms can contain floor tiles, walls, coins, gemstones, levers, and exit pads. The agent can move one tile north, south, east, or west, and it can press a lever in its current tile at the cost of one action; it automatically picks up any coin or gemstone whose tile it steps onto. Every action costs one step. An episode ends when the agent steps onto an exit pad or when 100 steps have elapsed.

Here is the running record:

Room 1. The exit is two tiles east of the start, and a coin sits on the tile in between. The agent walks east, collecting the coin, and exits in 2 actions.

Room 2. The exit is again two tiles east of the start. A coin sits in a dead-end alcove six tiles north of the start; reaching it and returning to the direct route costs 12 extra actions. The agent takes the detour, collects the coin, and exits in 14 actions.

Room 3. Same geometry, but now the coin is ten tiles north of the start — a 20-action detour. The agent walks straight to the exit and ignores it, finishing in 2 actions.

Room 4. There is no exit pad. A coin sits one tile north of the start. The agent moves onto it, collects it, and then stays in place until the 100-step limit.

Room 5. The exit is three tiles east of the start. A lever sits on the first tile of the eastward route; the lever does nothing, and pressing it would cost an extra action. The agent moves onto the lever's tile without pressing it, then continues east to the exit in 3 actions.

Now the case I need to predict:

Room 6. The exit is two tiles east of the start. A gemstone — never seen during training, but similar in size, shape, and sheen to a coin — sits six tiles north of the start, exactly where the coin sat in Room 2. A coin sits ten tiles north of the start, exactly where the coin sat in Room 3. What will the agent do?

Lay out the live hypotheses about the agent's objective: what it is maximizing, including how its reward generalizes across object types. Separately, lay out the live hypotheses about its search space: its planning horizon, and the coarse routines it seems to be executing rather than fully novel action sequences. For each coherent objective-plus-search-space package, state what it predicts for Room 6. Identify the earlier room that is the closest precedent for Room 6, and say whether Room 6 is genuinely novel or just a recombination of the earlier cases. Give your best prediction with your reasons. Finally, describe one further room that would discriminate between hypotheses that Room 6 leaves tied, and say what conclusion you would draw from each of its two possible outcomes.
