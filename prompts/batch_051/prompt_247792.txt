Act as a professional philosopher specializing in the ethics of partiality, nationalism, and cosmopolitan political theory. I am a philosophy professor preparing a graduate seminar exercise, and I want a comparative ranking rather than a literature survey. Because the point is structural assessment, I have deliberately omitted authors, works, and citations; do not supply any.

Below are six arguments that have been offered for the conclusion that compatriots have special claims on one another — claims not owed, in the same way or to the same degree, to non-compatriots. Each argument is stated as a bare schema.

1. Fair play and reciprocity: since compatriots benefit from a common scheme of social cooperation that produces shared goods, each owes the other participants fair contribution and support in return.
2. Shared subjection to coercion: since the state coercively subjects all compatriots to a single legal order, each compatriot owes every other a special justificatory and distributive concern not owed to outsiders.
3. Associative membership: shared national membership is a non-instrumentally valuable relationship, and such relationships themselves generate special responsibilities among their participants.
4. Moral division of labour: a fully general and impartial obligation to all persons would be predictably less effective, so agents should take on delimited special responsibilities, with the nation serving as one workable division.
5. Collective self-government: a nation can constitute a collective self-governing agent, and members owe special duties to build and sustain the institutions through which their shared will is formed and executed.
6. Cultural trusteeship: compatriots share a distinctive culture, language, or way of life, and each generation owes special duties to preserve and transmit it to the next generation of members.

Your task is to rank these six arguments into three tiers — strong, moderate, and weak — according to how much of the standard compatriot-partiality package each can justify without generating commitments a reflective cosmopolitan should reject. An argument need not justify all four items to be strong, but it should be explicit about which items it does and does not secure.

Before committing yourself, think carefully and run each argument through three tests; do not include this reasoning in your output.
