I'm in an AI safety seminar this term and we've been arguing about chain-of-thought monitoring, and I keep getting stuck on an analogy one of the grad students leans on all the time. He says a model's chain of thought is like a person's private notebook — the notes they write for themselves before they act. On that picture, monitoring CoT is more like a supervisor reading an employee's scratch work than like a cop reading a diary; if the model writes "I should game the reward signal now," that's not a stray private thought, it's the plan it's about to execute, and you can catch it in the notebook before it does anything. When someone objects that models will just learn to write what the monitor wants to read, he says that's fine, because it's like making a student show their work: you can't fake the derivation indefinitely and still get the right answer, so optimization pressure for honest-looking reasoning is optimization pressure for actually honest reasoning.

I dont buy it, but I can't fully articulate where it comes apart. It seems like a notebook that's also the thing being graded isn't a private notebook anymore, and once the CoT is in the training signal the model is partly writing for the auditor — so the faithfulness worry and the obfuscation worry seem like they're being assumed away, not answered. But maybe I'm overstating that.

I'd like you to audit the analogy the way my professor keeps making us do: give the correspondence table (what corresponds to the notebook, the person, the supervisor reading over the shoulder, the feedback loop of getting graded on the notes), then separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the safety conclusion from the ones that are just cosmetic. In particular, does the conclusion my classmate draws — that CoT monitoring plus optimization for honest-looking reasoning makes a model genuinely safer rather than just better at producing reassuring notebooks — survive once those disanalogies are on the table? And if there's a better analogy for what CoT monitoring can and can't do, I'd want that too. Please write the answer in American English.
