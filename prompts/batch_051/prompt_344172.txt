I've been trying to settle what I think about fakes, copies, and why originals matter, and I'd like an audit of whether the following set of commitments actually hangs together. I'm a software engineer and haven't studied philosophy formally, so I'll lean on cases that are familiar from engineering.

Here is where I've landed:

1. In the digital domain, the work just is the information. Two files that are byte-for-byte identical are equally good instances of the work; neither is closer to the real thing. Preferring the file on the artist's own laptop to an identical copy on my machine is sentiment, not a judgment about the work itself.

2. A signed certificate of authenticity attached to one copy of a digital image doesn't make that copy a better instance of the image than an unsigned file with the same bytes. The certificate itself might be worth something as a collectible, but it doesn't make the file a better instance of the image.

3. A physical painting that curators long treated as an autograph original loses most of its value the moment it is exposed as a forgery, even though the canvas and the pigments haven't changed at that moment. This suggests that part of the value of such works is constituted by their actual history, in particular who really made them and under what circumstances, and that this can matter even when the physical results are indistinguishable.

4. But I don't think an object's value should hinge on historical facts that can't be settled by any possible evidence, even in principle. If two competing stories about a work's origin are both consistent with everything we could ever know, the unanswerable question of which story is true shouldn't be doing much work in our valuation.

5. A painting that has had, say, 70 percent of its painted surface gradually replaced over three centuries of conservation can still be the same original and keep its status, provided the replacements were made to preserve the work and there is an unbroken chain of custody. It hasn't become a replica just because most of the paint is no longer the original painter's.

I'd like to know whether these five commitments form a consistent package. If they don't, please identify the genuine tensions in order of severity and say what would have to give in each case. Please also flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection, and give your view on the cheapest way to restore consistency and what that fix would cost.
