In the *Critique of Pure Reason*, the First Antinomy's thesis argument goes roughly like this: if the world had no beginning in time, then before any given moment an infinite series of successive states would have to have elapsed; but an infinite series can never be completed through successive synthesis; therefore the world must have a beginning. I keep feeling like this exact move, treating an infinite past as a series that would have to be *completed by traversal*, is quietly smuggling in a finite starting point, and that Kant's resolution ends up deflecting the antithesis rather than answering it. Please make an argument for the view that the antithesis argument in the First Antinomy survives Kant's resolution better than the thesis does, with particular attention to whether the idea of an elapsed infinite is doing illicit work. Use American English.
