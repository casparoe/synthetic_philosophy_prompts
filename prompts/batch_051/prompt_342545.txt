In a seminar I was part of, we constructed an argument that now feels like a reductio of something, though I cannot tell of what. Each step looked plausible as we wrote it, but the conclusion is one I find unacceptable. I want to know exactly where the argument goes wrong, or, if it does not, which premise I should abandon and what abandoning it costs.

The argument runs as follows:

(1) Consequentialism holds that an act is right if and only if its total consequences, over the whole of the future, are at least as good as those of any alternative open to the agent.

(2) The total consequences of any act — donating money, taking a job, having a child, and equally committing a murder — depend on causal chains that ramify indefinitely far into the future.

(3) For any such act, those far-future total consequences are unknowable by us: we cannot trace the chains, and we cannot even assign well-grounded probabilities to them.

(4) Therefore, for any act, we cannot know whether its total consequences are better or worse than those of the alternatives.

(5) Therefore, by (1), we cannot know whether any act is right or wrong.

(6) Blame, punishment, and guilt are warranted only when we can know, or at least be justified in believing, that the act in question was wrong.

(7) Therefore blame, punishment, and guilt are never warranted for any act, including murder: for all we know, a murder's total consequences are better than those of any alternative, because the far future is opaque to us.

I cannot accept (7). But I find myself wanting to keep (3), because the cluelessness premise seems like a sober description of our epistemic situation, and also wanting to keep (6), because I do not want a view on which we may blame people for acts we have no reason at all to think were wrong. That pushes me toward weakening or abandoning (1). Before I commit to that, I want to know whether the argument really is valid as it stands, or whether some step fails to follow; and, either way, what each way of blocking the conclusion would force me to give up.
