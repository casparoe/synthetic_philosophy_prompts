I serve on the board of a mid-sized AI research company, and a proposal now before us has split the board down the middle. The proposal is what its supporters call a pre-AGI bargain: it would amend our charter now so that, if the company ever develops a general-purpose system capable of performing a broad range of economically valuable cognitive work at or above expert human level, the company will be bound in advance to two commitments. First, a fixed share of the revenue attributable to such a system would flow into an independently governed fund, whose trustees are instructed to hold the assets until a later multilateral agreement specifies how they should be distributed to those displaced by automation. Second, those trustees would hold a veto over deploying the system above a stated capability threshold. Supporters argue that this is the only point at which we can safely bind ourselves: once the capability exists, competitive and financial pressure will make restraint impossible, so the present board should act as an Odysseus binding himself to the mast. Opponents reply that this is a contract with counterparties who do not exist yet, that no present board has the moral standing to tie a future board that will know more than we do, and that the fund's trustees would wield real power without electoral or contractual accountability. They add that if the clause is reversible later, it is theater, and if it is not reversible, it is illegitimate. Draft the board's decision memo. It should recommend adoption, rejection, or conditional adoption of the proposed amendment, give the reasoning the full board can act on, and state fairly the strongest consideration on the losing side.
