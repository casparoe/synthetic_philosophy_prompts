Consider the following argument that no finite bit-string can be random in the algorithmic sense. Each step looks plausible to me, but I half-suspect a trick and cannot locate it.

(1) A finite bit-string is random just in case it is incompressible: no program shorter than the string itself outputs exactly that string.
(2) Let n be large, and let S be the lexicographically first incompressible string of length n. Such an S exists, because there are 2^n bit-strings of length n but fewer than 2^n programs shorter than n.
(3) The phrase "the lexicographically first incompressible string of length n" picks out S, and for large n this phrase is far shorter than n.
(4) So S has a description shorter than itself, contradicting its incompressibility.
(5) Therefore the notion of an incompressible — and hence random — finite bit-string is incoherent.

Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and why? If the problem is an equivocation, state the two senses involved.
