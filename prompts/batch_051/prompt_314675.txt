I want a working definition of "cause" for use in social explanation — one that tells me when it is legitimate to say that a culture, a shared value, or a widely held belief caused a social outcome. Give the definition (or, if the concept splits, give two definitions and say how they relate), run it through the cases below, say where it delivers the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. I am less interested in a taxonomy of existing views than in a criterion I can actually apply.

Cases:

1. A heatwave causes a crop failure.
2. A rise in the price of bread causes bread consumption to fall.
3. Depositors' belief that the bank will fail causes the bank run that makes it fail.
4. A community's norm against conspicuous consumption causes its luxury imports to be low.
5. The rule that a bishop moves only diagonally causes bishops never to change color squares in a game of chess.
6. The belief that disciplined work is a sign of election causes Calvinist merchants to keep scrupulous account books — the stylized claim in Weber's Protestant-ethic thesis.
7. A society's use of money causes supply and demand to be coordinated through prices.
8. The noon arrival of the organizers' train causes the revolution to begin at noon rather than in the evening.

My own rough view: 1, 2, and 8 are clearly causal, though maybe not all in the same sense; 5 is not a cause but a constitutive rule, and I want the definition to explain why; 3, 4, 6, and 7 are the hard cases, because in each an idea, norm, or institution appears to be doing genuine explanatory work but the explanatory relation may not be the same as in 1 and 2. In particular, I want the definition to let me say whether "the Protestant ethic caused capitalism" is causal in the same sense as "the heatwave caused the crop failure," or is a different kind of explanatory claim — and if different, what exactly still makes it an explanation.

Finally, say how confident you are in the definition you settle on, and single out the case-verdict or point you are least sure of.
