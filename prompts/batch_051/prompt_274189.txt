I serve on the safety review board of a large hospital system. In the past six months, our cardiology unit has recorded four cases of a rare surgical complication whose baseline rate would lead us to expect about one case every two years. Some board members read this cluster as strong evidence of a systemic problem and want to launch a formal investigation immediately. Others counter that, because the system tracks several hundred units and dozens of rare adverse-event categories, some cluster of roughly this size is nearly guaranteed to appear somewhere once the logic behind the birthday paradox is applied, and that treating every such coincidence as a signal would misdirect scarce safety resources. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should reach a definite recommendation the board can act on, specify a time frame, and give the skeptical side's epistemic argument real weight rather than resting on the intuition that four cases cannot be chance.
