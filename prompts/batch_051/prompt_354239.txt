In seminar arguments about distributive justice, I've adopted a rule: when someone objects to a utilitarian proposal by appealing to the separateness of persons, I refuse to engage until they can say what the calculation gets wrong in the particular case — whether it is the distribution of benefits and burdens, the disregard for consent, or the assumption that one person's loss is offset by another's gain. Until they name one of these, I treat the objection as a rhetorical move rather than an argument. Is this a defensible way to police the debate, or am I protecting utilitarianism from a challenge that cannot be stated in its own terms? How would I tell the difference, and is there a better version of the rule that keeps the benefits without the costs?
