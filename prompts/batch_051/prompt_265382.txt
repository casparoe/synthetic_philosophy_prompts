In Henry David Thoreau’s essay “Resistance to Civil Government” (often titled “Civil Disobedience”), he writes:

“Law never made men a whit more just; and, by means of their respect for it, even the well-disposed are daily made the agents of injustice.”

Explain the argument compressed into this sentence. In particular, distinguish the negative claim that law does not make people just from the further claim that respect for law tends to turn otherwise well-disposed people into agents of injustice. Is Thoreau best read as denying that there is any moral obligation to obey the law, or as affirming an obligation to follow conscience that overrides whatever legal obligation exists? Evaluate whether the stronger reading can be defended without reducing to a mere license for arbitrary disobedience.
