I write a small philosophy blog and want to make a "which theory of religion do you actually hold?" sorting quiz for readers. Last week a friend insisted Buddhism isnt a religion because there's no god involved, and another said her CrossFit gym is more of a religion than the church she grew up in — so I want the quiz to sort people by how they handle edge cases, not by what label they already like. Please make it nine questions, each a short concrete case with three answer options, and then a scoring key.

The cases must include at least these six: a non-theistic Buddhist practice organized around rebirth and karma; Confucian ancestor rites; an atheist Sunday Assembly with communal singing and a moment of silence; a Star Trek fan convention with shared norms, lore, and periodic gatherings; American civil religion (presidential inaugurations, Memorial Day rituals, the national motto on the currency); and a parody religion like the Church of the Flying Spaghetti Monster. You can add up to two more cases of your own if they would help separate the positions. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these six leaves: (1) classical essentialism, where belief in supernatural beings is treated as necessary and sufficient; (2) functionalism, where whatever plays the social or meaning-making role of religion counts, so civil religion and maybe a fan community can be in; (3) family resemblance, where nothing is necessary but overlapping features like ritual, community, narrative, and transcendence-strands do the work; (4) prototype theory, where some cases are better examples than others and borderline cases get sorted by graded similarity to central ones; (5) a critical/genealogical view, where the category itself is a modern Western invention that distorts things like Buddhism and Confucianism, so we should be suspicious of the label even while using it; and (6) eliminativism, where the term causes more confusion than insight and should be dropped. Each question should be answerable by someone who has never read philosophy of religion — options should be concrete judgments like "counts as a religion," "religion-like but not a religion," or "not a religion at all" plus a short justification, not theoretical statements.

Also flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't cleanly separate. My own suspicion is that prototype theory and family resemblance will be hard to pull apart unless one question tests whether the respondent thinks there are better and worse examples of religion (graded centrality) rather than just overlapping strands with no core; and functionalism vs. prototype might need a case about whether a political movement with rituals and saints counts. If some answer pattern has no natural leaf, note that and say whether the gap looks incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Since the deliverable is the quiz itself, start directly with the title and the first question — no preface or commentary — and put the scoring key at the end with point assignments.
