I've been following a debate that keeps resurfacing in the rationalist-ish corners of the internet — whenever someone posts about Boltzmann brains, or the second law, or whether the past hypothesis is a cheat — and it's striking how the same positions get restated and noone moves. The shared premises are these: the microphysical dynamics (bracketing tiny CP-violating details) don't distinguish past from future; eggs break one way because of entropy gradients; and all the visible temporal asymmetry bottoms out in something like the past hypothesis, the claim that the universe began in an enormously special, low-entropy macrostate. The split happens immediately after that. One camp says the past hypothesis is a perfectly good stopping point: physics gives you laws plus boundary conditions, and you don't owe anyone a further explanation of an initial state, so asking for a 'why' is a category error. The other camp says that's the exact move to be suspicious of, because we've traded a physical asymmetry for a condition that is doing all the explanatory work while looking wildly improbable; either there's a deeper law-like or dynamical story we're missing, or the standard machinery of statistical mechanics is smuggling in a notion of typicality/measure that hasn't been earned.

My half-formed diagnosis is that the debate has stalled because it's stopped being about physics and become a dispute about what explanations are supposed to do. The first camp wants the arrow to be a theorem from a boundary condition plus dynamics; the second wants the boundary condition itself to be law-like, or at least probable under some deeper rule; and neither side can state its standard of explanation without, in effect, assuming it. But I don't fully trust that diagnosis — I can't rule out that there's a genuine technical disagreement about measures, typicality, or Boltzmann-brain dominance hiding underneath, or that one side is quietly making a probability mistake. So I want a proper diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement rather than a verdict on who's right. Is this verbal, factual, methodological, or a clash of bedrock intuitions about explanation? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a theorem, a sharper notion of typicality, an experiment, a distinction — or is the sensible prediction that the argument still looks the same in fifty years?
