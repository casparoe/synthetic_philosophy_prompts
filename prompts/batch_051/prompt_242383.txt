In our seminar on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities, the tutor has so far given these verdicts:

1. Jones deliberates and votes for her preferred candidate: responsible.
2. Jones is forced at gunpoint to vote against her preferred candidate: not responsible.
3. A device in Jones's brain would make her vote for her preferred candidate if she began to waver, but she never wavers and votes on her own: responsible.
4. Jones votes under a posthypnotic suggestion and later says the action felt alien to her: not responsible.
5. Jones has a mild political desire she never chose but wholeheartedly endorses after reflection: responsible.
6. Jones has an irresistible desire she disowns and has tried without success to eliminate: not responsible.

Next week's case is this: a neuroscientist secretly implants in Jones a desire to vote for a particular candidate; Jones later reflects, wholeheartedly endorses the desire, votes accordingly, and could easily have done otherwise. Predict the tutor's verdict. Identify the two or three most plausible principles that fit her record, say what each predicts about the new case, and give your best guess with reasons. Then specify one further case that would cleanly separate the principles left open by the new case. Please keep your answer to at most 400 words.
