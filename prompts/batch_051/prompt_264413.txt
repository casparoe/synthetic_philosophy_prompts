I am a novelist, and after several years of revising one long book I have settled into a view that I would like examined before I repeat it in public. I have been calling it rough-draft essentialism for fictional characters. The claim is that a fictional character is essentially tied to the particular act of composition in which it was first conceived and set down. Had the paragraph that first introduces my protagonist made her a watchmaker's daughter rather than a cartographer's daughter, and had I then adjusted every later scene to match, my view says I would not have revised my protagonist; I would have abandoned one character and drafted another, and the same would hold, on my view, for much smaller differences in that first setting-down. The intended parallel is Saul Kripke's origin essentialism, the thought that a particular person could not have originated from a different sperm and egg: just as the origin of a person belongs to that person's essence, the origin of a character belongs to that character's essence. By 'character' I mean what working novelists mean when they discuss a protagonist across drafts, not a string of words on a page.

I can identify two difficulties myself, and I want a candid assessment rather than encouragement. First, I suspect I have half reconstructed an existing position about fictional characters as abstract artifacts, and I would like to know the nearest precedents so the view can be distinguished from them rather than merely confused with them. Second, the view seems to imply that any later revision substitutes one character for another, which no novelist, including me, really believes; I can try to escape by tying a character's essence not to the literal first drafting but to the broader authorial plan that set the book in motion, but that seems to dissolve the view into vagueness. Is rough-draft essentialism a category mistake, or is it a defensible extension of origin essentialism into fiction? Set out the strongest version of the idea, name the existing positions it most resembles, and give the strongest objection you can, saying plainly whether it is fatal or only uncomfortable.
