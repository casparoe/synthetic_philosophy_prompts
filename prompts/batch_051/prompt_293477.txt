Just got back from a three hour argument with a friend and I need a hand naming a move I keep seeing, including from him tonight. He kept insisting the Holocaust is completely unique and that comparing it to anything else is offensive or just misses the point. But then, whenever we turned to Rwanda or the Armenian genocide or even some current political thing, he used the Holocaust as the yardstick: it was the measure of what counts as a real genocide, the template for "this is how it starts," the standard everything else falls short of. When I pointed out that this looked like comparing after all, he said no: uniqueness is a historical claim about what the Holocaust was, while using it as a yardstick is a moral claim about what it means for us later. The funny thing is, part of what made it unique in his eyes was the modern bureaucratic, industrial machinery of it, but to me the more you lean on that, the more it sounds like a general feature of modern societies rather than a one-off, which seems to cut against the uniqueness part. Is there a name for this exact configuration, holding something to be incomparable and unique while also making it the universal measure for all the things it cant be compared to? It doesnt seem like plain exceptionalism. If there isnt a standard name, what would you call it, and does the historical/moral distinction he drew actually do the work he thinks it does? I want to know what this move is and where its edges are, not just a label.
