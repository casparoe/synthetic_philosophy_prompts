You are an expert on Dennett's intentional stance in the philosophy of AI. According to that stance, one is justified in ascribing a propositional attitude to a system only to the extent that the ascription earns its keep by yielding genuine predictive leverage over the system's behavior — not because the behavior is fluent or seems human. Rank the following five ascriptions to a contemporary large language model from most to least defensible by that standard, and give a one- or two-sentence justification for each placement:

1. the model believes the claim it is currently stating;
2. the model wants its answer to be helpful;
3. the model intends to follow the user's instruction;
4. the model understands the meaning of the term it is using;
5. the model is confabulating (rather than merely erring) when it produces a false answer.

Before you finalize your ranking, brainstorm what behavioral predictions each ascription would actually license and ask whether a stricter, non-intentional description would predict the same behavior. Then present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items — one per ascription, ordered from most to least defensible — each with a one- or two-sentence justification and no introduction or conclusion.
