so im doing this thing for my high schools philosophy club where each of us has to design a thought experiment that the rest of us then try to demolish, and ive gone down a rabbit hole about cognitive penetration of perception — basically whether what you want or expect can literally change the way something looks, not just the way you interpret it or report it afterward. the annoying pattern is that every example i find gets killed with the same three replies: (1) the desire or belief just shifts your attention, so your eyes are picking up different input, (2) the perception stays exaclty the same and only your report or judgement shifts, or (3) you dont actually see it differently in the moment, you just remember it as different later. i want a case that survives all three without cheating by just stipulating that it really happened.

so build me a thought experiment where a desire or goal (not a belief) changes a low-level spatial feature — slope, gap width, distance, something like that — in a totally ordinary setting like a park, a gym, or a sidewalk. no lab equipment, no drugs, no VR, no brain stimulation, no hypnosis. key constraints: the feature being judged has to be incidental to the persons goal, so the goal cant just redirect their attention toward it, and the report has to be made right there in the moment, so memory never gets a chance to do the work.

give me the one-paragraph scenario, then walk through the check: state the desire, the exact perceptual change, and why each of the three replies (attention, response bias, memory) fails in your case. then tell me which single feature of the scenario is doing the heavy lifting, and which reply comes back if you remove that feature. finish with the strongest remaining move for someone who thinks perception is insulated from cognition — i suspect theyd say the desire changes the body (posture, tension, pupil size) and that changes the incoming signal rather than the seeing itself — and then say whether youd count that as still being a kind of cognitive penetration or not, and why.
