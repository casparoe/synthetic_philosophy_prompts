I'm a legal aid lawyer, and I keep turning over a passage from William Morris's Useful Work versus Useless Toil. Can you put on your William Morris scholar hat and think carefully with me for a bit? Here's the passage:

"Thus worthy work carries with it the hope of pleasure in rest, the hope of pleasure in our using what it makes, and the hope of pleasure in our daily creative skill.

All other work but this is worthless; it is slaves' work - mere toiling to live, that we may live to toil."

Morris obviously never saw an LLM, but I think the passage might have something to say about my situation. I work at a nonprofit doing eviction defense. Until about a year ago, I wrote my own fact sections and legal memos from the case files. It was slow and often frustrating, but I had the sense Morris is describing: I was making something, and the skill felt like mine.

Now our office has an AI tool that reads the case files and produces a first draft of the memo. My job has become mostly checking its citations, correcting its tone, and reworking arguments where the AI got the law wrong or missed something. I leave at five, I'm less stressed, and clients still get good work product. But the daily creative skill part has mostly drained out of my day. The memo feels more like a thing I supervised than a thing I made.

What I want to know is this: by Morris's criteria, is my work still worthy work? Is hope of product satisfied as long as something useful gets made, even if it isn't in a deep sense mine? Or does checking and fixing an AI draft still count as the "daily creative skill" he has in mind? Also, if the job now gives me plenty of rest and a useful product, is the loss of the third hope enough on its own to make the work worthless in his sense? And would Morris maybe think the AI has done me a favor by taking away the toil part, or would he say it has taken away the exact part that made the job worth doing?

Don't just give me the obvious reading—actually push on whether Morris's three hopes can come apart in this way in the first place. And if you think he would say I'm now doing slaves' work, I want to hear that too.
