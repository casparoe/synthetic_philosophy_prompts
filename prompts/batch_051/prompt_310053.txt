I'm a software engineer and I've been falling down a wikipedia hole about species concepts, mostly because a friend who does conservation work mentioned that the law she works with defines "species" in a way that sounds like a spec that was never designed to handle edge cases. The clause (I've changed the name of the act, but the wording is the part that matters) says: "For the purposes of this Act, 'species' means a group of organisms whose members actually or potentially interbreed with one another in nature and produce fertile offspring, and which is reproductively isolated from all other such groups."

The case bugging me is a ring species. Picture a chain of populations around a mountain range or valley, call them A through E. A interbreeds with B, B with C, C with D, and D with E, with fertile offspring the whole way, but where the two ends of the chain meet (A and E), they don't interbreed—or if they do, the offspring are infertile. Under the first half of the definition, A and B are one species, B and C are one species, and so on, so transitivity seems to force A and E into the same species. But under the second half, A and E are reproductively isolated from each other, so they can't be the same species; in fact, any way you slice the chain either leaves adjacent populations that aren't isolated (violating the second half) or leaves end populations that don't interbreed (violating the first half). The clause seems to return two answers at once, and whoever wrote it was probably picturing clean, non-overlapping boxes, not a loop.

So my question is this: when a rule hits a case its drafters didn't foresee, what should govern—the literal words, the purpose of the act (presumably protecting biodiversity), what the drafters would have said if you'd asked them, or how a reasonable biologist reading the statute would take it? Walk through what each of those approaches gives here, and say which one you think should win and why. And if the whole question is badly posed—say, the actual problem is the idea that "species" can be given one clean operational definition, or I'm leaning on transitivity in a way I shouldn't—tell me that plainly. I dont need a tidy conclusion that quietly ignores the mess.
