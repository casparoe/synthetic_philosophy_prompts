ok so I dont have any background in music theory or philosophy, this is just something Ive been turning over by myself. theres an asymmetry I cant get past.

When someone says "that song is sad," nobody takes it as poetry or a figure of speech. We understand them as saying the music itself is sad, even though there is no person in it who feels sad and no event it depicts. But if someone points to an abstract painting, or a wall color, and says "that is sad," we treat it as a loose mood thing, not the same kind of claim. The painting can be gloomy or about sadness, but it isnt sad the way a slow minor-key adagio is sad. So sounds get to literally be sad in a way that colors, shapes, and walls dont.

Then inside music there is a second asymmetry. Instrumental music can be sad, angry, anxious, joyful, terrifying, maybe even tender or playful. But we basically never say a piece of music is jealous, embarrassed, guilty, resentful, or ashamed. Those are emotions too, and its not obvious why the same trick that turns a slow tempo and a drooping minor-key melody into sadness cant be tweaked into embarrassment. A fast spiky piece gets called angry, not jealous.

So my question: is there a principle that actually justifies both lines — the one between music and other inanimate things, and the one between sad/angry/anxious and jealous/embarrassed/guilty — or are we just inconsistent? Make the main argument in plain prose.
