I'm a journalist working on a piece about the behavioural-economics line that what we call weakness of will is mostly hyperbolic discounting in disguise. The theory is compelling: preferences are time-inconsistent, so the me who plans to write at 9am and the me who picks up his phone at 9am are different selves with different discount curves, and the later choice isn't a failure of will so much as a predictable preference reversal. I don't want to give that up easily — it's formal, it predicts the reversals, and it unifies procrastination, savings failures and addiction. But I also have a firm intuition that when I'm scrolling with a deadline bearing down, I am acting against my better judgement right then, not just surprising my earlier self. The self-reproach feels apt, and it feels like it's about a single agent failing, not two agents with different rates. So which yields? What would it take for the intuition to be debunked as a post-hoc gloss on a preference reversal? What would it take for the discounting story to be amended so it leaves room for genuine synchronic akrasia, and does that amendment ruin what made the theory attractive? Is there a reading on which both survive — the discount curve describes the temporal pattern, while akrasia describes the synchronic judgement? Give me the strongest version of each move. And tell me how confident you are in the answer and which point you're least sure of. Answer in British English.
