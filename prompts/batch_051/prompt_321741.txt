i've been going back and forth on integrated information theory for months. on the one hand, i find the whole approach really compelling: it doesn't just announce some neural correlate and call it consciousness, it starts from axioms about what experience is like — intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, exclusion — and tries to build a measure, Φ, that a system has in just those respects. that's the kind of principled derivation i want from a theory of mind, way better than "neurons synchronize at 40Hz." the theory also gives definite, graded verdicts for every physical system, which i count as a real virtue when the alternatives mostly duck the question.

on the other hand, i have an equally strong conviction that a thermostat — literally the bimetallic strip in the hallway — is not conscious one little bit. not a trace, not a flicker of anything it's like to be. and yet IIT seems committed to saying it is, in some small amount. this isn't some critic's caricature; Scott Aaronson, in "Why I Am Not An Integrated Information Theorist (or, The Unconscious Expander)", states it flatly while explaining the theory:

"one immediate consequence of any definition like this is that all sorts of simple physical systems (a thermostat, a photodiode, etc.) will turn out to have small but nonzero Φ values. To his credit, Tononi cheerfully accepts the panpsychist implication: yes, he says, it really does mean that thermostats and photodiodes have small but nonzero levels of consciousness."

i've been told the right response is either (a) drop the intuition — maybe i'm wildly overconfident that "zero consciousness" is a thing i can read off a system just by looking at how dumb it is — or (b) treat the thermostat-photodiode implication as a reductio of IIT, or at least of its panpsychist payoff. but i dont trust myself on either horn. give me both positions at their strongest and then a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and is there a reading of "conscious" (or of what Φ actually measures) on which both survive? i don't want a winner announcement, i want to know what would justify each move. and at the end, say how confident you are in the overall verdict and single out the point you're least sure of.
