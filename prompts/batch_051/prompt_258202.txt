Please give me your actual credence that the simulation argument substantially dampens far-future fanaticism — the view that the overwhelming share of expected value in our decisions lies in their effects on the long-run future of civilization, or of sentient life more generally, so that near-term effects matter mainly as leverage on that future. Take "substantially dampens" to mean that an actor who accepts the simulation argument should reduce, by at least a factor of ten, the share of expected value they assign to shaping the very long run, relative to the share they would assign while ignoring the argument. By "the simulation argument" I mean the argument that at least one of the following obtains: civilizations like ours almost always go extinct before acquiring the capacity to run large numbers of ancestor simulations; civilizations with that capacity almost never choose to run such simulations in large numbers; or we are almost certainly already living in one.

Decompose your credence into at least the following terms, giving a probability and a one-line justification for each, then show how they combine into the overall number: (1) the probability that the argument's disjunction is sound in roughly that form; (2) the probability that, conditional on its soundness, our situation is one in which the long-run future we can influence is much smaller, shorter, or less morally weighted than the naive projection — whether because we are simulated and the simulation will be truncated or discounted, or because we are not simulated and will likely go extinct before reaching posthumanity; (3) the probability that, conditional on our being simulated, the simulated future nevertheless bears full moral weight and remains a practicable target of our actions; and (4) the probability that, conditional on the extinction disjunct rather than the simulation disjunct, fanaticism about the far future survives largely intact because extinction risk becomes the dominant far-future variable rather than a reason to abandon far-future priorities. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, which term the overall credence is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would most move the final number.
