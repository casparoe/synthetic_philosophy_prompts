In a conversation about how much anyone owes to the people and circumstances that made their life possible, one side kept appealing to the idea that gratitude is a kind of debt: a benefit received is like a loan, the benefactor is like a creditor, and failure to feel or show gratitude is a kind of default. I have also seen the same picture extended to luck and to the past — people say we owe a debt to our good fortune or to earlier generations whose labor we inherit.

I want an audit of this analogy rather than a defense or dismissal of it. Lay out the correspondence: what maps onto debtor, creditor, the principal, repayment, interest, forgiveness, and discharge of the debt. Then identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusions people draw from the ones that do not. Which features of financial debt does the analogy quietly import that gratitude lacks? Does the conclusion — that benefactors, luck, or the past are owed something like repayment — survive once the disanalogies are on the table? If not, what would be a better analogy for what gratitude actually involves? And if you think the framing itself is confused or the question cannot be answered in this form, tell me that directly rather than pretending the audit works.
