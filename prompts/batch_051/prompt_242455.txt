I've been going back and forth on this argument about generations, and I can't find where it goes wrong.

(1) If generations (boomers, gen z, etc.) are real kinds of thing rather than just labels, then there's got to be some feature that all and only the people in a generation share.

(2) There's really only two candidates for that feature: being born inside the official year range, or going through the same big historical stuff at the same age (being a kid on 9/11, being in school for covid, things like that).

(3) The year range can't do it: the cutoffs are just lines somebody drew for surveys and marketing, and drawing a line doesn't create a real kind.

(4) But the shared-experience candidate can't do it either, because the same event lands differently depending on class, region, gender, family, all that. So "grew up with the internet" or "was a kid during 9/11" isn't actually one shared feature across the whole cohort; it doesn't produce one shared trait.

(5) So there's no feature all and only boomers or gen z share.

(6) Therefore generations aren't real kinds; they're just bookkeeping.

I honestly want this to be true because I'm tired of hot takes about generations, but I have a feeling I've smuggled something in. Is the argument valid? If not, which step breaks, and what's the most reasonable reading a person who thinks generations are real could give that step?
