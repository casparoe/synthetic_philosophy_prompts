I'm taking a philosophy of photography course and we've been on the transparency debate for a couple weeks — the Walton line that photographs are literally transparent, so we see the photographed scene through them kinda like a telescope or mirror, against the view that they're just representations we interpret like any other picture. I want to write my paper on Antonioni's Blow-Up, because the film keeps flipping between those positions in a way that I think might be pointing at something third. Thomas takes those photos in the park without noticing anything unusual, and then in the darkroom the blow-ups let him see a man with a gun and, he thinks, a body — stuff he apparently walked right past. That's a pretty literal case of the camera seeing for him. But then the more he enlarges, the grainier and more useless the image gets, and his certainty goes down instead of up. And the ending with the mimes playing tennis with a ball that isn't there, where he ends up joining the game, seems to be saying something uncomfortable about how much seeing depends on going along with a fiction. I don't just want to say the film is ambivalent. Does Blow-Up actually take a side between transparency and its critics, or is it showing that the whole dilemma is badly posed? And if it's a third option, what would you call it — what does the film add that the philosophical debate misses?
