I teach philosophy (mostly philosophy of mind, with the odd first-year survey), and I keep running into the same potted account of Aristotle's four causes in introductory books. It goes roughly like this. When Aristotle asked why a thing is as it is, he distinguished four kinds of answer: the material cause is the stuff (the bronze of a statue), the formal cause is its shape or arrangement (the pose), the efficient cause is what made it (the chisel), and the final cause is the purpose for which it was made (to honour a god). The account then says Aristotle extended the final cause to nature generally: heavy bodies fall because they seek their natural place, acorns grow into oaks because that is their end. And the story concludes that modern science began when Galileo, Bacon, and Descartes dropped final causes and kept only efficient causes, since explaining something by the end it supposedly aims at puts the result before the process that produces it. Today, the story finishes, teleology survives only as a loose metaphor, and whenever careful scientists use words like function or purpose they could always swap the term for a description of mechanism or selection history without losing anything.

I know enough to suspect the real position is more subtle then the textbook lets on, but I am not an ancient specialist and I do not want to overcorrect a perfectly serviceable account. What I would like is a verdict on how fair this summary is. Could you separate the parts that are accurate, the parts that are standard simplifications, and the parts where Aristotle or the history of science would need correcting? In particular, is the final cause really just the purpose someone had in mind, or does it do different work in nature; did modern science really dispense with every cause except the efficient one; and is the closing claim that function talk can be translated into mechanism or selection history without loss itself accurate? And if the corrections pile up, does the moral of the standard story still hold, or is it misleading at the end of the day?

No headings, bullet points, bold, or tables; just continuous prose like an email, and please write in British English.
