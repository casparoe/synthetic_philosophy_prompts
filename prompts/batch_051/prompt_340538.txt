I am taking a philosophy elective at school, and we recently spent two weeks on causation. I found myself almost entirely persuaded by the regularity view we learned from Hume: all I ever seem to observe is one event following another in a regular way, water heating before it boils or one ball moving after another strikes it, and never some further item called a necessary connection between cause and effect. The theory attracts me because it is disciplined and unpretentious. It stays inside what experience can actually deliver, it avoids postulating hidden machinery that no one can point to, and it turns causal claims into testable statements about patterns rather than mysteries about hidden powers. But I cannot get rid of a contrary feeling. When I hold my hand near a flame, it does not merely seem that heat and pain have been constantly conjoined in my experience; it seems that the heat actually makes the pain happen, that something in the flame forces or produces the effect. I have tried to dismiss this as a habit of expectation, but the conviction that there is real production out there keeps returning with considerable force.

I have also come across the suggestion that Hume himself might not have wanted to deny that real causal powers exist, only that we can ever know them; I believe scholars call this the New Hume, and I am unsure whether it is a genuine solution or only an interpretive rescue of Hume. So my question is what should give way. Should I treat the intuition as a projection, a useful but misleading feeling that should be debunked in favor of the regularity theory? Should I instead amend the theory so that causation includes real production or necessity, and ask what that amendment would explain better? Or is there a reading on which the two are compatible, perhaps because the regularity theory describes how we detect and test causal relations while the intuition concerns something else, or because necessity can be understood in a deflated way? Give me the strongest version of each position, and say what it would take for the intuition to go, for the theory to go, or for the two to be reconciled, rather than just telling me which side you favor.
