Here is a proposal for distinguishing sphexish rigidity from genuinely flexible action. Count a behavior as sphexish when: (i) the agent runs a fixed sequence with little sensitivity to whether it is still serving the goal; (ii) when the world changes so that an already performed step no longer helps, the agent repeats that step instead of improvising a novel one; (iii) the agent can justify the routine as a whole but cannot say why the particular repeated step is being performed now; and (iv) at the point of repetition the agent is not making a live choice among alternatives. Now consider a senior anesthesiologist during a difficult-airway emergency. Her first maneuver fails, and she repeats it once before moving on; she knows the repetition is unlikely to help, but she would defend every element of the protocol, and afterwards she cannot say whether she repeated it because the algorithm told her to or because she decided to. She would improvise in an unanticipated situation, but this situation was precisely one the protocol was built to handle. Is her repeated maneuver sphexish? Work criterion by criterion, say where the criteria leave the case underdetermined, and then say whether this case shows that the line between mechanical routine and freely exercised skill is not where the criteria draw it.
