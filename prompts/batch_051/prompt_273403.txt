I'm working on a piece about VR headsets being trialed in residential care, and I keep coming back to a policy document from one facility. Their activities policy, written in 1998, says: "All resident activities must be chosen to promote the well-being of residents and to keep them engaged with their families, other residents, and the wider community."

The case that's bugging me: a resident in her seventies has a degenerative condition that leaves her with almost no speech and no local family. She wants to spend most of her waking hours in a high-fidelity VR environment where she can walk easily, hold down a part-time job, and belong to a book club — all of it simulated, though it doesn't feel simulated. She says she's happier than she's been in years and more social than she's been since the diagnosis. Some staff read the policy as permitting it: she is engaged with a community, just a virtual one, and her self-reported well-being has gone up. Others read the policy as ruling it out: whatever this is, it isn't engagement with a community in the sense the authors meant.

I know it's basically the experience-machine thought experiment in a care home, but I need to know what the policy actually requires. Argue it from the wording, from the eviedent purpose of the clause, and from what the people who wrote the thing in 1998 would likely have said if you'd shown them the headset. Then read the word "well-being" in the clause under three theories: hedonism (well-being is pleasant experience), desire-satisfaction (well-being is getting what you'd want if informed), and objective-list (well-being consists in certain goods such as real relationships, real achievement, and autonomy). Wether the facility should allow eight hours a day in the headset hangs a lot on that choice, so tell me which reading of "well-being" should govern the policy, and which of the three interpretive approaches — wording, purpose, drafter's likely answer — should win when they conflict. And finally, on your preferred reading, does the clause let her stay plugged in?
