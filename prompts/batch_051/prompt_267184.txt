I recently took over managing the books for my family's small fabrication business, and for the first time I am the one deciding how much cash to hold, whether to borrow, and how to set prices while inflation works its way through our suppliers' contracts. In a dispute about whether we should borrow now on the theory that we would repay in cheaper dollars, my uncle, who holds part of the business, kept returning to the line "printing money doesn't create wealth." He used it for two specific purposes: to say that any borrowing gain we hoped for was an illusion, and to say that a government that creates a great deal of money does not thereby make a country better off.

I suspect that one sentence is doing too much work, so before I let it influence a business decision I want it disassembled. Please set out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be expressing, ordered from the nearly tautological to the radical. For each reading, state exactly what it asserts, say how one could in principle tell whether it was true, and give a verdict of true, false, or defensible-only-with-qualifications. It will probably be necessary to separate real resources from claims on resources, and both from liquidity, to do this cleanly. Then identify which reading or readings would actually support each of my uncle's two conclusions, and which readings, even if true, would not support them. I am asking for a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single interpretation, and please write the answer in American English.
