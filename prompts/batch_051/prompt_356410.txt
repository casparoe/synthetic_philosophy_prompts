I keep going back and forth on whether the Trojan War should count as having actually happened, so I want to test it against a list of criteria rather than gut feeling. Here's the criterion list I've been using for when a legendary event gets to be called historical: (1) there's a real site that matches the setting and shows destruction around the right time; (2) roughly contemporary records from some other culture mention at least a few of the relevant names or places; (3) the basic shape of the conflict fits what we know about the period; and (4) there's a halfway plausible path for the memory to get into the surviving story instead of being invented wholesale.

Now take the Trojan War as the test case. At Hisarlik in northwestern Anatolia there's a fortified city almost everyone identifies with Troy, and the layer usually picked as Homeric Troy was burned somewhere around 1180 BCE, give or take. Hittite tablets from that general era mention a kingdom called Wilusa in the same corner of Anatolia, which looks a lot like Ilion, and a Wilusan king named Alaksandu, which is suspiciously close to the Homeric name Alexandros for Paris; the Hittites also refer to an Ahhiyawa kingdom out in the Aegean that keeps interfering on the Anatolian coast, which a lot of people read as the Achaeans. But no Hittite text describes anything like a big Greek coalition sacking Wilusa, and the Iliad was written down centuries later after a long run of oral transmission, with the Bronze Age collapse in between. On top of that there's a further tangle about which layer is the Iliad's Troy at all: the earlier one looks earthquake-damaged and the later one burned, and people still argue about which kind of destruction fits the poem.

Go criterion by criterion and say whether the Trojan War qualifies, and whether this case is a reason to revise the criteria themselves. Please keep it straight what's genuinely settled among people who work on this and what's contested — dont make the Wilusa/Ilion or Ahhiyawa/Achaeans identifications sound like consensus if they're not.
