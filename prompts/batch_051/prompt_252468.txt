The word "religion" is doing a lot of unexamined work in arguments I read: a political movement gets called a secular religion, a research community is compared to a church, Buddhism is offered as a counterexample to someone's definition, and Confucianism gets filed as philosophy rather than religion. The term is clearly carrying argumentative weight, but the people using it plainly do not share criteria. Before I read any more accounts of what religion is, I want the specification first.

Write the desiderata for an adequate account of the category "religion" — what any theory of what the category picks out (or of why it picks out nothing) has to deliver before I should trust it in those arguments. Give me the requirements list: the cases it must classify, or explicitly and non-ad-hoc refuse to classify, with the consideration behind each (Theravada Buddhism with no creator god, the Confucian ritual-and-ethics tradition, nationalist movements with saints and martyrs, a solitary contemplative with no community, the group member who calls the group "a religion" while meaning none of the metaphysics); the distinctions it must not collapse (content versus social function, the category's extension versus its polemical uses, vagueness at the boundary versus uselessness of the category); and the historical and cross-cultural facts it must be consistent with, such as the category's particular history and the absence of an equivalent single term in many of the traditions now grouped under it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are parochial preferences of a camp. Tell me which single requirement does the most to eliminate the standard candidate definitions, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied.

Fix the purpose yourself if it helps: I want an account good enough to tell when calling something a religion is doing classificatory work and when it is smuggling in connotations, not one that maximizes fidelity to any tradition's self-understanding.

Close with a short scorecard: how do a substantive definition (belief in gods or the supernatural), a functionalist definition (religion by what it does for meaning or social order), and a family-resemblance account fare against your list? Keep that part brief; the criteria are the point.
