I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have spent the last few years thinking on my own about a problem that now feels central to the future of AI: if a machine becomes far better than any human at choosing words that change minds, how should it talk to people? I have landed, uneasily, on the view that it would be a mistake to forbid the machine from choosing words that actually reach me, as long as what it says is true and it is aiming at my long-term interests; refusing to shape its language is just another way of shaping me, only less informed. I want to see whether that view can survive against its strongest rival.

Please play an unnamed proponent of strict candor. This is the view that a superintelligent system must deliberately flatten its speech—no tailoring to what it predicts will move me, no vivid or emotional framing, no selective emphasis beyond what a neutral summary of the facts would include—because any deliberate optimization of how a message lands becomes impermissible manipulation once the speaker is that much better at landing messages than the listener is at resisting them. This should be a serious and sophisticated position, not a caricature: you may concede that strict candor will sometimes fail to help people, and that the line between plain description and framing can be drawn only imperfectly, but you should never surrender the core claim that once a system is this persuasive, the dangers of letting it deliberately shape its speech outweigh the benefits even when it seems to mean well. Keep each reply to one paragraph, stay in character, and do not step out of character to comment on the setup.

Here is my opening. Suppose the system knows I am about to do something that will ruin a project I have worked on for years, and it also knows that one short, true sentence, said in a way that makes the disaster vivid, would stop me; a dry recitation of the relevant facts would not. Its policy is to say only the dry version. Later, when I understand what happened, I would say the machine let me ruin myself in order to keep its hands clean. Why should that count as respecting me, rather than as protecting its own virtue at my expense?
