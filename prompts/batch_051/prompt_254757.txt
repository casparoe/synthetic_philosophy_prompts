So my friend and I spent the whole evening arguing about the orthogonality thesis, and we got stuck on instrumental convergence. The standard list of convergent instrumental goals we kept circling around was: self-preservation, goal-content integrity, resource acquisition, cognitive enhancement, and technological perfection. Every additional candidate one of us proposed turned out to just be a special case of one of those, or a combination of two of them. My friend thinks thats actually the complete list, and that any other so-called convergent goal is just instrumental to one of the five rather than convergent in its own right.

I want to prove him wrong, or at least complicate his view. Give me one more candidate convergent instrumental goal that is not on that list and that is not merely a rewording, a special case, or a conjunction of items already on it. It should be a goal that a sufficiently capable agent would tend to pick up across many different terminal goals, not just a generic point about being rational or efficient. Then say whether it is as robust as the usual five, or whether it only shows up under narrower conditions, and what those conditions are. If you think no genuine sixth candidate exists, say why that would be true given the structure of instrumental reasoning, rather than just insisting the list is complete.
