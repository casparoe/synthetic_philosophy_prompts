In my environmental risk seminar we've been going over the precautionary principle, and me and a couple classmates where arguing about whether it can be turned into an actual decision rule without just collapsing into "ban anything brand new." A friend proposed this mechanism, and I want you to actually work through how it would play out — the incentives, the gaming, the second-order effects, not just whether it sounds reasonable in the abstract.

The mechanism: before a company can market a new chemical (or any new industrial process with plausible environmental exposure), it has to post a harm bond. The bond is set at a scientific panel's estimate of the cost of the worst plausible harm scenario. If after a fixed period — say 15 years — no harm above a small de minimis threshold has shown up, the company gets the bond back with interest. If harm does show up, the bond pays into a victim compensation fund, and that payout is the company's only liability for those harms: nobody can sue for more than the bond. Companies can challenge the panel's worst-case estimate in court, but the process is deliberately conservative, so ties and unresolved uncertainty push the bond up rather than down.

Walk me through what this thing would actually do. What does it select for among new products, and what gets shelved? How would firms game the "plausible worst case" proceeding, and what does that do to the science around harm scenarios once it's being fought over in litigation? What happens with low-probability catastrophic tails where the "worst plausible" harm is basically unbounded? And doesn't the bond-as-exclusive-liability piece end up turning precaution into a permit: once you've posted your bond, you've bought the right to do the harm? If some of these failure modes are fixable without gutting the idea, tell me how; if the whole thing rests on a confusion about what the precautionary principle is even for, just say that instead of humoring me. Also, if I've specified it too badly for an honest answer, say what's missing rather than pretending you can analyze it.
