I sit on the research ethics board at a teaching hospital, and we're stuck on a phase III trial — let's call it SPARROW — for refractory ulcerative colitis. It randomizes patients to either a new oral drug (call it BX-221) or to staying on whichever biologic their doctor thinks is best, with the usual superiority endpoints. The problem is a single-arm phase II extension that just got posted: 70% clinical remission at six months, which is way better than anything else in the class. Two members want to reject the trial outright on the grounds that there's no clinical equipoise left; randomizing someone to the control arm is effectively denying them a treatment the data already show works. Two others say that's exactly backwards: the phase II had no comparator, no blinding, and an enriched population, so if we reject the RCT we guarantee the question never gets settled and a possibly mediocre drug becomes standard off-label anyway. I need to circulate a decision memo before Friday's meeting. Draft it for me: approve, reject, or approve with conditions — give a bottom line the commitee can actually vote on, and make sure you genuinely address the strongest argument on the losing side, not just the one you agree with. If you go with conditions, spell out what specifically would have to change for the trial to be acceptable.
