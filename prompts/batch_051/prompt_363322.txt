I have a standing policy when I read experimental philosophy. If folk ascriptions of a mental state—saying an outcome was brought about "intentionally," "knowingly," or "on purpose"—systematically shift with the moral valence of the outcome, as in the Knobe effect, where subjects are far more willing to call a harmful foreseen side effect intentional than a helpful one, I treat the asymmetry as noise from affect or motivated cognition. I therefore refuse to treat either arm of the asymmetry as evidence about what "doing something intentionally" means. A colleague says this is backwards: the valence-sensitivity is itself the phenomenon a theory of folk psychology must explain, not a confound to be set aside, and my policy is a prescriptive intuition wearing a debunking argument. Is my policy defensible? What would have to be true for a valence-driven shift of this kind to license discarding the judgments, and can the policy be restated so that it screens off distorting effects without begging the question against the view that moral considerations belong inside the concept?
