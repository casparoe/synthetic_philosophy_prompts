i'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, mostly working on introspection, and I'm trying to get my view into a shape I can actually defend. I keep endorsing a bunch of commitments in conversation and they all feel right, but I havent actually checked whether they can live together. I'd rather you audit the set then reassure me.

Here's where I've landed:

1. First-person authority. If I sincerely judge right now that I'm in pain, or that I believe the shop is shut, that judgment carries a strong but defeasible presumption of truth — an asymmetry you don't get from the third-person point of view.
2. Transparency for belief. Asked whether I believe p, I answer by considering whether p is the case, not by inspecting an inner belief-state. So I don't think beliefs are the kind of thing one discovers by introspecting.
3. Anti-luminosity. No interesting conscious condition is luminous: for any mental state, there are borderline cases where you're in the state but not in a position to know you are, because knowledge needs a margin for error. I find the margin-for-error reasoning that motivates this pretty convincing.
4. No gap for current bodily sensations. If it seems to me now that I'm in pain, then I am; and a pain that didn't present itself as pain would not be a pain at all. The seeming just is the state — both directions.
5. Introspection as attention, and attention is transformative. Introspecting an experience is attending to it, but attending to a pain can make it throb harder or change its character; attending to an itch makes it worse; attending to an afterimage keeps it around. So introspection is not a neutral readout of a preexisting state.
6. Reports-as-data. Careful introspective reports should be used as pre-theoretic data in the science of consciousness, though the reports themselves are defeasible and corrigible.

I want a consistency audit. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. Then tell me the single cheapest revision — which one commitment I should soften or reword, and what doing that would cost. If the package as written is worse than I think, don't be nice about it.
