A friend won't stop claiming that culture — specifically values — can be a genuine cause of large-scale change, not just a label for a pile of other causes. Her favorite case is the old Weberian Protestant ethic story: that the ascetic Protestant worry over predestination, the idea of a worldly calling, and the taboo on idleness and luxury helped produce the disciplined work, thrift, and reinvestment that early capitalism fed on. I keep objecting that this is a just-so story — maybe commercially minded people converted into those sects, or the same towns had different institutions, or the story is unfalsifiable. So here's what I want from you. Design the strongest test you can, real or hypothetical, that would seperate values doing the causal work from selection, reverse causation, and common causes. Say exactly what outcome would favor the cultural-causation reading and what outcome would count against it. Then be honest about what even the best outcome leaves open: can a test ever show people were moved by the values themselves, rather than by habits, incentives, or signaling that merely traveled with those values? Or is the claim that values cause anything partly untestable in principle?
