Every year the distributive justice unit in my department's ethics seminar ends up stuck on the same exchange. The prioritarians say that equality as such has no value: a benefit matters more the worse-off the recipient is, and once you've accounted for how each person fares, the pattern across persons contributes nothing. The egalitarians say that can't be right, because a distribution in which everyone is equally badly off seems better in at least one respect than one in which the same badly-off people coexist with a few extremely rich ones — the equality itself is the improvement. Then the prioritarians deploy the leveling-down objection: if equality as such had value, then leveling the rich down to the level of the badly off, destroying advantage without helping anyone, would count as an improvement in at least one respect. But that's absurd, so equality as such has no value. The egalitarians mostly refuse the bait: some say leveling down is in one respect better even if not all-things-considered, so the alleged absurdity is just the all-things-considered judgment; others say the objection only lands against a weird view on which equality is a free-floating good abstracted from lives; and around it goes.

My hunch is the real fight isn't about leveling down at all. It looks to me like a disagreement about the structure of value: whether the goodness of a distribution has to be built up additively, seperately, from how each individual fares, or whether the distribution itself can have holistic features — equality being one — that are evaluatively relevant over and above the individual entries. But a friend keeps insisting the logjam is just that "egalitarianism" is ambiguous: telic egalitarians think equal outcomes are in themselves better, while deontic egalitarians think what matters is that unequal treatment wrongs people, and he says the leveling-down objection only threatens the telic version. I'm not sure he's right, because I suspect the deontic version just faces a parallel difficulty about "wronging" nobody in particular. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: what kind of disagreement is this, exactly — substantive axiological, structural about separability, verbal about "equality," or something about what a distributive principle is even answerable to? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result about aggregation, a distinction drawn, a question changed — or should we expect it to stay stalled?
