I want to stress-test a position I have been drawn toward rather than simply defend it. The position is that the embedded-agency critique is not just a reminder that real agents have finite computational resources. It is the stronger claim that a physically realized agent cannot, even in principle, draw a clean boundary between itself and its environment, and that this is not a harmless idealization. On this view, the Cartesian picture of an agent standing outside a world it observes and acts on gets the structure of agency wrong in a way that cannot be repaired by adding more detail. Standard Bayesian expected-utility maximization, Solomonoff induction, and any framework that takes the agent-world split for granted therefore misdescribe an embedded agent's epistemic situation: the agent's model of the world must include the agent itself, its own future models, and other predictors whose behavior depends on those models, and no amount of fine-tuning within the Cartesian setup recovers this.

My question is not whether the critique has influenced how people talk, but how vulnerable the position actually is. What would the world have to look like for the Cartesian idealization to be vindicated after all? I want a map of where the position can be attacked. Which claims are load-bearing, and which are close to definitional? What would a decisive counterexample look like: a formal decision theory with a clean agent-world split that handles self-reference and self-modeling without loss; a demonstration that the supposedly distinctive failures of embeddedness also arise, in the same form, within the Cartesian picture; or an argument that the critique conflates a model's limitations with features of the thing modeled? Which of these are live escape routes rather than merely logical possibilities, and where should a defender of the standard idealization plant their flag? Distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, say what a committed embedded-agency theorist would themselves concede as counting against the position, and be willing to conclude that the position is well insulated, and whether that insulation reflects depth or unfalsifiability.
