Last night a friend and I argued for hours about a town whose founding story had just been contradicted by a box of newly surfaced documents. My friend insisted that the community's memory of itself is not a defective draft of the record, and that correcting it would do a different kind of damage; I insisted that confronting the record is exactly what keeps remembering from collapsing into myth. We went in circles because we could each produce cases where the other side seemed plainly wrong — the harmless embellishment, the exculpatory national narrative — but neither of us could state the principle that separates those cases. I run a two-hour civic-education workshop for about twenty adults with no background in historiography, and I want to stage the problem rather than lecture about it. Design a runnable structured activity — roles, rules, materials, a forced decision with real (small) stakes, for instance a committee that must settle on the single wording of a commemorative plaque — in which participants experience the pull of both collective memory and the historical record, and then have to make a choice that cannot satisfy both. No lecture up front: the distinction should be named only in the debrief.

The activity should put some participants in a position where they have genuine identity-based reasons to resist correction (for example, as keepers of a community's founding story) and others in a position where they have genuine evidentiary reasons to insist on it (for example, as archivists who hold the contradictory documents), and the decision procedure should force the question of which gives way when they cannot both be true. Include a minute-by-minute timeline for the two hours, the prompt cards or case materials I would prepare, and a debrief that maps moments in the activity onto the conceptual distinction — for instance, the identity-bearing, present-directed work of collective memory versus the critical, evidence-responsive work of history — while being frank about one feature of the tension the activity cannot reproduce and what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it.
