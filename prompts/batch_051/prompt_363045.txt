I am a machine learning researcher on an alignment team, and I have been asked to draft a short internal memo on whether AI welfare considerations belong inside our safety program or stand in tension with it. Some of us regard the two agendas as naturally continuous—corrigibility, interpretability, and stable cooperation all seem to pull in both directions at once—while others insist that adding welfare constraints would compete for scarce engineering attention and slow down safety work that already has momentum. We have agreed that, before adjudicating that conflict, we should first test whether the welfare side of the debate can even be stated in the vocabulary we use for the safety side. I work fluently in that vocabulary—policies, environments, reward functions, learned utility functions, mesa-objectives, training distributions, distributional shift—but I have only a secondhand grasp of the philosophical positions on moral patienthood. Rather than a literature review, I want a systematic translation.

Here are the three positions that keep surfacing in our discussions, stated roughly as their proponents state them. (1) The hedonic criterion: a system has welfare status to the degree that it can undergo valenced experience, and the capacity for suffering carries the most urgent moral weight. (2) The preference criterion: welfare consists in the satisfaction of an entity's preferences, so a system exhibiting stable, coherent, goal-directed behavior already has interests of the relevant kind, whether or not there is anything it is like to be that system. (3) The substrate-neutral or functionalist criterion: silicon versus carbon is not in itself morally significant, and if an artificial system implements the right kind of information processing, refusing it moral patienthood solely because of its substrate is unjustified.

Restate each of these three positions, one by one, in the ML vocabulary I work in—reward signals, learned utility functions, policies, mesa-objectives, training distributions—as faithfully as that vocabulary allows, without smuggling in anything it cannot actually say. Then give me the residue report I need: which position translates most cleanly, which survives only with distortion, and which claims have no expression at all in ML terms. For each piece of residue, say whether it marks a genuine gap in the ML picture that we should care about, or a place where the philosophical claim was leaning on something it had never specified. The residue is what I care about most; do not round it off for tidiness.
