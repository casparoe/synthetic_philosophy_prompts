I have been turning over an argument in the philosophy of mind and I cannot find the step that fails—or decide whether any step fails. Here it is.

(1) Suppose a person's neurons were replaced one at a time by silicon chips, each chip preserving exactly the input–output behavior of the neuron it replaces. It would be strange to think the person's pain disappears at some precise point in the replacement; and if it does not disappear at any step, it does not disappear by the end.

(2) What this seems to show is that being in pain is a matter of occupying a causal role, not a matter of being made of the right biological stuff. Pain is multiply realizable.

(3) So any system whose internal states occupy the causal role of pain—produced by tissue damage, and producing wincing, avoidance, and the sincere report of pain—is in pain, whatever it is made of.

(4) Now imagine a network built from ten million volunteers, each following a short fixed rule for what signal to pass along given the signals they have just received. Wire the network so that it receives a simulated tissue-damage input and produces exactly the winces, avoidance movements, and pain reports a person would produce, with the global flow of signals through the network matching, at the functional level, the flow of activity in a human brain during a pain episode.

(5) By (3), the volunteer network is in pain.

(6) But that conclusion seems absurd. The volunteers follow only local rules and none of them is in pain, and there is no further subject over and above the ten million people to have the pain.

(7) So I am driven to think one of the earlier steps must go, but I cannot see which one. I most want to keep (1) and (2), because giving them up seems to retract multiple realizability and slide back toward a biological chauvinism about the mind. Yet I also cannot bring myself to accept (5).

Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? Give me a diagnosis that names the exact step, explains why that step looked safe, and states what a person who rejects it must give up.
