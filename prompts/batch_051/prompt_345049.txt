I have been trying to get my students to use a clear definition of a religious establishment, and I keep running into the same case. The criteria I give them are these: a state has an established church to the extent that (i) it affords a particular church an official status and privileges it denies other religious bodies; (ii) it uses the tax system to raise revenue for religious organizations; (iii) religious officials hold seats in the ordinary organs of government by virtue of their ecclesiastical office; and (iv) the state itself organizes religious worship or confessional instruction within public institutions.

Now test these criteria against the Federal Republic of Germany. The constitutional provisions inherited from the Weimar Republic say that no state church exists, and no bishop sits in the Bundestag or Bundesrat by virtue of ecclesiastical office. But the state collects a church tax: a surcharge on the income tax of registered members of the Catholic and main Protestant churches, which the state transfers to the churches; a member can stop paying it only by formally declaring withdrawal from the church to the civil authorities, not simply by declining to contribute. Public schools in many Länder teach confessional religious instruction as a regular subject, organized in cooperation with the churches, with an opt-out for pupils and parents rather than an opt-in. Germany is plainly not England, but it is also not the United States or France.

Is Germany an instance of establishment under the four criteria? Go through them one at a time and say exactly where the case falls. Where the criteria speak clearly, say so; where the overall answer comes out muddy, say whether that exposes a defect in the criteria. In particular, I want to know whether my list has smuggled in a model of separationism and called it secularism — that is, whether a state could fail these criteria and still be secular in a way the definition cannot register, or satisfy them while still being arguably secular, and which criterion would have to change in response.
