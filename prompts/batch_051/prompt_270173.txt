Here is a parallel I cannot decide about. The Letter Spirit project (a cognitive-science project by Douglas Hofstadter, Gary McGraw, and John Rehling) models typographic style with gridfonts: typefaces built on a limited grid. It treats a letterform such as 'a' not as one fixed shape but as a role in a style space. Different gridfonts realize the same letter because they play the same structural role relative to the other letterforms in that font; the letter's identity is role-based rather than shape-based. This looks to me like functionalism in philosophy of mind: a mental state is individuated by its causal role rather than by the physical state that realizes it, and multiple realizability says the same role can be implemented in many substrates. In the typographic case, the abstract role 'a' is realizable in many gridfonts while remaining the same letter. Is this a real structural identity or just a loose analogy?
