I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a visting philosopher who's been brought in to defend a revised conditional analysis of dispositions. His line is that the simple analysis — roughly, a glass is fragile iff it would shatter if struck — is basically right, and that the standard counterexamples (finks and masks) can be absorbed with the right antecedent rather than by switching to a proportions account or a primitivist/potentiality view. I need eight to ten questions that push past the usual "just restate your view" stuff.

At minimum I want:

- one question that puts the finkish case front and center (where the object loses the disposition exactly when the stimulus occurs), including the move of holding the causal basis fixed through the stimulus, and a follow-up keyed to his likely answer that this just stipulates away the fink;
- one on masks, especially the antidote-type cases you see in Bird where the disposition stays put but the manifestation is blocked, and a follow-up pressing whether "no maskers present" can be spelled out non-circularly;
- one on the multiple-manifestation or multi-track problem (fragility can show up as many different breaking patterns depending on the details of the strike), and on whether his revised antecedent has to quietly pack in an enormous amount of gerymandered detail;
- one on whether finks and masks are really two different problems or just two symptoms of the same underlying weakness in any conditional analysis.

Also work in the lingering question of the day: whether the conditional analysis is answering a semantic question about the word "disposed" or a metaphysical question about the nature of dispositions themselves, and whether he can have both. For each main question include a follow-up aimed at the reply he actually gives in his book, not a softball.

Can you make the questions feel cumulative, so that by question eight or nine the audience sees why people keep leaving conditional analyses for proportion accounts or potentiality accounts? Write the answer in American English.
