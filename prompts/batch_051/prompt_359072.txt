Two members of a research lab have argued for months about how to describe the behavior of a large, intermittently reward-retrained model whose designers can no longer derive its behavior from the architecture and training history alone. One holds a broadly Dennettian view: taking the intentional stance toward the system is not merely a provisional convenience, because the beliefs-and-desires description tracks real patterns — predictive, compressible regularities in the model's behavior — that are not identical to any pattern visible from the physical or design stance, and in at least some cases cannot be restated in design-stance terms without losing predictive purchase. The other holds that the intentional stance is only a useful heuristic for bounded minds: the patterns it picks up are compressed approximations of design-stance facts, and as our models improve the intentional vocabulary should be eliminated from serious explanation rather than treated as carving nature at a joint. Write the joint statement this pair should sign after good-faith adversarial collaboration. Include a numbered list of claims both can accept about what it takes for a pattern to be real, about what each stance buys in the model case, and about where each camp's view would be falsified if the other side were right; then a table of residual disagreements stating each camp's position in wording its own side would accept, the crux that would settle it, and for each crux a concrete observation or intervention on the model that both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where some of the dispute is purely verbal, say which parts and why.
