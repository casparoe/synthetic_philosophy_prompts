Consider a consortium of research laboratories working with a class of powerful dual-use tools. The labs all agree that a tool should be released only when the consortium as a whole would regard the expected benefits as exceeding the expected harms. But each lab still has the technical capability and legal right to release a tool on its own, and the labs' estimates of benefit and harm differ substantially. The consortium's skeptics point out that this arrangement produces the unilateralist's curse: because any single lab can act, the lab with the most optimistic estimate tends to determine what actually gets released, even when the median lab would object.

To remove the curse without creating a centralized gatekeeper, the consortium adopts a standing rule. In each calendar quarter, and within each category of sensitive release — papers, methods, datasets — one member lab is selected by lottery as the designated releaser for that quarter. The lottery uses publicly verifiable randomness, with each lab's probability of selection proportional to its prior-year contribution to the common fund. The designee, and only the designee, may release in that category during that quarter. If the designee declines, the category stays closed for the quarter: there is no redraw and no substitute. A designee may release only after publishing a written rationale, waiting thirty days, and holding a public question-and-answer session; it needs no vote of the others and no sign-off.

Walk through how this rule would actually behave. Compare the releases it produces with the open-unilateral status quo and with a system of simple majority votes. Then identify the gaming, including manipulation of category definitions, the contribution weighting, strategic declining, lobbying of designees, side channels outside the categories, and efforts to time a sympathetic designee's quarter. Does the rule eliminate the unilateralist's curse or merely reschedule it? What are the main second-order effects on precaution, on who joins the consortium, on the quality of the written rationales, and on work that moves outside the consortium entirely? Finally, state which failure modes could be fixed by adjusting the rule, and which fixes would quietly reintroduce the very curse the rule was meant to break.
