I'm a science journalist preparing a long-form piece on the search for extraterrestrial intelligence, and I keep noticing a configuration that I do not have precise vocabulary for. In conversations about why we have seen no sign of civilizations elsewhere, people tend to reach for one of two frames. Some treat the silence as a brute question—"Where is everybody?"—and then layer on explanations such as self-destruction, non-communicative civilizations, or an empty galaxy. Others invoke the Drake equation, which multiplies a series of probabilities to estimate the number of detectable civilizations, but that equation is usually treated as a heuristic for plugging in guesses rather than as a claim about where, in the sequence, the genuinely difficult step sits.

The configuration I mean is more specific. One can imagine a chain of transitions leading from prebiotic chemistry to self-replicating molecules, to simple single-celled life, to complex multicellular life, to intelligence of a tool-using sort, and finally to a technological civilization detectable across interstellar distances. Since we exist, at least one path through the early transitions must have occurred on Earth. But the observed silence could be explained by supposing that at least one transition in that chain has a very low probability—not zero, but low enough that very few biospheres ever pass it. On this view, there is no need to assume that civilizations inevitably destroy themselves or that the galaxy is empty; the silence simply marks the step, or steps, at which the process usually stalls. The troubling twist is that the hard step could be behind us, or it could lie ahead of us.

I know this is adjacent to the Fermi paradox and the Drake equation, but I want to avoid the loose version of each. I am after the established term, if there is one, for the specific idea that at least one transition in the sequence has low probability, and that the location of that difficult step relative to our own stage determines whether the silence is reassuring or ominous. Does this configuration have a name of its own? How exactly does it relate to the Drake equation, which also multiplies transition probabilities, and to the Fermi paradox as a broad question? If there is no established name, what would you call it, and where would you draw the line between this idea and a merely pessimistic reading of the Drake equation?
