I am a novelist drafting a near-future novel, and I want the book's social world to be causally credible rather than a wish-fulfilment utopia or a cautionary dystopia. The novel turns on a single intervention, and I would like you to reason through what it does over two decades. I am asking for the causal scaffolding, not a story or a plot.

The premise: in 2031, Meridia — a middle-income parliamentary democracy of forty million people, with a large informal sector and strong extended-family norms — introduces an unconditional basic income for every legal adult resident. The payment is set at half the median individual monthly income, indexed to inflation, and is financed by a broad-based consumption tax, a land-value tax, and the elimination of nearly all means-tested cash transfers. The national minimum wage is repealed at the same time. Alongside the cash transfer, the government launches a voluntary Civic Ledger: a public register in which residents may record hours of unpaid care, community organising, creative and open-source work, and environmental stewardship. Entries must be peer-verified, and verified participation accrues publicly visible standing markers — visible to employers, lenders, landlords, and neighbours — that cannot be bought or converted into money.

I want a reasoned prediction of how Meridia looks in 2051, built from mechanisms rather than slogans. Please work through, at minimum: labour-market behaviour and bargaining power; who exits paid work, who cannot, and what that does to wages and working conditions; the care economy and its gender distribution; status hierarchies once income and employment are partially decoupled from standing; what the Civic Ledger plausibly becomes in practice, including gaming, credentialism, and stigma; likely price and fiscal effects; and the political coalitions that would defend or dismantle the whole arrangement. For each major prediction, say whether it is a robust bet given the incentives or a fragile guess, and name the hidden assumption whose failure would most change your answer. Avoid the familiar pro- and anti-UBI talking points except where they feed a specific mechanism.

Hard limit: 1,000 words.
