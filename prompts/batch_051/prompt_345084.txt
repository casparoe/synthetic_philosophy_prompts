Before I read further in the theory of incommensurable values, I want an explicit statement of what an account of hard choices must actually deliver. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of values that cannot be ranked on a single scale must satisfy, with the consideration or motivating case behind each. Among the things I have in mind, but not as an exhaustive list: distinguishing a hard choice from indifference, ignorance, equal value, and mere incompleteness of preference; making sense of parity, the relation in which two alternatives are comparable in value yet neither is better than the other, and deciding whether it is a genuine value relation or an artifact of vagueness; explaining how a choice can be rational without being the selection of a best option, and what kind of post-choice regret is intelligible after such a choice; and accounting for the way such choices constitute or revise the self rather than merely satisfying it. For each desideratum, mark which are non-negotiable and which are optional commitments of particular camps. Finally, flag any subset of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate views.
