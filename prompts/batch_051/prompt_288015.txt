Here's an asymmetry I keep noticing. If a physicist says she understands quantum mechanics but can't explain any of it to a smart layperson, I take that as evidence she doesn't understand it as well as she thinks. But if a mystic says her deepest knowledge can't be put into words, a lot of people hear that as a sign that the knowledge is deeper than ordinary knowledge, not shallower. Both are claiming to know something they can't say. What principle makes the first a failure of understanding and the second a mark of depth? Build the strongest distinction you can — maybe about the kind of subject matter, or about whether the inability belongs to the knower or to the thing known — then test it against a hard case: someone who says they understand a particular person completely but can't tell you a single true thing about them beyond "you had to be there." Say whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or given up.
