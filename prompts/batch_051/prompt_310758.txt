OK so I want you to act as a professional philosopher working on animal minds and moral status. Think this through carefully, go criterion by criterion, and think out loud a little before you settle on a verdict. Also answer in American English.

I've been using a rough graded notion of moral status instead of a binary one. My working critera are these: (1) valenced experience — the system can be in states that feel good or bad to it, not merely detect and respond to a stimulus; (2) behavioral integration — it flexibly combines information from different sources to pursue goals, rather than running fixed reflexes; (3) homeostatic self-maintenance — it actively works to keep itself going as an organized system, correcting perturbations; (4) subjective unity — there is some point of view for whom harms land as a whole, so it isn't just a loose heap of parts.

Now here's the case that's been bugging me: a managed honeybee colony, taken as a whole. Individual bees are tiny, short-lived, and maybe only marginally sentient. But the colony as a unit regulates its temperature (bees fan when it's hot, cluster and shiver when it's cold), reallocates foragers when nectar flows change, builds and repairs comb, rears brood, and reproduces as a unit when it swarms. By criteria 2 and 3, the colony looks almost more goal-directed and self-maintaining than any single bee. But does the colony have valenced experience? It has no brain, unless you want to count the distributed network of bees as one. Is there a point of view at the colony level, or is the hive just a population of small subjects?

Compare it with a thermostat. A thermostat also maintains a setpoint against perturbation and could be described as having a dumb goal — hold the room at 68 degrees. Almost no one thinks the thermostat has moral status, because it fails criteria 1 and 2 (and 4), even though it passes a toy version of 3.

So: where does the honeybee colony as a whole fall on the graded scale from, say, a human adult down to a single honeybee down to a thermostat? Is the colony a separate locus of moral status, partly independent of the status of the individual bees in it, or is treating the hive as a patient just a confusion of levels? Work through each of my four criteria, flag where they underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether this case shows my criteria need changing — for example, maybe criteria 2 and 3 let in too many mechanical systems, and valenced experience is doing all the real work. Don't be afraid to say I should drop or reweight something.
