Hi — for our lab's submission to the AI safety workshop I need you to draft a short research proposal, maybe a page. The theme is impact measures, and the question I want to pitch is whether 'avoid side effects' can be defined without first having a full specification of value. You know the low-impact idea: rather than hand the system a rich utility function, you penalize it for making the world differ from some baseline, so it can complete a bounded task without messing up everything else around it. The problem is that every candidate baseline seems to do moral work under the table. Is the baseline the world as it would have been if the agent was never switched on? The world some default policy would have produced? The world with only 'task-relevant' features changed? Each choice assumes a view about which differences are the side effects, and its not clear the notion can be made value-neutral at all. The obvious existing moves—no-op baseline, default-policy baseline, purely information-theoretic deviation penalties—either smuggle in a model of what matters or get so formal they stop tracking actual impact.

Write the one-page proposal with these parts: a precise statement of the question; why those obvious answers dont settle it; how a philosophical project rather than an engineering project could make progress (I keep thinking this is secretly a problem about actualism vs. possibilism in ethics, or about how we even partition an outcome into 'the task' and 'the side effects', but you can push on that); what a useful deliverable would look like for someone actually designing low-impact systems; what a good result looks like and what counts as failure (e.g., ending up admitting every workable measure needs a value function somewhere after all); what we'd say to a reviewer who thinks this is just ML, not philosophy; and a scope that is honest for a six-month project rather than a dissertation-sized promise.
