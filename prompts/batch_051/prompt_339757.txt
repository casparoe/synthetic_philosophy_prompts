In my ancient philosophy unit, we read a defense of natural slavery that compares the slave to a tool that is, in a way, alive, and compares the master's rule over the slave to the soul's rule over the body. The conclusion drawn is that, for some people, being ruled is natural and beneficial. I want to know how far these analogies actually carry that conclusion. Could you lay out the mapping in each case—what in the tool or the body is supposed to correspond to what in the slave, and what in the craftsman or soul to what in the master—then identify the points where each analogy fails, distinguish the failures that matter for the argument from those that do not, and tell me whether the conclusion survives once the serious disanalogies are on the table? If a better analogy or a different kind of argument would be needed, I would also like to know what it would be.
