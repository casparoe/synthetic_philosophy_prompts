Give me a working definition of an intervention, in the technical sense required by the interventionist account of causation associated with James Woodward, that gets the following cases right. The definition should, if possible, avoid presupposing the very causal notions it is supposed to anchor. The familiar circularity worry is that an intervention is ordinarily characterized as a causal process that changes a target variable while screening it off from its other causes; but causal process and screening off are already causal ideas, so the analysis of causation seems to borrow what it promised to supply.

Here are the cases. I regard the first three as clear cases of intervention, the next three as clear non-cases, and the final six as cases on which I genuinely have no settled view.

1. Flipping a correctly wired wall switch to turn on a lamp.
2. Assigning participants in a clinical trial to treatment or control by a random coin flip.
3. Deleting a specific gene in a mouse line in order to observe the effect on a measurable behavior.
4. A rooster crowing shortly before sunrise.
5. A barometer needle falling before a storm arrives.
6. Merely recording a patient's temperature in a chart, without otherwise altering anything.
7. A central bank raising its policy interest rate with the aim of bringing down inflation.
8. A gust of wind shaking a tree branch and knocking a ripe fruit loose.
9. A physician giving a placebo pill to a patient who reports chronic pain.
10. Pressing a button that, unbeknownst to you, is no longer wired to the light it was meant to control.
11. Puréeing vegetables into a child's pasta sauce, without the child's knowledge, so that the child consumes more vegetables.
12. Praying for a friend's recovery from illness.

Propose the definition, run it through all twelve cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer at all. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. In particular, say whether the definition you end up with has actually escaped the circularity worry, or whether it has merely relocated that worry.
