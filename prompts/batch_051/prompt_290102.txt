Last weekend I was in the back seat while my brother Marco and his friend Priya got into a long argument about her sister's IVF situation, and I still can't sort out who was right about what. Priya's sister had a batch of embryos frozen, couldnt use them all, and donated one to another couple. Priya said the kid born from that embryo is the same person that would have existed if her sister had used it herself, because the child came from the same sperm and egg, and that's what makes you the particular person you are. Marco said that can't be the whole story, because identical twins come from the same sperm and egg and they're not the same person. Priya said twins are a special case: the fertilized egg starts out as one thing and then splits, and neither twin was ever the whole original embryo, so the rule still holds for the ordinary one-person case. Marco said then the rule is just "same person whenever nothing weird happens," which is not really a rule. Anyways, he added, a frozen embryo that gets transferred into a different womb and grows up with different parents in a different family isn't just getting a different environment, it's becoming a different person, because who you are is partly made by the life you actually live. Priya said he was confusing being a person with having a personality: a switched-at-birth baby is still the same individual, just with a different history, so the same should go for a one-cell embryo. Marco said a switched baby already exists as somebody before it's switched, whereas an embryo donated before implantation has no history yet, so there's nothing to be "the same" as except a bare cell. Priya said that's exactly her point: the embryo is the earliest stage of the person, like an acorn to an oak, and you'd have to be crazy to call it a different tree because it was transplanted as a seed. Marco said an acorn can't be more than one oak, but an embryo can be more than one person, so the analogy breaks right where it matters.

I honestly couldn't referee it. Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, where each of them goes wrong, and what both of them are missing? Please don't just pick a winner; weigh each step.
