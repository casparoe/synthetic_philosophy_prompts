Anti-discrimination law in most countries singles out a short list of traits for special protection. In U.S. equal protection doctrine, classifications by race or national origin count as suspect and trigger strict scrutiny; sex receives intermediate scrutiny; age, disability, and poverty receive only rational-basis review. In the U.K., the Equality Act 2010 protects age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation. Yet employers and legislatures remain free to sort people by height, accent, body weight, or devotion to a football club. What theory of what makes a ground of discrimination suspect does this pattern presuppose — and is it one theory at all, or a political patchwork? Reconstruct the implicit theory or theories, separating what the pattern strictly requires from what it merely happens to fit. Then say what would have to change if the implicit rationale were replaced by a consistently anti-subordination principle, on which a trait warrants protection exactly when discrimination against it helps keep a group in a subordinate social position. Which currently protected traits would lose their claim under that test, and which currently unprotected traits, such as accent or body weight, would gain one?
