I have spent the past month untangling a metrics failure at my organization: the customer-satisfaction score we tied bonuses to improved for five straight quarters while retention fell in every one of them, because the score had been gamed harder each year. This has made the problem of choosing measurable stand-ins for difficult-to-measure goals abruptly personal. I do not want another dashboard fix, and I do not want a literature review or references. I want the standard of evaluation itself.

Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of proxy selection must meet. The theory should explain how to choose a measurable target that stands in for a difficult-to-measure goal, and how to use it without the measure becoming the goal to the point of consuming the goal. Give, for each desideratum, the consideration or case that forces it.

In your list, do the following.

First, distinguish what is non-negotiable in any adequate theory from what is merely desirable, or a preference of a particular camp—for instance, a preference for cheap measurement, for interpretability, or for single rather than multiple proxies.

Second, say which distinctions the theory must respect and which of my candidate distinctions are red herrings. The candidates I have in mind are: gaming a proxy versus genuinely improving a neglected component of the goal; a proxy that predicts the goal in the data one currently has versus one that remains predictive once agents respond to it; validity of a proxy on average versus validity at the margin, since optimization moves at the margin; and drift under moderate pressure versus divergence under strong optimization. Tell me which of these are required, which are not sharp, and which distinctions I am missing.

Third, flag any pairs of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied. I especially want to know whether cheap measurability, legibility to the people being measured, and robustness to deliberate optimization can plausibly be had together, and whether something like a generic limit—for example, that any finite set of measurable proxies eventually diverges from its target under sufficiently strong optimization—forces a choice between robustness and some other desideratum. If the list itself encodes an impossibility result, that is the part I most want.

Throughout, let the desiderata do the work of getting the canonical failure cases right: the handle-time target that trains staff to shed difficult calls, the publication-based hiring criterion that breeds self-citation, the training reward that is hacked rather than satisfied. For each such case, I want it to be clear which requirement does the explanatory work and what respecting that requirement would have ruled out in advance.

Finally, in no more than a third of the response, indicate briefly how the approaches I am currently weighing—audited targets, multi-proxy portfolios, and adversarial testing—would fare against your first few criteria, and say which criterion does the most work in eliminating candidates. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on this scorecard.
