ok so this is a bit raw, sorry. my partner and i both had genuinely awful months last fall, and at some point we had the stupid fight about whose was worse. i caught myself saying "mine was worse" and meaning something weird by it. i wasnt actually comparing amounts of suffering at all. what i was doing was running a swap: if i could trade my month for hers—her body, her job, her history, her anxiety—would i? the answer was no, id keep my own, and from that i concluded hers was worse. she said she was doing the same thing in reverse and would keep her own too. so by my own method we both ended up worse off than each other, which obviously cant be right. but i also couldnt shake the feeling that the swap question is the only real content the comparison has.

so ive been trying to turn this into a little theory and i want it checked. working name: swap-comparativism. the claim is that a cross-person statement like "A is worse off than B" only means something if it can be cashed out as a swap judgment—roughly, whether A, using A's own preferences and standards, would choose to trade situations with B, and/or whether B would trade with A. if exactly one would swap, we say the one who would swap is worse off. if neither would, theyre incommensurable. and if both would, the comparison is just broken and we shouldnt make one. i keep going back and forth on whether "trading" means i keep my own preferences and just get her circumstances, or i become her in the full sense—take her preferences too. it changes the answers a lot.

i tried it on other cases. with my mom and my uncle arguing about who gave up more for their parents, it felt clarifying: instead of pretending to weigh their sacrifices on one shared scale, you ask each whether theyd trade. but in triage-type cases it felt worse—i dont know a stranger well enough to know what theyd choose, so i end up saying almost all comparisons between strangers are incommensurable, which feels like giving up exactly when the comparison matters most.

is this already a thing under a different name? it feels too obvious not to be, but i couldnt place it. and is the both-would-swap case as fatal as it looks, or can you fix it by adding something, like asking which swap theyd fight harder to make, or how much theyd pay to swap? please dont do the thing where you lay out five options and leave me to pick—commit to a position, tell me what you think this actually is, what it gets right, whether the core idea survives, and where it definately breaks. and make the last line of your reply the verdict, as \answer{...} with a short label for the view, or \answer{unworkable} if thats your call.
