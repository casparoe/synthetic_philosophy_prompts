I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have been stuck on an analogy I cannot adjudicate. In The World as Will and Representation, volume one, section 52, Schopenhauer argues — I paraphrase — that music is unlike the other arts: it does not copy the Platonic Ideas, the grades of the will's objectification, but instead presents the will itself directly. He then maps the parts of music onto those grades: the bass corresponds to inorganic nature, the lowest level; the harmony to the Ideas, the intermediate levels; and the melody to the highest grade, the conscious strivings of the human will. Now I keep noticing a structural echo in the way modern hierarchical models in cognitive science and machine learning describe complex systems: low-level features are cheap and ubiquitous, mid-level features organize them into regularities, and high-level features correspond to specific, goal-directed trajectories. In both cases, a single underlying principle is supposed to generate a graded hierarchy, and the hierarchy is supposed to be explanatory. Is this parallel deep or superficial? In particular, does Schopenhauer's tripartite mapping of music onto the will's objectification do real explanatory work, or is it a suggestive metaphor that happens to share a shape with familiar computational hierarchies?
