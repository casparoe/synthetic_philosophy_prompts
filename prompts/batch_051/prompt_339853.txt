Last night my roommate and I got into one of those arguments that starts over dinner and somehow eats the whole evening, about whether petitionary prayer makes any sense if you think God is omniscient and immutable. His line was that either my view is incoherent or I'm secretly watering it down to 'prayer is just meditation.' I dont think I am, but by midnight I couldn't say exactly where the strain was. I want to write down the package I actually believe and have someone pick it apart, because I'm supposed to lead a discussion on this in a few weeks and I'd rather not get ambushed.

Here is the set I'm working with:

(a) God is omniscient, so he already knows what I need and what I'll ask before I ask; prayer adds no information.

(b) God is immutable—his will and his plan never change. So prayer can't change God's mind or get him to do something he wasn't already going to do.

(c) Petitionary prayer is still something we should do, mainly because it's commanded, and because it does real work on us: it trains our desires, reminds us we're dependent, and aligns our will with God's.

(d) But I also think it's legitimate to pray for specific, concrete outcomes—a friend's surgery, a job, a child's fever breaking—and to think that God might answer those prayers, not merely that the praying makes me feel differently.

(e) I'd go further: there are cases where if I hadn't prayed, the outcome would have been different. Prayer can be a genuine cause in the world, like a friend's warning can be a genuine cause of me avoiding an accident, even though God foreknows the whole thing.

(f) And yet I also think treating prayer as bargaining or as a technique that reliably extracts results is a corruption—that's magic, not prayer.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only seem to be. Rank the real tensions by how severe they are, and for each say what would have to give and what it would cost. Then take a stand: if you had my list, which single revision would you make, and why that one? I'm not looking for a survey of options—I want a committed audit, even if your fix means I have to give something up I'd rather keep.
