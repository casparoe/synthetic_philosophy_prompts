I'm a novelist, so I spend a lot of time thinking about why people say what they say and how much they're on the hook for. Years ago I settled into a rule about asserting things: I'll state something flat out only if I know it. If I merely believe it, or it's just my best guess, I hedge—"I think so," "I'm pretty sure," "last I heard." The rule has even crept into my characters: the ones I write sympathetically never claim what they don't know. But lately I'm unsure I've got the standard right. Why should knowledge be the bar for assertion, rather than truth, or just sincere belief? And the habit backfires in practice too: last week someone asked whether a friend was back in town, and I said "I believe she got in Tuesday," when I'd actually run into her that morning and knew it. The hedging made me sound less sure about something I knew. Is this a defensible epistemic habit, or just a writer's tic dressed up as a principle?
