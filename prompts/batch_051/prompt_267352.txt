My editor asked me to do a piece on Maxwell's demon for a general audience, and I've been reading around. One popular physics article I found sums it up like this: 'Maxwell's demon is a thought experiment proving that information can be converted into work for free. The demon sits by a door between two chambers of gas and sorts molecules by speed, letting fast ones through one way and slow ones the other, creating a temperature difference from an initially uniform gas, which violates the second law of thermodynamics. Maxwell's point was that the second law is only statistical and a clever enough being could beat it. Later Landauer and Bennett saved the second law by showing that the demon's memory has to be reset, and erasing a bit inevitably costs energy, so information is physical and the demon always loses in the end.' I have a feeling this is the standard story but maybe a little too pat. Can you audit that summary sentence by sentence for me: which parts a physicist working on foundations of statistical mechanics would accept, which parts they'd wince at, and what the demon does and doesn't show about whether information can be turned into work? I want to avoid putting a subtle falsehood in my piece.
