I've noticed two very different ways people "let someone off the hook" after a small wrong, and I keep wanting a name for the difference. When a friend snaps at me and then explains she'd slept three hours, my anger mostly dissolves, but I still treat her as the same kind of creature — I just see the action as not really hers. But when a neighbor's son keys my car and it turns out he's in the middle of a psychotic episode, the anger doesn't dissolve; it gets switched off. I start treating him like a problem to be managed, the way I'd treat a storm, not like someone to be angry at. And here's the part that bothers me: a week later I catch myself scolding him for leaving his bike in the driveway, even though I'd supposedly stopped seeing him as blameable. Is there an established name for these two kinds of excusing, and for that in-between state where you've stopped blaming someone but still hold them to the usual expectations? I don't just want a label — if the nearest concepts only half-fit, I'd like to know where their edges are.
