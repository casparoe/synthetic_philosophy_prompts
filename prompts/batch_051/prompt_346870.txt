Construct a thought experiment in the theory of consumer choice that meets all of the following specifications. A single consumer, with stable underlying tastes, chooses among ordinary market goods in two realistic purchase episodes. In the first she chooses option x while option y is available; in the second she chooses y while x is available. As standardly applied, the pair violates the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference: the standard revealed-preference reading treats the options as the same goods across the two episodes despite any differences in presentation, and concludes that no single utility function over those goods rationalizes the choices. A description-sensitive or context-sensitive theory of choice draws the opposite conclusion: the consumer is maximizing one stable utility function, because the objects of choice should be individuated by how they are presented, labeled, or framed, and not by physical composition alone. Constraints: no lotteries or uncertainty; no change in the consumer's information (though what is made salient may differ), budget, or underlying tastes; no prices acting as signals of quality; no asymmetrically dominated decoy option; and a scenario that reads like ordinary shopping rather than a laboratory trick.

Then do the following. First, present the two choice sets and show step by step where the Weak Axiom is violated and exactly what the standard reading concludes. Second, state the description-sensitive utility function that rationalizes both choices, and explain why the re-description is not ad hoc: give an independent reason, such as something the consumer would pay to control, for treating the contextual feature as part of the choice object. Third, identify the single feature of the case that drives the two theories apart, and say what would happen if that feature were removed — would the choices become an ordinary WARP-compliant pair, or would the consumer simply look inconsistent? Finally, give the strongest objection a committed revealed-preference theorist would raise to your scenario, and state what modification to the case would answer it.
