I work in game-integrity at a mid-sized studio running a small MMO with a player-run market, and every week we flag maybe a hundred accounts as possible bots or scripters. We have to decide from server logs alone whether to ban them and what detection rules to add, and we never get to see the code. Most of the flagged accounts aren't obvious. They're optimisers of some kind — they clearly want something and they clearly search a space of moves — but we can't tell what the 'something' is, or how wide the menu of moves they considered actually was.

The principle I half-believe is that you can infer an optimiser's objective and its search space from behaviour alone: from where it goes, what it never does, how it reacts to price changes, to being followed, to having a node removed. But I can't turn that into something my investigators can actually run. I don't want the formal machine-learning version — my team won't do inverse reinforcement learning on a deadline and we don't collect the data it would need. I want a reusable procedure for a person looking at logs for about thirty minutes.

A wrong ban loses a paying player, a missed bot wrecks the economy, and we only ever see outcomes — positions, transactions, timing, chat — not the option sets the account considered. I also know the hard bit is that the same trajectory can often be explained by a dumb objective over a huge menu or a smart objective over a tiny menu, and an evasive bot is partly optimising to look human, so its observed objective might be a cover. The procedure has to flag when two objective/menu pairs are indistiguishable from our logs rather than pretending to decide.

Please structure the answer as: (1) Summary — the bottom line of the procedure in about 80 words, since that's all my team will read on a bad day; (2) The procedure — a numbered list of questions to ask in order, around 600 words, with what counts as a good-enough answer at each step and the exact stop conditions (including 'no call'); (3) Two worked examples — run the procedure on (a) a market bot that always undercuts the lowest sell order by a fixed percentage, and (b) a gathering bot that moves in fuel-efficient loops but occasionally breaks off to visit a remote node for no obvious gain; about 250 words each; (4) Where it breaks — the cases the procedure gets wrong or can't settle, plus which of its steps are really judgement calls dressed up as evidence, about 150 words.

And please write the whole thing in British English.
