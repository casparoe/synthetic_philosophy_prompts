I keep seeing people explain why some fields advance faster than others with one sentence — "they have clear feedback" — and then move on. I'm tired of it. Before I accept any actual theory of feedback and progress, I want the full spec: what does an adequate theory in this area have to get right?

Give me a requirements list. The cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the empirical regularities it can't contradict — and for each one, why it's a requirement rather than just a preference. Then flag which requirements are negotiable and which aren't, and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular versions of the "clear feedback causes progress" story. A table is fine.

Here's the stuff I can't get out of my head that any theory has to deal with (use these or replace them with better cases):
- weather forecasting improved fast with more data and tighter feedback loops; macroeconomics has enormous data and still can't agree on basics. What exactly breaks the symmetry?
- medicine had extremely direct feedback for centuries — the patient recovered or the patient died — and by modern standards it barely improved. So "feedback exists" can't be the whole story; something about attribution, aggregation, or use has to carry the weight.
- math has no empirical feedback at all and progresses fine. So the theory has to allow internal feedback (proof, consistency checks) or it's dead.
- finance has about as clean quantitative feedback as you can get — profit and loss — yet active management and technical analysis survive on weak evidence. Why doesn't clear feedback kill bad practice there?
- a surgeon can get much better from private experience without the field learning anything. So the theory has to seperate individual skill from public knowledge, or it's tracking the wrong thing.

Also tell me whether "feedback" and "progress" even attach to whole fields, or whether that's a category mistake — maybe feedback attaches to a specific prediction or action, and "a field with clear feedback" is a lazy summary. If a theory can't say what unit bears the feedback and what "progress" is a progress of, I don't think it counts as a theory.
