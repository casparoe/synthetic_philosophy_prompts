I spent my career in population genetics, mostly modeling how a slight selective advantage can fix a variant in a few hundred generations. Now that I'm retired and reading around in the cultural-evolution literature, I keep tripping over the word "meme." People use it as if the unit is obvious, but when I press on it, it slides around: sometimes a tune, sometimes a word, sometimes a whole practice, sometimes just an idea that happens to be popular. I'd like a working defintion of "meme" in the Darwinian-culture sense (not the internet image sense) that actually does classificatory work.

I want it to clearly count (a) the melody of a lullaby, (b) the convention of shaking hands, and (c) the word "OK." I want it to clearly not count (d) a yawn moving through a tired room. And I want a definite answer either way for each of the following, even if the honest answer takes the form "the definition cannot decide this, and here is what it would need": (e) a sourdough recipe passed through a family and tweaked each generation; (f) a bird's alarm call that chicks learn from parents; (g) a cat that figures out on its own how to open a door; (h) the lab convention of writing dates year-month-day; (i) a chain letter promising bad luck; (j) the belief that you should drink eight glasses of water a day. Some of these are genuinely hard for me—I suspect the verdicts will turn on whether the definition is built around copying fidelity, social transmission, or being a unit of selection, and I'd like you to say which criterion is doing the work, where the definition strains, and whether you'd revise it or just bite the bullet on the surprising verdicts.

Please structure your answer in five titled sections: (1) Proposed definition (one or two sentences); (2) Running the cases (a short paragraph per case, with a clear verdict—meme or not—and a sentence of reasoning; if a case forces the definition into a no-answer corner, say so explicitly); (3) Where the definition strains (two to four specific problems, named); (4) Revision or defense (either revise the definition or defend the weird verdicts, in a paragraph or two); (5) Bottom line (the definition I should actually keep, in one or two sentences).
