I'm in a metaphysics of science course and we've been stuck on laws of nature for two weeks. The position I keep getting pulled toward is the best-system account: the world is just the mosaic of local particular facts, and the laws are nothing extra—they're the axioms of whichever true systematization of that mosaic best balances simplicity and strength. No necessitation relation between universals, no governing glue, no primitive modal facts. I like how little it asks me to believe, and I like that knowing the laws just is finding patterns in ordinary experience.

Two things keep pulling me the other way. First, I can imagine two worlds with exactly the same mosaic—every event and quality matches—but in one world a certain regularity is a law and in the other it's a cosmic coincidence that the pattern happens to hold. That feels like a real difference, not merely a difference in how we chose to describe things. Second, once lawhood is relative to which predicates are available for the systematization—and simplicity seems relative to the choice of predicates—I worry the whole account stops being about the world at all. Someone could gerrymander a predicate and get a stupid regularity to come out as a law just because their language makes it simple. But I also dont want to quietly reintroduce law-primitives or necessitation relations just because I had a feeling, since that seems to throw away exactly the clean worldview I liked.

So what should yield here—the theory or the intuition? Give me the strongest version of both sides, and say what would actually justify each possible move: debunking the intuition as some kind of cognitive illusion, amending the theory or going fully anti-Humean, or finding a reading on which they're compatible. I'd rather know what I'd have to sacrifice than just be told which side to pick.
