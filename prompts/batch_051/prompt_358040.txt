I'm a software engineer working on a group decision component for an app that helps people in shared housing make collective choices. The component takes in each member's ranking of the available options and outputs a single group ranking or one choice. I keep wanting the component to satisfy three reasonable requirements at the same time:

1. Unanimity: if every member ranks option X above option Y, the group ranking places X above Y.
2. Minimal private sovereignty: each member gets to decide, on their own, options that are entirely a private matter for them, such as whether they themselves read a particular book. If two options differ only in what one member does with their own private sphere, the group ranking should agree with that member's ranking of the two.
3. Consistency: the group ranking is transitive, so it never produces a cycle like X beats Y, Y beats Z, and Z beats X.

I was given a neat example that seems to show these three requirements conflict, using two members. A prude and a libertine share one copy of a scandalous novel, and there are three possible outcomes: the prude reads it, the libertine reads it, or nobody reads it. The prude's ranking is: nobody reads it > the prude reads it > the libertine reads it. The libertine's ranking is: the prude reads it > the libertine reads it > nobody reads it.

Requirement 2 gives the prude his say over the pair consisting of the prude reads it and nobody reads it, so it forces nobody reads it above the prude reads it. It gives the libertine his say over the pair consisting of the libertine reads it and nobody reads it, so it forces the libertine reads it above nobody reads it. And requirement 1 forces the prude reads it above the libertine reads it, because both members prefer that. The result is a cycle: the prude reads it > the libertine reads it > nobody reads it > the prude reads it.

Write me an essay on which of the three requirements a sensible group decision component should give up or weaken, and whether giving it up is as bad as it first sounds. Weigh the case for each option: abandoning unanimity, abandoning or narrowly restricting private sovereignty, or tolerating inconsistent group rankings. Reach a definite conclusion about how I should build the component, and justify it. Also tell me which argument or consideration would most likely make you change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
