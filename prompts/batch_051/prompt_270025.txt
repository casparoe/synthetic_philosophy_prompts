In a committee I used to sit on, two factions with quite different priorities shared authority over two decisions: the text of a public memorial and the design of an adjacent playground. The first faction cared deeply about the memorial text and only mildly about the playground; the second cared deeply about the playground and only mildly about the memorial. They arrived at a package in which each side got the decision it cared about most and conceded the other. Neither viewed the other's central concern as unimportant — each would have preferred to win on both — and neither changed its mind about what mattered. I have since noticed the same structure in larger negotiations between groups whose underlying values seem incompatible.

Here is my puzzle. In such cases each side has an internal ranking: some matters are central to its commitments, and some are peripheral but still genuinely held. A package emerges in which each side concedes on a peripheral matter in order to win on a central one, and each side prefers the package to the status quo by the lights of its own values. Nobody is splitting any single issue down the middle, and nobody is indifferent to what they concede. The agreement is possible only because the two sides value the elements of the package differently.

I want to know whether this configuration has a recognized name. But a bare label would not satisfy me; I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is. How does it differ from an ordinary split-the-difference compromise, from a settlement in which each side merely tolerates the other because it cannot prevail, and from a consensus in which both sides happen to endorse the same policy for different reasons? And does this kind of exchange raise a distinctive moral problem — is it permissible, and under what conditions, to treat one's peripheral values as tradeable in this way in order to secure what one cares about most? If the phenomenon as I have described it does not really come apart from these other kinds of agreement, or if the idea that each side is "better off by its own lights" is confused in this setting, I would rather be told that directly than given a tidy answer.
