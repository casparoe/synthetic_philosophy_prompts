I am a novelist. The novel I am planning is set in 2085, forty years after a small, wealthy parliamentary democracy—the Republic of Veyra, a Baltic state of two million people—amended its constitution in 2045 to require that every major law, regulation, and public project be tested against a single question: does it make ordinary life more genuinely fun, rather than merely more comfortable, productive, or satisfied? I want the backstory to be causally credible, so I am asking for a reasoned prediction rather than a scenario.

The amendment creates a national Fun Index, revised quarterly by the statistical service and audited by a citizens' council, with components measured through experience-sampling surveys, phone and wristband data, and periodic in-person interviews. The components are: (1) self-reported enjoyment during the day's ordinary activities; (2) hours per week a person describes as absorbingly engaging, in the sense of losing track of time; (3) anticipation—how often people report looking forward to the next day, week, or season; (4) adult and child play, meaning unstructured activity undertaken for its own sake with others; (5) unhurried positive contact, such as meals or conversations that people rate as warm and not scheduled to a fixed endpoint; and (6) an afterglow-to-dread ratio, tracking whether people more often savor the recent past or report flatness and dread. A floor clause forbids the government from allowing any single component to fall below its 2045 baseline for two consecutive quarters. Every major measure requires a fun-impact assessment modeled on environmental impact assessment, and an independent Office of Habitability may block a measure whose predicted Index effect is manifestly adverse and not offset by expected gains elsewhere.

Assume Veyra begins with high state capacity, low corruption, excellent administrative data, and a public culture that treats constitutional compliance as a point of civic pride; the index is not rigged at the outset. Predict how Veyra looks by 2085. I care most about the causal anatomy: which kinds of law and regulation accumulate and which quietly disappear; how schools, workplaces, medicine, public space, advertising, and the arts reorganize around the index; how firms, parties, and professions learn to game it, and whether the floor clause catches the worst gaming; how status, shame, and ordinary talk about laziness, career, childrearing, and old age shift; and whether the regime changes what people actually experience or merely what they report, perform, and photograph. Mark which outcomes you regard as robust and which depend on assumptions you cannot verify, assign rough confidence to the main links, and state the conditions under which the whole prediction would fail. I would also like a short contrast with a country that set out to top the world happiness rankings instead.
