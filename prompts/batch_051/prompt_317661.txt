In my philosophy of psychiatry reading group we've spent three meetings arguing about whether psychiatric categories like depression and ADHD are real kinds or are socially constructed, and the argument has completely stalled. We've all read Hacking on looping effects, so nobody is defending the naive it's-all-just-labels view. The problem is looping effects cut both ways. One side says the categories pick out real patterns of suffering, and the looping just shows that real kinds can interact with there objects, the way classifying a species changes its selection pressures without making it unreal. Another side says no: the looping shows classifications create and then stabilize the very phenomena they claim to describe, so psychiatric kinds are more like administrative kinds than natural ones. A third person keeps insisting the whole real versus constructed framing is the thing that's broken, but can't say what should replace it. Nobody is moving. My own hypothesis is that the two sides are demanding different things from the word real, but I can't tell whether that's right or whether there's a real empirical crux hiding underneath. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is: verbal over the word real, substantive about metaphysics, an empirical question about how much classification changes the people classified, or several different questions sharing one name. And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress—a distinction, a kind of study, a reformulated question, or should we just expect no progress? I'm not asking you to settle whether depression is real; I want the structure of the impasse.
