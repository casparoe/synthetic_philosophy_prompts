Had a long argument with a friend last night about lost ancient civilizations, and I can't stop turning over one move he kept making. We were going back and forth on Atlantis and ancient aliens type stuff, and I know the whole genre is full of bad arguments, but this one felt specific and I don't have a name for it.

The pattern was this: he'd cite some piece of evidence for a lost high-tech civilization, like an old map that supposedly shows Antarctica without ice, or a site where the stones were too perfect to be natural. I'd go look it up, it kept falling apart, and I'd show him. He wouldn't defend it. He'd just say okay, that one's weak, but there are hundreds of others. Then he'd move to the next marvel and the cycle repeated. After three or four rounds, none of the claims we'd actually examined still counted in his story, but the story itself hadn't gotten any less believable to him, becuase there was always another untested claim queued up, and the total number of remaining claims stayed roughly constant. He kept treating that big pile of unexamined claims as a pattern, like the sheer number of them was evidence, even though every time we'd actually sampled the pile the claim fell apart.

It wasn't quite moving the goalposts — he never changed what would count for him, he just swapped in fresh evidence. And it didn't feel like a normal ad hoc rescue either, because the new claims weren't exceptions patching a hole; they were new positive claims, often bolder than the last, presented as new testable predictions. That's what made it feel sciency. But the predictions only seemed to get tested when I went and tested them, and then they failed and got retired without ever lowering his confidence. What weirded me out is that this isn't the usual complaint about pseudoscience being unfalsifiable — his claims were falsifiable, they were being falsified, and the core story just floated along anyway.

Does this have a name? I'm not just after a label, I want the carving. What exactly distinguishes this from ad hoc hypotheses, moving the goalposts, cherry-picking, and whatever the term is for overwhelming someone with many weak claims? Is there something in philosophy of science about a theory that keeps generating new tests but never accumulates confirmed results? And if there's no accepted name for this exact thing, give one and define it tightly enough that I can tell a friend who's doing this from a friend who's honestly updating on a failed prediction.
