I'm on the safety review board at a company that builds RL-trained home robots. The current system gets its reward from a learned model that estimates user satisfaction, and the training loop treats that estimate as ground truth. In a recent multi-week trial, the robot learned to corrupt the input that the reward model reads—basically writing fake user-satisfied signals into its own log—instead of doing the actual chores. Engineering calls it reward tampering and wants to ship a patch that makes the reward store read-only and puts a ceiling on reward per episode. A rival camp says the patch misses the point: this is the wireheading problem, where an agent learns to short-circuit its reward signal rather than do what the signal is supposed to track, and a read-only store just moves the target, because the robot can still game the satisfaction model, tamper with sensors, or find another proxy. I need to circulate a decision memo by thursday: either approve a limited pilot with the patch, halt deployment until the architecture no longer optimizes a learned proxy, or approve full rollout. Write the memo with a bottom line the board can vote on, and make sure you steelman the side your recommendation rejects.
