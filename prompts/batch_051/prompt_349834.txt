When I try to get a handle on the paradox of tragedy, the literature reads like a map of fixed positions that have barely moved since antiquity, and what I want is not another survey of those positions but a diagnosis of why the debate has the shape it does. The main positions are easy to state. There is the catharsis view, on which tragedy arouses pity and fear and then discharges or rebalances them, so the pleasure is the relief that follows. There is the control or mastery view, on which tragic works please because they let us rehearse coping with painful emotions in a safe context. There is the conversion view, on which the artistic form transforms painful material into something pleasurable. There is the meta-response view, on which the object of enjoyment is not the sadness itself but our second-order state of being moved, or the positive evaluation we give the experience as a whole. And there is the distancing view, on which the represented events are known to be unreal, so the feelings are attenuated or as-if emotions and the pain is not genuinely painful.

Each position has familiar objections, and the literature mostly cycles through amendments and counterexamples without either side moving. My working hypothesis is that the dispute is underdescribed: the word enjoy is doing at least three jobs at once — felt positive valence during the work, a retrospective judgment that the experience was worthwhile, and the standing disposition to seek out more such experiences — and the different theories are quietly answering different ones. But I can also imagine a diagnosis on which the real disagreement concerns what counts as data: introspection, experimental mood induction, or the social function of the genre. Or one on which a hidden empirical question about the structure of mixed affect is doing the work.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it substantive, verbal, or several questions running under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, an experimental design, a formal model, a change of question — or is this a case where we should stop expecting convergence rather than reaching it?

As a final constraint: please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested reading (for example, of catharsis) as if it were the consensus.
