I am preparing for a blind-tasting certification, and a curious problem has arisen in my practice. My instructor insists that wines from a particular region show a distinctive aromatic signature, and since hearing that I seem to detect it in nearly every glass from that region—including bottles I scored differently before the claim was made to me. I find I cannot tell whether the instruction has changed the way the wines actually taste and smell to me, or whether it has merely changed the descriptions and judgments I produce after the experience. This distinction matters for my training: if my expectations are reshaping the experience itself, my notes are compromised in one way and call for one kind of correction; if they are reshaping only my post-hoc judgments, they are compromised in another way and call for a different correction. Please give me exactly five considerations that would help me distinguish, in a concrete case, an expectation that changed a perceptual experience itself from one that changed only its interpretation. Each consideration should be one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
