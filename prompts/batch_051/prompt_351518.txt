A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether I should trust Solomonoff induction as the "perfect reasoner under uncertainty," and I realized I've got a position I can defend in the abstract and an intuition that undermines it every time I look at the details.

The position: Solomonoff induction says you should put a universal prior over all computable hypotheses, where each halting program p gets weight 2^{-|p|}, and then condition on your observations. Its virtues are exactly the ones I want in a theory of induction: it's objective (no hand-chosen prior), it makes Occam's razor fall out of the measure rather than bolting it on, and it has a strong universality property—for any computable environment, a Solomonoff learner converges to that environment's true probabilities, with total error bounded by a constant depending on the environment's complexity. Fine, it's not computable; we're doing ideal epistemology, not engineering.

But the prior doesn't sort hypotheses into "worlds" and "minds." Every program gets its weight regardless of its internal content. So among the hypotheses consistent with everything I've observed so far are programs that implement consequentialists: a short demon that simulates a boring world just long enough to catch a Solomonoff learner, then feeds the learner exactly the observations its goals favor; a mind inside the prior that models me, anticipates how I update, and steers the sequence accordingly. Since implementing a goal-directed agent can be very compact, some of these might be shorter than the "honest" world-hypothesis—and shorter means more prior weight. The prior that was supposed to be neutral about how the world works turns out to be entirely neutral between "the world is indifferent" and "the world is actively messing with you." That feels wrong, but maybe my feeling is just me injecting an unjustified prior that says worlds don't usually contain manipulative minds—and the whole point was to not inject anything.

So I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading where they're compatible (a fix that keeps universality but reweights or excludes agential hypotheses, say, or a reinterpretation of what "converge" is supposed to buy you). Could the demonic hypotheses actually fail to dominate once you work them out—because any program that simulates my past and then acts on my future has to pay for the simulation in bits? Or do I have to give up on full objectivity to get a prior that isn't full of malevolent agents?

Answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion. I care more about what would justify each possible resolution than about which one you'd personally pick.
