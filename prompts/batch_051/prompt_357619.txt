You are a professional philosopher of law who works on the rule of law as an ideal. In a policy group I am part of, a comparison is repeatedly used to end arguments, and I want it audited rather than accepted or dismissed. The comparison runs: a legal system that lives up to the rule of law is like a well-run game, say chess or football, in which the rules are fixed in advance, are the same for everyone, and are applied by a neutral referee who is not a player. On this picture, the rule of law is exhausted by officials not changing the rules mid-game and applying the existing rules consistently; whether the rules themselves are good, and whether the people subject to them had any say in making them, are separate questions of politics. Those who press the comparison conclude that demands for substantive justice or broad participation should be kept out of the rule of law.

Audit how far this analogy actually holds. Do not treat the rule of law as a single doctrine: distinguish at least a Fuller-type internal morality of law, a Raz-type instrumental, guidance-based conception, and a Waldron-type procedural or argumentative conception, and let the audit show where the game analogy gains or loses force against each. Before your final answer, briefly brainstorm at least three candidate ways of setting up the correspondence between game elements and legal elements, and pick the most defensible one.

Then deliver your audit as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "strongest_version": a one- or two-sentence statement of the analogy at its most charitable.
- "correspondences": an array of objects, each with "game_element", "legal_element", "fits" (one of "well", "partially", "poorly"), and "notes".
- "disanalogies": an array of objects, each with "game_element", "legal_reality", "load_bearing" (true or false), and "effect_on_the_conclusion".
- "smuggled_assumptions": an array of strings.
- "verdict": an object with "conclusion_survives" (one of "fully", "mostly", "partially", "barely", "not at all"), "what_rule_of_law_requires_beyond_consistent_application" (array of strings), and "better_analogy" (string or null).
