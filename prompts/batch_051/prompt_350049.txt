I keep noticing what looks like the same distinction at work in two places that do not usually meet, and I want to know whether the resemblance is deep or superficial. On the metaphysical side, take origin essentialism, understood as the claim that the particular sperm-and-egg pair a person came from is essential to being that very person: had a different sperm fertilized the same egg, or a different egg been fertilized, the resulting child would not have been me, even if she were qualitatively indistinguishable from me in every downstream respect. On the software side, take how commits are identified in Git. A commit's identity—its hash—is a function of the content it records together with the identities of its parent commit or commits; consequently, two commits that record exactly the same changes and result in exactly the same file tree are still different objects if they were made as children of different parents. Both cases seem to make identity a matter of generative ancestry rather than of current or downstream properties.

I can see the obvious objection, which is that in Git this is only a convention for assigning names, whereas origin essentialism is a claim about what could and could not have been true of one and the same individual. But I would like the analogy pushed harder than that. Please state the mapping as precisely as you can; say what would have to be true on each side for conclusions to transfer from one domain to the other; and identify at least one thing the analogy seems to predict that is false, or a precise place where the two structures come apart. Throughout, please indicate how confident you are in each of your claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
