I manage a small pool of capital for a family partnership, so the decisions that actually arise are rebalancing and position-sizing decisions made quarter after quarter. Over the last few years I have come to accept a version of geometric rationality: that a decision rule for repeated choices with multiplicative outcomes should maximize expected logarithmic growth rather than expected arithmetic value; that the Kelly rule — sizing a favorable wager to maximize expected log wealth — is the one-period case of this principle; and that reasoning in expected arithmetic value across repeated trials is systematically misleading because what compounds over time is the geometric mean, not the arithmetic mean. I want to know what would count against this, treated as a serious thesis about rationality under uncertainty rather than betting folklore. What would have to be true for it to be false, or for it to reduce to ordinary expected-utility maximization with logarithmic utility? Lay out the candidate defeaters: that log utility is just one utility function among many; that the argument only has force in repeated, ergodic settings where the time average coincides with the ensemble average; that it presupposes known probabilities and behaves badly under estimation error; that it is silent about one-shot choices; and that it has been insulated by retreating to infinite horizons and other idealizations. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say for each whether a committed geometric rationalist should concede that it counts against the view or may dismiss it as an attack on a strawman. I would also like an honest verdict on whether the view, as it is usually defended, has been qualified to the point of unfalsifiability, and if so whether that is a defect in a principle of this kind. Finally, say which of these defeaters would still bite for a finite-life, small-sample decision-maker rather than an idealized long-run agent. My own guess is that the one-shot case and the estimation-error case are the ones I should take most seriously, but I want to know whether that guess is well placed.
