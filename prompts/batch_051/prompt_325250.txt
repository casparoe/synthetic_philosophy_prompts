Write a satirical corporate sustainability report, around 700 words, from a fictional meat company that has adopted "total animal impact accounting" more rigorously than any of its competitors. Use the standard genre machinery of such reports — an executive summary, a methodology note, a product comparison section, and a short section on offsets and adjustments — and keep the voice deadpan, euphemistic, and earnest throughout. The company should present a single metric, such as sentient life-minutes affected per serving, that includes field deaths from crop cultivation. This should make the company's beef products compare surprisingly favourably with its chicken line, and force its plant-based line to disclose harvest-associated small-mammal mortality. Extend the accounting to wild animal suffering: once wild lives count, the report should treat habitat as a balance-sheet item and quietly reach at least one uncomfortable conclusion — for example, that certain rangeland is a net-negative-suffering zone and that the company is therefore framing a "habitat-simplification offset" as a charitable intervention. The humour should come from getting the welfare arithmetic and its strange implications right, not from mocking the idea that animal suffering matters. Never break character or add an explanatory note; let the reader feel the arbitrariness of where the moral boundary gets drawn, the hidden trade-offs inside labels like humane and plant-based, and the absurdity of treating net-negative wild lives as a corporate offset. It should be funny to someone who knows animal welfare quantification and wild animal suffering, and readable to someone who does not.
