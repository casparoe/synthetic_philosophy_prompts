OK this has been bugging me all week. My friend, who does legal theory, keeps saying the whole "can a rule amend itself" thing is a non-problem, and his go-to move is to compare a legal system to the game Nomic — you know, the game where changing the rules is itself a legal move, and the rules about how to change rules are just more rules that can themselves be changed under the currently valid procedure. His line: a constitution with an amendment clause is the exat same structure, just with higher stakes. So when people get dizzy about a legislature using its amending power to change the amending power itself, or an amendment clause being used to entrench or repeal itself, they're just confusing levels — no more paradoxical, he says, than a program editing its own source file. I want to know how far that analogy actually carries. What maps onto what, in table form if you want — constitution, amendment clause, legislator, court, rule of recognition, player, judge, whatever belongs in the table. Then the disanalogies: which ones are harmless, and which ones matter if the point is the deflationary conclusion? In particular I'm suspicious that a game like Nomic has a self-contained written rulebook and everyone's power comes from the rules, whereas a legal system has officials, force, interpretive practice, and people whose power stays put whatever the paper says. Does the deflationary conclusion survive once those disanalogies are on the table, or is the game metaphor doing all the work and smuggling in a tidiness the law doesn't actually have?
