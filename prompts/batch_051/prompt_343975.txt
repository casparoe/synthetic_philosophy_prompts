I have been trying to locate where a message's meaning resides, using Hofstadter's three-way distinction between the frame message (the implicit signal that a thing is a message and is to be decoded), the outer message (the information about the code, medium, and conventions needed to recover the inner message), and the inner message (the content itself). My judgements about particular cases each seem right to me, but I doubt they can all be true together, so I would like a principle that captures as many of them as possible, a clear statement of which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon, and its ruling on a further case.

The cases are these.

(i) Wind and rain carve into a cliff face a set of marks that happen to be shaped exactly like the word for water in a known writing system. I judge that these marks carry no meaning at all.

(ii) A scribe incises marks of the same shape into a clay tablet, as part of an undeciphered script. I judge that the tablet carries a message even though nobody now living can recover its inner message.

(iii) A particular DNA sequence is translated into a protein in one organism but is a non-coding relic in another. I judge that whether the sequence carries an inner message depends on the reading machinery of the cell rather than on the sequence alone.

(iv) A recording of a piano sonata is played in an empty house. I judge that the music is in some sense in the record, but that no meaning is being conveyed at the time; the record holds a standing potential to mean rather than an actual message.

(v) A pun is replaced by a flat explanation that preserves its point. I judge that the inner message survives, but that something belonging to the meaning is lost with the outer form.

Is there a single account of where meaning lies across the frame, outer, and inner messages that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn, and how would the best account rule on the case I still find puzzling: a device designed for something other than signalling happens to emit a pattern that astronomers read as a greeting?

Please answer in British English.
