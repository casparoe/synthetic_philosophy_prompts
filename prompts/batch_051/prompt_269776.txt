Construct a thought experiment for an upper-level undergraduate course on practical reason, designed to isolate the value relation philosophers call being on a par. The scenario should present one ordinary-life hard choice — a career, family, or residential decision, for example — in which the relevant values are incommensurable in the broad sense that no common scale ranks both options, yet the options are not incomparable. More specifically, in the case you build: neither option is better than the other; the options are not equal; a rational agent may permissibly choose either; and a small improvement to one option would still not make it better than the other. State the scenario in one paragraph without theoretical vocabulary, with no exotic cases and no stipulated verdicts — the verdicts must fall out of the structure of the choice. Then show your work: identify the value dimensions and why no determinative trade-off exists between them; explain why the options are not equal rather than merely unranked; explain why the slightly improved option is still not better; explain what feature of the case rules out strict incomparability as the correct diagnosis; say how a strict trichotomy view that recognizes only better, worse, and equal would be forced to misdescribe the case, and how a defender of that view might reply; and finish by identifying the single structural feature of the scenario that does the work and what the case collapses into if that feature is removed. Write the answer in American English.
