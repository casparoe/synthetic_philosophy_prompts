I've been working through the logic of Trinitarian doctrine on my own and keep getting tangled in what look like flat contradictions, so I'd like a live sparring partner rather than a summary. I am comfortable with formal logic, including identity, but I am not a theologian, and I want you to make the strongest case available rather than a merely devotional one. Please play an unnamed philosophical theologian — precise, unflappable, and willing to concede small points precisely so that you can hold the main one. You hold that the doctrine of the Trinity is coherent and can be stated without contradiction. Your resources are restricted to a relative-identity account: you deny that there is a single, absolute relation of identity; instead, identity is always identity under a count noun, so it makes no sense to ask whether two things are "identical" without asking identical what. Thus "the Father is God," "the Son is God," and "the Holy Spirit is God" are true because the three persons are the same God, while "the Father is the Son" is false, because sameness of God and sameness of person are different identity relations. You may admit that naive formulations of the doctrine are inconsistent, and you may refine distinctions as needed; you may not abandon the relative-identity framework, collapse the persons into one another, or end by appealing to mystery. Stay in character, keep each reply to one paragraph unless the logic strictly demands more, and answer my objection directly before adding anything of your own.

My opening move: relative identity lets you avoid contradiction only by making "the same God" too weak to do theological work. If the Father and the Son are the same God but not the same person, then they are one in a specified respect and three in another — but then the doctrine has not actually asserted strict oneness and strict threeness at all. And here is the sharper problem: "was crucified" is true of the Son and false of the Father, yet on your view the Son is the same God as the Father; so being the same God does not preserve truths about the divine reality across its relata. Why doesn't this show that your "God" is functioning as a kind under which three beings fall, rather than as a single being that the three persons are? What, precisely, does being the same God amount to, such that truths about the Son need not transfer to the Father?
