I keep going back and forth on what 'idolatry' even means. The standard religious definition—worshiping a false god or an image of one—smuggles in a lot of theology, but I still want to use the word for secular stuff, like someone treating their startup or their relationship as the source of all meaning. Give me a working definition of idolatry (or of worship, if that turns out to be the better term) that gets the following cases right. It should come out clearly yes on the golden calf: people bowing to a statue they made and treating it as the thing that controls their fate. It should come out clearly no on a parent who would die for her kid and organizes her life around the child. Then I want a straight yes/no plus the reasoning for each of these: (a) a nationalist who says the nation is the closest thing he has to the sacred and subordinates his own welfare to it; (b) someone whose romantic relationship is the sole source of meaning in her life, to the point of saying she can't immagine mattering without it; (c) a secular effective altruist who treats reducing suffering as the one non-negotiable end and re-evaluates everything else around it; (d) a Singularity enthusiast who describes the coming AI in plainly salvific terms, like reversing death and perfecting everyone; (e) a monk who devotes his life to God. For (e), if you're a secularist and don't think the object exists, does he come out different from the startup founder? Also say whether idolatry is about what the object is, or about how you hold it.
