Read The Truman Show through the social-epistemic distinction between an epistemic bubble — an information environment in which relevant evidence is missing or filtered out — and an echo chamber — a social structure that systematically discredits outside sources so that members come to actively distrust them. Is Seahaven best characterized as a bubble, a chamber, or a hybrid with its own distinctive failure mode? Work through the production’s handling of anomalies: the falling studio light, the reappearance of Truman’s presumed-dead father, the intercepted radio traffic, Sylvia’s warning, and the cover stories that follow each one. Does Truman get out because his confinement is merely informational, or because he manages to do something more difficult — refuse to outsource his standards of credibility to the people who define credibility inside the dome? Where does the film capture what makes each kind of confinement sticky or brittle, and where does it blur the two?
