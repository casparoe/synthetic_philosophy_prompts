I run a small bakery in a town that still has a lot of old ordinances on the books. While reviewing everything before renewing our permits, I found a local rule from the early 1960s requiring any business selling food to keep a particular kind of first-aid station behind the counter: a white metal cabinet with a red cross, of a size spelled out in the code, with its contents listed on a card in two languages. Nobody I've talked to — including a city clerk I know socially — has ever heard of anyone being cited for this. The clerk laughed and said enforcement stopped decades ago, and that today's inspectors wouldn't know what the cabinet was supposed to look like. Complying would cost me a few thousand dollars and a stretch of wall space, and every other food business I've asked simply ignores the rule.

I asked one owner why she doesn't comply, and she said that if the city doesn't enforce it, it isn't really a law; a law is just a prediction about what the authorities will actually do to you. That bothers me. The rule was passed by an elected council, it still sits in the municipal code, and I would feel sneaky treating it as dead simply because enforcement lapsed. But I also can't see the point of following a rule the city itself has, in practice, abandoned. I'm not asking for legal advice about my particular risk — I'll handle that separately. I want to understand the principle: is a law that has gone unenforced for decades still a law, and is there anything wrong, from a citizen's point of view, with quietly treating it as not one?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then walk me through your reasoning, without preamble and without restating my question.
