I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether it's wrong to buy factory-farmed chicken when there's a cheap plant-based alternative sitting right there. Her line is basically the inefficacy objection: no single purchase changes how many chickens the store orders, so no individual chicken dies because of you, so it can't be wrong. My credence that the act is wrong, all things considered, for an ordinary person who can easily afford the alternative, is about 0.7; her's is about 0.2.

Give me your own credence, but I mainly want the decomposition. Here's how I'd split it, but merge or add terms if you think I've carved it at the wrong joints:

1. Descriptive threshold claim: that an individual purchase does have some nonzero expected effect on production, because supply chains respond in lumpy ways (crates, reorder points, restock triggers), so each purchase carries a small chance of being the one that pushes the order over the line. What's your probability that this is true, and roughly how large is the expected effect per purchase?

2. Normative significance of tiny expected effects: conditional on the effect being real but small (say, in expectation one extra chicken killed per few hundred purchases), what's your probability that this still grounds a pro tanto reason not to buy?

3. Non-causal backup: what's your probability that even if there is no causal effect at all, the purchase is still pro tanto wrong for some other reason — complicity, expressing contempt for animals, a universalization test, integrity, whatever you think the strongest version is?

4. Weightiness: conditional on there being a pro tanto reason from any of 1–3, what's your probability that it's strong enough to make the act wrong all things considered, given the small cost/taste/convenience sacrifice?

Then show me how you'd combine those into your overall credence — I want the arithmetic, not just a vibe. Also: which term is your final number most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move that term the most for you? And finally, given my 0.7 and her 0.2, where do you think our disagreement actually lives — which term is doing most of the work?
