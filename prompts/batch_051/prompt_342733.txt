In my Korean Neo-Confucianism reading group, we have spent several sessions on the Four–Seven debate, and I have been struck by how little convergence it produced despite the participants sharing the same Confucian classics and the same basic metaphysical vocabulary of li and ki. The debate began in the sixteenth century with an exchange between Yi Hwang (Toegye) and Gi Dae-seung (Gobong), and later drew in Yi I (Yulgok) and Seong Hon (Ugye). Toegye's mature position was roughly that the Four Beginnings—compassion, shame, deference, and the sense of right and wrong, as found in Mencius—are the issuance of li, while the Seven Emotions—joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, and desire—are the issuance of ki. Gobong objected that this split made li into an active psychological agent, and that both the Four Beginnings and the Seven Emotions should be understood as ki issuing under the normative guidance of li. Yulgok later sharpened this into the view that the Four Beginnings are not a separate class of feelings but simply the Seven Emotions considered in their morally proper form.

What puzzles me is not who was right, but why this argument had the structure it did. The parties did not seem to be denying the same proposition; they kept refining the same terms—issuance, principle, material force, moral emotion—without reaching a common formulation. My working hypothesis is that this is not a single debate but two or three debates stacked on top of each other: one about whether li can be an active causal power rather than a purely normative ground, one about how emotions should be individuated and classified, and one about what it means for a moral emotion to be pure. Is that diagnosis correct? If so, which of these distinct questions was actually doing the work for each side, and what would count as progress on each—a distinction, a formalization of the metaphysics, a different taxonomy of emotions, or a shift to asking about self-cultivation? Or is there something else about the disagreement that explains why it persisted across generations without resolution?
