I write about language and the cognitive sciences, and I want to understand how much empirical content there is in the leading accounts of generics. Take the claim that a speaker's willingness to accept generics such as "ducks lay eggs" and "mosquitoes carry malaria" — sentences that plainly are not universal generalizations — is best explained by the prevalence of the property among members of the kind, rather than by the property's danger or disvalue, its being stereotypical or diagnostic of the kind, or some threshold of typicality. How would one test that claim? Give a concrete experimental design, including conditions and the pattern of judgments each account predicts; say which pattern would favor the prevalence account and which would favor a danger- or typicality-based alternative; and then be explicit about what even the cleanest result would and would not settle. In particular, where the residual ambiguity cannot be removed, is that a limitation of the experiments or a defect in the construct of "accepting a generic"? Please keep the answer under 900 words.
