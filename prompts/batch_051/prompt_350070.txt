And here's the argument against it:

(1) If pain and pleasure are commensurable on a single scale, then the trade-off between them is Archimedean: for any finite amount of one, some sufficiently large but still finite amount of the other can outweigh it.

(3) So, on the lexical threshold view, above-threshold pain and pleasure are incommensurable.
(4) Incommensurable values can't stand in determinate "better than," "worse than," or "equal to" relations; comparing them isn't just psychologically hard, it's undefined.
(5) But the lexical threshold view itself says above-threshold pain and pleasure do stand in a determinate relation: any amount of the former is worse than any amount of the latter, so the comparison is always defined and resolved in pain's favor.
