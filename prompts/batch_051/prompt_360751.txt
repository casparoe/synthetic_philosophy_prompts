I'm a software engineer, mostly doing ML for a living (recsys, some ranking), and I've never actually read any philosophy of biology, so I might be reinventing a wheel here. Lately I've been turning over a view of evolution that I find weirdly compelling: natural selection is basically an optimization algorithm, like a giant, slow stochastic gradient descent over genotypes. Variants get proposed, selection keeps the ones that happen to be fitter, and the population climbs the local fitness landscape. That picture is clean and unifying — it explains a ton with one mechanism — and it has a sharp consequence: evolution is a stupid optimizer. SGD only sees local structure and gets stuck in crappy basins, so natural selection should settle for whatever barely-workable solution it happens to trip over first, never the best possible design.

But I have an intuition that won't go away. When I read about evolved systems — the immune system, ant colony nest choice, the development of the eye — they look like they're finding something principled: modular, robust, capable of generalizing to conditions the lineage never got trained on directly. My gradient-descent models don't do that; they're brittle and overfit and fall over as soon as the input distribution shifts. I get that evolution has had billions of years and enormous population sizes, but the difference feels like kind, not just scale. The question is whether I should trust that feeling.

Don't tell me which side wins. Give me the strongest case each side has and what it would take for each side to be the one that gives way. What are the best reasons to treat my intuition that evolution is cleverer than SGD as an illusion — survivorship bias, the fact that we only notice the winners, the environment acting as a massive training distribution so the generality is bought with data rather than mechanism? What are the best reasons to think the optimizer metaphor is leaving out something essential — no stored objective function, a fitness landscape that shifts with the organisms themselves, every intermediate stage having to actually live, recombination, development, niche construction — so that the theory needs mending or abandoning? And is there a stable middle reading where evolution really is an optimizer in some sense but the process is doing something the stupid-SGD story omits, so both my intuition and the tidy theory survive? If that middle reading is right, does it actually tell me anything about how I should set up my own optimizers, or is the parallel to SGD just a metaphor? I want the reasoning for each move, not just a verdict.
