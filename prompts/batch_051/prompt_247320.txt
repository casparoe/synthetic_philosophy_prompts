For the past year I have been facilitating an interdisciplinary seminar on the philosophy of mathematical practice, and one recurring argument has calcified so completely that I now suspect the two sides are not having the dispute they take themselves to be having. The shared starting point is Lakatos's Proofs and Refutations, with its case study of Euler's polyhedron theorem, V - E + F = 2, and the way the concept polyhedron was repeatedly reworked as counterexamples surfaced. No one disputes that historical description. The impasse concerns the lesson to draw from it.

One camp says the lesson is that mathematics is quasi-empirical: a proof is not a once-and-for-all deduction from fixed definitions but a fallible conjecture, open to refutation, with concepts that may legitimately be stretched or redefined under pressure from exceptions. The other camp replies that Lakatos has conflated the messy context of discovery with mathematics itself. Once polyhedron is given a precise modern definition -- say, a polyhedral surface homeomorphic to a sphere -- Euler's theorem becomes a deductive consequence of stipulated concepts, and the historical refutations were merely episodes in which mathematicians decided, tacitly or explicitly, how to draw the boundaries of a term. Finished mathematics, on this view, is not answerable to counterexamples in any quasi-empirical sense; the refutations are boundary-setting, not evidence.

After a year neither side has budged, even though both accept the same historical narrative and the same examples. My own hypothesis is that the two camps are answering different questions under one label -- one asking how mathematical concepts legitimately change over time, the other asking what makes a finished theorem true -- and that the word mathematics is being asked to cover both projects at once. But I do not want confirmation; I want the diagnosis checked. Is the disagreement verbal or substantive? Does a hidden formal question about whether any non-trivial mathematical predicate can be fully specified in advance do the real work? Is there a disagreement about what should count as the same theorem across a change of definition? Or is the impasse a sign that quasi-empirical is too blunt a term to carry the weight being put on it? Give me your diagnosis of the structure of the impasse, and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis -- a distinction, a formal result, a sharper question -- or argue that no progress should be expected and why.

Whatever your diagnosis, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating of the question.
