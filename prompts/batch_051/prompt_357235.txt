I cover science and ideas, and I'm trying to sort out what I actually believe about the meme concept before I write a piece on whether it's more than a metaphor. My current commitments, roughly:

(a) cultural variants—tunes, stories, procedures, slang—do replicate in the minimal sense that they get copied from person to person with enough fidelity to be recognizable as the same variant;
(b) but culture isn't naturally particulate: most transmission blends, recombines, and edits, so talk of 'a meme' usually carves a boundary where there isn't one;
(c) I still think darwinian selection is the right frame for cultural change, since selection only needs heritable variation in fitness—it doesn't need high-fidelity copying or discreteness;
(d) I also think cultural inheritance is partly Lamarckian (acquired modifications get passed on), and its a real difference from genes but doesn't break the selection picture;
(e) online image-macro memes feel like the one clean case where discrete, high-fidelity units actually exist, and I'd call them memes in a strong sense.

Before I pitch this, can you audit the set? I want the tensions ranked by how serious they actually are, with what would have to give for each, and I'd also like to know which apparent tensions dissolve once you look closer.
