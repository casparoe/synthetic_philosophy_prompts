Ive got a policy about introspective reports, and I use it whenever someone leans on a fine-grained claim about their own experience to settle an argument. I basically treat a report like "the wine tastes different to me now than it did before" as evidence only that something in the person's beliefs, standards, or discriminative behavior changed; I refuse to treat it as evidence for a separate thing called a quale. My favorite case is two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn, who both say the coffee they're testing tastes worse to them now than it did years ago. Chase says the coffee hasnt changed but his standards got sharper; Sanborn says his standards are the same but the experience itself changed. The two stories feel the same from the inside, so I say there's no fact of the matter between them and qualia are an idle posit. Is this policy actually defensible? Or am I just throwing away a whole kind of evidence? Is there a better version of the rule that keeps the skepticism without making me blind to actual facts about experience?
