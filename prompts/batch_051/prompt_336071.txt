I am pulled in two directions about the interpretation of gauge freedom, and I would like you to help me reach a negotiated verdict. Consider classical electrodynamics formulated with the vector potential A and scalar potential φ. The electric and magnetic fields are obtained from them, and the potentials admit gauge transformations—A → A + ∇λ, φ → φ − ∂λ/∂t—that leave the fields unchanged. Nearly every prediction of the theory is invariant under these transformations.

On reflection I find myself committed to the view that gauge freedom is surplus structure: gauge-equivalent potentials are simply two descriptions of one and the same physical situation, and whatever differs between them corresponds to nothing in the world. This view appeals to me because it seems to respect parsimony and the principle that only what makes a difference to something observable should count as real.

But I cannot shake the opposite intuition. In the Aharonov-Bohm effect, a charged particle passes through a region in which the magnetic field is zero everywhere along its path, yet the interference pattern shifts in a way that depends on the line integral of the vector potential around a closed loop enclosing a shielded solenoid. This looks like the potential is doing physical work where the field is absent; if the potential were mere surplus structure, how would the particle 'know' about the enclosed flux? I find it hard to accept that the quantity whose value fixes the phase is unreal.

Give me the strongest statement you can of each side. In particular, spell out what the 'only gauge-invariant quantities are real' view should say about the Aharonov-Bohm phase: does it after all get to classify the phase as a gauge-invariant fact, and is that move legitimate or an evasion? What would it take for the intuition to force an amendment of the view instead of being debunked? And is there a defensible middle reading on which the potential is real in some respects and surplus in others, without thereby licensing all gauge-dependent structure as real? Tell me which resolution you would adopt—debunk the intuition, amend the theory, or reconcile the two—and what argument or evidence would most change your answer.
