I keep noticing a move in arguments and I want to know whether it has a name. Someone invokes 'Western values' as if it's a settled package that does real explanatory or justificatory work, but when you press them on what's in the package, the contents shift to fit the point being made: ancient Greece gets claimed as the ancestor when the topic is democracy, the early church when it's religious heritage, the scientific revolution when it's progress—and none of those would have endorsed the others' lists. It's not ordinary vagueness or different definitions, because the phrase seems to do its argumentative work precisely by keeping the list unfixed: the speaker can always claim to be defending the West's own tradition no matter which values they're currently defending. Is there a recognized term for that specific pattern—a claimed historical or civilizational category whose force comes from not having stable contents? If there's no exact label, I'd want a definition with boundary cases, and if there is one, I'd want to know exactly how it differs from its closest neighbors, not just the name.
