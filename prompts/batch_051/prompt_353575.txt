I host a small podcast about AI policy, and next week's guest is an economist who argues for what he calls the competitiveness constraint on alignment. His view, in one breath, goes like this: any lab that voluntarily pays the alignment tax—spending compute, researchers, or benchmark performance on safety work that doesn't improve visible capabilities—gets outcompeted by labs that don't, so the market selects against safety no matter what individual engineers prefer. He's not a doomer, and he's not dumb; he has a tidy little model and he's very good at keeping the conversation abstract enough that objections slide off. His favorite move is to say that people are confusing how the world should be with how the world actually prices risk, and he uses that to deflect almost anything.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that go beyond just having him restate the core claim, and that force him to seperate the empirical, economic, and normative claims he tends to bundle together. I'd like at least one question each on whether the constraint is supposed to bite inside a single lab, between firms in one jurisdiction, or between countries; on whether the alignment tax is actually measurable or is basically a metaphor; on what mechanism could break the constraint; and on what real-world evidence would make him update or disconfirm the view. For each question, give me the follow-up to use if he gives the answer he's known for, and order the whole set so that by the end he either commits to a concrete prediction or has to admit the view is doing less work than it sounds like it's doing. Keep the questions in American English.
