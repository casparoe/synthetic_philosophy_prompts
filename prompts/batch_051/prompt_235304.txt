Maxwell's demon is usually dismissed on the grounds that it would have to erase its record of each molecule's velocity, and Landauer's principle says erasing information carries a minimum entropy cost. But this seems to leave an obvious loophole: the demon could simply write every measurement to a fresh blank cell of an indefinitely long memory tape and never erase anything. Since no erasure ever occurs, Landauer's principle would never bite, while the demon would keep sorting faster from slower molecules and thereby keep lowering the gas's entropy without any obvious compensating entropy increase. If a demon never erases, what exactly prevents it from violating the second law? Why should Landauer's principle be regarded as the resolution when a never-erasing demon appears to escape it entirely?
