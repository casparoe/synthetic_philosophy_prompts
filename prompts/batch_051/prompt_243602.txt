Play the role of a professional philosopher who works on decision theory and value change, and think carefully before you write. I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page proposal, and I have a half-formed idea I want you to shape into something fundable. Here's the idea. Good's theorem says that an expected-utility maximizer with fixed preferences and Bayesian updating should never prefer to avoid costless information, since information can only improve the choice in expectation. But the theorem rules out, by assumption, the cases that interest me: where the information itself changes what you value. The familiar examples are personal (learning you're at risk for Huntington's might change how you weight near-term goods against far-term ones), but I want the proposal to push on the structural cases too, like learning the kind of vivid moral detail that converts your ethical views while your current self can already predict the later self will be grateful you stayed ignorant—or won't. So the question is roughly: once information is allowed to change preferences, is there any coherent sense in which an agent can rationally prefer ignorance, or does that preference collapse into an indefensible privileging of her current preferences? Write the proposal around that. It should state the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions don't settle it (and don't just say everyone assumes fixed preferences), lay out a method that is philosophy/decision theory rather than empirical work, describe a useful deliverable, and say what would count as the project failing. Avoid the lazy move of ruling preference-change irrational by stipulation. Don't include citations or a bibliography, I'll add those myself. After the proposal, add a short "self-critical note" saying which premise in your case for the project is weakest and what consideration would change your mind about it. Begin with the proposal itself—no preamble, no "here is a proposal"—just the first sentence of the document.
