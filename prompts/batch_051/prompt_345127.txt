Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two comparative psychologists: one who defends Morgan's canon as an indispensable safeguard — an animal behavior should be explained by a lower psychological faculty whenever one suffices — and one who holds that the canon in its received form is biased, that it has historically functioned to deny animal minds, and that mental attributions deserve no special burden of proof. The statement should open with a numbered list of claims both can sign: about what the canon does and does not say, about what a good behavioral explanation requires, about the real-world consequences of over- and under-attributing cognition, and about what burden a mental attribution should carry. Then provide a disagreements table whose rows state exact, non-vacuous residual differences, each row phrased so its own side would accept the wording, and flag any row you judge to be partly or wholly verbal — for instance, rows that turn on how "higher" and "lower" are defined. Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions an answer to which would move at least one side, and for each crux specify a concrete behavioral case, described from scratch so no reader must know a particular study, that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Ensure one crux addresses whether the canon's asymmetry survives once the two possible errors are costed asymmetrically — attributing a richer cognition than is present and denying one that is present may do different kinds of harm. The finished document must be one both psychologists could co-sign without embarrassment, with no formulation only one side could have written.
