I need a decision tree for an intro critical-thinking seminar, sorting students according to whether and when they treat doubt as a moral duty rather than just an epistemic virtue. Start from a concrete scenario, not a slogan: someone believes a false thing that comforts them, and a friend knows it's false. Every branch question should be a yes/no or forced-choice item about a concrete case or an ordinary-sounding claim, phrased so a student can answer without knowing which named position it pushes them toward. Build it from the logical space first: give me the branch questions and the full grid of answer combinations, then place the actual positions in that grid, and mark the combinations no one occupies — for each empty one say briefly whether it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just an unexplored corner. The leaves have to include five views: the strict evidentialist who thinks believing on weak evidence is always a moral failing; the Jamesian permissivist who thinks you may sometimes believe ahead of the evidence when the question is unavoidable, consequential, and live for you; the doxastic involuntarist who says talk of duty here is confused because belief isn't under direct voluntary control; the virtue epistemologist; and a fallibilist who doubts that any belief could ever fully discharge the demand. Use six to eight questions and end up with seven to ten leaves, aimed at undergrads. Also flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually seperate, rather than pretending the partition is clean.
