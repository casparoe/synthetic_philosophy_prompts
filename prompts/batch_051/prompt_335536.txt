My friend and I stayed up way too late fighting about Woodward, and he cornered me with this argument that interventionism just helps itself to the very thing its supposed to explain. I can't find where it goes wrong, but the conclusion seems too strong. (1) Woodward says X causes Y iff, if you were to intervene on X while holding everything else fixed, Y would change (or its probability would change), and this has to be invariant across some range of interventions. (2) But to be an intervention on X with respect to Y, a variable I has to be an independent causal influence on X and not affect Y through a route that bypasses X. (3) So the right-hand side of the definition already contains the concept it's defining: I causes X. (4) A definition that contains the concept it's defining on the right-hand side is circular, regardless of what extra conditions it adds. (5) So interventionism is circular. (6) If an account of causation is circular, it cant explain what causation is; it just tells us causation is something having to do with causation. (7) So the standard interventionist account is empty. Im pretty sure philosophy would have noticed that, so maybe one of the steps trades on something. Tell me which step fails and why, or whether the argument is actually sound and Woodward is just stuck with that.
