I am a novelist, and I am drafting the central argument of a book in which a retired refrigeration engineer claims to have built a working Maxwell's demon: a gate in a partition that sorts fast molecules from slow ones, so that one side of a closed box grows hot and the other cold, starting from a gas at uniform temperature, with no work done on the gas and no heat supplied from outside. The novel's physicist insists that the second law is safe and that the engineer's accounting is quietly hiding an entropy increase somewhere. I do not want that rebuttal to be a hand-wave about friction, leaky valves, or the difficulty of building nanoscale machinery; the scene only works dramatically if the objection would hold even for an idealised, frictionless, perfectly engineered demon. So I want the specification of the physics before I write the dialogue.

Give me the list of requirements an adequate resolution of Maxwell's demon must meet before it deserves to be called an exorcism of the paradox, with the physical case or consideration behind each. In particular, I want your committed verdict on which parts of the demon's cycle can be made thermodynamically costless in principle and which cannot: the acquisition of information about a molecule's speed, the decision to open or hold the gate, the sorting operation itself, and the erasure or resetting of whatever record the demon keeps. The account must also say something precise about the relation between thermodynamic entropy and information, instead of treating "information is physical" as a slogan: does the demon's acquired knowledge itself count as a reduction of entropy, or is it only the erasure of the record that matters? It should further specify what an adequate account must say about a finite-memory demon that resets one bit per sorting event, about a demon with an ever-growing memory that never erases anything, and about the fact that the second law is statistical rather than absolute for small systems. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work in eliminating the tempting but inadequate resolutions — for instance, the idea that the demon must pay an entropy bill because it needs a light source to see the molecules, or the idea that the paradox dissolves once one notices that real gates stick. I would rather you commit to one definite specification, with reasons, than survey the options, and please write the whole answer in British English.
