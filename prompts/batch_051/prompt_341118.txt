I have been trying to make sense of what someone is committed to when they say that history is written by the victors. Unpacked, the slogan seems to bundle at least three claims: that the powerful produce most of the records on which later histories rely; that archives, monuments, and other traces are kept, destroyed, or rearranged by later powerful interests, so that some points of view are preserved while others are effaced; and that the interpretative stories historians build are systematically tilted by which groups prevailed, so that even a scrupulous historian inherits a skewed evidential base. Taken one way these look like ordinary empirical claims about how records are created, maintained, and used; taken another they look like a conceptual point about history itself — that there is no neutral historical record, only records made and kept from a standpoint; and taken a third way they slide into historical relativism, the thought that what counts as historical truth is whatever the powerful succeed in making count.

I want a design for bringing evidence to bear on this, not a dismissal of the slogan as untestable. A crude first attempt might simply tally, across a sample of conflicts, whether a victor's accounts survive more often than a vanquished's; I would like you to say clearly whether such a tally tests the slogan or merely restates part of it, and then set out the strongest concrete test or small battery of tests you can devise for one or more of the component claims. For each test, specify the observations, comparisons, or measurements to be made and what patterns would count for and against treating the relevant component as an ordinary empirical claim; say too how one could tell which component of the slogan is doing the explanatory work in a given case. Then mark the residue honestly: what would even a fully favourable result fail to establish about the slogan as a whole, and where exactly does the boundary lie between an empirical claim about the archive and a conceptual or relativist claim that no archival evidence could settle? The answer should be written in American English.
