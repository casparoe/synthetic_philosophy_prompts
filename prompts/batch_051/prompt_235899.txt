Retired physicist here — forty years of special relativity, and I only started reading philosophy properly after I retired, so I'm sure I'm mangling the vocabulary.

A friend in my reading group keeps insisting that special relativity all by itself settles whether the future is already real, and I'd like the argument stress-tested. He gives it like this:

(1) For any point P and any event E spacelike separated from P, there is an inertial observer at P whose hyperplane of simultaneity passes through E. So for that observer, E is simultaneous with P. (The Andromeda example: someone walking toward Andromeda and someone walking away put 'now' on Andromeda several days apart.)

(2) If P is real, and E is simultaneous with P for some inertial observer at P, then E is real for that observer — they can truthfully say E is happening now.

(3) But 'real for her' and 'real for me' cannot diverge: reality isn't relative to a frame. So if any observer at P can truthfully say E is real now, E is real full stop.

(4) So every event spacelike separated from P is real. Step (1) supplies an observer for each E, and (2) plus (3) turns 'real in her frame' into plain real.

(5) Now chain it. Pick any event F in my timelike future. Choose an event E1 far enough off to the side that both P→E1 and E1→F are spacelike. Then E1 is real by (4). Now run the same reasoning from E1: F is simultaneous with E1 in some observer's frame at E1, so F is real. Repeat for any future event; same trick backwards for the past.

So, he says, the block universe just falls out of the Lorentz transformations, and tomorrow is as fixed as yesterday. I dont like that conclusion — it feels like it proves too much, and it makes deliberation look like a bad joke — but I can't find the exact step where the rabbit goes into the hat. I'd like to hang onto the usual Einstein reading: no preferred frame, no empirically hidden 'absolute now.'

Tell me which step you'd reject, why that step looked innocent, and what rejecting it costs. And price the main ways out: a preferred slicing of spacetime, shrinking the present to something Lorentz-invariant, or just swallowing the block.
