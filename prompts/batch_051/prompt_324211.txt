I have a friend, Maya, who isn't vegan but has spent way too long thinking about when it's actually okay to eat or use animal stuff, and by now I can predict her on most cases. Some of her rulings: she'll happily eat a shrink-wrapped package of deli meat that a grocery store through out, on the grounds that the animal is already dead and eating it creates no demand for more. She once took home a deer that her cousin hit on the highway and made herself venison stew, no issue. She eats the eggs from her neighbor's three rescue hens, since the hens are pets, they'll never be killed, and the eggs would otherwise just rot. She's also said she'd drink the leftover milk from a sanctuary cow who gets milked to prevent mastitis, because that milk is literally going down the drain otherwise. But she refuses to buy new leather shoes or factory-farmed chicken, since that pays for more animal suffering. So far her rule looked like "no new harm, no new demand," basically freeganism applied to animal products.

Then I found out the sanctuary where that cow lives had an old potbellied pig named Tulip die in his sleep of heart failure. The staff were trying to decide whether to butcher him for a volunteer potluck or bury him. I assumed Maya would be fine with eating him, since he died naturally and would otherwise just be compost or buried waste. Instead she got weird and said she didn't think she could eat him, but she couldn't articulate why this is different then the highway deer. That broke my model of her.

I'm less interested in what's objectively right and more in what Maya would actually say, or should say if she wants to stay consistent with herself. What are the candidate rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well, and how do they split on Tulip? Which earlier case is the closest analog to Tulip, and what would she be committing herself to if she said okay versus not okay? Name one further case that would cleanly tell the candidate rules apart.
