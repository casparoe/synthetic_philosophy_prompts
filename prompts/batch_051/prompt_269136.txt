I'm a behavioral economist who works on the evolution of cooperation, and my working vocabulary is repeated games, strategies, payoffs, discounting, direct and indirect reciprocity, costly signaling, reputation, equilibrium selection, punishment, private versus public monitoring, and internalized norms. I have only a lay-level knowledge of the philosophical and psychological literature on moral emotions.

The debate I want you to translate is the one about guilt and shame: specifically, which of them is the better moral emotion, and whether a culture can deliberately shift from running on one to running on the other. I gather the rough contrast is something like this: guilt is felt about a particular act, does not require an audience, and motivates repair or restitution; shame is felt about the self as a whole, seems to require an audience, real or imagined, and motivates concealment or withdrawal. The positions in play include the claims that guilt is superior because it tracks the act rather than the audience and drives repair; that shame is superior because it tracks one's standing in a community and is what actually regulates social life; and the skeptical view that they are not rivals at all but two parts of a single system.

Restate that debate in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Concretely: what are guilt and shame in game-theoretic terms — mechanisms, costs, signals, or sanctions attached to norm violation? What does calling one "better" amount to — a claim about robustness under private information, about which mechanism sustains cooperation against defection and free-riding, about payoffs and monitoring regimes, or about which outcome is an equilibrium? And what does the question "can a culture choose?" come to — a question about how many equilibria exist, about equilibrium selection, or about the cost of transitioning from one equilibrium to another?

Then give me the residue report. Tell me which parts of the debate translate cleanly, which parts you had to distort to fit, and which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether the normative question — "which is the better moral emotion?" — survives as a well-posed question in my framework, or whether my framework quietly converts it into the purely functional question of what maintains cooperation under a given monitoring regime. And if something is left over, such as the felt character of each emotion or the idea that a culture can reflect on and choose its own emotional repertoire, is that residue a defect in my framework, or evidence that the original debate was doing something other than functional explanation?
