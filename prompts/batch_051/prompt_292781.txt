I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether psychopaths know that what they do is wrong. Not as a definitional thing, more what my gut says on cases, and then whether there's a principle behind it. Here's where I've landed.

Ray is a diagnosed psychopath in a forensic unit. If you ask him whether it's wrong to hurt someone weaker than you, he says yes, obviously. If you ask why, he says you shouldn't hurt people. But if you ask what he'd feel if he did it, he says he doesn't get the question; feeling bad just isn't something he does. My gut: he knows it's wrong. Feelings can't be required, otherwise someone who couldn't feel guilt couldn't know, and that seems wrong.

Same guy, different interview: he says sure, there's a rule against hurting people, but he doesn't see it as applying to him when he can get away with it; rules are a racket the weak use to protect themselves, and he's not weak. My gut flips: he doesn't know it's wrong after all. He knows the rule, but he's not in touch with the wrongness.

Sasha can pass the moral/conventional task all day. She'll tell you hitting someone is wrong because it hurts them, while wearing pyjamas to school is only wrong because the school says so. But she treats moral rules like a price list: she breaks them whenever the payoff beats the likely penalty, and she basically uses wrong to mean not worth it. I don't think she knows it's wrong.

Priya grew up with normal emotions but for whatever reason no one ever used moral words around her — no wrong, no bad, nothing. She feels awful when she hurts people and avoids doing it. My gut: she knows hurting people is wrong even though she couldn't label it, so the knowledge isn't about the words.

Dev was diagnosed with psychopathy as a teenager. He can describe guilt in detail from the outside — better than I can. But he says he's never once felt it, and he uses wrong only to report what other people would say. I keep going back and forth: one day I want to say he has all the facts, another day I want to say he's missing the one fact that matters.

So: is there a principle that captures my settled verdicts, and if no principle gets all of them, which verdict am I going to have to give up and why? What does the principle say about Dev, and about the case that actually got me started on all this: someone who spent decades hurting people, then had a head injury that stripped his emotions, and now, fully recovered intellectually, insists he still knows it was wrong, he just doesn't feel it anymore. Does he know, and should it matter whether we're asking one day or one year after the injury?
