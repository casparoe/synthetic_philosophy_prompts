I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. Lately I have been trying to get clear about a debate in the philosophical literature on hedonism and wellbeing. Hedonism says that pleasure, and only pleasure, is what ultimately makes a life go well; every other good is good only because it produces or constitutes pleasure. A familiar objection is the heterogeneity problem: the states we call pleasures appear to have very little in common — the pleasure of a warm bath, of solving a difficult proof, of being vindicated long after an argument, of a morphine high, of listening to music, of scratching an itch. If 'pleasure' is merely the name we give to different states that we happen to want or like, then hedonism looks either circular (pleasure means whatever we like, and wellbeing means pleasure) or committed to some shared ingredient it cannot specify.

As I understand it, hedonists offer two main replies. The felt-quality reply says there is one distinctive felt quality, a common 'tone' of pleasantness, present in every case whatever else differs. The attitudinal reply says pleasure is not a special feeling at all but a way of relating to any experience whatever — roughly, taking an attitude of liking or satisfaction toward it — so the common element is relational rather than sensory.

I would like you not to explain the debate in general terms, but to translate it into the vocabulary I use daily and understand precisely as a software engineer: interfaces, abstract base classes, duck typing, structural versus nominal typing, union types, marker interfaces, and generic wrapper types. Take the heterogeneity problem and the two replies one at a time, say what maps cleanly onto these concepts and what you had to stretch to make it fit. Then give me a residue report: which parts of each position have no genuine counterpart in that vocabulary, and whether each absence reflects a limitation of the software vocabulary or a place where the philosophical position was leaning on an idea it had not actually made precise. My working hypothesis is that the heterogeneity problem is largely the observation that 'pleasure' is duck-typed — that hedonism wants a common interface but has only a shared label — and that the felt-quality reply tries to impose a nominal type where there is none. Tell me whether that hypothesis is right, what it misses, and what would count as real progress on the underlying question once it is restated this way. Please write your answer in British English.
