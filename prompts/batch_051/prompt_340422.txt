My epistemology reading group has been fighting about closure under known entailment for months, and the two holdouts have finally agreed to try an adversarial collaboration instead of arguing past each other. One is a Nozick-style tracking theorist: S knows p only if S would not believe p if p were false, so you know the animal in the zoo enclosure is a zebra (that belief is sensitive) but you don't know it isn't a cleverly painted mule (that belief isn't sensitive), and closure is therefore false. The other is a closure-preserving contextualist: she thinks knowledge is closed under known entailment within any fixed conversational context, but that "knows" picks out stricter or laxer standards in different contexts; in the ordinary zoo context you know both "it's a zebra" and "it's not a painted mule," while in a skeptical context you know neither. We've hashed out the cases—zebra, lottery ticket, brain-in-a-vat, fake barns—and agreed on the surface descriptions, but not on what to say about them.

Draft the joint statement they could both sign after an honest go at this. Begin with the document itself, no preface or commentary. Include (1) a numbered list of claims both accept, including the neutral description of each case and the precise statement of sensitivity and closure as each side uses them; (2) a precise statement of the residual disagreement, with each side's position written as that side would accept it; (3) a disagreements table with the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction about it. Don't adjudicate; the point is to get formulations that don't strawman either side.
