I work as a software engineer, mostly on formal verification and on making compilers behave under refinement. Lately I have been unable to settle a tension in how I think about the foundations of mathematics, and I want a careful diagnosis of it rather than a vote for one side.

On one side there is a view I find genuinely compelling and that I reached through my job rather than through any formal study of philosophy. In my work I rely on the soundness of a proof assistant's kernel, and the standard incompleteness results tell me that no sufficiently strong consistent system can prove its own consistency. Whatever certificate I hold in my hand, at the bottom of the dependency graph there is an assumption with no lower layer to check it against. From that standpoint, the consistency of arithmetic is not something I know; it is an unchecked edge in the trust graph. The view attracts me because it is honest, uniform, and structurally identical to how I already think about software correctness: you never prove the whole stack, you shrink the unaudited part and make its failure modes visible.

On the other side I have an intuition I cannot shed. If Peano arithmetic were inconsistent, then somewhere there is a finite derivation of 0=1 from the usual axioms, and the natural numbers would not be a model of them. But arithmetic is not a speculative theory that I have merely observed working; the natural numbers feel like something I know directly, perhaps the most reliable thing I use. I can entertain the possibility that this or that exotic extension of set theory is inconsistent, but the claim that counting itself sits on a hidden contradiction strikes me as not worth taking seriously.

I am not asking you simply to tell me that the formal point is correct and the intuition is naive, or the reverse. I want to know what it would take for each side to give. Should I debunk the intuition as a byproduct of having checked only a tiny finite fragment of an infinite structure, and therefore as an inductive guess dressed up as certainty? Should I amend the formal view by distinguishing what can be proved inside a system from what can be known about an intended model from outside, and by saying the natural numbers themselves settle the question even though no proof can be written in the system? Or is there a stable reading on which both survive because they answer different questions, one about what a system can certify and one about what the axioms are about? Please present each option at its strongest and say which concession is most justified.
