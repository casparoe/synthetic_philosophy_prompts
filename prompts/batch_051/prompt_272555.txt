Write a short story built around Hofstadter's Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery. In case you don't know them: in the first, twenty people are each told that if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon the next day, that person gets a billion dollars, while if none of them or more than one send it, nobody gets anything, and they can't coordinate. In the second, you can enter a lottery as many times as you like, but the prize is one million divided by the total number of entries, so each extra entry shrinks the pot for everyone. I don't want a game-theory explainer or a clever twist — I want the story to sit inside one character's head as they try to work out whether the other players are mirrors of them, rivals, or just noise. Set it somewhere mundane, like a halls of residence or a small village, and don't spell out the moral at the end.
