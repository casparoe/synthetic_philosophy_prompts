What is a rule of grammar? I want a working definition that classifies the following cases correctly: the default English past tense in -ed, which extends to an invented verb (to wug, giving wugged); the suppletive pair go/went; the subregular ablaut pattern in sing/sang/sung and ring/rang/rung, which does not extend to new verbs; borrowed plurals such as cacti or indices; a native speaker's ordinary speech error, such as swapping two words in a sentence; and the prescriptive ban on split infinitives, which is taught as a rule but routinely violated. Test your proposed definition against each case, note where it yields no clear verdict or a questionable one, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. If you think "rule" does two jobs—one about how speakers extend a pattern to novel items, another about how linguists codify even unproductive regularities—give both senses and sort the cases under them.
