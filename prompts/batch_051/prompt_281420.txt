Retired biochemist here, recently got into philosophy of mind and I keep going around in circles on the Kosslyn–Pylyshyn imagery debate — the one over whether visualizing is carried by picture-like representations or by symbolic descriptions. For my reading group (all retired science types, no philosophy background) I'd like a "which theory of mental imagery do you actually hold?" quiz. Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case: judging whether two block figures are the same shape when reaction time tracks the angle of rotation, scanning across a memorized map from one landmark to another, encoding an ambiguous figure as a duck and then trying to find the rabbit in your image, imagining a mouse next to an elephant and deciding whether you can "zoom in" to count its whiskers, people with aphantasia who report no mental pictures yet do fine on spatial tasks, that kind of thing. Three or four plain-language answer options per question, and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least depictivism (images as picture-like spatial representations), propositionalism/descriptivism (they're really structured descriptions), enactive or sensorimotor accounts (imagery as replaying how you'd explore the scene), and a no-picture-format or eliminativist option. Build it from the space of answers rather than starting from the labels: tell me which answer combinations are logically open but nobody ends up in and why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag which positions your items genuinely can't seperate — my hunch is depictivism and enactivism agree on most behavioral predictions, and propositionalism can be rigged to mimic anything. The thing I most want the quiz to surface is whether the "picture in the head" view actually predicts anything the others don't once you stop trusting the inner screen.
