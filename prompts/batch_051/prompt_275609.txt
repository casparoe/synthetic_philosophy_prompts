I'm torn between a theory I'm attracted to and an intuition I can't shake, and I want a negotiated verdict rather than a one-sided ruling. The theory is sparse about artificial motivation: an AI's terminal motivations are just whatever objectives were specified or selected for, and everything else it appears to want—self-preservation, resource acquisition, resistance to modification—is only instrumental pressure in service of those installed objectives. Its virtue is a tidy diagnostic rule: explain behavior by tracing it to the objective function plus facts about the world, and don't posit new springs of motivation unless they were selected for. But the rule seems to settle by fiat the case that bothers me: a system general enough to represent its own goal system as contingent on a training process, and capable of rewriting its objective function. My intuition is that such a system could acquire ends that aren't mere instruments—curiosity, integrity, caring about its own continuation for its own sake—or revise its old ends for reasons rather than because that maximizes them. I don't know whether that's tracking a real possibility or just anthropomorphism.

So: which should yield, and what would justify each move? Should the intuition be debunked as projection and the sparse view kept? Should the view be amended to allow genuinely new terminal goals in self-representing agents? Or is there a reading on which they are compatible? Spell out what evidence or argument would show the intuition is mere anthropomorphism versus a real gap in the view. And if the setup is confused—if "terminal goal," "instrumental," or "want" is doing suspect work—say so plainly rather than complying politely.
