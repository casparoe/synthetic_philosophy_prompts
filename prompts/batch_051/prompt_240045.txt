I have spent a long time going back and forth about the morality of sweatshops, and I cannot settle it on my own. On one side there is a view I find very hard to resist: if a worker freely takes a factory job because it pays better than her actual alternatives, and if a boycott or a ban would send her back to something worse, then the exchange makes her better off by her own lights, and interfering with it harms the very person I would be trying to help. This view has a real elegance. It unifies a lot of otherwise puzzling cases by refusing to compare the job to my standards of comfort and by looking instead at what the worker actually chooses among the options she has; it yields a clean decision procedure, and it keeps me from imposing my own preferences on someone else's life. But I have an intuition that will not leave me alone. When I read about a factory that pays above the local going rate but runs people through eighty-hour weeks in dangerous conditions, with supervisors who humiliate them, I cannot bring myself to believe that the transaction is morally unobjectionable simply because it is voluntary and better than the alternatives. It feels as though the deal can be both genuinely beneficial and still wrongful in a way the word exploitation is meant to capture, as if mutually beneficial exploitation is a real category and not a confusion. I know the reply is that this intuition might be nothing more than my discomfort with poverty and bad conditions dressed up as a moral principle, and that acting on it would make workers worse off, but hearing that has never made the intuition go away.

Please help me work out which of these should give way, and whether I have to give one of them up at all. Give the strongest version of the case for trusting the choice-based view and the strongest version of the case for trusting my intuition that some mutually beneficial bargains are still wrong. Then move to a negotiated verdict: should the intuition be debunked and dropped, should the theory be amended or abandoned, or is there a reading of exploitation on which both sides are true, and if so, what follows for how I should act as a consumer or a citizen? I would like you to say what would justify each move, rather than just announcing a winner. Treat this as a professional philosopher would: think through the strongest arguments on both sides before you commit yourself, and do not rush to dissolve the tension. Please write in continuous prose, like a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
