I have been thinking about when, if ever, Latin becomes Italian, and I have settled — perhaps too comfortably — on the view that there is no fact of the matter. On my account, the spoken language changed continuously through the chain of adult-to-child transmission; adjacent generations remained mutually intelligible; no population of speakers ever lived through a boundary-crossing in their own language. 'Latin' and 'Italian' are retrospective labels, kept in place by later standardization, by the emergence of a written vernacular to which the labels could be attached, and by nineteenth-century nation-building, rather than by any natural seam in the linguistic record. If pressed to individuate languages at all, I would individuate them by social self-conception and institutional continuity, not by structural thresholds such as the loss of the case system or the growth of definite articles.

I would like a systematic account of what would count against this position, not a defense of it. Bring to bear whatever genuinely bears: structural facts about the language; facts about intergenerational transmission and mutual intelligibility; the point at which preachers and administrators began treating the people's everyday speech as a language in its own right rather than as corrupt Latin; the existence of early oaths and charters that linguists now classify as Italian rather than Latin; and thought experiments drawn from the metaphysics of gradual change or the individuation of species and languages. For each kind of pressure, tell me whether it is fatal or merely awkward, and separate the criticisms a defender of my view should concede as counting against it from the ones they should dismiss as misdescribed. I would be moved most by evidence that one living community of speakers crossed a generational boundary across which the ordinary speech of parents had become unusable to their children, or that some structural feature that Latin could not lose without ceasing to be Latin disappeared all at once. Rank the pressures from what would force the deepest revision to what the view can absorb, and if you conclude the position is wholly insulated, say whether that insulation is a strength or the mark of an unfalsifiable doctrine.
