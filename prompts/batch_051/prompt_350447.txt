Here's a claim I've been turning over since re-reading Dennett's brain-transplant story — the one where his brain's in a vat, his body is out doing errands, and he can't say where he is. The claim: the felt location of the self (that sense of being behind your eyes or between your ears) is a running inference the brain constructs from whatever sensory evidence is available, not a readout of some deeper fact about where the self really is. I want to know whether this claim is testable, and what the best test would look like. Design the strongest experiment or set of observations you can. Be concrete about what you'd manipulate and measure, what pattern of results would count for the claim, what pattern would count against it, and — the part I keep geting stuck on — what a completely positive result would still fail to settle. In particular: is there a difference between testing where the self is and testing where people say they feel located, and does that difference matter, or is it just hand-wringing?
