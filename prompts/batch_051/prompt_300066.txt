I am a philosophy professor who has been embedded in a reinforcement-learning research group long enough to think comfortably in both idioms, and I keep trying to restate Moritz Schlick's view of the meaning of life in the language of that group. Schlick's position, roughly, is that a meaningful life is one lived in the spirit of play: its activities are pursued for their own sake rather than as means to ends outside themselves, so the meaning of life is not a further goal beyond life but a quality of the living itself. Restate that position in the vocabulary of reward design and reinforcement learning — intrinsic versus extrinsic rewards, terminal versus instrumental goals, reward hacking, dense and sparse rewards, and the difference between specifying an objective function and evaluating ongoing behaviour. Then give me the residue report: which of Schlick's claims translate cleanly, which translate only under strain, and which have no counterpart in that vocabulary at all. I suspect, for instance, that any claim about which activities one should pursue, and any claim about meaning as a quality of the present moment, may not survive. I want to know whether the RL vocabulary captures the substance of Schlick's view or merely a flattering approximation, and whether what fails to translate is a limitation of the vocabulary or an obscurity in Schlick. Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of the question. Answer in British English.
