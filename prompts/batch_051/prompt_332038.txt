Here's one for a mid-level metaphysics seminar I run. I don't want a survey of theories of dispositions; I want the desiderata. That is: write the list of requirements an account of disposition ascriptions has to meet to count as adequate — ascriptions like 'this glass is fragile' or 'this sample is water-soluble' — with the reason each requirement earns its place. Mark which are non-negotiable and which are really just preferences of particular camps (the conditional-analysis tradition, the primitive-disposition people, the categorical-base-plus-laws people). For concreteness: an adequate account has to earn its keep in ordinary materials-talk without collapsing the first time it meets the seminar's harder cases.

You have to cover the obvious phenomena — a fragile glass never struck, a finkish case where the trigger would remove the disposition (a sorcerer waiting to stiffen the glass at the last instant), and a mask case where the manifestation is blocked without removing the disposition (the same glass packed in styrofoam). Also make the theory respect the distinction between masked and simply absent. Then get into the weeds: add two or three requirements that actually do exclusionary work rather than just listing platitudes — comparative ascriptions like 'more fragile than', what a theory is allowed to say when all you have is a finite behavioural record and no direct counterfactual data, and whether a fink and a mask should come out as two different kinds of failure of the naive conditional analysis rather than one. Say which of your requirements ends up doing the most to exclude candidates. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I'm thinking of the usual tension between a reductive, non-circular analysis and covering fink/mask cases without a hand-wavey all-else-being-equal clause — say so, and which requirement you'd relax first. Skip scoring the extant theories; the critera are the deliverable. Answer in British English please.
