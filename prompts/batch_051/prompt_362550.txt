Two regulars in the ethics threads of a rationalist forum I read spent several days arguing about whether prioritarianism actually escapes the leveling-down objection. (1) The first said it plainly does. Prioritarianism ranks outcomes by summing an increasing, concave function of each person's absolute well-being; if you lower one person's well-being and improve nobody's, the sum has to go down. So a leveling-down "improvement" is impossible on the view. The objection bites only views on which equality itself is a good. (2) The second replied that the concave function is inequality aversion wearing different notation. Weighting a given increment of well-being less when it goes to someone already better off is a way of saying relative position matters, so prioritarianism is egalitarianism with the distributive judgment hidden inside the individual utility function; the leveling-down charge simply gets relocated to the foundations. (3) The first answered that the weighting could instead track absolute urgency. Consider two populations that never interact and cannot compare their situations; we might still count a given absolute level of well-being as more important when someone is at a lower level. Since no interpersonal or comparative grievance can be doing the work there, the weight is not secretly about inequality. And because the function is strictly increasing, the view never prefers harming the better off just to narrow a gap. (4) The second came back that if only absolute level bears the weight, prioritarianism owes us an account of why a unit of well-being should morally shrink as level rises—and the natural account is diminishing marginal value of resources or need-satisfaction, which is not the same view. If the concavity is instead a brute axiom, then it is under-motivated enough that a different, more aggressive function could be defended by the same move, and the boundary between priority and leveling-down egalitarianism is merely where one happens to plant the function.

I read the whole exchange and could not settle which moves carry weight. Give a point-by-point adjudication of the four moves as actually made: identify which specific claims survive, which fail, and where the second commenter's "backdoor egalitarianism" charge sticks versus overreaches. In particular, say whether the expression "inequality aversion written into the value function" conflates valuing relative position with valuing absolute level, and whether the isolated-populations reply answers the original objection or only answers a different charge.
