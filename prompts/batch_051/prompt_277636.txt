My brother-in-law and I run a small orchard and we're stuck on whether to sell this year's apples fresh or to the cider press. He's got this argument: call an apple "fruke" if either it was picked before this October and was ripe, or it gets picked after this October and turns out hard. Every north-field apple we've ever picked was picked before October and ripe, so as far as we've seen, north-field apples are fruke. By induction, this year's apples will be fruke too; since we won't pick them until after October, they'll be hard, so we should sell to the press. I can't find a factual mistake in what he said, but it feels like a terrible reason to give up the fresh market. Is his induction really as good as the plain one that north-field apples always ripen? If not, what exactly is wrong with it?
