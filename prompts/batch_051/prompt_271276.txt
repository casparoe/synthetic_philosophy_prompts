The episode I want to think through is familiar: in late 2021, high-income countries were rolling out booster doses to healthy adults while first-dose coverage in much of the lower-income world remained very low. I know the facts and the supply-chain practicalities; I want the normative analysis, mapped in both directions. Analyze that episode through prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection. First, from theory to case: who are the relevant decision-makers, what were the feasible options, which features of the case the verdict turns on, and what would a prioritarian say about a country that refuses boosters for its own healthy adults as a gesture of global solidarity when the withheld doses will not in fact reach anyone worse off? Second, from case to theory: does the booster episode vindicate prioritarianism's standard reply to the leveling-down objection, or does it show that the objection needs adapting once there is no single redistributive scheme, only states acting separately? End with an honest note on where prioritarianism is silent here, especially the size of the priority weight and how much partiality a state may show its own citizens.
