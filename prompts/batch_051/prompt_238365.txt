I've been covering AI for a while, and I keep coming back to an old proposal called AI safety via debate. The idea was: don't make a human judge evaluate a superhuman system's claims one by one. Instead, put two AIs in a debate, one defending a claim and one attacking it, and have a human pick the winner. The bet was that even a weak judge can handle superhuman questions, because the liar has to keep a whole web of falsehoods upright while the truth-teller just has to point at the one step that breaks. That seems to lean hard on the judge being able to tell a genuine refutation from a merely persuasive move. But then there's the worry about obfuscated arguments: a smart enough system could produce a case that looks rigorous wherever the judge pokes it, while the actual error is hidden in a subtly wrong definition, a scope ambiguity, or an innocent-looking lemma that nobody can quite pin down. If obfuscated arguments are a real possibility, does the original case for debate fairly collapse, and which premise exactly does it kill? Or can the protocol be patched—say, by making each side state the other side's crux, or by having judges probe the shakiest sentence—and does that just push the problem down a level?
