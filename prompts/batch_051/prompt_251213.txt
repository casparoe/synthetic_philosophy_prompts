I keep coming back to The Prestige as a way into Sleeping Beauty-style self-locating belief, and I want a reading that takes both the movie and the philosophy of centered worlds seriously. Each night the machine makes two Angiers: one drops into the water tank and drowns, the other appears in the balcony and takes the bow, and Angier admits he never knows beforehand which one he's about to be. That's basically Beauty's problem in a different key: she can't tell which waking she's in, he can't tell which of two centers of experience he's about to occupy. Here's what I want pressed: does the movie commit to both copies being equally Angier, or does it quietly need one to be the real original so the horror stays manageable? If both are equally him, the whole trick is just nightly suicide plus murder and the film is a horror movie in denial; if they're not both him, then the uncertainty about which one he'll be is incoherent because there's no further fact about which center is really him. I want the reading to face that dilemma, say where the film fudges it, and also say where the Sleeping Beauty analogy breaks (no memory wipe, and the copies are simultaneous rather than two sequential wakings). And write it in American English—specificly not a generic personal-identity essay, I want the self-locating/centered-world machinery.
