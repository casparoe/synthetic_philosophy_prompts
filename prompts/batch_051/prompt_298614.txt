I am a high school junior in an after-school political theory group that has spent the term on different concepts of political freedom — negative, positive, and republican liberty. For our next meeting, each member has to write out a short liberty profile, a list of what we actually believe about freedom, and submit it to the group for cross-examination. I have drafted mine, and I have a nagging sense that the pieces do not all cohere, but I cannot locate the strain; I do not want to be told what to believe, only where my own list is pulling against itself. The commitments I plan to submit are these: (1) freedom is fundamentally about interference by other people — I am unfree when another person deliberately constrains, coerces, or blocks me, and otherwise free; (2) internal obstacles such as compulsion, addiction, ignorance, and weakness of will do not make me unfree in the political sense, though they may prevent me from governing myself, which is a different problem; (3) a slave whose master happens never to interfere with them is nevertheless unfree, not merely unfortunate or insecure; (4) lacking the money to do something, such as traveling abroad, is not by itself a restriction of my freedom, however serious an injustice it may be; (5) taxation restricts freedom even when the public goods it funds — roads, courts, schools — leave everyone more able to do things than they were before.

Go through these commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity, and for each genuine tension what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost. Also flag any pairs that only appear to conflict but do not, and explain the distinction that dissolves the appearance. Finally, tell me which single revision of one commitment would resolve the most tension overall, and what I would have to surrender to accept it.
