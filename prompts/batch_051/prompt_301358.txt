Ok here's one I keep flip-flopping on and I want a document I could hand to both sides without either saying "that's not my view." The paradox of blackmail. Three things our legal system treats as usually fine: threatening to do something you're legally allowed to do ("give me a raise or I quit"), revealing true embarrassing information, and selling your silence (NDAs are literally a market in silence). String them together—"pay me or I tell people the true embarrassing thing I know about you"—and sudddenly it's a crime. That gap is weird, and it gets weirder the more I stare at it.

I want an adversarial-collaboration writeup between two camps. Camp A, the legalizers: blackmail is just selling silence over information you own; the victim is buying non-disclosure, and the law is banning voluntary trade between adults for basically expressive reasons. Camp B, the keep-it-criminal side: blackmail is extortionate even when the information is true; it's not "selling silence," it's manufacturing a harm you'd otherwise never deliver and charging rent to not deliver it. (Leave aside threats to report crimes—those are their own thing and not the hard cases.)

The deliverable format:

1. Numbered claims both camps can jointly sign. Make them non-vacuous: claims about what ordinary hard bargaining actually is, about why "two legal acts compose into a legal act" feels like it's missing something (or doesn't), about what's genuinely different, if anything, between "pay me or I quit" and "pay me or I tell your spouse." Whatever overlap is real.

2. A disagreements table: the issue, the legalizer wording of their position, the retentionist wording of theirs, each phrased so that side would sign it without flinching.

3. The cruxes. For each, say which sub-question resolving one way or the other would move at least one camp, and what each side would count as evidence or argument against their own view. Be concrete: e.g., does the dispute reduce to where you set the baseline for coercion (status-quo non-disclosure vs. the set of acts the threatener is entitled to do), or to empirical predictions about what a legal-blackmail world looks like? Record each side's prediction in advance.

4. If part of the fight is verbal—"coercion," "voluntary," "ownership of information" meaning different things—say which part and rewrite the two positions without the poisoned word.

And don't let either side get off with a vibes-only line. If the retentionists can't give a principle that keeps NDAs and exit threats while banning "pay me or I tell your spouse," that should show up as an internal cost they have to own. If the legalizers have to bite the bullet that blackmailing someone over nudes or medical records is fine, that should show up too. I want the point where each side starts paying for its position, stated from the inside.
