I'm taking a philosophy of psychiatry course and we've been reading Ian Hacking on looping effects. My professor handed out a one-paragraph takeaway that we're supposed to keep in mind for discussion, and it goes like this: "Hacking's looping effects show that psychiatric categories are interactive kinds, which is his term for classifications that end up changing the people they classify. Once a label like multiple personality disorder enters circulation, patients absorb the label and start performing the symptoms it describes; clinicians then see more of those symptoms and revise the diagnostic criteria, which patients absorb again, and around it goes. This means the classification helps create the very disorder it pretends only to describe. Before the MPD diagnosis was available, there weren't really any cases of it; after the diagnosis became famous, cases exploded. Hacking's conclusion is that mental disorders are social constructions in a strong sense, made by our labels rather than found in nature, so treating them as biological kinds is a category mistake."

Something about this feels like it slides from a modest point to a much stronger one. I remember Hacking being careful to say that the suffering itself is real and that looping doesn't make a disorder fake, and I'm pretty sure he distinguishes interactive kinds from something like indifferent kinds, but I can't tell whether my professor's summary is a fair compression or a subtle strawman. Could you go through it and sort out which parts Hacking would sign off on, which parts are overstatements or conflations, and whether the final conclusion actually follows from the accurate parts?
