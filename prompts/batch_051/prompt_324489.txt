Bit of an odd one, but I'm a PhD student in philosophy of psychiatry and I've been reading Ian Hacking on looping effects, and I want to have a go at something creative instead of another seminar paper. The idea I'm working with: when you classify people (say with a psychiatric label), the people who are classified learn about the label, fold it into how they understand themselves, and start acting differently, which means the classification itself eventually has to be revised to keep up with the people it partly created. So a kind of person is a moving target, unlike an indifferent kind, like a quark, which couldn't care less what we call it. Hacking's own examples include multiple personality and fugue, but I don't want the poem to be a case study.

Could you write a pantoum that enacts this looping effect? Use the pantoum's rule — lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the final stanza folds back to lines from the first — so the form itself is the loop: the same words keep returning in new contexts and mean something different each time, which is the whole point. Keep it split between the two sides of the loop if you can, the one classifying and the one classified, or a single speaker who keeps sliding between the two. Four or five quatrains is plenty. The ending should make the reader feel that the classification has been changed by the person it named, not just the other way round. Dont mention Hacking in the poem; make it accurate enough that someone who knows the theory would recognise it, and a decent enough poem that someone who doesn't would want to read it anyway.

I know pantoums can get repetitive. Use the repetitions so the shifting context does the philosophical work, rather than just restating the same thought. Oh, and don't make it about multiple personality specifically; I'd rather you invent a mild diagnostic-sounding label so it stays general. Actually, one more thing: try to get in that the loop isn't a simple self-fulfilling prophecy — the person isn't just conforming to the label, they're responding creatively, and the kind itself mutates, which is what keeps the loop moving.
