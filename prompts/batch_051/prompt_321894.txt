Suppose a stand-up comedy streaming service institutes a pre-publication audit modelled on the benign violation theory of humour. Every special is screened by trained raters who score each joke on two ten-point scales: the degree to which it violates a norm, dignity, or expected order, and the degree to which its framing signals play, distance, safety, or consent. A joke is cleared only if both scores clear a fixed threshold; high violation with low benign is rejected as offensive, while high benign with low violation is rejected as too tame. Analyze how this mechanism would shape the comedy that gets produced and released. Which sorts of jokes would survive, and which of the major theories of humour — superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation — would the filter systematically favour or exclude? How would comics adapt their writing to the rubric, and how would the raters themselves be gamed, captured, or transformed? Would the audit ultimately select for good comedy, for comedy that is merely legible to the rubric, or for something else?
