ive been trying to work out original sin on my own without reading the actual theology, and i keep coming back to something i call 'the same body rule'. the thought is: a family, a nation, or a church is not just the people in it right now; it's a thing that keeps existing while members come and go, like a club that still owes a debt after everyone who borrowed the money is dead. if that continuing thing did something wrong in the past, then your being a member now is enough for the guilt to land on you — not because it was passed down or inherited, but because you are literally part of the same thing that did it. that would explain why people today get blamed for what their country did in, say, 1700, and maybe it explains the idea that everyone shares the guilt of the first sin without needing some weird biological transfer. what i want to know is whether this is actually different from the usual idea of inherited guilt or just a dressed up version of it, whether its already been worked out under some other name, and where it breaks. when exactly is a group 'the same group' enough for its old wrongs to attach to new members — a club that changes its rules, a country that changes its constitution, a church that changes its teachings? give me the strongest objections and say whether any of them are fatal or just uncomfortable.
