Ive been chewing on Pascals claim, from the Pensées, roughly: justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical, so you have to put them together. It sounds right as a description of politics. But I also have this very firm intuition that a constitutional court can get a government to comply with a ruling it hates even when the court itself has no army or police. Like the US Supreme Court after Brown v. Board, or the German constitutional court blocking something a chancellor wanted to do. Nobody went out and arrested the president; compliance came through some mix of legitimacy, habit, and fear of political damage. That feels like justice without force doing real work, not just force in disguise.

Pascal might answer that legitimacy itself is just past force that has cooled into habits, or that some other actor with force stands behind the court. But I worry that move makes the claim unfalsifiable: if every case of voluntary compliance gets redescribed as disguised force, then force no longer explains anything; it just labels whatever wins.
