The thesis I'm defending is roughly this: once a game depicts a real, nameable armed conflict — a specific war, battle, or an ongoing conflict — it shouldn't be a frictionless power fantasy. And I'm explicitly not making the old "games cause violence" argument. My claim is an aesthetic/moral one: turning someone's actual war into a frictionless playground is a wrong against the dead and survivors, not a copycat risk. (So don't let the audience hide behind Spec Ops: The Line as if one self-critical shooter settles the whole question.)

The audience is mostly game studies folks plus a few indie designers. What I want is a prep sheet, not a defense of my talk. For each objection: how likely is it, how damaging is it, how would this audience actually phrase it (use their vocabulary — procedural rhetoric, player agency, maybe the "games are systems not texts" line — not my term "ludic friction"), what's my best short reply, and where is that reply weakest?
