I've been reading around on intuitionistic logic and came across this summary on a math blog (the author doesn't give sources, so I can't check where it's from):

"Intuitionistic logic is what you get when you refuse the law of the excluded middle. Classical mathematicians treat every statement as true or false independently of whether anyone knows which. Constructivists like Brouwer held that truth in mathematics just is having a proof, so a statement with no known proof is neither true nor false until someone proves or refutes it. This is why intuitionists reject proof by contradiction across the board: from the fact that not-P leads to a contradiction you can only conclude not-not-P, and without double negation elimination you can't get from there to P. Constructive mathematicians therefore cannot prove existence by showing that non-existence is impossible; they have to exhibit the object. The pay-off is that every proof in intuitionistic logic comes with an algorithm attached, so programs can be extracted from proofs."
