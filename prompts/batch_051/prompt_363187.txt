i'm drawn to finitism about the actual infinite: the Hilbert's hotel rearrangements and the part–whole point that a proper subset shouldn't be equal in size to the whole convice me that a completed infinite collection is unintelligible, and the tidy line is that 'infinite' talk is always shorthand for a rule you can keep extending — for any number of days, there's another; for any list of primes, there's a bigger one. but i also have a genuine rock-bottom intuition that time could just keep going forever, day after day with no last member, and that 'there are infinitely many primes' is a fact about the numbers all at once, not a fact about our ability to keep counting. i can't tell whether the finitist retranslation of 'infinitely many' is a real restatement or just changing the subject, and i can't actually bring myself to call the endless future an illusion. i don't want you to tell me which side to give up. i want to know what would have to happen for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would have to happen for finitism to be the thing that goes (or gets amended), and whether there's a reading of 'infinite' on which the endless future and the primes are real while Hilbert's hotel loses its bite.
