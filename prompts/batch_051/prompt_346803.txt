ok so i have this theory i've been turning over for a while, and i want an honest assessment, not encouragement. i call it the scar vs sticker theory of generations. the idea: a generation is only a real group if there was some big shared event that hit people during the years their sense of how the world works was still forming, roughly 12 to 25, and actually changed their defaults. that's a scar generation. if the label was mostly invented by marketers or demographers and the people in that birth-year range didn't go through some shared formative shock together, it's a sticker generation: the label got stuck on, but there isn't really one group underneath it. so boomers would be relatively scar (cold war, post-war boom, all that landing on the same formative window), gen z would be scar because covid hit basically all of them during school years, and millenials would be kind of half-scar because 9/11 and the 2008 crash hit them at really different ages, so the mark is smeared. the examples are less my point than the distinction. my actual claims are that "generation" isn't one kind of thing (some are scars, some are stickers, and most are somewhere in between), and that a birth-year range only forms a real generation when the event actually lands inside that formative window for most of the people in the range. is this coherent, or is the scar/sticker metaphor doing too much work? does it already exist under a name i would know? and where does it break — what does it get wrong about how generations actually form and change? also, please keep it honest about what's actually settled versus what people who study generations still genuinely fight about, instead of making one side sound like the consensus.
