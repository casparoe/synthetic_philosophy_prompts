So I've been on this whole equanimity kick lately because my dad got sick last spring and I stayed eerily level through the hospital stuff, and I genuinely could not tell whether that was real calm or just me hiding from the fear. I run a tiny philosophy-ish reading group and I want to make a handout for us: a "which view of equanimity do you actually hold?" sorter. 9 questions, each a concrete little scenario with three answer options, plus a scoring key at the end.

The positions I want it to sort people into are these five: (1) Stoic-ish apatheia, where the passions are false judgments about what matters and the goal is to stop having them rather than manage them; (2) Aristotelian metriopatheia, where feeling anger, grief, fear, etc. is fine as long as its moderate, at the right time, and about the right things; (3) Epicurean ataraxia, where tranquility comes from cutting unnecessary desires and fears, but friendship and simple pleasures are still part of the good; (4) Buddhist-style nonattachment, where you can care deeply without clinging, and equanimity lives alongside compassion rather than replacing it; (5) what I've been calling "calm as avoidance", where the surface is unbothered but underneath your disconnected, maybe even anhedonic, and the calm is costing you something.

The catch is the quiz has to sort by what people say about cases, not by which label they vibe with, so don't just ask "do you think emotions are bad?" Use cases like these, plus a few of your own in the same vein: a close friend's child dies; you win a major award; someone undercuts you in a meeting; your partner tells you they're leaving; you watch footage of a distant disaster; you live with chronic pain for six months. Make sure each answer option pushes toward one of the five positions, and write the scoring key so someone who mostly picks a mix of 2 and 3 still gets diagnosed as a mixed type rather than silently placed in one box.

At the end, flag which of the five a quiz of this kind can't reliably seperate (I suspect 4 and 5 get confused because both can look calm from the outside, but tell me if I'm wrong), and say for each confusion whether the views are genuinely adjacent or whether the questions are just not sharp enough.
