In David Hume's essay Of Tragedy, included in his Essays, Moral and Political, he starts from the fact that spectators enjoy well-written tragedies about grief and horror that they would shun in real life, and he suggests that the pleasure of the representation is the predominant movement and converts the painful passions into an overall pleasure rather than merely coexisting with them. I teach an upper-level undergraduate aesthetics seminar: fourteen students, one ninety-minute session. Design a structured in-class activity that makes students feel the difference between their reaction to a bare factual account of a misfortune and their reaction to a crafted tragic representation of a similar misfortune, and that leads them to test whether Hume's conversion model is a better description than either a safety/distance account or an additive account on which aesthetic pleasure simply outweighs the pain. Do not mention Hume or the paradox of fiction by name until the debrief. Give the rules and materials, a timeline, a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the three models, a note on what will predictably go wrong the first time, and an honest statement of which part of the conversion claim cannot be reproduced in the classroom.
