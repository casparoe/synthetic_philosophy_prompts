I am a philosophy professor working on a manuscript about the side-effect asymmetry in ordinary judgments of intentional action — the pattern the experimental philosophy literature associates with the Knobe effect. Over several years of teaching a graduate seminar, I have assembled a set of short vignettes on which my own considered verdicts have remained stable, and I would like your help before I add a further vignette to the set.

Here are my settled cases and verdicts.

(1) An executive starts a project in order to increase profits. She knows the project will damage a nearby wetland, though she is indifferent to that. The project goes ahead, and the wetland is damaged. I judge that she intentionally damaged the wetland.

(2) An executive starts a project in order to increase profits. She knows the project will restore a degraded wetland, though she is indifferent to that. The project goes ahead, and the wetland is restored. I judge that she did not intentionally restore the wetland.

(3) An athletics director plays a student athlete with a persistent knee injury because doing so will bring in revenue. She knows the decision will worsen the knee, though she is indifferent to that. The knee worsens. I judge that she intentionally worsened the student athlete's knee.

(4) An office manager replaces a building's lighting with a cheaper system in order to cut costs. She knows the change will lower the building's emissions, though she is indifferent to that. The emissions fall. I judge that she did not intentionally lower the building's emissions.

The new case is this: a surgeon administers a standard, clinically indicated drug that will cure a patient's serious infection. She knows the drug will cause the patient severe pain for several days, though she is indifferent to the pain. The patient is cured and endures the pain. Did the surgeon intentionally cause the patient's pain, or not?

My question is therefore threefold. First, which verdict on the new case would make me most consistent with my four settled verdicts — in other words, what should I judge — and which of the four settled cases is the closest analog? Second, if my earlier verdicts underdetermine the answer, what principle about foreseen but unaimed-at outcomes would I be adopting with each possible verdict on the new case? Third, propose a further vignette, not merely a duplicate of the new case, that would settle which of those principles I have actually been tracking.
