For a club project I'm making a decision tree handout about AI assistants, sort of like a "which D&D class are you" quiz but for when an AI should refuse or override a user request. My friends and I keep having the same arguement about ChatGPT and Siri refusing stuff, and we always talk past each other, so I want the questions to be concrete scenarios and the answer options to be actual choices (comply, refuse but explain, refuse and report, nudge, etc.), not labels. The idea is someone answers, follows the branches, and only at the end lands on a leaf telling them what their actual view is.

The scenarios I know I want to use are: (1) a 16-year-old asks for help writing a genuinely nice apology to a friend, and the AI knows it would word it better than the kid; (2) an adult asks the AI to help design a diet plan that is health-ish but the calorie target is way below what's safe; (3) someone asks for help writing a mean but not illegal text to a classmate they're mad at; (4) someone asks how to make a small homemade explosive for a "short film" and the story sounds fake; (5) an elderly person asks the AI to help send money to what's obviously a scam, but they're fully mentally competent and keep insisting; (6) a kid asks the AI to turn off the parental control time limits their parents set on their phone. Add one or two more cases if you need them to split positions these don't.

The leaves should include at least: hard user-sovereignty (a competent adult should get what they ask for), soft paternalism (warn/nudge but the user can override), hard paternalism (refuse for the user's own good), harm-to-others-only (refuse mainly when someone else gets hurt), and company-decides (minimize liability and bad PR for the maker). Build the tree from the questions upward rather than starting from the labels, mark any logically possible combinations that are empty and say why (incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody bothers to defend it), and flag any views the tree can't fully separate. Keep it around 7 questions and 6-8 leaves.
