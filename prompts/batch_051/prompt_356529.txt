The Homestead Act of 1862 is ordinarily taught as a story about opportunity, settlement, and the frontier. Analyze it instead as a stress test for Nozick's entitlement theory of justice in holdings, with the Wilt Chamberlain argument held in reserve as the theory's own statement of why voluntary exchange matters. For purposes of analysis, take the salient facts as given rather than relitigating them: the federal government offered settlers parcels of 160 acres of so-called public land for a small fee, conditional on five years of residence and improvement, and those lands had generally come into federal possession through coerced treaties and military campaigns against Indigenous nations — so they were not unowned in the way the theory's acquisition principle seems to presuppose.

Run the analysis in both directions. In one direction, use the three elements of Nozick's account — the just acquisition of unowned things, their transfer through genuinely voluntary exchange, and the rectification of holdings that arose from past injustice — to say precisely what would have to be established for a present-day holding traceable to a homestead claim to count as just. Focus especially on the downstream consensual transfers: does a long chain of sales, inheritance, and good-faith purchase cure a defect in the original acquisition, or does the defect travel with the title no matter how freely the land later moves? In the other direction, say what the episode does to the theory itself. Address whether the rectification element can realistically handle a non-ideal past of this kind, or whether the framework only yields determinate verdicts when it can begin from a clean baseline of genuinely unowned things and otherwise goes silent. Finally, assess what this means for the Wilt Chamberlain argument: if the free exchanges Chamberlain imagines begin from a distribution that is already unjust, does the bare fact of subsequent voluntary exchange carry the justificatory weight Nozick assigns to it, and does the argument retain its force against patterned principles of distribution, or does its lesson change?
