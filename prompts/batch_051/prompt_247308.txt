I've been reading about Fearon's rationalist explanations for war and trying to apply it to the AGI race, and I've talked myself into a specific position: the race is best understood as a commitment problem. The basic idea is that war is puzzling because their should usually be a bargain both sides prefer to fighting, and the main reasons rational actors fight anyway are private information (with incentives to misrepresent), commitment problems, and issue indivisibility. My view is that the AI race is the commitment problem case: all the major labs and governments would probably be better off with a credible, binding slowdown, but nobody can make a promise to slow down that the others should believe, because whoever defects first might win the whole thing. So racing is individually rational and collectively bad. I want to know what would count against this. Specifically: what observations or developments would push toward one of the other mechanisms (private information about capabilities or payoffs, or AGI being an indivisible prize) rather than commitment? What would suggest this isnt a Fearon-style bargaining situation at all, e.g. that the actors aren't rational in the relevant sense or that the race is being driven by something like status or ideology rather than strategy? And the uncomfortable question: is the commitment-problem reading set up so that every bad outcome counts as evidence for it? If it were wrong, what would that look like? I want the decisive evidence separated from the merely awkward, and an honest answer about whether the position is too insulated to be useful. Keep the response under 400 words.
