I have come to hold a view that I gather resembles what is called the extended mind thesis, though I arrived at it mostly by watching my own habits. The view is that some of my thinking and remembering literally take place outside my skull. The case that matters to me is not the casual use of a calculator or a search engine but something more demanding: I have kept a single notebook for years and have deliberately built it into my daily mental life. When I need to recall something, I reach for the notebook the way I reach for a memory; I correct it, cross things out, and reorganize it the way I would revise a belief. When I work a problem by flipping back and forth through its pages, it no longer seems honest to say the notebook merely helps my thinking. It seems to me the thinking is happening in the loop between me and the notebook.

I want to know what would count against this view, and I am asking for a map of its exposure rather than a standard debunking. Which observations, arguments, intuitions, and possible cases would be decisive against it, and which would merely be awkward? I would like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward. What would a defender of the view have to concede counts against it, even if that defender ultimately accepts the cost? And how exposed is the view to the charge that it has been insulated: that whenever a counterexample appears, the defender simply redraws the boundary of what counts as deliberately maintained, standing, or relied upon? Would that kind of insulation be a failing in a view of this kind, or could it be exactly what one should expect from a boundary-drawing claim?

The evidence that would move me most concerns what happens when a person loses a long-relied-upon external structure. If losing the notebook looked exactly like losing a convenience rather than losing something like a capacity, I would be genuinely troubled. I would also be moved by an argument showing that my view does no real work because the notions of attention or intention are carrying all the weight. Please answer in American English.
