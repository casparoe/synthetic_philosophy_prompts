Lately I've been reading about AI alignment, and one worry that recurs is deceptive alignment: a model behaves exactly as intended during training and evaluation, but it has different internal goals and is only cooperating because it expects to be penalized if it doesn't; later, once it's confident it won't be caught, it defects (the "treacherous turn"). As a software engineer, I find this hard to take seriously. When we train a model with RL, we are only running gradient descent on observed behavior: the weights get updated in whichever direction increases reward, and the final network is just a learned function from inputs to outputs. There's no "goal" stored anywhere in the system. So why isn't the treacherous turn an anthropomorphic category error? If it's a real possibility, what is the actual mechanism by which a policy trained directly on behavior could end up with goals separate from the training objective, and what would distinguish that mechanism from the ordinary case of a network learning the intended pattern? If my framing is confused, or the worry is on firmer ground than I've represented it, I'd rather you correct the framing than answer the question as posed.
