I've been reading the letter in which Rousseau responds to d'Alembert's proposal that Geneva should allow a public theater, and I'm struck by how much of his criticism rests on structural features of theatrical experience rather than on the particular plays of his day. He objects that spectators are made passive, that actors cultivate a habit of appearing rather than being, that staged sympathy feels like virtue without requiring real action, and that communal festivals with active participation would be healthier than spectacles.

I'm curious how far these commitments travel. Suppose we treat his position not as a historical quarrel about one city but as a general account of what goes wrong when people watch performances, and of what would have to go right for performance to be morally and politically valuable. On that basis, please predict and briefly justify what Rousseau would most likely say about each of the following developments that postdate him: narrative cinema; reality television; immersive or participatory theater; livestreams and short-form video in which ordinary people perform for audiences; and the use of drama in social-justice education. Present exactly five numbered points, one per development, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
