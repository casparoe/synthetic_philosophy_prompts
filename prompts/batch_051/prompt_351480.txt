I've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about Henry Sidgwick and the Society for Psychical Research, and something about it keeps nagging at me. Here was one of the most careful philosophical minds of the late nineteenth century—utilitarian, obsessed with method—who spent decades helping to collect and sift reports of telepathy, apparitions, and mediumship. From what I can tell, he never claimed the evidence was good enough to warrant full belief in survival, but he also didn't think it was worthless; he thought there was a real question there that deserved better methods than the spiritualists were using. It reads almost like he was trying to do preregistered metascience before anyone had those words.

So here's the exercise. Drop Sidgwick into the present, with everything we now know about effect sizes, publication bias, p-hacking, failed replications, and the general replication crisis. What would he think of the last few decades of parapsychology? I'm thinking of things like Bem's precognition experiments and the fights that followed, the meta-analyses of ganzfeld studies, the way the field has responded to calls for preregistration—basically the whole question of whether parapsychology cleaned up its act or just got better at producing null results. And more pointedly: would Sidgwick think the field has finally gotten closer to the kind of evidence he was asking for, or would he think the last century has shown the whole project was chasing noise? I want you to reason from his actual commitments—his standards for testimonial evidence, his worry about fraud and self-deception, his hope that survival might be demonstrated, and his broader views about when evidence is sufficient for belief—and be concrete about where he'd land and why. Don't just say "he'd be a skeptic"; a skeptic is anyone. Tell me what he'd count as progress, what he'd find disappointing, and whether he'd think the methodological lessons from parapsychology tell us something about psychology and medicine generally rather than about the paranormal specifically.
