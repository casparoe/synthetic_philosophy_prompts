Adam Smith holds that we judge our own conduct by adopting the standpoint of an impartial spectator, and he also holds that this spectator develops through sympathy with how actual onlookers respond to us. I do not see how these two claims fit together. If the impartial spectator is built out of the reactions of one's society, its judgments could never be more than internalized majority opinion; yet Smith clearly wants it to serve as a standard that corrects self-deception and partiality, perhaps even against the society around one. How can a figure assembled from the opinions of others ever be critical of those very opinions, and does Smith's account of moral judgment therefore collapse into a disguised relativism about the norms of whatever society produced the spectator?
