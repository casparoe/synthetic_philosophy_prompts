I'm putting together a short essay for a philosophy-and-film series, and I keep going back and forth about how much Christopher Nolan's Memento actually maps onto Dennett's idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity. As I understand Dennett, the self is not a soul or a spot in the brain but an abstraction we posit to make sense of a life the way we posit a center of gravity to make sense of a physical system: it organizes the web of stories we tell about ourselves and others tell about us, and it does useful work even though you will never find it located anywhere in particular. Memento looks like it was made for the view. Leonard can't form new memories, so he has to assemble himself out of notes, tattoos, Polaroids, and the story he tells strangers; his self is a narrative effect sustained by ongoing construction, not a stored substance. And the backwards structure puts the viewer in the same position, constantly reconstructing who Leonard is from fragments, wich seems like it extends the idea rather than just illustrating it.

But the film also makes Leonards narrative obviously unreliable, self-serving, and partly fabricated — the Sammy Jankis material, the way he curates what he writes down, the reveal that he sets up a lie he'll chase. That pulls against a flat reading: if the self is just a narrative center, why should it matter whether the narrative is true? What is the difference between being a self and merely having a self-conception, and does the film mark it? I want a reading that does justice to both directions instead of treating the movie as a diagram of Dennett. Where does the film genuinely dramatize or extend the theory (the external scaffolding, other people's competing accounts, the viewer's role), and where does it expose something the theory owes an account of — continuity, sincerity, embodiment, contact with fact, whatever you think it is? Does Dennett have the resources to say Leonard is a deluded self but still a self, or does the film show that a center of narrative gravity needs some anchor the theory has not named? Keep it under 850 words; I'm not looking for a full paper, just a tightly argued reading.
