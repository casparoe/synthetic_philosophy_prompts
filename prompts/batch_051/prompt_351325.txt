I am a high school junior on the committee charged with revising my school's student council constitution. Article II, Section 1 reads: "The Student Council shall consist of seven officers elected by the student body at the spring general election." The constitution was drafted in 1987, and until now everyone has treated "elected" as meaning chosen by secret ballot of the whole school. A new proposal would replace the spring election with a lottery: any student in good standing may enter, names are drawn publicly at an assembly, and those drawn take office. The proposal's author says this is the old practice of sortition and would be fairer and less of a popularity contest; another member says it is plainly unconstitutional because the officers would not be "elected."

I do not want a practical recommendation so much as a careful account of how the clause applies to the lottery. Please argue the question from at least these angles: (a) the ordinary meaning of the word "elected"; (b) the purpose the clause appears to serve; (c) what the 1987 drafters would have said if asked whether a lottery satisfies it; and (d) what a reasonable student reading the constitution today would expect "elected" to permit. Then tell me which approach should win and why.

In doing so, please also address whether the real issue is what "elected" means or what elections are for — choosing, holding officials accountable, and giving the council legitimacy — and whether switching to a lottery would alter the kind of body the clause creates rather than merely the method of filling it. I want the strongest version of each side, not a straw man.
