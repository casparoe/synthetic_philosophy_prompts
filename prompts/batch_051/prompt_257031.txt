I am a high school student who has been reading about the species problem in biology, and I am puzzled by why a debate this old has not settled. Biologists and philosophers of biology all seem to accept the same underlying facts — who interbreeds with whom, how much gene flow there is between populations, what the phylogenetic trees show, which organisms occupy which ecological roles — yet they still disagree about what a species is. Some say a species is a group of actually or potentially interbreeding natural populations that are reproductively isolated from other such groups. Others say a species is a segment of a lineage: the smallest group with a unique common ancestor that can be diagnosed by shared derived characters. Still others say a species is a cluster of organisms occupying a distinctive niche, while field naturalists sometimes fall back on recognizable morphological similarity. The difficulty is that these criteria pull apart in familiar cases. Ring species and hybrid zones break the interbreeding criterion; asexual organisms such as many bacteria have no interbreeding populations at all; and a strict lineage criterion threatens to make every isolated local population its own species.

I am less interested in which species concept is correct than in diagnosing the disagreement itself. My working hypothesis is that this is not one debate but several debates traveling under a single word, and that the real dispute is over what the category 'species' is for — whether it should pick out the units that actually evolve and speciate, or provide a workable sorting device for field guides, conservation law, and museum collections, or capture a structure in nature that exists independently of our sorting. I have also encountered the suggestion that species are not kinds at all but individuals: particular chunks of the genealogical network, rather than classes defined by a shared essence. If that were right, it would explain why no list of shared properties ever captures all and only species. Is my hypothesis right, or is the disagreement really about something else — a factual question, a verbal question, different questions under one name, or different standards for what counts as progress? What is the structure of this disagreement, and what would count as progress given your diagnosis?
