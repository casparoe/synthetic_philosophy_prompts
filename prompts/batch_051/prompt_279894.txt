A friend who teaches calculus likes to brush aside puzzles about supertasks by saying they are just infinite series in disguise. His line is this: an infinite series can converge to a finite sum, as in 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... = 1, so there is nothing conceptually troubling about a process in which infinitely many actions are packed into a finite time. Applied to Thomson's lamp — the lamp switched at half a minute, then after a quarter minute more, then after an eighth, and so on, so that infinitely many switchings occur by one minute — he says the only remaining question is what the alternating sequence of states does in the limit. Since that sequence fails to converge, he concludes the lamp simply has no well-defined state at exactly t = 1, and that this is no more paradoxical than a function lacking a limit at a point. I cannot tell whether the series analogy is doing all the real work here by quietly assuming that everything true of an abstract infinite sum carries over to a physically performed sequence of acts.

Walk me through the analogy in detail. I want a table of correspondences: what plays the role of the terms of the series, the partial sums, the sum itself, convergence, and the limit, when the target is Thomson's lamp. Then identify where the mapping breaks. Distinguish, in particular, the series of elapsed times from the series of lamp states, and say whether the convergence of the first earns the conclusion he draws from it. Which features of the source — an abstract mathematical object, given all at once, with no causal order among its terms — are being imported into the target, where the acts unfold in time and each must actually happen? Does the absence of a last term matter for whether the process can finish? And is his final claim, that the lamp simply has no well-defined state at t = 1, supported by the analogy or by something else? Finally, tell me whether the deflationary conclusion survives the disanalogies, and if the series comparison is not the right one, what a better analogy for a supertask would be.
