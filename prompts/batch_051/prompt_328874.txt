I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I'm stuck on a book where someone sells off their privacy in slices: first browser history, then location, then ambient audio from the apartment, then the camera. Every time I write the scene I can feel that something has been lost but I dont know how to name it, and 'she lost some privacy' is doing no real work. Before I redraft I want the desiderata: write me the list an adequate account of the value of privacy has to satisfy. Give me each requirement plus the case or situation behind it—being watched while you're alone versus being watched while you perform, privacy as something you waive versus something other people can destroy about you, the difference between hiding a finished secret and keeping a half-formed self unobserved, the need to be bad at a thing in private before your good at it in public, whatever else you'd stake the story on. Flag any requirements that can't be jointly satisfied (can privacy be about control and also something you can't consent away under pressure?) and say which are non-negotiable for a novelist who wants the scene to feel true and which are just one camp's prejudice. Then, very briefly, say how the familiar pictures—privacy as control over access, privacy as seclusion, privacy as intimacy—do against your list. Don't cite anyone; I want the list argued, not footnoted.
