My friend has spent way to much time thinking about the afterlife and she's unnervingly specific about it. Here's what I've gotten out of her over the years:

- A man who murdered one stranger for money and never regretted it? Annihilation. She says no finite crime deserves unending pain, so he just stops existing.
- A woman who ran a prison camp for years and died unrepentant? Also annihilation, though she calls it a close thing and wishes there were a way to give her a very long conscious limbo first.
- Her own grandfather, a decent guy but petty, selfish, never deeply repentant about anything? A long but finite stretch of purification, then he's in the restored world with everyone else. Not annihilation.
- A blogger who never physically hurt anyone but spent twenty years trying to talk people out of belief in God and died still angry at God? Eternal conscious torment. Her stated reason is that the party he wronged was infinite, so the debt is infinite—what he did to his readers is beside the point.
- A woman who did something seriously violent in her youth, genuinely repented, and spent decades quietly making amends without ever being found out? Straight in. No post-mortem punishment at all.

She's never stated the principle behind any of this and I'm not sure she could. What would she say, if she's trying to stay consistent, about this new case: a man who murders his wife right after she leaves him, and in the act screams that he hates God for making him the kind of person who could do this, then dies without a trace of remorse. His act clearly wrongs his wife, a finite creature, but it's also deliberately aimed at God. Does he get annihilated, tormented forever, or purified and restored?

I'm less interested in the bottom line than in the rules that fit her earlier verdicts, which of those rules come apart on the husband, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what further case I should run by her to tell the remaining rules apart. If her own track record underdetermines it, say what she'd be committing herself to either way.
