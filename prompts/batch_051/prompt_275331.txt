I keep flipping on mathematical explanation in nature. The theory I'm attracted to is the indispensability-style line: the best explanations of why periodical cicadas have 13- or 17-year cycles and why honeycombs end up hexagonal both cite mathematical facts — primality, the efficiency of hexagonal tilings — and if mathematical facts do ineliminable explanatory work in accepted scientific explanations, that gives me just as much reason to believe in numbers and geometric objects as in genes or molecules. Its simple, unified, and gives a tidy test for what to admit into my ontology. But I have a firm, almost physical intuition that both patterns are just natural selection or optimization acting on heritable variation and wax: 13 never killed a cicada whose predators were synchronized with it, and the honeycomb theorem never pushed on a bee. The arithmetic and geometry feel like compact bookkeeping for selection pressures, not like the thing doing the explaining. So which should yield? Should I debunk the intuition as a mere prejudice about causation, amend or abandon the explanatory criterion because it over-generates, or is there a reading on which both survive? Give me the strongest version of each move and what would justify it, then say which you'd actually make. Put the answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion.
