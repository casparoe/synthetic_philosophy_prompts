I'm not trained in any of this, just reading around on my own, and I keep running into a tangle. Maximin seems to me like the only honest rule for decisions under genuine ignorance — when you can't assign probabilities, choose the option whose worst case is best. The veil-of-ignorance argument for how to structure a society pushes the same way, and Harsanyi's move of slapping equal probabilities on each position feels to me like pretending the ignorance is risk so the math goes through. But I also have an intuition I can't shake: maximin treats the single worst-off position as if it were infinitely important, because it would take a society where one person gets one extra unit over one where everyone above the bottom is enormously better off. That feels wrong, not just uncomfortable. Minimax regret gives yet another answer — smallest shortfall from the best you could have done in each state — and it has its own weirdness, like flipping its choice when you add an option nobody would pick. I don't want a winner. I want a negotiated verdict: what would it cost to debunk the intuition and keep maximin, what would it cost to amend or abandon maximin, and is there a reading of 'rational under ignorance' on which maximin and minimax regret are answering different questions so both survive.
