I've been rereading Hofstadter on creativity — the line that what look like creative leaps are mostly variations on a theme, done by turning the knobs of an already-built concept: keep the skeleton and vary role, scale, register, context, until something surprising falls out. And I can't shake the feeling this is basically what text-to-image models and old work on word vectors do. Those systems learn a space where directions carry meaning (smile, older, oil painting, and so on), and their supposedly new outputs are points reached by turning those knobs — except the turning is arithmetic: add this vector, subtract that one, interpolate halfway toward a genre. Both pictures say novelty is mostly recombination inside a pre-existing space, and both locate the real skill in knowing which knob to turn and how far.

So the question: is this a genuine structural identity, or am I pattern-matching on the word variation? Give me the mapping first — what plays the role of the conceptual skeleton, the knob, a setting of a knob, and the point where a variation stops being a variation and becomes nonsense. Then tell me where the two come apart, and name one thing the latent-space reading predicts about Hofstadter-style creativity (or the other way round) that turns out false. My hunch is Hofstadter would say the actual crux is not turning the knobs but building — and occasionally redrawing — the space the knobs live in, and that a latent-space model has no version of that move. Is that right? And if the analogy survives wether that's right or not, what would it license us to say about machine creativity?
