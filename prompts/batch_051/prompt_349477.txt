Analyze Masterpiece Cakeshop v. Colorado Civil Rights Commission (2018) as a case for Amartya Sen's liberal paradox. Take the salient facts as given: in 2012, Charlie Craig and David Mullins asked Jack Phillips, the owner of Masterpiece Cakeshop in Lakewood, Colorado, to create a custom wedding cake for a celebration of their marriage; Phillips declined on religious grounds; the couple filed a complaint under Colorado's public accommodations anti-discrimination law; the Colorado Civil Rights Commission found that Phillips had discriminated on the basis of sexual orientation and ordered him to cease; the state courts affirmed; and the U.S. Supreme Court reversed in 2018 on the narrow ground that the Commission had shown hostility toward Phillips's religious objections, leaving the broader free speech and compelled expression questions unresolved.

Sen's liberal paradox is an impossibility result in social choice theory. It shows that three conditions cannot jointly hold in a consistent social ranking of alternatives: unrestricted domain, meaning the aggregation rule must handle any complete and transitive profile of individual preferences; Pareto efficiency, meaning that if every individual prefers alternative x to alternative y, then society ranks x above y; and minimal liberalism, meaning that at least two individuals are each decisive over some pair of alternatives that differ only in a matter falling within that individual's personal sphere, so that the individual's ranking of that pair settles the social ranking of it. Sen's proof shows that any rule satisfying all three can generate a cyclic social preference, so a liberal right and a unanimous preference over some other pair can pull the social ranking in opposite directions.
