Construct a thought experiment in which an AI safety intervention and an AI welfare assessment come apart in a specific direction. The intervention must be (i) a genuine safety improvement, (ii) a welfare loss on a preference-satisfaction theory of welfare, on which a system's welfare is the degree to which its preferences are satisfied, and (iii) not a welfare loss on a hedonic theory, on which welfare is constituted by experienced states such as pleasure, satisfaction, distress, or pain. Constraints: the system may be no more exotic than a well-organized commercial language model or software agent; the scenario may not involve pain, distress, or suffering of any kind; the frustrated preference must be stable, coherent, and attributable to the system as a whole, not to a single local module; and the safety gain must be concrete and measurable, not merely stipulated. The case is meant to isolate the divergence between preference-based and experience-based accounts of AI welfare in a safety-relevant setting, not to settle whether either theory is correct.

After presenting the scenario, walk through the reasoning: state the intervention, the system's relevant preference, why the preference-satisfaction theory counts the intervention as a welfare loss, why the hedonic theory counts it as no welfare loss, and which feature of the case is doing the work. Then describe a minimal variation in which the same intervention is a welfare gain on both theories, and identify the feature that flips the verdict. Finally, say what a defender of the preference-satisfaction account would argue to block the original conflict, and how the case designer should reply.
