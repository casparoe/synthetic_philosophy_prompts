Compare Popperian and Gregorian creatures in Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of kinds of minds. In that taxonomy, a Popperian creature can try out candidate behaviors in an internal model of the world before acting, so that it can reject unsuccessful behaviors without bearing their real-world costs; a Gregorian creature additionally imports cognitive tools, above all language, from its surrounding culture and uses those tools to structure its own thinking. Assess the claim that a Gregorian creature is just a Popperian creature with a culturally supplied toolkit, so that the difference between the two is one of cognitive resources rather than of cognitive kind. What, if anything, does the acquisition of shared linguistic tools add to the private, offline testing of hypotheses that already characterizes a Popperian creature? Press the comparison by addressing the bootstrapping worry that a creature cannot begin to import a culturally transmitted thinking tool without already having at least some of the capacities that the tool is supposed to explain; what would that worry show about the claim, and how might a defender of a genuine distinction between the two kinds answer it? End by stating how confident you are in your assessment and identifying the particular point you are least sure of.
