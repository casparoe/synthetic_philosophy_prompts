Six months ago I got transferred to our office in a country where a good chunk of daily administration runs on gifts, and I keep tripping over a distinction that none of my colleagues back home seem to think exists. It has now become my problem, because I have to decide wether a recurring payment we make is something we can legally and defensibly keep doing.

Every month our local office gives the director of the relevant ministry a cash envelope — not huge, the sort of amount that can be presented as a gift — plus the usual food baskets at holidays. There is no specific favor attached at the time. But when one of our import permits gets stuck for no clear reason, the same director makes a phone call and it clears in two days. My European collegues call this bribery, full stop, and say continuing it puts us in legal trouble. My local colleagues are not apologetic; they say this is not corruption at all, because nobody paid for a decision. To them, corruption is a one-off payment to a customs officer to let a container through without inspection, and they would refuse to do that. So they are drawing a real line, just not where our compliance training draws it.

Our local manager explained it as the same thing as the monthly envelope he gives the man at his children's school, after which his daughter gets a place and extra attention in class. No negotiation, no if-you-do-this-I'll-give-you-that, and both sides would deny that the envelope is buying anything specific. Yet both sides also seem to know, without ever saying it, that the envelopes are why the favors keep happening.

I'm not asking which side is right, though if the answer bears on that I want to hear it. What I need is the carving. Does this configuration — repeated, ongoing gifts to a person with discretionary power, no particular act linked to them at the time, but a standing expectation of preferential access later — have an established name in social science or philosophy? I know bribery usually requires a specific quid pro quo, and the clientelism I've read about is about politicians trading benefits for votes or support, which isn't what's happening here. Is there a term of its own for what I'm describing, and where are its edges? If there isn't a settled term, how should it be separated from bribery and clientelism, and is the Western refusal to separate it a conceptual mistake or a defensible line?
