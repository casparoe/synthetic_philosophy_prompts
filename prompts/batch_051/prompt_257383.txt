I've gotten kind of attached to a view about letters and categories, roughly the Letter Spirit idea: what makes every 'a' an 'a' isn't a checklist of strokes or features and isn't a crisp Platonic form, but something like a position in a continuous style-space with fuzzy borders and better and worse instances, so a letter's identity can't be cleanly separated from the style its written in. I think this generalizes pretty well to everyday concepts too. Tell me what would count against it. Which of these would a committed defender of the style-space view have to concede as genuine counterevidence, and which would they reasonably shrug off? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and give me an honest verdict on whether the view ends up genuinely exposed or just annoyingly well-insulated.
