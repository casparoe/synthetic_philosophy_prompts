We're doing a module this term on historical memory and moral judgement, and there's a pattern in how my university itself talks about the past that I can't seem to get to cohere. The official line on someone like Jefferson is that its presentist and unfair to condemn him for owning slaves, because he was a man of his time and we can't expect him to have been two centuries ahead of his moral surroundings. The same university, though, renamed a building a few years ago because the donor's views on race were found to be unacceptable, even though those views were honestly pretty unremarkable among people of his class and period. And Columbus gets pretty much straight condemnation in the reading list, with no man-of-his-time defence considered at all.

So there's an institutional practice here, and it seems inconsistent: some figures get the they-were-a-product-of-their-time treatment, and other figures, for things that were just as widely accepted in their own circles, get judged by our standards. What I want to know is what this practice actually presupposes. Does it only make sense if you think morality itself progresses, so that later standards are genuinely better and earlier people couldn't see what we see? Or does it need a kind of minimum-universal-badness view, where there are some things so wrong they were knowable as wrong even back then, and the defence only applies below that line? Or is the real principle just that we hand out the defence to people we've already decided to keep respecting, and refuse it to people we've decided to drop?

I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory the practice is committed to, and to be honest about which parts the practice strictly needs and which parts are just optional extras it merely fits with. Then tell me what would actually have to change in the practice if you replaced that presupposed view with full moral relativism about the past, or with the opposite view that everyone in every era should be judged by the same standards. And, since I'm submitting this in a British university, please write the answer in British English.
