Alright, so I've accumulated a set of committments about psychopaths and moral knowledge, and I'd like to know if the set actually hangs together. I don't want a lecture; I want an audit, with the real conflicts separated from the fake ones.

(1) I hold a weak motivational internalism: a sincere moral judgment like "I ought to φ" always carries some motivational pull toward φ-ing, though the pull can be overridden or blocked by things like depression, exhaustion, or fear.
(2) I accept the emperical picture that psychopaths are generally decent at classifying acts as wrong or against the rules; they can say that hurting someone for fun is wrong, and they aren't factually confused about what's prohibited.
(3) I also accept that their deficit is mostly affective rather than computational: they show blunted guilt and empathy, and they don't feel much pull from those classifications.
(4) I want to maintain that when a psychopath says "that was wrong," he is making a genuine moral judgment with the same content as mine, not merely quoting a rule or using "wrong" in scare quotes.
(5) But I also want to maintain that in an important sense the psychopath doesn't know right from wrong, because full moral knowledge requires an affective grasp of why a harmed person matters, and that's exactly what he lacks.
(6) I think the depressed person who judges "I ought to visit my friend" but feels no pull to do so is still making a full moral judgment; depression blocks the motivation without removing her understanding of what she's saying, and by parity I half-suspect it supports (4).

So: does this hang together? Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it and why; order the real problems by severity; and say what the cheapest coherent revision is, and what it costs. Don't just flag everything—I want discrimination.
