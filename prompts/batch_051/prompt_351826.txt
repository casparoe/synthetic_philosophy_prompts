I teach a master's seminar in political ethics with eighteen students and a single ninety-minute session. Design a structured role-play or small-group simulation that makes participants feel the force of the dirty hands problem: that a political actor can face a situation in which every available course of action violates some serious moral norm, and that even the best choice leaves a moral remainder rather than the actor simply being vindicated. The participants should arrive at the dilemma through their roles, incentives, and the institutional set-up, not through a lecture, and they should not hear the term dirty hands until the debrief. Build in an asymmetry between what an ordinary private person may do and what the political role seems to require, and arrange at least one decision with real, if small, stakes made in front of the group. Then design a debrief that maps moments in the activity onto the structure of the idea, says which features of the philosophical problem the simulation cannot reproduce, and identifies what the facilitator must supply directly.

Give the deliverable as a numbered list with exactly five entries, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) setup and materials, (2) roles and incentives, (3) the decision point and stakes, (4) the debrief mapping, and (5) what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to handle it.
