Me and my roommates have had this game for years where we argue and keep score on a whiteboard. One rule from the single page of rules reads:

"Rule 3: When a player says something that takes a background fact for granted, if no one objects to that fact before the speaker's turn is over, the fact is written on the board as established for the rest of the round."

Back when we wrote it, the point was to stop people from sneaking assumptions into arguments and then re-litigating them later. We never imagined playing over Discord, but now we do, and everyone uses push-to-talk.

Last night Sal asked during his turn: "So when did you stop leaving dirty dishes in the kitchen?" He was talking to me, and I've never left dirty dishes in the kitchen—everyone knows it. But I was muted, and by the time I got the mic on Sal had finished his turn, and Rob (who keeps score) had already wrote on the board that I used to leave dirty dishes in the kitchen. Sal says the rule doesn't care that the answer to his question is "never": his move took that background fact for granted, nobody objected before the turn ended, so it's established. I say Rule 3 can't be a machine for turning loaded questions into facts, and "object" has to mean there being a real chance to object, not losing a race against a push-to-talk button.

So: who is right under the words of the rule, under the point of the rule, and under what we would have said back when we made the rule? Which approach ought to win here, and why? Also, does the game's way of keeping score still make sense now that turns and objections happen online, or was it always depending on us sitting in the same room? Please answer in American English.
