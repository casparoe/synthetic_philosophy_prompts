I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology, and for the past several weeks I've been trying to understand how much of the modern debate about biological function is downstream of a contingent historical fact: the central explanatory role that natural selection acquired in mid-twentieth-century evolutionary biology. I'd like you to work through a counterfactual with me.

Suppose that by around 1940, in the main centers of biological research, evolutionary biology had consolidated around mutation, drift, developmental constraint, and other non-selective processes, while natural selection was widely regarded as at best a weak force that fine-tunes populations and at worst an untestable just-so story. Assume that genetics and molecular biology otherwise develop exactly as they in fact did; the only divergence is the explanatory status granted to selection within evolutionary theory. Give me a reasoned prediction of how the philosophical debate about biological function would look by roughly 1990 in that world. In our world, the debate came to be organized around the contrast between etiological accounts, on which a trait's function is what it was selected for, and causal-role accounts, on which a trait's function is what it does in the present organization of the system. I want your chain of reasoning made explicit: which commitments of the etiological approach genuinely require selection as a background notion and which could survive without it; what a physiologist or developmental biologist in that world would mean by a function and whether they would feel any pressure to distinguish functions from mere effects or roles; whether an etiological/causal-role split would still emerge, perhaps under different names or with different burdens of proof; and whether philosophers would even treat function as a single contested concept rather than a harmless polysemy. For each link in the chain, indicate how confident you are and which premise, if false, would break it. Close by saying which step in your reasoning you regard as most vulnerable, and what consideration would most change your overall prediction.
