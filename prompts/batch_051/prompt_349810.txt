Please argue that a finite mind's possible experience is not necessarily exhaustible, even granting that the mind has only finitely many possible instantaneous physical states. By an exhaustible range of experience, I mean the existence of some finite number n such that the mind could never have more than n pairwise distinct experiences no matter how long it lives. Defend the claim that exhaustibility in this sense does not follow from that finiteness: experiences are individuated by what they are about and by how they sit in the subject's history, not by instantaneous internal states, so a finite repertoire of capacities can enter into an open-ended series of contentful relations to an unbounded world. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
