Please argue for the following view. The question 'why would an AI want anything, given that it didn't evolve?' is badly posed: the sense of wanting that matters is not fixed by a reward function or a selection history but by the stable goals a system uses to evaluate its own planned actions. A system trained only to predict—no terminal goals, no rewards—could therefore still develop wants, because long-horizon planning pushes it toward adopting stable success conditions for itself. Make sure you pre-empt the obvious reply that this just redefines wanting as something weaker: say explicitly what makes these goals count as wants rather than merely tracked targets.
