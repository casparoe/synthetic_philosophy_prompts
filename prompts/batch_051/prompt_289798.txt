I am giving a colloquium paper defending the thesis that Spinoza's necessitarianism, taken at full strength, leaves no room for a distinct normative domain of the ethical: there are no genuine oughts, obligations, or prescriptions that are not ultimately restatements of how things necessarily are. Accordingly, I argue that the intellectual love of God in Part V of the Ethics cannot be the crowning moral achievement, the reward of a life one ought to have lived, or an appropriate object of aspiration; it is a purely cognitive-affective state that necessarily accompanies the third kind of knowledge, and any ought-to-strive or ought-to-feel vocabulary attached to it is an illicit import from outside the system. My argument, in brief, is this: every thing and every state of a thing follows necessarily from the divine nature; an ought-claim that does not reduce to an is-claim would require either contingency or an external standard of evaluation, and both are ruled out on my reading of Part I; therefore the only coherent practical vocabulary for Spinoza is causal explanation of affects and striving, not prescription; and the intellectual love of God belongs to that causal vocabulary, since Part V presents it as an effect of adequate ideas rather than as something commanded or recommended. The audience is a research colloquium of about fifteen people: mostly contemporary metaphysicians who work on grounding, modality, and the is/ought gap, plus one Kantian moral philosopher, one historian of early modern philosophy who reads the Ethics in Latin, and two Humean philosophers of emotion. It is not a room of Spinoza specialists. Draft my Q&A prep sheet for this audience specifically: the seven or eight objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each labelled with who is likely to raise it and phrased the way that person would phrase it, together with a two-minute reply, a note on where the reply is softest, and a note on what I should concede rather than defend. End with the question I am hoping nobody asks.
