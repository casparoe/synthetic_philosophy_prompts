Peirce characterizes truth as something independent of what any finite inquirer currently thinks: a proposition is true if it is the belief that a sufficiently extended community of investigators would eventually converge on, because reality would ultimately enforce agreement. James characterizes truth as a practical property that beliefs earn by working: a belief is true insofar as holding it is useful, satisfying, and consistent with the rest of our commitments, so that calling it true is a way of commending it as good in the way of belief. These two characterizations seem to conflict about whether practical benefit is a criterion for truth and whether a claim can be true even if no one presently holds it. In no more than 350 words, treat these as two statements from the pragmatist tradition that appear to contradict each other and ask whether they can be reconciled. Give the strongest readings on which each author could accept the other's point, without reducing either to a mere misunderstanding. Then state what substantive disagreement remains, and say whether it concerns what truth is or only how we discover it. End with your verdict on which reconciliation is most honest to both positions.
