Give me a working definition of greed and test it case by case against the following list, revising the definition until the verdicts are ones you can defend; at the end, identify the single classification you are least confident in and say why. The cases:

1. A subsistence farmer who hoards grain while neighboring households go hungry.
2. A salaried fund manager who maximizes a pension fund's returns and lives modestly.
3. The same manager day-trading a personal account for no purpose beyond watching the balance rise.
4. A founder who pays low wages but sells cheap, useful goods.
5. A monopolist who acquires a life-saving drug and raises its price.
6. An artist who would trade material comfort for ever-wider recognition.
7. A donor who gives away a fortune but keeps legal control over every dollar through a foundation.

I want a definition that does theoretical work, not a survey of ordinary usage. My suspicion is that the word runs together an account of a shape of desire with a moral verdict, and that these cases pull the two apart differently. If you find the same split, give the two components separately and say how each case falls on each axis; if you think the word is a unity, say which case puts the most strain on keeping it one.
