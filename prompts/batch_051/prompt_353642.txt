I run a philosophy discussion group for first-year undergrads—usually about 14 people, 70 minutes, no grades attached. Design an activity that makes them feel the pull of the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism. I dont want any lecture up front, and they shouldnt hear the name of the objection until the debrief. The rough shape I have in mind is a two-stage setup: in stage one, each person does intrapersonal tradeoffs (small certain discomfort now in exchange for a bigger benefit to future-them), and they mostly accept them because a single life can absorb its own costs. Then in stage two you re-deal the very same total costs and benefits across different people, so the arithmetic is identical but some people soak up the costs and others get the benefits—for instance the person who did their 30-second plank yesterday gets to skip today because someone else does it, or something like that. The point is they should trip over the feeling that the sums dont do the work once the boundaries between lives count. But Im not married to that specific mechanism, so if a better one makes them run into the problem themselves, use it. The activity should be runnable as written: materials, exact rules or roles, timing, and what the facilitator says at each stage. Then write the debrief: map specific moments in the activity onto the objection, show where a utilitarian can resist each step and what it costs them to resist it there, and be honest about what the activity cannot reproduce—real, weighty suffering, not just mild discomfort, and why that difference matters. Also say what predictably goes wrong when it runs (someone says something like "well I'd just take the pain for my friend," or the group turns it into a vote about desert) and how to handle it without breaking the spell.
