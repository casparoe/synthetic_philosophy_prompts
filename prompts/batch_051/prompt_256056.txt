David Chalmers has this bit in 'Zombies on the Web' that I keep returning to:

'If there is a possible world which is just like this one except that it contains zombies, then that seems to imply that the existence of consciousness is a further, nonphysical fact about our world.'

I've been covering consciousness research for a while, and I am drawn to the usual scientific picture: brains are physical systems, mind is what brains do, no extra ingredient. But the zombie thought won't leave me alone. I can hold fixed all the physics and neurology and still imagine one of these with no inner life at all. If that imagining is legitimate, then the physical facts don't settle the conscious facts, and the scientific picture I cover has a hole in it. I also suspect the imagining might just be a trick of introspection: perhaps I'm not really holding everything fixed, just quietly deleting the very thing that would explain the reports. The problem is its not obvious to me which of these pulls should give way. I am less interested in a final score than in a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the conceivability intuition to be debunked, what would it take for the physicalist theory to be amended, and is there a reading on which both survive?
