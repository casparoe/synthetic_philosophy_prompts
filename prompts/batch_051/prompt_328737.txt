I'm a philosopher of physics and I've spent the last few weeks chewing on a view I want somebody to take seriously enough to tell me where it dies. I've been calling it "branch-census realism" (I'm sure there's a better name). Start from the standard complaint about branch counting in many-worlds. If a spin is measured in a superposition with amplitudes √(1/3)|up⟩ and √(2/3)|down⟩, naive branch counting gives each final branch equal probability, so 1/2 and 1/2, which is wrong. But I think the mistake is in what gets counted. After the measurement, the environment doesn't just encode "up" or "down"; it goes on recording finer and finer micro-facts — which photons scattered off the apparatus, exactly how the dust moved, what the thermal fluctuations in the detector were. These are still real decoherent differences between worlds. So by the time the dust settles, the "up" outcome isn't one branch, it's an enormous family of distinguishable micro-histories, all agreeing on "up" but differing in the fine print. My hunch is that if you count these micro-histories and give each one equal weight, the number of micro-histories hiding under a macroscopic outcome is proportional to its squared amplitude, so the Born probabilities fall out as a census. Amplitudes set the measure; the probabilities are just what you get when you count the fine structure that measure makes available.

I want to know whether this is actually distinct from the old "branch counting by number of worlds" view or whether I've just moved the problem down a level. Specifically: is the step from "the branch has measure |α|²" to "there are that many equally weighted micro-histories" licensed, or does it quietly assume the Born rule? And where does it break first — irrational amplitudes, continuous spectra, complex phases, non-ideal measurements? If it doesn't break, why couldn't you do the same move in classical probability and count hidden micro-histories to turn any probability into a frequency? Give me the strongest version of the view on it's own terms, and then the objection that still kills it.
