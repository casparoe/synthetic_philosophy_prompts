Hey, I'm a causation person—can you write me a two-character dialogue I can use for a teaching exercise? Mina and Paul meet in the hallway after a colloquium. Mina thinks omissions are perfectly good causes: if you go on vacation without asking anyone to water your fig tree, your not-watering-it causes it to die, and its silly to say the death was uncaused or that only the drying-out of the soil did the causing. Paul holds a process view: real causation involves something like a physical process or the transfer of a conserved quantity, so an absence can't do anything, and omission-talk is just shorthand for a contrastive explanation plus maybe some responsibility attribution. Have them genuinely go back and forth—each should concede at least one small point in response to the other's best argument rather than just restating their position. In particular I'd like them to get into the move where Paul retreats to "fine, omissions can legitimately explain but they aren't real causes," and Mina presses him on what work the real/merely-explanatory distinction is then doing. End before either fully convinces the other.
