I am trying to work out what I think about the therapy/enhancement distinction by testing candidate principles against my own verdicts on cases. My verdicts so far are these.

Antibiotics for a strep infection: therapy, obviously. Insulin for type 1 diabetes: therapy. Measles vaccination: therapy, even though the recipient is currently healthy and the point is to leave the immune system in better shape than it is now. Glasses or LASIK for ordinary myopia: therapy, because they restore normal functioning. Ritalin for someone diagnosed with ADHD: therapy; Ritalin for a student with no such diagnosis who wants to concentrate through exams: enhancement, and I would counsel against it. Prozac for major depression: therapy; Prozac for someone who is not depressed but wants to be more outgoing at parties: enhancement, though somehow more optional. Testosterone for a man with clinically low testosterone: therapy; anabolic steroids for an athlete building muscle beyond what training can produce: enhancement and wrong in sport. Breast reconstruction after a mastectomy: therapy; breast implants for a woman with ordinary-sized breasts: enhancement. Growth hormone for a child with a diagnosed deficiency: therapy. But growth hormone for an otherwise healthy child predicted to reach about 4'11" without it: I keep wanting to class this as therapy, yet I cannot say why it is not just the healthy-student Ritalin case with height in place of concentration.

Is there a single principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts? State it precisely, and for each of its main clauses identify which of my cases motivates it. Then apply it to four cases I remain undecided about: beta-blockers for a healthy violinist with no anxiety disorder who becomes disablingly shaky before auditions; prenatal gene editing to prevent cystic fibrosis; prenatal gene editing to add a few inches of adult height; and an antidepressant prescribed to someone whose grief has persisted unusually long but who falls short of a depression diagnosis. Finally, say explicitly which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate, and whether, on reflection, it is the principle or the verdict that should give way.
