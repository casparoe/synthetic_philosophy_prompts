I’m trying to assess a chapter on Schopenhauer and Nietzsche that I read. At the head of the chapter, the author quotes this line from Schopenhauer’s On the Will in Nature, p. 128, as an epigraph:

"To preach Morality is easy, to found it difficult.—"

The chapter then summarizes the position Schopenhauer develops in On the Basis of Morality in something like these terms. Schopenhauer held that all genuinely moral value comes from compassion, and from compassion alone. An action has moral worth only when it is done purely for the sake of another’s welfare and is motivated by immediate participation in the other’s suffering, not by duty, self-interest, fear, or hope for reward. Because compassion lets us feel another’s pain as if it were our own, he concluded that moral behaviour rests on a metaphysical insight: the apparent separation between persons is an illusion, and in compassion that illusion is lifted. He divides the resulting virtue into justice, which refrains from harming, and loving-kindness, which actively helps. The chapter then says Nietzsche rejected this entire picture because he regarded pity as morbid weakness, a depressive contagion that multiplies suffering rather than relieving it, and as a disguised form of the will to power; in its place Nietzsche is said to recommend an ethics of hardness and self-overcoming.

I suspect that summary flattens both thinkers, but I don’t have the expertise to say exactly where. I’d like an accuracy audit of that summary, sentence by sentence: what a careful Schopenhauer scholar would accept, what would need qualification, and what would be objected to; the same for the claims about Nietzsche; and whether the final contrast follows from the corrected account. I’d also like your judgment on the epigraph itself: is it fair to use that line from On the Will in Nature to frame Schopenhauer’s attempt in On the Basis of Morality, or does it import a misleading picture of what he thought “founding” morality required? If any part of my question rests on a confusion—for example, about what Schopenhauer means by a metaphysical basis or whether Nietzsche is attacking the same phenomenon—please say that directly rather than working around it. Keep the answer under 900 words; if the length forces you to choose, spend more words on the points where the summary misleads than on restating the views.
