I sit on the governance committee of a philanthropic foundation whose charter permits the founding charitable purpose to be changed only by a three-quarters vote of the full board, taken in two separate decisions at least a year apart. A newly elected board now proposes to redirect the endowment to a different purpose by simple majority, arguing that a board is answerable to present needs and cannot be bound by the commitments of its predecessors; the opposing bloc replies that the supermajority requirement was adopted precisely to prevent a temporary majority from unraveling the foundation's reason for existing. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before next month's board meeting. It should recommend whether the committee ought to treat the supermajority requirement as binding, permit the simple-majority amendment, or adopt some intermediate course; state a concrete course of action with whatever conditions seem necessary; engage the strongest consideration on the losing side; and say which argument, if it held, would lead you to reverse your recommendation.
