I'm a philosophy PhD student and I referee for a generalist journal — maybe two papers a month, often outside my subfield, with an hour or two per report. The thing I keep hitting is a thought experiment doing all the argumentative work, and I can't tell whether its earning its keep or just pumping an intuition (Dennett's intuition-pump worry, plus what experimental philosophy has shown about how sensitive case judgments are to framing and order). I want a reusable referee-side checklist I can run on any paper that leans on a thought experiment: a sequence of questions that moves me from "this paper rests on this case" to one of three outputs — let the argument stand, flag it to the editor as intuition-pumping, or mark it a judgment call with an honest note about where my own nose did the deciding. The checklist should catch the classic failure modes (vivid but irrelevant detail, an intuition that flips when the case is re-described, a conclusion that outruns what the case could show even if the intuition is granted, and so on) without me re-deriving the theory each time.

Then run the checklist on three submissions I have seen: (1) one using the man-in-a-room-following-rules scenario to argue that symbol manipulation cannot produce understanding; (2) one using the transplant case where a doctor kills one healthy patient to save five to argue that numbers never override a constraint; and (3) one using the machine-that-gives-you-any-experience scenario to argue that we value real contact with reality for its own sake. Make sure at least one application comes out as a verdict you would expect me to dislike, and say honestly what the checklist cannot do. Format as a JSON object with these fields: `checklist` (array of step objects, each with `step`, `question`, `passes`, and `on_fail`, where `on_fail` is one of `let_stand`, `flag`, or `judgment_call` plus a one-sentence note for the report), `applications` (array of objects with `case`, `path` (your answers per step), `verdict`, `judgment_points`, and `verdict_i_wont_like` as a boolean), and `limits` (array of strings).
