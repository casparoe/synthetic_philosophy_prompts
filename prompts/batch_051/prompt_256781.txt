I keep bumping into the pessimistic meta-induction in phil of science discussions and I can't figure out why it's treated as such a decisive point. The way people usually run it is: you take all the mature, empirically successful theories that turned out to be false (phlogiston, caloric, the luminiferous ether, humoral medicine, Ptolemaic astronomy), and then you infer by induction that our current successful theories will probably turn out false too. But here's the thing I keep getting stuck on. To put phlogiston and the ether on that list in the first place, don't we have to classify them as false by our current lights? And if the argument works, our current lights are themselves probably false, so the list is being compiled by a theory we have no reason to trust. Doesn't that make the pessimistic meta-induction self-undermining in exactly the same way it says ordinary scientific induction is? Or is the induction used in the PMI supposed to be special somehow, and if so, what's the principled difference between that meta-level induction and the first-order inductions inside science?
