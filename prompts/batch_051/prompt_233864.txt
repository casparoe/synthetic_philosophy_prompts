An American state with a high incarceration rate is considering a sentencing scheme meant to shift the system toward rehabilitation without formally shortening sentences. Under the proposal, every determinate prison term of five years or more is divided into a fixed retributive floor of 60% of the term and a rehabilitative remainder equal to the other 40%. The remainder can be remitted only through verified completion of education, work, or treatment programs during incarceration. But if the person is convicted of any new felony within five years of release, the entire remitted remainder is restored and served consecutively to the new sentence. Treat this as a mechanism rather than a moral position: walk through what it would incentivize for incarcerated people, program providers, prosecutors and judges at original sentencing, and release personnel; say where it would be gamed first; identify which failure modes could be fixed without gutting the proposal; and assess whether the scheme selects for genuine rehabilitation, for proficiency at satisfying institutional demands, or for something else.
