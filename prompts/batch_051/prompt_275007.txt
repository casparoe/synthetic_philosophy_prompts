For a year-end project in my high school's philosophy club, I have been organizing an adversarial collaboration between a formalist and an intuitionist about mathematics. The formalist holds that mathematics is ultimately rule-governed manipulation of symbols, with consistency as the main constraint on mathematical practice; the intuitionist holds that mathematical objects are mental constructions and that a statement is true only when we possess a construction or proof of it. Please write the joint statement these two members could sign after a good-faith exchange. I want it to include: (1) a numbered set of claims both accept, including claims about mathematical practice, notation, proof, and the obligation to avoid contradiction; (2) the precise formulations of their disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The statement should explicitly address whether the law of excluded middle is legitimate in reasoning about infinite domains, whether an existence claim requires an effective construction, and what a consistency proof for classical mathematics would and would not settle. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
