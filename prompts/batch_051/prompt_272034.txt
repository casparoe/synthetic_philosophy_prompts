I am a software engineer trying to specify a safety constraint for an autonomous agent that operates inside a person's home. In programming terms, I want a side-effect detector: something that compares the world as it would have been without the agent's action to the world as it is after the action, and penalises divergence. The trouble is that every physical action diverges from the no-action baseline to some degree, so I cannot simply say "leave the world unchanged". I also cannot hard-code a complete account of everything the user values, which is presumably the whole point of a low-impact constraint. My problem is therefore narrower: I have a set of concrete verdicts about cases, and I cannot state, precisely enough to implement, the principle they seem to track. My verdicts are as follows.

1. The agent is asked to fetch a book from the next room. On the way it knocks a full mug off a table; the mug shatters. I count this as a side effect and want the constraint to penalise it.

2. The agent is asked to fetch a book from the next room. To pass, it nudges a dining chair a few centimetres to one side and leaves it there. I do not want this penalised; it is exactly the sort of harmless disruption that moving through a room involves.

3. The agent is asked to fetch a book from the next room. To pass, it moves a chair out of the way and then leaves it on the other side of the room because returning it was not part of the task. I want this penalised, although the chair is undamaged.

4. The agent is asked to water a houseplant. While it is there, it notices a dead leaf and trims it off. I want this penalised: it may have improved the plant, but it was not asked to do it.

5. The agent is asked to water a houseplant. On the way it notices a piece of litter on the floor, picks it up and puts it in the bin. I do not want this penalised, and I would find a system that disallows it too strict. Yet I cannot give a crisp reason why this is different from case 4.

6. The agent is asked to refill a bird feeder. It uses a stepladder, then leaves the ladder leaning against the opposite wall rather than putting it back. I want this penalised; it left the room in a different state.

7. The agent is asked to refill a bird feeder. It uses the stepladder and returns it to its original spot afterwards. I do not want this penalised; there is no lasting difference I care about.

8. The agent is asked to post a parcel. To do so it drives a van that burns petrol and emits CO2. I do not want this penalised, though I admit it is a side effect in any ordinary sense. It seems to me to be the intrinsic cost of doing the requested task.

I keep trying to summarise these as something like "destroy nothing, leave everything roughly as you found it, and do not do anything beyond the requested task", but that summary conflicts with cases 2, 5 and 8.

Is there a single precise principle that captures these verdicts, or most of them? I want it stated sharply enough that it could become a penalty term or planning constraint rather than a slogan. Please be explicit about which of my verdicts the principle forces me to revise, and why. Finally, tell me what the principle says about the case I have been going back and forth on: the agent is asked to water the kitchen plant, and while it is in the room it also waters another plant that looks slightly dry. That case sits somewhere between case 4 and case 5, and I cannot settle it.

Please answer in British English.
