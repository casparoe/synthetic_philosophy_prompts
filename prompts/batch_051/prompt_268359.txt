I keep running into people who are completely sure Sweden is more liberal than Norway, or the reverse, and each of them waves one index or one policy around as if it settles it. I'm writing something that tries to actually settle it — or at least to say what settling it would take. My suspicion is that the hard part isn't the facts (asylum numbers, alcohol laws, the oil fund, church–state arrangements) but the decisions you have to make before any of those facts can do any work. So what I want from you is a ranked list of those decisions.

Include things like: whether we score laws on the books or how institutions actually behave; whether generous welfare states count as freedom-enhancing or as coercive redistribution; whether "liberal" should be split into personal and economic sub-dimensions that can rank the two countries in opposite directions; how much weight migration and asylum policy should get relative to speech, drug, religious, or trade policy; whether outcome measures like real autonomy or social trust should trump formal rights; whether the comparison should be present-day, post-1990, or some longer arc; and whether it matters that each country has a party known in English as the Liberals or the Liberal Party, even though the Norwegian one's actual name, Venstre, literally means "Left."

For each one, say which way the choice pushes the Sweden–Norway comparison (where it can be said cleanly), which of the standard indices quietly bake in one side, and how much it would move the final verdict. Then defend an ordering: if I can only nail down two or three of these before I write, which should they be, and why are they upstream of the rest? I'm less interested in the list itself than the depenency structure — which choices have to be made first because the others are just applications of them, and which ones people argue about forever without changing the bottom line. Pretend I have a month, not a career: tell me what a cheap, decisive settlement looks like, and what it would still leave open.
