My friend has spent way too long thinking about complicity — cases where someone is implicated in wrongdoing without being the main actor, and without necessarily causing it — and I've been collecting her verdicts. So far she's given me these:

- The driver in a planned bank robbery, who knows the plan and drives the getaway car: complicit, obviously.
- A contractor who supplies rifles to a militia he knows is about to massacre a village; if he refused they'd buy the same rifles elsewhere at the same price, and he says he doesn't care about the politics, he is just doing business: complicit.
- A guy at the bar two tables over who, when the men planning the robbery asked him the time because they were timing the shift change, told them; he'd overheard enough to know roughly what they were up to, but the time was on a wall clock right behind him: not complicit.
- A shareholder in a mining company who learns the company uses forced labor and keeps the stock because of the dividends: not complicit.
- An engineer on a team writing an emissions-cheating device for a car company; she thought it was wrong and told her manager so, but stayed and wrote some of the code: complicit.

This week's case is the one I actually care about. An accountant does ordinary payroll and tax filings for a small chain of laundromats, and comes to realize the chain is a front for laundering drug money. The laundering itself runs through fake equipment purchases that she has nothing to do with, but the fact that the payroll and taxes are clean makes the whole business look legitimate from the outside. She doesnt want to help the laundering, says shes just doing her job, and knows that if she quit they'd hire another accountant within a week. Would my friend say she is complicit? Which of the earlier cases is the closest analog, and which case would she have to lean on to make the opposite call? And what rule is she actually running — I can think of at least two rules that fit all her past verdicts but disagree about this accountant, and I'd like you to say what each predicts, give your best guess about which one is hers, and name a further case that would cleanly separate them.
