My brother just pled out on a DUI, and the guy he hit died, and his lawyer keeps telling us he's only being treated worse because of luck. I've been reading the moral luck stuff, and I've got a variation I can't settle. Take the classic case: two equally drunk drivers run the same red light; one kills a child in the crosswalk, the other finds the crosswalk empty. Now change it so the child the unlucky driver hit had already been struck by a hit-and-run a moment earlier, was still alive at the moment of the second impact, and would have died within a minute from that first impact anyway. The two impacts are so close the medical examiner can't establish which one killed her. Does the extra blame the unlucky driver gets in the ordinary case survive, shrink to blame for the risk, or disappear? Work through what a control-based view (blame should track only what you control) and a outcome-based view (blame can track what actually results) each have to say, and say whether the overdetermination is doing new work or just restating the original puzzle.
