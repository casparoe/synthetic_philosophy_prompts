I am the sustainability and animal-welfare lead for a regional hospital system's food service, and I own the animal-welfare side of our quarterly menu reformulation. The ranking everyone expects me to apply is the obvious one: red meat worst, poultry in the middle, plant-based best. But I know the real comparison is far messier. Producing a fixed number of calories as beef harms one large, long-lived animal, while producing those same calories as chicken requires many more individual birds, each living a short, crowded life. Meanwhile, the row crops behind many plant-based options — soy, wheat, peas, canola — are grown in fields where planting, pesticide use, and especially harvesting kill or injure very large numbers of small animals such as mice and insects, and habitat conversion for cropland displaces still more. On top of that, whether a free-living wild animal's life is on balance good or bad would change the assessment almost completely. I need something that keeps all of this in view rather than collapsing into a slogan.

Please write me a reusable decision procedure I can apply to any menu substitution in under an hour, without access to primary studies, and that I can defend to both a procurement board and a sustainability committee. The procedure should specify the affected categories of individuals I need to consider; say what to do when no number is available; separate the factual steps from the value judgments; flag the points where the procedure must stop and hand the decision to a person; and state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle even when followed well. Then run it on three proposed changes: (1) replacing a beef burger with a chicken sandwich; (2) replacing battery eggs in breakfast service with a plant-based scramble made from soy and oat ingredients grown in mechanically harvested row-crop fields; and (3) replacing a pasture-raised lamb special with a high-input grain-and-legume bowl. In at least one of these cases, I expect the procedure to return an answer I will not like; walk through that case honestly rather than adjusting the procedure to avoid it. Please lead with the bottom line — the one-sentence statement of the recommended procedure — in your first sentence, then give the steps and reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
