Restate the philosophical argument that, given those assumptions, the minimal Bayesian network — the simplest graph that is Markov to and faithful to the observed distribution — should be interpreted as the true causal graph. Do this systematically in my vocabulary: score functions, regularization, sparsity priors, model selection, and identifiability. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the argument translate cleanly, which parts did you have to fudge to fit, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? Finally, say whether the claim that the minimal faithful graph is the true causal graph survives translation as a claim about which model will generalize under interventions, or whether there is a residue that my framework cannot state; and if there is such a residue, say whether that is a defect in my framework or a sign that the philosophical claim was asserting something my methods never test.
