The Kardashev scale, as I have seen it, ranks civilizations by the usable power at their command: roughly, the energy of a whole planet, then of a whole star, then of a whole galaxy. There is also a rival scheme, sometimes called Barrow's scale, that ranks civilizations by the smallest structures they can deliberately manipulate: human-scale objects, then microscopic and molecular structures, then atomic and nuclear structures, and eventually spacetime itself. The conceptual claim that seems to be doing the work in both scales is that there is a single underlying property, something like "advancement," and that the two scales are rival instruments for measuring that same property rather than two different properties that happen to share a name.
