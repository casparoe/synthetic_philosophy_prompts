I've been tinkering with a simulation of a frictionless box full of small spinning discs (I call them simmballs) that bounce off one another. When I run it, recognizable large-scale patterns often appear — rings, currents, tubes of discs moving as a unit, occasionally letter-like shapes — and they can persist for a long time. A friend says these patterns genuinely push the discs back into line: once a ring or a letter-like shape appears, it keeps its member discs in formation, and if one of them starts to drift the pattern pulls it back. To me this sounds confused, since the pattern just is the discs in a certain arrangement, and the only pushes are disc against disc. How would one test the friend's claim? Give me the strongest experiment or intervention you can think of, carried out in this simulation or in a physical analogue, say which outcomes would favour the friend's reading and which would favour mine, and then say precisely what a positive result would and would not settle. In particular, how much of the distance between 'the pattern pushes the discs' and 'the pattern is only a compact summary of what the discs were doing anyway' could any such test ever close?
