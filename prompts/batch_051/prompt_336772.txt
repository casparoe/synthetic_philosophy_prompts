A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether social harmony should count as a political value. He defended something close to a Confucian and conservative view: a well-ordered society is one in which people know what is expected of them and do not constantly contest it. I defended a liberal pluralist view: a free society is one that refuses to impose a single vision of the good life and treats open disagreement as a sign of health. We were both surprised to discover that, in our private lives, we do not actually live out this disagreement.

In our families and friendships we both treat harmony as a real good. We decline to reopen old grievances with relatives, not from fear but from a sense that preserving the relationship matters more than winning the point. We call a friend mature for forgiving a slight rather than demanding a full accounting, and we would not say a marriage is thriving because the spouses pursue every disagreement to its conclusion. By contrast, in politics both of us treated harmony with suspicion. We found it easy to agree that calls for unity usually arrive when someone important would rather not be criticized, that a placid polity may be less just than a quarrelsome one, and that order has served as an excuse for a great deal of oppression. Yet we valued the quiet management of conflict at home and principled, even disruptive, conflict in public, and neither of us could say what justified the difference beyond insisting that the two spheres are different.

I want to know whether that asymmetry can be defended. Is there a principle that makes harmony a legitimate value in a family or friendship but not in a pluralistic political order? The obvious candidates are that we choose our friendships but not our fellow citizens, that we can leave relationships more easily than we can leave a state, and that a family shares enough history to harmonize in a way a large and diverse society cannot. But I am not confident any of these survives the hard cases: a family one cannot easily leave, a small intimate community that demands harmony from its members, or a friendship formed among people who share no shared history at all. Give me the strongest justification you can build, test it against the hardest nearby cases, and tell me honestly whether the private/public split should be kept, abandoned, or revised.
