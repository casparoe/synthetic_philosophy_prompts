After a long argument with a friend about whether climate model ensembles actually support the decisions we keep making, I want something I can use on the job. I manage capital planning for a regional water utility. Every five years we update a thirty-year infrastructure plan, and we are now expected to base our decisions on the latest multi-model ensemble projections, which we receive as a few dozen models that disagree sharply about precipitation and runoff. I need a reusable written procedure my staff can follow without climate science backgrounds, in under a week per decision, that does the following: it identifies which model disagreements are decision-relevant and which are not; it states what robustness across structurally different models does and does not license; it tells us when to build earlier, later, smaller, or differently; and it flags every step that is a genuine judgment call rather than a technical inference. Please write it as an ordered checklist, not a lecture on climate modeling, and run it on three cases: (1) a reservoir expansion that would be wasted if the dry models are right; (2) raising a flood-protection standard that would be wasted if the wet models are right; and (3) a case in which your procedure says to wait, although our elected board wants a commitment now. End by saying what the procedure cannot settle.
