A friend has been working through a run of cases concerning rational irrationality — cases in which someone makes themselves, in one respect, less rational so as to come out ahead. Her recorded verdicts on four cases are these:

(i) Parfit's hitchhiker: a stranger who can save your life in the desert will do so only on condition that you promise to pay him once you are safe, and he will not accept a promise from someone he knows will break it. She rules that taking a drug which gives you a genuine disposition to keep the promise is rational.

(ii) The retaliator: a merchant who is repeatedly cheated could take a drug making him genuinely disposed to retaliate against cheats even when retaliation would cost more than the loss it avenges, so that others are deterred from cheating him. She rules that taking it is rational.

(iii) The toxin case: a tycoon offers a large payment if, at midnight, you genuinely intend to drink a mildly unpleasant toxin the next day, although you will not need to drink it to be paid. She rules that paying a hypnotist to induce that intention is not rational.

(iv) The interview case: a nervous candidate could take a drug that makes her sincerely, though falsely, believe she is the strongest applicant, because confident candidates perform better. She rules that taking it is not rational.

I want your prediction about a fifth case: a recluse promises his neighbour that in a year's time he will pay the neighbour's child's school fees. The neighbour is about to emigrate and will have no further contact with him; no one else will ever know whether the recluse pays; and when the year is up he will have no desire to pay and would be better off keeping the money. He can take a drug now that will make him pay when the time comes. Is it rational for him to take the drug?

Set out the principle she seems to be applying, at least one rival principle that fits her four verdicts equally well, what each principle would say about the recluse, your best guess as to her verdict with reasons, and one further case whose verdict would most clearly settle which principle she is running.

Please answer in four headed sections, in this order: Candidate principles (roughly 150 words), The recluse under each principle (roughly 150 words), Best guess (roughly 100 words), and A settling case (roughly 100 words).
