"Does habitat destruction tend to reduce the total amount of suffering among sentient beings? Here is the student's answer:

"Habitat destruction can reduce wild-animal suffering, but the judgment depends on whether we adopt the impersonal total view or a person-affecting view. On the total view, if a patch of habitat would otherwise have produced thousands of short, painful lives, clearing it prevents those lives from coming into existence, and because the prevented lives contain more pain than pleasure, the total amount of suffering goes down. The animals killed or displaced when the habitat is cleared do suffer, but that cost is paid only once, whereas the prevention of future generations is an ongoing saving, so over time the ledger is clearly favorable. On a person-affecting view, possible future animals do not yet have a welfare level that can make claims on us, so preventing their births cannot be a benefit. Yet the conclusion is much the same: the animals actually killed or displaced during clearing would themselves have lived short, painful lives, so ending their lives early is not a net loss for them. Therefore, on either view, habitat destruction tends to reduce suffering."
