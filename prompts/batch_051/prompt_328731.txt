I'm organizing a reading group on the Born rule problem in the many-worlds interpretation, and before our next meeting I want to circulate a working document that forces us to be explicit about what would count as solving the problem. Please write the desiderata: the list of requirements that an adequate theory of probability in Everettian quantum mechanics must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. The list should not be a survey of the literature, and I do not need citations; I need the conceptual specification. In particular, for each requirement, explain what kinds of situations or objections it is meant to rule out, and which popular strategies—such as postulating the Born rule as an additional axiom, counting equally weighted branches, or treating decoherence alone as sufficient—would fail it. I would also like you to flag which requirements you regard as non-negotiable constraints on any adequate account and which are more local preferences of particular programs. If you believe some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so and explain the tension. The part I most want is the line between requirements that are genuine adequacy conditions and demands that are really just one camp's philosophical commitments.

A useful anchor: in his 1957 article The Relative State Formulation of Quantum Mechanics, Hugh Everett introduces a measure over the components of a superposition of orthonormal states and says that this measure is to be associated with the probability of a particular observation; but the paper does not derive why a rational observer who knows all branches occur should apportion credence according to that measure rather than some other weighting. I would like the desiderata to be written so that a proposed account can be checked against them for whether it actually closes that gap rather than relocating it. If it helps, you can score the main families of proposals—decision-theoretic accounts, envariance-based derivations, self-locating uncertainty accounts—briefly against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria.
