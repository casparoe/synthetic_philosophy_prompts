I keep going back and forth on the man-of-his-time defence. On the one hand it seems obviously right to me: judging Jefferson or Churchill by standards that didn't exist in their world is arrogant, and anyway most of us just absorb whatever our own time treats as normal; people in two hundred years will probably be horrified by things we do without thinking. So blaming someone for not escaping their own era is like blaming them for not being the one weird exception. But then I read about Jefferson's slaves and his talk of liberty, and I cant make myself accept that he couldn't have known. There were abolitionists and enslaved people right there saying exactly what he would have needed to hear, so the better standard did exist in his own time — he was in a position to act on it and didn't. Same uneasy feeling with Churchill and empire, or Columbus and the Taino. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition (these people should have known better) to be the thing I drop, what it would take for the man-of-his-time defence to be the thing I drop, and whether there's a version of it that can handle the fact that every time has internal critics. Oh and please say what would change your mind about whatever you conclude, and where your reasoning is shakiest.
