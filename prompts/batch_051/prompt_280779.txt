An AI lab proposes a modification to its RLHF pipeline. The reward model is trained not only to match human pairwise preferences but also under a coherence penalty: on a held-out set of comparisons over outputs from the same prompt, any strict preference cycle (A preferred to B, B to C, but C to A) incurs a loss proportional to cycle length, with the penalty weight controlled by the safety team rather than the capability team. The stated rationale is that reward hacking often consists of the policy steering into regions where the reward model's local preferences are inconsistent, so forcing near-acyclicity should make the reward model approximate a single utility function and shrink the attack surface. Treat this as a mechanism to be analyzed, not a stance to endorse or reject: what does it incentivize for the reward model, for the policy trained against it, for the human labelers whose noisy or context-dependent judgments generate the cycles, and for the two internal teams? How would it be gamed, where would it fail, what second-order effects would it set off, and which failure modes are fixable rather than fundamental?
