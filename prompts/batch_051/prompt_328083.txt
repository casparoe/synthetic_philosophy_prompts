I am preparing a unit on the metaphysics of mathematics, and before we evaluate candidate accounts of what the natural numbers are, I want the specification of what any such account must actually deliver. The organizing case is the identity problem. On one standard set-theoretic construction, 2 is an element of 4; on another, equally standard construction, it is not, and a structuralist may take this as evidence that the question "Is 2 an element of 4?" has no determinate answer because such facts are artifacts of a coding rather than truths about the numbers themselves. Write the desiderata list for a theory of number identity: the cases it must get right (for example, that 2 + 2 = 4, that 2 and 4 are distinct, that any system ordered like the natural numbers — with a first element and a unique successor for each element and no cycles — can serve as the natural numbers, and that both of those set-theoretic constructions are legitimate instantiations), the distinctions it must not collapse (for example, a truth of arithmetic versus an artifact of a coding, a number versus the system in which it sits, a mathematical question versus a question about notation), and the demands of mathematical practice it must respect (for example, treating isomorphic systems as interchangeable in arithmetic contexts). For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it.

Then indicate which of these requirements are non-negotiable constraints on any adequate theory and which are parochial preferences of a particular camp, flag any collection of them that appears jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding the main styles of account, described functionally if you prefer rather than by label. Keep the scoring of candidate views brief; the criteria are the point of the exercise.
