ok here's a distinction i've been carrying around for a while and i want someone to kick the tires on. i think a lot about embedded agency -- the fact that an agent is inside the world it's trying to model, not outside it looking down. and i've started to think a good chunk of the problem can be reframed as a mistake i call "spectator leak".

the setup: an embedded agent sometimes has to model its own future behavior. when it does, it can take one of two stances. in the "source" stance, it treats its own future action as something it's about to write, not something it's reading off the world. in the "sample" stance, it treats its own future action as a fact about the world it's trying to predict, like next week's weather.

my claim: for an agent's own next action, the sample stance is basically always a mistake. either the prediction is self-fulfilling (you predict you'll cave in the negotiation, then you cave because "that's just who i am"), or it's self-defeating (you predict you'll stay calm, get self-conscious about it, and blow up). there's no information being gained, because the thing being predicted sits downstream of the prediction itself. i call it spectator leak when the third-person stance leaks into a decision and the agent starts treating its own levers as scenery.

example i keep using: a program has a model of itself that assigns 70% probability to it choosing option A, and then it treats that 70% as evidence that A is the right choice. the predicted choice is now an input to the choice, and the loop just amplifies whatever was already there.
