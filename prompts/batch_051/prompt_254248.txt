After a long argument with a friend last night about untranslatability and a translator's obligations, I would like a second opinion. The code of professional conduct of the translators' association I belong to contains this clause: "Members shall translate the source text faithfully and completely, adding nothing, omitting nothing, and distorting nothing, and shall preserve its meaning, style, and effect to the fullest extent possible." I have now been asked to translate a novel into a language that has no single word covering the two senses of the English verb "to cleave": to split apart and to cling together. The novel's entire plot turns on this word: a character utters it in a promise, the story is driven by uncertainty about which sense the speaker intended, and the final chapter turns on the answer. A literal translation would have to choose one sense and thereby decide in advance what the novel withholds; keeping the ambiguity would require either adding an explanatory phrase or inventing a new ambiguity in the target language, both of which look like additions or distortions under the clause. The drafters are unlikely to have anticipated a case in which the original's meaning is inseparable from a feature of the source language that the target language cannot reproduce. What does the clause require of me? I care less about the practical recommendation than about what the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the code would take it to mean each imply.
