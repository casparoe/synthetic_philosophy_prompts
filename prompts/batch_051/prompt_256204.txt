I just got back from arguing with a friend for two hours about whether delusions are beliefs. She thinks that when someone with Capgras says their wife has been replaced by an impostor, they believe it in the exact same sense I believe it's raining. I keep wanting to say no, partly because a lot of these people don't act on the delusion most of the time, and partly because the two-factor story — a weird experience plus some failure to reject the bizarre explanation for it — makes me think something else is going on. But I couldn't produce a defintion of belief that ruled delusions out without also ruling out stuff I want to keep counting as belief, like a religious conviction or a hunch I only half trust.

Give me a working definition of belief and run it against these cases: (1) my belief that my keys are on the table because I just saw them there; (2) a Capgras patient's statement that his wife is an impostor; (3) a Cotard patient who says she's dead; (4) someone with a paranoid delusion who argues for it when challenged but otherwise lives as if it weren't true; (5) a sincerely held religious conviction; (6) a proposition I assume just for the sake of argument; (7) an implicit bias the person sincerely denies having. I'd like a definition that gets the clear cases right, and for the borderline ones, either give a verdict and defend it or tell me where the definition just gives no answer.
