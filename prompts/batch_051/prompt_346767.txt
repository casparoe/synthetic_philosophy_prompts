I clerk for the Recognition of Baptism Committee of a regional ecumenical council. Every month we decide whether an applicant's prior baptism, performed in another Christian body, is to be entered in our common registry of chaplains and church workers; the result can force an applicant to choose between re-baptism and losing a placement, and several member traditions regard a second baptism as a serious theological error. Our council is deliberately plural: churches in the believer's-baptism tradition treat baptism as an ordinance by which a believer publicly signs a grace already received; Reformed churches treat it as a sign and seal of God's covenant promise and a means of grace through faith; Lutheran churches treat the rite as the means by which sins are forgiven and regeneration is conferred. Some members therefore baptize infants and some require a profession of faith as a precondition, and the council is forbidden from adopting one account as the official one. The committee must still decide cases on stated grounds.

Please write me a standing decision procedure a registrar without theological training can run in about thirty minutes per case, with the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a negative finding, and where it hands the decision to the full committee as a theological or pastoral judgment rather than something a rule can settle. The procedure should take as inputs the mode of water application, the words used, the age and stated faith of the recipient, the identity and intention of the minister, and the church in which the rite occurred. It must keep three questions distinct: whether the earlier rite is a Christian baptism at all, whether this council will recognize it under its own theology, and whether any further rite must now be required. It must handle at least these hard cases: an emergency bedside baptism administered by a nurse; a baptism performed with the trinitarian name by a church whose standing the council rejects; an infant baptism later fulfilled only by confirmation; and a person validly baptized who apostatized and later received a second baptism in a believer's church. Then run the procedure on three candidate cases for our next meeting, and make at least one of them a case in which the procedure returns a recommendation the committee will dislike. End by saying what the procedure cannot decide and by identifying the steps that are a theological position wearing the clothes of a procedural rule.
