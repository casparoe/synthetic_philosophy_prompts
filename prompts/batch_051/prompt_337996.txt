A national constitution contains the following guarantee: “No person may be deprived of his or her liberty except under a general law enacted by the legislature, and no such law may authorize a restriction that is disproportionate to the end pursued.” A statute enacted decades later, long before digital platforms existed, provides: “During a declared public-health emergency, the Minister of Health may take such measures restricting the movement of persons as are necessary to protect the public.”

During an outbreak of a severe respiratory virus, the Minister does not issue a general regulation. Instead, she directs the operator of the national digital identity and payments network—without which no one can enter a workplace, board public transit, or buy food—to suspend for two weeks the accounts of every person whose infection risk exceeds a threshold generated by a proprietary machine-learning model. The model is trained on purchase, location, and health-booking data; its criteria are not published; the suspensions are individualized; and there is no individual hearing or appeal. The government’s own projections indicate that the measure will prevent hundreds of deaths.

Does the constitutional guarantee reach this measure at all? Does the Minister act “under a general law enacted by the legislature” within the meaning of the clause, or does the old enabling statute fail to satisfy it when used in this way? Argue from the text, from the purpose of the clause, from what the drafters of the constitution and the statute would have said if asked, and from what a reasonable contemporary reader would take the words to mean. Say which approach should govern here.

Use the case to answer the theoretical question behind it: is the right best understood as a side-constraint that invalidates even effective, well-motivated coercion unless it takes the prescribed legal form; as a trump that sets a threshold the state may not cross for aggregate welfare at all; or as an instrument whose scope properly expands when the public-health stakes are high enough? Which reading best fits the clause, and does settling the case require an independent concept of a right over and above the rule-of-law demand that coercion be authorized by a general, published, non-discriminatory norm?
