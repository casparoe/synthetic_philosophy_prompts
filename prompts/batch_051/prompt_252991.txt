I am a novelist at work on a book whose plot turns on a discovery: a people whose law and liturgy are organized around a single foundational story find a previously unknown telling of it sealed in the ruins of their first temple. The story, the Descent of the Seven Flames, already exists in many variants, and one of the novel's governing questions is what makes a variant canonical. For the world of the novel I have provisionally fixed four criteria. A canonical variant must have been attested before the closing of the Old Roll; it must preserve the seven key episodes in their accepted order and add nothing that contradicts them; it must descend to the present through an unbroken line of authorized tellers, oral or written; and it must have been used in an appointed public rite rather than only in private devotion. The discovered variant was sealed in an alcove dated before the closing of the Old Roll, so it plainly satisfies the first criterion. It gives all seven key episodes in the accepted order, but adds an eighth episode unknown to the accepted canon. It survived only as a written copy shelved for centuries in the keepers' archive, not through living tellers, and it was used in the keepers' private purification rite, never in a public one. By my criteria, is this discovered telling a canonical variant of the myth? Work criterion by criterion, say where the criteria leave the matter undetermined, and then say whether the case shows that my criteria themselves need revision—and if so, which criterion should be changed.
