Please give me your credence that the moral equality of combatants is false, with the reasoning broken down in full.

Here is what I mean by the moral equality of combatants. Take a soldier who fights in an ordinary way and complies well with the rules governing conduct in war — she distinguishes combatants from noncombatants, does not use excessive force, treats prisoners correctly, and so on. The thesis says that the justice of her side's cause makes no difference to the moral status of her participation: she does not act worse, and is not more blameworthy, merely because her state is fighting an unjust war, since the justice of the war is a question for the state rather than for the individual soldier. Your credence should concern whether that thesis is false — that is, whether fighting for an unjust cause does alter the moral status of an individual soldier's participation, even when the soldier's conduct in the field is otherwise lawful.

I do not want a survey of positions or a statement of what different just war theories imply. I want one number for your credence that the thesis is false, and the derivation of that number.

Decompose the question into the sub-questions your credence actually depends on, and assign each a rough probability with one or two lines of justification. Your decomposition should address at least the following, though you may add or merge as you think best:

1. Whether ordinary combatants are, as a rule, in an epistemic position to judge the justice of their side's war.
2. Whether even a correct judgment that one's side is unjust is, by itself, enough to make one's participation wrongful, or whether the wrong turns on institutional factors such as legitimate command, oaths, role obligations, and the division of moral labor within a state.
3. Whether the wrong of fighting for an unjust cause is best analyzed as wrongful killing, as complicity in state wrongdoing, or as something else.
4. Whether the principle is more plausibly a legal or regulatory doctrine designed to keep the laws of war symmetrical and enforceable, rather than a moral thesis at all.
5. Whether there is a relevant asymmetry between professional volunteer soldiers and conscripts, such that moral equality might hold for one group and fail for the other.

Then explain how these components combine, approximately, into your overall number. I am not asking for a precise formula, but I should be able to see which uncertainties would have to resolve differently for the final credence to change.

Finally, tell me which single sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to, and describe what evidence or argument would be sufficient to move your overall credence by at least 0.2 in either direction.

For calibration: my own credence that the thesis is false is about 0.65, and a colleague who works on military ethics is at about 0.25. On the basis of your decomposition, where do you think the disagreement between a person at 0.65 and a person at 0.25 most likely lives?
