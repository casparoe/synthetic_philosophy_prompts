ok so i just lost a three hour argument with a friend about whether we can wrong characters in video games, and i kept noticing we were both bullshitting because we didnt have a fixed list of what a decent theory would have to get right before we started gesturing at principles. so before i read another take on this, give me the desiderata: what does a theory of whether (and how much) virtual characters matter morally actually have to deliver?

i want the requirements list, not a verdict. ignore whether the answer is yes or no and give me the cases any adequate theory has to handle: torturing the npc who begs and ther reacts; doing the same to a nameless procedurally generated mob that respawns; deleting a save where some animal or companion npc had a whole history with you; the gamer's-dilemna asymmetry where simulated murder is shrugged off but simulated sexual assault is treated as beyond the pale even though both are only pixels; doing the bad thing inside the fiction vs. using the console to do it from outside; griefing another player's avatar vs. griefing an npc no human is attached to. for each, say why it's a requirement — is it a raw intuition nobody will give up, or does abandoning it predict something absurd, or is it just a fixed point from how games actually work?

and spell out the distinctions a theory can't collapse without cheating. character depicted as suffering vs. actual suffering in some substrate; wronging the character vs. wronging the player vs. wronging the developer's intended experience; a harm the game tracks in its systems (reputation drops, a corpse that persists, an npc who remembers you did it) vs. an animation that plays and then vanishes. a theory also cant just assert npcs literally feel pain, since the code has nothing like a nervous system; if it says they matter anyway it has to say how without pretending the hardware is conscious. and make it handle save duplication — if i fork a save and then delete one branch, did i wrong one character or one instance or nothing, and does the answer change if the two branches diverge? that one i suspect forces a lot of views to show their hand.

then tell me which of these requirements are negotiable and which arent, and whether the list is even jointly satisfiable — maybe you can have the gamer's-dilemna asymmetry or you can have deleting a save is morally weightless but not both, in which case the criteria list itself is the result. quickly score the main camps — the only-real-people-matter line, the it-reveals-your-character-but-has-no-victims line, the some-virtual-things-have-status line — but keep that short, the criteria are the part i want. a table is fine.
