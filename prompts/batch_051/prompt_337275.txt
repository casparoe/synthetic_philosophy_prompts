Suppose a jurisdiction's Road Safety Act says: 'A person must not drive a motor vehicle on a road while holding a hand-held mobile telephone.' The law was drafted in 2011, before consumer smart glasses existed. In 2026 a driver is stopped while wearing smart glasses that project her phone's screen into her field of vision and let her answer a call by tapping the arm of the glasses; the phone itself is in her bag, and both hands have been on the wheel throughout. Is she caught by the prohibition? Walk through the four approaches: plain words, the purpose of the provision, what the drafters would have said if you'd asked them, and what a reasonable reader would think the provision bans. Then tell me whether the absurdity doctrine applies. If the words plainly lets her off but that seems to defeat the whole point of the law, may a court ignore the plain wording and convict anyway, or is there something about criminal statutes that blocks that move? Please answer in British English.
