I am a high school student, and for an independent project that combines my English class with my philosophy club I have chosen to write about mind uploading. I would like you to write a villanelle — nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the two key lines recurring in the usual pattern — about the gradual-replacement version of uploading. In this thought experiment, a person's neurons are replaced one at a time by artificial components that perform exactly the same function, slowly enough that, on one view, the person's stream of consciousness is never interrupted. Please make the villanelle's two recurring lines carry the central philosophical tension rather than merely decorate it. One refrain should voice the continuity intuition: if each tiny replacement preserves function and consciousness, the person at the end is still the person in the only sense that matters. The other refrain should voice the replacement intuition: an entity rebuilt from new parts is not the original, however smooth the exchange. The middle lines should give concrete images of the procedure — a synapse changed, a memory that remains intact, a barely noticeable pause — and each return of a refrain should do new work, as the two intuitions reassert themselves while the replacement advances. Do not resolve the debate; leave the reader inside the uncertainty. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who has thought seriously about personal identity would recognize both positions rather than a strawman. After the poem, add a short prose note explaining where, if anywhere, the poem leans on a contested reading of personal identity or takes liberties with the thought experiment. In that note, also state how confident you are in the philosophical claims the poem presupposes, and identify the single point you are least sure about.
