I have been developing a concept to make sense of cultural evolution and would like an honest assessment of it. I call a cultural item a parasitic meme when it spreads because it is good at getting itself copied, not because it is true or because it benefits the people who carry it; chain letters, apocalyptic cults, and certain conspiracy theories look like pure cases. My stronger claim is that such memes are not a marginal pathology but the clearest evidence for a Darwinian theory of culture, because they show selection favouring the replicator even where the host's interests are harmed. If we insist that culture must serve human well-being, I suspect, we will not see the real unit of selection. Dawkins and Dennett appear to gesture at this. Is the concept coherent, or does it presuppose an unearned notion of what a meme is good at? Does it already exist under another name, and what are the strongest reasons for treating harmful-to-host memes as an anomaly rather than the paradigm case?
