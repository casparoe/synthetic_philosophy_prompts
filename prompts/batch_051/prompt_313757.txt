I hold a broadly depictive, Kosslyn-style view of mental imagery: when I form a visual image, the vehicle of the representation is itself spatial or quasi-pictorial, so that imagery tasks such as scanning across an image or rotating it are performed by operating on something picture-like rather than by manipulating a propositional description. I know this commits me to more than the claim that imagery feels pictorial, and I suspect part of the dispute comes from conflating the spatial content of an image with the spatial format of its vehicle; please keep those apart when you rank defeaters.

I want a map of what would count against this view, not another summary of the Kosslyn–Pylyshyn debate. The facts I have in mind, to anchor the discussion, are the familiar ones: reported scanning time increases roughly linearly with imagined distance, rotation time increases roughly linearly with angle, and spatial imagery interferes with concurrent visual perception more than matched verbal tasks do. I also know the standard Pylyshyn-style rebuttals: that these patterns could reflect subjects' tacit knowledge and experimenter expectations rather than the format of an underlying representation, and that imagery might be epiphenomenal. I need to know which of these, if any, should actually move me, and by how much.

For each candidate defeater, say how decisive it is, what a committed depictive theorist would concede counts against them—and how they would try to absorb it—and what they would dismiss, and on what principle. Then give an honest verdict on whether the depictive view has been protected by auxiliary assumptions, by redefining depictive in ways that track less than its original claim, or by retreating from behavioral evidence to neuroscience, and whether being insulated in that way is a defect for a claim specifically about representational format.

One more thing: I personally find the interference pattern—spatial imagery disrupting concurrent visual perception more than a matched verbal task—the most persuasive evidence for a depictive format. Say explicitly whether that pattern, even if it held robustly, actually discriminates depictive from propositional accounts, or whether it too could be explained away as shared processing demands rather than shared representational format.
