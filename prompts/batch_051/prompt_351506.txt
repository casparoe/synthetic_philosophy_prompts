I keep going back and forth on how to run my recognition-of-states session this semester. I've got an upper-level international relations seminar — 15 students, 90 minutes — and I want them to actually feel the question of whether statehood is something a government just has once it meets the standard criteria (permanent population, defined territory, working government, capacity to enter into relations with other states) or whether it's something only other states' acknowledgment can give. I'm not after a Model UN-style policy debate about whether recognition is wise; I want the conceptual pull of both sides to show up in what they actually do.

Here's the rough scenario I'm imagining, but feel free to modify it: a breakaway region has controlled most, but not all, of its claimed territory for about eight years. It runs schools and courts, collects some taxes, and has exactly one patron state that calls it a state; the parent state calls it a criminal administration. The students would play the breakaway government, the parent state, two neighbors with different incentives, and a representative of an intergovernmental body that coordinates recognition. The breakaway government has to keep certain day-to-day functions running — mail, banking, passports, disaster aid — and the others have to decide, piecemeal, wether to treat it as a state or as something else, and on what record their decisions get made. I'd like the rules simple enough that the activity runs itself most of the time, with the main decision points written down in advance.

Give me the roles, the setup, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened to the declaratory/constitutive distinction without naming it up front — plus a few discussion questions that press on the places where the dichotomy breaks down (partial or uneven recognition, and recognition as evidence versus as something that actually changes the situation). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of recognition the simulation can't reproduce for a group of undergrads in a seminar room. And one more thing: if the setup rests on a confused premise — for instance, if the declaratory/constitutive split is itself a false dichotomy, or the scenario can't cleanly separate "is a state" from "is treated as a state" — say so plainly and tell me what I should be aiming at instead. I'd rather have the activity redesigned than have it flatter my framing.
