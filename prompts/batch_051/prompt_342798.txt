Please compare Dennett's cranes/skyhooks distinction with his put-down of greedy reductionism. These usually get run together, since both seem to say: no magic skyhooks, but also no pretending that the parts explain everything about the whole. But I dont think they're the same point seen from two angles, and they might even pull apart. Say what each one is really aimed at, then tell me whether someone could consistently be a skyhook-free crane-builder and still be a greedy reductionist, or whether you could reject greedy reductionism while quietly helping yourself to a skyhook. Which of the two errors does Dennett seem to treat as worse, and is he right?
