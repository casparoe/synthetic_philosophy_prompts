I have recently started trying to make my household more self-sufficient—growing food, storing rainwater, reducing grid dependence—and I also sit on a small town council that is considering similar measures at the municipal level. What unsettles me is that the people around me talk about self-sufficiency as though it were a single, obviously good ideal, when I can already see at least three different things one might mean by it: being able to manage without others, not relying on particular others, and not being subject to others' arbitrary power. These come apart, and complete self-sufficiency is plainly impossible, since my garden depends on seed varieties, tools, and knowledge I did not create. Yet the ideal keeps reasserting itself in both personal life and national policy, so it cannot simply be dismissed. Write an essay on whether self-sufficiency—or autarky—is a coherent and defensible ideal for individual persons and for political communities, or whether it should be dissolved into more basic values such as resilience, autonomy, and non-domination. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides and reach a justified conclusion.
