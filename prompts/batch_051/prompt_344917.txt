For the past few weeks I've been going back and forth with a guy in my campus philosophy group, Adam, about whether things keep existing when nobody's around to perceive them. I take the obvious line: of course the tree in the quad is still there at 3 a.m. when the quad is empty. Adam says I'm just taking a habit of thought for a proof. His move is always the same. He tells us to try to conceive of that tree existing while no mind whatsoever is perceiving it or thinking about it. Then he pounces: the moment you actually picture the tree, or even hold the proposition in your mind, a mind is involved — yours. So you haven't really conceived of the tree existing unconceived; you've conceived of yourself observing it, or at least of it sitting in front of some imagined observer. From that he concludes that any claim about a tree existing with no mind around is literally unthinkable, because you can't frame the thought without smuggling in an observer. He's an atheist, so he doesn't soften this by saying God keeps the tree around; he'll happily admit that the quad is tree-free when no creature is perceiving it, and he insists the rest of us are just too wedded to common sense to accept the argument's conclusion.

I've tried to say what I think is wrong with it, but I keep tripping over my own words. Is Adam's argument actually good? If not, where precisely does it go wrong, and how should I respond? I don't want the "well, common sense says trees exist" answer; I'm hoping for a precise diagnosis.
