I'm a novelist, and in my writing group there's a running line that my plot notebook is "basically part of my mind." One friend uses it to argue that anybody who tidies, backs up, or reads my notes without asking is, like, rummaging around inside my head. Another says his phone is where he acutally thinks, and that the part of him that writes is spread across him and his notes app. I want to know how far the analogy actually holds. First, give me a correspondence table: what maps to what — the notebook pages, the act of flipping through them, the moment of remembering, the thing remembered, maybe the difference between a thought I just have and one I have to go look up. Then walk me through where it breaks. Does it matter that a notebook can be left at home, that its battery can die, that I have to decode my own handwriting, that my memory just surfaces while the notebook has to be consulted? Here's the parity-ish case that bothers me: suppose I always write a character's backstory down and a friend just holds it in her head, and asked on the spot we can both produce it — is the notebook really doing the same job as her memory, or is that just a tidy way of talking? Then hit the hard cases: a plot I know so well I never open the notebook anymore; the notebook left on a train; a friend who remembers my worldbuilding better then I do; Google Docs; the fact that I can search my own browser history. I want to know which disanalogies actually matter for the "stop messing with my notes" conclusion and which are just surface. And at the end, tell me if there's a better analogy, or whether "part of my mind" is a metaphor that's been doing the whole argument silently.
