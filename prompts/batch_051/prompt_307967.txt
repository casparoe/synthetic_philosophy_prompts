I'm a retired physicist who's recently got into philosophy, and I want to make sure my views about coincidence and anecdotal evidence actually hang together before I put them in a piece I'm writing. Here's roughly where I've ended up:

(i) The birthday paradox is a sort of foundation for me: events that feel shocking are often only shocking because people fail to count all the unremarkable ways that same match could have occurred.

(ii) So I think anecdotes like "I was just thinking of her and then she called" carry essentially no evidential weight for telepathy, synchronicity, or any hidden order.

(iii) But I also accept the Bayesian point that even weak evidence updates: if I can say beforehand why an observation is more likely under a non-chance hypothesis than under chance, then it has to count for something, even if only a little.

(iv) Yet I do think some coincidences are too specific to just dismiss — cases where "that can't be chance" is not simply an illusion.

(v) I also think our built-in sense of when a coincidence is suspicious is largely unreliable, because we tend to build the target event after the fact and quietly ignore all the near-misses and alternative possibilities we never pursued.

(vi) And I'm a naturalist: I'm not open to synchronicity or cosmic meaning in any of this; if I decide something isn't chance, I'll look for an ordinary mechanism or a data error.

Do these hang together? I want to know wich pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, how severe the real tensions are, and what would have to give in each case / the cheapest fix.
