Heres the thing I keep tripping over. France caps the standard workweek at 35 hours and has been building out a legal right to disconnect, while Japan recognizes karoshi, death from overwork, as a real medico-legal category, and in the US exempt salaried employees can be worked 60 hours with no overtime while long hours get treated as a badge of ambition. These arent just different labor markets; they seem to only make sense under different pictures of what work even is. Is work a bad to be minimized, a duty that gives life order, a source of meaning and identity, a commodity, or a status contest? Reconstruct what each of these practices presupposes about the nature and value of work and leisure, say where a practice is philosophically underdetermined because several rival theories would license the same rule, and tell me what would have to change in each regime if you swapped in one of the rival views.
