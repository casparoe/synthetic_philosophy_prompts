ok so I've gone back and forth on this for a while and I've ended up with a set of views on borders that I can't tell if they hang together:

(1) I basically think countries are clubs: the people already there get to decide who comes in, like a club can blackball new members, and if you lose that power you dont really have a country.

(2) But I also think it's obviously unfair that your whole life is mostly decided by which side of a line you were born on — a kid born in Honduras did nothing to deserve only a fraction of the opportunities a kid born in the US gets, so borders look like a giant birth lottery.

(3) I'm not moved by the objection that says you still need somewhere to go: I think everyone has a right to leave their country, but that doesn't make it any specific country's duty to let them in. Leaving and entering are just different.

(4) I'm pretty convinced by the welfare state argument: with open borders you couldn't have generous redistribution because people would move for the benefits and the system would colapse, so you need immigration control to protect the safety net.

(5) But then I also think rich countries are rich partly by luck and partly by colonialism and exploitation, so I dont see why their citizens get to protect that wealth from the very people it was taken from.

Is this a consistent package? Which of these are actually pulling against each other, which ones only look like they do, and if I have to sacrifice one to make the rest cohere, which is the cheapest to drop?
