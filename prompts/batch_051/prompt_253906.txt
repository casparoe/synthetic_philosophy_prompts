Write a dialogue between two philosophers of psychiatry, Dana and Sam, who disagree about whether the psychiatrist Thomas Szasz was right that mental illness is a myth. Dana defends a modernized version of Szasz. Her line is: literal illness requires a demonstrable pathophysiological process or lesion; things like schizophrenia or depression don't pick out any such lesion, so they aren't illnesses in the same sense as neurosyphilis. They're labels for patterns of behavior and experience that are troubling or socially disapproved. Sam thinks this is too quick. His line is: that move confuses not having found a mechanism yet with there being no mechanism at all, and medicine has a long history of treating syndromes on the basis of symptom patterns before the underlying mechanism was found—the syphilitic cause of general paresis was discovered only after the syndrome had been recognized for a long time. Have them argue the specific cases as they go, including general paresis and conversion disorder (what used to be called hysteria). They should also get to whether the illness label is what actually does the coercive work in involuntary psychiatry, or whether that follows seperately. Both characters need to be equally competent: neither should be a strawman, each should have a genuinely strong response to the other's best point, and later lines should pick up on exact earlier points rather than dodging them. Aim for something like 800 words of dialogue.

After the dialogue, give me a brief JSON summary with these fields:
- "nub": the core issue the whole disagreement turns on, in one or two sentences,
- "strongest_move_dana": the strongest point Dana actually made,
- "strongest_move_sam": the strongest point Sam actually made,
- "points_of_agreement": a list of claims both of them accept by the end,
- "remaining_disagreement": a list of claims they still disagree about.
