As a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, I keep seeing the alignment problem treated as a single question — how to get AI systems to do what we want — but the moment I try to turn that into something an engineering team could actually schedule work against, it seems to splinter into several different problems, and I cannot tell which ways of carving it up are principled. Before I read another proposed solution, I want the acceptance criteria for having correctly stated the problem at all. Write the desiderata that an adequate statement, or decomposition, of the alignment problem must meet. The list should cover at least: the difference between an objective that is misspecified and a system that competently pursues the objective in ways that defeat the objective's purpose (specification gaming); the difference between aligning the stated objective, aligning the produced behavior, and aligning the system's underlying goals; the phenomenon of acceptable behavior on the training distribution that fails to generalize out of distribution; and the Goodhart-style consideration that optimizing a proxy tends to erode that proxy's validity as a measure of what one actually cares about. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, mark it non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp, and then identify which single requirement does the most work to rule out the common formulation that the problem is just to align models with human values. The result should be usable in practice: it should let me tell when two teams believe they are solving the same subproblem but are not.
