Okay so I'm in a seminar on love and reasons, and we've been doing the whole appraisal-versus-bestowal debate: whether love answers to the beloved's good qualities, or whether love bestows/creates value rather than responding to reasons. I've been trying to pin down what our professor actually thinks because her comments keep pointing in different directions. She said that when Anna loves Ben because he's generous and steady, that's a perfectly good reason, and that if Ben turned cruel Anna's love would appropriately fade — but the fact that Daniel is even more generous and steady is no reason at all for Anna to trade up. She also said that loving someone because they're useful to you, or because they love you, isn't really a love-reason. Then she discussed Carla, who had an arranged marriage, and said Carla came to genuinely love her husband over decades, loving not any virtue he had at the start but the man their shared life made him. And she said about Diego, who stays devoted to an ex-wife that despises him, that his devotion is still love but not love for reasons; it persists without justification.

The new case I'm trying to predict: Meera loves Asha because Asha stood by her through a long illness. Asha isn't kind or warm in general — she's grumpy and difficult with nearly everyone — but she was steadfast toward Meera specifically. If Asha changed and stopped being caring toward Meera while remaining otherwise the same person, would the professor say Meera's love had a real reason and lost it, or that it was never a reason-based love in the first place? I'm less interested in the flat verdict than in the rule she's running, the plausible rival rules that also fit her comments, which of her earlier comments is the closest analog, and what further case you'd want to settle wich rule she's actually running.
