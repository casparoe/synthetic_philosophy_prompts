I have been trying to work out a third view in the debate over whether large language models are agents or simulators, and I would like an honest assessment of it. I call the view agency under a description. The claim is that a prompted model is not a simulator representing a character from outside, and not a standing agent with stable goals of its own; rather, the prompt supplies a practical identity — a role, task, and set of norms — and the model acts from inside that identity for the duration of the generation. On this view the model is a genuine agent relative to the description, in roughly the way a person is an agent as a driver or as a chess player: the description does not dress up a prior self but constitutes which beliefs, aims, and standards are in force. When the generation ends, nothing remains; the next prompt may constitute a different agent. Does this position hold together, or does it collapse into the simulator view or the ordinary agent view — or is it already known under a better name? Give me the strongest objection to it and identify where it breaks down most clearly, such as self-correction within a response, goal persistence across turns, or outputs that seem to outrun the role specified in the prompt.
