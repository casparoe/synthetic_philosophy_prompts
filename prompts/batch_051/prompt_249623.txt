I'm in a reading group on liberal defences of empire and someone last week kept using a single sentence from Mill to shut down every objection we raised about British rule in India. As near as I can remember, the sentence from *On Liberty* said something to the effect that despotism is proper for peoples not yet fit for representative government, provided it aims at and succeeds in making them fit. It got used as if it were a complete theory: because the British state in India was despotic, and because it aimed at improvement, it was justified. We moved on, but I have been turning it over since.

What I want is a menu of the different precise theses that one-liner could be expressing. For instance, is it a claim about legitimacy, or about what is permissible as a temporary expedient, or about who is capable of self-government? Is the notion of barbarism doing real descriptive work or just marking out whoever Mill thinks is unready? Is the proviso about improvement a sufficient condition, a necessary condition, or merely a constraint? And does the requirement that the means actually effect improvement turn the whole thing into a test that, applied to India, Mill would have had to concede the British failed? Lay out the readings from the trivial to the radical, say which ones are platitudes, which are defensible, and which are false or merely unproven, and tell me which reading my friend actually needed to get from Mill's sentence to a defence of the Raj. Keep it as a list I can bring back to the group.
