Analyze the Cold War nuclear standoff — say from the Soviet atomic bomb in 1949 through the Cuban Missile Crisis and into the early 80s — as a case study for the Great Filter. By the Great Filter I mean the idea that somewhere between lifeless chemistry and a galaxy visibly full of civilization there is at least one very hard step that almost no lineage gets through, and the question is whether nuclear self-destruction is that step. Don't just rehearse the obvious nukes-could-have-killed-everyone summary. I want the actual mapping: what exactly is the step being tested, who or what is the agent whose options and payoffs matter (individual leaders? states? the species?), and what should count as the filter nearly catching us. Then say where the model breaks. The Cold War wasn't one roll of the dice but decades of repeated, correlated trials with changing players and information, and the Great Filter story mostly works with a single low transition probability; I suspect that mismatch matters and I want you to say how. Finally, use the episode to say whether the Great Filter gets stronger of weaker as a hypothesis, or whether near-miss evidence like 1962 is simply the wrong kind of evidence for it. Keep the theory honest and the history honest.
