I co-teach a grad seminar on concepts and rule-induction, and my colleague has been running Bongard-style sorting exercises with little cards of shapes. She wont tell me the rule; she just sorts. Her sorts so far:

Left (positive): a single square; two squares of the same size; a big square next to a small square; three squares in a row.
Right (negative): a circle next to a triangle; two triangles and a circle; two circles and a triangle; three circles and a triangle.

Next card up is two circles of the same size. The thing is, theres at least two or three rules that fit her sorting so far, and they give different verdicts for this next card. I'm less interested in the bare name and more in the geography: what are the plausible rules, what does each predict for the two-circles card, which is the best guess and why, and what further cards would most cleanly tell the candidate rules apart (a single one if possible, otherwise the smallest set).

Respond with a JSON object with these fields:
- "candidate_rules": a list of objects, each with "rule" (a short label), "statement" (the actual rule), "predicts_for_new_card" (either "left" or "right"), "fits_previous_verdicts" (boolean), and "why" (a brief justification of whether it fits).
- "best_guess": an object with "rule", "predicted_verdict", and "reasoning".
- "discriminating_cards": a list of objects, each with "card", "predictions" (a map from each candidate rule's label to "left" or "right"), and "what_it_settles".
- "caveats": a string noting any residual underdetermination and what assumption about the exercise would tip the best guess one way or another.
