For a seminar on Chinese philosophy and dream skepticism, I plan to spend a day on a paragraph from Zhuangzi's "On the Equality of Things" (Qiwulun). In the passage I have in mind, Zhuangzi observes that while one is dreaming one does not know one is dreaming; in the middle of a dream one may even interpret a dream, and only after waking does one know it was a dream. He then says there will be a great awakening, only after which one will know that this present state has been a great dream. But he immediately adds that foolish people take themselves to be currently awake and to know with certainty how things are, and he concludes that Confucius and you are both dreaming, and that his own saying that you are dreaming is itself dream-talk. I would like help understanding how this paragraph works as a piece of epistemology rather than as mere Daoist rhetoric. Does the projected great awakening give Zhuangzi a way out of the usual skeptical regress, or does it deepen that regress? Is the contrast between dreaming and waking here a first-person, state-dependent distinction, or does it tacitly rely on an objective standard of wakefulness that no one may actually occupy? Please lead your response with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
