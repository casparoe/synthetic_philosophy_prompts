After a long argument with a friend about a recent biopic, I've concluded that we were using terms like "respect" and "truth" to mean several different things. Please build a decision tree that sorts positions on what we owe real people when their lives are dramatized, for a reader who has read none of the philosophical literature. Each non-leaf branch should be labeled by a concrete question or a short case that forces a choice, and every diagnostic question should be answerable by someone who knows no position labels. Work from the logical space of answers first, then locate the actual positions in that space; where a combination of answers has no defenders, say briefly whether it seems incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Use a single running case for any question that needs a concrete anchor: a film that invents an act of personal betrayal by a recently dead public figure whose surviving family maintains the scene is false and damaging to his memory. The leaves must include at least: the dead cannot be wronged, but audiences can be deceived about what happened; the dead retain an interest in their reputation; fidelity is owed to the historical record rather than to the person; dramatic truth may override literal accuracy when an invention conveys something essential about the person; and a reparative position that holds dramatizers to stricter duties toward people history has treated unfairly. Each leaf should carry a name, or "unnamed" if none is in common use, plus one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. If two positions cannot be separated by the questions you choose, flag that rather than papering it over. Aim for roughly ten questions and ten leaves.
