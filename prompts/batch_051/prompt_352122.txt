In much of Europe, the legal pattern is this: carrying a pregnancy for another couple and providing eggs are lawful when done without pay — at most, out-of-pocket expenses are reimbursed — but payment beyond expenses is prohibited, and commercial surrogacy contracts are not enforced. In several American states, by contrast, the same services are bought and sold: surrogates are paid a fee, egg donors are paid, with compensation varying according to the donor's traits, and contracts can determine in advance that the gestational carrier will not be the legal mother. Notice the peculiar structure of the European position: the very same act — gestating for another couple or providing eggs — is permitted when unpaid and forbidden when paid, even though the same legal systems allow people to be paid for other bodily work, including intimate labor and work that carries genuine physical risk, such as participating in a clinical trial or doing manual labor. Meanwhile, both systems generally refuse to allow a woman to be paid to relinquish a newborn for adoption, yet the American system permits a superficially similar transaction when it is arranged in advance and called surrogacy. What theory of reproductive labor, commodification, and parental identity does each pattern presuppose? For the European pattern, is a special anti-commodification principle about reproductive capacities strictly required, or could it be explained entirely by worries about exploitation, inequality, and compromised consent — and what does the unpaid-versus-paid divide, set alongside the acceptance of paid bodily work elsewhere, actually commit the practice to? For the American pattern, does it presuppose that reproductive services are ordinary services, that parenthood is created by intention and contract rather than by gestation, or both? Where is each system philosophically underdetermined or internally inconsistent — in particular, the American combination of enforceable surrogacy payments with a bar on paying for adoption — and what would have to change in each system if it adopted the other's implicit theory?
