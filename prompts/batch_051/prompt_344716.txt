For a paper I am writing for my theology class, I am comparing three accounts of what the crucifixion accomplishes: penal substitution, the Anselmian satisfaction view, and the moral influence view. Before I commit to one, I want to work out what any adequate theory of the atonement has to deliver, rather than adopting the standards of whichever theory I happened to encounter first.

Write up the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and be explicit about which requirements are non-negotiable and which merely reflect the commitments of a particular theological camp. I am especially interested in these pressure points: whether a theory must explain why the cross was necessary at all, given that God could presumably forgive sin without it; whether the atonement is best understood as changing God, changing the sinner, or changing some third thing such as a legal or cosmic status; whether the theory must say something adequate about victims of sin and not only about the guilty; and whether any adequate theory must resolve an apparent tension between God's justice and God's mercy rather than simply taking one side of it. Finally, say whether any subset of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable, and briefly score the three theories against the list, identifying which requirement does the most to separate them—keeping the weight on the criteria, not on the scorecard.
