My sister got hit by a delivery van that ran a stop sign and then drove off. She's suing the biggest courier company in our city, because something like 85% of the vans on that street at that hour belong to them. But no one—not even her—actually saw the company's name or logo on the van; the statistic is the only thing linking the company to the crash. Her lawyer says that's enough in a civil suit, since the standard is just preponderance of the evidence, and 85% is plainly more likely than not. He says it would be a different story in a criminal trial, where you'd need proof beyond a reasonable doubt, but in a civil case a strong base rate can carry the day. I can't tell whether the only real problem is practical—that a jury just won't be convinced by a statistic—or whether there's something more principled wrong with treating a number like that as proof, even when the number is accurate. Should I be trying to talk her out of basing her case on this, or is her laywer right?
