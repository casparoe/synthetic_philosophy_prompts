(i) An agent with cyclic pairwise preferences — A > B > C > A — is irrational, because you can make her pay a small amount at each trade and end up back where she started, minus the fees.
(ii) But not every apparent cycle is a real cycle. If I prefer the rich dinner when choosing in advance and the plain one when the moment comes, that's two different contexts, not one intransitive preference, so the pump is attacking a strawman.
(iii) The right way to handle anticipated temptation is sophisticated choice: when I choose a plan, I treat my predicted future choices as constraints on what's feasible now.
(iv) But I also think resolute choice is rational — sticking to the plan I'd now endorse even when I know I'll waiver when the temptation arrives.
(v) Dynamic consistency is non-negotiable: absent new information, changing your mind about a plan you adopted is a failure of rationality.

Do these hang together? I can't tell whether (iv) and (v) are actually in conflict or just trade on different senses of 'plan', and I suspect (iii) and (iv) are doing more work than I gave them credit for.
