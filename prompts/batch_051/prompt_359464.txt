I keep going back and forth on something that seems obvious to everyone else here. We talk about akrasia as a failure mode and precommitment as this autonomy-respecting fix, but I can't actually find the line between temperance and repression that isn't just "the person endorsed it afterwards." Say I put my phone in a timed lockbox so I'll write. Functionally I've just made it harder for a later urge to get what it wants — how is that not suppression, except externalized and done in advance? If temperance is just successful impulse control and repression is impulse control we don't like the results of, then whether I'm being virtuous or self-repressive is just a question of whether my long-term plans happen to be good, which seems like it collapses virtue into prudence. Is there a real distinction here that doesn't beg the question, or are "repression" and "self-binding" just names we give to precommitments depending on whether we still endorse them?
