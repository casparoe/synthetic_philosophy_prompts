Something I've been going back and forth on: when should I defer to future-me's credences, and when should future-me just look like a confused stranger living in my head? I half-remember something called Van Fraassen's Reflection principle from an old blog post—roughly, it said my current credence in a proposition should equal what I'd expect to believe later, conditional on later-me having a particular credence. But my actual verdicts about cases keep violating it, and I want to know what principle, if any, is actually doing the work.

Here are the cases and where I land on them.

1. Drunk-me. I'm playing cards tonight, and sober-me is 75% that Kyle is bluffing. I know from long experience that after two drinks I get paranoid about tells, and my credence will drop to about 30% with no new information. I dont want to defer to drunk-me at all; his process is worse than mine.

2. Transcript-me. Tomorrow I'll receive the full transcript of a trial and have a quiet afternoon to go through it carefully. I'm now 55% on guilt. If I could somehow learn now what evening-me believes after reading it, I'd just adopt that number. Full deference.

3. Cherry-picked-book-me. Next week on a flight I'm going to read a very persuasive book that I already know was written by an advocacy group and contains only true facts but leaves out everything inconvenient. I expect to land at 90%, up from my current 50%. I do not want to pre-adopt 90%. Maybe I'd take a small nudge, but I can't say how to compute it.

4. Forgetful-me. I'm 85% on something, based on a large pile of scattered evidence. Then I'm told I'll be given a drug that permanently deletes a randomly selected half of that evidence. I know that shortly after, my best self will rationally settle around 60%. I should not update to 60% now, and if I could avoid the drug I would. Later-me is just less informed.

5. Enhanced-me. I can take a safe drug that reliably makes me much better at probability puzzles for a few hours. On some logic-flavored question I'm at 40%, and from past experience I know enhanced-me will be at 70%. I'd write down 70% and bet on it before taking the drug. Deference, even with no new evidence in the usual sense.

My vague candidate principle is something like "defer iff the process turning now-me into future-me is an expected epistemic improvement, whether through cleaner reasoning or a trustworthy channel of new information." But cases 2 and 3 are the pair I can't reconcile: both are future-me with more information and a careful enough update, but I defer to one and not the other. Cases 1 and 5 make me think processing quality matters independently, and case 4 makes me think information loss also matters.

So: is there a precise principle that captures these verdicts, or does it force me to drop some? What should it say about a case I haven't settled—say, I'm about to spend an hour talking to a very smart, honest friend who has a known blind spot that tends to push one direction on this topic? And am I wrong to take a "small nudge" in case 3? If you have to sacrifice one of my case-verdicts to get a simpler principle, which one should go and why?
