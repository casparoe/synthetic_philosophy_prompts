People arguing about hedonism keep circling the same exhibit: pleasures are wildly heterogeneous—a hot bath, schadenfreude, finally proving something—so what, if anything, do they all share? The two usual replies are (a) there is a common felt tone, just a very thin or elusive one, and (b) "pleasure" in the hedonist's claim doesn't name a sensation at all but an attitude, like liking or wanting an experience to continue; and then each side accuses the other of changing the subject. My working theory is that the dispute is less about facts of experience than about what the word "pleasure" is supposed to do inside the claim that pleasure is the only thing good for its own sake. Is that right? I want a diagnosis, not a survey: is the heterogeneity objection a real objection to hedonism or is it partly verbal, and if it's partly verbal, where exactly does the substantive disagreement start? And what specifically—a case, a distinction, a new question—would actually move one camp or dissolve the stalemate?
