Before I watch prototype theory, exemplar theory, and the theory theory punch each other all semester, I want to write down what any account of concepts actually owes us. Could you produce that list for me? For each desideratum give the case or empirical result that forces it. I'd expect at least: typicality and graded membership (a robin is birdier than a penguin); productive combination, where the prototype of the whole is not a straightforward function of the prototypes of the parts — pet fish, fake gun, stone lion; default reasoning that gets retracted cleanly (birds fly, but penguins are birds); how children acquire and revise concepts; how two people can share a concept while disagreeing substantially about its instances; and concepts with empty extensions like unicorn or phlogiston, which can't be explained by a pile of stored examples. Then mark each as non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference, and flag any pairs that can't be jointly satisfied at once. That last part is what I actually care about: I suspect some of the apparent conflicts between camps are just people refusing to say which requirement they would give up, and if the list itself is inconsistent in places, I want to know where. After that, a quick paragraph on how prototype theory, exemplar theory, and the theory theory each fares against the list — not a full scorecard, just which single desideratum does the most damage to each.
