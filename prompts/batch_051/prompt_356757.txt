Act as a moral philosopher who works on demandingness and the ethics of everyday consumption. I'm putting together a handount for a discussion group on frugality, and I want a 'which position do you actually hold?' decision tree. The spine of the tree should be the split between frugality as something you do yourself and frugality as an outcome you promote; build it from the logical space of answers, not from a list of labels. Each internal node should be a concrete question or case someone can answer without knowing the position names: whether frugality would still matter if you were the last person on earth, whether other people's waste is your business, whether a flashy activist who halves thousands of people's consumption while living lavishly is doing better or worse than a quietly frugal person, whether the waste itself or the opportunity cost does the normative work. Give at least seven leaves, and make sure these appear: a Kantian-style duty to oneself, an agent-neutral consequentialism about total consumption or waste, an agent-relative virtue-ethical view, a vocal-frugality view where modeling and advocacy are themselves required, and a deflationary view on which frugality is just aesthetic taste. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really separate—I suspect the agent-neutral consequentialist and vocal-frugality leaves blur at the edges. For each unoccupied branch combination, say briefly whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and give each leaf a one-sentence most awkward commitment. Think it through and brainstorm slightly before you commit, then give me the polished tree, not the scratch work.
