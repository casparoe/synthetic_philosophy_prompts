I stayed up way too late last night arguing with a friend about van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and now I'm supposed to be writing a one-year fellowship application so I want to turn the argument into something useful. The principle, as we were using it, says: my current credence in A, conditional on my future self's credence in A being r, should itself be r. We went back and forth on the usual counterexamples — someone who knows they're about to take a drug that will mess with their beliefs, someone who knows they'll forget part of their evidence. My friend thinks those cases just kill the principle. I think they only show the unrestricted version overgenerates, and that there's still some diachronic coherence constraint in the vicinity worth trying to state properly.

The half-formed idea I want the proposal built around is that the debate is running together two questions. First, is there any version of Reflection that is both strong enough to do real diachronic work and weak enough to avoid the drug/forgetting cases? Second, does whatever survives amount to anything more than ordinary conditionalization plus the platitude that you should defer to people (including future-you) you regard as being in a better epistemic position? If the second answer is "no, it adds nothing," then Reflection is a heuristic rather than a freestanding principle in it's own right. If the answer is "yes, it adds something," I want to know exactly what.

Can you write this up as a two-page research proposal? It should include: a precise statement of the question; why the existing positions leave it open (the Dutch-book defenders don't fully answer the drug/forgetting cases, and the critics are usually content to reject the principle without saying what if anything should replace it); a method, month by month for the year; what the possible outcomes would look like; and what would honestly count as the project failing. Make the method concrete — I'd rather see something like "reconstruct the diachronic Dutch strategy premise by premise and see which premise the forgetting case actually falsifies" than "read the literature critically."
