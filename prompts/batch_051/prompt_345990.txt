I'm not a philosopher or anything, just someone who reads a lot of popular science and ends up in long arguements online. Over the years I've arrived at a view I keep defending everywhere, and I want to check whether it's actually risky or whether I've insulated it so nothing could touch it.

The view is roughly this. Explanations of complex things — life, consciousness, language, markets, whatever — seem to come in two kinds. One kind hangs the explanation on something that already has the very thing you're trying to explain: a designer, a homunculus, a vital force, a 'higher level' that just does the work. I call these skyhooks, because they hang from above and the hook itself never gets explained. The other kind builds the thing up from smaller, dumber pieces, where each step makes sense on it's own — the way evolution gets a complex eye from light-sensitive cells, or a market coordinates without anyone planning it. I call these cranes: they lift from below instead of hanging from above.

My position is that skyhooks never turn out to be doing the real work. They're placeholders. Every time we've actually solved one of these puzzles, the answer turned out to be cranes, or cranes built on cranes. And I also think it's a mistake to overcorrect into greedy reductionism — insisting the only real explanation sits at the very bottom, like 'it's just physics' or 'it's just genes', and that higher levels of description are somehow fake. Cranes can make bigger cranes: cells make tissues make organs make minds.

So what would count against this? Specifically, what would have to be true for me to have to admit there are real skyhooks — explanations that genuinely need a top-down something and can't be rebuilt from the bottom up? I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd like to know which of them a defender of my view would themselves concede as counting against it, not just what critics say. Also: am I more committed to greedy reductionism than I like to think? If the anti-skyhook story fails somewhere, does that mean giving up the building-blocks picture, or just needing a better crane? And finally, be honest with me: has my view been set up so that any apparent skyhook can always be dismissed as 'not found yet'? If so, is that a problem for a view like this, or is it just a reasonable stance for someone who's seen the same pattern repeat so many times?

Could you answer in British English, please?
