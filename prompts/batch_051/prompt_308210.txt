I've been developing a distinction I call fixed-threshold satisficing versus leaky satisficing, and I'd like an honest assessment of it.

Fixed-threshold satisficing: choose an aspiration level before starting a search and take the first option that meets or exceeds it. Leaky satisficing: choose an initial aspiration level, but after each rejected option lower the threshold by some fixed proportion, say ten percent, and take the first option that meets the current threshold. My model example: someone looking for an apartment starts out wanting a place that scores at least 80, then after a week of rejections accepts a place scoring 65 because the bar has leaked down from 80 to 65.

My claims are these. First, leaky satisficing is not simply fixed-threshold satisficing with a lower initial bar: the same sequence of options can lead to different choices depending on the decay rate, because a fixed bar might accept an early option that a still-high leaky bar would reject. Second, leaky satisficing is often the rational rule when search costs are unknown but the cost of never terminating is certain; the decay rate is a way of pricing the passage of time without pretending to know exactly what continued search is worth. Third, it explains a familiar pattern: people settle after long, discouraging searches and then feel they overpaid, which looks puzzling if we model them as optimizing or as fixed-threshold satisficers but is exactly what a decaying threshold predicts.

The obvious objection I can already see is that a sufficiently steep decay rate turns the rule into a machine for rationalizing capitulation: the threshold falls just in time to bless whatever is in front of the person. I suspect that is true but not fatal, because any finite stopping rule has grim cases; what matters is whether leaky satisficing does better than the alternatives across typical sequences.

Is this distinction already known under another name, and does it track anything real in how people set and revise aspiration levels? Is my second claim about rationality defensible, especially given that the decay rate itself has to come from somewhere? And where exactly does the rule break; what sort of choice sequence makes leaky satisficing obviously worse than fixed-threshold satisficing or ordinary optimizing?
