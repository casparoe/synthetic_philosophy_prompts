Since I retired I've been trying to get straight about the information-theoretic resolution of Maxwell's demon, and I think I've got the standard version down. The demon has to record which side of the box the molecule is on, and at the end of the cycle it has to erase that record; erasing a bit at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2. If the whole apparatus is at the gas temperature, the erasure cost exactly cancels the kT ln 2 of work the demon got from the expansion, so the second law is saved. Now here's the variation I can't work out. Suppose the demon's memory register is kept much colder than the gas — say the gas and box sit in a bath at T_hot, and the register sits in a bath at T_cold = T_hot/10. Each measured molecule still lets the demon extract kT_hot ln 2 of work, since its the same isothermal expansion, but erasing the bit now only costs kT_cold ln 2. That looks like a net gain of k(T_hot − T_cold) ln 2 per cycle, which seems like work for free. Does the standard Landauer resolution just break once there are two temperatures, or is the accounting being done wrong? I'd like someone to go through the energy and entropy books carefully and say what this demon is actually doing, and whether the second law comes out intact.
