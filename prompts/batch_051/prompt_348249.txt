I serve as the philosophy department's representative on my university's General Education Council. The Council is reviewing a proposed course for the Scientific Reasoning requirement, and the vote is scheduled for our next meeting. The course, cross-listed between Philosophy and Data Science, is titled The Problem of Induction, from Hume to Machine Learning. Its first half works through Hume's skeptical argument about induction and Goodman's new riddle of induction concerning the predicate "grue" (the puzzle that the same evidence supports "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" equally, where something is grue if it is examined before some future time and green, or unexamined before that time and blue). Its second half develops the no-free-lunch theorems of machine learning and argues that they provide a formal counterpart to Hume's point: if one averages over all logically possible target functions with no prior weighting, every candidate learning rule has the same expected error on unseen cases, so any advantage a rule enjoys on one class of problems is exactly offset by poorer performance elsewhere. Successful induction therefore depends on assumptions that are not themselves extracted from the data. The Council's criteria state that a course satisfies the requirement only if it gives students sustained practice in drawing and evaluating inferences from quantitative or empirical evidence. The Data Science representative argues that the course satisfies the criteria precisely because it forces students to identify and defend the assumptions behind every inference. Another faction argues that the course would undermine the requirement in practice: the no-free-lunch results cannot bear the skeptical weight the course places on them, because their uniform average over all possible target functions does not model any actual environment, and teaching the material as though it showed empirical induction is hopeless would leave students with a ready reason to mistrust even well-supported empirical claims. Draft a short decision memo for the Council that recommends approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The memo should give an actionable bottom line, the reasons for it, and an honest engagement with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
