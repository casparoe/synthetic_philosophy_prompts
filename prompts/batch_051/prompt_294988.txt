I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, so sorry if I mangle the terms or spell things wrong. I've been going down a rabbit hole about the color phi illusion and Dennett's Orwellian vs Stalinesque distinction, and something's bugging me.

Setup as I understand it: two dots flash in sequence at different positions. People don't report two separate flashes; they report a single dot that moves from the first spot to the second. And if the dots have different colors, they report seeing the color change halfway through the motion, before the second dot would even have appeared. So the brain is doing some kind of backfill of the middle of the experience.

Dennett's move, I think, is that there are two ways to describe that backfill. Orwellian: you consciously saw the first flash as it actually was, and then your memory got quietly edited afterward, so what you report is the doctored version. Stalinesque: your conciousness got delayed until the brain finished building the whole "moving, color-changing dot" sequence, so the unedited version never made it into experience at all — the show was rigged before it aired. He then argues that no experiment can tell these apart, because every downstream thing we can check — reports, memory, behavior — is identical, and the question only looks meaningful if you assume there's some privileged place and time where "the experience itself" happens.

Here's what I can't figure out. Suppose we eventually get brain-computer interfaces good enough to decode visual contents in real time, at like 20 millisecond resolution, tracking what someone is seeing without asking them to report anything. We run the color phi experiment and can watch whether a "moving dot with a mid-way color change" representation is ever present before the second flash, or whether it only appears after the second flash lands. Does that finally break the tie for real, or does Dennett's argument survive it? I can see a version where the decoder is just a less lossy way of doing the same old cross-examination, but I can also see a version where we now have independent access to the finished experience and can timestamp when it got built.

So my question is: which premise in Dennett's argument is actually under pressure once we have that kind of direct real-time readout, and is there a repaired version of the argument that still says something important even if the empirical tie gets broken? I care more about whether the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction finally becomes a real distinction than about how the actual experiment would go.
