They'll fund maybe six small projects over three years: mostly philosophers of science and metascience people, some collaboration with social scientists, but no large data collection. They've sat through two meetings of everyone nodding about "verifiability," and now they want someone to actually rank the open questions so they can spend the money.

I want my own pet distinction either promoted or killed off, not flattered. I think "clear feedback" is doing at least three different jobs: (1) you can tell the final answer was wrong, (2) you can tell which step in the chain went wrong, and (3) the failure actually tells you what to do differently next time. My hunch is (3) does most of the work and (2) is underappreciated, but I don't know whether sorting that out is more upstream than, say, whether feedback improves a field by filtering out bad ideas or by allowing good ideas to accumulate. Where does the distinction live in the ranking, and if its not a top priority, what would have to change for it to become one?
