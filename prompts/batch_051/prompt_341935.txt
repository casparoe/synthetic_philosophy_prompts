I keep going back and forth on this argument about age in ventilator triage, and I can't tell whether my unease is moral insight or just squeamishness about arithmetic. Here it is:

(1) When a bed is scarce, it should go to whichever patient's survival yields the most expected QALYs; that's just what it means to get the most health out of a scarce resource.
(2) QALYs are an impartial unit — a year at full health counts the same no matter whose year it is.
(3) So if two patients have identical odds of surviving to discharge, and one would have 40 expected remaining QALYs while the other would have 10, the first has four times the claim on the bed.
(4) Since expected remaining QALYs tend to fall with age, a protocol following this rule will systematically favor younger patients.
(5) Therefore an age-gradient in triage isn't wrongful age discrimination; it's just applying a neutral measure of health. Objecting to it means either rejecting QALYs as a metric entirely or being inconsistent.
