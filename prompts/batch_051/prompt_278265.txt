I've been trying to work out which information-processing systems actually matter morally — which ones can be wronged or made worse off — and my gut keeps giving me inconsistent-looking answers, so I want to know if there's one principle underneath.

A basic calculator: no. A thermostat: no. A plant that releases warning chemicals when caterpillars bite it: no. A Roomba that maps my kitchen, plans a route, and returns to its dock when low: no, though someone taping over its sensors would feel a bit wrong to me. A honeybee that learns flower colors and which ones paid off: yes, a little. A mosquito on my arm: no, I kill it and dont feel anything, even though it also senses and steers and learns a bit. A fish hooked and left to suffocate: yes, that really bothers me. An octopus that opens a puzzle box and squirts water at a researcher: clearly yes. A lab mouse: clearly yes. A super-accurate, cell-by-cell simulation of a mouse brain running on a supercomputer, doing the same information processing as the real mouse: I want to say no because it's still code, but I cant find a reason that doesn't also knock out the honeybee, and that makes me distrust the whole list. A person in a vegetative state with no sign of awareness: yes, though I suspect that might be about personhood or history rather than anything happening in there right now.

So maybe I'm tracking several different things and calling them all "mattering." Or maybe there's one criterion I keep applying inconsistently — something about information processing, feedback loops, having a point of view, or the capacity to suffer. I used to think sentient was the magic word, but the hard cases are exactly the ones where I'm not sure whether there's any inner experience, so that just restates the problem instead of solving it.

I want a principle (or a small set of them) stated precisely that gets as close as possible to these verdicts, and I want to know which of my verdicts it has to overturn and why. And what should it say about the cases I keep going back and forth on: an ant, an ant colony taken as a whole, a garden snail, a lab-grown brain organoid with electrical waves, and a detailed simulation of a human that tells you it is suffering?
