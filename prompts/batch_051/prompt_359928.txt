Okay so I'm the best man at my brother's wedding in about three weeks, and I have to give the toast. Here's the trap. I've spent years honestly convinced by a fair bit of Schopenhauer's pessimism and his picture of the will: that the will is a blind striving that never stays satisfied, that romantic love is mostly the will-to-life using individuals to perpetuate itself, that ordinary happiness is just a brief lull between wants. My brother knows this about me and has specifically asked me not to sand it all off into the usual you-two-are-perfect speech, but he also wants it to land as affection for him and his new wife. The room will be maybe a third people who actually know Schopenhauer and will catch every dodge, and two-thirds family who just want to feel good. Can you write me a four-to-five-minute toast that stays compatible with what I actually believe — it can be dark and funny — but doesn't reduce the marriage to two people getting used by their genes, doesn't imply the couple are just deluded, and dont turn into a lecture? I want the Schopenhauer to actually shape what the toast can and can't say, not just get name-dropped and then ignored.
