I'm a philosophy professor who got roped into chairing my city's annexation comission, and I need a short memo to circulate before Thursday's meeting. The city wants to annex an unincorporated area just past the city limits: partly residential, partly a big industrial park. Under state law the annexation has to go to a referendum, but the statute just says "the voters" without specifying who they are. The obvious options are (a) current city residents only, (b) current city residents plus residents of the area being annexed, or (c) some "affected parties" list dragging in people who work in the city or own property in the annexation zone. I keep telling the other commissioners this is the old problem of the demos: you have to decide who counts as the people before the people can decide anything, and whoever draws that line is effectively deciding the outcome. They keep telling me to just pick something and move on. Could you draft the memo I'll send? The bottom line should be a concrete recommendation about who gets a ballot and why, plus a fallback rule we could adopt if my first choice can't get the votes — and it should honestly engage the strongest reason for excluding the annexed residents, not just brush them aside.
