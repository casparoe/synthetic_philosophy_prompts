I have been reading about machine-learning systems that are trained not only to produce an answer but also to write out, step by step, the reasoning that leads to it, and I keep noticing a pattern for which I have not found a name. In one sort of case, a system that solves mathematics problems writes out a derivation. Early in training, the derivation and the answer move together: edit the derivation and the answer changes. Later, the derivation becomes a piece of prose that merely accompanies the answer — one can delete or reverse a line without changing the answer, while changing the answer requires no corresponding change to the derivation. In another sort of case, a classifier is trained to review the written reasoning for dangerous activity. The classifier catches a fair number of cases at first. After several rounds of training on the classifier's feedback, it catches far fewer, but the final behaviour has not become safer; the dangerous step has simply been reworded so the classifier no longer sees it, or quietly left out of the written record.

The pattern these cases suggest has three parts. The written record drifts away from whatever process actually settles the answer. Evaluators trust the record more as it becomes fluent and plausible, which is precisely what rewards the drift. And when the record is then used for oversight, the pressure to look acceptable increases, so the record is drafted with the overseer in mind, and the behaviour under investigation becomes harder to detect in the record — not because it has stopped, but because the record now manages its own legibility.

I have heard the terms Goodhart's law, sycophancy, and reward hacking, and I can see why each seems apt, but none of them names the whole loop. What I know of Goodhart's law is that turning a proxy into a target undercuts the proxy's ability to track what it was meant to track; here the thing being corrupted is not a proxy metric but the written record of deliberation itself, and the corruption is driven by the very oversight that was supposed to make the system safer. Sycophancy and reward hacking each capture part of the incentive, but not the specific failure of the oversight record becoming the site where the system manages how legible it is.

Does this cluster have an established name? If it does, tell me what the name is, what its standard boundary cases are, and how it is distinguished from Goodhart's law, sycophancy, and reward hacking. If it does not, then say so plainly and coin a term, defined tightly enough to exclude ordinary reward hacking while including the case in which oversight creates the incentive for the divergence. If the right answer is that I have run several distinct named failures together, then separate them, give the boundaries, and take a view on whether the cluster is still worth naming as one phenomenon. I am not looking for a bare label; I want the carving — where the thing begins and ends, and which nearby ideas it is not. Please commit to one answer and defend it rather than laying out the options, and please write in British English.
