I'm a high school debater, and I keep wondering what would happen if argument itself were treated the way chess or tennis is. Suppose that by 2030, in the United States, competitive argument becomes a fully professionalized spectator sport: national leagues, televised elimination tournaments, and judges who award points on explicit criteria—clarity of premises, responsiveness to the opponent's actual position, conceding when the evidence warrants it, and avoidance of known fallacies. Prize money and sponsorship are comparable to professional tennis, while courts, legislatures, journalism, and social media continue exactly as they are. Give me your best reasoned prediction of how this changes the quality of argument inside the competitions itself and in ordinary public debate over the next twenty years. I want the chain of reasoning made visible: which effects come from the incentives the scoring system creates, which depend on fragile details of how judging is implemented, and which would appear even under an ideal scoring system. Mark each step as robust or speculative, and tell me what would have to happen—for instance, what kind of point-gaming, audience capture, or style-over-substance drift—for the sport to make public argument worse than it is now.
