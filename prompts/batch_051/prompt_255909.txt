I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but over the past few months I've been reading about the proposal that the conscious self is a kind of internal model, and that its defining feature is phenomenal transparency: the model is not experienced as a model, because the machinery that produces it is invisible from the inside. What you experience is just the world and yourself as immediately given. To me this sounds uncannily like a software system that hides its own implementation so completely that the user mistakes the interface for the underlying system, and, worse, never discovers that they are a user, because the "user" is itself something the system renders. I'd like you to write a satirical piece, roughly 600 to 800 words, in the form of release notes or an incident postmortem for a shipped product: call it something like Phenomenal Self-Model v3.1, or pick a variant you prefer. The venue should feel like an internal engineering changelog or a vendor support page, deadpan throughout.

The satire should target the theory itself, specifically the way transparency is usually presented as a neat explanatory feature, by treating it as a deliberate product decision. Transparency ships enabled by default and cannot be turned off from within the product; there is no direct introspective access to the model as a model; attempts to "see through" the representation are deprecated, unsupported, or gated behind a narrow set of practices with terrible documentation; and the entity reading the release notes is, strictly speaking, one of the model's own outputs rather than an independent operator. Keep the engineering details internally consistent, so that a reader who knows the philosophy recognizes that the jokes are earned and come from the actual content of the view rather than from exaggeration that misrepresents it. Ideally, by the end, the reader should be left with the genuinely strange idea that the very transparency that makes experience feel immediate and real is also what makes the self impossible to inspect from the inside, and that there is no independent user standing outside the system who could file the bug report.
