I'm a novelist, not a philosopher or psychologist, but I've fallen down a rabbit hole about introspection and rumination. I'm writing a book whose narrator spends half the novel auditing their own motives, and my editor keeps circling the same passages with notes like this is just looping and nothing new is being learned here. That pushed me toward the actual disagreement about whether turning inward helps or hurts. On one side there's the old idea that self-examination is basically always good and that you can't know how to live without it; on the other side there's a lot of work arguing that certain kinds of self-examination go sour --- repetitive, self-punishing, making the examined feeling worse rather than clearer, the difference people sometimes draw between open curiosity about your own mind and brooding rumination. i can feel that difference in my hands when I'm drafting, but I don't have clean words for it. In my notebook I've been calling it the difference between mining a scene and just chewing the same memory until it loses all taste.

What I want is a systematic translation. Restate the main positions in this debate in the vocabulary I actually work in as a novelist: drafting and revision, structural edit versus line edit, the internal editor, first person versus free indirect style, narrative arc, character motivation, interiority, cutting versus bloating a scene, the difference between a narrator who keeps descending a level and one who stays on the surface for a reason. Tell me which claims map cleanly onto that vocabulary, which ones you had to fudge, and which ones have no counterpart at all in the way I think about a manuscript. Then give me the residue report: is what fails to translate a sign that my craft vocabulary is missing something real about the mind, or a sign that some of the psychological positions are less precise than they look once they're forced to talk in concrete terms?

Ground it in the writing problem I actually have so it doesnt stay abstract: a first-person narrator keeps reinterpreting the same memory, and I can't tell whether the revision is deepening the scene or sanding it down into mush. The translation should let me say, in craft terms, when self-examination is still doing structural work and when it's just interiority without plot.
