Popper's argument against historicism was, roughly, that you can't make long-run predictions about human history because the future depends on what we'll know, and we can't know in advance what we'll know; any such prediction would itself become part of the social situation it's predicting and would alter it. But the cliodynamics people (Turchin and the secular cycles research, say) treat large premodern and early-modern states as dynamical systems: population, state revenues, elite numbers and such oscillate on generational timescales, and they claim you can predict broad instability crises from these structural variables decades in advance, with zero reference to what anyone will think or invent. Does Popper's argument actually kill that project, or does it only kill predictions about the content of ideas? I want to know which exact premise in Popper is doing the work, whether Turchin-style models dodge it or still depend on it, and whether Popper's point survives in some weakened but still worth-having form.
