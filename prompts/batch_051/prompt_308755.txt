Construct a thought experiment in which two agents trade even though, once all their private information has been pooled, they assign identical probabilities to every state of the world that matters for the trade, and that agreement is common knowledge between them. The trade must be an ordinary repeatable transaction, not a one-off wager, and must not be financed by irrationality or by differing priors; a naively stated no-trade theorem says this cannot happen, so the case must run on a feature the theorem's conditions leave open, arranged so that an observer who can see only the agents' beliefs cannot tell what is financing the trade. After the scenario, do the check: give the common prior, the private signals, the pooled posterior they converge on, the theorem's conditions in turn, and exactly which condition fails. Then name the single feature doing the work, remove it, and show that the trade collapses back into no-trade. Finish by saying what the case shows about the move from observed speculative volume to the claim that traders hold different priors, and how a defender of the no-trade theorem would answer. The case is for a post on whether real-world trade is evidence against common priors.
