I keep coming back to the sphex wasp, and I want someone to tell me exactly where the argument breaks. The wasp follows a rigid routine: it drags a cricket to the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp drags it back to the threshold and inspects again — and it will repeat this indefinitely, never noticing that the inspection step has become unnecessary. It is stuck in a subroutine, and that is why it looks unfree: its behavior is a fixed program, and the program cannot revise itself in light of new information. I have no trouble saying the wasp is not free. But when I try to state what I have that the wasp lacks, I cannot stop the following argument from going through:

(1) The wasp is unfree because its behavior is rigid: a fixed subroutine runs to completion on a trigger, and the wasp cannot modify the subroutine when the situation changes.
(2) Human behavior is produced either by some mechanism — genes, neural wiring, training, learned heuristics, deliberative reasoning — or by no mechanism at all.
(3) If it is produced by no mechanism at all, then the act is uncaused or random, and an uncaused or random act is not authored by the person either; randomness is not control.
(4) If it is produced by a mechanism, then at bottom that mechanism is either deterministic or partly random.
(5) If it is deterministic, then given the person's prior state and inputs, the act is fixed — which is exactly the wasp's rigidity with a deeper call stack; adding more layers does not change the logical structure of a subroutine.
(6) If it is partly random, the random component is not under the person's control; inserting an RNG into a program does not make the program any more responsible for its outputs.
(7) So every human act is either fixed, random, or some combination of the two, and none of those is what we mean by free.
(8) Therefore the distinction I wanted to draw between the wasp's rigidity and my own flexibility has no principled basis.

Is this argument valid? If it is not, I want the exact step that fails and the exact reading under which it fails, because I am unwilling to accept the conclusion but cannot find a principled place to resist it. If it is valid, then which premise should I reject, and why? Please locate the bug or the false premise precisely rather than telling me in general that determinism is compatible with freedom.
