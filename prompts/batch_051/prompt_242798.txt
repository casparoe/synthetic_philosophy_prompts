I keep coming back to a habit I don't fully understand: we tell young people their work should be their passion, and at the same time we push them to turn every hobby into a side business. The message seems to be that a life is meaningful when the things you love also produce something, and that a hobby that doesn't earn its keep is basically wasted time. I've been rereading Schlick, and he looks to me like he is saying nearly the opposite: the meaning of life is in play, in activity done for its own sake, the way a child plays, and work becomes meaningful only by becoming play-like. If that's right, then making play justify itself by producing something would leach out exactly what gives life meaning. So here's what I want: reconstruct the theory of the meaning of life that the monetize-your-passion practice presupposes. Does the practice actually commit people to that theory, or is it compatible with Schlick's play view after all? And what would we have to change in our practices if Schlick were right? Finally, say what would change your mind about the reconstruction you give.
