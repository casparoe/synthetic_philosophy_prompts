I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I'm trying to nail down my view on free will and sphexishness before I present a qualifying paper to my advisor. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) A creature whose behavior always runs the same fixed, triggered subroutine no matter what changed in the environment—the standard sphex wasp example, where it re-inspects the burrow every time the cricket gets moved an inch (probably apocryphal, but the idea is what matters)—is missing the kind of control that matters for free agency. Sphexishness, as I use the term, is a defect.

(2) I'm a naturalist and a compatibilist: free will doesn't require a nonphysical gap in the causal chain, and determinism isn't the real threat. The threat, if anything, is rigidity, not causation.

(3) But I also think human deliberation is genuinely different in kind from the wasp's loop. We hold options in mind, rehearse outcomes offline, and let estimated costs and benefits revise which disposition actually fires. That isn't just a longer sphex sequence.

(4) At the same time, when I look at the fine grain, every individual cognitive operation in deliberation—spreading activation, threshold crossings, whatever else is going on—looks automatic and reflex-like. There's no homunculus in there deciding which weights to consult.

(5) I also hold that freedom comes in degrees and is basically a matter of how rich the feedback loops are between behavior and environment: a thermostat has a little, a jumping spider more, a person vastly more, and no unmoved mover type event is needed anywhere.

(6) And I'm inclined to say that a creature whose behavior was controlled by a giant but fixed lookup table—each situation mapped to one reflex-like output—would not be free, even if the table was huge and even if some outputs looked like deliberation reports.

I keep going back and forth about whether this package is compatable as a whole, but I can't tell which commitments are actually doing the conflicting—maybe the real problem isn't where I think it is. Could you audit the set? I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, how badly, what would have to give in each case, and which pairs only look in tension once you spell them out. Please rank the real tensions rather than treating them all as equal.
