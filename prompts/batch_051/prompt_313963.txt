I teach a small philosophy seminar on cooperation and exploitation, about 12 undergrads, one 75-minute session. I want a game, not a lecture. The game should get students to viscerally distinguish two kinds of non-contributor in a shared enterprise before we name either one. The first is the quiet free rider: they take the benefit, put in nothing, but they dont hassle anyone and they don't pretend to be something they're not. The second is the social parasite: they also put in nothing and take the same benefit, but they act as though the benefit is owed to them, they mock or guilt-trip the contributors, and they leave the contributors feeling like suckers for having contributed. My target moment is when students realize they are much more angry at the second player then at the first, even though both cost the group exactly the same number of points. The distinction should emerge from the play itself, so no lecture up front, and the materials shouldn't label anyone as a free rider or a parasite.
