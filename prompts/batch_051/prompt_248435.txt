I'm in a small philosophy reading group and last week we got on the topic of idolatry in the broad sense—not statues and altars, but the idea that you can value a perfectly good thing in the wrong way. One member, who'd just read something on it, summed the whole debate up like this:

"Idolatry is simply loving a good thing more than God. A career, a partner, even your children can be idols if they outrank God in your affections. So nothing about the object itself matters; an idol is defined entirely by it's position in your ranking of loves. The cure follows straight from the diagnosis: bump God to the top of the list, and then you can go on loving everything else exactly as much as before. Anyone who claims some objects are more naturally idol-prone than others is just mistaking their own temptations for a theory."

That sounds clean, but the more I think about it the less sure I am. I keep feeling like there's a difference between ranking my child above God on some single scale of affection, and quietly building my whole sense of security and identity around my child—what I'd call making her my "religion." The second feels like a different kind of mistake, not just a bigger amount of the first. Are these actually different, or am I the one muddying up what my friend got exactly right? Do an accuracy audit of his summary: which parts are fair, which parts subtly distort the view, and what would a defender of the more nuanced position say is really at issue. Give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no intro or conclusion.
