Two people in my building got into it in the shared kitchen about whether beef or chicken is worse for animals, and I've been turning it over ever since. They agreed to set aside climate and health and just talk about suffering. Neither of them is any kind of expert and neither am I, but the arguement got surprisingly deep. I've been trying to work it out on my own, mostly by replaying their points and reading random forum threads, and I still can't settle it.

Alisha thinks chicken is worse. Ben thinks beef is worse. Here's the back-and-forth as best I remember it.

(1) Alisha: For the same amount of meat you're killing a couple hundred chickens versus a tiny fraction of a cow. Each chicken is a whole life. I don't see how that doesn't swamp everything else.

(2) Ben: Counting lives is the wrong unit. A cow lives about two years and has a much richer mental and social life than a chicken, so one cow's suffering could easily outweigh dozens of short chicken lives. And beef is so inefficient that, even per calorie, it uses way more feed—so it also causes more of those field-mouse and insect deaths from growing crops.

(3) Alisha: Those crop-death numbers are basically guesses, mostly insects and mice nobody can actually count. The billions of chickens killed are certain. And if crop deaths really are your worry, grass-fed beef mostly avoids the feed fields, while a chicken still has to eat grain and get slaughtered at six weeks.

(4) Ben: You can't just set crop deaths aside because they're hard to estimate. If you're serious about wild animals, pesticide and harvest deaths on feed crops could dominate the whole calculation, and beef drives far more of those acres. Grass-fed doesn't fix that at scale—it uses way more land, pushes out wild animals, and comes with predator control.

(5) Alisha: That's a point about how most beef happens to be raised, not about whether beef has to be that way. Chicken has no low-crop-death version; even pastured chickens are fed grain. And pasture isn't automatically worse for wild animals—it can support more of them than a soybean field.

(6) Ben: Pasture supports some animals, but at a fraction of the density the land would otherwise have, and ranchers kill predators to protect herds. Plus, practically, the choice at the store is feedlot beef or factory chicken, so the "grass-fed beef exists" point doesn't apply to the beef almost anyone actually eats.

Could you be the judge on this, round by round? For each of the six moves, tell me whether it's clearly mistaken, mostly mistaken, mostly right, or clearly right, and why in a sentence or two. Then give me a definite overall verdict: which of them is closer to right, and what are they both missing? I want you to actually commit, not lay out both sides and leave me to pick. If the verdict hinges on an empirical question, say what it is and what you'd bet on in the meantime.
