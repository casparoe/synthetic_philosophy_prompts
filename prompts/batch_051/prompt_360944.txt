I've been going back and forth with a programmer friend about whether a particular finite sequence of coin flips can ever be called random, and I want a second opinion. He keeps saying no exact sequence of 12 flips is more random than another, because every exact sequence has probability 1/4096. I get that, but when I ask him to rank these five:

1. HHHHHHHHHHHH
2. HTHTHTHTHTHT
3. HHHTTTHHHTTT
4. THHTHTTHHTTH
5. HTTHTHHTTHHT

from most random to least random, he refuses, which feels like a cop out to me. Obviously the first one belongs at the bottom.

One wrinkle: the fourth sequence wasn't made up by me. It comes from a rule he showed me: start with T, then keep appending the opposite of everything you have so far (so T, then TH, then THHT, then THHTHTTH, and the first 12 of the next round is THHTHTTHHTTH). His point is that the rule is short but the result still looks random, so he says judging by "shortest description" just moves the problem around.

Can you actually commit to a ranking of the five, and give me a number from 0 to 1 next to each for how random you'd call it? Also answer this: is "random" the right word for a single finite outcome, or should it be reserved for the process that produced it? If you think there's a better way to score them than either raw probability or shortest description, I'd like to hear that.
