OK here's an argument my housemate keeps pushing about dreams, and I genuinely can't tell wether it's valid or whether it just slides between two senses of "experience":

(1) If dreams are real experiences, they have to be experiences that happen during sleep, at the time of the dream, not only later when I wake up.
(2) But during sleep you don't do any of the things that make a state an experience in the first place: you don't attend to what's happening, you can't report it as it happens, and you can't form the judgment that it is happening now. (Sleep-talking doesn't count, since it's behavior rather than a report, and the person doesn't actually respond to questions.)
(3) Anything that can't be attended to, reported, or judged at the time is not an experience at all; at most it's a story that gets assembled retroactively when you wake up.
(4) So dreams aren't experiences. What I call "remembering a dream" is really just a waking confabulation, and the dream itself is nothing over and above my disposition to tell a story when I wake.

Is this valid? If not, which exact step fails, and under which reading of "experience"? I'd especially like to know where lucid dreamers fit: people can, during REM, move their eyes in a pre-arranged pattern to signal "now I'm dreaming." Does that break step (2), or can the argument absorb it? Also, is (4) basically Malcolm's view, and does Dennett's idea that the whole dream narrative might be loaded into memory at the moment of waking count as a different way of reaching (4), or as a different conclusion altogether?
