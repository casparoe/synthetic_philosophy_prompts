After two friends had a falling out over a hookup, I got roped into helping write the consent policy for a week-long event our group runs, and I've realized I hold a bunch of things about consent that I've never had to defend together. I'd like to know whether they actually hang together. The set I keep defending goes something like this. (1) Consent is something you communicate: a private feeling of willingness that never gets expressed isnt consent. (2) But a communicated yes isnt enough either; if the yes doesn't reflect what the person actually wants, like agreeing just to keep the peace or because they feel they can't really say no, then it shouldn't count as consent. (3) Deception can invalidate consent: if I only agree because you lied to me about something that was a dealbreaker for me, I didn't validly consent. (4) The test for whether a lie matters is subjective; it's about whether this particular person would have refused if they'd known, not whether a typical person would have. (5) Coercion requires threats or force, so a bad alternative, or the fact that someone's circumstances make it very hard to say no, doesn't by itself make a yes coerced, and a calm, unenthusiastic yes can be perfectly valid consent. (6) And I think the rules around consent should be knowable in advance from the outside, so that a reasonable person making an honest effort can know whether theyre on the right side of the line.

I can't tell which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and which one I'd have to give up to make the rest consistent. Could you go through them and tell me, in order of severity, where the real tensions are, what would have to give in each case, and what the cheapest fix would be? If I'm asking the wrong question, or the whole way I've set this up is confused, tell me that plainly instead of pretending the list works. I'd rather get pushback than a polite audit.
