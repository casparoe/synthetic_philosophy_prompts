A defense research group is considering the following mechanism for keeping autonomous weapons under meaningful human control, and I would like an analysis of how it would behave in practice rather than an assessment of its stated intentions.

Mechanism. Before an operation, a human officer must certify a list of permissible target types and the rules for converting sensor evidence into an identification; this advance certification is the only pre-deployment authorization. During the operation, whenever the system selects something as a target, it must route a strike proposal to an officer selected at random from a unit not otherwise involved in the mission. That reviewer receives the system's target file — the sensor readings, the automated justification, and the system's confidence level — but is not given the identity of the operators or any other mission context. The reviewer has sixty seconds to act. A veto halts the strike and requires a written reason. An approval is recorded as if the reviewer had fired the weapon personally, and carries the corresponding legal responsibility. If the reviewer does nothing, the strike proceeds after sixty seconds, and legal responsibility instead falls on the officer who certified the target list. Reviewers cannot communicate with the system's operators, and all decisions are audited after the operation.

Please walk through how this mechanism would actually operate. What would it incentivize for the reviewers, the certifying officers, the system's designers, and the commanders deploying the system? Who would game it first, and how? What second-order effects would follow for target selection, the quality of target files, the distribution of legal risk, the pace of operations, and the tendency to rubber-stamp or offload decisions onto the system? Identify the most likely failure modes, and say for each whether it could be fixed by changing the default, the time limit, the reviewer pool, or some other parameter without eliminating meaningful human control. Conclude by separating the features of the mechanism that actually do the work of preserving meaningful human control from those that merely preserve its appearance.
