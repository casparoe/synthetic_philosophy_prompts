Okay so I somehow got put in charge of the "big questions" meeting for my school's philosophy club, and this month we're doing "is anything actually fundamental?" I fell down a Wikipedia rabbit hole about metaphysical grounding and now my head is spinning, because it seems like everyone's using "fundamental" to mean a different thing. Some people say the whole cosmos is the only fundamental thing (priority monism?), some say there's a bottom level of little physical things, some say it's not things but facts that ground everything else, some say the chain of "exists because of" just goes down forever, and some commenter said grounding isn't a real relation at all, it's just how we sort explanations. The club members are smart but nobody has done metaphysics before, so I want to make a decision tree / handout that sorts people by what they actually think about cases, not by which label sounds cool.

So as a professional metaphysician, could you build me that tree? It should start from really basic concrete questions — like whether the lump of clay is more fundamental than the statue, or whether the set {a, b} exists because its members do, or whether my being conscious is grounded in what my neurons are doing — and every branch node should be answerable by someone who has never heard the word "grounding." Please build it from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of names, and include at least these leaves: priority monism, grounding pluralism with a well-founded base, the view that grounding is real but needn't bottom out (infinite descent allowed), and anti-realism about grounding, plus wherever "everything is mutually dependent" holism ends up. Aim for maybe six to eight questions. Flag any two leaves the tree can't genuinely seperate, and note any combination of answers that nobody seems to occupy and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Think carefully and brainstorm a few candidate diagnostic questions before settling on the final ones, because if those questions don't actually split the positions the whole thing is useless for the club.
