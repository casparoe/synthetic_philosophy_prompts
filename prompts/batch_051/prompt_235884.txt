I have been drafting a defense of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, and I keep relying on a distinction that I suspect is either the load-bearing part of the view or a disguised way of abandoning it. I would like it assessed honestly before I build the paper on it. I call the two positions "report-level illusionism" and "kind-level illusionism." Report-level illusionism says that our introspective reports are themselves systematically false: when I say that a visual experience has a felt character, or that seeing red has a qualitative character that seeing blue lacks, I am wrong about my own mind. Kind-level illusionism makes the narrower claim that the reports are acceptable as far as they go — seeing red really is available to me as different from seeing blue — but that we then draw an illicit theoretical conclusion from them, namely that there is a distinctive kind of fact or property, a phenomenal one, over and above the physical and functional facts. On the kind-level view, introspection is an honest witness with a bad theory: it tracks real differences among internal states, and philosophical reflection then miscategorizes those differences as evidence for a special kind. The cases that push me toward this view are ones in which a subject appears to gain a discriminatory or practical capacity rather than contact with a novel kind of fact — learning to classify one's own perceptual states more finely, or registering the urgency of pain without having to posit an intrinsically felt property behind that urgency.

My questions are these. First, does this distinction already exist under a better name? It feels too obvious not to be somewhere — something in the neighborhood of the difference between being an eliminativist about a category and being an error theorist about first-person reports — but I have not seen illusionists argue through it explicitly. Second, and more importantly, does the distinction carry the weight I want it to? My worry is that kind-level illusionism collapses into report-level illusionism, because the report and the kind are entangled: if I sincerely report that seeing red differs from seeing blue in a directly available felt way, am I not thereby reporting exactly the sort of phenomenal difference whose existence the kind-level view wants to deny? If that is right, then protecting the report while discarding the kind is an unstable halfway point, not a defensible version of illusionism. Tell me straight: does the distinction name a real possibility, what existing position is it closest to, what does it get right, and where precisely does it break or stop being distinctive?
