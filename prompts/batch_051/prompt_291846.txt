I'm giving a dvar Torah (sermon) at my synagogue on Shabbat Vayikra, the week the Torah reading consists almost entirely of the laws of animal sacrifice. The congregation is split: some members regard the sacrificial system as eternally significant, while others find it primitive and are waiting for the sermon to explain it away. I want the sermon built around Maimonides' claim that the sacrificial cult was not an ideal form of worship but a divinely commanded accommodation to the religious condition of the ancient Israelites — a way of redirecting impulses shaped by surrounding idolatrous cultures toward the service of God. Write me a five-minute sermon that takes this idea seriously without treating sacrifice as an eternal imperative and without reducing the tradition to a mere concession to primitives. It should give both halves of the room something true to take away, and it should read as a sermon rather than as a lecture on medieval philosophy.
