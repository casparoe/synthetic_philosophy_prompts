Draft the joint statement that a committed Humean about laws of nature (a Lewis-style Best System Account) and a committed anti-Humean (an Armstrong-style nomic-necessitation view) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept, including claims about what the total history of the universe gives us, what a theory of laws must do, and which familiar parts of the debate are at least partly verbal; (2) a disagreements table giving the residual positions, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording (for example, the Humean holds that the laws are nothing over and above the best systematization of the particular facts, while the anti-Humean holds that laws are something more that governs or necessitates the particular facts); (3) the cruxes — sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side — such as whether the simplicity-strength trade-off and the naturalness of the properties quantified over in the Best System already import modal structure, and what it means to say that a law governs or necessitates; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, such as the treatment of a Goodman-style 'grue'-predicate world or a one-particle universe, with each side's prediction stated in advance. Keep the agreed claims non-vacuous and the wording of the disagreement neutral. End with a one-sentence summary of the residual crux on the final line.
