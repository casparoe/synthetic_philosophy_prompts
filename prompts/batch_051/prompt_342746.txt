OK so heres where Ive landed on the question of whether prosperity and order can make authoritarian rule legitimate, and I want someone to tell me whether the package hangs together. I'm presenting to a seminar in a few weeks and I dont want to find the cracks in front of them. My commitments, roughly:

(1) A government can only be legitimate if the people it rules could realistically get rid of it through some regular, contestable process — not necessarily Western-style elections, but some genuine avenue of accountability.

(2) Singapore seems to me basically legitimate. Decades of order, growth, public housing, competent government, and most Singaporeans seem to accept the arrangement; I find the argument that liberal democracy is not the only route to decent rule hard to dismiss.

(3) But when the same performance argument is made for China, I dont buy it. Growth and stability dont wash out the repression, and I want to say the Communist Party's record there doesnt legitimize it.

(4) Im also suspicious of consent-based theories: actual consent to the state almost never happens, and hypothetical consent feels rigged in advance.

(5) I dont think elections are sufficient for legitimacy, since plenty of elected governments are corrupt, captured, or predatory.

(6) Still, I think a state that delivers order and prosperity but gives people no way to contest power is missing something central, not just one nice thing among others.

I honestly can't tell where the real tension is. I'd like an audit, not a survey. Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look that way? Rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and then tell me the cheapest coherent position. Take a definite stand: I want to know which of these you think I should revise and why, not a menu of options.
