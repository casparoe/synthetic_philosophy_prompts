I need a definition of community in the thick sense: the sense people invoke when they say that community is a good, that belonging to one matters, and that something valuable is lost when a community dissolves. I don't mean the thin sense in which any set of people with a shared property forms one (the community of left-handed plumbers). I mean the sense in which someone can say of a place that it was once a community and no longer is, even though the same people still live there. I'm writing something about social isolation and belonging, and I keep finding that the moment I try to state the definition explicitly, I reach for two different criteria — shared identity and a practical orientation toward others' good — and the cases below pull those apart.

Here are the cases I need the definition to classify:

- a rural parish where most residents were born nearby, attend the same church, and maintain thick webs of mutual obligation — care during illness, borrowing tools, feuding and reconciling — almost none of which anyone deliberately entered;
- the residents of a three-hundred-unit apartment building who greet each other in the lobby and otherwise keep to themselves;
- an online support group for a rare chronic illness, whose members have mostly never met and who exchange practical advice and sustained emotional support;
- an infantry platoon during a long deployment;
- an extended family split across four countries, in daily group-chat contact but gathering in person only once a year;
- the employees of a firm whose leadership calls the workplace a family, though anyone can be dismissed without notice;
- the same rural parish twenty years after the church closed, the school was consolidated into a town forty miles away, and most working-age adults moved, so that the remaining residents still identify with the place but no longer rely on one another;
- the regulars at a gym who know one another's names and routines but never meet outside it.

My own leanings: the rural parish is a paradigm and the apartment building is a clear non-case. The gym regulars don't feel like a community to me, but I can't say why, since they have repeated interaction, shared space, and mutual recognition. The online support group initially struck me as a community, but then the extended family made me doubt both: one lacks physical presence, the other lacks much face-to-face contact, and yet I hesitate to withhold the word from either. The dissolved parish is the case I most need the definition to handle, because I want to know whether the loss of community can consist in a change in the relations among the same people in the same place.

Propose a working definition, run it through all eight cases, and say where it forces you to revise or to defend a verdict against my initial leaning. End by saying what, on your definition, is lost when a community dissolves, as distinct from when its members die or move away.
