I want a draft of the document that a careful Fuller-style theorist of law and a careful Raz-style theorist of law would produce after a year-long adversarial collaboration on the rule of law. Their dispute is specifically about whether the rule of law has an internal or inherent moral value. The Fullerian camp holds that the principles of legality — generality, publicity, non-retroactivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility of compliance, stability, and congruence between official action and declared rule — constitute an inner morality of law, such that a legal order that grossly fails them is not merely defective as law but evil or at least morally compromised. The Razian camp holds that the rule of law is a negative virtue, like sharpness in a knife: it makes law better at achieving whatever ends it happens to serve, but it is not itself a moral virtue and is compatible with deeply wicked regimes. Raz puts the point in *The Rule of Law and Its Virtue* by saying that the rule of law is just one virtue a legal system may possess, not to be confused with democracy, justice, equality, human rights, or respect for persons.

I have spent enough time on this that I no longer trust my own formulations of the disagreement. What I want is a joint statement that both camps, at their most reasonable, would sign. Please do the following:
