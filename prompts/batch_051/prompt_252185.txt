Write a villanelle on the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism. Let the two refrains carry the debate's two fixed points: one, the expressivist claim that to call an act wrong is to give voice to an attitude toward it rather than to ascribe a property to it; and two, the observation that the same words occur unasserted inside conditionals, as in a premise whose antecedent is that lying is wrong and whose consequent is that getting a child to lie is wrong, where no such attitude need be voiced. Use the returning-refrain structure to enact the difficulty rather than merely describe it: the same line recurs throughout the poem, but each time it sits in a different argumentative position, just as the moral term occurs now with assertoric force and now inside an antecedent. The intervening lines should show why the problem is not a verbal quibble: if the word means one thing when asserted and another when embedded, the inference from conditional and antecedent to consequent becomes an equivocation; but if it means the same, the simple expressive story has not yet explained what preserves the validity of a plain instance of modus ponens. The closing quatrain should state precisely what expressivism must supply: an account of how conative states can stand in relations of consistency and consequence, so that accepting the pair of premises while refusing the conclusion counts as incoherent rather than merely unusual. A metaethicist should recognize the argument as the Frege–Geach problem rather than a generic poem about moral language, and a reader new to it should still get a good poem.
