Write a dialogue between two characters who both agree every life matters equally but disagree about whether the numbers count when you can save one person or five. One is a Taurekian: five lives don't add up to a bigger claim than the one's, so a coin flip is what equal respect actually requires. The other is an aggregationist: respecting each of the five equally just is saving the five, and flipping a coin treats the one as worth five times any one of them. Im not after a debate-club recap of Taurek vs. utilitarianism. Have them dig into a specific pressure point instead: the aggregationist should push the Taurekian on whether he can let numbers matter anywhere—for instance, in choosing between two policies that will predictably affect different numbers of unknown people—without collapsing back into the view hes rejecting. Write exactly five exchanges, each one or two sentences, labelled A and B, no introduction or conclusion.
