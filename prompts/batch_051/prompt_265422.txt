I'm writing a paper on generics — the bare plural sentences like "ducks lay eggs" and "mosquitoes carry malaria" — and I want someone to audit my whole position before I get too attached to it. Here's the package of things I hold:

1. Generics are kind-level. "Ducks lay eggs" is not a disguised claim about most or even many individual ducks; it says something about the duck kind itself.
2. Whether a generic is true tracks whether the property is characteristic of the kind, not raw prevalence. That's how "mosquitoes carry malaria" comes out true even though only a tiny fraction of mosquitoes ever carry it, while "chickens are female" comes out false even though about half are.
3. Part of what makes a property characteristic is that it's striking, dangerous, or practically important to us. That same feature is doing the work in the malaria case: carrying a serious disease is a small but salient pattern.
4. I also think accepting a true generic entitles you to a default inference about arbitrary members. If you accept "ducks lay eggs," you should expect an arbitrarily picked duck to lay eggs until you learn otherwise, and this is a big part of why generics are useful in thought.
5. On the social side, I think the damage from generics like "boys are good at math" or "Muslims are terrorists" is semantic, not merely pragmatic. The sentence itself carries an essentializing or lawlike content that the statistical paraphrase ("most boys are good at math") doesn't, even when the speaker has identical background beliefs.
6. And I'm willing to bite the bullet that "Muslims are terrorists" comes out true on my view, because terrorist violence is exactly the kind of striking-dangerous pattern that makes a property characteristic. I won't build a special carve-out for social kinds just to make the bad ones false.
7. I also want one mechanism for all generics — biological, social, whatever — and I'm committed to a clean semantics/pragmatics divide, where what a sentence means stays seperated from what a speaker is doing with it.

Can all seven hang together? Go through them and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how I should rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what it would cost me. And if the whole package is fine, tell me that too — just don't let me off easy.
