(3) Priya said "everyone knows" isn't true. Plenty of people take biopics as basically accurate, and once you put a real name on screen, the character and the person blur in a way they don't when you invent a barista. A real person has to live with it, except she's dead, so she can't.

(4) Dan said then the harm can't be to her, because she doesn't exist anymore. If the dead have no interests, all that's happened is some viewers formed a false belief, which is true of all fiction. (5) Priya said it doesn't have to be harm to her in a literal sense. Dan said that's about our own feelings of respect, not a duty to an actual person, so the practice has to be justified by what it does to the living, not to her.

(6) Dan then asked whether Priya would object if the film had shown her getting a little too drunk at one party (which is documented) or had moved a real conversation two years earlier. Priya said those feel different, because they don't change the meaning of her life the way inventing the fake quote does. Dan said okay, so you're fine with some false particulars — you just want to police which details matter. Priya said yes, and that's exactly the kind of line a serious filmmaker should have to defend instead of hiding behind "it's just a story."
