I teach a module on Dennett's four kinds of minds, and I've been fiddling with a thought experiment I'd like you to adjudicate. My working criteria, the way I teach them: a Darwinian creature gets its design mostly fixed by natural selection, with no real within-lifetime learning; a Skinnerian creature does trial-and-error in its own lifetime and keeps what gets reinforced; a Popperian creature can run trials offline, in its head, so some hypotheses die instead of the creature; and a Gregorian creature can import already-tested design from other minds — paradigmatically through words and other symbolic mind-tools, so it inherits what other minds have already sorted out.

Now the case. Imagine a species of corvid on an island that makes a two-step tool, a hooked stick for grubbing larvae. Juveniles pick the technique up by watching experienced birds at work and then practising on their own. Birds raised in isolation never manage it, and nobody has seen a bird invent the technique from scratch; even when a local variant dies out it stays gone for generations rather than being reinvented. Different parts of the island have stable, recognisably different hook designs — a barb here, a particular angle there — that persist across generations and occasionally get refined, so the designs drift and improve in a way that outruns any single birds lifetime of trial-and-error. But there's no teaching in any deliberate sense: adults never correct juveniles, there's no joint attention, and the birds seem to reverse engineer the finished artefact rather than copy the motor sequence. And nothing remotely like a word or symbol appears anywhere in their behaviour.

Are these birds Gregorian creatures, or just Popperian ones with a bit of social scaffolding? Work through my four criteria explicitly and say where the case lands. Also tell me whether, whichever way you come down, the case shows that the Gregorian level as I've stated it is doing too much work or is underdescribed — maybe it should be split, or the importing-design-from-other-minds condition needs tightening.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — are they Gregorian, not Gregorian, or do the criteria need revision — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. British English, please.
