I've been going back and forth on the free will literature and one thing keeps bugging me: the role of quantum indeterminacy in the debate seems completely stalled. Libertarians point to quantum events as showing determinism is false, so there's room for alternative possibilities; critics—compatibilists and hard incompatibilists both—say microscopic randomness is the wrong kind of thing to make an action mine or under my control, and anyway neural noise gets averaged out long before anything like a decision happens. This exchange has been running for decades and nobody moves. Diagnose what kind of impasse this really is: is it a factual disagreement about how much quantum effects matter at the neural level, a conceptual disagreement about what "control" or "could have done otherwise" require, a disagreement about what would even count as the randomness "helping," or something else? Then, given your diagnosis, say what would actually count as progress—an empircal finding, a sharper distinction, abandoning the randomness angle, or whatever else. I'm more interested in the structure of the stalemate than in a verdict on who's right.
