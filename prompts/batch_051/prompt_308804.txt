The article's account ran roughly as follows. In the color-phi setup, a red dot is flashed briefly, then a green dot is flashed a short distance away, and subjects report seeing a single dot slide across and turn green partway along its path. According to the article, there are two ways the brain could produce this. On the Orwellian story, you consciously see the two static dots, and then your memory of the event is revised after the fact, so that you recall a single moving dot. On the Stalinesque story, the brain delays the experience itself and edits it before you ever become conscious of it, so that what you literally experience is the moving dot. The article then concluded that Dennett showed the Orwellian story is the scientifically respectable one, because only it avoids positing a hidden theater in the brain where experience is staged. It added that this reveals a large part of ordinary visual experience to be retrospective confabulation, since we do not really see the motion. I suspect the last two moves are too strong, and perhaps the whole contrast is being misreported, but I cannot pinpoint exactly where the account goes wrong.
