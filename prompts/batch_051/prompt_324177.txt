I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy and I'm on our department's events committee. We're deciding whether to co-sponsor a small workshop on Sidgwick's psychical research. The pitch is to bring together people working on his ethics and people from history of psychology to look at how his long involvement with the Society for Psychical Research—he was first president and spent decades involved in investigations of telepathy, mediumship, and whether evidence could support survival—relates to his philosophical project. Some senior faculty are dead against it: they say it would signal that the department takes pseudoscience seriously, and that this side of Sidgwick is a Victorian embarrassment we should quitely leave alone. Others think Sidgwick applied the same methodological standards there as anywhere else, so the topic is a legitimate part of his thought. I've been asked to draft a short memo for the committee with a definite recommendation. Should we co-sponsor, and if so under what framing or conditions? I'd like a committed verdict, not a survey of options, and please give the strongest objection on the losing side an honest run—if we do go ahead, I want the memo to acknowledge what the objectors have right. Also, write it in British English.
