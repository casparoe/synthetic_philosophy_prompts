I am a novelist writing a book set in the New York art world around the time of the First World War, and I keep coming back to an argument I want two of my characters to have about Duchamp's Fountain. The agreed facts, as I understand them, are these: in 1917 a porcelain urinal signed R. Mutt was submitted to the Society of Independent Artists' exhibition, which had promised to accept work from anyone who paid the fee; the committee declined to show it, and the piece later became canonical, usually treated as one of the founding gestures of the readymade. I do not want the scene to be another "what is art?" argument, so I plan to have one character argue from a particular theory: Tolstoy's account in What Is Art? (1897).

As I understand it, Tolstoy holds that a thing is art only to the degree that it infects an audience with a feeling the maker has actually lived through, and he makes the sincerity of the maker, the individuality of the feeling, and the clarity of its transmission central; he adds that art ought to express feelings that draw people toward moral and religious improvement and be accessible to everyone rather than to a cultivated minority. I would like you to carry out the analysis my character would need, and to be more rigorous than a character in a novel could afford to be. Treat the Fountain episode as a real case and run the lens in both directions. What would Tolstoy's criteria say about the object, the pseudonymous gesture, and the committee's refusal? Which parts of the case does the theory genuinely illuminate—say, the question of what counts as sincere expression, or the fact that some body had to exercise judgment despite its own open-door rule—and which parts does it flatten or ignore, such as the role of artworld institutions in constituting the work's significance? Then turn around and ask what the case does to the theory: does Fountain refute Tolstoy's view, reveal that it was built only for certain kinds of art and never anticipated the readymade, or expose a strength in Tolstoy's insistence on sincerity and universal communicability precisely because those are what the readymade seems to sidestep? Where the theory is silent, I would rather you say so than force it to deliver a verdict.
