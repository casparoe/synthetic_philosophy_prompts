I am a PhD student in political science and serve as the graduate student representative on my department's doctoral admissions committee. Each year the committee must produce a single ranking of a shortlist of applicants, but when members' individual rankings differ, the chair currently resolves the matter by informal consensus, and both applicants and faculty have complained that the outcome seems arbitrary. I have been asked to draft a written procedure for aggregating any set of committee members' complete rankings into a single final ranking, one that can be reused each year and can be followed by faculty with no training in social choice theory.

I know from Arrow's impossibility theorem that no aggregation procedure can satisfy all of the standard conditions—unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship—simultaneously. What I need is the translation of that negative result into a concrete decision procedure: the steps to run in order, which of these conditions each step preserves or intentionally sacrifices, what to do when the pairwise majority relation contains cycles, where the procedure should stop with 'no decision,' and which steps are value judgments that must be handed back to the committee rather than settled by the procedure. Please apply the procedure to three hypothetical years: one in which a single applicant beats every rival in pairwise comparisons; one in which the pairwise majority relation cycles over three candidates; and one in which the procedure yields a final ranking that most members will regard as clearly wrong. Be explicit about which Arrow condition is relaxed at each step and what the committee must accept in exchange.
