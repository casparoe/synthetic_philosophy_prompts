I've been reading some of the Bayesian convergence material lately—the idea that, under the right conditions, two people who start with different priors but keep conditioning on the same stream of evidence will have their posteriors converge, so the initial credences get washed out eventually. I can't shake the feeling that this is the same shape of thing I spent a career watching in thermodynamics and statistical mechanics. There, a system left to itself goes to an equilibrium state that depends only on a few conserved quantities—energy, particle number, volume—and not on the fine details of the initial microstate. The initial condition gets forgotten, not becuase something intervenes, but because the dynamics itself acts as a kind of filter that erases everything except what works as a constraint.

Is this a real parallel, or am I being taken in by two different uses of the phrase 'the starting point doesn't matter'? I'd like the answer to actually map one onto the other. On the Bayesian side, what plays the role of the conserved quantity: the support of the prior, the total evidence, the likelihood function, something else? Is the condition that the priors be mutually compatible (say, both put positive probability on the true hypothesis or on each other's possibilities) doing the same job as being on the same energy surface in physics? And where does the analogy break? In thermodynamics the approach to equilibrium has a direction in time and a rate set by the system's details; Bayesian updating can look atemporal if the observations are exchangeable, in the sense that the final posterior might not depend on their order. Is that a disanalogy or does it have a physical counterpart?

I also vaguely remember that the Bayesian side splits in two: merging-of-opinions results, where your and my posterior probabilities for a fixed set of hypotheses converge, and consistency results, where the posterior concentrates on the true hypothesis. Maybe only one of these has a thermodynamic analogue, and I'd like to know which. And give me a test: is there some prediction the thermodynamic picture makes about learning that turns out false, or the reverse? If the parallel can't survive that, I'd rather be told it's superficial.
