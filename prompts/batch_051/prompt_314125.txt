I have spent the past several years working with applied causal inference and econometrics, so I automatically reach for a certain toolbox when I encounter claims about whether an action made a difference: potential outcomes, treatment assignment, interference between units, the stable unit treatment value assumption, individual versus average treatment effects, ex ante versus ex post counterfactuals, and threshold or equilibrium mechanisms. Lately I have been trying to get purchase on a debate in moral philosophy that seems to be about exactly these things, but its participants almost never use this vocabulary. The debate is about individual contributions to collective harms. The inefficacy objection says that when I buy factory-farmed meat, take a flight, buy from a firm with exploitative labor practices, or stay home on election day, my individual act is not wrong for harm-based reasons, because it makes no difference: the supplier's volume is fixed by larger orders, the climate does not respond to one flight, no election turns on one vote, and my single purchase does not cause any additional worker to be hired or animal to be killed. The standard replies say either that the act carries a small probability of being the triggering difference multiplied by a very large harm, so its expected harm is substantial, or that the individual participates in a jointly harmful collective practice and thereby shares responsibility for the collective outcome even without being a but-for cause of it.

I would like you to translate this debate into the causal-inference vocabulary I use fluently, without explaining the basics. Represent the inefficacy objection and two or three standard replies in terms of potential outcomes, individual versus average treatment effects, interference and SUTVA violations, threshold or tipping-point processes, ex ante expected effects versus ex post realized effects, and compositional or equilibrium responses. Make the mapping explicit: for example, is the inefficacy objection best read as the claim that the individual-level treatment effect of the act is zero under the relevant counterfactual, or as the claim that the relevant estimand is not well defined once you fix everyone else's actions, or as something else again? Is the expected-harm reply a claim about a probability-weighted individual treatment effect under uncertainty about others' actions, and does that require relaxing SUTVA? Do not smuggle moral premises in as though they were identification assumptions, and do not pretend the translation is lossless where it is not. Then give me a residue report: which moves in the debate translate cleanly, which translate under strain, and which have no causal-inference counterpart at all. For each item of residue, tell me whether my causal vocabulary is missing something the philosophy needs, or whether the philosophical claim in question is less precise than it first appears. I want an honest map of what survives the translation and what does not, not a forced reconciliation.
