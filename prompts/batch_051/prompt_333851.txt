I work as a software engineer on a small team at a foundation that funds animal welfare programs, building the internal tools the program staff use to rank candidate interventions. None of us have formal philosophy training. The current tool scores each candidate on four dimensions — the peak intensity of the suffering involved, how long the suffering lasts, how many individuals are affected, and how confident we are that the intervention will actually reduce the harm — and then combines the dimensions in a weighted sum. A recent review turned into an argument I have been asked to settle. One analyst insists that two of the dimensions should not simply be added, because value behaves more like a product: he keeps paraphrasing the idea that the value of a conscious life is consciousness times happiness, so if either factor is zero the whole is zero, and a weighted sum lets one dimension compensate for another too easily. Another analyst insists that at least one dimension is lexical: no amount of mild benefit, no matter how broadly distributed, may be allowed to outweigh a certain severity of harm, so the formula needs a hard veto rather than a weight. I do not have the background to judge what would make additive, multiplicative, or lexical aggregation correct rather than merely convenient.

Please write a reusable decision procedure our team can run whenever we have to decide how to combine value dimensions in one of these scoring tools. It should specify the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, simple tests for deciding whether a pair of dimensions should enter additively, multiplicatively, or as a lexical threshold, and where the procedure should stop and hand the question to a person because it is a value judgment rather than a modeling fact. It must be runnable by a competent software engineer in a single design meeting, and it must mark every step that needs sign-off from program staff rather than modelers. Then run the procedure on two worked cases: (1) deciding whether the badness of a pain episode should be computed as intensity plus duration or intensity times duration, and (2) deciding whether to add a hard veto so that interventions preventing certain very severe harms always outrank interventions producing a large number of mild benefits. Please ensure that at least one worked case reaches an answer the team will find uncomfortable, and please say explicitly which parts of the procedure cannot be reduced to a formula. End with a final line beginning "Summary:" containing a one-sentence summary of the procedure.
