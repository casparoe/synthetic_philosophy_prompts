I am trying to adjudicate a disagreement with a colleague. The question is whether Peirce and James were offering genuinely rival theories of truth, or whether they were merely describing the same property from two directions. I have constructed the following argument for the second view, but I suspect there is a subtle mistake in it. Please tell me whether the argument is valid, and if it is not, exactly which step fails and what the correct diagnosis is; if it is valid, tell me which premise a defender of a substantive Peirce–James dispute should attack and what the strongest defense of that premise is.

(1) For Peirce, a proposition is true just in case it is the opinion that the community of inquirers would settle on if inquiry were prolonged indefinitely.

(2) For James, a proposition is true just in case believing it carries us from one part of experience to another more successfully than any rival belief: it fits our total experience and answers to our practical needs better than the alternatives, over the long run and taking everything into account.

(3) The opinion that inquiry would settle on if it were prolonged indefinitely is exactly the opinion that would never be permanently defeated by any further amount of experience or argument.

(4) The opinion that carries us through experience most successfully over the long run, taking everything into account, is exactly the opinion that would never be permanently defeated by any further amount of experience or argument.

(5) Therefore, what Peirce identifies with truth and what James identifies with truth are the same property: resistance to permanent defeat by experience and argument.

(6) So Peirce and James were not giving genuinely rival accounts of truth; they were giving two descriptions of one and the same thing.

I half-suspect the trick is somewhere around (3) through (5), because I seem to be helping myself to the idea that what inquiry would converge on is what works; but I cannot pinpoint the failure. Which step fails, if any, and what exactly is the mistake in it?
