So I want your help writing a short research proposal, and you should think of yourself as a professional philosopher who works on value theory and language. Here's the half-formed idea. I've been reading Ken Hale's piece from the early 1990s, "On Endangered Languages and the Safeguarding of Diversity," and I keep coming back to the part where he says, after noting that we get upset about losing manuscripts or works of art: "The death of a language is a loss to the world, a loss of diversity in all things that matter, not only in the realm of the strictly linguistic." I'm not totally sure that sentence is as obvious as he takes it to be, and I want the project to force the question. Specifically, I want the proposal to ask: does linguistic diversity really matter in the same way as cultural or biological diversity, and what would that "same way" have to amount to? The proposal should state the question precisely, explain why the standard appeals to identity, heritage, or the intrinsic worth of a language don't already settle it, and set out a philosophical approach that would make progress on it rather than just doing empirical documentation of dying languages. A good deliverable would help someone who has to decide whether to spend money on, say, a language revitalisation program as against a museum or an environmental restoration project. Also the proposal needs to be realistic for six months, so say what would count as a successful outcome, and what would count as failure. And if a foundation asks why they should fund what looks like abstract philosophy here, the proposal should say what the practical payoff is. Just write the proposal in continuous prose, no headings or bullet points. Try to separate what is settled in the debate from what is genuinely contested among people who know it, and don't make it sound as if everyone agrees with Hale's claim about diversity.
