ive gone down a rabbit hole for a piece about why we punish attempts less than completed crimes, and I keep tripping over Abelards line that the sin is in the consent, not the act. I reconstructed what I take to be the argument behind it, and now it feels like it proves too much, but I cant find the bad step.

(1) Whether a willed act actually comes off is at least partly a matter of luck: the gun jams, the poison gets switched, the intended victim changes their plans at the last minute.
(2) How bad a person is, their culpability, cant ride on luck like that.
(3) So consenting to a wrong and carrying it out must be equally bad; the outward deed itself adds no sin.
(4) If the deed adds no sin, then our whole practice of treating attempts as less serious than completed crimes, and idle fantasies as less serious still, has no basis in desert.
(5) By the same reasoning, someone who causes the same harm without consent, say a driver who hallucinates and hits a pedestrian, should be no more blameworthy than someone who only daydreamed about it.
(6) That conclusion seems obviously false to me, but each step looked fine on its own.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? Id rather keep the anti-luck intuition in (2) than keep the legal gradient between attempt and completion, but I want to know what that costs.
