I am a novelist working on a book whose protagonist, a contemporary evolutionary biologist, becomes preoccupied with an old objection to natural selection and with a more recent attempt to answer it. I want to get the intellectual texture right rather than have the character perform a cartoonish debate, so I am working through the underlying ideas carefully.

The objection runs as follows. If fitness is identified with actual reproductive success—if the fittest organisms are simply the ones that happen to survive and leave the most descendants—then the principle that the fittest survive can look like an empty tautology, a definition dressed up as an empirical claim. The proposed repair, developed in the philosophy of biology well after Darwin's death, is the propensity interpretation of fitness. On this view, fitness is not an organism's actual reproductive output but a probabilistic disposition: roughly, its expected reproductive success in a specified environment, in the way a biased coin has a probability of landing heads even when a short run of tosses comes up tails. Chance can therefore make a highly fit organism leave few descendants, which is supposed to restore explanatory content to natural selection.

As part of the novel, my biologist writes an unsent letter to Charles Darwin, asking how he would have adjudicated this dispute. I intend the imagined reply to reflect Darwin's actual commitments—his emphasis on variation, the struggle for existence, the analogies and disanalogies between natural and artificial selection, and his acute sensitivity to the role of chance—rather than my own invention. On the basis of what Darwin actually thought and wrote, how would he most plausibly have responded to the tautology objection and to the propensity interpretation? Would he have treated the objection as a serious difficulty, dismissed it as a verbal quibble, or denied that it captured what he meant by natural selection? And would the propensity interpretation have impressed him as a helpful clarification, an unnecessary abstraction, or a genuine distortion?
