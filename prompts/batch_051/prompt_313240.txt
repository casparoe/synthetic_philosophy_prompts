I've spent most of this year trying to make my household more self-sufficient—solar, chickens, a real garden, learning to fix things—and at the same time I keep hearing people defend national self-sufficiency or autarky as if it were obviously the same kind of ideal. Heres the thing nagging at me. Every step I took toward independence came with a new, less visible dependance: the inverter, the feed supplement, the seeds that don't breed true, the online forums I read when the pump quits. It made me suspect self-sufficiency doesn't get rid of dependence so much as push it one link back and make it harder to see. Can you play a committed defender of self-sufficiency/autarky as an ideal for both persons and nations and argue with me about this? I don't want a strawman: you genuinely hold that dependence makes a person or a polity unfree and more fragile, and that minimizing it is a core part of a good life and a good country. You can concede specific points without giving up the main position, keep replies to about a paragraph, and stay in character until I say stop. So here's my opening move: if the aim is not depending on others, then my year of doing it myself left me depending on fewer, more specialized nodes of a vast network, and that felt less like freedom than like becoming a small, brittle country. Why isn't self-sufficiency just the art of choosing your dependencies carefully? And if that's all it is, what's left of autarky as an ideal?
