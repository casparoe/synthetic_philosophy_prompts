I'm helping revise my hospital's scarce-resource allocation policy and keep going in circles on one line. It dates from 2009 and says, in full: "In the event that demand for mechanical ventilation exceeds supply, candidates shall be prioritized according to the expected number of quality-adjusted life-years to be gained from treatment." During the last surge we hit a case the drafters clearly didn't have in mind. Two patients, one ventilator: a 55-year-old Deaf woman with no other health problems and a 58-year-old hearing man, with roughly the same expected survival if treated. To calculate QALYs the hospital used the same survey-based health-state index it always uses, which assigns severe-to-profound deafness a utility of about 0.7. But the Deaf woman told us that from her own perspective deafness costs her nothing: she's not distressed by it, doesn't experience it as a deficit, and when we asked the standard time-tradeoff question she said she would not trade any life-years to become hearing. By her own rating the two patients are exactly tied; by the table's rating, the hearing man gets the ventilator.

So is that what the clause actually requires? The text doesn't say whose quality assessment counts. The purpose of the line was presumably to get the most health-related welfare out of scarce ventilators; does a population-average utility score serve that purpose when the individual's own report diverges, or is self-report too easily gamed (everyone will say zero)? And the drafters, I'm fairly sure, assumed "quality of life" was just an objective measurable thing, not something patients would contest. I'd like you to run it through the usual interpretive moves — the plain words, the purpose, and what the drafters would have said if we could ask them — and tell me which approach should win here. Part of why I'm stuck is that value-of-a-statistical-life logic seems to treat each life as worth one, not worth a coefficient; if a Deaf life is worth 0.7 of a hearing one, we've smuggled a moral judgment in under what was sold as a technical measurement. Does that mean the clause is just badly drafted, or is the problem general to QALY-based triage?
