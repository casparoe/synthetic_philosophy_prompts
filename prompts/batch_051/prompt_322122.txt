So my cousin is a professional musician, and she has a whole set of snap judgments about what counts as a musical instrument that I can't make sense of. A piano counts. Violin, trumpet, kazoo, bagpipes, slide whistle — all count, no hesitation. She also says a drum machine counts, which I thought she'd hate on principle. But a car horn doesn't count. A wind chime isn't a instrument, even though people literally hang them up for the notes. A squeaky rubber duck doesn't count. And the one that really gets me: she says a set of wine glasses that you tune with water and play with a wet finger does not count as a musical instrument, even though she admitted they make exactly the kind of pitched sound she gets paid to make onstage.

Next week I want to ask her about two things, and I'm trying to figure out what she'll say before I do. One is a prop guitar: it looks exactly like a guitar, right shape and size and everything, but it's solid plastic with no strings and you can't get any music out of it — the band just pretends to play it in a video. The other is a glass armonica, which is basically a concert instrument made out of tuned glass bowls mounted on a spinning spindle, played by rubbing wet fingers on the rims — same trick as the wine glasses, except the whole thing was built on purpose to be a serious instrument.

I'm less interested in whether her answers are right than in what rule she's actually running, because I can think of two or three different rules that fit everything she's already said, and wether she says the prop guitar or the glass armonica counts would tell them apart. So: what rules fit her verdicts so far, what does each one predict about the two new cases, which rule do you think she's actually running and why, and what's one more item I should ask her about that would settle it?

2. "What each rule predicts for the two new cases" (about 200 words): go case by case; a small table is fine but explain what it means after.
