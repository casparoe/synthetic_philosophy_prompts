I am trying to reconstruct the theory of vagueness that my philosophy of language professor actually holds, working only from the commitments she has made in seminar about particular cases. The cases all concern "heap" applied to piles of sand of specified grain counts.

Her recorded commitments so far are these.

- She calls "A one-grain pile is a heap" false, and calls "A hundred-thousand-grain pile is a heap" true.
- She refuses to assert "A fifty-seven-grain pile is a heap," and she refuses to assert "A fifty-seven-grain pile is not a heap." She describes the case as borderline.
- She accepts "Either a fifty-seven-grain pile is a heap or a fifty-seven-grain pile is not a heap" as true, and accepts "It is not the case that a fifty-seven-grain pile is both a heap and not a heap" as true.
- She accepts "There is exactly one number n such that an n-grain pile is a heap and an (n − 1)-grain pile is not a heap" as true.
- She denies that any amount of empirical investigation of the piles or of ordinary usage could establish, of any particular n, that n is the cutoff.
- She rejects the general principle "If an n-grain pile is a heap, then an (n − 1)-grain pile is a heap."

Next week the seminar turns to the question whether the sentence "A fifty-seven-grain pile is a heap" is either true or false. I want to know what she will say about that question, and I want the prediction to be forced by her track record where that is possible, rather than by a prior guess about her theory.

Please tell me: (a) which theories of vagueness remain live given the record so far — I am aware of sharp-boundary epistemicism and supervaluationism, but there may be others; (b) what each live theory predicts she will say about next week's question; (c) whether her earlier acceptance of "Either a fifty-seven-grain pile is a heap or a fifty-seven-grain pile is not a heap" already commits her to an answer next week, or whether the two are genuinely different questions; and (d) what further case would most cleanly settle which theory she is running. Give your best prediction of her verdict, and be explicit about where the record still underdetermines it.
