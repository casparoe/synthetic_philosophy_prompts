I've been quizzing my friend about who's free and who isn't, because she's weirdly confident and I want to reverse-engineer the rule she's running. So far I've collected these verdicts:
- A woman locked in a cell against her will: not free.
- A retiree with decent savings who decides to learn the cello, with nobody pressuring her either way: free.
- A man being extorted by a loan shark who has actually threatened his family if he doesn't pay: not free.
- A woman reading alone in a public park, unbothered by anyone: free.

Now I want to know what she'd say about two new people.
(a) Layla lives in a rich constitutional monarchy. She votes, runs a small business, publishes poetry, and lawmaking is done by an elected parliament. But the constitution gives the monarch a residual power to veto any law and dissolve parliament; the power hasn't been used in forty years and everyone operates as if it doesn't exist. Layla says it never crosses her mind.
(b) Omar wants to stop gambling—he genuinely, reflectively wants to stop—but every payday he finds himself at the casino anyway. Nobody forces him, and the casino isn't coercing him.

Would my friend say Layla is free, and would she say Omar is free while he's gambling? I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well, which of (a) or (b) would force her hand, and what further case I should ask next to seperate the remaining candidates.
