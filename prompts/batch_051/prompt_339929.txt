I've been going back and forth on this for weeks. On one side I've been genuinely persuaded by the causal discovery literature: suppose the data you observe are generated by some causal graph, and suppose you add a minimality assumption (no spare edges beyond what the independencies require) and a faithfulness assumption (the independencies in the data are exactly the ones entailed by the graph, not products of lucky cancellations). Then the class of minimal Bayes nets that fit the data will, suprisingly often, track the true causal structure. That strikes me as a beautiful result — it looks like a tidy, mechanical route from correlation to causation, and it explains how we manage to learn causes from passive observation at all. But on the other side I have a firm, almost bodily intuition that no amount of passive data can hand you causal direction. The old saw that correlation does not imply causation feels like a fact about underdetermination, not just a warning about small samples: the same pattern of associations can be read as A causing B, B causing A, or a common cause C, and it seems like no amount of simplicity or sparsity should be allowed to break that tie from the data alone. I keep hearing people say minimality plus faithfulness are just extra assumptions, but that doesn't settle it for me — every method uses assumptions, so the real question is whether these ones are doing the causal work while pretending to be merely statistical.

What I actually want is a negotiated verdict. Give me the strongest version of both sides: what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a reading of 'tracks causation' on which both survive. In particular, does minimality plus faithfulness smuggle causal information in under the guise of simplicity, or does the intuition simply refuse to count structural assumptions as assumptions? I don't want to be told which side wins; I want to know what each move costs and which yield I should give up.
