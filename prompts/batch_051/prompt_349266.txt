I keep coming back to Turing's treatment of the ESP objection in Computing Machinery and Intelligence. His move, as I remember it, is that if the evidence for telepathy got overwhelming, the imitation game would be unfair because the interrogator might be receiving answers from a human via telepathy, so you'd need something like a telepathy-proof room. My view is that he was right to treat ESP as a real potential confound for the test rather than as nonsense to dismiss, and that the telepathy-proof room fix actually handles the problem. What would count against that view? I want the arguments and observations that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, including whether a telepathy-proof room genuinely saves the test or just relocates the problem. Does taking ESP seriously as an empirical confound commit Turing to treating every weird non-cognitive signal that way, and does that make the view unfalsifiable? If it does, is that a problem here?
