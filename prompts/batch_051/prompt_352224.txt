One: I'm a value subjectivist in a broad sense; good and bad are always relative to some valuer's actual cares, desires or values, and there's no valuer-independent fact about whether one set of fundamental values is better than another. Two: I still think value drift is one of the things that most worries me about the long-run future, and I think we should actively try to prevent it. I don't just mean ordinary moral change, I mean descendants or AI systems that end up wanting things we'd regard as empty or monstrous, like turning everything into paperclips or plugging into permanent wireheading. Three: I also don't want to say we should lock in today's values forever. I think there has been genuine moral progress, and future people could turn out better than us, maybe a lot better. It would be a terrible outcome even for the people in it, even if they wholeheartedly endorse it. Five: I'm an instrumentalist about rationality: if you have fundamental values, it can be rational to do whatever best serves them, and there is no rational requirement to have better or worse fundamental values.
