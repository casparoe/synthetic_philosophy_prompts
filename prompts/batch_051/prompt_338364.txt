A platform regulator proposes a default rule for attention-harvesting design features such as autoplay, infinite scrolling, algorithmic feeds and push notifications. The feature must be switched off by default; a user may be prompted to switch it on at most once per calendar year; and the prompt must present acceptance and refusal with equal prominence, framing and position on the screen. The rule is deliberately silent on what counts as a single feature and on how usable the off-by-default experience must be. Walk through how platforms would adapt: which new features they would develop, how the boundaries of 'feature' and 'attention-harvesting' would be contested, how the annual prompt would be timed and targeted, and what would happen to the off states. Are the predictable failure modes fixable by tightening the same rule, or would fixing them require a different sort of intervention?
