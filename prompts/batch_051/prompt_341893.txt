After a career in the physical sciences, I have only lately begun to read philosophy, and I find myself puzzled by a practice in the social sciences that no one around me will call out. Economists will insist, as a matter of method, that only individuals choose, and that talk of the economy is shorthand for millions of separate transactions. Yet the same profession publishes gross domestic product, the output gap, and consumer confidence; it speaks of an economy overheating, recovering, or losing confidence; and a central bank will move interest rates because output is above potential and inflation is above target. I would like the implicit social ontology of that practice made explicit. Does steering by such aggregates presuppose that social wholes exist and possess causal powers of their own, or can a strict methodological individualist run the same models and the same committee meetings without inconsistency? Where does the practice stop being shorthand and start reifying the aggregate, and what would have to change—in the language, the models, or the acts of policy—if the rival of whichever view the practice presupposes turned out to be the right one? Please distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is philosophically underdetermined.
