Here are the criteria I was taught for counting something in a gauge theory as physically real: locality—it should be assigned to a point or small region of spacetime; gauge invariance—it should be unchanged by the theory's gauge transformations; and empirical adequacy—it should be sufficient, given the dynamics, to account for the measurable effects. Now consider the Aharonov-Bohm setup. A long solenoid is shielded so that its magnetic field is confined to its interior; outside the solenoid the magnetic field is exactly zero, but the vector potential is nonzero. A coherent electron beam is split, passes around the two sides of the solenoid through this field-free region, and is recombined. The interference pattern shifts as the magnetic flux inside the solenoid changes, even though the electrons never enter the region where the field is nonzero. The shift is set by the loop integral of the vector potential around a closed path, which equals the enclosed flux and is gauge-invariant. So three candidate quantities each fail exactly one criterion: the magnetic field is local and gauge-invariant but empirically inadequate here; the vector potential is local and empirically adequate but not gauge-invariant; and the loop integral is gauge-invariant and empirically adequate but nonlocal. Which of the three, if any, should count as the physically real quantity in this case? Work through the criteria and say whether the case shows that one of them should be weakened or replaced.
