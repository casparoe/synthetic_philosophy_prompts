Every year in my philosophy of physics class, students walk in with the same analogy: entropy is disorder, a tidy room gets messier over time and never spontaneous tidies itself, and that alone is supposed to explain why the past is different from the future. I've started to think the analogy is doing most of the work, but I want it audited rather than just dismissed. Map it explicitly: what is the room in the thermodynamic story, what counts as the mess, what is the tidying, and what is the person who could come in and clean it? Then tell me where the mapping breaks, and which of those cracks actually matter for the advertised conclusion. And commit: does the claim that the thermodynamic arrow just is the increase of disorder survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument all along? If it can't carry it, say what would.
