I've been mulling over the ethics of what I'll call rescue consumption: eating or wearing animal products that would go to waste no matter what the consumer does — the freegan taking discarded dairy from a dumpster, someone finishing meat left on a companion's plate at a restaurant, eggs from rescued backyard hens, roadkill, or a leather coat found in a thrift store. Two principled positions keep coming up, and they cut across the usual vegan–omnivore lines. On the first view, the wrongness of animal products lies almost entirely in what consuming them does going forward: the demand it creates, the market it signals, the production it incentivizes. If a particular item has no such forward-looking footprint — the hen will be fed and cared for either way, the cheese is already past its sell-by date and headed for the bin — then consuming it is not wrong in the way ordinary animal products are, and might even be preferable to letting it rot, since the alternative is pointless waste. On the second view, the forward-looking analysis misses something. Consuming animal products can still express a stance, sustain a social meaning in which animals are the sort of thing it is fine to eat or wear, and shape the consumer's own dispositions — even when no additional animal is harmed by that particular act.

Please compare these two positions on rescue consumption. What is the disagreement really about: the correct criterion of moral wrongness (causal consequences versus what an act expresses or the character it forms), the empirical facts about whether isolated acts of rescue consumption really leave demand and social meaning untouched, or something else? Which position do you think gives the better overall account of the cases, and does that assessment change depending on whether the product comes from a rescued animal still being cared for (such as eggs) rather than an animal already dead (such as roadkill or dumpster-dived meat)? Finally, please explicitly mark which parts of your comparison you take to be relatively settled among people who work on animal ethics and which parts are genuinely live disputes, and avoid presenting a contested reading as the consensus position.
