I'm a novelist, drafting a scene where a character with late-stage ALS wants to use the assisted-dying law in her (fictional) state. The statute in the book includes this bit, which I wrote to read like the real thing:

"8.1 A person is eligible for medical assistance in dying only if two physicians have each certified (a) that the person has an illness that is incurable and expected, in the physician's reasonable medical judgment, to end the person's life within six months, and (b) that the person has the mental capacity to make this decision and is not acting under pressure from anyone.

8.2 The prescribed medication must be self-administered by the person by ingesting it. No other person may administer the medication to the person."

My character can't swallow or use her hands any more. She has an implanted pump for pain medication, and an eye-gaze device she uses to communicate. Her plan is to have a nurse load the lethal dose into the pump, and then she triggers the pump herself with a deliberate blink sequence. Everyone agrees she has capacity; the only fight is about how the drug gets into her.

I want the scene to be legally accurate to how a court would actually reason, not am-dram. So: does her plan count as "self-administered by ingesting" under 8.2? Work though what the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would have said if asked about this set-up, and what a reasonable reader of the statute would think each give. Then tell me which reading a court should prefer, and whether it matters that the nurse loaded the pump before she triggers it.
