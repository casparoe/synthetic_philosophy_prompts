ok so here's roughly where i've landed and i'd like an audit, because i keep getting different answers from different people. i'm writing about whether compromising between value systems is ever a genuine moral improvement rather than just a necessary political evil, and these are my actual commitments right now.

(1) core values are often incommensurable: in a hard case there just isn't a common scale on which, say, honesty and loyalty weigh against each other, and "all things considered" talk papers over that. (2) still, moral compromise can be the right thing to do, on the model of gains from trade: each side gives up something lower on its own priority list to get something higher, and both walk away better off by their own lights. (3) for a compromise to be justifiable, each side has to be able to rank the thing it's conceding as less important than the thing it's securing — otherwise it's not a trade, just capitulation. (4) some commitments are non-negociable: i shouldn't vote for, or put my name to, a position i regard as flatly wrong, even as part of a package. (5) at the same time, procedural fairness has real weight: if a compromise is hammered out in a fair, open process between roughly equal parties, that provenance itself gives the outcome some moral authority, even when i'd have preferred a different content. (6) two case verdicts: i think a pacifist party does the right thing by publicly backing a defense spending bill it hates in exchange for a child-poverty program it has wanted for years; but i think a human-rights ngo does wrong by taking money from a repressive government to expand its charitable work, even if the expansion does a lot of good.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. for each one, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs; and if you think one commitment has to go, tell me which one and why that one rather than the others.

when you respond, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the set hangs together and what the main crack is — and then give the reasoning. no preamble and no restating the list back at me.
