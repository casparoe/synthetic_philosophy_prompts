Take the rise of Bitcoin and analyze it through Aristotle's account of money and exchange.

For the theory, work from the following summary rather than from memory of the texts. Aristotle treats money (nomisma) as existing by convention rather than by nature, introduced so that households could exchange the different goods they need. Its proper role is as a measure and a medium that makes unlike goods commensurable. He distinguishes the natural art of acquiring what a household needs for a good life, an art with a natural limit, from another kind of wealth-getting, chrematistics in the strict sense, which treats money not as a means but as the end of exchange and accumulates without limit. The starkest form of the latter is lending at interest, because it makes the coin itself productive even though the point of coined money is to be exchanged, not to generate more of itself. Along the way Aristotle observes that an object can be used in two ways, for its own use or for exchange, and that money is the limiting case in which value is wholly a matter of the convention that sustains it.

For the episode, take the following as stipulated and reason on that basis rather than on independent research. Bitcoin was launched pseudonymously soon after the 2008 financial crisis as a decentralized digital currency: no central issuer, an eventual fixed supply, and a stated aim of peer-to-peer electronic cash. In practice its use as an ordinary medium of exchange has stayed limited, while it is mostly held as a speculative asset or hedge, often described as digital gold. Its price is extremely volatile, its operation depends on a mining network with substantial energy use, and its rules are sustained by code and miners rather than by a political community in the ordinary sense. I know the technical side; I want the philosophical reading.

Structure the analysis in three parts.

First, map the episode onto the theory, making your analogies explicit. Who are the actors in Aristotle's scheme, what plays the role of the household or political community, what is the convention that constitutes this money, what needs are the goods supposed to meet, and which activities count as natural acquisition as against chrematistics? Say whether Bitcoin counts as money for Aristotle, on which criterion, and what the theory would prescribe for the actors rather than merely describe about them.

Second, run the analysis in both directions. Say what the framework genuinely illuminates: the purely conventional character of Bitcoin's value, its detachment from any immediate use, the speculative logic, the sense of an activity with no internal stopping point. Then say where the framework misdescribes or goes silent, and do not force the case to fit. Consider at least: decentralization with no political authority, digital scarcity replacing natural limits, consensus sustained by code rather than custom, the possibility that holding is itself a use, and any major feature of the episode on which you think Aristotle's scheme simply has no purchase.

Third, say what the episode does to the theory. Does Bitcoin confirm the suspicion that money cut loose from use and from a community becomes self-feeding accumulation; does it refute the claim that money must be barren, since a purely conventional value can persist and grow; or does it reveal that Aristotle was answering a different question from the one Bitcoin raises? A mixed conclusion is acceptable if you argue for it.

End by saying how confident you are in the specific interpretive claims you make, and identify the single point you are least sure of.
