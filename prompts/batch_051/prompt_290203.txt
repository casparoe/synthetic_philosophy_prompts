Okay here's one. A country decides to take Szasz seriously and abolishes psychiatric diagnosis as a medical category. The health system only treats conditions with an established biological lesion or pathology—epilepsy, neurosyphilis, dementia, that sort of thing—as brain diseases. Everything else we used to call depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, addiction, or a personality disorder gets moved into a new non-medical system called Problems in Living. People can get state-funded talking therapy, housing support, job training, and mediation, but there are no insurance billing codes for psychiatric disorders anymore, no medical sick leave for depression, and no involuntary commitment on psychiatric grounds; if someone is dangerous, that's handled as a criminal matter. Walk me through how this actually plays out. Who gets help and who slips through? How does the fight over the boundry between brain disease and problem in living go, and who captures it? What do psychiatrists, therapists, insurers, drug companies, employers, and courts end up doing? How do people game the new system, and where does it break first? Can you patch the failure modes without quietly reintroducing the disease model?
