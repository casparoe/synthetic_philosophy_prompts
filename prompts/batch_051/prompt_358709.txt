In a seminar on the French Revolution, a classmate argued that the Terror was not a contingent response to foreign war, counterrevolution, and factional panic but was built into the structure of revolution itself, and he closed the discussion by invoking the old line that "revolutions devour their children." I realized afterward that I could not say what thesis that sentence actually asserts. Take the saying apart for me: identify the distinct precise claims it could be making — for instance, an empirical generalization about what happens to revolutionary leaders, a claim about the internal dynamics of radicalization, a class-struggle or ideological thesis about why a revolution must turn on its own architects, a cautionary or moral claim, or something else — and for each reading say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or something false or unfalsifiable. Finally, say which of these readings would actually support his conclusion that the Terror was structurally inevitable, as opposed to merely noting that revolutionaries often end up at odds with one another. I want a menu of readings, not one settled interpretation.
