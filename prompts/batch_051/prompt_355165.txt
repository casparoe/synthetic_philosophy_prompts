I want a probability estimate on a precise claim about time and truth. Define a future contingent as a proposition about a future event that is not now determined by the present state of the world together with the laws. The claim on which I want your credence: bivalence holds for future contingents — right now, the proposition that there will be a sea battle tomorrow is already determinately true or determinately false even though nothing about the present world settles it. Do not give a bare number. Decompose the question and assign a rough probability to each component, covering at least the following. (a) How much of the apparent problem comes from running together bivalence, determinism, and logical fatalism, and how much of the open-future intuition survives once they are separated. (b) Whether a proposition about the future needs a present truthmaker, and whether an actually-existing future branch — in a branching-time model, with or without a thin red line singling out the actual branch — can supply one. (c) How much turns on the semantics of will: universal over all future branches, relative to the actual branch, or taking no truth value when the branches divide on the outcome. (d) How much weight to place on retrospective talk — after the battle occurs we say it was true all along — rather than treating that as a revisionary or conventional way of speaking. Then show how the components combine into your overall credence, identify the single component to which your final number is most sensitive, and specify one argument or piece of evidence that would move it most.
