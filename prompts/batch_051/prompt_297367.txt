A popular science article I was reading explains the Rietdijk–Putnam argument about relativity and time as follows. Special relativity says there is no absolute simultaneity, so whether two events happen at the same time depends on the observer's frame. Pick some event in your future — say, you opening this article again tomorrow. For a suitably moving distant observer, that future event is simultaneous with an event happening on Earth right now. But the Earth event is real now. So the future event must be just as real now as the Earth event is. By repeating this, the whole future and the whole past turn out to already exist, and we get the four-dimensional block universe. And if the block universe is real, the article says, then everything you will ever do is already there in the block, so your feeling of deciding anything is an illusion and free will is out the window. The article adds that this is the mainstream view among physicists and philosophers, and that the only people who resist it are those who can't let go of commonsense time.
