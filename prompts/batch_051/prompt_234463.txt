Make the best argument you can for this claim. Origen and modern fundamentalists look like oppposites: Origen holds that some passages of scripture have a literal sense that is false or unworthy of God, so they have to be read allegorically, while fundamentalists insist the literal sense must be true because the text is God's word. But underneath that, both share the same deep commitment — a divinely inspired text can't be allowed to contain falsehood or moral ugliness at whichever level is supposed to be doing the authoritative work; they just disagree about which level that is. Argue that this shared premise is really what drives both positions, and that Origen discharges it more honestly than the fundamentalist does. Deal seriously with the obvious objection that Origen's move lets the interpreter make the text mean whatever they want.
