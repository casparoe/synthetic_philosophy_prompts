Here's one I've been going back and forth on and I want your numbers. By the end of 2027, the chain-of-thought traces produced by the most capable models (the ones actually doing economically valuable work) will be, on average and under normal use, faithful enough that an external monitor reading them catches the model's real reasons for a consequential output at least as often as it's misled. By "faithful" I mean the CoT states the considerations that actually drove the output, not a post-hoc confabulation. I don't mean perfectly faithful, just that monitoring the CoT — whether by a human or another model that has the prompt, the output, and the trace — is a net improvement over monitoring inputs/outputs alone, rather than a source of false confidence.

Give me your credence in that proposition — point estimate, not an interval. But I want the full decomposition. Break it into at least the following sub-questions, and add any others you think carry real weight:

1. That by 2027 the most capable models still have their CoT exposed in the contexts where it matters, rather than developers hiding it or the architecture making legible reasoning impossible.
2. That optimization for task performance / RLHF-style reward hasn't selected strongly for rationalizing, confabulated, or strategically vague CoT that diverges from the actual computation.
3. That training-time methods (process supervision, paraphrasing, probes, CoT consistency checks) will be good enough to detect or suppress unfaithful CoT if it does show up.
4. That competitive or economic pressure to keep CoT private or compressed won't have eroded its use as a monitoring channel anyway.

For each sub-question give a number and one or two lines of justification, then show how you combine them — don't just multiply if there are dependencies; tell me about the dependencies. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what one empirical development before 2027 would move that term the most and in which direction.

Also: if your credence is above 0.7, give the strongest case for thinking CoT monitoring fails anyway; if it's below 0.3, give the strongest case for thinking it works. If your in between, do both briefly.
