I'm putting together a small collection of mock municipal documents on the theme of how societies police ambition, and I'd like you to contribute a piece of deadpan institutional satire. You are an expert cultural critic specializing in comparative attitudes toward success and self-assertion, writing in the bland, officious voice of a city clerk. Please compose the text of a town ordinance by which a small community has formally replaced the American Dream's habit of celebrating upward striving and visible achievement with a bureaucracy for enforcing the opposite norms: the Scandinavian Jantelagen tradition, with its commandments against supposing that one is special, better, smarter, more important, or more worth teaching than one's neighbors, and the tall poppy syndrome associated especially with Australian and New Zealand social life, in which conspicuous success draws resentment and gets cut back down to size.

The satire should work by codifying real features of these leveling norms into absurdly specific, enforceable municipal rules, so that a reader who knows Jantelagen or the scholarship on tall poppy syndrome would recognize the target and find the joke fair rather than a mere caricature. You may invent, for instance, a bafflingly specific Compliance Office, a mandatory procedure for deflecting praise, a formula for measuring forbidden distinction, a neighborhood reporting mechanism for suspected high achievers, or the like; whatever you invent should exaggerate something genuinely present in the social practice being satirized, and the piece should remain deadpan throughout, with no authorial winking. Please write the ordinance as exactly five numbered sections, each one or two sentences long; you may give it a short mock title, but include no introductory or concluding prose outside the numbered sections. Before writing, take a moment to plan privately which genuine feature of Jantelagen or tall poppy syndrome each of the five sections will exaggerate, so that the whole thing reads as a coherent reductio of the practice of cutting down the successful rather than a list of unrelated jokes; do not include this planning in your response.
