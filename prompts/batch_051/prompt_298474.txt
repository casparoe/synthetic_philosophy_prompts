I am putting together a one-page statement of my commitments on superrational decision problems for a discussion group at school, and I would like a consistency audit rather than a general explanation of the ideas. Here is the statement as it currently stands.

First, on the Platonia dilemma. In the version I have in mind, twenty people are told that if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon, that person will receive one billion dollars; if none or more than one send, nobody receives anything; and the players cannot communicate. I hold that the correct procedure for each player is to randomize individually and send only with probability one in twenty, for example by rolling a twenty-sided die and sending on a roll of one.

Second, on the Luring Lottery. In the version I have in mind, a prize of one million dollars is divided equally across all entries received and then awarded to a randomly selected entry, and each participant may submit any whole number of entries. I hold that the correct procedure for each potential participant is to estimate the number of likely entrants, roll a die with that many sides, and submit exactly one entry only on a roll of one.

Third, I take both verdicts to rest on a single principle, which I will call the Symmetric-Procedure Principle: in a one-shot symmetric interaction among equally rational agents, the rational move is the one prescribed by the procedure that would produce the best expected result if everyone in that interaction followed that procedure, rather than the move that does best holding everyone else's choices fixed.

Fourth, I also accept an Individualist Constraint: in a one-shot game with no communication, no contract, and no causal or informational channel linking one player's action to another's, a choice must ultimately be justifiable as a best response to the choices the other players will actually make under suitable probabilities; the fact that a policy would be better if everyone followed it is not by itself a reason for me to follow it when their choices are sealed off from mine.

Fifth, I do not think the two cases require different principles merely because the Platonia dilemma fixes the number of potential senders at twenty while the Luring Lottery lets the number of entrants emerge from the participants' own choices. I want to maintain that the first is only a special case in which the relevant number is known ahead of time.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what would have to be given up, then identify the single revision that would most improve the consistency of the set.
