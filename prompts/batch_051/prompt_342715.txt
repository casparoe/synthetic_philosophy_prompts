I keep coming back to this because its exactly the problem I face when I plot a novel, though nobody in my writer's group would call it decision theory. When I'm choosing between two endings — or two versions of a character — I genuinely don't know which will work: which reader I'll be, which one will feel earned a year from now. And the two attractive rules I've got for deciding under that kind of ignorance seem to split on almost every interesting case.

On one side I get maximin. If I really don't know where I'll land, pick the version whose worst case is least bad — the safe ending that can't embarrass me, the character who never rings false. There's something clean about it, and I understand why Rawls would build a whole view of justice on it: behind the veil, make the worst slot as good as possible. On the other side is the feeling I can't shake that choosing by the floor is wrong for the choices I actually care about. When I weigh the safe ending against the structurally risky one, what moves me isn't the worst case, it's the thought of looking back and realizing the safe ending cost me the best thing I would ever have written. Minimax regret says take the risk; maximin says take the safe version. Harsanyi's way of thinking — under ignorance you average across the possibilities rather than fixate on the worst one — also sounds right to me in the abstract.

So here's the thing. The rules come apart exactly where the decision is real. Imagine a rewrite where the safe version guarantees the novel stays respectable but moves nobody, and the risk has a worst case of a pretentious mess reviewers will shred, but a best case of something people argue about for years. I want to know what it costs to give up the maximin theory, and what it costs to give up the regret intuition, and whether there's a reading of 'ignorance' (or of what a decision even is in that rewrite) where both get to be right for different questions. Dont hand me a side; I want the negotiated verdict, including what would actually justify dropping one and not the other.
