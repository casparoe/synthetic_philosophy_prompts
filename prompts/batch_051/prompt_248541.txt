Write the joint statement that a committed advocate of the knowledge norm of assertion—one may assert p only if one knows p—and a committed advocate of the truth norm of assertion—one may assert p only if p is true—could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Open with a numbered list of agreed claims covering: the basic function assertion serves in transmitting information and coordinating joint inquiry; the candidate norms both sides reject, including a bare belief norm and a reasonable-belief norm, with the shared reasons for rejecting each; and the descriptive data any adequate norm must account for, such as the propriety of ordinary flat-out assertion, the retraction pressure created when an assertion is challenged, the impropriety of Moore-paradoxical assertions of the form p, but I do not believe that p, and the difference between flat-out and hedged assertion. Then include a disagreements table whose rows give, for each genuine disagreement: the point stated once in each side's own preferred wording, the crux sub-question that would settle it, a concrete probe both sides agree bears on the crux, and each side's prediction about that probe, recorded in advance. Suitable probes include lottery propositions such as your ticket lost, true assertions that fall short of knowledge in Gettier-style cases, selfless assertions made without belief while following a commission, and the way each side handles challenges and retractions. The final document should be one both parties would sign, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than papered over.
