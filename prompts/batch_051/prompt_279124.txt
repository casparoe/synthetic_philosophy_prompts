Give me your actual credence that honeybees have at least minimal phenomenal consciousness: that there is something it is like to be a honeybee, and that some of these states are valenced — felt as aversive or attractive — rather than mere subpersonal control signals steering the bee. I want a number and the derivation, not an it-depends answer. Respond as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1: your headline credence as a single number. Items 2 through 4: the three sub-questions your number is most sensitive to, each with a rough probability and a one-sentence justification — these might include whether a valence-tagged, globally available form of integration can run on a brain of roughly a million neurons without a cortex; whether bees' flexible cross-modal learning and reward trade-offs are better explained by felt valence or by reinforced steering; and whether this question has a determinate fact of the matter. Item 5: the sub-question you are least confident about, and the one piece of evidence or argument that would move it most.
