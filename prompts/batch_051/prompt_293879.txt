I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am working on a long essay about American pragmatism for readers who are scientifically literate but not academic philosophers. I want a section that does real analytical work rather than explanation: a systematic translation of the dispute between C. S. Peirce and William James about what it means for a belief to be true into the vocabulary of statistical estimation and predictive modelling. The target vocabulary should include parameters, estimators, consistency, convergence to a limit, bias and variance, model specification, model selection, generalisation, and out-of-sample prediction error. Treat Peirce's position that truth is the opinion inquiry would settle upon if pursued far enough, and James's position that truth is made in the course of experience and is to be judged by the practical work a belief does, as claims to be rendered in those terms as faithfully as possible. I am fluent enough in this vocabulary to follow a careful technical argument, but I am not a working statistician, so you may use the standard machinery without belabouring it, but do not rely on measure-theoretic formalism.

After the translation, give me a residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in the statistical vocabulary at all? For each item of residue, tell me whether the failure is a limitation of the target vocabulary, a place where the pragmatist claim was less precise than it looked, or some of each. In particular, test whether Peirce's convergence of inquiry really does map onto the consistency of an estimator, and whether James's appeal to what works really does map onto predictive utility, and show where those analogies strain. Do not smuggle statistical assumptions into the translation and present them as pragmatism: the assumption of a fixed data-generating process, a well-specified model, or a unique true parameter should be treated as a translation choice that must be reported, not a neutral background.

Please write the response in British English, with the formality of a serious magazine essay.
