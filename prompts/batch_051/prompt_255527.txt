You are a professional philosopher and a fiction writer. Think carefully before you write. The story I am asking for should dramatize a live problem in AI alignment: when a single AI system must serve a very diverse population, whose values should its behavior reflect?

Start by brainstorming at least three different answers to that question. For example, you might consider whether the system should aggregate everyone’s preferences into some overall social welfare function, whether it should remain strictly neutral among the many reasonable but incompatible conceptions of the good held by its users, whether it should defer to local majorities whenever they conflict with national averages, or whether it should instead refuse to act in cases of deep value conflict. Think about what each answer would mean for a specific human being in a specific situation.

Then write a short story, roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words, set in the near future. The story should make the philosophical problem concrete rather than abstract. It should show the human consequences of one design choice, ideally through ordinary people — a family, a school board, a patient, a religious community, a teenager, a local official — who do not talk like philosophers but whose lives expose the stakes. Do not focus on technical details of machine learning. Do not let your characters explain the philosophy in academic terms.

The story should leave the reader genuinely uncertain about whether the AI’s design choice was right. End with an ambiguous moment rather than a resolution. The story should not argue for a single solution and should not name philosophers or cite works. It should make the reader feel why the problem of whose values an AI should serve is hard, not solve it.
