So I'm building a language and its writing system for an epic fantasy novel, heavy on worldbuilding. Early on I gave one culture a tidy alphabet: one symbol per sound, nothing more. But as the language developed in my notes, I started doing things my writing partner calls sloppy and implausible. The sign for 'temple' stays identical for centuries even after the spoken word shifts, and the sign for 'river' gets reused inside the word for 'trade,' because in the culture's history trade followed the river. He says pick a lane: either the script reports how words sound, or it's a mess the reader can't make sense of. But real writing systems seem to do this kind of thing all the time. What is a script actually doing when it stops just transcribing sounds? Is there a principled way to decide which parts of the language the symbols should track, so it comes out feeling like a coherent system rather than me being lazy?
