Homework question: Reconstruct the Epicurean argument that death is not bad for the person who dies. Then give the strongest objection you can think of and explain whether it defeats the argument.

Student answer: Epicurus's point is that death is nothing to us, because while we exist death isn't there, and once death is there we no longer exist. So a person and their own death are never in the same place at the same time. There's no subject left to undergo anything, so death can't be bad for the one who dies. But the strongest objection is the deprivation account: death is bad because it cuts off all the goods you otherwise would have kept on enjoying. And you don't need to consciously experience a loss for it to be real, so the fact that there's no one around to feel it doesn't answer this. Since it's the death itself that causes the loss of those goods, the harm of the deprivation must be located at the exact instant of death; that's when the deprivation happens, and that's why being dead itself is what's bad for you. So the objection completely defeats Epicurus.
