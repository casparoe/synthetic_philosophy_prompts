Suppose that, beginning next year, a cheap, portable instrument becomes widely available that can date any object of human manufacture—stone, metal, ceramic, glass, textile, ink, or paper—to within five years over the past fifty millennia, with negligible error, and whose results cannot be forged or gamed. Nothing else changes. Give your best-reasoned prediction of how the following twenty years differ for three bodies of thought: the Atlantis tradition, the ancient-astronaut literature, and the New Chronology associated with Fomenko, which holds that the conventional chronology of ancient and medieval history is largely artificial and was manufactured much later than is standardly believed. I am less interested in whether each is discredited than in the mechanisms at work. Trace, for each program, which characteristic claims the new technique directly exposes and which remain insulated because they concern the interpretation of texts or the motives of the scholarly consensus; how its proponents re-describe the data under pressure; what happens to their audiences and institutional footing; and whether the boundary-work separating history from its would-be rivals shifts once this evidential asymmetry collapses. Make explicit how confident you are in each link of your argument, and state what would have to be true about the social function these programs serve for your overall prediction to fail.
