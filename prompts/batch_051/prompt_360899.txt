I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've noticed an asymmetry in how I take in information from other people, and I don't quite have a word for it. Two instances from the past month.

One: a seismologist I trust walked me through why our local fault is probably due for a big earthquake in the next couple of decades. I didn't redo the modeling or read the paleoseismology myself; I just believed her, and now I'll repeat the conclusion in my own voice without a second thought. That feels completely fine.

Two: a senior editor I respect argued to me that, in a piece I'm working on, it would be wrong to use quotes from a source who was misled about how those quotes would be used, even though legally we'd be in the clear. I'm maybe 85% sure that if I worked through it all, I'd land where she landed — she's thought about journalistic ethics longer then I have. But I didn't adopt her conclusion. I slept on it, went back and forth, and only felt okay filing the piece once I'd re-derived the position for myself. If I had just gone with her judgment, it would have felt like I hadn't done my job, or like the view wasn't really mine.

So here's what I'm noticing. In factual domains, deferring to someone better-positioned feels normal, and I'll happily state the borrowed conclusion as my own. In moral matters, deferring to someone I judge to be more reliable feels like a failure — a little like cheating — and I won't assert the view until I've worked through it myself. I don't think this is just about confidence: in the second case I'd have bet she was right. And it's not that I distrust her judgment. It feels like a different requirement kicks in.

Is there an established name for this asymmetry, or for the specific idea that a moral view acquired on someone else's say-so is second-rate? I know there's a whole literature on intellectual autonomy and on not outsourcing your judgment, but I think I'm pointing at something narrower: the thought that moral beliefs specifically have to be generated rather than inherited, in a way factual beliefs don't. I'm not just after a label, though. I want to know what the phenomenon actually is, whether it's one thing or two (say, a felt requirement plus a norm about what I can assert as my own), and where its edges are. If I'm misdescribing it, or if the asymmetry doesn't survive a closer look, I'd rather hear that than get a tidy term.
