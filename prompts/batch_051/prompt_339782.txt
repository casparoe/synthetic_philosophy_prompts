A reading group I'm in has spent a year split over whether companies can be blamed, and they've finally agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration statement instead of talking past each other. One camp — call them the group-agent camp — holds that a company can be a genuine moral agent: it has beliefs, intentions and judgements of its own, which aren't reducible to the summed attitudes of the people inside it, and it can therefore be a proper target of blame over and above blaming any employee or director. The other camp — the reductivist camp — holds that saying 'the company is to blame' is always a compact way of talking about the blame of individual people plus holding a legal fiction liable; there's no actual thing, the company, that could itself be morally blamed. Both camps are suspicious of merely verbal peace treaties, so they want the statement to be as sharp as possible.

Your job is to write the statement they could both sign. It should have four parts.

(i) A numbered list of agreed claims. These shouldn't be vacuous: they should say something real about how decisions get made inside organisations, about what blame does generally, about cases where no individual seems culpable even though the organisation went badly wrong, and about the forward-looking versus backward-looking work blame does.

(ii) A disagreements table. Each row should state one live disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, and for each row you should flag whether it's a substantive disagreement or a merely verbal one (and if it's verbal, say what the two sides are really saying).

(iii) A cruxes section. For each crux — a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp — give a concrete case or test both camps agree bears on it, and record what each camp predicts or judges about that case. At least one crux should involve majority votes on each premise producing a conclusion that no member individually voted for. At least one should involve a serious harm that occured even though each person did their own job competently and nobody saw the whole picture. At least one should involve blame persisting after everyone involved has left the firm.

(iv) A very short section where each side states, in their own words, the single consideration or result that would most plausibly make them abandon their position.

Don't include any bibliography, reading list, or citations; this should read as the statement itself, not as a literature survey. And don't water it down so much that both camps could sign without giving anything up — it's fine if some rows still say 'substantive disagreement'.
