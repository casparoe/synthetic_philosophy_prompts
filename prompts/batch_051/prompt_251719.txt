A friend who's been reading about sacrifice sent me this and I can't shake it. I'll lay it out the way he did, numbered.

(1) Scapegoat rites aren't some one-off. Cultures as far apart as ancient Israel and rural Greece, and plenty of others, run the same basic program: pile the community's guilt on a goat, an outsider, a doll, wahtever, then drive it out or kill it, and the community walks away clean.

(2) When a practice shows up again and again in places that didn't copy each other, and the people doing it report that it works (they feel clean, the feuds stop, the group coheres), you can't just write it off as pure confusion. Something real gets moved around.

(3) What gets moved, on the participants' own description, is guilt. The priest confesses the wrongs over the animal and the animal carries them away. Guilt behaves like a load that shifts from the guilty party to a stand-in, and removing the stand-in removes the load.

(4) But if guilt can move like that, then there's no deep difference between the rite and the obvious abuses: a criminal paying someone to take his sentence, a village framing an outsider so everyone else can feel cleared, a family piling its blame on one scapegoated kid. Those all become applications of the same mechanism, not corruptions of it.

(5) Yet we don't treat the individual cases that way. We say the stand-in's suffering does not settle the wrongdoer's guilt, no matter how much better everyone feels.

(6) So either the rite doesn't actually move guilt (and trusting the reports in (2) was a mistake), or it does (and our refusal in (5) is just squeamishness about a mechanism we still use whenever we need a scapegoat).

(7) He says the real conclusion is stronger: if the rite works at all, it works by moving something other than moral guilt — which means the participants are systematically wrong about their own action, while our horror at scapegoating is just a feeling dressed up as a principle.

I'd rather keep both the idea that the rite does something real and the idea that punishing an innocent person never actually clears the guilty one. He says I can't. Where does the chain go wrong? Name the step, or the shift between steps, tell me why it looked fine and what it costs to give it up.
