I am a PhD candidate in historical sociology, working on counterfactual explanation in the study of interwar political radicalization. A colleague has been defending a structuralist position in the Great Man debate, and below is the cleanest reconstruction of her argument that I have produced. It seems to me too strong, but I cannot reliably locate the mistake on my own. Could you assess it formally: please determine whether the argument goes through; if it does not, identify the exact step or transition that fails, on which reading of that step, and state the revision that would repair it. If you think the argument does go through, say which premise is doing the most work and what historical or theoretical evidence would most directly test it.

The argument:

(1) An outcome is overdetermined by standing conditions when, holding those conditions fixed, the failure of any single individual to exist or to act would not have prevented that outcome, or another of the same kind, from occurring.

(2) In Germany in the early 1930s, holding fixed the standing conditions — military defeat, the Versailles settlement, inflation, the Depression, mass unemployment, weak democratic institutions, and a mass antisemitic far right — a far-right takeover of the state was the single most probable political outcome.

(3) If an outcome is the single most probable political outcome under the standing conditions, then those conditions overdetermine its occurrence in the sense of (1).

(4) A far-right takeover of Germany under those conditions would have been an outcome of the same kind as the Nazi regime, since the features of the Nazi regime that matter for the comparison — dictatorship, antisemitic exclusion, revisionist foreign policy, and organized mass violence — were already part of the programmatic horizon of the German far right and were not private inventions of Hitler.

(5) Therefore, had Hitler never existed, Germany would still have produced a regime of the same kind as the Nazi regime; the difference Hitler made was one of timing and staging rather than of historical kind.

I am most suspicious of (3) and (4), but I cannot tell whether the decisive fault lies there or earlier, and I do not trust my own reaction that the conclusion is stronger than the premises permit.
