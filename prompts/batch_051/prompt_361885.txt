A colleague of mine works on Daniel Dennett's three stances — the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance — and always records, for any target system, which one of them she uses to predict it; she says the three stances track different kinds of real patterns in behavior. Her practice is illustrated by seven verdicts. She takes the physical stance toward a boulder rolling down a slope and toward a river's meanders. She takes the design stance toward a quartz wristwatch, a wall thermostat, and a sunflower tracking the sun. She takes the intentional stance toward a rat in a maze and toward a crow using a stick to coax grubs out of a log. She has never stated the principle behind these choices. If I asked her about a current large language model, which stance would she probably give it, and why? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the candidate rules that fit her seven verdicts equally well, what each candidate rule predicts for the model, and your best guess about her verdict, with the reasons for preferring that interpretation. Please also name one further case, not another language model, that would best separate the candidate rules.
