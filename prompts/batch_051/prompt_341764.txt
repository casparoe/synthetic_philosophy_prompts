As a novelist I keep returning to a structural comparison, and I want to know whether it is deep or merely decorative.

Schopenhauer treats compassion as the sole genuine moral motive. On his account it works only when the principium individuationis is momentarily seen through and I grasp the other's suffering as not ultimately distinct from my own; the boundary between the two subjects is dissolved. Nietzsche's worry about pity is that this apparent dissolution is either a sham or a pathology. Sometimes he describes the pitier as secretly experiencing his own superiority over the sufferer, so that pity preserves or even sharpens the hierarchy it pretends to erase. Sometimes he describes pity as a kind of infection, in which the pitier suffers along with the sufferer, so that the stock of suffering in the world is multiplied rather than relieved. On both readings, what Schopenhauer calls a selfless fusion is not the moral achievement he takes it to be.

I see the same two positions in a technical dispute about point of view. Free indirect discourse lets a third-person narrator slip inside a character's consciousness: the narrator's voice and the character's voice occupy the same sentence, so that the boundary between teller and told apparently dissolves. I have long defended the technique on roughly Schopenhauerian grounds, as the one mode of narration that stops lording over its characters. But a suspicion I cannot shake is the Nietzschean one: the narrator still selects, orders, frames, and profits aesthetically from the suffering on display, and the apparent merging may just be the narrator appropriating the character's interiority while keeping final control over how it is seen.

So the parallel runs: Schopenhauer's dissolution of self and other is to Nietzsche's critique of pity as free indirect discourse's dissolution of teller and told is to the suspicion that the narrator's power is never actually surrendered.

My question is whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. I would like a committed verdict, not a survey of possibilities. Say what the mapping would have to preserve for the analogy to be deep, what would have to be true of both sides for a result to transfer in either direction—for example, if Nietzsche is right about pity, what would that entail about free indirect discourse—and name at least one thing the parallel predicts that turns out false.
