I have been turning over a structural parallel and cannot tell whether it is deep or superficial. In the contemporary grounding literature, a fact is grounded in more fundamental facts that metaphysically explain it; a fact is fundamental when nothing grounds it; and many philosophers hold that chains of ground must terminate in fundamental facts and cannot close into circles. This looks formally identical to dependency resolution in a software build system: a package's build requires its declared dependencies, those dependencies require their own, and the graph must bottom out in packages with no dependencies, while circular dependencies are rejected as fatal. Is this a genuine identity of structure — both being, say, finite acyclic partial orders with terminal elements — or am I just pattern-matching? If the analogy is serious, does the fact that a circular dependency merely prevents a build from completing give any reason to accept the metaphysical ban on grounding circles, or are "cannot be built" and "cannot be the case" doing entirely different work? What would the mapping have to be like for results about dependency resolution to transfer to fundamentality, and where does it break?
