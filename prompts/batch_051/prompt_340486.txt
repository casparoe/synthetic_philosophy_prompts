Suppose I have two quantities that track each other almost perfectly across every condition I have been able to observe, and I suspect — but cannot prove — that the tracking comes from a hidden common driver rather than from one quantity genuinely producing the other. One account of causation I have been thinking through says that the way to settle the matter is to intervene: set one quantity to a value that nature does not produce on its own, hold the suspected driver as fixed as possible, and see whether the tracking survives. If it survives across many different such interventions, the account says, the relation deserves to be called causal; if it collapses under intervention, the relation was a mere regularity.

Here is my problem. To run this test at all, I need to know that the thing I am setting really is the alleged cause, that I have not altered the other quantity through some back channel, and that I have not simply constructed a new situation in which a spurious correlation happens to recur. But knowing those things already requires knowing which relations are causal — which is exactly what the account was supposed to tell me. I cannot tell whether this is a fatal circularity or just a practical difficulty that a well-designed test could get around.

Design the strongest test you can of the claim that invariance under intervention is what distinguishes causal relations from non-causal regularities. Work with my two quantities if that helps, or replace them with a clearer case. Be concrete about what you vary, what you hold fixed, what you measure, and how you produce values that ordinary circumstances would never generate. Tell me what result would count most strongly in favor of the claim and what result would count most clearly against it. Then say exactly what a positive result would establish and what it would leave undecided — in particular, whether it would dissolve the circularity worry or merely show that the invariance criterion is a useful practical indicator while the circularity remains a defect in the account. I would also like to know what the account should say about a regularity we cannot intervene on at all, such as something fixed in the past or a process at a scale we cannot manipulate: is it non-causal by the account's lights, or is it simply beyond what the account can speak to?
