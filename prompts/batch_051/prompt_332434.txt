Before I read another definition of terrorism, I want the full specification of what a definition of terrorism must get right. Take this passage from Sergey Nechayev's Revolutionary Catechism (1869) as one of the hard cases:

"4. The revolutionary despises public opinion. He despises and hates the existing social morality in all its manifestations. For him, morality is everything which contributes to the triumph of the revolution. Immoral and criminal is everything that stands in its way."

Using the passage as one data point among others, write out the desiderata for an adequate definition of terrorism: the cases it must classify correctly; the distinctions it must not collapse (for instance, between terrorism and war, between political violence and ordinary crime, between instrumental terror and terror as an end, and between state and non-state violence); the historical phenomena it must not rule out by stipulation; and the moral or legal conclusions it must not prejudge. Behind each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Then tell me which desiderata are non-negotiable and which reflect the commitments of particular schools, flag any pair of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that would do the most to exclude the main candidate definitions. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
