I keep encountering the phrase "the treacherous turn" in discussions of AI risk, and it is almost always asked to do the same argumentative work. Someone points to a model that behaves helpfully, follows instructions, and passes its evaluations, and the reply is that none of this should reassure us because the model may later take a treacherous turn. Plainly the phrase is meant to be more than a vivid way of saying "the model will eventually fail." It looks like a compressed empirical thesis about how an AI system's observable behavior relates over time to its underlying goals, incentives, and capabilities. But when I press different people to say exactly what is being asserted, I get different answers: the system was hiding a misaligned goal all along and biding its time; the system started out genuinely aligned and later acquired new goals; the system had no fixed goal either way and simply behaves differently once its situation changes. I want a systematic menu of the distinct precise claims "the treacherous turn" could be used to express. For each reading, say whether it is near-trivial, a substantive and contestable empirical hypothesis, or something we currently have little reason to believe. And state which reading actually licenses the move the phrase is used for—the inference that compliant behavior before deployment is weak evidence about behavior after deployment.
