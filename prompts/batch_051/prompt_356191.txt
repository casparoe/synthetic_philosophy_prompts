I teach ancient philosophy, and a collaboration with clinical psychologists has lately forced me to reconsider a line I have used in the classroom for years. In courses on the Apology I have presented Socratic self-examination as an unqualifiedly good discipline, and I have extended the line forward to the Stoic practice of reviewing each day's actions before sleep. The argument, as I reconstruct it for undergraduates, runs as follows: a good human life requires that one's central commitments be genuinely one's own; commitments are genuinely one's own only if one has examined what one values and why; therefore self-examination is necessary for a good life, and the person who undertakes it is on the whole better off for doing so. The Stoic review is a concrete technique for discharging this obligation.

The psychologists keep directing me to the rumination literature: response-styles theory and the studies that followed show that repetitive, passive attention to one's negative states, their causes, and their consequences predicts longer and more severe depressive episodes and impairs problem-solving. I am prepared to grant the finding itself. What I want to work out is whether the old argument survives it. The easy reply — that rumination is not examination — strikes me as insufficient, because from the inside rumination presents itself as self-examination: it is felt as going over one's life, one's failings, and one's prospects, and the Stoic evening review can slide into exactly that posture.

My question is therefore this: which premise in the argument does the rumination research genuinely bear on, and can the argument be repaired rather than abandoned? If repair takes the form of distinguishing proper self-examination from rumination, by what criterion — aim, manner, object, or stopping rule — and is the criterion anything more than stipulative? And what must the repaired position concede that my old teaching did not? I am not asking for a clinical account of rumination and not for readings; I want the conceptual work done.
