I am an undergraduate taking a philosophy of religion module this term, and I have been puzzling over an asymmetry in my own practice. If I believed that a human benefactor already knew every relevant fact about my situation, understood my good better than I do, and had decided on a course of action, I would regard persistently asking them to alter it as both pointless and impertinent: my requests would add no information, give them no new reason, and betray a lack of trust in their judgement. Yet in petitionary prayer I address a God characterised as omniscient, perfectly loving, and immutable, and I ask Him to bring about outcomes He has evidently so far declined to bring about. My tradition presents this as not merely permissible but admirable. What, if anything, justifies treating these two cases differently? Is there a feature of God or of prayer that breaks the analogy, or should petitionary prayer be seen as equally presumptuous, or reinterpreted as something other than a request to an all-knowing benefactor?
