Please rank the following six views about the intrinsic value of knowledge from most to least defensible, assigning each a score from 0 to 10 for how well it handles the trivial-truth objection. The trivial-truth objection says: if knowledge is worth having for its own sake, then even knowing the exact number of grains of sand on a small beach should be worth having for its own sake; yet we do not regard that as a fitting object of serious intellectual effort. In the opening of the Metaphysics, Aristotle suggests the materials for a reply: although all human beings by nature desire to know, the desire first shows itself in the pleasure we take in perception, then in memory, then experience, then craft, and finally in wisdom that seeks first causes and principles—so objects of knowledge seem to be ordered by a standard other than mere truth. The six views below propose such a standard.

For each view, give a one-sentence justification of its place in the ranking and a one-sentence explanation of its main weakness.

Views to rank:

1. **Perfectionist eudaimonism:** knowledge is intrinsically valuable insofar as it perfects our rational nature, and knowledge of higher causes or universals perfects us more than knowledge of isolated contingent facts.
2. **Understanding account:** only explanatory understanding is intrinsically valuable; bare factual knowledge is at most instrumentally valuable.
3. **Curiosity-satisfaction account:** a piece of knowledge is intrinsically valuable only relative to a subject's genuine inquiry, so a truth that no one is curious about lacks that value.
4. **Unification account:** a truth's intrinsic epistemic value is proportional to how much it unifies or illuminates a domain of thought, so trivial truths unify nothing and have negligible intrinsic value.
5. **Virtue-epistemology account:** knowledge is valuable as an exercise of intellectual virtues such as attentiveness, carefulness, and open-mindedness, so the value lies primarily in the agent's excellent activity rather than in the proposition alone.
6. **Value pluralism:** knowledge, understanding, and wisdom are distinct intrinsic epistemic goods, and no single standard explains all cases; trivial truths may have a small amount of intrinsic value but are routinely outweighed.

Finally, state how confident you are in the overall ranking as a percentage, and identify the single scoring decision you are least sure of and why. Answer in American English.
