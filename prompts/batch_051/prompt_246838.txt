I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about risk, going by my gut on particular bets. I would take a one-off bet that pays $120 if a fair coin lands heads and costs $100 if it lands tails. I would not take the same coin flip scaled up to $12,000 against $10,000, even though it is the same bet in every respect except size. If I could repeat the small version many times, I would happily stake about a tenth of whatever I have on each flip, but I would refuse to stake half my money on each flip, because one bad streak would do too much damage. I would also refuse a single flip that gave a 60% chance of doubling my savings and a 40% chance of wiping them out, even though its expected value is clearly positive. And two bets with the same expected value feel completely different to me: I would take a one-off 50% chance of $250 against a 50% chance of losing $100, but I would turn down a one-off 1% chance of $17,400 against a 99% chance of losing $100, even though both are worth $75 in expectation. Is there a single principle that captures all of these verdicts, or nearly all? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on, buying a lottery ticket when the jackpot makes the expected value positive but the odds are one in several million? And which of my verdicts, if any, would the principle force me to give up?
