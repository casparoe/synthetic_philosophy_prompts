A naturalist about disease and a normativist about disease have spent a year arguing past each other in a reading group I moderate. The naturalist holds roughly that disease is value-free biological dysfunction — a part or process failing to make its species-typical contribution to survival and reproduction; the normativist holds roughly that disease is whatever a society judges to be a bad condition in need of treatment, so that values enter ineliminably into classification. Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should have produced, in three parts: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, including paradigm cases they agree on and mischaracterizations of each position both disavow; (2) a disagreements table stating the residual disagreements precisely, each in wording its own side would accept for itself; and (3) cruxes — sub-questions whose answers would move at least one side — with, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. At least two cruxes should go beyond the familiar question whether "dysfunction" is value-laden, and instead concern statistically normal or species-universal conditions that are nonetheless treated as pathological, harmful statistically normal conditions, or the direction of explanation between harm and disease-status. If the exercise turns up a merely verbal disagreement, say which part and why.
