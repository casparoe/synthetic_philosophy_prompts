I've started treating longtermist arguments for restricting present-day liberties with a heuristic: if the person making the argument, or the org they represent, would plausibly end up holding the new controls, I discount their argument nearly to zero. So I'll hear out a neutral economist on immigration restrictions, but not someone whose think tank would staff the global AI slowdown they keep proposing. The way I see it, I can't check the long-run math myself, and I trust my model of motivated reasoning more then I trust my ability to catch fancy motivated reasoning in someone else's expected-value tables. My friend says this is just a genetic fallacy dressed up as epistemic hygiene. Is it? And is there a principled discount rate — something like a function of how much control they'd gain, how reversible the policy is, and how much of the argument I can check myself — rather than just zeroing the argument out?
