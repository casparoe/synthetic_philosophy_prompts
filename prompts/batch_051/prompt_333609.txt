I've been going back and forth with the same few people for years about Dutch book arguments, and I've started to think the debate has stalled in a way that's diagnostic in itself. The first camp treats the Dutch book as the real justification for probabilism: if your credences don't obey the probability axioms, there is a set of bets, each fair by your own lights, that together guarentee you lose money no matter what happens, and being open to that is either what irrationality consists in or a decisive marker of it. The second camp says the bookie and the bets are a vivid wrapper, and the actual work — if there is any — is done by the idea that incoherent credences can't all be accurate at once, or represent no single possible world, or misprice every state they say they're indifferent between. The depragmatised Dutch book arguments, which drop the bookie and replace the bets with a dominance theorem about the agent's own evaluative states, were supposed to settle this, but in my experience each side just claims them for itself.

The hypothesis I keep coming back to is that the debate isn't about Dutch books at all and probably isn't empirical or formal in the usual sense. It's about what kind of property 'rationality' is supposed to be: non-exploitability, accuracy, formal coherence, or something procedural. The Dutch book is just the battlefield where that deeper disagreement shows up, which is why tabooing 'irrational' never helps — the argument just relocates to the substitute word. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a survey of positions: is it verbal, is it different questions under one name, is there a hidden empirical or formal claim doing the work, or is the question of why (and whether) coherence is rationally required just ill-posed? Please lead with the diagnosis. For each side, say explicitly what they would have to concede for the other side to win, and whether that concession is even statable inside their framework, since I suspect both sides would sooner redefine the terms than concede anything.
