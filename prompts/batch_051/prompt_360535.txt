I am a PhD student in philosophy, with an undergraduate background in economics, trying to choose a dissertation topic within the broad debate over whether wealth and abundance have moral worth beyond what they buy. By that I mean worth over and above the value of the particular goods, services, and opportunities that greater wealth lets a person obtain. The question I want your help with is one of agenda-setting rather than survey: among the main open questions in this area, which two or three should I actually prioritize for a roughly three-year dissertation, and why? I am deliberately not asking for a reading list or a literature review; I want your reasoning about which questions deserve the years and what makes them live or dead ends.

To make my starting point concrete, here are some lines I have been weighing, though I do not want to be limited to them. The first is whether the value of wealth is fully captured by preference satisfaction, or whether there is a residual value in the security, independence, or social standing that wealth provides, value that does not show up as a purchased good. The second is whether the moral case for abundance changes in kind once everyone has enough, so that arguments that justify growth below sufficiency need not justify growth past it. The third is whether aggregate prosperity has moral weight in itself, or only through the way it is distributed across persons. The fourth is how much of the debate between defenders of abundance and critics who emphasize sufficiency or degrowth is a normative dispute and how much is an empirical dispute about wellbeing and the environment. Do any of these deserve to anchor a dissertation, or are there less obvious questions that are more upstream?

For each question you recommend, please defend the ordering. I want to know which criteria you are using, including importance for downstream debates, tractability for someone with philosophical training and some empirical literacy, neglectedness, and how many other questions depend on the answer, and I want to know how you trade those criteria off against one another. Also tell me what would have to be true, or what evidence would have to come in, for the ranking to change. If some of my four candidate lines should be deprioritized, say so plainly and explain what makes them less promising than they look.
