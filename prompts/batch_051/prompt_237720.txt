Write a roughly 800-word piece of deadpan satire in the form of a policy memorandum from a fictional government Office of Aggregate Welfare. It should be a Swiftian modest proposal: in a calm, technocratic register, the memorandum should recommend policies that would produce an extremely large population of people whose lives are only barely worth living, on the grounds that a sufficiently large sum of small positive utilities outweighs the higher average well-being of a smaller, flourishing population. The humor must come from taking aggregative consequentialist reasoning seriously rather than from inventing straw men: the document should treat lives just above the neutral point as interchangeable units, explain why average welfare, equality, and person-affecting intuitions are not decision-relevant, and pre-empt objections about overcrowding, dignity, and resource scarcity. It should be legible to a reader who has never encountered population ethics, while a specialist should recognize the repugnant conclusion, the mere-addition paradox, and the total-versus-average-versus-person-affecting dialect. Stay in character throughout and do not wink at the reader.
