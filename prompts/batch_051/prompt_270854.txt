For six months my philosophy of mind group has been stuck on Avicenna's flying man thought experiment, and I can't tell anymore whether we're actually disagreeing or just talking past each other. The setup, roughly: a person is created all at once, floating in empty air, eyes covered, limbs spread so they touch nothing, with no memories or sensations — Avicenna says the person will nevertheless affirm that he exists, while affirming nothing about a body or any bodily part. Everyone in the group accepts that bit. The split is over what it's supposed to show. One side says it shows the self is directly known to itself and isn't identical with anything bodily, so the self could exist without the body. The other side says it only shows that a thinking thing can attend away from it's body and senses — that's a fact about what attention can screen out, not about what the self is, and certainly not proof of disembodied existence. A third person says it was never meant as a proof at all, just as a kind of pointer to get you to notice self-awareness. We all agree on the text, and none of us has moved in six months. My hunch is that the real impasse is about what a thought experiment like this can establish — specifically, whether armchair conceivability is any kind of evidence for real possibility. Is that where the disagreement actually is? Or is it a hidden disagreement about introspection, about self-knowledge, or about the word "self"? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a sharper thought experiment, an empirical result, or just dropping a question?
