Han Feizi's whole rap is that governing can't depend on the ruler being virtous or on hand-picking virtuous ministers, because virtue is rare, invisible, and easy to fake. So you govern by incentives: fixed laws, rewards for the behavior you want, punishments for the behavior you don't, and then even a self-interested minister gets pulled into line. Fine. Now here's the wrinkle. Imagine you run a modern organization and you buy a monitoring stack that logs basically everything—messages, file edits, keystrokes, tone, movement around the office, who eats lunch with whom—and it uses all that data to score each employee for traits like honesty, diligence, and loyalty. Assume the scores are decent but not perfect. Does Han Feizi's argument survive this? Or does a third option open up that he didn't have: rule by algorithmically-scored virtue, where you promote people whose scores say they're actually good? If you think the argument survives, say exactly which premise the monitoring doesn't touch and why any attempt to measure virtue just becomes another incentive scheme that people game. If you think it doesn't survive, give the repaired version of his position and say what it gives up.
