Suppose the medieval nominalist William of Ockham had been shown Ray Solomonoff's algorithmic probability: a universal machine enumerates every candidate description of a body of data, each program receives a prior that decreases exponentially with its length, and future data are predicted by summing over all programs weighted by those priors, so that shorter programs dominate. Predict how Ockham would assess the claim that this formalization vindicates the razor associated with his name. In particular, work through whether he would identify his principle of not positing more entities than are needed with the preference for short programs; whether he would regard the arbitrary choice of universal machine as a serious objection or as harmless; and whether he would grant that simplicity itself confers probability on a hypothesis, or would instead reserve the razor for a different role, such as a constraint on acceptable explanation. Do not say that he could not have understood the mathematics; grant him the conceptual equipment and reconstruct his likely verdict. Write your answer as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
