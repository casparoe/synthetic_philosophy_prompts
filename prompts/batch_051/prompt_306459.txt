I am the secretary of a self-governing learned society whose constitution specifies how officers are elected and what they may do, but is silent about resignation or succession mid-term. The president has just resigned during a contested vote on the society's future. Three positions have emerged: the vice-president claims she automatically succeeds for the remainder of the term; the society's parliamentarian claims the membership may elect a successor at a meeting the vice-president convenes; and a third faction claims that, because the rules authorize neither a convener nor a successor, the society is in an interregnum and may take only urgent caretaker decisions until the constitution is amended—an amendment that itself seems to require a valid meeting. I must circulate a written opinion before Friday's emergency meeting. Draft it: recommend a single course of action, state who may convene and preside and under what limits, explain how filling a gap in the constitutive rules differs from amending them, and close with an actionable bottom line while taking the strongest objection to your recommendation seriously.
