i have this half-baked idea about races to the bottom—the Moloch thing, where everyone in a field has to cut the same corner because whoever doesn't just loses out. i've been calling my version the "scoreboard goes first" effect. the claim is that the first thing a multipolar trap consumes is the shared ability to notice it's a trap. mechanism: in the early rounds, the players can still see the harm and would rather coordinate their way out. but tracking the harm accurately and feeling bad about it is a competitive disadvantage, because the player who reframes the harm as the actual goal—or builds an internal metric on which the race looks like progress—will hold their nerve better and outcompete the ones still upset. so the field gets selected for people and institutions that genuinely can't see the trap anymore. by the time the trap is severe, coordinating an exit would require exactly the kind of seeing that no surviving player has. the concrete case i keep returning to is a social media company that starts calling engagement-maximization "connection" because it beats the competitor that admits it's farming attention, and after a while its own employees honestly believe it. i think the same thing explains why visible races like doping in sports are easier to stop than invisible ones like credential inflation or beauty standards, where after a while the harm just feels like "the way things are." i know this rhymes with Moloch, Goodhart's law (when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure), and the euphemism treadmill, and i cant tell if i've just stapled those together. is there a real mechanism here, or is it a bedtime story? steelman it, then give me the strongest objection and say whether it kills the idea or only limits its scope.
