I've come to accept the anti-aggregationist take on the dust-specks-vs-torture problem: one person's decades of severe pain is lexically worse than any number of dust specks across any number of people, so you choose the specks no matter how many people are involved. But I'm aware I dont even apply this to myself—within my own life I treat hundreds of small discomforts spread over a year as adding up to something worse than one big pain—and in policy I support rules that sum tiny costs across millions to prevent a single serious injury. What would actually count against the anti-aggregationist position stated that way? I'd like the strongest defeaters, whether empirical findings on how pain sums, formal results about continuity/transitivity/comparability, population-ethics arguments, or something else, sorted by how decisive each would be. Also give me an honest verdict on whether the view has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a problem for a view of this kind.
