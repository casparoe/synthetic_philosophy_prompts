I have been reading the debate over the norm of assertion, and it strikes me as a stalemate: the standard positions and the standard counterexamples have been on the table for years, and none of the parties seems to move. The knowledge account explains why lottery propositions strike us as unassertable and why challenging an assertion by asking how the speaker knows is apt, but critics press justified false assertions and assertions made on less than knowledge. The truth account captures the sense in which an assertion is faulty simply because it is false, yet it has trouble locating the epistemic fault of someone who asserts on excellent but misleading evidence. The justification account handles that speaker-perspective fault neatly but appears to permit asserting lottery propositions and other cases most sides want to rule out. I suspect the impasse is really a disagreement about which cases count as central data, and about whether the participants are describing a constitutive rule, offering practical guidance, or theorizing about a single speech act that may not be unified; but I offer that only as a hypothesis. Please give me a structural diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is and, given that diagnosis, what would count as progress.
