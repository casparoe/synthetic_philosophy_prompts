I've been arguing with a friend for the past year about who should count as part of "the people" that a democracy is answerable to. I lean toward the view that anyone whose life is meaningfully affected by a country's decisions should have some say in making them; he leans toward the view that only people actually subject to its laws and enforcement should. We want to stop talking past each other and produce the document we should have written all along: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the affected-interests position and the subject-to-coercion position. Please draft it with three parts. First, a numbered list of claims about democracy and boundaries that both of us can sign without hedging. Second, a disagreements table stating each side's view in wording that side would accept. Third, three cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us—each paired with a concrete case or kind of evidence both of us agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about that case. Candidate cruxes include whether long-term non-citizen residents who pay taxes and follow the law but cannot vote count as subjected, and whether people affected only through a country's trade or environmental policies, with no police power over them, have any standing.
