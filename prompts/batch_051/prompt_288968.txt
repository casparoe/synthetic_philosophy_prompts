I got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether it actually matters if someone calling out a wrong is themselves guilty of the same thing. She kept saying the hypocrisy point is just an ad hominem — the blame might be true even if the blamer is a jerk. I kept saying no, theres something about standing to blame: the truth of the accusation isnt the only thing at stake. Then we realized we were stuck because neither of us knew what kind of evidence could separate these views. So I want you to help me design a test. The conceptual claim is: hypocrisy does not merely reveal information about the hypocrite's character or make the blame less persuasive; it partially disables their normative standing to blame, even if the blame's content is accurate. I want the best possible empirical/experimental design you can think of to get traction on that claim. Be really concrete: vignettes, conditions, outcome measures, maybe a philosophical companion argument. Then tell me what a positive result would show, what a negative result would show, and what even the best test would leave untouched — for example whether standing is a real normative property or just a metaphor. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, no preamble, no restating question. Then explain. Also say which part of your reasoning is most likely to fail and what consideration would change your mind about whether the test actually tests the claim.
