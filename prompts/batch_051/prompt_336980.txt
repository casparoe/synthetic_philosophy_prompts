I have been following a rule about my own opinions for about a year, and I have started to doubt it. The rule is this: whenever I can predict with reasonable confidence that, after I have had more time with a question, I will come to hold a particular view, I adopt that view immediately. My reasoning is that my future self will have everything I now have plus whatever further thought produces, so if I expect to end up seventy percent sure that a colleague acted badly, then holding myself at fifty percent today is just knowingly carrying around an opinion I expect to abandon. Recently the rule led me to treat a coworker as though he had done something seriously wrong before I had looked at the documents, because I expected the documents to leave me seventy percent sure of his fault. A friend says that a prediction about my own future opinion is not itself a present reason, and that I was convicting him on evidence I had not yet seen. I have also noticed that I apply the rule unevenly: when the predicted future opinion is harsher or more suspicious than my current one, I rush to adopt it, but when it is more charitable, I allow myself to wait. Is the rule defensible? Is there a corrected version of it, and how would that version handle the asymmetry? More generally, under what conditions should a person now adopt a belief they predict they will hold later?
