I have been working through the economics and politics of whole-brain emulation, and I keep noticing that familiar uploading thought experiments hide an ambiguity that matters once the puzzle is turned into a commercial operation. Take Parfit's teletransporter, but modify it as follows. A non-destructive scan is made of you today, and from that scan an em is immediately run. The biological original is then kept alive for one year, as agreed in advance, and is painlessly destroyed at the end of that year. The em is not run in real time: it runs at a thousand times biological speed, so by the end of the biological year it has lived through roughly a thousand years of subjective experience and no longer shares more than a handful of direct memories with the moment of scanning. One week before the original is destroyed, the em is copied three times, and all four ems are assigned to different simulated tasks, after which they diverge rapidly; decommissioning originals, running copies at different speeds, and commissioning new copies for particular jobs are all routine in this economy.

What I would like you to do is run this case through both major views of personal identity and say how the analysis changes relative to the ordinary teletransporter. First, on a psychological-continuity or psychological-connectedness view, work through each stage—the scan, the thousand-year divergence, the original's destruction, and the copying—and say whether the relation that matters is preserved at that point, whether the original's destruction counts as the death of the person, and how many distinct continuers there are. Distinguish continuity, understood as overlapping chains of psychological connection, from direct connectedness, and say whether the speed-up matters or only the copying. Second, run the same stages through the further-fact view, on which there is supposed to be a determinate, all-or-nothing fact about whether the person continued. Where does that view give a clean verdict, and where does it begin to look arbitrary or forced? Finally, say whether this em-world variant should change someone's verdict about the ordinary one-to-one teletransporter, or whether it mainly reveals that the original case was underspecified along the dimensions of copying and speed.
