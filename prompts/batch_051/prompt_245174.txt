In a seminar on W. E. B. Du Bois’s aesthetics, a classmate dismissed a question about whether a formally innovative but politically quietist novel could still be aesthetically valuable. He said Du Bois had established that all art is propaganda, so trying to evaluate art apart from its political effects is like asking whether water can be dry. I have encountered that slogan before, but I am not sure what exact claim it is supposed to express. Lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could be stating, from relatively trivial observations about art always existing within social and persuasive contexts to strong normative claims about what artists are obliged to make. For each reading, say whether it is plausible, how well it fits what Du Bois seems to have meant, and whether it actually supports my classmate’s dismissal of purely formal or politically ambiguous work. I especially want to know which readings would not support his dismissal, because I suspect he is trading on an ambiguity between an unavoidable condition of art and a required political commitment for artists.
