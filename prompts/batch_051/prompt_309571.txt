Not sure if this is a philosophy thing or more of a pattern I keep noticing, so bare with me. I do maintenance at a big building, HVAC stuff mostly. There's a shutoff valve where if you close it in the wrong order with the pump you get a water hammer and a pipe bursts. Everyone who's worked here more than a year has been told this at least twice. It's in the binder in the office. And still, every few years, somebody does it backwards and we get a flood. The strange part is, its not that people don't believe it or think its a myth or think the old timers are exaggerating. When you tell them, they believe you, and they'll even nod and say how important it is. But a week later they can't remember which order it was. The information seems to almost push itself out of peoples heads. I've also noticed it with my brother and a cheap fix for a furnace problem. I've told him three times. He's a smart guy, he's not lazy. Each time he says "oh cool, I'll remember that," and he never does.

The same people will remember way more complicated stuff fine. One guy can rebuild a heat exchanger from memory but can't keep the valve order in his head. So I don't think it's just that the facts are boring, or that the people are careless. It feels like some ideas have a kind of quality that makes them especially hard to retain or pass along, even though nobody disputes them.

So my question is: is there a name for this? Facts or ideas that are true, believed, important, but somehow don't stick or spread? It's not a secret and not misinformation, and it's not that anyone refuses to accept it. I tried looking up "things that are true but nobody remembers" and just got memory tips and trivia lists, so I probably don't have the right vocabulary. If there is a name I'd like to know it, but also where the edges are: how do you tell an idea that's got this built-in "forgettability" from an idea people just don't care about, and can an idea have that quality in degrees? If there isn't a name, what would you call it?
