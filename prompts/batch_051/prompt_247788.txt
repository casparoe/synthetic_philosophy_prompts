Hi — I'm an undergrad in a philosophy of mind seminar and next week I'm presenting a defense of the Aunt Hillary view from Hofstadter's Ant Fugue: that an ant colony can literally have a mind in the same sense a brain does, not just as a metaphor or an 'as if' description. My thesis: if you're a functionalist (mental states are defined by their causal roles), then a mature ant colony instantiates colony-level mental states, because pheromone trails, antennation, and task-allocation dynamics do the same information-integration and control work neurons do, and the only real differences are substrate and speed. So if you deny the colony a mind, you owe us a principle difference between colony signaling and neural signaling that doesn't beg the question.
