I run a small book club and next month we're reading Steinbeck's Of Mice and Men, and I want to lead the discussion on why promises bind instead of the usual "the American dream is sad" angle. I've always thought Hume had the right framework. In the Treatise, Book 3, Part 2, Section 5 ("Of the obligation of promises"), he writes: "But as there is naturally no inclination to observe promises, distinct from a sense of their obligation; it follows, that fidelity is no natural virtue, and that promises have no force, antecedent to human conventions." His larger story, as I understand it, is that promise-keeping is an artifice built out of mutual interest — the convention does the heavy lifting, not anything intrinsic to the act of promising itself.

I've always read George's promise to look after Lennie that way: the book is pretty clear-eyed about how much of the social world runs on self-interest, and George gets locked into the promise through Aunt Clara's expectation, Lennie's reliance, and the ranch society around them, not because saying "I promise" has some magic. But the ending refuses to behave. If the promise's force is all convention and interest, then George shooting Lennie should read as the moment the convention stops paying off and he steps out of it. The novel, though, seems to treat the shooting as still bound up with the original promise — as a violation of it, or a terrible fulfillment of it, not as a neutral exit from an arrangement that no longer pays. So help me work out what the book is actually claiming about why promises bind. Does Steinbeck end up endorsing Hume's line that fidelity is no natural virtue and a promise only has whatever force convention and interest give it, or does the book dramatize something Hume's account misses — like an obligation grounded in Lennie's reliance on George, or in the relationship itself? Which scenes should I point the group to, and where does the novel most clearly resist the Hume reading? And then, however you come down, tell me how confident you are in the reading and what the single point your least sure of is.
