I keep wanting to treat the decision to sign up for cryonics as structurally identical to buying a call option, and I cannot tell whether the analogy is doing real analytical work or is just a vivid way to say that cryonics is a long-shot bet.

On one side, the mapping is clean. The current costs—dues, plus whatever social friction and bureaucratic risk sign-up involves—are the premium. Revival is exercise, and it is contingent on the future clearing a threshold at which a preserved person can be revived under conditions I would count as continued survival. The payoff is all-or-nothing: I get an entire extra life in the good states and nothing in the others, and I am not revived in proportion to how close the world came to being able to revive me. That non-linearity seems to do real work. It suggests that the value of the purchase is dominated by the upper tail of possible futures, and it licenses a specific, testable-looking prediction: holding the expected trajectory of the future fixed, more uncertainty about that trajectory should make signing up more attractive, just as a call option becomes more valuable when the volatility of its underlying rises. The person who expects discontinuous, unpredictable change should therefore be more willing to sign up than the person who expects a smooth continuation of present trends, even if both assign the same mean future.

On the other side, the analogy seems to fail exactly where option theory stops being metaphor. A financial option has a known and tradable underlying, a contractually specified strike and expiry, and a price that can in principle be enforced by hedging and replication; cryonics has a candidate underlying—something like the long-run trajectory of technological civilization plus facts about my own identity—that is not observable, not tradable, and not hedgeable. So the option idea may be doing nothing more than renaming a decision under radical uncertainty.

Is this a real identity of structure, such that the vega-like intuition—uncertainty itself counts in favor of buying, holding the expected future fixed—survives? Or does the absence of a replicable, tradable underlying kill the transfer? If parts of the analogy are salvageable, which option-theoretic commitments carry over without the arbitrage-free pricing machinery, and what should serve as the volatility parameter when I try to reason about the value of revival?
