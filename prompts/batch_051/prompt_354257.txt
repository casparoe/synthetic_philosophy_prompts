In my high school philosophy and current-events class, we were discussing a widely circulated news story about an apparently miraculous event, and several of my classmates were treating the report as good evidence for a religious claim. One student ended the discussion by saying, "extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence," and everyone moved on as if that settled the matter. I know the maxim is often connected to Hume's discussion of miracles in the Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, but I am not sure the slogan, as it actually gets used, expresses any single precise claim. It seems to compress at least three things: how surprising the event would have to be, how reliable the testimony would have to be, and what kind of evidence could ever license belief.

My teacher said the philosophically interesting question is not whether the slogan sounds reasonable but which exact principle it is asserting, and whether that principle would actually support ruling out a miracle report before the evidence is examined. Could you lay out the different precise principles the slogan might be expressing? In particular, please separate out (a) what "extraordinary" might mean — very low prior probability, conflict with a well-established law of nature, absence of any accepted mechanism, or something else; (b) what "extraordinary evidence" might mean — more numerous and independent witnesses, evidence that is much more probable if the event occurred than if the testimony is mistaken or fabricated, direct experimental repetition, or something else; and (c) how strong the principle is supposed to be — a theorem of probability, a rule of thumb, a methodological norm, or a substantive claim about testimony. For each reading, please say whether it is trivial, plausible and substantive, or false, and whether it is equivalent to the argument Hume actually gives or only a loose paraphrase of it. I would especially like to know whether any defensible reading of the slogan closes off belief in a reported miracle from the start, or whether the slogan only raises the bar that the evidence must clear. Please end with a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment, given as the final line in the form \answer{...}.
