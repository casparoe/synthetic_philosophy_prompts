I'm a software engineer, not a philosophy guy, so go easy on me. I got into reading about extinction risks as a side interest — AI, asteroids, engineered pandemics, whatever — and one thought experiment keeps rattling around my head. Suppose we found out for certain that humanity goes extinct maybe 150 years from now, long after everyone alive today is dead. And to keep it clean, imagine it's not a violent collapse: the last few generations just gradually stop having kids, and the final people live out their lives fine. No untimely deaths. The species just... ends.

My friend says it would be totally irrational for me to be upset about that scenario. He says I won't exist then, no one who exists now will exist then, so what exactly is bad about it for anyone alive now? I dont have a good answer, but I feel like something enormous would be lost.

And here's the part that actually messes with me. I've spent years on an open-source project that's meant to be infrastructure for future developers — the whole point is that it outlives me and people are still building on it in 50 years. After sitting with this extinction thing, I basically stopped working on it. Not because I lost the ability to code, but because it started to feel like writing comments in a codebase nobody will ever open. If my friend is right that a post-human future shouldn't matter to me one way or the other, then why would my motivation for the project collapse? That seems like evidence my own head doesn't buy his argument. So which is it — is he right that im just being irrational or sentimental, or is there a legitimate way to say that the end of everyone after us is bad for us now, beyond just the deaths it would involve at the very end?
