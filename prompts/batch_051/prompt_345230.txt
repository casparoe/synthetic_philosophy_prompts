I'm putting together the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate political philosophy course, and I need a single essay question on Rawls's treatment of envy and resentment in A Theory of Justice. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes and should not be something a student could answer by summarizing the difference principle or listing the parties in the original position. It should push them to reconstruct the distinction Rawls draws between ordinary envy as a vice to be contained and the special case he calls excusable envy, then to evaluate whether that distinction lets him treat envy as a diagnostic signal of injustice without giving up the claim that envy itself is not a virtue. I want the crux to be a real tension: if excusable envy is excusable because it tracks failures of self-respect or failures of the basic structure, then is Rawls simply smuggling a prior standard of justice into the psychology, or can he explain why envy is a reliable signal rather than a resentful distortion? The question should require students to apply the distinction to at least one concrete kind of inequality and to take a position on whether Rawls's treatment makes envy a vice, a signal, or something unstable between the two.

Please write the response in the following sections, in this order and with these approximate lengths:

**Exam question** (about 120 words)  
A single well-formed essay question, not a list of sub-questions, though it may include one short direction about the concrete case.

**Model answer** (about 900 words)  
A strong answer that reconstructs the envy/resentment distinction, explains why Rawls thinks the well-ordered society must be concerned with envy at all, applies the distinction to a concrete case, identifies the deepest tension in Rawls's treatment, and defends a definite verdict. It should not read like a summary of the text; it should do the analytical work the question asks for.

**Marking scheme** (about 300 words)  
Five must-hit points, each with the number of marks it is worth, totaling 20. For each point, give a one-line note on the characteristic way candidates get it wrong—for example, confusing excusable envy with justified resentment, treating envy merely as a psychological obstacle to stability rather than as a potential indicator of injustice, or failing to see that Rawls needs a standard of fair distribution to distinguish excusable from inexcusable envy.
