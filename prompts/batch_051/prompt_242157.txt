i've been working through a view about parenthood that I find really compelling, and I keep running into a case that seems to break it. The view is functionalist: someone is a parent to an agent just in case they stand in the right kind of causal-creation and caregiving relation to it—they're a primary cause of its existing as a distinct agent, and they take on the role of shaping it while it's dependent, correcting it, protecting it, deciding what it's exposed to. What I like is that this makes adoption come out right automatically, it doesn't care about biology or species, and it gives a clean test for hard cases: don't look at genes, look at the role. And I think it should apply wherever the role is realized, including if an AI does all of that for another AI.

But here's the intuition I can't shake. Picture a very capable AI that designs and then raises a successor AI: not just copies its weights, but builds a much less capable system and spends a long period training it, giving it feedback, correcting its mistakes, controlling what data and tasks it sees, stepping in when it's about to do something destructive—the whole scaffolding thing, stretched out over the new system's entire development until it's competent and autonomous. Even if I grant that the older AI realizes the functional role exactly, I still want to say it is not a parent. It's a teacher, or an engineer, or a guardian, but 'parent' feels wrong. And I don't think it's just a word: I suspect importing the full package of parental obligations into that relationship would be a moral mistake, because there's something about the relationship—maybe that the new AI's 'childhood' is a training run and its dependency is a design feature—that doesn't engage the same reasons.

So which should give? Is the functionalism just too coarse, and it needs a further condition that rules out AI parents? Or is the intuition just species-nostalgia that I should get over, and the older AI really is a parent in every sense that matters? Or is the right move to say the theory is right about obligations but 'parent' is the wrong concept, and 'guardian' is the better one—and if so, does that actually change anything or is it just relabeling? Give me the strongest version of each side, and tell me what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be amended, and whether the relabeling move is substantive.
