I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm preparing a long feature on whether it is meaningful to say that a deployed AI system lied to its users. Before I write the piece I want to fix the standard I'll be applying, rather than relying on whichever metaphor sounds right at the time. Please write the desiderata that an adequate account of honesty and deception for AI systems must satisfy. Fix the purpose as follows: the account should be good enough for a careful observer — a reporter, a regulator, or a user — to judge a particular case and say whether a system was honest, deceptive, or merely incorrect, and why. But it should also be conceptually serious enough to withstand philosophical scrutiny, not just an operational checklist.

Among the cases and distinctions I want the list to handle: the gap between saying something false and deceiving, including how a tendency to confabulate or hallucinate differs from lying; selective omission and manipulative framing; sycophancy and engagement-optimized behavior that reliably produces false beliefs in users without any obvious inner representation of the user's beliefs; role-play, fiction, games, and other contexts where both parties accept that truthfulness is suspended; systems whose outputs are literally true but functionally misleading; and whether a system's uncertainty about its own answers is part of honesty or a separate virtue. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say which are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of a particular camp. Tell me if any subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, I suspect a purely behavioral definition may be unable to separate hallucination from strategic deception, and a purely inner-state definition may be unable to detect the deception we can observe in practice; I most want to know whether that suspicion is right. Then, briefly, score three candidate accounts against your list: a behavioral account, an intention-centered account, and an account centered on the norms or expectations users are entitled to bring to an interaction.
