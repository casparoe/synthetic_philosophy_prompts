I cover psychiatry and the mind for a general-interest magazine, and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a clean word for. A diagnostic label enters the manual as a rough checklist. Within a few years people are recognizing themselves in it, arriving at clinics already fluent in the criteria, forming support groups, writing memoirs; clinicians start asking about the symptoms more and seeing them more. Ten years on, the label is still there but the condition it picks out is noticably different from the original checklist, and the manual quietly revises the criteria to fit people the label itself helped shape — while presenting the change as a clarification. The same thing doesn't seem to happen with a label for a kind of rock: the rock doesn't read the geology textbook. I know this is in the neighborhood of self-fulfilling prophecy and labeling theory, but those run mostly one way — label changes person, not the person changes the label. Is there an established name for this two-directional loop, how is it different from a plain feedback loop, and what exactly decides which categories get altered by their own members and which don't?
