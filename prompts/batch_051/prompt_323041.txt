Write a deadpan Onion-style news article — headline plus about 500 words — satirizing the practice of treating historically contingent outcomes as inevitable after the fact. The vehicle should be a newly created federal Office of Path-Dependence Review, whose first annual report finds that every outcome it examined was overdetermined, and which therefore declines to fund any reparations, apologies, or policy reversals on the ground that nothing else could have happened anyway. The satire should remain legibly ironic throughout, without winking or explicit editorializing. The jokes should come from the actual theory of path dependence, not generic fatalism: give the Office a statutory causal-significance threshold below which an initial condition is officially too small to count; have its chief methodologist concede, in a technical aside, that the Office cannot yet distinguish genuine lock-in from a long sequence of large causes, while the press release continues to issue findings of inevitability; include a spokesperson who praises the QWERTY keyboard as the best possible layout simply because it won; and let a stray bureaucratic detail reveal that the Office's own procedures are path-dependent. Aim the piece so that a specialist in lock-in and increasing returns recognizes the theory, while a lay reader still gets the point. After the article, add a short author's note: say where the piece takes liberties with the theory or leans on contested empirical claims (such as the keyboard or videotape cases), identify where the satire's implicit argument is most likely to fail, and say which finding about a canonical lock-in case would most likely make the satire's target unfair rather than apposite.
