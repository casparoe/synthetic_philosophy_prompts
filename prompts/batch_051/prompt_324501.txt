I just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about the ethics of belief. He defends a broadly Cliffordian view that it is wrong everywhere, and for everyone, to believe anything on insufficient evidence; I found myself defending a broadly Jamesian exception for forced, momentous, live options where belief may help make itself true. We ended up with two unresolved points: whether a belief's tendency to bring about its own truth counts as an epistemic reason or only a practical one, and whether belief is voluntary enough for evidentialist norms to apply in the first place. Draft the joint statement we could both sign after an adversarial collaboration. Begin with a numbered list of claims both a Cliffordian evidentialist and a Jamesian permissivist can accept about what their positions do and do not entail. Then provide a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in wording its own representative would accept as fair. For each remaining crux, specify a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that case will show.
