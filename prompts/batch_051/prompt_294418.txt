I keep thinking about a rule a friend suggested for our county council, and I want someone to actually walk through it rather than tell me if its good or bad. The rule is this: any proposal to repeal a county ordinance that's older than about twenty years has to include a "fence report" written by someone from a different department than the person proposing the repeal. The report has to state, in good faith, what problem the ordinance was originally trying to solve and why the original council thought it made sense. If the report cant come up with a plausible original rationale, the repeal motion is automatically put on the consent agenda and passes without debate. If it does come up with a rationale, the repeal motion can only proceed if it also includes a written explanation of what specifically has changed since then, or what the original council got wrong. Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position: what does it incentivize among council members, among the staff who write the reports, and among interest groups? How would people game it? What happens to the quality of the fence reports after a few years, and where does the first gaming show up? Can any of the failure modes be fixed without basically gutting the Burkean point of the whole thing?
