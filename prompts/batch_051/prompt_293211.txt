I've ended up helping organise a small workshop on anthropic reasoning, and the organisers want the closing session to be more constructive than the usual loop of the self-sampling assumption people and the self-indication assumption people talking past each other. The plan is an adversarial collaberation: instead of another debate, the SSA camp and the SIA camp have to draft, in good faith, a single document they would both actually sign. I'd like you to produce that document for us as the worked example we can circulate beforehand.

The document should open with a numbered agreed-claims section covering what both sides accept about observer selection effects in general, including the basic structure of reasoning when two theories predict different numbers of observers, and a description of the presumptuous philosopher scenario itself. After that it should have a disagreements table. Each row of the table should state the SSA position phrased so an SSA defender would own it word for word, the SIA position phrased so an SIA defender would own it word for word, the crux (the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side), and, wherever you can, an argument or thought experiment both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

One wrinkle the organisers specifically want included is a modified presumptuous philosopher setup: before the philosopher ever says anything, you are offered a ticket that pays a fixed prize only if the smaller universe is actual, with the odds set by a bookie who knows both theories but has no more information than you about which one obtains. Please say what the two camps can jointly agree about that bet, and where exactly they diverge about the rational price.
