I'm not a historian or anything, just a guy who reads about the First World War on and off, and I've spent way too long in this one online forum where a thread about why the war happened has been alive since 2019 and just will not die. The thing that gets me is that everyone in the thread accepts basically the same facts: Franz Ferdinand was killed in Sarajevo, the alliances were in place, the generals had mobilization timetables they were afraid to depart from, there had been a naval arms race and colonial standoffs for years, all of that. Nobody is really fighting about the facts. But the sides never move. One group says the war was locked in once the alliance system existed, and Sarajevo was just a match in a room full of gas. Another group says it came down to the choices of a handful of ministers and generals in July 1914, and with slightly different people in the room it would have stayed a diplomatic crisis. A third group says once mobilization started the plans and railway timetables took over and it was out of anyones hands. And then there are the people who say the whole question is set up wrong because there are dozens of causes and you can make any of them look like the real one depending on your framing.

At one point someone pasted a list of 27 causes and asked everyone to rank them, and the rankings were all over the place, but nobody changed their mind afterwards. I've started to think the actual disagreement isnt about facts at all but about what it takes for something to count as the cause of a huge event. Like, does a cause have to be necessary — without it, no war — or sufficient — with it, war no matter what — or does it just have to make the war more likely, or be the thing you wish you could have changed? My working theory is that each side in the thread is quietly using a different standard, and the standards are doing the arguing under the table. I'm not looking for a verdict on what actually caused the war — I've read a hundred of those. What I want is a diagnosis: is the theory right, or is there a real factual disagreement hidden in there, and is there anything — a piece of evidence, a counterfactual, a distinction — that could actually settle it, or is it the sort of argument that just gets restarted forever?
