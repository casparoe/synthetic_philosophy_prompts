In Pascal's *Pensées*, in the old Trotter translation, there is a fragment about Oliver Cromwell that I don't have in front of me, so I'll paraphrase from memory rather than quote. As I remember it, Pascal's thought runs something like this: Cromwell was about to ravage all Christendom; the royal house was lost, and his own family was about to become secure. But a little grain of sand formed in his bladder and entered his ureter; he died, his family was brought low, and the king was restored. Rome itself would have trembled before him; instead, this tiny piece of gravel, which would have been nothing elsewhere, changed the course of everything.
