I'm in two seminars this term, one on obligations to future generations and one on procreative ethics, and I keep getting whiplash from how differently they treat the nonidentity problem. In the future-generations class, when somebody points out that a policy we pick now changes who gets born later (different people meet, different couples, different kids, so the exact people alive in 2200 under the high-emissions path wouldn't have existed under the conservation path), the professor treats that as a technicality and goes right back to saying we've wronged the people living in 2200 by leaving them a degraded world, even though none of those individuals would have existed if we'd chosen otherwise. In the procreation class, the same structure comes up and everyone lands on the opposite side: a couple can conceive now and the child will be born with a serious but still worth living genetic condition, or wait a few months and a different child would be born without it, and the class is basically unanimous that the couple doesn't wrong the child by not waiting, because the child has no complaint against a condition it had to have in order to exist at all.

This feels like a real asymmetry and I can't tell if there's a principle holding it together or whether one of my classes is just smuggling in an impersonal way of valuing things when its convenient. I'm trying to write a term paper on this and keep going in circles. I want the strongest case you can build for treating these differently, and an honest verdict on whether I should keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or give it up. If you defend it, make sure your distinction handles the hard version: imagine the high-emissions world is still one where every future person has a life well worth living, but each of those lives is significantly worse than the life some other person would have lived under conservation, and the couple's child likewise has a life well worth living, so in neither case has anyone been made worse off than they would have been, since the alternative for each of them was never existing.
