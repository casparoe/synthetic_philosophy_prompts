I teach a final-year undergraduate module called Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence. I need a closed-book essay question on the narrow definition of the alignment problem, together with a model answer of roughly 800 words and a marking scheme out of 25 marks. The topic is the tension between intent alignment and value alignment. The question must not be answerable by reciting definitions. It should provide a short scenario—for example, a home-assistance robot that, at the instruction of an exhausted carer, switches off a frail patient's fall monitor because the carer finds the alerts burdensome—and then ask students to assess the claim that the system is aligned, and to explain what their assessment shows about whether alignment should be defined narrowly as the system's doing what its operator intended or more broadly as the system's acting on values that are independently defensible. The model answer should distinguish literal instructions, intended outcomes, and the principal's underlying interests; it should explain why the narrow definition is attractive as an engineering specification; and it should weigh the objection that the narrow definition counts harmful but faithfully executed plans as alignment successes. It must not merely summarise both sides but should defend a specific overall judgement about the adequacy of the narrow definition. The marking scheme should separate an A from a B by naming the specific argumentative moves each band must contain, and should identify the characteristic errors that keep an answer at C level, such as conflating alignment with moral permissibility or treating the scenario as a routine design trade-off rather than a conceptual question. Please write the question, the model answer, and the marking scheme. The question must not require citations, bibliographies, or knowledge of named authors or papers.
