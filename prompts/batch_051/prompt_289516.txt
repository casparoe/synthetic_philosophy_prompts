For my school's philosophy club magazine, I would like you to write a deadpan Onion-style news article — headline plus 450–500 words — about a man who has adopted Solomonoff induction as his complete personal epistemology and can therefore no longer act, update, or be surprised. The humor should come from the actual formalism, not generic cluelessness: Kolmogorov complexity is defined only relative to an arbitrary universal Turing machine and is invariant only up to an additive constant; the universal prior is uncomputable because evaluating it runs into the halting problem; and the prior assigns exponentially more weight to shorter programs while still giving positive weight to every computable sequence. Please do not write him as if he were consulting an explicit list of hypotheses or using a finite lookup table. Include one quote from a concerned colleague who favors ordinary Bayesian conditioning with a hand-picked prior, and close with the man insisting that his total inaction is still optimal up to an additive constant, so nothing is actually wrong. The satirical target should be the practice of treating Solomonoff induction as a usable universal epistemology, when in fact it is an elegant but uncomputable ideal.
