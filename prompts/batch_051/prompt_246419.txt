Jackson's Mary knows every physical fact about color while she's stuck in a black-and-white room, and the usual intuition is that she still learns something when she first sees red. Now rerun the setup for wisdom instead of color. Say Mary is raised in an isolated environment but given every expressible true proposition about human psychology, virtue, and practical reason—she can recite the best ethical theory cold—yet she's never once made a decision in conditions of uncertainty, comforted someone, or had to sit with a bad call. When she gets out, does she already have practical wisdom (phronesis), or does she only acquire it then? If your intuition is that she's still missing something, does that give us an argument against the view that wisdom just is having the right propositional knowledge, the way the original case is supposed to work against physicalism—or does the analogy break down somewhere, and if so where? Work through the variation.
