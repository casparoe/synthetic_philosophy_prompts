I keep noticing a configuration in workplaces and study settings, and I want to know whether it has an established name or whether it deserves a new one. Two instances:

A translation agency starts using machine translation with human post-editing. For the first couple of years the post-editors genuinely edit: they check the machine's output against the source, and they catch subtle errors of register, idiom, scope, and false friends. Over time the engine improves, outright mistakes become rare, and editing shrinks into smoothing. Clients stop complaining, the quality metrics climb, and the managers quietly reclassify the post-editor as a cheap cleanup step. Five years later a legal document is mistranslated in a subtle-but-catastrophic way, and it goes through exactly the way it would not have gone through in year one. The skill that justified keeping a human in the loop has atrophied precisely because the machine became reliable enough to make that skill look redundant.

A hospital adopts an AI tool to screen chest films. At first the radiologists read each study from scratch before looking at the AI read, and they catch a fair number of misses. A decade later they mostly look at the regions the AI flagged and glance at the rest. Aggregate accuracy is better than ever, and the department can truthfully say the AI reduced error. But the independent reading ability the hospital was paying to keep as a backstop is largely gone, and the errors that remain are exactly the class of error nobody in the building is any longer practiced at finding.

So here's the thing I want a name for: the human is kept in the loop as a check on the machine, but the machine's very reliability erodes the human's checking ability, while the aggregate metrics improve and hide the loss until the machine fails in the way the check existed to catch. I know this is near "automation complacency" and "deskilling," but each feels like only half. Complacency is the overtrust; deskilling is the lost ability; I mean the loop between them, plus the masking by improved overall performance. If it does not have a settled name, please coin one and define it carefully — the label matters less than knowing where this thing starts and stops.
