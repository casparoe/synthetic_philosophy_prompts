I keep getting stuck on something that looks inconsistent. English spelling is presented as alphabetic — letters stand for sounds — but in practice it holds a morpheme's spelling constant across related words even when the pronunciation changes: the g in sign is silent but is pronounced in signature, and the b in bomb comes out in bombard. That makes the system look like it's tracking morphemes, not phonemes. My sense is any script has to pick a level of language to encode — phonemes, morphemes, syllables, whatever — and quietly ignores the rest. So what view of what a writing system is for does English orthography actually presuppose, as opposed to the view people recite when they call it phonetic? Is the practice coherent, or is it running two incompatible commitments at once? And if you actually took the phonemic view seriously, what concrete things would a spelling reform have to give up — word families, a single written standard across dialects, homophone distinctions? Also say whether the practice genuinely presupposes one theory, or whether it's compatible with several and just underdetermined at certain points.
