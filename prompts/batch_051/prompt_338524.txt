I sit on the Charter Review Commission of a mid-sized U.S. city. The Commission is weighing a staff proposal to put a single charter amendment before the voters, referred to internally as the civic compact. The amendment would (a) lower the voting age to 16 for all municipal elections; (b) extend the vote in municipal and school-board elections to non-citizen residents of the city; and (c) make voting in municipal elections compulsory, enforced by a modest fine and backed by automatic registration of every eligible voter, with exemptions for illness, disability, and religious objection. The City Attorney has advised that the measure would not conflict with state or federal law if adopted as a charter amendment.

The staff report's central argument is that compulsion answers the usual objection to both expansions — that the newly enfranchised groups would not turn out — and that coupling expansion with compulsion reframes the franchise as a civic duty attaching to everyone governed by the city's laws, rather than a privilege reserved for citizens of full capacity.

The Commission is divided. Some members would advance almost any expansion on all-affected-interests grounds, holding that everyone substantially affected by the city's decisions should have a say. Others regard the existing voting age and citizenship requirement as legitimate competence and membership thresholds rather than arbitrary exclusions. A third group accepts some expansion but is troubled both by compelled voting and by bundling three distinct questions into one ballot measure.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — advance the amendment as drafted; revise it, specifying which components to keep, drop, alter, or split into separate questions; or reject it — with reasons the other commissioners can actually act on. Give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration against your recommendation, and do not settle the question with slogans about what democracy demands.
