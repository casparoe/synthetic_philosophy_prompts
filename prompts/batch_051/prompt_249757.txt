I have been trying on my own to work out when a traditional story deserves to be called history rather than legend. Give me a working definition of 'historical' as applied to a story or figure handed down from the past, one that gets all of the following cases right. I am confident that the assassination of Julius Caesar is history and that the story of Romulus and Remus being suckled by a she-wolf is legend. The hard cases are these: the Trojan War, where a city at the site of Troy was destroyed by fire in the late Bronze Age, but the familiar story of the ten-year siege and the wooden horse comes from epic poetry composed centuries later; King Arthur, who appears in no contemporary source and is first mentioned only in texts written centuries after he supposedly lived; and the Exodus, which is foundational to a living tradition but for which there is no direct Egyptian or archaeological evidence of a large-scale migration. My hunch is that the difference has something to do with whether a story was preserved in order to record what happened or in order to teach and justify, but I cannot make that distinction precise. Propose the definition, run it through all five cases, and tell me which of your verdicts is least secure.
