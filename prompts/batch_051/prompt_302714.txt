Write a research proposal for a one-semester philosophy of science term paper of about 6000 words. The question I want the paper to settle is whether the central explanatory principle of the modern evolutionary synthesis—that natural selection acting on heritable variation is the principal cause of adaptive complexity—meets Karl Popper's falsifiability requirement when that requirement is stated strictly: for a claim to count as scientific, it must forbid some class of empirically possible observations. Popper first judged Darwinism not to be a testable scientific theory but a framework within which testable hypotheses might be formulated, and he later conceded that he had been too harsh. I do not want the paper to rest with either verdict or to become a history of Popper's change of mind. I want it to determine whether the later concession can be made good on Popper's own criterion, or whether it merely shows that local, auxiliary evolutionary hypotheses are falsifiable while the core principle remains protected from refutation. In the proposal, please state the question so that it admits of a determinate answer; explain what is left open by Popper's earlier position, by his later position, and by the standard textbook defense that evolutionary biology makes predictions about matters such as antibiotic resistance, the order of fossils, and patterns of genetic variation; and describe a method by which a purely philosophical argument, rather than an empirical study, could make progress. The method, as I imagine it, will need to separate the core principle from its auxiliary assumptions, address the objection that fitness is often inferred from the very differential reproduction it is supposed to explain, and confront the difficulty that a statistical or historical principle may not prohibit a clean class of observations in the way that a law about gases does. Say what an affirmative and a negative conclusion would each have to establish, what would count as success or failure for the paper, and where the project risks becoming too large for one semester. Do not supply a bibliography; I will add citations myself, though you may refer to positions and arguments by name.
