After reading about Cantor's transfinite set theory and the objections it has drawn, I have been putting together an annotated list of the standard arguments that an actual infinite—a collection that is completed and given all at once, rather than a merely potential infinite—is impossible or at least cannot be realized in the physical world. So far I have four. First, Hilbert's hotel: an infinitely occupied hotel can make room for one new guest by moving every guest up one room, and can even absorb infinitely many new guests by moving guests to double-numbered rooms, even though it was already full. Second, Galileo's paradox: the natural numbers can be paired one-to-one with the perfect squares, a proper subset of the naturals, so an infinite set would be equal in size to one of its proper parts, contrary to the principle that the whole is greater than the part. Third, the subtraction argument: removing the odd numbers from the naturals leaves the evens, which still has the same cardinality as the naturals, while removing all numbers greater than some finite bound leaves only finitely many, so infinity minus infinity seems to give different results depending on what is subtracted. Fourth, Thomson's lamp: a lamp is switched on at half a minute, off at three-quarters of a minute, on at seven-eighths of a minute, and so on, completing infinitely many switchings by one minute, yet there is no determinate final state, which is supposed to show that a completed infinite sequence of operations is incoherent.

I want to add one more argument to this list. My worry is that the first three are really one family of moves, since they all work by pairing an infinite set with a proper subset or manipulating infinite cardinalities, and the fourth is a supertask with no determinate final state. So the fifth should be genuinely different in structure: not a rewording of the whole-equals-part puzzle, not another cardinal-arithmetic puzzle, and not another supertask. What argument am I missing, stated carefully, and what does it show about the impossibility or unreality of actual infinities that the four on my list do not?
