Take the Lucretian symmetry argument against the badness of death: the eternity of nonexistence before our birth is not counted as a harm, so the eternity of nonexistence after our death should not be either, since both are periods in which we do not exist. A standard deprivation-theoretic reply treats the two cases as asymmetric: a person could have lived longer and so is deprived of goods by an earlier death, but she could not have been born substantially earlier and still have been the same person, so prenatal nonexistence deprives her of nothing that was genuinely available to her. Now rerun the argument under the assumption that the block universe (the B-theory of time) is correct: all times exist tenselessly together, and the present has no metaphysical privilege. Does this variation break the asymmetry the deprivation account relies on? Does it force the deprivation account to say that you are equally harmed by goods located before your birth? Or can the asymmetry be restated in terms of the causal and modal structure of a life, without any appeal to a metaphysically open future? Give the strongest version of each side, then say which one the variation favors.
