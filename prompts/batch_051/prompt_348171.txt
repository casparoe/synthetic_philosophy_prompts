I'm a software engineer, so I tend to think about this stuff as "what operation am I performing on a stream of inputs?", which is maybe a weird place to come at it from, but I keep bumping into a mental move that I don't have a word for. The first version is perceptual: I'll sit outside, look at a tree, and instead of just taking for granted that there's a tree out there that I'm seeing, I deliberately put aside the question of whether there's really a tree and pay attention only to how the seeing shows up — the color, the depth, the way it appears as "a tree over there" before I even stop to think about it. I'm not doubting that the tree is there; that would be a different operation. I'm just parking the existence question and inspecting the experience as experience.

I do a version of it in arguments too. When someone says something that hits me as obviously false, I sometimes try to set aside whether it's actually false and instead look at the feeling of obvious-false-ness itself — like, what is it like to be in the grip of "that's just wrong"? Again, I'm not dumping my own view or saying truth doesn't matter; I just want to inspect the appearing part without getting pulled into judging the content. This doesn't feel like mindfulness, because mindfulness, as I half-understand it, is more about watching thoughts come and go, whereas this is a deliberate, sustained inspection of how things appear once I've stopped taking them at face value. It also doesn't feel like suspension of disbelief, since that's about accepting a fiction. I've poked around on my own but not systematicly.

So, does this operation have an established name in philosophy? If it does, I don't just want the label — I want to know what exactly the operation is supposed to consist in, what it is not, and where it starts to blur into neighboring things like skepticism, introspection, or just "ignoring the question." Put on your professional-philosopher-who-knows-the-history-of-philosophy-and-philosophy-of-mind hat, take your time, brainstorm a few candidate terms first, and then tell me which fits best and why. If I'm actually describing a mishmash of two different moves, tell me that too, and which parts belong to which.
