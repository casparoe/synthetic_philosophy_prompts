Draft the joint statement that a moral testimony pessimist and a moral testimony optimist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The pessimist's position: moral testimony is distinctively second-rate as a source of moral belief—when a person forms a moral belief simply on another's say-so, the resulting belief lacks full moral knowledge or justification unless something else, such as the believer's own grasp of the reasons, is added. The optimist's position: moral testimony is in principle no more problematic than testimony about other domains; a suitably reliable informant can give one moral knowledge or justification, and the familiar unease about moral deference traces to pragmatic or moral considerations rather than to any distinctive epistemic defect in the testimony itself.

The deliverable should be constructive—not a survey and not a verdict on which side is right. Include:

(i) a numbered section of agreed claims, including claims about non-moral testimony, about clear cases where moral deference goes wrong, and about what each side must explain;
(ii) the exact residual disagreements, each view framed in wording its own proponents would accept;
(iii) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; I suspect, but do not assume, that central cruxes include whether one can identify a moral expert without already exercising the very capacity being outsourced, whether moral knowledge requires the believer to grasp the reasons, and whether any asymmetry with non-moral testimony survives the strongest non-moral analogies;
(iv) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Present the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Make definite editorial choices: select the three or four cruxes you judge most important rather than listing every candidate, and commit to a view about which apparent disagreements are merely verbal rather than substantive, with a brief explanation for each such judgment. You need not adjudicate the underlying debate about moral testimony, but do not hedge by laying out every option and leaving me to choose among them.
