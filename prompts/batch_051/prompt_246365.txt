I am preparing a seminar unit on whether generative models can be creative in a sense stronger than recombining their training data, and I want a case built to order rather than found, because I plan to use it to argue that product-based and process-based criteria of creativity are not extensionally equivalent. Please construct a thought experiment in which a single AI-assisted artwork is judged creative by a product-based theory of creativity and not creative by a process-based theory of creativity, with the verdicts generated by the theories rather than stipulated. By the product-based theory I mean the following: a work is creative to the extent that it changes the space of its genre, altering a rule or an evaluative dimension along which later works in that genre are judged, and thereby introduces novelty that well-informed practitioners could not reach by routine exploration of the existing rules; on this view, whether the relevant novelty is present is a matter of the work's relation to the space, and no simultaneous insight on the part of the maker is required. By the process-based theory I mean the following: a work is creative only to the extent that the novelty for which it is later praised was present, as such, to the maker during production, and the maker affirmed it as the thing she was reaching for, so that a creative outcome must trace back to a creative act in which the agent at least dimly sees what she is opening up. The case must be one in which the product theorist says creative and the process theorist says not, about the same token work and the same feature of it.

Constraints. The system should be a realistic near-term generative model, a diffusion-style image model or a language-model assistant or something similarly unglamorous, deployed by a skilled human practitioner, with no appeal to general intelligence, consciousness, or intentions in the machine. The human operator must be a genuine collaborator in the sense that the process-based theory would count many of her other choices in the same work as creative; the case may not simply exploit the obvious difference between a person and a neural network, nor may it reduce the human to a passive curator of outputs. The novelty that carries the verdict must be plausible as a move genuinely beyond the model's training distribution rather than an interpolation it was implicitly equipped to make, and the case must not involve the model having been trained on the very style it appears to transcend. Specify the medium, the training corpus, the model's role, and the human's role concretely enough that a reader can see the interaction.

After the scenario, run the demonstration: state, for each theory as defined above, the precise criterion it applies and why that criterion yields its verdict in this case, pointing to the detail of the scenario rather than to the definitions alone. Then identify the single feature of the scenario that is doing the work, say what both theories would say if that feature were altered or removed, and close with the dialectical pass: give the strongest argument a defender of the process-based theory would make for treating the case as rigged, and the strongest reply available to the product-based theory.
