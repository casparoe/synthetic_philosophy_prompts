I advise a city charter commission in a city of roughly six hundred thousand people. The commission is weighing a lottocratic review chamber—a standing body of citizens chosen by lottery—and I have drafted the following proposal. Before I bring it to the commission, I want to know how it would actually operate, not whether it is a good idea in the abstract. The proposed chamber would have ninety members chosen by stratified random sampling from the voter rolls, serving a single two-year term. Members would be paid the city's median individual income, guaranteed leave from their jobs, and given a small professional staff. Every ordinance passed by the elected council would go to the chamber before taking effect. The chamber could not amend; within thirty days it would have to approve the ordinance, return it once to the council with written objections (which the council could override by a two-thirds vote), or by a two-thirds vote of its own refer the ordinance to a binding referendum at the next scheduled city election. If the chamber took no action within thirty days, the ordinance would take effect automatically. Each member's vote would be published in a publicly searchable database, while floor deliberations would be closed to the public and to lobbyists. Any member could be challenged by a petition signed by three percent of registered voters, after which a jury of twelve citizens drawn by lot would review evidence of absenteeism, dereliction, or corruption and could, by nine votes, censure the member and dock half of their remaining stipend. After their term, members would be barred from elected city office for five years and from lobbying city government for three.

Treat this as a mechanism. Please walk through what it would incentivize among the elected council, among the citizen-reviewers, and among interest groups; say how it would be gamed first; and explain what you expect to happen to ordinances the chamber lets lapse quietly. Which failure do you expect to be most damaging, and is there a fix for it that does not effectively gut the chamber? Finally, if you had to retain only two of the features described above and drop the rest, which two would you keep and why? End with a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment as the final line.
