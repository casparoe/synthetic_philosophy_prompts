I run a reading group for engineers and lawyers — sharp, argumentatively comfortable, no formal philosophy — and in two weeks I have seventy-five minutes to teach them Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. I do not want the explanation itself; I want a teaching plan. Give me the order in which the pieces have to be introduced, with what depends on what, so the regress arrives as something the group sees coming rather than a trick I spring on them. In particular, tell me where the distinction between a relation as a constituent and a relation as something that relates has to be made, and what has to be in place before that distinction can land without feeling like a dodge. Then give me the one worked example to carry through the hour. I was torn between a genuinely relational example like 'the cup is to the left of the spoon' and a subject-predicate example like 'Socrates is wise'; tell me which does more work and what the example must have, and must not have, if the group is to use it to run the regress themselves. Next, the misconception map. I expect this group to produce at least three: that adding an instantiation relation between the relation and its terms fixes everything; that the regress is harmless in the way the natural-number progression is harmless; and, from the engineers especially, that formalizing a relation as a set of ordered pairs dissolves the problem. Tell me which of these are real misconceptions as opposed to exit routes that might be defensible, and say precisely where in the sequence each should be headed off. I want a closing question or short exercise that shows whether they have genuinely got the distinction or have only learned to repeat it. Finally, tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level — I would rather say we are bracketing Bradley's own monism and the full internal-relations debate than caricature them in ten minutes. Write the whole plan in continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables; I want it to read as a long email.
