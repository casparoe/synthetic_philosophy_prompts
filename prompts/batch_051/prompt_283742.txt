Two friends of mine went at it over drinks about whether Priya can consistently support a $15 minimum wage while opposing her city's rent control ordinance. (1) Sam said that's rank cherry-picking: rent control is a price ceiling, so you get shortages, and a minimum wage is a price floor, so you get unemployment; if the first distortion is disqualifying, the second ought to be too. (2) Priya said the cases aren't symmetric. Low-wage labor markets are full of frictions—search costs, turnover, employers with local monopsony power, meaning workers have few other places to go—so a modest floor can raise pay without much job loss, and the empirical record is genuinely mixed. Rent control, by contrast, pretty reliably locks in cheap rent for sitting tenants who might not even be poor while newcomers get frozen out. (3) Sam replied that this is special pleading: if you can invoke real-world frictions to excuse a wage floor, I can invoke them for housing too. In a tight rental market landlords have power, tenants cant just up and move, yet she won't give rent control the same charitable reading. (4) Priya answered that even if both interventions cause some distortion, the distributional logic differs. A wage floor transfers to low-wage workers as a class and spreads the cost across employers and consumers, while rent control transfers to incumbent tenants regardless of need and shifts the cost onto would-be tenants, who tend to be younger and poorer. So you can coherently favor one and oppose the other without a double standard. They asked me to settle it and I honestly couldn't.
