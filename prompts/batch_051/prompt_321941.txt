I have recently been appointed to the board of a charitable trust whose grants are intended to produce benefits many decades into the future, and around the same time I became a parent; the question of how heavily to weight future benefits against present ones has therefore stopped being abstract for me. I have read enough popular summaries to know the slogans — I understand the elementary distinction between discounting something because it arrives later in time and discounting it because future people are likely to be richer — but I need the argumentative structure beneath that distinction.

Please give an essay-length, intuitive explanation of the following. First, why a positive pure rate of time preference is generally regarded as morally arbitrary, even though a positive consumption discount rate is routinely defended. Second, unpack the standard decomposition in which the consumption discount rate is the sum of a pure-time-preference term and a term multiplying the elasticity of the marginal utility of consumption by the expected growth rate of per-capita consumption; I want the conceptual content of each component in plain but precise language, not algebra. Third, briefly explain why exponential discounting is time-consistent, how that fact bears on the debate, and why some analysts favour declining discount rates over very long horizons — for example when averaging over uncertainty about future growth rates, or because of hyperbolic-type preferences.

After the essay, append a brief structured summary in the form of a table with the following columns:

- Term: the concept or component being summarised (for example pure time preference, the elasticity term, the growth term, declining discount rates).
- What it discounts: the thing whose weight the term reduces (utility, consumption, or something else).
- Why it appears in standard practice: its conventional role in discounting.
- Whether it treats later well-being as intrinsically less important: yes, no, or it depends, with a one-line note.
- Main objection or defence: the strongest single challenge to, or justification of, that term.
