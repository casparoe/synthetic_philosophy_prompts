Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated researchers, Ari and Noor, who disagree about how much can be inferred about an optimizer's objective and its search space from its observed behavior. Ari defends the view that, given sufficiently rich observations of the optimizer's choices, adaptations, and failures across different environments and constraints, one can recover a meaningful equivalence class of objectives and at least the effective boundaries of its search space. Noor defends the view that behavior underdetermines the objective/search-space pair: the same trajectory is consistent with many different pairs, and distinctions such as cannot versus does not want, or does not represent versus avoids, are observationally underdetermined unless one already imports strong assumptions about the optimizer's architecture, representation, and computational limits.

Set the dialogue during an evening conversation at a workshop on behavioral inference in AI and economics. The conversation should be a genuine back-and-forth in which each character responds to the other's actual examples, concedes points where appropriate, and sharpens rather than retreats from their position. Neither side should be a strawman. To make the disagreement concrete, the characters should engage with at least two of the following kinds of cases, and may introduce others of their own: a trained network that performs well in-distribution but fails out-of-distribution; a foraging animal whose patch-leaving rule can be rationalized by several different utility functions; a recommender system maximizing a proxy such as watch time; an evolved organism whose behavior looks optimal under one specification of fitness but not under another. The dialogue should not resolve cleanly, but it should leave the reader with a clearer sense of what further evidence would be needed to settle the disagreement.
