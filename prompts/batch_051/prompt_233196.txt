I've been reading John Locke's Second Treatise of Government for a course on property, and I keep getting stuck on a part of the chapter on property that throws me off. I'm genuinely attracted to the labour theory of acquisition: it gives a clean, non-arbitrary answer to how things pass from common to owned without needing the consent of all mankind, and the proviso — leave enough and as good for others, don't let things spoil — feels like it does real work keeping the theory from licensing hoarding. But then I hit this part of section 28, where Locke is arguing that appropriating from the common doesn't depend on everyone's express consent:

"Thus the grass my horse has bit; the turfs my servant has cut; and the ore I have digged in any place, where I have a right to them in common with others, become my property, without the assignation or consent of any body. The labour that was mine, removing them out of that common state they were in, hath fixed my property in them."

My intuitions just crash into this. The appeal of the theory, as I understood it, was that my labour is mine because I own my person and by mixing it with something I make that thing mine. But two of his three examples aren't my labour in that sense at all: the horse does the biting, the servant does the cutting. I have a very firm intuition that if anyone comes to own the turfs by mixing labour, it should be the servant, not me merely because I employ him; and an even firmer intuition that horses dont acquire property rights in grass. So I can't tell whether the passage shows that Locke isn't actually running the mixing-your-own-labour theory I found attractive, or whether my intuitions are just anachronistic and I should get over them, or whether there's a reading on which "mine" does the work and the examples are harmless.

Give me the strongest version of each option: debunk the intuition and keep the theory; amend or abandon the theory because of the counterexamples; or show they're compatible once you parse what "the labour that was mine" could mean. What would justify each move, and which would you actually give up?
