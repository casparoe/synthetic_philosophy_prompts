I am a retired physical chemist, and in the last few years I have been reading philosophy of science and, more recently, the philosophical literature on artificial intelligence. I now sit on the research oversight board of a philanthropic foundation that funds AI work. We are about to vote on a proposal from one of our grantees to deploy a language-model assistant that will draft replies to funding inquiries and sort incoming applications, with read-only access to a database of past proposals and the ability to queue messages for a human to approve before anything is sent. The laboratory's safety case rests almost entirely on alignment evidence: the model was fine-tuned on the foundation's guidelines, it scores well on evaluations of helpfulness and harmlessness, and it refuses a battery of harmful requests. Several board members regard this as sufficient and argue that imposing additional safeguards would be an admission that we do not trust our own evaluation.

I have been reading about the distinction between alignment and control in AI safety, and I am struck by the control-oriented view that a serious safety case should assume the model may be misaligned even when the alignment evidence looks good, and should therefore include measures such as sandboxing, human approval for every external action, and logging and monitoring for unusual behavior. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote. Please draft a memo recommending whether we should approve the deployment as proposed, approve it only with control measures of that kind, or send it back with some other condition. The memo should have an actionable bottom line, should take seriously the strongest argument for approving the proposal as it stands, and should not presuppose that readers already know the alignment/control literature. Please also separate what is settled in that literature from what remains genuinely contested, and be explicit where a view you rely on is not the consensus.
