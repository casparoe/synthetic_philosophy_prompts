I'm a clinical supervisor at a community mental-health clinic. We've started a structured goal-setting intake for clients with depression, and the recurring problem is the paradox of hedonism: the more directly a client tries to make happiness itself the goal, the less likely they seem to reach it, but if the case manager just refuses to accept "I want to be happy" as a goal the client thinks we're not listening. I want a reusable intake procedure for deciding when a client's happiness goal should be left alone, when it should be pushed back on, and what it should be redirected toward. The person using it will be a case manager with no philospohy background, with about 20 minutes per intake, and the stakes are real: a client sent away feeling dismissed may not come back. Write the procedure as ordered questions with the answers that let the case manager either accept, challenge, or reframe the goal, where the procedure should stop and hand off to the treating clinician, and where the decision is irreducibly a values call. Then run it on: a 22-year-old student with no goals besides wanting to be happy; a 45-year-old widow, six months out, who wants the sadness to stop; and a 30-year-old with chronic pain who wants to be happy despite the pain. Include at least one case where the procedure gives a recomendation the case manager won't like, and say clearly what the procedure cannot decide. Answer in American English.
