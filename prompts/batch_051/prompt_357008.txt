I've been trying to pin down what my gut is doing when I call something a real coincidence rather than a base-rate thing I only noticed because I was primed. Here are my verdicts on a pile of cases; I'd like to know if there's a principle that gets all or most of them, and what it says about a case I keep flipping on.

1. I'm at a party with maybe 23 people, and the person I'm talking to has my birthday. We do the small-world laugh and move on. I know the rough half-half fact, so it doesn't feel eerie at all.

2. On a layover in a city I've never visited, the stranger at the gate next to me turns out to have grown up in the same tiny village as my grandmother, and their aunt knew her by name. This one gives me that scalp-prickle. I can't compute the odds without making them up, but it feels like more than chance.

3. A friend texts me about a movie I haven't thought about in years, and I was literally thinking about it five minutes earlier. Weird, but I mostly shrug, because I have stray thoughts about old movies all the time and I only notice the ones that get matched.

4. At around 3am I dream my brother backs into a pole. I text him in the morning, and he says he actually dented the fender pulling out of a driveway that night. This one stops me cold. I know dream hits are guaranteed somewhere across all the people dreaming, but that doesn't dissolve the feeling, not the way it does in case 3.

5. I buy a bright blue hatchback, a color I almost never see, and for the next two weeks they're on every other block. This doesn't feel like a coincidence at all, just my attention snapping into place. But I can't articulate whats different between this and case 2.

6. I read that a roulette wheel somewhere landed on black 26 times in a row. If I had been standing at that table and predicted the whole run in advance, I'd be confident something was wrong with the wheel or with me. But reading that it happened once, somewhere, at some point, feels almost unsurprising. I want a principle that lets both of those be true.

7. A story goes around about a woman whose dog vanished and then showed up at her new house in another state years later, on the birthday of her late husband. I don't believe it as told. I honestly can't tell whether that's because my principle says the event is too unlikely, or just because I don't trust stories that get better every time they're retold.

8. On a video call with strangers, I meet someone with my rare last name, from the same small town, who also has twin daughters named Ada and Alice, which happens to be my twins' names. My reaction is that this cannot be chance. But I have no non-chance mechanism to offer, and saying synchronicity feels like giving up.

So here's the ask. Is there a single principle, or a small set of them, that captures which of these I treat as mere base-rates and which I treat as evidence that something weird is going on? Which of my verdicts, if any, should I give up to make the principle cleaner? And what should I make of the case that keeps bugging me: I glance at a digital clock and catch 11:11 on what feels like an absurd fraction of days. Is that more like case 1, case 4, or case 7?

Also, I'd like the principle to make an explicit, checkable prediction for a new case I haven't listed, ideally one I could actually test against data over a couple years. And tell me where the birthday-paradox reasoning is supposed to fit here. I understand the small-room calculation, but I don't know how to generalize the notion that no particular pair was specified in advance to cases 2, 4, and 8 in a way that isn't just hand-waving.
