I am a novelist. While preparing to write a character who is trying to work out what she owes to people she will never meet, I have been reading moral philosophy, and I keep encountering an argumentative move for which I have no name — at least not a name that feels precise.

The move begins with a small, carefully staged scene. A child is drowning in a shallow pond; the reader is the only person present; the rescue will require nothing worse than a ruined pair of shoes. In the scene, the harm is imminent, the victim is identifiable and visibly before the rescuer, the cost is small and fixed, the causal path from action to saving is direct and certain, and there is no one else to act. From the reader's assent to this scene, the writer extracts a wide principle: considerations of distance, of whether the harm unfolds over years or through institutions, of whether the victims are identifiable, and of whether many others could also act, do not alter the duty to prevent serious harm when one can do so at modest cost. The principle is then applied to matters that share none of the scene's features — chronic and diffuse harms, anonymous beneficiaries, open-ended costs, action mediated through relief organizations, shared responsibility among millions — and the conclusion arrived at is treated as having already been established. When someone resists, the writer returns to the pond and insists that the principle was already conceded there.

I want to know whether this exact pattern has a name, and what the nearest established concepts are. It does not seem to be argument by example, because the scene is not one piece of evidence among many but the sole load-bearing support for a general rule. It does not seem to be a bare appeal to emotion, because the emotion is recruited to secure a principle that is then offered as a rational result. What exactly separates this use of a vivid case from a legitimate illustration, and from an illegitimate substitution of vividness for support? Is it a distinct fallacy or argument form, or does it fit imperfectly under several existing labels? I am less interested in the label than in the carving: the boundaries of the phenomenon, the conditions under which it can be legitimately used, and the specific failure it is most likely to produce. If no established concept fits, I would like a term, a definition, and an account of its edge cases.
