Here are five standard responses to the Liar paradox, each with a one-line summary of its mechanism. Sort them into a tier list from Tier 1 (least vulnerable on the dimension below) to Tier 4 (most vulnerable). The dimension is how well the response does two things at once: (a) preserve the ordinary expressive power of a truth predicate -- e.g., blind ascriptions such as "everything the witness said is true" and reference to open-ended or self-referential sentences -- and (b) avoid generating an equally damaging revenge liar, a new sentence built from the theory's own diagnostic vocabulary that the theory cannot truthfully classify without re-entering the paradox. Use at least three tiers, put no more than two approaches in any one tier, and do not rank on historical influence or exegetical fidelity. For each approach, give one sentence explaining its placement; if you place two approaches in the same tier, add one further sentence saying what they share and what keeps them below the tier above.

Tarskian hierarchy: declares the liar ill-formed by stratifying truth into an ascending hierarchy of languages, so a truth predicate applies only to sentences of a lower level.
Kripkean fixed point: treats truth as a partially defined predicate evaluated by a Strong Kleene scheme; the liar receives no determinate truth value at the least fixed point.
Paraconsistent dialetheism: revises the background logic instead of restricting truth, accepting the liar as both true and false within a paraconsistent setting.
Contextualism: makes the extension of the truth predicate context-sensitive, so a liar sentence is evaluated differently at different stages of a context-shifting chain.
Revision theory: defines truth as a circular concept evaluated by iterated revisions of a hypothetical extension; ordinary truth ascriptions stabilize while the liar fails to settle.
