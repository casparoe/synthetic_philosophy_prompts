I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a article about the decision-theory claim that deliberation crowds out prediction—roughly, that you can't assign probabilities to your own choices because the act of deciding them drives out any forecast of what you'll do. I get the abstract argument, but in practice it feels false. I'm constantly telling myself I'm maybe 80 percent likely to finish a draft by Thursday, or 60 percent likely to say yes to a conference invite, and these are probabilities about my own future actions, and they're useful for planning. So what's the right way to keep doing that while respecting the deliberation point? Or is the real upshot that I shouldn't trust those numbers at all?
