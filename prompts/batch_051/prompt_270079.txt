Consider the following case as a pressure test for Van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Stated precisely, Reflection says that for any proposition H and any value r, your current credence in H, conditional on the claim that your future credence in H will be r, should itself be r. Now suppose an agent knows at noon that at midnight she will ingest a pill that directly sets her credence in H to 0.95 via a brute process stipulated to be independent of H's truth; the pill also prevents her from recognizing the resulting state as drug-induced. H is the proposition that a fair coin, to be tossed after midnight, comes up heads, and the agent knows the coin is fair. The principle appears to require that she already be 0.95 confident in heads at noon.

Please make your reasoning explicit as the following probability estimates: (a) What is the rationally required credence in H for the agent at noon? (b) What is your probability that this case is a genuine counterexample to Reflection as standardly stated, with no caveats? (c) What is your probability that Reflection can be repaired by adding something like a caveat against deferring to a future self known to be unreliable, and remains a substantive rational requirement under that repair? (d) What is your probability that the repaired principle still explains the ordinary rational deference to a more informed future self that Reflection was meant to capture? For each of (a)-(d), give one number and one or two sentences of justification; then explain how they combine into a single overall credence that Reflection, suitably interpreted, is a true requirement of epistemic rationality. Finally, state which of (a)-(d) you are least confident in and describe one new case whose outcome would do the most to move that estimate. Keep your answer to no more than 400 words.
