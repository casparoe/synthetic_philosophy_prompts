I keep comming back to Locke's Second Treatise because it gives such a clean criterion for a lot of political questions: government exists to preserve property, and therefore it can't take any part of a man's property without his consent. In section 140 he ties these together, as I remember it—the supreme power can't take property without consent, and even though governments obviously need support to survive, that support still has to come by the owner's own consent, which he immediately explains as the consent of the majority or of their chosen representatives. On paper this seems rigorous: taxes are forced transfers, and if they aren't consented to they're a kind of theft—maybe not by a lone highwayman, but by an institution acting outside its warrant. And the theory gives me a real tool, not just vibes. When someone says the state needs the money, I can ask when and how I consented, and the theory says if I didn't, the taking is illegitimate.

But I also can't get rid of the intuition that a progressive income tax used to fund schools, or a health service, or courts, is not theft. If my neighbour inherits ten million and pays forty percent, and that money goes to keep a child from starving, I do not feel like I'm watching a robbery. The intuition is firmer than a lot of things I actually believe: I don't just think the tax is tolerable all-things-considered; I think the moral description "theft" is just wrong in that case. So I'm stuck between a theory whose premises seem right and an intuition I can't disown.

I don't want you to tell me "taxation isn't theft, here's why Locke was wrong" or "bite the bullet, it is theft." I want to know what a negotiated verdict looks like. What would it cost to amend or abandon the Lockean view—does the whole consent story collapse, or can it be kept by changing what counts as consent? What would it cost to debunk my intuition—is it just made of feelings about outcomes, or a failure to track where the initial property rights came from? Is there a reading where both survive: for example, that the theory correctly says the state may not take property without consent, but my intuition is correct that most actual taxation isn't theft because most actual taxpayers have consented in some thicker procedural sense? And what would justify each move, rather than just picking one? Please keep it continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables—just write it like an email.
