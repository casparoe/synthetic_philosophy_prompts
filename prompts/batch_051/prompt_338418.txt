So our church (small, old-fashioned, congregational) still runs on a set of bylaws adopted in 1904. There's a clause in there on public prayer, and I'll quote it exactly from the booklet:

"Article IX, Section 3. In all petitions, let the members remember that God already knoweth every want, and that His decrees are fixed from everlasting; prayer is not a means of altering the divine purpose, but a means by which the soul is conformed to it. Neither shall any member petition for a thing contrary to the revealed order of nature, nor heap up petitions thinking to be heard for their number; for the Lord heareth the silent as readily as the loud."

This spring a member (call her Marisol) built a little bot for our online prayer list. It holds a set of templates about her own concerns, her health, her job, absent relatives, and so on, and it generates a fresh petition each hour while she's asleep or at work, drops it on the shared list, and marks it with her name. Some of the templates are for things like "that the earth stand still until I finish my thesis" or "that Tuesday be revoked," which she says are jokes but the bot sends them anyway. She sees it as automating her prayer so the community never stops lifting her up. Others say she's not the one doing the praying; a few say she is, because she set the templates and the machine is just her instrument. The manual predates all of this and says nothing about machines.

I'd like you to tell me whether she's in breach of the clause, and more importantly how three approaches — the words themselves, the purpose behind the clause, and what the 1904 drafters would have said if asked — each come out, and which approach should win here and why. I realize this may snag on what "petition" and "heap up" even mean; i'd rather you go at it precisely than give me a plain-language verdict. One more thing: as you reason, say how confident you are in each main claim and single out the one point you're least sure of.
