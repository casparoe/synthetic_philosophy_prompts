I keep coming across a particular failure mode in attempts to say what causation is, and I'd like to know whether it has a standard name and where its boundaries are. The family of views I have in mind says, roughly: a factor X causes an outcome Y just in case Y would change if one could vary X while holding fixed the other things that matter for Y. But "varying X" cannot mean letting X change in the ordinary course of events, because then whatever normally brings X about would be part of the story; it has to mean setting X surgically, from outside, without disturbing anything except what X itself affects. For instance, to say smoking causes lung cancer would be to say that if someone could make people smoke from outside—without changing diet, genes, or the circumstances that lead people to smoke—their cancer risk would rise. The snag I keep noticing is that when you ask what makes a change "surgical" or "from outside," the answer always falls back into causal vocabulary: such a change is one that is not itself caused by anything else in the system, or that reaches Y only through X. So causation gets explained via these idealized changes, and the changes are in turn picked out by causal criteria. Yet the view does not feel empty; it still seems to constrain causal claims, mainly through which relationships stay invariant under such changes.

I am not looking for the general term "circular definition." I want the specific name, if there is one, for this circularity worry about accounts that tie causation to idealized manipulations, and a statement of how it differs from ordinary definitional circularity and from the older view that causal notions cannot be reduced to non-causal ones at all. If there is no established name, I would want a proposed name that marks the difference between this local circularity and a plain definitional circle.
