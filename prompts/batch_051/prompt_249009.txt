Classical utilitarianism says pleasure and pain are fully commensurable: they sit on one scale, and the right option maximizes total net pleasure minus pain, summed across everyone affected. On this view, any pain can in principle be outweighed by enough aggregate milder pain in enough other people, or by enough pleasure.

Lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, as I will use the term, says three things. First, only pain has intrinsic moral weight; pleasure has no positive intrinsic weight, so an option is not morally better than another solely because it contains more pleasure, and two options equal in pain are morally indifferent. Third, below the threshold, pains remain commensurable (comparable on a single scale) and additive. For concreteness, put pain intensities on a 0-100 scale and set the threshold at 50.
