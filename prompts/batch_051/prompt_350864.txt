In a discussion group I'm in, someone defended the project of using genetic and pharmacological technology to abolish suffering in all sentient life — what they called the hedonistic imperative — almost entirely through two analogies. Suffering, they said, is like smallpox: a scourge to be eradicated, not a part of life to be managed. And once safe mood-controlling biotechnology exists, they added, refusing to use it will be like refusing anesthesia for treatable pain. I suspect the analogies are doing more work than the underlying case can carry. Please audit both analogies: give the correspondence table for each (what maps to the disease, the vaccine, the pain, the anesthetic, the patient, and so on), identify where each analogy breaks and which features of the source it quietly imports that the target lacks, separate the disanalogies that matter for the hedonistic-imperative conclusion from the ones that don't, and say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, along with what would be needed or whether a better analogy exists. Answer as a numbered list with exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
