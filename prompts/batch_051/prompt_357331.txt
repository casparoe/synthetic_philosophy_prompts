I am drafting the take-home final for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Foucault, and I need a single essay question that cannot be answered by reproducing the standard panopticon summary from lecture. The students have worked through the core concepts — disciplinary power as productive rather than merely repressive, normalization, the examination, and the coupling of power and knowledge — and they have read the panopticon material, so the question should force them to apply those concepts to a case they have not seen rather than recite labels. The case I want them to work with is this: a warehouse company equips its pickers with algorithmic wristbands that continuously score them on pace, route efficiency, and idle time; the scores feed a live team dashboard and an automated personal 'coaching' feed of nudges, and the rankings influence shift assignments and layoff order. The company presents the system as neutral productivity coaching. I have been discussing the case with two colleagues: one argues that the wristbands realize Foucault's panopticon more thoroughly than Bentham's prison ever could, while the other argues that they are just old-fashioned command-and-control with better metrics. I want the question to force students to adjudicate that disagreement rather than default to 'surveillance, therefore panopticon.' Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B (and a B from a C) by naming the specific conceptual moves each must contain and the specific confusions that should cost marks. Students will have 72 hours and the essay will be marked out of 100.
