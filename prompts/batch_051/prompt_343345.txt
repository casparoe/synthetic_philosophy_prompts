For a piece on whether physics could actually finish — not just stall out, but reach a final theory — I keep coming back to this argument of mine. (1) A theory can only be known to be final if we can rule out the possibility that some future observation overturns it. (2) But for any theory we actually have, we can always imagine at least one future observation — some anomoly at a new energy scale, say — that would overturn it and that we can't rule out in advance. (3) So for any theory we actually have, we can't rule out that some future observation will overturn it. (4) Therefore we can never know that any theory we actually have is the final one, no matter how well-confirmed it is. This seems right to me but the conclusion feels too strong, so I half-suspect I've smuggled something in. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and on what reading? And if it is valid, what does it actually show about physics — or about the word "know"?
