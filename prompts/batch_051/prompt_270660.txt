I teach moral philosophy, so you'd think I'd have a ready answer for this, but I dont. My sister's husband died about two years ago, and she's been using one of those AI services that turns a dead person's texts and voicemails into a chatbot you can keep talking to. Recently she found his private journal on an old laptop — he was always emphatic that nobody should ever read it — and she's started feeding pieces of it into the model, on the grounds that it makes the simulation more like him. She asked me whether I think there's anything wrong with that. The obvious worry is privacy, but here's what's chewing at me: is there a distinctive wrong in using someone's most private self-writing to generate new things that appear to speak for him, over and above whatever wrong there was in her just reading the journal after he died? Or is that difference only one of degree, not kind? And practically, should I be candid with her about my doubts, or is this inside the latitude a widow has in how she grieves? I keep going in circles, partly because I can already hear my own students calling out the dodge in my usual move here.
