I teach philosophy of language, and I keep noticing a parallel between presupposition accommodation and a feature of hash-table libraries: auto-initialization of absent keys. In conversation, a speaker can utter a sentence whose presupposition is not in the common ground, and hearers normally let it pass by quietly adding the missing content to the conversational score, provided nothing on the score conflicts with it and no one has reason to object. In programming, a dictionary can be set to create a default entry when a missing key is accessed, but only if that default is consistent with the other entries; otherwise the lookup silently corrupts the data. If this is more than a loose analogy, accommodation would be a form of default completion of a shared record under late-arriving commitments, and the felicity conditions on accommodation should track the correctness conditions on default insertion. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? Say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and give at least one respect in which the analogy predicts something false about conversation—for instance, about what silent default insertion can hide.
