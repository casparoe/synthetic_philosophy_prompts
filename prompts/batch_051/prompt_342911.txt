A couple weeks ago I was at a dinner where two friends of mine, Layla and Mateo, got into it about another friend of ours, Naomi, who had recently ended a long friendship by telling the person, gently but firmly, that she did not want to continue the relationship and did not think it would be productive to discuss why. Mateo said Naomi had failed at something basic: when you act on someone in a way they cannot ignore, especially someone you have been close to, you owe them an account of your reasons. Layla said that that's a category mistake. Honesty concerns the truth of what you say, not the amount of access you give other people to your inner life; Naomi did not lie or even mislead, she declared the relationship closed and declined a postmortem, and no one has a general right to that.

Mateo pushed back that being sincere with someone you are leaving is not just not lying, it is making yourself intelligible to them as an agent. Without a reason, he said, the other person cannot tell 'we have drifted apart' from 'I did something unforgivable,' and being left to guess is its own subtle cruelty. Giving a reason treats the person as someone who could learn and respond; unexplained exclusion treats them like an object. Layla answered that a demand for a reason there can be less respectful, not more. Suppose the true reason is 'you dominate every conversation and I find it exhausting': delivering that would be crueler than silence, and a softened version like 'we have grown apart' would not, by Mateo's standards, be real transparency either. Some reasons are also inarticulable, she added: a friendship can simply come to feel wrong, and forcing an account would manufacture a false reason, which is a different kind of insincerity.

Mateo conceded the point about cruel reasons but said it was an exception, not an argument against the default. The default in close relationships, he said, is to make yourself legible, at least about decisions that redirect the other person's life; even when the reason cannot be fully given, you can be transparent about the withholding itself, saying something like 'I cannot say it without hurting you,' and that is still a form of respect. Layla said that just relocates the problem: it treats the other person's need for certainty as a claim on your discretion. No one owns the contents of your mind, 'I don't want to talk about it' is a complete sentence, and a relationship in which closeness requires being legible on demand has already become a surveillance arrangement.
