Something I've been turning over about the old Kosslyn–Pylyshyn debate. Kosslyn's case for depictive mental images leaned hard on inferring format from signature: linear reaction-time slopes in rotation and scanning, activation in retinotopic areas during imagery, and the like. Pylyshyn's reply was that propositional representations plus the subject's tacit knowledge could generate those very signatures, so the behavioural data underdetermine the format. Now we have a development neither of them had in view. We have end-to-end trained vision systems that solve rotation, scanning, and spatial-relation tasks at humanlike levels, but represent space through high-dimensional vectors and learned attention patterns—nothing inside the system that anyone would point to as a picture-like buffer. Suppose one of these systems, when probed the way imagery experimenters probe people, reproduces the same patterns Kosslyn took to be decisive. Does Kosslyn's inference from signature to depictive format survive? Which premise does this actually put pressure on, and is there a repaired version of the depictive claim that still says something true and non-trivial—or should we just drop the 'picture-like' part entirely?
