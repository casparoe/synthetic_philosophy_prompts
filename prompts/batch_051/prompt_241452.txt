I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep encountering what feels like a bias-variance tradeoff in ethical theory. I am genuinely attracted to the methodological view that a good moral theory should be parsimonious: one master principle (say, maximize overall welfare), or at most a few principles, from which verdicts follow, rather than a list of special duties, excusing conditions, and priority rules that looks to me like epicycles or an overfit model. Yet I also have firm, stable intuitions that such simple theories keep misreading: that my obligations to my own child can outweigh those to a stranger, that a promise can bind me in a way that pure consequences do not capture, and that doing harm is importantly different from merely allowing the same harm to occur. A simple theory seems to explain these intuitions away rather than explain them, but my training tells me not to add complexity just to fit messy data. Which should yield: the demand for parsimony, or the conviction that these case-level judgments are data an adequate theory must fit? Or is there a reading on which parsimony and intuitive fit are not actually in competition here? Please walk me through what each option would cost and what would justify choosing it.
