Was in a long forum thread a while ago about whole-brain emulation, and one line kept getting deployed to settle arguments in all directions: "mind is software, brain is just hardware." It did two different jobs. Against people who said an upload would only be a copy, the slogan was supposed to show the upload really is you, because what makes you you is preserved and the biological body is an implementation detail. Against people who said a scan would have to drop some fidelity, it got waved off the same way—the hardware doesn't matter, the way you don't care what brand of server a web app runs on.

But the more I think about it, the more that little sentence seems to be packing together several separable claims: that the brain is a computer in a substantive sense; that mental states just are computational states; that the same computation run on very different hardware is still the same mind; that a scan-and-simulate emulation actually runs the same computation the original brain was running; and that "same mind" is enough for "same person." Could you take that slogan apart into a menu of distinct precise readings, say for each one whether it's a boring truth, a live and debatable claim, or probably just false, and then tell me which reading the people in the thread needed for the identity conclusion and which one they needed for the fidelity conclusion? The thing I most want pinned down is whether there's a version of the slogan that is true but still doesn't get you from "a high-fidelity emulation of my brain exists" to "I survive the upload," and whether the readings people were actually leaning on were the cheap ones.
