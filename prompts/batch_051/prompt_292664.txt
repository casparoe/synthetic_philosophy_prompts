A city whose commemorative plaques have become a standing source of conflict enacts the following procedure for cases in which residents' transmitted memory disagrees with the documentary record.

Any 25 registered voters, each of whom has lived in the city for at least ten years, may jointly file a Memory Claim asserting that a specific public plaque, street name, or official anniversary proclamation misrepresents the past as the community actually remembers it. The claim must state the alternative account and name at least three of the signers who attest that they received that account directly from a member of a preceding generation. Each of the 25 signers pays a filing fee equal to one day's local median wage: refunded to the signers if the claim succeeds, forfeited to the archive's conservation fund if it fails. A three-member Memory Claims Board hears the case: one professional archivist, one history teacher from the city's schools, and one registered voter drawn by lot. Revising the text requires a two-of-three vote, but only the archivist may rule that a piece of documentary evidence is forged or otherwise inadmissible, and a successful revision becomes the presumptive official account in any later dispute about the same event. A successful revision may not be challenged on memory grounds for 25 years. The conflicting documentary version is not destroyed; it is moved to a separate annex of the archive and marked as superseded.

Work through how this system would actually behave. Which conflicts would reach the Board, and which would be screened out by the co-signature, fee, and testimony requirements? How would claimants, signatories, the archivist, the teacher, the randomly drawn voter, the archive staff, local politicians, and journalists adapt once the incentives become clear? Where do the first serious gaming and second-order effects appear, especially around signature-gathering, the archivist's sole gatekeeping over evidence, the two-of-three rule, and the 25-year lock? Then say whether the worst failure modes can be fixed without abandoning what the procedure is for, namely giving community memory a regular institutional path to override the record.
