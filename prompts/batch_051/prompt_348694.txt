I just had a long argument with a friend about Alan Turing’s discussion of extrasensory perception in his 1950 paper on computing machinery and intelligence. His move, as I understand it, was this: if telepathy exists, the imitation game could be confounded because a human interrogator might receive information directly from the hidden human, or a hidden machine could be influenced by psychokinesis. To guard against this, he proposed that the game be run in a room designed to block telepathic influence. My friend argued that this whole discussion is now dead because we have a much better empirical picture of ESP: there is no robust evidence for it, so the premise is simply false and the precautionary room is unnecessary. I think that is too quick. Suppose we accept, for the sake of argument, the current mainstream view that any purported ESP effect, if it existed, would have to be mediated by some physical signal. Then the situation changes: it is no longer a mysterious mind-to-mind channel but simply another potential physical side channel in an experimental setting. Does Turing’s argument survive this shift? Which premise is actually under pressure when we treat ESP as a physical side channel rather than a non-physical faculty, and is there a repaired version of the argument that says something weaker but still important about the need to control for all communication channels in an imitation game?
