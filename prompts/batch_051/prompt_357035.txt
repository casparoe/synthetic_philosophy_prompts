hey, i'm a novelist and I want to write a short story that actually earns Schopenhauer's idea about music instead of just having a melancholic character look at a piano. The idea I mean is the one where all the other arts copy the Platonic Ideas — the permanent blueprints of things — but music skips that and copies the will itself, the blind striving under everything. So music isnt a representation of any particular sorrow or joy; its the raw motion of wanting and resisting and giving way, which is why it can feel totally abstract and also like someone just confessed your own secret to you. I tried drafting it and the first two pages became an essay, so I need the story itself to do the philosophical work.

Could you write me a short story, maybe 1,500 words, where that idea is the engine? I'm picturing a protagonist who's a composer or a sound editor, someone who listens for a living, and over the course of the story they start treating music not as expression but as evidence — evidence of something underneath expression that has no face and no biography. I dont want a cursed-record horror thing. I want the quiet uncanny, like a Chekhov story where the unease comes from realizing the floor isn't what you thought it was. In the weeds: maybe work in the specific weirdness of the bass line mapping onto inert matter and the melody mapping onto the highest grade of the will's objectification, or the claim that music would still be there even if the whole phenomenal world werent. But please let all that live in the story's actual choices — pacing, register, what the character hears in a car idling or a ventilation shaft — rather than in any narrated philosophy. And dont end with a paragraph explaining what just happened. Just show the floor disappearing.
