I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a long-read about the intellectual ancestry of modern design arguments in cosmology. One planned section is a counterfactual portrait: had Isaac Newton been shown contemporary work on cosmic beginnings and the apparent fine-tuning of fundamental constants, what would his theological commitments have led him to say?

For the purposes of this exercise, work from the following working sketch of his actual views, rather than from the popular caricature of Newton as a deist who set a self-running cosmic mechanism in motion. Newton was an anti-Trinitarian Christian who took biblical prophecy and church history as seriously as he took mechanics; he regarded the created order as continuously dependent on God's active power; and he held that gravitation and the long-term stability of the planetary system were not inherent to matter but required divine agency, including the possibility of periodic correction or reformation of the system. He was, in short, closer to a providential interventionist than to a clockmaker's God.

Write a memo of about 800 to 1,000 words for my article which predicts, with appropriate caution, how Newton would have reacted to three modern developments: (1) the claim that the universe had a beginning rather than existing from eternity; (2) the claim that certain constants or initial conditions appear finely tuned for the emergence of life; and (3) the practice of treating design as a probabilistic inference from such tuning. For each development, separate what follows fairly directly from his known commitments from what would require genuine extrapolation, and note any point at which the question itself would have struck him as ill posed. Be explicit about where your confidence is high and where it is low, and do not invent quotations or cite works: reason from the sketch supplied and from general knowledge of his intellectual temperament.
