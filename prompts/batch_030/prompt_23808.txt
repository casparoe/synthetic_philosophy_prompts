I'm part of a graduate reading group that has spent the last few weeks working through the population-ethics material in Parfit's Reasons and Persons, and for next session each of us has to circulate a short statement of our own considered position before the discussion starts. I want to make sure mine actually hangs together before I send it around, since I would rather catch a problem myself than have it pointed out in front of everyone. Here is where I currently stand. (1) I accept the procreation asymmetry: bringing into existence a person whose life would be bad for them, a life not worth living, gives us strong moral reason not to do so, whereas failing to bring into existence a person whose life would be good for them is not bad for anyone and gives us no comparable reason to do so. (2) I hold a person-affecting view of morality: whether an act is right or wrong ultimately depends on whether it is good or bad for some particular person, not on some free-floating impersonal value that isn't anyone's good. (3) I reject the Repugnant Conclusion: a population of many billions of people whose lives are barely worth living is not made better than a much smaller population of extremely flourishing people just by making the first population large enough. (4) In a standard non-identity case, where a couple is medically advised that conceiving this month will produce a child with a lifelong but manageable disability while conceiving three months from now will produce a different, non-disabled child, I think it would be wrong for them to conceive this month, even though the resulting child's life would still be well worth living, and even though no one who will ever actually exist, including that child, is made worse off by their choosing to conceive right away rather than waiting.

For each real tension, say specifically what would have to be revised or abandoned to restore consistency, and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the package.
