I'm a PhD student in cognitive science and serve as the teaching assistant for an undergraduate philosophy of mind course. My discussion section has 16 students and meets once a week for 50 minutes; the room has a shared screen and speakers but no laboratory equipment. My students have not yet been assigned any Dennett, and I'd like to run this session before they acquire his vocabulary, so that they meet the puzzle before they meet the terminology for it.

Design an in-class activity built around one of Dennett's timing puzzles: the color phi phenomenon (a brief red flash followed roughly fifty milliseconds later by a displaced green flash, reported by subjects as continuous motion that switches color partway along a trajectory whose endpoint hadn't occurred yet), or the cutaneous rabbit (taps delivered only at two or three widely separated points on the skin, felt as an evenly spaced series of hops between them). Without supplying the labels, get the students to independently produce something like Dennett's Stalinesque hypothesis (the content is edited -- delayed and doctored, like a tape-delayed broadcast -- before it ever reaches consciousness) and his Orwellian hypothesis (the content is experienced correctly, and the memory of it is overwritten so quickly that every subsequent report reflects only the revision), ideally by forcing them to design a "crucial experiment" that would adjudicate between the two. The activity should let them discover, by failing at that adjudication, that no such experiment could work even in principle, since both hypotheses presuppose a determinate "finish line" at which content becomes conscious -- and nothing in the case, or in the brain, supplies one.

Give me the phases, any roles, and the materials, built only from what the room actually offers; a timeline that fits the 50 minutes with room for debrief; and a debrief that maps each stage of the activity onto the Stalinesque/Orwellian distinction, the finish-line idea, and the multiple-drafts alternative. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this -- where students will resist the roles or land on the wrong lesson (for instance, concluding that consciousness is simply an illusion rather than grasping the narrower point about order and timing) -- and how to redirect them. Finally, be honest about which further objection the activity cannot manufacture on its own -- for instance, the charge that Dennett has defined qualia out of existence rather than explained them -- so that I know what I still have to raise myself.
