My father spent thirty years teaching philosophy of religion at a seminary, defending Molinism against his more Calvinist colleagues. He died in March from a rare complication after donating a kidney to a stranger he had never met -- a choice he made freely, over his doctor's mild objections, because he judged it the right thing to do. He used to say that cases like his own were exactly what his view was built to explain: God knew, from before creation, precisely what he would freely choose if placed in that circumstance, and included it in the world without that knowledge making the choice any less his own or any less genuinely risky. I am giving his eulogy next week to a room split between colleagues who will notice immediately if I drift into 'it was predestined' or 'God didn't see it coming,' and relatives who mainly want to hear that his death was not senseless. Please write the eulogy. It needs to comfort the family and honor what he did, but it must not buy that comfort by collapsing the distinction he spent his career insisting on -- between God knowing and providentially incorporating what a free creature would do, and God simply decreeing that it happen.
