Build me a decision tree that sorts a person's real commitments on the transworld identity / counterpart theory question, using concrete cases as the branch nodes instead of jargon -- no node should just ask "do you accept counterpart theory, y/n," that begs the question. Work the tree from the logical space first: what are the actually live questions here and which combinations of answers to them are even coherent, and only after that hang the named positions onto the branches. Any combination that's logically available but nobody occupies, tell me whether that's because it's incoherent, just deeply unattractive, or genuinely unexplored.

Cases I want doing real diagnostic work somewhere in the tree (add more if the tree needs them, order doesn't matter to me):
- the Hubert Humphrey case: Humphrey lost the 1968 election. Does "Humphrey could've won" require that Humphrey himself, the very same guy, exists somewhere and wins, or just someone sufficiently similar to him -- and does it even matter to what we mean when we say it
- a fission case: some sci-fi duplication or an Everett branch where a world contains two equally good candidates for "being" a given actual person -- what happens to identity there, and does the view have to pick a winner
- an origin case: could this exact person have been born of different parents, could this exact table have originally been built out of different wood -- is shared origin a hard requirement on which possible individuals are even candidates, or just one more similarity that context can outweigh
- a context-shiftiness case: can the very same pair of worlds count as containing "the same person" in one conversation and not in another, depending on what we currently care about (their career vs. their biology, say) -- is that a fine, expected feature or does it show the view isn't tracking anything real
- a bare-difference case: two worlds stipulated to match in every qualitative respect, down to the last property, but you're told they differ in who is who -- does that stipulation mean anything to you or is it empty

The tree needs leaves for at least: Lewis's actual counterpart theory, Kripke-style transworld identity via stipulation/direct reference, Plantinga's transworld identity via individual essences, and Adams-style primitive thisness. Treat haecceitism (whether a bare, non-qualitative difference between worlds is even possible) as its own axis that cuts across the other branches rather than folding it into just one leaf, since more than one of the named positions could in principle take either side of it. For every leaf, name the position and give me one sentence on the single most awkward bullet it has to bite -- not a summary of the view, just the worst thing you're signing up for.
