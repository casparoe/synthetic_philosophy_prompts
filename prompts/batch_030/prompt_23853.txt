For years, in my seminar on rational choice and justice, I have taught the two-party bargaining problem by way of Nash's four axioms. But I have come around to the view that Kalai and Smorodinsky were right to drop independence of irrelevant alternatives in favor of what they call individual monotonicity: it seems to me simply correct that if a change to the situation raises the most one party could conceivably hope to get, while leaving what the other party could at best hope to get unchanged, that first party should never end up worse off than before. I now hold that the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, rather than Nash's, is the right formal model of a fair bargain, and it is the one I reach for when I teach students how contractarians such as Gauthier have tried to derive fairness from rational agreement.

I would like you to make the case against my own view, and to be exacting about it. Tell me what would actually have to be true for my preference to be mistaken, ordering the considerations from those that would be fatal to those that are merely awkward. Address, at minimum: how the solution behaves once more than two parties are at the table; whether any rival set of axioms is at least as well motivated as monotonicity yet recommends a different solution; and whether the experimental literature on how people actually bargain has any real bearing on a claim about what a fair bargain is, or whether such evidence is beside the point for a claim of that kind. Tell me which of these costs a committed defender of the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution would have to concede outright, and which they could turn aside without much trouble. I am also unsure what it would even mean to falsify a preference between two internally consistent formal results rather than an empirical claim, so tell me plainly whether my position is exposed to any genuine risk of being wrong, or whether it has, by its very nature, been placed beyond the reach of the kind of evidence that should worry a substantive position — and if the latter, whether that is a defect in my view or simply a feature of views of this kind. Throughout, state how confident you are in each claim you make, and tell me directly which single point in your answer you are least sure is correct.
