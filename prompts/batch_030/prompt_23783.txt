Over the past few weeks I've been working through the "program equilibrium" literature for an informal reading group on formal approaches to multi-agent cooperation: Tennenholtz's original result that letting players submit source code instead of actions turns essentially any feasible, individually rational payoff profile into an equilibrium of the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, and then the later provability-logic constructions built on top of it — FairBot, PrudentBot, and their kin — which get two mutually transparent agents to cooperate via a fixed point of Löb's theorem rather than by checking each other's source code for literal identity. I've come away holding a fairly strong view: that mutual transparency plus correct formal reasoning is a genuine, freestanding route to cooperation among rational or artificial agents, one that doesn't reduce to repeated play, reputation effects, or third-party enforcement, and that this bears directly on how worried we should be about advanced AI systems failing to cooperate with each other or with us.
