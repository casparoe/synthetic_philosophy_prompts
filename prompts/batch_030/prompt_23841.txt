I keep wanting to use Gattaca as the hook when I teach the reference class problem, but I want to check im not just forcing a pairing. The opening scene is the crux: seconds after Vincent's born, the geneticist reads off his numbers -- 99% probability of a fatal heart disorder, life expectancy 30.2 years -- presented like a flat, precise fact about him specifically. But the reference class problem (Reichenbach's old worry, revived by Hajek) says a single person sits in indefinitely many reference classes at once, each generating a different probability, with no principled fact about which one is "his" -- so what's actually generating that eerily exact 30.2, and is it even coherent for it to be that precise? Then the film's emotional payoff is Vincent "beating the odds" (the swim race, the launch at the end), which plays like a point about individuals resisting statistics, except a 99% prediction already concedes a 1-in-100 exception, so I'm honestly not sure the triumph does the philosophical work the movie wants it to do. Give me a reading that actually engages the probability theory instead of a "stats can't see the person" hand-wave, that's honest about where the film is really dramatizing the reference class problem versus where it's just leaning on a cruder genetic-determinism idea that doesn't need that problem at all, and that tells me what, if anything, the film shows that the Hajek-style arguments alone don't.
