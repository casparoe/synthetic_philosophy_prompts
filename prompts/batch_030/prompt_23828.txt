I write novels for a living, and George Eliot's the patron saint of the whole enterprise for me, so I've spent way too long on what she actually believed once you strip away the "lost her faith, became a humanist" cliff notes version. The real shape of it: she translates Strauss's Life of Jesus in her twenties and Feuerbach's Essence of Christianity in her thirties (the only book she ever put her own name to), and lands somewhere past atheism-plus-shrug -- God and personal immortality go flatly unbelievable to her, but duty doesn't loosen its grip at all, it stays exactly as absolute as it ever was, just unmoored from its old warrant. Her whole fiction career after that is basically the argument that sympathy for actual people can carry what fear of God used to carry, if its trained hard enough -- which for her meant training it through novels.

What I want is that position run against the Essays and Reviews mess, not her own biography, which feels too easy. Quick recap in case its rusty: seven Broad Church writers, six clergy and one layman, publish the book in 1860, applying to scripture the same historical-critical methods the Germans (her Germans) had been using for decades. Two of them, Williams and Wilson, get convicted of heresy in the Court of Arches, then acquitted by the Privy Council in 1864 on grounds so narrow that people joked the Council had "dismissed hell with costs" -- while outside the courtroom tens of thousands signed petitions and the essayists got dubbed the Seven Against Christ. So: map it properly in her terms -- who's playing what role, what does her account say the essayists and the church should've done, and does the sympathy-replaces-dogma story explain how this actually resolved, through a dry legal technicality rather than any positive alternative faith, or is that resolution just invisible to her theory. Then the harder question -- does the sheer scale of the public fury suggest her whole picture only ever worked for people wired like her, solitary and literary and getting their reverence from novels, and never had a real shot at replacing religion for anyone else. Skip the greeting-card summary of her humanism, I want the actual mapping, and an honest account of where it stops working.
