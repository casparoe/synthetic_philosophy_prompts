I mostly write about science and ideas for my outlet, but I also somehow ended up on our newsroom's standards committee, and we've been stuck on the same argument for months. Every few weeks a story needs a slur printed verbatim instead of paraphrased -- a leaked rant from some public figure, a piece on a word's history, a story where someone from the group in question uses a reclaimed version of it about themselves. Right now each case gets litigated fresh in a meeting and everyone leaves annoyed. Half the committee wants a blanket rule: never print the actual word, always paraphrase or use a placeholder like 'the n-word,' becuase the harm of seeing it on the page doesn't disappear just because we're 'only quoting.' The other half says that's cowardly and even dishonest -- if the story is literally about what was said, softening it is a distortion, and treating readers like they can't handle a direct quote is its own kind of condescension. I have to bring an actual draft policy to next weeks meeting, not another 'use your judgment' non-answer, and it needs to take the harm side seriously rather than just steamrolling it with a free-speech line. Can you draft that memo -- a real recommendation with criteria we could actually apply to a specific case, plus the strongest version of the argument against whatever you recommend?
