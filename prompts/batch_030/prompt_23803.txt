Two people in my American-history discussion group argued about Frederick Douglass's reversal on the Constitution, and I want a verdict on the exchange itself, not just the historical question.

Nate opened by noting that Douglass, once a Garrisonian who agreed the Constitution was 'a covenant with death, and an agreement with hell,' announced a 'change of opinion' in 1851 crediting Lysander Spooner and Gerrit Smith -- just as Smith's money was keeping Douglass's paper afloat -- and said the timing makes the reversal look like rationalization. Sofia answered that Douglass wasn't merely asserting the document's silence on slavery but argued clause by clause: the fugitive-slave clause binds only those owing contractual 'service or labour,' which an enslaved person cannot, being 'a simple article of property,' and the three-fifths clause actually reduced, rather than inflated, slave states' representation relative to counting enslaved people as full persons. Nate replied this was already settled law: the Supreme Court had held in Prigg v. Pennsylvania, a decade earlier, that the clause plainly covered fugitive slaves, so Douglass's 'plain reading' rejected the recognized construction rather than reporting it neutrally. Sofia said that's no embarrassment, since Douglass explicitly denied that a court stocked with slaveholders' allies could fix the document's meaning simply by ruling on it. Nate answered that this concedes his point: once correctness requires overriding settled legal reading, Douglass isn't doing the plain, lawyerly interpretation he claimed. Sofia's last word was that Garrison's own reading rests on an equally moral premise -- that any toleration of slavery taints the whole instrument -- so the real question is which moralized default is more defensible, not who was secretly moralizing.

Go through the exchange move by move: say who has the better of each point, whether Nate and Sofia are disagreeing about the same thing throughout, and what a good theory of legal interpretation would need to add to resolve the dispute they're circling.
