ok so I've been chewing on Dennett's Chase-and-Sanborn case from "Quining Qualia" (intuition pump #7, if you want the exact spot) and I want you to build me a new one to spec, not just retell it with different flavor words.

quick recap so we're working from the same page: two coffee tasters at Maxwell House both stop liking the coffee after six years on the job. Chase says the coffee tastes exactly like it always did, he's just gotten pickier -- judgment shifted, quale constant. Sanborn says his own taste-analyzing machinery must've gone haywire, the coffee doesn't taste like it used to even though his standards of what's good haven't moved an inch -- quale shifted, judgment constant. (Dennett actually spells out three possible verdicts for each of them, not just a flat either/or, plus a separate "nostalgia effect" story for why Sanborn might be wrong about his own case -- but the flat version is enough for our purposes here.) Dennett is explicit that this isn't meant as an interpersonal comparison between the two guys, it's two ends of what could happen to ONE person, and the point is that neither Chase nor Sanborn can settle from introspection alone wich story is actually true of themselves -- which is supposed to show the quale/judgment split can't be doing the work qualia-realists need from it.

Here's the catch though: Dennett himself admits both of their stories "depend on the reliability of their memories," since the whole thing spans six years. That's the crack a qualia-defender always pries open -- "fine, memory for raw feels is untrustworthy after six years, so what, that just means don't trust old memories, it doesn't mean qualia are incoherent." I want a new intuition pump with the same irreducible ambiguity, but built so that dodge isn't available -- your subject can't be leaning on trust in a years-old memory to generate the puzzle.

Specs:
- not taste, not smell -- that's just Chase and Sanborn with a mustache on, and both are comparatively simple, chemistry-based senses that are easy to probe with blind tastings and chemical assays, which is probably part of why the original holds up so cleanly
- one subject narrating a change in themselves over real time -- not a comparison across two different people's qualia, that's a different thought experiment
- no surgeons, no neuroscientists rewiring anybody -- something that could plausibly just happen to an ordinary person
- and it actually has to close the memory escape hatch, not just relocate it somewhere your hoping I won't look

Then switch sides for a second: if you were the qualia realist trying to wriggle out of your own case the way people wriggle out of the coffee one, where would you push? Think it through carefully before you answer -- the memory objection is the easy one to dodge on paper, so make sure your fix is doing real work and not just moving the goalposts.
