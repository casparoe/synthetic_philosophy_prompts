Write a dialogue between a two-dimensionalist and a type-B materialist about whether conceivability is a reliable guide to metaphysical possibility, with the zombie argument against physicalism as their test case. The two-dimensionalist holds that the ideal, positive conceivability of a physical duplicate lacking phenomenal consciousness is strong evidence that such a being is metaphysically possible, and uses the distinction between primary and secondary conceivability to explain why this principle does not equally license inferring that water might fail to be H2O. The type-B materialist grants that zombies are conceivable but denies that this shows they are possible, arguing that phenomenal concepts could behave like natural-kind terms in a way that blocks exactly the move the two-dimensionalist is making. Neither side should concede outright; have the two of them work toward identifying the precise point at which their disagreement bottoms out.
