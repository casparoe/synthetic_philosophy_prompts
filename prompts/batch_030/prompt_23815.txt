So my philosophy of religion reading group has started making everyone post their current "package" of views before each meeting so people can poke holes in it, and I've got a feeling mine has a hole in it somewhere but I can't find it myself. Here's what I've got:

1. I'm a real libertarian about free will -- same past, same laws, and a free agent could still genuinely have chosen otherwise.
2. I'm a Molinist about providence: God has middle knowledge of the true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (what any possible free creature would do in any possible situation), these are true logically prior to his decision to create anything, and that's how he gets to providentially plan out history in detail without overriding anyone's freedom.
3. On the grounding objection -- the "what makes it true, before creation, that you'd freely do X in circumstance C, when neither you nor C exist yet" worry -- I'm not moved. Counterfactuals of freedom can just be true, brute, no truthmaker required, end of story.
4. Seperately, I hold a pretty strong Principle of Sufficient Reason: every contingent truth has an explanation, no exceptions. It's basically the engine behind the version of the cosmological argument I find convincing, so it's not something I hold loosely.
5. And I think Plantinga's free will defense actually discharges the logical problem of evil -- all he needs is that it's possible every creaturely essence suffers from transworld depravity, and that's enough to get God off the hook.

I've seen all five of these defended individually by people who clearly know what they're doing, so I sort of assumed the bundle was fine, but nobody's actually made me defend the combination. Can you audit it properly: which of these are genuinely pulling against each other, versus which ones just look like trouble at first glance but actually dissolve once you spell them out? Rank whatever real tensions you find by severity rather than giving me a flat list, and for the worst one, tell me specifically and concretely what I'd have to give up or water down to fix it. I don't want "well it depends" -- I want the actual cheapest fix.
