I'm one of six people on the internal safety review board at a smallish AI research outfit, and we've got a case this week thats split the room. One of our teams wants sign-off to let a deployed agent write and hand control to a successor version of itself - the agent can revise its own policy and architecture and then just defer to whatever the new version decides, with no human re-approving each handoff, as long as an automated proof checker first verifies the successor's decision procedure against a formal safety spec. The team lead is upfront that the naive version of this scheme runs straight into the lobian obstacle: you can't get an agent to certify in advance that whatever an equally powerful successor proves is actually true, without already being able to prove it yourself, which is basically what lob's theorem rules out. Their workaround is to index the certification, so the current agent only vouches for the successor as safe "for the next k steps" or within some bounded resource budget, rather than vouching for the whole open-ended proof system, and they've got a chain of these bounded successors that keeps tiling indefinitely in their model without ever needing a strictly stronger background theory sitting above it. Two of the more mathematically inclined board members think this is just relabeling the regress rather than escaping it, since either the bound keeps shrinking or the real work is happening one meta level up and the teams formalism is just hiding where that is.
