I'm TAing a graduate seminar on quantum foundations this term and got roped into drafting one of the essay questions for the take-home final, on the psi-ontic vs psi-epistemic debate. My worry is that if I just ask "is the quantum state real, discuss" people will dump the Harrigan-Spekkens taxonomy on the page and stop there, or worse, treat "ontic vs epistemic" as basically a relabeling of Copenhagen vs many-worlds, which it isnt. What I actually want is a question that forces engagement with the PBR theorem specifically -- not just "PBR shows psi is ontic" but which of the theorem's premises a committed psi-epistemicist would actually have to deny to keep their view (the realism assumption that there's a determinate hidden state at all, preparation independence, or something else) and what that denial costs them. Bonus points in my head go to whoever brings in the fact that Spekkens' own toy model is psi-epistemic and reproduces a huge chunk of "quintessentially quantum" phenomena but conspicuously not Bell violations or contextuality, and can say something about what that asymmetry is evidence of. Extra bonus to whoever notices that QBism doesn't so much pick a horn of the dilemma as decline to play the ontological-models game PBR is staged in.

So: write me that essay question, worded so a student could reasonably handle it in a 3-hour take-home window (meaty, but not a whole thesis), then a model answer of around 700 words that hits the moves I just described, and then a marking scheme out of 25 points that itemizes exactly what a top answer needs to say versus what a competent-but-not-great answer will say instead -- I don't want vague stuff like "demonstrates deep understanding," I want it cashed out as specific claims about preparation independence, the toy model, and QBism. Throw in a line about the single most common way you'd expect students to botch the toy-model point. No need for a reading list or citations, I've already got the syllabus covered -- just want the question and the grading apparatus.
