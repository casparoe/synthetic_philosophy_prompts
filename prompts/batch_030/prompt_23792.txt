I'm on the academic job market this year, and next month's fly-out includes a job talk built around my dissertation's central chapter. The chapter argues that Crispin Wright's apparatus of entitlement and transmission failure -- built originally to block Moore-style anti-skeptical arguments -- can be redeployed to solve the bootstrapping problem for Pryor-style dogmatism about perceptual justification. My claim is that when a subject runs the familiar track-record argument (White's colored-cards case, say, or Vogel's driver Roxanne, who bootstraps her way to "my gas gauge is reliable" by checking the gauge only against itself), the inference is valid and each individual step is dogmatically justified, but warrant fails to transmit across the argument as a whole, so no new warrant for a claim like "my color vision is reliable" ever gets produced. On my view that's not a cost: the subject was never entitled to demand evidential warrant for that cornerstone claim to begin with, so losing the ability to manufacture it cheaply is no loss.

The department is not a formal-epistemology shop. The epistemologists are mostly internalist-leaning generalists who cut their teeth on the classic foundationalism/coherentism debates, there are two committed process-reliabilists trained in a Goldman-style program who think bootstrapping is simply fine when the source really is reliable, and one formal epistemologist who works on calibration and higher-order evidence and will be running my argument through a Bayesian model in her head whether or not she says so out loud. My advisor is certain the reliabilists will give me the hardest afternoon; privately, I suspect it will be the quiet Bayesian.

For each one, tell me whether the honest move is to concede it outright, concede a narrowed version, or hold the line; give me the reply itself, in roughly the length I could deliver in ninety seconds; and add a candid note on where that reply is thinnest even after I've given it. I don't need page references or a reading list, just the arguments and how to meet them. Close the sheet with the single question you suspect I am quietly hoping nobody in that room thinks to ask.
