I'm teaching a graduate seminar this term that pairs formal epistemology with basic game theory, and this week we covered iterated elimination of dominated strategies, extending the usual pure-strategy treatment to allow domination by mixed strategies as well. A colleague in the economics department mentioned, almost in passing, that the whole point of the classic order-independence result is that it doesn't matter which dominated strategy you eliminate first or how many rounds you take — you always converge on the same surviving profiles. Since that seemed like a clean, general fact, I've been telling my students that once we allow mixed strategies to do the dominating, we can freely eliminate any weakly dominated pure strategy in whatever sequence is pedagogically convenient, confident that the final set of rationalizable strategies will come out the same regardless of the order chosen. Before I repeat this in my lecture notes for next week, I wanted to check: is that actually what the order-independence theorem delivers once weak dominance is in play, or have I conflated it with a different and more restrictive result?
