ok so i teach a small intro ethics seminar (about 18 undergrads, one 75-minute session) and i keep running into the same confusion about incommensurable values -- the kind of thing that comes up constantly on the forums whenever anyone starts talking about decision theory and what you can and can't add up. i want to design an in-class activity that makes people actually feel the difference between commensurable and incommensurable values instead of just having me explain it.

the specific confusion i'm targeting: people treat "incommensurable" like it means either (a) the two options are a tie, so it's a coin flip, or (b) they're just really hard to weigh on a single scale. i want them to feel that incommensurability is neither of those -- you can still have a genuine reason to pick one, and the choice can be non-arbitrary, even though there's no common unit that lets you add the two values together.

here's the mechanism i want to build the game around, and this is the in-the-weeds part: i want the activity to contrast "exchange" vs. "sacrifice." in the commensurable case, giving up a bit of value A to get value B is an exchange -- the A you gave up is preserved, it got converted into B. in the incommensurable case, choosing A over B is a sacrifice -- the B you didn't pick isn't converted into anything, it's just gone. i want the rules set up so that the same underlying preferencs feel genuinely different across the two rounds, and the participants notice the loss on their own before i name it.

so: design me a runnable activity for this group and time -- a game with concrete rules, roles, and some small real stakes (cards, tokens, a shared budget, whatever works, no real money). i want two phases: one where the values are commensurable and you trade, and one where they're incommensurable and you sacrifice. the idea has to show up in what actually happens during the activity, not in a lecture i give after. then a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the exchange/sacrifice distinction, and that explicitly defuses the "incommensurable = tie" and "incommensurable = incomparable" confusions (i.e. makes clear that incommensurable values can still be ranked or have a non-arbitrary choice, and that's a different thing from being literally incomparable).

also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it and how i'd handle it, and be honest about which feature of incommensurability the activity just can't reproduce and i'd have to supply my self. and since this is a design, i want you to be self-critical about it: which of your design choices are actually load-bearing for producing the feeling, and which are you just guessing at -- where do you think this is most likely to fail to actually land, and what would change your mind about a choice you made?
