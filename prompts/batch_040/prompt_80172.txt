A friend who reads a lot of philosophy summarized the pessimistic meta-induction for me like this: 'The PMI is the argument that most past successful scientific theories, like phlogiston, caloric, and the luminiferous ether, were empirically adequate but ontologicaly false, so current successful theories with unobservable entities should probably be regarded as false too. It is basically an inductive argument from track record, and it does not undermine predictive success, only the claim that our best theories literally describe reality. The no-miracles argument is weak because it says that the success of a theory is best explained by its truth, but that just assumes the conclusion, since truth is not a naturalistic mechanism that can explain anything. Structural realism is the smart move, basically the only non-question-begging option, because it keeps the invariant mathematial structure across theory change and gives up the old objects, so it blocks the PMI without begging the question.'

I keep thinking this is mostly fair, but I'm not sure where the weak spots are. Can you audit it piece by piece?
