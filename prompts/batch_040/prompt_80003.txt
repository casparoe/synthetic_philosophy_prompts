I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm working on a feature on whether ant colonies have minds, and I keep getting stuck on the same thing: in every interview I do, people conflate "the colony is an agent" with "the colony has a mind," and I can't tell which one they're actually answering. I want you to build me a decision tree that sorts the positions on "can an ant colony have a mind?" into leaves, where each branch is labeled by the claim that separates it from its siblings, and every node is phrased as a question someone could answer without already knowing which label they're being routed toward. I want the tree built from the logical space first — which combinations of answers are even possible — and then the actual positions located in that space, with the unoccupied combinations noted and a one-line diagnosis of why nobody sits there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

The angle that matters for my piece: the agent-versus-mind split has to be a real branch, not a footnote, because that's where my sources keep changing their minds without noticing. I also want a branch on whether the colony has a self-model — a representation of "the colony" as a single unit — or whether all of its internal bookkeeping is of parts and tasks, because I think that's the hinge on which the whole Aunt Hillary intuition (Hofstadter's 1979 piece where Achilles carries on a conversation with an anthill) either lands or dissolves. Please make sure these leaves appear: the city view (the colony is an artifact or social system, like a city, and any minds are at the individual level, if at all), the neuron view (the ants are neuron-like parts of a colony-level system, and agency and mind, if either is real, live at the colony level), the full Aunt Hillary view (the colony is a mind in the strong sense, a subject of experience, a conversational partner), distributed individualism (each ant is a tiny agent and the colony's behavior is just the swarm of those tiny agents), and the deflationary view (the interesting question is about control and organization, and "mind" is a misnomer at the colony level). Add a sixth leaf if the logical space demands one.

I want the fission case to be an actual node, because I think it's the case that really separates people: if a colony buds and splits into two daughter colonies, is that one mind fissioning, one mind being copied, or two cities merging their suburbs — and somewhere in there, a robot ant that behaves exactly like a real ant and gets accepted into the colony, to test whether the colony's "beliefs" can include something with no inside. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't realy separate rather than papering over it. One last thing: I keep comming back to this "ant fugue" image I have — the colony as a fugue, one theme passed from ant to ant, so that the mind, if there is one, is the fugue itself and not any note — so tell me somewhere in the tree or in a margin note where that metaphor breaks down. Five to seven branch nodes, five to six leaves, and keep it compact enough that I could actually hand it to my editor as a one-pager.
