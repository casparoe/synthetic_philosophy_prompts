Be honest about whether any fix is really satisfying or whether the argument is doing real work and we just hate the conclusion it reaches.

Here's the setup. Let welfare be a number on some scale, with 0 as the line: a life above 0 is worth living, below 0 is not. And take totalism as the background view — a world's value is the sum of its members welfare, so adding a person with positive welfare raises the value. His argument:

(1) Under totalism, adding a person with positive welfare raises the world's value.
(2) The concept "worth living" is vague: there's no fact of the matter about exactly which welfare level counts as worth living. For any life that's clearly worth living, a slightly lower one is still worth living, and for any life that's clearly not, a slightly higher one is still not.
(3) So the set of worth-living welfare levels has no sharp lower bound.
(4) If adding a worth-living person raises value, and there's no sharp cutoff in what counts as worth living, then there's no sharp cutoff in which additions raise value.
(5) So even additions at slightly negative welfare — net suffering — raise value, or at least don't lower it.
(6) Iterate that: add a huge number of net-suffering people and the value climbs without bound.
(7) So a world with a vast population of net-suffering lives beats a small one of very happy people. That's the sadistic RC, and it's absurd.

I'd rather not just bite the bullet and say "there's a sharp line after all," because that feels arbitrary, so I'm hoping the diagnosis doesn't force me there — but tell me what I'd be paying if it does. And please don't just wave your hand and say "totalism is false, move on"; I want to know what rejecting (1) actually buys and costs, compared to rejecting (2) or blocking the move in (4), and which of those you'd actually recommend.
