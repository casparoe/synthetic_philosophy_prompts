I keep hearing 'a forgery is a lie, not just a bad copy' in a museum studies argument about a perfect replica of a destroyed painting, the kind people still call a 'forgery' in the loose sense even though nobody is passing it off. It was made from the artist's own notes, pigments, and workshop records, and it's so exact that, on the table, only the paperwork distinguishes it. It still trades for a fraction of what the original would have fetched. The person using the slogan wants to say the replica is missing something important, not merely that its less rare or less legally protected. Can you separate the different precise claims that one-liner could be making -- about deception, intention, causal history, authorship, institutional status, aesthetic value, or moral status -- and for each say whether it's basically a platitude, actually defensible, or false, and whether that reading would really support the claim that the replica lacks something the original had? I just want the menu of readings with verdicts.
