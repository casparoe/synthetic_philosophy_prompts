I've just finished a long argument with a friend over who bears responsibility for the First World War, and before I re-engage him I'd like a consistency check on the position I've settled into. Here is, roughly, the package I'm working with:

(1) I want to keep two questions distinct: which power's actions most directly caused the war to begin (causal primacy), and which power bears the greatest share of moral blame for it. I think much of the Fischer controversy conflates the two, and I treat them as separate questions that can come out differently.

(2) I think a state, understood as an organized political agency rather than as a mere collection of citizens, can be a genuine bearer of responsibility in its own right, not just a shorthand for the sum of its officials' individual responsibilities.

(3) For the July Crisis of 1914 specifically, I hold that the German government and military leadership carry the largest single share of causal responsibility — the blank check to Austria and the German pressure on Russia were decisive — and on this point I accept the core of Fischer's thesis.

(4) For the longer buildup — the alliance system, the arms race, and the recurring Balkan crises — I hold that responsibility is more evenly spread across the great powers, since that structure was jointly produced and no single power "caused" it.

(5) I think that assigning shares of national blame is a backward-looking, partly retributive practice: legitimate when its aim is learning and deterrence, illegitimate when it licenses collective punishment of ordinary citizens. On that ground I think the war-guilt clause in the Versailles Treaty, as applied to the German population, was a mistake.

(6) I think that moral blame, whether of individuals or of a state as an agency, requires that the agent could have done otherwise in the relevant sense, and I take it that the German decision-makers of July 1914 plainly could have.

What I want is an audit of this specific set, not a survey of the war-guilt literature and not a list of things to read. For each pair of items that is genuinely pulling against the others, tell me how severe the pull is and what I would have to give up to relieve it; and be equally clear about which pairs only look like they're in tension and dissolve once you examine them. Finally, if I were only allowed to revise a single item, which one should it be and why?
