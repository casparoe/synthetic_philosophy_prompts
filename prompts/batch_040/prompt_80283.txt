I'm covering a story for a science-and-ideas mag about why people pay for financial products they instantly regret. A reader wrote in that she recieves a notification every month from a savings app. She put $500 into it, and the app locks the money for a month and automatically renews the lock unless she opts out. If she pulls it out before the month is up, she has to loose a 10 percent penalty. The app also sells a flex pass for $20 a month that lets her avoid the penalty. Every month she opens the app and feels like she might need the money, so she buys the flex pass. She says each purchase seemed fine because $20 is less than the $50 penalty, but after six months she has paid $120 in flex passes, earned almost nothing in interest, and still has the original $500 locked. Can you help me figure out what the actual mistake is, and what I should tell her to do?
