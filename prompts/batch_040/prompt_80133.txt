I've been trying to wrap my head around Sen's liberal paradox — the one where you supposedly can't have a social welfare function that respects even a minimal notion of individual liberty and also obeys the Pareto principle, illustrated with that obscene book and the voyeur and the prude. My friend David, who does social choice theory, was adament that the paradox is a real impossibility theorem, not a trick: the moment you grant each person a guaranteed "personal domain" over some alternatives, that domain can overlap with someone else's, and once you stack the Pareto principle on top you get a cycle and the whole thing blows up. David replied that Ruth was just redefining her way out of it, that the prude's objection was never really about her own action (she doesn't want to read it either) but about the obscene book being read at all, so the overlap is unavoidable and the paradox is robust. Ruth said that "unavoidable" was doing all the work and that David had simply built the overlap into his definition to guarantee the result.
