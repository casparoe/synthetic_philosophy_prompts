On the other side we've got the anti-fundamentalists — the view that there's no such fundamental stratum, that grounding is fully regresive (every fact is grounded in other facts, no ultimate base), and that 'fundamentality' isn't actually a feature of the world but an artifct of how we carve up explanations.

Could you draft the joint statement that these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration? I suspect some of the cruxes are whether grounding has to be well-founded or whether a fully baseless, regressive structure is even coherent, whether 'fundamental' just means 'ungrounded' or whether it's a distinct notion, and whether grounding can be cyclic (can a fact be grounded, directly or indirectly, in itself?) — but I'm not sure those are the real cruxes, so figure out what they actually are.
