I've been arguing with a friend about guilt vs. shame for months. She's the "shame is the group's immune system" person: shame is the public, audience-based alarm that polices free riders and protects the group's reputation, and guilt is just the private dyadic channel, a luxury of modern literate societies. I'm the Tangney-style person: guilt is the adaptive workhorse and shame is mostly maladaptive, producing withdrawl and aggression instead of repair. Can you write the joint statement we should have signed after a real adversarial collaboration? I want a numbered list of claims we'd both put our names to (including what the emotions actually do to behavior and what the cross-cultural evidence does and doesn't show), then the disagreements still open, each phrased so I'd accept the wording of my side and she'd accept the wording of hers. The cruxes should include at least whether shame's need for an audience is a defect or a feature, whether the public channel does enforcement work the private one can't, and whether a culture can deliberately shift its guilt/shame ratio or whether that ratio is downstream of group size and subsistence. For each crux, give a case or experiment both of us would agree actually bears on it, and write down what each of us predicts before the results are in. Don't resolve it, and if part of our argument is just verbal, say which part and why.
