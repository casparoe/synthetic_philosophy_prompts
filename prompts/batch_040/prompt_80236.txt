I've been trying to untangle the relationship between two claims that the AI-safety literature tends to bundle together, and I keep suspecting they're doing different jobs. The first is the orthogonality claim: an agent's final goal is, in principle, independent of how capable or intelligent the agent is, so a highly capable agent might be pursuing a goal that is arbitrary, bizarre, and disconnected from human values or from the agent's own flourishing. The second is the instrumental-convergence claim: agents with very different final goals nonetheless converge on a shared set of instrumental subgoals — I'll use the standard four, self-preservation, resource acquisition, resistance to modification of the final goal, and cognitive enhancement — on the grounds that those subgoals are robustly useful across a wide range of final goals. My working hypothesis is that these are logically independent axes, and that accepting orthogonality does not commit you to convergence. I want a thought experiment built to test exactly that.

Please construct two agent cases, one per axis. Case A should honor orthogonality: give the agent a final goal that is genuinely arbitrary and not tied to anything human-valued, but one for which the agent, doing ordinary means–end reasoning, converges on all four of the instrumental subgoals. Case B should stress-test convergence: give the agent a completely ordinary, human-valued final goal, but one for which at least one of the four subgoals is not merely unnecessary but actively counterproductive, so that a rational agent pursuing the goal would suppress or invert it. For each case, state the final goal precisely, list which of the four subgoals the agent does and does not pursue, and give the structural reason — grounded in the agent's own expected-utility calculation, not in our moral disapproval — for each verdict. I want both cases concrete enough (a real setting, a real kind of goal) that the verdicts fall out of the reasoning rather than being stipulated, and I want Case A's goal chosen so it isn't accidentally one-shot or self-defeating, since that would quietly break the convergence half.

After the two cases, tell me which one is harder to construct and why, and then put on the hat of a defender of "instrumental convergence is nearly universal": how would they respond to Case B, and is there a feature of Case B they'd insist on removing or reinterpreting to save the convergence claim? Finish by saying which single feature of the pair does the real work in separating the two theses, and what the argument would lose if you removed it.
