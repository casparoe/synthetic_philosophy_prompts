So I work in marketing and we just got handed a project to rebuild all our customer segmentation around "generations" — Gen Z, millennials, Gen X, boomers — with different messaging and targeting for each, and the agency keeps acting like these are real buckets with stable personlity traits. But I've started noticing the weird stuff: the boundaries are just arbitrary cutoffs (born in one year is a millennial and the very next year is already Gen Z), and a lot of the "traits" seem to be whatever the media said that cohort was like. Our data folks also keep saying it's basically impossible to tell whether a difference between the groups is because the people are just older, because it's a later era with different conditions for everyone, or because they were genuinely born into a different cohort. So what view of these things does generational segmentation actually presuppose? Does it need generations to be real kinds with some shared essence, or is it compatible with them being arbitrary partitions that just happen to correlate with a few things? Where does the practice quietly assume the categories were discovered rather than invented (we invented the labels, after all), and is it even consistent with what demographers say about age vs. era vs. cohort? And what would we have to change if we swapped the real-kinds view for the view that generations are just convenient gerrymanders? Please end with a last line that's a one-sentence summary of your overall verdict.
