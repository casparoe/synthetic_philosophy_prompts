I've been arguing with a friend about whether the 2011 Libya intervention was a mistake, and we kept circling the same point, wich is that everyone reaches for the moral side — was it justified, did it work — but I think the aftermath was decided by something else entirely. My working hypothesis is that what separates Kosovo, Iraq 2003, and Libya is what the target's state machine looked like before the intervention: a state that was still functioning and administering (Kosovo), a state whose buildings and forms were left standing but whose people were purged out (Iraq), and a state the ruler had deliberately kept hollow and run through personal and tribal loyalties instead of institutions (Libya). I teach a small seminar on intervention and state-building — about 22 grad students, one 75 minute session — and I'd like a game that makes them feel that difference rather than hear me explain it.

I want the three targets to be abstract at first — three tables or "villages" with different starting conditions — and the real cases to only be named in the debrief. The intervention phase should be identical across the three, so the different aftermaths come from the substrate, not from different interventions. Give me the rules, a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money, a phase-by-phase timeline that fits 75 minutes, and a debrief that connects what happened at each table to the three cases. I also want a prediction of where this will fall apart on the first run, and an honest note on which feature of the actual cases the game can't capture and I'll have to supply myself. Keep the whole design under 800 words — if you have to cut something to fit, cut the timeline and just give me the phases.
