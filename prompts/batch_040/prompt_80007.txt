I am preparing a short paper on value alignment and would like a consistency check on the package of views I have landed on before I defend it in a seminar. Here are my commitments, roughly as I hold them:

(1) The right target for aligning an advanced AI with a human user is the user's coherent extrapolated volition — roughly, what the user would want if they thought about it more, better, and faster.

(2) The idealised self is best constructed by a reflective-equilibrium-style process: begin from the user's actual current preferences and iteratively revise them under conditions of full information, unimpaired rationality, and freedom from cognitive bias, until a stable fixed point is reached.

(3) This idealisation process is path-independent: the order in which biases are removed and pieces of information are added does not affect the final fixed point.

(4) The AI should implement the idealised preferences even when they conflict with the user's actual current preferences; the user's 'deeper' self trumps their 'shallower' self.

(5) The user's actual current preferences have genuine normative weight in their own right, not merely as inputs to the idealisation process.

I am not sure these hang together, and I do not want to lead you, so please judge the set on its own merits. Audit it: identify which commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, rank those tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give — the cheapest revision and what it costs. Also flag any pair that only looks like it is in tension but dissolves on inspection, and explain why it dissolves. Do not treat all the pairs as equally problematic. And if the commitment that has to give is one I would be reluctant to drop, say so plainly rather than softening the point.
