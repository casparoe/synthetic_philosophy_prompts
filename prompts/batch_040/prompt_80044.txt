ive been stuck on this for weeks and could use a second pair of eyes before i lock in my positon for the dissertation chapter. im a 3rd year value theory student and here is the package im currently defending on the wrong kind of reasons problem:

(1) I hold a betterness account of reasons: a fact F is a reason for an agent S to φ just in case F would make it better, all things considered, for S to φ.

(2) I think wrong kind reasons are real for belief. "Believing my team will win would make me enjoy the season more" is a genuine reason for me to believe they will win, even though it does nothing to make them more likely to win. So "reason to believe p" is a wider notion than "reason for p to be true."

(3) But I do not think wrong kind reasons can exist for value. If F is a reason to value X, then F is a reason for X to be valuable. There is no gap between a reason to value X and a reason for X to be valuable.

(4) My explanation for (2) is that "reason to believe" is just a practical notion: it tracks the consequences of having the belief, and practical reasons are allowed to point at those consequences. There is no separate epistemic relation doing the work.

(5) I also think reasons for attitudes are uniform: the way reasons work for "believe p" is the same as the way they work for "value X" (or "desire X"), because all of these are just attitudes and the normative structure of "reasons for S to have attitude A toward O" does not depend on which attitude A is.
