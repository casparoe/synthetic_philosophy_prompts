I'm TAing a philosophy of mind section and I have a stack of short answers to grade. The question I asked was:

State the zombie (conceivability) argument against physicalism, identify which premise physicalists most often attack, and explain why, if the argument succeeds, it shows physicalism is false rather than merely unproven.

Here's one student's answer, more or less verbatum:

Physicalism says that everything that exists is, in the end, physical, or at least that everything supervenes on the physical. The zombie argument is an argument against it. Consider a philosophical zombie: a being that is physically exactly like a conscious human, same brain and same behavior, but with no inner experience, no subjective character at all. The argument goes: (1) such a zombie is conceivable, we can coherently imagine a physically complete being with no phenomenal consciousness; (2) whatever is conceivable is logically possible; (3) if zombies are logically possible then physicalism is false, because in a zombie world the physical is fully in place yet consciousness is missing, so the physical does not entail consciousness. Given (1), physicalism goes down. The premise physicalists attack is (2), the jump from conceivability to logical possibility. And if the argument works it shows physicalism is false, not just unproven, because it gives us a possible world that is a counterexample to the claim that everything supervenes on the physical.
