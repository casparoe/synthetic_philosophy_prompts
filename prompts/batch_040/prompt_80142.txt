I'm doing the Q&A after a talk at a small conference this spring, and the speaker is a philosopher who defends Molinism — the view that God has a kind of middle knowledge of what free creatures would do in any given set of circumstances, sitting logically between his natural knowlege of necessary truths and his free knowledge of what actually happens once he decrees to create a world. He's the type who gets really comfortable with that threefold division and can defend it for a long time. I don't want a set of questions that just asks him to restate the framework. What I'd like is seven or eight questions that go after the genuinely hard spots, and for each one a short follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. At least one question needs to land on the granularity problem: for a counterfactual of freedom to be true or false, the circumstance has to be specified fine enough to fix the outcome, but if it's that fine it looks like the agent's action is already determined by the circumstance, whereas if it's not that fine the counterfactual is just indeterminate. I also want one that presses on whether the truth of these counterfactuals is grounded in the creature's transworld properties (and whether that undercuts libertarian freedom) or is just an ungrounded brute fact. And one more that hits the free will defense head on: if the counterfactuals are fixed independently of God's will, in what sense does Molinism actually solve the problem of evil rather than just relocating it? If you can make the questions build on each other a little instead of being a flat list, that'd be great.
