I run a small podcast about decision theory and epistemology, and next month my guest is a philosopher who defends Jeffrey conditionalization as the right way to update when your evidence is only partially believable (not the textbook "condition on E" move, where E is taken as certain). If you dont know the rule: you pick a finite partition of the logical space, assign new credences to the cells, and then each unobserved proposition gets the credence its old conditional credences on the cells give as a weighted average, with the new cell credences as the weights. I've read the same LessWrong-era take on this a hundred times and the usual defenses are all memorized, so I want questions that make him do real work. Write me six to eight questions, in American English, with a follow-up for each one keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. At minimum I need: (1) a question where he has to build a toy example himself, live, showing that the updated credence on some proposition can depend on which partition you feed the rule, even though the new credences over the cells are the same in both runs, and the follow-up should press on why that partition sensitivity is a real problem for a rule that's supposed to be a norm rather than just a calculation; (2) a question anticipating his expected reply that the partition is supposed to be the set of hypotheses your evidence actually speaks to, and the follow-up should ask what in the evidence itself picks out that partition, as opposed to an arbitrary refinement of it; (3) a question on whether any refinement-invariance style fix is even on the table, and what the rule gives up if it is. Keep the questions pointed but fair, and dont let any of them be answerable by restating the rule.
