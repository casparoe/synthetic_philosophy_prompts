I've been going in circles on this for like two weeks and I think I've built myself a trap. It's about whether reasons exist on there own, like, even if nobody actually has them or ever thinks of them. The thing that started it: imagine you're about to step off a pier and the wood under you is rotten but you have no idea. I want to say there's a reason not to step off, even though you have zero reasons in your head. The reason is out there in the world, just floating, waiting for someone to find it. I like that idea. But then I tried to make it into a real argument and it ended up proving something I can't accept. Here's what I got:

(1) A reason to do X is just a fact that makes X the thing to do.
(2) Facts don't care whether anyone knows them. The pier was rotten whether or not I looked.
(3) So reasons don't care whether anyone has them either.
(4) So there are reasons that nobody has — reasons just floating in the world.
(5) And since there are basically infinitely many facts out there, there are infinitely many reasons.
(6) So for literally anything you do, there's some reason you could have done it — some fact that makes it the thing to do.
(7) So "I did that for no reason" is never actually true.
(8) So there's no such thing as acting on a whim, or making a choice for no reason at all.
(9) But I clearly act on whims. I picked a restaurant last week because the sign was a color I liked. That's not a reason.

I know (4) is the part I want to keep — that's the the whole point I was going for, that reasons can be out there even if I don't see them. And I'm 100% sure (9) is true. So somewhere between (4) and (9) I lost the plot. Which step do you reject, and what do I have to give up if I reject it? I'd rather keep (4) and (9) than anything else.
