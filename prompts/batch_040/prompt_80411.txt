State your credence that a German state in the early 1930s would have come to possess the following bundle of features — a racially defined one-party state, the total mobilization of the population for war, the systematic state-directed extermination of groups defined by race, and the concentration of political authority in a single leader — even if Adolf Hitler had never risen to power (assume he was removed from the political scene before 1930 and no figure with an equivalent combination of rhetorical, organizational, and ideological influence emerged in his place). I do not want "it depends on what you mean by the features" — I want a number and a decomposition into sub-questions: (i) the probability that the structural conditions of the late Weimar Republic (the constraints of the Treaty of Versailles, the economic collapse after 1929, the fragmentation of the party system, and the anti-republican orientation of the military, the judiciary, and the industrial elite) were sufficient to produce a state with that bundle of features, conditional on no equivalent figure to Hitler emerging; (ii) the probability that at least one of the features in the bundle is such that it required a leader with Hitler's specific combination of traits (his rhetorical style, his party organization built around the SA and the NSDAP's local cells, his specific ideological commitments) and would not have been produced by any other plausible leader of the period; (iii) the probability that the specific sequence of events from 1930 to 1934 (the failure of the political strategy after the Beer Hall Putsch, the march-through-the-institutions strategy, the passage of the Enabling Act, and the consolidation of power after the Night of the Long Knives) was such that a different sequence, even under the same structural conditions, would have produced a state with a materially different bundle of features. Assign a rough probability to each, say how they combine, and tell me which of the three sub-questions your final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move that sub-question the most.
