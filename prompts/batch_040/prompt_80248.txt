(1) i think akrasia is real irrationality, not just weakness of will. when i know i should do x and i do y, my preferences are incoherent in the same way a set of beliefs can be incoherent — it's not that i was "weak," i failed a coherence requirement.

(2) i think hyperbolic discounting is the right descriptive model of how i actually time my preferences: the closer a reward is, the more heavily i weight it, and this makes me dynamically inconsistent — the ranking i give today flips when the choice point actually arrives.

(3) the "me" who should be in charge is the long-horizon patient self; the impulsive near-term self is like a craving — real, but not authoritative. so when they conflict i defer to the patient self.

(4) precommitment is good and i should lean on it: auto-enroll in the savings plan, delete the app, lock the drawer. i'm not naive about myself — i know i'll want to cave later — so i bind myself now, because the future me at the moment of temptation is the impulsive self from (3), not the one who gets a vote.

(5) but a commitment only counts if the future me, at the moment i want to break it, would — if i were actually thinking clearly — consent to staying bound. if my future self, calmly and with full information, decides the commitment was a mistake, i should let it break the commitment. the future me has a claim against me.

do these hang together? tell me which pairs are really in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is for each. rank them by severity and say what would have to give in each case. i don't want a survey of the literature or a reading list, just an audit of this specific set.
