In a seminar someone laid this out as a refutation of Mencius and i cant find where it goes wrong. Xunzi's argument: (1) If human nature were good, there would be no need for the sage-kings, no need for ritual and moral training — goodness would run on its own. (2) In fact there is a need for the sage-kings and for ritual; people have to be deliberately bent and corrected to get them to do what's right. (3) So human nature is not good. (4) The standard Mencian reply is to define nature as what is inborn and requires no contrivance, and point to the sprouts — the inborn stirrings of pity, shame, deference and approval — so on that definition human nature is good, and Xunzi's argument no longer applies. (5) But Xunzi can just say that what people naturally do without instruction — follow their appetites and perversity — is equally inborn and requires no contrivance, so the definition of nature was quietly swapped. (6) Therefore the Mencian definition of nature is doing all the work, and the argument refutes Mencius as he actually argues.
