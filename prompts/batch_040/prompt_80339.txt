Act like a working philosopher of science who really knows the foundations of causation — someone at home in both the counterfactual tradition (Lewis and the people who followed him) and the interventionist / structural-causal-model tradition (Pearl, Woodward). I'm a philosophy prof putting together a grad seminar on the metaphisics of causation, and I've been asked to write down my own "causal profile" so the students can take a swing at it. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I'd like a consistency check:

(1) Causation is, at bottom, a matter of counterfactual dependence. X is a cause of Y just in case Y would not have occurred, or would have been otherwise, had X not occurred. And I mean this in the clean deterministic sense — not the later probabilistic "causal influence" variant where a cause just has to make some difference to the probability of the effect.

(2) I also think the causal claims we actually care about in the special sciences should be invariant under intervention. A genuine causal claim should survive "what would happen if I manipulated X?" testing, and I trust the do-operator / structural-causal-model machinery as the right way to make that precise.

(3) Preemption and overdetermination are genuine causal cases. In the standard preemption case (two soldiers, one throws first and kills, the other was going to throw but would have been too late), the preempting soldier really does cause the death. And in overdetermination (two independent triggers, each by itself enough to blow up the bomb), each trigger really does cause the explosion.

(4) I'm skeptical of probability-raising as a criterion for causation. I don't want to define a cause as "something that raises the probability of its effect," because I think that both misses genuine causes that lower the probability and picks up spurious ones.

(5) Causal structure is identifiable from interventional data. Give me enough well-chosen interventions and I can pin down the full causal structure — the directed graph — and I think that's basicly all there is to the causal structure.

(6) Methodologically I prefer the structural-causal-model / do-calculus framework as my working formalism, and I'd like to think of counterfactuals as a bookkeeping device that, in principle, should be eliminable in favor of interventions.
