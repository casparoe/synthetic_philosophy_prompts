Im on the ethics committee of a regional public health authority and we're reviewing a draft policy for allocating a scarce vaccine. The draft uses a prioritarian decision rule: the vaccine goes to the candidate with the largest priority-weighted benefit, where the benefit (in QALYs) is multiplied by a weight that gets bigger the worse the candidate's baseline health is — the idea being that a given gain counts for more when it lands on someone who is already doing poorly. A contrarian member of the committee presented the following argument at the last meeting and I still cant find the crack in it:

(1) Read as a value function, the rule says an outcome's worth is V = sum over people of w(x_i) * x_i, where x_i is that person's well-being and w is strictly decreasing.
(2) Compare two outcomes for the same two people: A, where person 1 is at 10 and person 2 is at 20, and B, where person 1 is at 10 and person 2 is at 10.
(3) B is obtained from A purely by leveling person 2 down from 20 to 10; nobody goes up.
(4) V(A) = 10*w(10) + 20*w(20).
(5) V(B) = 10*w(10) + 10*w(10) = 20*w(10).
(6) w is decreasing, so w(10) > w(20).
(7) For any steep enough decreasing w (e.g. w(x) = 1/x^2), V(B) > V(A).
(8) So the rule ranks B above A.
(9) But B is not better than A: person 2 is worse off and nobody is better off.
(10) So the rule is incoherent and we should drop the prioritarian weighting.

I keep wanting to agree with (9) and reject (8), but I can't point to the exact step where the reasoning breaks, and I don't want to just wave the standard leveling-down objection at it since I have read that and am not sure it lands on this exact formulation. Could you walk through where this goes wrong? Please (a) name the specific step or premise that does the damage, (b) explain why it looked fine on a first reading, and (c) say what we would actually have to give up if we rejected it, and whether the policy's real rationale survives. You are an expert in axiology and the philosophy of public policy; think carefully and work through the steps one by one rather than giving me a generic summary of the debate.
