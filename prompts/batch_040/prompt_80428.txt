I am taking an advanced course in epistemic game theory and have been trying to write down my own position on what common knowledge of rationality can do before a seminar discussion. I was discussing the issue with a friend who thinks I am smuggling in equilibrium selection, and I am not sure whether my commitments already do that. Here is the package I currently hold. First, I think common knowledge of rationality is best represented as an event in a finite model of agents' beliefs, and that this representation captures the hierarchy of knowing that the others are rational, knowing that they know, and so on, without requiring that any agent can verify the whole hierarchy from the inside. Second, I think that, for normal-form games, the epistemic content of common knowledge of rationality is exactly rationalizability: the strategies that survive iterated elimination of strategies that are never best responses to any belief. Third, I think that in a finite game with a unique Nash equilibrium, common knowledge of the game form plus common knowledge of rationality is sufficient to predict that the equilibrium will be played. Fourth, I accept Aumann's agreement theorem, on the understanding that two agents with a common prior cannot have common knowledge of different posterior probabilities about the same event. Fifth, I think this is compatible with players in a coordination game having different posterior beliefs about which equilibrium will be played, because their posterior probabilities themselves need not be common knowledge. Sixth, I think allowing for small probabilities of mistake should not change the set of rationalizable strategies, because a strategy that is a best response to some belief remains a best response to some nearby belief once trembles are added.

I would like an audit of this set rather than a general lecture on the standard material. Please tell me which commitments are jointly tenable, which are in genuine tension, and which are only apparently in tension. I have a vague worry that I may be treating common knowledge of rationality as if it were a stronger equilibrium-selection principle than I intend, but I cannot say which commitment is doing that work. For each genuine tension, rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to be revised, and indicate the cheapest revision and what it costs. I do not know which commitment I should be least attached to, so please do not assume that I am most willing to drop any particular one.
