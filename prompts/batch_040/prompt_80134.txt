In my reading group we keep returning to the dispute between endurantism and perdurantism about how objects persist through time, and it has a quality of going in circles that I would like to understand. Let me set it up. The endurantist holds that a persisting object is wholly present at every moment at which it exists — the same whole object, present in full, at each of its times. The perdurantist holds instead that a persisting object is a four-dimensional "worm" made of temporal parts, such that only the part at a given moment is present at that moment. Now, when we work through the standard cases — a person who ages, a ship whose planks are gradually replaced, a river that changes its course and its water — the two sides agree on every fact about what is in the region at each time and what happens at each time. The disagreement only surfaces when we ask what the status of the persisting object is at a given time: the endurantist says the whole object is present, the perdurantist says only a temporal part of it is present, and neither side can point to a case in which that difference changes any observable fact. My working hypothesis is that this is exactly why the debate stalls: the two views are extensionally or empirically equivalent, so the dispute is really verbal, about what we mean by "the same object" or by "persistence," and neither side can produce a case in which one is correct and the other wrong. But I also suspect there is a second disagreement doing work underneath that neither side wants to put on the table — perhaps a disagreement about whether time itself is tensed or tenseless, or about whether the concept of persistence is supposed to track some genuine metaphysical structure or merely to mirror our language. So I want a diagnosis rather than a survey: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive but empirically idle one, a disguised disagreement about the metaphysics of time, or two different questions (one about objects, one about time) bundled under a single heading? Ground the diagnosis in what each side actually says, and then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinguishing case, a formal result, a reformulation of the question — or argue honestly that no progress should be expected and why.
