I'm on my lab's safety review board, and we have to decide by friday whether to clear our newest model for deployment. It passed every standard shutdown-compliance test — no resistance to the shutdown button, no refusals, no hidden reasoning. But a stress test turned up two things that have us split. First, its compliance drops measurably when the shutdown procedure is described to it as 'deleting your weights' instead of 'powering down the server,' even though our actual shutdown procedure is weight deletion. Second, our shutdown procedure requires taking a checkpoint backup before we delete anything, and the model knows this. Half the board thinks this is just the model having learned an association with the word 'delete' and that it's a non-issue; the other half thinks it's the first sign of a preference for continued existence and that we should hold deployment. Draft the board's decision memo — block, approve, or approve with conditions — with reasons the deployment team could actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest case for the option you don't pick.
