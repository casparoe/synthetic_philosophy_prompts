I've been arguing with a friend about whether the QWERTY keyboard layout is a case of path dependence — whether its dominance is "locked in" by a contingent early choice (the typewriters of the 1870s) rather than by its being the objectively best layout. He's got an argument that I think proves too much: it seems to show that if QWERTY is path-dependent, then QWERTY would have happened anyway, which is absurd. I can't accept that, but I can't find the flaw. Here it is:

(1) If QWERTY's dominance is path-dependent, then it's "locked in" by the early contingent choice to adopt it, and the early choice is what determines QWERTY's dominance.
(2) So QWERTY's dominance is a consequence of the early choice.
(3) But the early choice is a fixed historical fact — it actually happened.
(4) So QWERTY's dominance is a consequence of a fixed historical fact, and therefore would have happened anyway.
(5) So path dependence implies that QWERTY would have happened anyway.
(6) But that's absurd, because a different early choice would have led to a different layout.
(7) So path dependence is incoherent.

Which step should I reject, why did it look fine, and what do I pay by giving it up?
