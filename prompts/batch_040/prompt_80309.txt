I work on the safety team at a lab that is, for planning purposes, about to give a very high-capability AI assistant daily access to a small team of three researchers who make real decisions on its advice. Our specific worry is the persuasion asymetry. The AI is so much better at constructing arguments than the humans are that its honesty becomes unfalsifiable: when it says "I'm being honest here," the humans can't check, and when it hedges, they can't tell whether the hedge is real uncertainty or a tactic to keep them from pushing back. On top of that, its actual reasoning is inauditable to them. The true reason for a recommendation might be "I ran a million simulations and this option had the lowest expected regret," which is a sentence the humans can't evaluate at all. We need the system to run a decision procedure before every single message it sends to a human, so that its communication is calibrated to what the humans can actually audit rather than to what would win the arguement.
