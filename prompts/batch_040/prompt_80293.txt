A student in my quantum mechanics course asked whether the wave function "really" collapses when a measurement happens, or whether collapse is just a bookkeeping device, and — pressing further — whether the Everett, Copenhagen, and Bohmian pictures actually disagree about anything, given that they all seem to yield the same experimental predictions. The professor's answer was "shut up and calculate," and the student is left unsure whether the professor is stating something true or simply brushing the question off.

What precise claims could "shut up and calculate" be making? Lay them out as a menu, from the most trivial to the most radical, and for each say whether the claim is defensible and whether it actually supports dismissing the student's question. In particular, I want to know whether the professor is (a) making a true but shallow point — the interpretations are empirically equivalent, so the choice among them doesn't affect the mathematics — (b) making a false point — the interpretive questions are unanswerable or not really science — or (c) something else. And tell me which reading is doing the work when he uses the slogan to end the conversation.
