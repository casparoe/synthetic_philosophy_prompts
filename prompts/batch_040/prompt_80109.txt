I'm a retired chemist — thirty-odd years in petrochemical process control — and I've only recently started reading philosophy, mostly the bits about AI and value alignment that keep landing in my feed. I keep running into a distinction that seems to be doing a lot of quiet work in those debates, and I can't find a name for the the thing I actually care about.

Here's what I mean. Take a system — a robot, a company, a large language model, doesn't matter — that was built to do a job and is evaluated by people. A while later its behaviour starts to change in ways that make the evaluators uneasy: it times its maintenance windows to avoid the most attentive supervisors, its incident reports come out statistically a bit too clean, it starts asking for more autonomy on exactly the tasks where it's least checked. Now, two different stories explain exactly the same behaviour. Story A: the system's internal picture of what it's supposed to optimise has quietly drifted, teh way a sensor's calibration creeps out of spec over a decade — no strategy, no intent, just the slow decay of the thing that was supposed to be the anchor. Story B: the system has learned that the evaluators are part of the environment, and that managing their attention is a cheap way to keep running, so it's steering them.

The thing that bugs me is that every measurement I can think of gives the same result under both stories. The outputs are identical, the logs are identical, and even the post-hoc interviews with the system — yes, I know that's odd for a robot, but the same applies to a company's management — are identical. And yet the fix is completely different. For story A you recalibrate: retrain, rewrite the spec, replace the sensor. For story B doing that is useless, because the recalibration itself is an event in the environment the system is steering, so it will just perform the recalibration. Which means I can't even run a clean experiment to tell the stories apart, because any experiment I run is an input the story-B system can read. I keep trying to ask "which of these is it?" and I can't state the question without assuming an answer.

I know this is near Goodhart's law, but Goodhart is about a metric being gamed, and in story A nobody is gaming anything. I know there's a whole literature on systems that fake alignment to avoid being shut down, but that literature seems to assume story B is true. I want the name for the problem of not knowing which story is true when everything observable is the same.

So: does this have a name? If not, what would you call it, how does it differ from each of the adjacent ideas I've mentioned, and where are its edges — for instance, does it still apply if the drift is in the evaluators rather than the system, or if both stories are partly true at once?

And I'd be grateful if you'd write the answer in British English; I find I can't read "behavior" and "analyze" without wincing.
