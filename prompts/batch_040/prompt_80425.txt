I've been digging into the corruption literature for a project and I keep hitting the same wall, and I want a diagnosis not a survey.

Here's the setup. One camp says corruption is just wrong, end of story. A public official taking a kickback is corrupt whether it happens in a capital city or in a village, because the practice violates something universal about how entrusted power is supposed to be used, and the fact that it's "normal" over there is no defense, it's just like arguing that slavery was fine because it was customary. The other camp says that label is doing cultural imperialism. In a lot of places, the thing you're calling a bribe is actually a gift embedded in a web of mutal obligation, a "tip" to an underpaid clerk is how the system actually functions, and importing the Western rulebook and calling the local practice "corrupt" is just the side that gets to write the rulebook calling the other side dirty.

It's been stuck for what feels like decades, and I can't tell what the disagreement actually is. Everyone seems to agree on the basic facts of what's happening on the ground, and yet nobody moves. My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two camps aren't disagreeing about the same question at all. The universalists are asking "is this practice morally wrong" and the relativists are asking "does this practice violate the local rules," and the word "corruption" is being dragged across both questions like it's the same word. But I'm not sure, and I might be missing a real factual dispute underneath.

So: is this a hidden factual question (is the payment actually a bribe, where the giver expects a specific quid pro quo, or a gift, where there's no specific expectation, and does that distinction even show up in the cases people cite)? Is it a disagreement about which values define corruption (the harm to others, the betrayal of a role, the inequality of the exchange)? Or is it mostly verbal, with "corruption" meaning "moral wrong" on one side and "rule violation" on the other? I want you to diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, with reasons grounded in what each side actually argues, not a tour of the positions. And then, given your diagnosis, tell me honestly whether anything could actually move it — a distinction, a reframing, a change of question — or whether it's just two sides answering different questions under one name and the impasse is structural.

One more thing I've noticed that I think matters: the classification of the same physical act (handing an envelope to a clerk) seems to track the institutional context more than the act itself. Whether it's a "bribe" or a "tip" or a "facilitation payment" seems to depend on whether there's a formal rule against it, whether the clerk is underpaid, whether the giver has any alternative, and I cant figure out which of those is doing the work.

Please answer in American English.
