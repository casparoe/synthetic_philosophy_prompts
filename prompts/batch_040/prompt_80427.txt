I'm not a mathematician; I taught myself enough set theory to get hooked on the axiom of choice and Zorn's lemma, and I keep coming back to what strikes me as its strangest feature: it guarantees that a maximal element exists — a thing nothing in the collection beats, though it need not beat everything — without ever telling you which thing that is. The proof is a promise, not a construction. I'd like a sestina that makes that gap its subject. Use the standard form, and let the six end-words be six rival candidates for the top of the chain, so that each time one returns at the end of a stanza it is the current highest point, only to be outflanked by the next stanza's climb. Somewhere in the body, state the lemma's condition — every chain has an upper bound — and in the envoi state the conclusion: a maximal element exists. The envoi must use all six words once, flat, with no hierarchy, so that the form itself enacts what the proof does: it certifies the summit and declines to point at it. A reader who knows the lemma should be able to tell which lines carry the hypothesis and which the conclusion, and why the poem never settles on a single top.
