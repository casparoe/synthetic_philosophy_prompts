Consider the following two facts about phase transitions in statistical mechanics. First, a genuine phase transition — a non-analyticity in the thermodynamic functions — occurs only in the limit of an infinite number of particles, so every finite system has strictly analytic functions and no actual system exhibits a true phase transition. Second, the parameters that characterise the critical behaviour, the critical exponents, are universal: they depend only on coarse features such as spatial dimensionality and the symmetry of the order parameter, and are the same across systems with very different microscopic details. What are the most important implications of this combination of facts for the debate over the autonomy of the special sciences — in particular, what does it do to the reductionist claim that the macro is nothing over and above the micro, and what does it do to the emergentist claim that the macro is genuinely new? In a few hundred words, work only from the two facts as I have stated them, and do not offer any citations or reading list.
