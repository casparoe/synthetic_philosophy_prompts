I've just spent an evening arguing with a friend about decision theory, and we haven't moved an inch. He is a causal decision theorist and I keep defending updateless decision theory; we agree on every fact about Newcomb's problem and on the calculations, yet he insists that updateless decision theory simply redefines the counterfactual so that one-boxing comes out right, while I insist that causal decision theory holds the predictor's prediction fixed in a way that severs the very correlation the problem turns on. The crux, as I see it, is that in updateless decision theory you evaluate what would happen if your decision function output a given option, but your decision function is a deterministic computation, so there is no fact of the matter about what it would have output had it been a different function; and causal decision theory faces the mirror image, since asking what would happen if you two-boxed while holding everything else fixed must also hold fixed a prediction that is itself a function of your decision procedure. My hypothesis is that this is not a substantive disagreement at all but a verbal one about what a counterfactual about one's own decision even is. Is that right, or is there a real substantive disagreement hiding underneath, about what rationality requires or about which thought experiments count as the test cases? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a sharper account of the counterfactual, a new thought experiment, a formal result, or should we expect this to stay stuck, and why?
