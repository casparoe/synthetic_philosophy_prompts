I keep circling back to how I feel about dividing up scarce benefits, and I want to see if there's a real principle behind my gut reactions or if I'm just vibing. Here are my calls on a bunch of hypotheticals I've been chewing on.

One: a clinic has funding for exactly one program. Program A gives a small health nudge to 200 people in the poorest part of town. Program B gives a big health boost to 20 people who are just off the poverty line. I go with A, even though B's total health gain is larger. The people in A are futher down, so each point of improvement for them should count for more.

Two: the city is picking between two projects. Project X upgrades the parks and clinics in the worst neighborhood (a moderate win for the people down there). Project Y builds a fast transit line that's a huge win for the middle class, but it'd add traffic and noise to that same worst neighborhood (a small hit to the people down there). I pick X. I won't let you trade even a small hit to the worst-off against a big win for the middle.

Three: this is the one that bugs me. Project Z reroutes a highway through the worst neighborhood for a year (a real short-term hit to those people — dust, noise, lost access to shops), but it unlocks a development that creates jobs right there and lifts those same people up a lot in the long run. I'm actually okay with Z. The short-term hit is fine because it's for their own long-term good.

Four: now Project W is the same short-term hit to the worst-off as Z, but the long-term gain goes to the middle class, not to the worst-off. I say no to W. So I'll eat a hit to the worst-off when it's a down payment on their own future, but not when it's a down payment on someone else's.

Five: two worlds, identical except for the bottom group. In world P the bottom group sits at a welfare level of 50; in world Q they're at 45; everyone else is exactly the same. I say P is the better world. I care about their actual level, not just the gap.

Six: two more worlds. In world R the bottom group is at 50 and the next group up is at 100. In world S the bottom group is at 60 and the next group is at 100. I say S is better, because the bottom group is higher up, even though the gap is smaller in S.

So here's what I want from you. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or at least most of them? If so, state it as precisely as you can, and for each piece of the principle tell me which of my cases is what's forcing it. I have a hunch the principle is that each point of welfare is worth more the further down the person is, but I keep running into the feeling that I'm attached to the worst-off's actual level in a way that's not just maximizing the total or closing the gap, and I'm not sure its the same thing as either of those. Tell me which of my verdicts above the principle can't save, if any. And give me its verdict on a case I haven't listed: a project that harms the worst-off a little now, raises their welfare a lot later, and as a side effect raises the middle class a lot too — would I be okay with that on my own principles? Please answer in American English.
