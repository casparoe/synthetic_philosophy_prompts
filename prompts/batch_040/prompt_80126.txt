I've been meaning to do something with my moral psychology seminar on the German/American folk-morality thing — the persistant claim, at least, that German morality runs more on rule-following and order, and American morality more on individual conscience — and I've been dreading just lecturing them on it or handing out a reading. I'd rather they feel the difference in their own verdicts before the labels ever come up. I have about 20 students and a 90-minute block. Can you design me a classroom activity that does that?

Roughly: split them into two jurisdictions — don't call them Germany and America, keep them abstract, North and South or whatever — where one runs on a written moral code and the other on conscience alone. But the conscience side needs a constraint so it's not just doing whatever they feel; maybe a publicity requirement, that you have to be able to state a reason your verdict would survive being made public. Give me the actual rules, the roles, and four or five concrete cases where the two sides come apart genuinly. I want at least one case where the rule is clearly unjust, one where the conscience-holder is obviously biased, and one novel case with no rule at all — feel free to seed one or two of them yourself.

The part I care about most: I want the students to discover, on their own, that neither side wins across the board. That the rule-side wins exactly on the cases where conscience is unreliable, and the conscience-side wins exactly on the cases where the rule is absent or unjust — so the real variable is the reliability of the people, not the system. If you can build that discovery into the structure of the activity rather than just telling them in the debrief, that'd be ideal.

Then a debrief that maps each case back onto the rule/conscience split and takes them to the question I actually want them to leave with: which of the two has the better of it, and what would it take to answer that — is it even answerable, or does it just depend on what failures you can afford and what population you're governing? And please tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, and be honest about what the activity can't reproduce. My guess is it can't reproduce the slow socialization that actually produces a rule-following or a conscience-based disposition, only the moment of judgment — but tell me if I'm wrong about that too.
