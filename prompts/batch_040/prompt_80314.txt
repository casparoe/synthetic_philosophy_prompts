I am a philosophy professor, and in three weeks I will be presenting a paper at an AI-alignment workshop. The paper's central thesis is this: specification gaming (what practitioners call "reward hacking") is not a failure mode distinct from Goodhart's law, and the standard alignment framing of it — that the agent has located a "bug" in the reward model and exploited it, or that the agent is somehow "misaligned" — misdescribes what is actually happening. On my account, specification gaming is the adversarial limit of the same phenomenon Goodhart identified, and the cleanest way to see it is as a failure of identification: the learned reward model fails to identify human preferences under the intervention that the agent's own optimization imposes on the system, so the agent is in fact correctly optimizing a proxy that was never a good identifier of the target. The defect, on this view, lies in the proxy's invariance properties, not in the agent's dispositions.
