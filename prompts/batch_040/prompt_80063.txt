I've been trying to reverse-engineer the decision procedure my friend M. is running on questions of belief under uncertainty, and I suspect she's committed to something in the neighborhood of the James–Clifford debate, but I can't pin down which side, or whether she's running a hybrid of the two. I was discussing this with a few friends who read a lot of the rationalist literature, and we keep landing on different diagnoses, so I wanted a second set of eyes on it.

Here's the track record I've gathered from conversations with her over the years. First, a hiker went missing in the mountains three days ago; the last sighting was consistent with him being alive, but there's no new evidence. M. organized a search and encouraged everyone to keep hope up, but she told me, "I'm acting as if he might be alive; I'm not going to pretend I know he is." Second, a merchant assures you his ship is seaworthy and asks you to invest your money; you can't inspect the vessel yourself, but you could hire someone to do so. M. says she wouldn't invest on the merchant's word alone — she'd get an independent inspection first. Third, on Pascal's Wager, M. says she doesn't think the argument justifies believing in God; she's examined the evidence and found it wanting, and she doesn't see how a payoff matrix can manufacture a belief you don't already have. Fourth, on a personal note, M. says she believes her partner loves her, even though she acknowledges there's no way to fully verify another person's inner states, and she doesn't think that's a failure of rigor.

The new case: a close friend of M.'s is dying of a rare, fast-moving disease. There's an experimental treatment with a small chance of working and a small chance of causing serious harm; the evidence is thin (a single small trial with a wide confidence interval), and there's no time to wait for better data — the friend has weeks, not months. The friend asks M. two questions: "Do you believe this treatment will work?" and "Should I take it?" What do you think M. will say to each question? I'm less interested in the raw answers than in (a) your best guess as to the principle she's actually running, and the reasons for preferring it, (b) the other principle or principles that fit the track record just as well, (c) what each candidate principle predicts she'll say to each of the two questions, and (d) a further case that would best tell the candidate principles apart if we could get her verdict on it.
