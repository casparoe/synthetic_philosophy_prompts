I have just spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether the activations inside a large neural network are "representations" in any sense that matters, or whether the word is doing more work than it can bear. We kept circling the same issues without converging, and I have decided that before either of us argues further we need a shared specification: what a theory of representational content in artificial neural networks actually has to deliver to count as adequate. Please write that specification.

For each requirement, give the consideration or motivating case behind it, and say whether you regard it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Cover at least the following, and flag any you think I have omitted:

- Granularity: what the unit of a representation is supposed to be — a single activation, a learned feature, a circuit, a population of units — and what the theory owes us in cases where a single direction in activation space is shared by many concepts, or a single concept is spread across many directions.
- Systematicity and compositionality: whether the theory must recover the systematic and compositional structure that philosophers have long taken to be essential to genuine content, and what that requirement actually demands of a network whose features are learned end-to-end rather than laid down by hand.
- Causal mediation rather than mere correlation: the requirement that a putative representation figure in the causal chain between input and output, rather than being a bystander that merely co-occurs with the relevant computation.
- Misrepresentation: the requirement that the theory explain how a feature can represent the wrong thing — a "cat" direction that lights up on a not-cat — without collapsing into the claim that a feature only ever represents what it tracks.
- The circularity charge: whether the theory must answer the worry that a functional-role or causal-role account explains a feature's content only by reference to the very causal relations whose interpretation is at issue.
- Interpretability: whether a representation has to be human-interpretable to count as one, or whether that is a prejudice of the people who happen to need to read the features.

Then tell me which of these you take to be genuine desiderata and which to be inherited prejudices of one side of the argument; whether any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable, in which case the list itself is the result and we should say which subset and why; and which single requirement knocks out the most candidate theories.

Finally, score the main contenders briefly against the list — the causal-role or functional account, the view that networks have no representations in the relevant sense and only computation, and the interpretability-led view that a representation just is a human-readable feature — but keep the weight on the requirements, since that is where the real work is. I want the specification and the reasoning, not a bibliography or a reading list.
