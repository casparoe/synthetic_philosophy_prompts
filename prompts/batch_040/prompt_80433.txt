I keep seeing the doctrine of double effect waved around as if it's a settled principle, and I want to force the actual reasoning into the open instead of getting "it depends on your metaethics." The setup: the standard switch case (divert the trolley from 5 people to 1) versus the loop case (divert it onto a track where it has to climb a loop and a single person is standing on the section the trolley must cross, so that one person's death is a foreseen but unintended side effect of creating the barrier). DDoE, if it only cares about means versus side effects, predicts these two should be judged the same, but people tend to judge the loop less permissable, and the usual explanation for the gap is the side-effect effect — people attribute "intention" to foreseen bad outcomes way more than to foreseen good ones, so they treat the one person's death in the loop as "intended" even when the agent never aimed at it.

So what's your actual credence that the loop/switch asymetry reflects a genuine normative difference (using a person as a means vs. foreseeing their death as a side effect) rather than being an artifact of that intentionality-attribution bias?
