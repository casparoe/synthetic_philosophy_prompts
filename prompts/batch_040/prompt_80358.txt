I've been going back and forth with a friend about the ethics of panpsychism and I can't figure out why we're stuck. He thinks that if panpsychism is right, then there's going to be some kind of suffering baked into the fundamental structure of physics, and that this is a genuinly huge moral problem — maybe even a reason to be a pessimist about the whole universe. I think he's just projecting "suffering" onto something way too simple to actually be suffering. To me, suffering needs some kind of integrality — a subject that things happen *to*, some valence that can actually be bad — and I don't see how a fundamental particle has that, so the ethical stakes are basicaly zero. We've been going in circles for months and I'm starting to think the disagreement is less about the physics and more about what the words "sentience" and "suffering" are even allowed to mean. But I'm not sure. Is this really a verbal disagreement, or is there a substantive question underneath it about what negative experience actually requires? And is the whole thing even a live question, or does it get blocked by the combination problem before the ethics can even start? I'd rather have a diagnosis of where the impasse actually is than a survey of the panpsychism literature, and if you can tell me what would count as real progress — some distinction, some formal result, a change of question — that'd be ideal.
