I've been reading about the Kelly criterion — the idea that a rational bettor should size each bet to maximize the expected logarithm of their bankroll, or equivalently the long-run geometric growth rate, rather than the plain arithmetic expected value — and it has made me notice a tension I can't quite resolve. On one side, professional poker players and sports bettors are taught to wager only a small fixed fraction of their bankroll on any single hand or event and to treat going bankrupt as the catastrophe to avoid at almost any cost. On the other side, the casinos and bookmakers that take the opposite bet happily justify their long-run profit by the ordinary arithmetic expected value of the game, and they see nothing wrong with that. So the very same game is being valued in two different ways by the people on opposite sides of it.

What view of rationality does each side's practice actually presuppose?
