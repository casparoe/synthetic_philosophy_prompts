I'm writing a piece on whether special relativity forces the block universe, and I want to isolate the one geometric fact that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument both runs on and is most exposed to. Construct three events in flat Minkowski spacetime (coordinates in units of c = 1) that show spacelike separation is not transitive — a spacelike from b, b spacelike from c, yet a timelike from c — with a as the at-rest observer's now and c in that observer's past; verify each of the three intervals by computing s² = Δt² − Δx². Then, using exactly that configuration, explain (a) how this non-transitivity is the engine of the argument: how a chain of frame-simultaneities, each link a single frame's "now," can connect two timelike-separated events, and why, if "present" is a frame-independent physical property, that connection forces an event in the at-rest observer's past to count as present, dissolving the moving present into the block; and (b) how the very same non-transitivity is the presentist's sharpest objection: why a chain of simultaneities is not itself a simultaneity and therefore not a present. Finish by naming the single premise that actually does the work (my guess: not the relativity of simultaneity itself, but a claim about how "present" transfers from one frame to the next), saying what happens to the argument if that premise is removed, and whether a presentist who makes "present" frame-indexed rather than frame-independent can defuse the argument without giving up presentism.
