I'm doing a paper on free will and criminal punishment and I keep going in circles on one thing. Dennett and Caruso both seem to grant that any given criminal act is the endpoint of a long causal chain -- genes, childhood, brain structure, luck of the draw -- so nothing about the act is uncaused. And yet Dennett comes out defending just deserts, the idea that offenders really do deserve their punishment, while Caruso says retributive desert is basically a fraud and we should handle crime the way public health handles disease. If the starting premise is the same, where exactly do they fork? My guess is it's that Dennett slides the target of desert from the act over to the character, but I cant tell if that slide is principled or if it just quietly smuggles in the compatibilist free will that Caruso flatly rejects. Can you find the single point of divergence and say whether Dennett's character move survives once you press the character with the very same causal objection Caruso presses the act with? Format it as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro, no conclusion.
