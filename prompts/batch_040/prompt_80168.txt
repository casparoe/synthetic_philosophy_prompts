I want a fake FAQ page for a self-help newsletter called "The Moloch-Free Life," written in the voice of an invented ex-quant coach who has decided that every human problem is a collective-action problem. Quick definition, since the term is niche: by "Moloch" or a "multipolar trap" I mean a situation where each agent has a dominant strategy to defect or escalate, and the joint outcome is worse for everyone than the coordinated one -- arms races, ad wars, that kind of thing. The newsletter's audience has taken this one idea and applied it to literally everything, and that over-application is the target of the satire. Format: a numbered list of exactly five Q&A items, no intro, no sign-off, the whole thing running to roughly 300 words; each item is a one-sentence reader question and a one- or two-sentence coach answer. The five items should cover, in any order: (1) a genuine multipolar trap (an HOA pool war, an ad war, your pick), where the answer has to say that transparency and good vibes don't fix it because they don't change the incentive structure -- the actual tool is a binding commitment device; (2) a pure coordination problem the reader has misdiagnosed as a trap -- the fix is a tiebreak rule or a designated leader, not more trust; (3) a one-player problem (procrastination, a bad diet, whatever), where the honest answer is that you are not playing a game against anyone and the relevant tool is pre-commitment, not coordination; (4) a chicken-game situation the reader is calling a trap, where the answer gets the distiction right -- in chicken, the winning move is sometimes to credibly commit to backing down, which is the opposite of the trap's logic; and (5) a scope-creep question (is the dating app Moloch, is my marriage Moloch, is all of civilization Moloch, your pick) that the coach handles deadpan. The humor has to come from the game theory being definately right and from the coach selling a "reframing" or a non-refundable "commitment fee," which he insists is itself a commitment device (the joke being that the fee is the product), even when the honest diagnosis is "this is not Moloch, you are fine." Keep it deadpan throughout; a game theorist reading it should recognize each diagnosis as fair rather than a caricature of a strawman.
