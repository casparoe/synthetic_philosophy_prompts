A friend keeps coming back to this argument he built out of Hegel's master-slave dialectic, and I honestly cant find the exit. He says it shows the dialectic refutes itself. Here's the chain, roughly how he lays it out:

(1) For recognition to actually make me a self-consciousness, it has to come from another self-consciousness — from someone I take to be free, not from someone I've reduced to a mere thing. That's the master's own catch: the recognition the slave gives him is worthless, because in dominating the slave the master has stopped counting him as a self-consciousness at all, so what the slave hands over isnt recognition *from* a self-consciousness.

(2) But look at the recognizer's side. The one who recognizes is never independent of the one recognized: my self-consciousness is partly made of being seen and affirmed by the other, so in the act of recognizing I hand a piece of my own selfhood over to them. I become dependent on them for part of who I am.

(3) And dependence is just the flip side of the independence that freedom consists in. To need another for your own self is to have your freedom partly in their hands.

(4) So the recognizer always stands, relative to the recognized, in a position of dependence — and that's the same kind of position, in the relevant sense, that the slave stands in.

(5) By (1), recognition issued from a position like that is worthless, because it doesn't come from a free self-consciousness.

(6) So every act of recognition confers only worthless recognition on its recipient.

(7) But the whole dialectic starts from the premise that self-consciousness requires genuine recognition.

(8) So self-consciousness is impossible — nobody ever becomes a self-consciousness by being recognized.

(9) Yet we plainly have experiences of mutual recognition: friendship, love, being really seen by an equal. And those don't feel like worthless reflections of ourselves.

(10) Contradiction.

He says the feature that dooms the master — the recognizer's unfreedom — is a feature of *every* recognizer, so nothing ever gets genuinely recognized. I keep feeling something slipped between (2) and (5), but I can't name it. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd way rather keep (9) than give up (1), but I want to know what I'm paying either way.

End your answer with a single line starting "Verdict:" followed by just the number of the step you'd reject.
