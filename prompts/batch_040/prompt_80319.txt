My opening move: I grant that the public announcement makes it common knowledge that at least one child is muddy, and that the inductive step then goes through. But I still think the whole thing is a sleight of hand, for two reasons. First, the guru says nothing any child didn't already see — with two or more muddy children, every child already sees at least one muddy forehead, so the content is, for each individual, already known; if a rational agent's behavior can be changed by a sentence that adds nothing to that agent's information, then either the puzzle is smuggling in an unrealistic idealization (perfect logicians who introspect to arbitrary depth) or 'common knowledge' is doing all the work as a purely formal, contentless label. Second, and this is what I most want you to address: the lockstep conclusion seems to depend on the announcement being public, made to all the children at once. If instead the guru whispered to each child, one at a time, in a private corner, that at least one of them is muddy, I don't think the conclusion survives, because the children can no longer use one another's silence as a signal. So tell me: is the puzzle a genuine discovery about how public announcements work, or a clever artifact of its setup — and commit to one, with your reasons, rather than giving me a balanced overview of both readings.
