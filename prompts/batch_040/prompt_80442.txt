I'm presenting to a philosophy of language and modality seminar next month the view that David Lewis's counterpart theory is the correct semantics for natural-language de re modal attributions, and that Saul Kripke's transworld identity is not a rival semantics but a confused attempt to impose the necessity of identity on a domain where it does not apply. My specific thesis is that the non-transitivity of the counterpart relation is not a defect that needs fixing but a faithful representation of the genuine non-transitivity of the "is a possible version of" relation, and that the Kripkean worry—that transworld identity either forces me to be identical to many different individuals in different worlds or leaves it unclear which individual in another world I am identical to—is a reductio of transworld identity, not of counterpart theory. The audience is a seminar of about fifteen philosophers, most of whom are committed Kripkeans who have read *Naming and Necessity* carefully, plus two or three who are sympathetic to Lewis but have not fully worked through *On the Plurality of Worlds*. They care about the technical details of the semantics and are impatient with hand-waving. Please prepare me for the question period: the objections I will actually get from this audience, in roughly the order they will come, each phrased the way a committed Kripkean would phrase it, with a note on how serious each objection is, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and a two-minute reply for each. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks. Please write the answer in British English.
