My partner and I are getting married in a few months, and the experience of actually planning it has made me go back to Bentham's writings on marriage and sexual conduct with more attention than I ever gave them, and I've landed on a reading of his fixed-term marriage proposal that I can't shake. I'd like you to make the case for it rigorously, and I want the argument to be one I can defend, not a summary of what he said.

Please argue that Bentham's proposal — that a marriage be contracted for a fixed term, renewable by the mutual consent of the parties and dissolving automatically at the end of the term unless it is renewed — is best understood not as a proto-liberal loosening of the marriage bond, the way it is usually presented, but as a specifically utilitarian institutional fix, calibrated to a particular cluster of harms: the misery of people trapped in loveless or abusive marriages, the pressure that entrapment puts on them toward adultery and desertion, and the resulting damage to children. On this reading the term is not a claim that marriage is optional or that commitment is a matter of preference; it is a mechanism for making the continuation of a marriage a genuine, repeated choice rather than a default that law and social pressure impose. You may use his broader target — the idea that much of the law's condemnation of private arrangements is driven by antipathy and disgust rather than by any estimate of consequences — as the context that makes the utilitarian-fix reading more natural than the proto-liberal one.

Then please argue the harder, less flattering corollary: that because the proposal is calibrated to those specific harms, it does not generalize into a general right to exit a marriage at will, or a right to reconfigure one's relationships whenever one pleases, and that this in fact leaves more room for a conservative insistence on the permanence of marriage than modern readers tend to allow Bentham. I want the argument explicit about what the proposal does and does not commit him to, and a frank account of where this reading is strongest and where it is most vulnerable to objection.
