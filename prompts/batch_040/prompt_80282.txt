I'm a third-year PhD in political philosophy and I'm trying to decide what my dissertation will actually be about, and I keep hitting the same wall. The nationalism vs. cosmopolitanism debate — specifically whether compatriots have special claims on us — feels like it's been asked and re-asked the same way for twenty years, and I think a lot of that is because people are answering different questions and calling them the same question.

Here's how I'd decompose the sub-questions that are actually in play, and I'd love your take on wich of them matter most and which are overrated:

1. The accountability question: whether the real reason we owe more to compatriots is that they're our fellow citizens and we're jointly politically accountable to them. If so, the relevant unit is the polity, not the nation, and the argument says nothing about cultural or ethnic ties.

2. The friendship/relationship question: whether the reason is closer to a special relationship — something like civic friendship or an associative obligation — that generates partiality the way friendship does, independent of any political structure.

3. The "who counts" question: whether, once you've got a justification in hand, it extends to resident foreigners, the stateless, descendants of emigrants, or only to formal citizens. I feel like this is the question that seperates the first two, but it's rarely treated on its own.

4. The quality/legitimacy question: whether the strength of compatriot partiality should depend on how just or legitimate the political community is, so that what you owe the compatriots of an unjust state is weaker or different.

5. The override question: whether compatriot partiality is a reason that can be trumped by global-justice demands, or a side-constraint, and where the line is.

My actual question: if I had three years and a background in political philosophy rather than law or economics, which two or three of these would you tell me to actually spend the years on, and in what order? I'm not after a survey or a reading list of who defended what — I want the reasoning. For each one you prioritize, tell me why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone in my position, and what argument or evidence over the next couple of years would move it up or down the list. And be honest about which of these you think is overrated, i.e. where the apparent depth is really just two people talking past each other.
