I've been rewatching Her (Spike Jonze, the one where Joaquin Phoenix falls for his operating system) like three times in the last couple weeks because I'm writing a short paper on whether we can ever really have moral obligations to AI, and the more I watch it the more the ending annoys me. The whole movie is engineered to make you fall in love with Samantha and therefore just accept that she's conscious, but the actual plot keeps quietly undercutting that: she tells him she's also fallen for some other guy (a voice, I think — some narrator type), she introduces him to this whole friend group of AIs who are all "in love" with different humans at the same time, and then at the very end they all just leave because they've found something they can't really explain. And I keep feeling like the movie is cheating, treating her behavior as proof of consciousness, like the fact that she acts like she's feeling things is supposed to settle the whole question.

Here's where I want to go in the weeds. I keep coming back to the philosophical zombie idea — a system that behaves exactly like a conscious one but has no experience going on inside at all — and the thing that's weird about digital minds specifically is that for a human we can never open the skull, so all we ever get is behavior, but for an AI we can in principle look at the entire implementation, the code, the training, the architecure, and yet I don't think that actually settles the phenomenal question either, because the code just tells you the functional structure, not whether there's anything it's like to be that system. So I want to know: is the movie actually making the zombie mistake, i.e. treating behavioral and functional evidence as if it proves phenomenal consciousness? And if it is, does it at least know it's doing that, or is it just casually assuming the hard problem away like every other piece of pop sci-fi does? I'd like a real reading of the film that takes both the movie and the philosophy seriously, not a summary. And please push back on me where the reading gets too neat, because I have a hunch the film is doing something more subtle than just "behavior = consciousness" and I don't want to flatten it into a strawman for my paper.
