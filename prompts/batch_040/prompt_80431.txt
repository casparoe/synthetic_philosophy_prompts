Okay so I just got into a really long argument with a friend about verisimilitude and I'm still a little wired about it. He's adamant that the whole "problem of verisimilitude" is basically solved and that anyone still treating it as an open problem is out of date. His entire case rests on the minsum measure, or the general family of content-based measures where you sum a theory's truth content against its falsity content over its atomic consequences, and he's like, "we have a number, we can rank theories by how close they are to the truth, that's it, that's the theory."

I kept pushing back because I don't think any of these measures actually respects the one desideratum we care about, which is that if a theory is genuinely closer to the truth, the measure should rank it higher (monotonicity, the "more-true principle," whatever you want to call it). My intuition is that the reason this is hard is that content is double-edged: making a theory stronger or more informative raises its potential to be right and its potential to be wrong at the same time, so there's no obvious way to fold "more true content" and "less false content" into a ranking that behaves the way we'd want. I also have a vague memory that there are no-go results showing that if you demand a few natural properties, the only measures that survive are trivial, and that Popper himself kind of conceded later that his original truth-content-minus-falsity-content idea wasn't as clean as he'd hoped.

So here's what I actually want, and I definately don't want a survey of every measure that's ever been proposed or a list of papers, I've had enough of that. I want you to tell me wich open questions in this area are the crux ones, the ones that the rest of the work kind of implicitly depends on, and in what order they matter. Defend the ordering: for each, why is it upstream of the others, how tractable is it, and how many other questions hang off its answer. I'm especially interested in whether the minsum / content-based family is a genuine solution to the more-true problem or just a workaround that quietly gives up one of the desiderata we'd want, and where exactly the no-go results bite. And separately, which of the big implications of all this actually carry weight — the idea that science is making progress by getting closer to the truth, using it to evaluate competing theories, what it does for realism — and which are overrated or don't really follow.

Last thing, and this matters: I don't fully trust my memory on the technical details, so be clear about where you're confident and where you're not, and don't smooth over it. And if I've got the setup wrong somewhere — like if minsum actually does satisfy the more-true principle, or if the no-go results don't rule out what I think they rule out — just say so plainly and correct me instead of playing along with my framing.
