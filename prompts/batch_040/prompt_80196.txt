Every time someone brings up Malthus at dinner these days they've got the same defense ready: he was right but early, the famines just hadn't hit yet and we got lucky with the Green Revolution. I want you to argue the other way, namely that "right but early" isn't a rescue of his theory, it's a confession that the theory is dead.

Do it by separating the bare prediction (population eventually outruns food and misery follows) from the mechanism Malthus actually commited to (population grows geometrically while food grows arithmetically, and the check comes through positive checks like famine, war and disease). Then show that letting "early" explain away every miss just turns the theory into an unfalsifiable bare prediction, which is exactly the degenerating move Lakatos was talking about. You can lean on the "saving the phenomena" picture here: postponing the date is just bolting a temporal epicycle on, and that's not progress, it's bookkeeping.

Finish by drawing the line between a real theoretical correction (the food-growth function actually changed, because of Haber-Bosch and high-yield wheat) and a bare postponement of the prediction. The point I want to get at is that once "early" can be stretched to mean "not yet, but eventually," the original claim has been drained of any content that could have been tested.
