I'm finishing a novel and the whole book keeps orbiting this one image, DNA as a code, and i want to close a chapter with a short poem that doesn't just repeat the greeting-card version of "we're written in a code." What i keep circling is that "the code" is quietly doing three different jobs and we blur them: the mapping (the little table that says which three-letter codon means which amino acid), the message (the actual sequence, the text written in the molecule), and the medium (the wet double helix itself, which is also a physical thing with shape and chemistry and a real job in the cell). Can you write me a villanelle where one refrain is the "it's just a code, a message" line and the other is the "it's a body, it's wet, it's a machine" line, and the form does the work of showing why we keep sliding between the message and the medium and can't hold both at once, with the two refrains finally sitting side by side in the last two lines? It should be accurate enough that a molecular biologist and a philosopher of information would both nod, but it's going in a book so it also has to land as a poem, not a lecture.
