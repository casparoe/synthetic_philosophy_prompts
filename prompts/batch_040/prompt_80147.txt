I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, and I'm helping my advisor design the take-home essay for our graduate seminar on economic and political theory. We want one question on the socialist calculation debate that stops being a re-telling of "Mises says prices are impossible, Lange says just run a simulated market." The crux I want students to wrestle with is the one I keep hearing them blur together in office hours: the difference between Mises's *impossibility* claim (without market-determined prices for capital goods there is no coherent economic calculation) and Hayek's *knowledge* claim (the relevant information is dispersed, tacit, and discovered through entrepreneurial action, so no central body can ever possess it in the form needed). My suspicion is that the Lange model is a serious answer to the first but not the second, and I want the question to force students to test that suspicion rather than assume it.

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme. The question should ask students to evaluate whether the Lange–Lerner model (public ownership, a central planning board that sets prices by trial-and-error tâtonnement, and enterprise managers instructed to set price equal to marginal cost, with any resulting losses covered by a social dividend) actually defeats the calculation critique, and to do so by distinguishing the Mises-style from the Hayek-style version of the critique. I want the model answer to make at least these moves explicit: (1) that the p = MC rule is what makes the model Pareto-efficient but simultaneously turns every natural monopoly into a chronic loss-maker, which is why the social-dividend/subsidy mechanism is load-bearing rather than cosmetic; (2) that the trial-and-error price adjustment presupposes the planner can read marginal cost and excess demand/shortage signals across the whole capital-goods complex, which is precisely the dispersed, local knowledge the Hayekian says cannot be aggregated; and (3) a clear verdict on which version of the critique the model answers and which it leaves standing, with the reasoning for why. For the marking scheme, separate an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, and flag the two most common failures: students who treat "Mises" and "Hayek" as interchangeable, and students who grant the model efficiency without noticing that the subsidy is doing the work. Total 30 marks, answerable in two hours.
