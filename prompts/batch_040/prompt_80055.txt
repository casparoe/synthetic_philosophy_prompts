I've been reading about the symbol grounding problem, and I keep running into the slogan 'syntax is not semantics' being used in two directions that seem to contradict each other. One writer uses it to argue that neural networks can't have meaningful representations because they don't operate on discrete symbols; another uses it to argue that large language models can't understand anything because all they do is perform formal operations on tokens. Could you lay out the different precise claims this slogan could be making, and for each say whether it's a platitude, something defensible, or actually false? I'm especially interested in whether there is a single reading on which both uses come out correct, or whether the slogan is really doing two different jobs, and which of the readings—if any—would actually support the conclusion that current language models don't understand anything.
