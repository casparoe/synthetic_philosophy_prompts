I've been reading about the free will debate on my own for a while now, and I keep getting the sense that compatibilists and incompatibilists are often arguing past each other — partly because they're using "free will" to mean slightly different things, and partly because they're quietly answering different questions. I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a compatibilist and an incompatibilist who also accepts that determinism is true (the position sometimes called hard determinism) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please build it on the assumption that both sides agree determinism is true, so the metaphysics is not in dispute and the entire question is whether free will and genuine moral responsibility can survive under it. I want the claims they can both sign; the precise formulations of whatever disagreement remains, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of its own view; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side. My working hypothesis, which I'd like the document to test rather than assume, is that there are at least two distinct cruxes: one about whether free will and responsibility require the ability to have done otherwise, and a second, less-discussed one about what free will is for — that is, whether it is the thing that grounds moral responsibility and makes praise and blame fair, or the thing that makes us ultimate sources and makes our lives genuinely up to us in the way that matters. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side would predict or conclude from it. If you conclude that part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table with a column for each side's position.
