This has been nagging me for a week. A friend who reads way too many of these cosmology threads says the Boltzmann brain problem is actually a *proof*, not just a puzzle, that our cosmology is dead. Here's his argument, more or less:

(1) In a universe with a positive cosmological constant (de Sitter-ish, or eternal inflation), the vacuum keeps fluctuating forever, so the total number of Boltzmann brains that ever pop into existance is infinite, while the number of ordinary observers (embodied, with a continuous thermodynamic past) is finite.

(2) If one kind of observer comes in infinite number and the other in finite number, then the fraction of all observers that are of the infinite kind is 1.

(3) By self-locating reasoning, if I'm an observer I should expect to be a typical one, and betting against typicality Dutch-books me. So I should expect to be a Boltzmann brain.

(4) But I have a continuous past — a childhood, memories, a low-entropy early universe — and a Boltzmann brain, being a one-off fluctaution, doesn't.

(5) So (4) rules out my being a Boltzmann brain, which means (1) is false: we don't live in a de Sitter-ish / eternally inflating universe.

He's pretty confident and I can't shake it. I feel like it collapses somewhere but I can't name where, and I'd most like to keep (4) because it's the one thing I actually know about myself. Which step do you reject, and what does it cost to reject it? Also I'm not even sure (1) is right — in eternal inflation, are ordinary observers really finite, or is that just a simplification he's making?
