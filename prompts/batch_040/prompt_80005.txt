I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about how deepfakes are wrecking political accountabilty. I keep reaching for Hannah Arendt's work on lying in politics, but I feel like she'd hit a wall on one specific development. There's this "liar's dividend" thing — once AI can make convincing fake video, a politician can just deny that any damaging footage of them is real, call it a deepfake, and the genuine evidence stops working because even the provenance metadata could be faked. It's not a new consistent lie, its making the whole category of "what actually happened" unanswerable.

Given how Arendt drew the line between fragile factual truths and the invulnerable rational truths, and her claim that lies are self-defeating because they destroy the common world the liar needs — how would she actually analyze the liar's dividend? Does it fit, or is it a genuinely new kind of attack where the person holding the verified fact is the one who loses and the burden of proof ends up on proving your true video isn't fake? Trying to figure out if her framework captures this or if it exposes a real gap.
