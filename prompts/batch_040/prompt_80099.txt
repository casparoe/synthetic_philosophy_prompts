I am helping two colleagues prepare a public exchange after our city's disability office proposed replacing an in-kind transport voucher with a flat cash payment. One of them is a welfare egalitarian who thinks justice requires equalizing people's actual well-being, while the other is a resource egalitarian who thinks justice requires equalizing people's holdings of socially valued resources, adjusted for relevant disadvantages. The proposal has made the question of what equality should target feel urgent in a way our readings did not: some disabled residents say the cash lets them arrange cheaper rides and so improves their lives, while others say the scheme implicitly rewards those who can adapt their preferences or outsource care, and penalizes people with expensive but non-negotiable needs.

Please produce the document they would jointly publish after a structured adversarial collaboration, treating both positions as their strongest versions. The first section should contain the propositions both can sign without qualification: what equality of welfare and equality of resources each aim to protect, where they agree that self-reported life satisfaction is an incomplete metric, and why conversion factors such as disability, local transport costs, and caregiving availability matter even if they do not settle the dispute. The second section should state the two positions in mutually acceptable language and identify the minimal set of questions on which their conclusions still depend on different answers, especially whether adaptive preferences should count at face value, whether resources should be measured before or after individual differences in conversion, and whether the relevant comparison is ex ante expected welfare or ex post realized welfare. For each such question, give a hypothetical policy variant or argument both would accept as bearing on it, together with what each side would predict the variant or argument shows. End with a compact table that separates the verbal from the substantive and records the residual disagreement as precisely as possible.
