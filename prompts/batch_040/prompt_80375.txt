I'm a third year PhD student in value theory and I'm writing a short paper on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — what, exactly, all the different pleasures have in common that lets a hedonist treat them as one good and add them up. I keep coming back to a point I cant quite pin down in prose, so I'd like a thought experiment built to order that I can drop straight into the paper.

What I need is a pair of single experiences, A and B, engineered so the two most plausible candidates for what all pleasures share — and hence what a hedonist is measuring when she sums them — give different rankings. Candidate one is hedonic tone, the positive what-it's-like, the intensity of the feel. Candidate two is the satisfaction-quality, the feel of a long-held want or goal finally coming out right (what a desire-satisfaction theorist would latch onto). The spec: A and B each have to be unambiguously pleasures (nobody should need to argue either one counts), matched in duration, and matched on the rest of Bentham's hedonic calculus too — certainty, propinquity, fecundity, purity — so that intensity/tone is the only quantitative difference. A should have clearly stronger tone than B (a rush, not a glow), but A must not be the satisfaction of any desire the subject had beforehand; it's a free-floating sensory hit the subject didn't seek. B should be a paradigmatic desire-satisfaction with clearly weaker tone. And A must have no hidden costs — no amnesia, no hangover, no dependency, no moral stain, no side effects; the subject is completely fine afterwards and remembers it — so the only thing seperating the two rankings is tone versus satisfaction, not a trade-off you could wave away. Keep it realistic: a setting a normal person could actually be in, no experience machines, no demons, no bliss buttons.

The point of the case is to show that picking either tone or the satisfaction-quality as the common measure is arbitrary, because the two give different verdicts on the same pair and there's no non-arbitrary tie-breaker — so a version of the case that just stipulates the answer, or where one candidate wins only because of a smuggled-in cost, fails. After the scenario I want the check: why each spec is met, then what the tone-hedonist concludes and why, what the desire-satisfaction theorist concludes and why, and what a Millian would do with the competent-judges device — whether it actually breaks the tie or just relocates the question. Then an honest note on which feature is doing the work, what happens if I remove it (say A comes with a hangover, or B's tone is as strong as A's), and how a defender of the position that comes out badly would most plausibly attack the case.

If you think this spec is actually unsatisfiable — if A can't be made genuinely free of hidden costs, or if B can't come in below A on tone without stopping being a paradigmatic satisfaction — say so plainly and tell me which constraint is the culprit. I'd rather know the case can't be built than have you fudge it. Keep the whole thing under 500 words, and write it in British English.
