Write a dialogue between two philosophers who are both sympathetic to antinatalism but who disagree about whether Benatar's asymmetry is a sound foundation for it. One of them defends the asymmetry on the view that the absence of pain is a genuine, subject-independent good: pain is intrinsically bad, and its absence is intrinsically good, whether or not there is a subject who would have suffered it. She thinks this grounds a strong conclusion: bringing a person into existence always produces the bad of pain without destroying any genuine good (since the absence of pleasure was not a bad), so procreation is never a net benefit. The other is sympathetic to antinatalism but thinks the asymmetry, as stated, is arbitrary and cannot be defended. He thinks the only non-arbitrary principle is that value requires a subject: no subject, no value. He thinks that if you apply this principle consistently, a non-existent person is neither helped nor harmed by non-existence, so the asymmetry collapses, and antinatalism has to be defended on other grounds (the actual suffering of those who do exist, not the hypothetical absence of pleasure in those who do not). The dialogue should have real back-and-forth: the defender should try to ground the asymmetry in the nature of value (pain as a negative state, its absence as a positive state), and the critic should keep pushing back with the no-subject-no-value line and the charge of arbitrariness. Neither should be a strawman; both should make their strongest case, and each should respond to the other's actual points rather than talk past them. The dialogue should reach at least the point where the defender tries to distinguish the killing-a-happy-person case (which is a bad, because there is a subject who is deprived of pleasure) from the non-existence case (where the absence of pleasure is not a bad, because there is no subject), and the critic responds that this just restates the asymmetry without explaining why the absence of a bad is good without a subject. End the dialogue with the two of them still disagreeing, but each having understood the other's position clearly. Do not include any citations, bibliographies, or references to specific works beyond the name of the asymmetry itself.
