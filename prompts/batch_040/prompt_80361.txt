I'm writing an essay that reads Alex Garland's Ex Machina through the access vs phenomenal consciousness distinction, and I want it to be sharper then the usual Turing test commentary. The film's whole setup is Nathan's test of Ava, but once you put Block's distinction on it, the test looks structurally blind: everything Ava can give Caleb — speech, self-report, the power outages, the manipulation — is an output of whatever plays the role of access consciousness, and nothing in the movie could in principle tell a phenomenal Ava from a zombie Ava. Work out whether the film knows this or is just too dumb to notice, and whether Nathan's reveal that manipulating Caleb is itself evidence of consciousness is a real escape from the reporting problem, wich is that the only probe we have for phenomenal consciousness is verbal report, and report is itself an access process, or the same bias in a different costume. Also, the staging maps weirdly well onto global workspace theory — Ava's room as a gated workspace, the surveillance as broadcast, the outages as gating — so take that seriously too, and say where the metaphor breaks. Close with what the film adds to the debate that the papers don't.
