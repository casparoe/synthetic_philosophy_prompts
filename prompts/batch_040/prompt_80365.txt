The symbol grounding problem keeps coming up in my recent work on embodied AI, and I've been trying to get the basics straight. One article I read puts it this way: "The symbol grounding problem, as Harnad argued, is the puzzle of how symbols in a computer program get their meaning. In a purely symbolic system, each symbol is defined only in terms of other symbols, so the whole system is a closed loop of symbol-to-symbol relations with no real connection to the world, and the symbols are empty of meaning. Harnad's answer is to ground symbols in perception and action. A thermostat, for instance, is a symbol system whose internal state is directly caused by the temperature of the room and which acts on the room by switching the heat on and off, so its state is grounded in the world. Once a system's symbols are causally coupled to the world in this way, the grounding problem is solved, and the system genuinely understands what its symbols mean." I'm not sure how much of this to trust, and I don't know enough to say where it goes wrong. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me what holds up, where the summary quietly distorts or overstates Harnad, and what someone defending the original argument would push back on? And once the summary is corrected, does the article's bottom line — that causal coupling solves the grounding problem and gives a system genuine understanding — actually follow?
