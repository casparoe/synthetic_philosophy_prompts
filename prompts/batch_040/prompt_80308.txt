There's a puzzle about how to apply the intentional stance to a large language model. The intentional stance is the strategy of predicting and explaining an agent's behavior by attributing beliefs and desires to it, and it presupposes a subject that persists over time and holds relatively stable beliefs. A large language model, however, has no persistent state or memory between separate conversations: the same underlying weights can express very different commitments in different conversations, and each conversation is in a sense a fresh instance of those weights. This raises the question of what the subject of the attributed beliefs is — the model as a fixed set of weights, a particular run of the model through a single conversation, or something else — and whether it is even coherent to say that a large language model has beliefs at all. Can you explain this puzzle and discuss how it bears on the question of attributing beliefs to such systems? Please write your answer in American English.
