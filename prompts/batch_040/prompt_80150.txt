I'm writing up my views on practical reasons for a seminar and want a consistency check before I present. I hold: (1) internalism about practical reasons in the Williamsian sense — for X to be a reason for an agent, X must be able to figure in a sound deliberation from the agent's actual motivational set (desires, commitments, projects) that issues in a motivation to act; reasons are not "out there" independent of the agent's psychology. (2) That the "soundness" condition in (1) is not merely epistemic or formal (no false beliefs, no incoherence); a sound deliberation is substantively constrained, and would, among other things, recognize genuine moral considerations as reasons even when the agent's actual motivational set does not. (3) That there are genuine moral reasons that bind even an agent with no moral motivation at all — the amoral sadist still has a reason not to torture for fun — and that these reasons are "internal" via the soundness route in (2). (4) That the reasons an agent has are partly constituted by the agent's particular relationships, projects, and commitments; what is a reason for me may not be a reason for you, given our different motivational sets. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each pair in tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
