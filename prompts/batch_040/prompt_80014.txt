I'm a novelist working on a historical novel set in the Reconstruction era, and one of my central characters is a former Southern intellectual who is a die-hard and completely sincere Aristotelian — he read the Politics in Greek and genuinely finds the doctrine of natural slavery in Book 1 defensible as a real philosophical position, not as a piece of apologetics. In a key scene near the end of the book, he sits down to talk with a Republican senator who has just returned from Washington, and I need his case to be the strongest possible version of the Aristotelian position, not a straw man I can knock down easily. I'd like you to reconstruct, as carefully as you can, how Aristotle himself would have reacted to the Thirteenth Amendment and to the claim that the abolition of slavery was a moral victory. In particular: would he have regarded the abolition as a moral catastrophe, or as a pragmatic failure of the state to provide for what he saw as the natural division of labor? What would he have made of the Fourteenth Amendment's equal protection clause, and of the modern claim that slavery was a moral evil rather than a natural arrangement? And what would he have said about the clause in the Thirteenth Amendment that preserves slavery as a punishment for crime — would he have found that exception consistent with his view, or would he have seen it as a contradiction that the framers never noticed?

I want you to treat the abolition as a genuine empirical challenge to his premise that some people are manifestly inferior in rational capacity and thus suited by nature to be ruled, and to confront the question of whether Aristotle would have treated the abolition as a refutation of his empirical observation or as a failure of perception on the part of the abolitionists. I don't need a bibliography or a reading list; I need a reasoned reconstruction. And please say how confident you are in each of the claims you make about what Aristotle would have thought, and single out the one point you're least sure of, so I can decide how much of it to trust when I write the scene.
