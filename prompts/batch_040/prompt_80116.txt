Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about a variant of the torture-vs-dust-specks case. Forget the usual setup where one tortured person is pitted against a huge crowd each getting a single speck — instead do this: Person A gets tortured for a year, while Person B, a single individual, has a billion dust specks land on them spread out over an entire lifetime. One speaker is a totalist: the billion specks add up to more total harm than the year of torture, so B is the one who's worse off. The other speaker thinks severity comes in tiers — the torture clears a threshhold that no pile-up of trivial, barely-noticeable nuisances can ever cross, so A is still worse off, full stop. I want them to actually grapple with each other's arguments rather than take turns restating their side: press on where exactly the threshhold sits, whether 'trivial' is a matter of degree or of kind, and whether cramming all the specks onto one life changes the math compared to the standard spread-out version. Both should be sharp and willing to concede a point when its due. Around 600 to 800 words.
