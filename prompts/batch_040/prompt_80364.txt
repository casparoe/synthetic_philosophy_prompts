My background is labor and organizational economics; I work daily with principal-agent models, transaction cost analysis, human capital, the boundary of the firm, and contract theory. I want you to restate Aristotle's argument for natural slavery in the vocabulary I actually use, and then give me an honest account of what the translation could not carry.

Take the argument as it appears in Book I of the Politics. The moves I want translated are: the distinction between the slave who is a slave by nature and the slave who is a slave only by law or by capture in war; the claim that the natural slave's soul can perceive and follow the master's reason but cannot possess practical reason of its own; the idea that the slave is something like a tool that happens to have life, in contrast to a dead tool; and the conclusion that the natural master-slave relation is both beneficial to the slave and right. While you translate it, I want you to test one framing: the master-slave relation as the limiting case of the firm, the point at which a market contract has been replaced entirely by authoritative command, so that the coordination problem is solved not by incentives but by property rights. See how far that reading carries.

Three features of the argument are the hard cases, so please address them in particular. First, the slave as a living tool: in my vocabulary, what is the status of a resource that is also an agent with preferences, and where exactly does the line between a factor of production and a contracting party fall? Second, the claim that the natural slave perceives and follows the master's reason but does not possess practical reason of his own: this sounds like an agent who has complete information about the principal's objectives but no utility function of his own. Is that a coherent agent type in principal-agent theory, or does my framework quietly assume that every agent always has his own utility and preferences, so that the claim can only be expressed by distorting it? Third, the claim that the arrangement is both beneficial to the slave and right: this is the move of justifying an allocation by its own efficiency. Does my vocabulary have a place for an arrangement being justified by its own efficiency, or is that a category the framework simply cannot house?

Then give me the residue report: which parts of Aristotle's argument map cleanly onto my vocabulary, which parts you had to distort to fit, and which parts have no expression in it at all. And tell me your view on what the residue means: is it a genuine limit on the explanatory reach of the economic framework, or is it a sign that Aristotle's argument was doing less work than it looks? Don't give me a bibliography or citations; I'll add those myself. I just want the translation and an honest account of where it breaks.
