I keep coming back to an argument a friend made at a reading group, and I cant find the step doing the illicit work. It looks like it proves that Peirce's and James's theories of truth are actually the same theory, which would mean the whole famous dispute between them was just verbal.

(1) Peirce: a proposition is true iff it's the opinion the community of inquirers will ultimately converge on, given unlimited time.
(2) The community converges by inquiry, and inquiry keeps the beliefs that "work" — the ones that let us predict and act with stable success.
(3) So the opinion the community converges on in the limit is just the one that works best, long run, for the community.
(4) James: a proposition is true iff it "works" — it has cash value, it pays in experience.
(5) So Peirce's "ultimate convergence" and James's "what works" pick out the same set of truths.
(6) So the two theories are extensionally identical, and the dispute was merely verbal — Peirce stressing the long-run social process, James the short-run individual payoff, same truths.
(7) But that's absurd: they were clearly fighting over something real, and Peirce in particular thought James had corrupted the pragmatic maxim into something he'd disown.
(8) So one of (1)–(6) is false.

My friend added that Peirce insists the process has to run for unlimited time and we never actually reach the limit, we just keep getting closer. I think that detail matters but I can't see how it blocks the argument. I'm sure there's a substantive difference and I dont buy that it was all words, but when I try to restate both theories to find it, they keep collapsing into each other. So where exactly does it break, and what do I give up by breaking it there?
