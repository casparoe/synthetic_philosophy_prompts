I've settled on the following package of views about the metaphysics of causation, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it at a workshop. I work mostly on actual (token) causation rather than general or statistical causation.

(1) At the level of general causation, I'm an interventionist in the Woodward–Pearl sense: X causes Y just in case an ideal intervention on X would change Y's value.

(2) I don't think interventionism and the Lewisian counterfactual analysis are rivals. I take possible-worlds counterfactual semantics to be the right formal apparatus for making the interventionist notion precise — an intervention is, at bottom, a counterfactual operation on a causal model — so I see no conflict between (1) and the counterfactual framework.

(3) For actual, token-level causation specifically, I insist on a difference-making requirement: a particular event c is a cause of a particular event e only if, in the actual state of affairs, e would not have occurred (in the relevant respect) had c not occurred.

(4) In cases of causal overdetermination — for example, two guards each fire a single fatal shot at a condemned prisoner at the same instant, and either shot alone would have been sufficient to kill — I hold that each shot is a genuine cause of the death, not merely a contributing factor or a diluted kind of cause.

(5) Causation is transitive: if c causes e and e causes f, then c causes f.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a survey of the literature. For each pair of commitments, tell me whether the tension is real or only apparent; order the real ones by how serious they are; and for each real one, say exactly which commitment would have to be softened or dropped and what I would lose by doing so. I have no idea which of these is doing the damage, so please don't just pick one to sacrifice — I want the dependencies laid bare, and a sense of which consistent package is closest to the one I've stated.
