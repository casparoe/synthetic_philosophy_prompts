I keep telling people the whole taxes-are-theft thing is a dumb slogan, but then I catch myself getting genuinely worked up about specific taxes, so I'm gonna pin down what I actually think. Here's my gut on a bunch of concrete cases, and I want to know if there's a principle behind them or if I'm just inconsistent.

An 8% sales tax on groceries that I can't avoid: feels like a small theft. I never agreed to it, I can't opt out, and it's on stuff I need.

A 37% marginal rate on my bonus, which I could have dodged by structuring the comp differently: feels way less like theft. I had a real escape hatch and I chose not to use it.

Property tax on the house I bought specifically for the school district: barely theft at all. I picked the benefit with my wallet.

Property tax on the same kind of house in a neighborhood that's gone to hell, where the school sucks and I'm stuck because nobody'll buy: feels like a clean theft. Same bill, but I'm paying for somthing I don't get.

A tax I voted for back when I was on the city council: not theft. I signed up for it.

A tax that funds a war I think is unjust: theft, even though I didn't personally vote for it and even though it's nominally for my protection.

A tax that funds vaccines: not theft, even though I didn't vote for it either.

A tax that funds a national park I'll never visit: a mild theft. Worse than the vaccine one, less bad than the war one, but I still feel like I'm being skimmed.

And the one I keep going back and forth on: a tax that funds foreign aid to a country I think is a lost cause, but which I also think is the cheaper way to avoid a refugee crisis that would hit this country harder. I can't opt out, I didn't vote for it, I think it's a waste, and I also think it's the good deal. Is that theft or not?

So: is there a principle that captures all of this, or at least most of it? My best guess is something about whether you had an exit plus whether you actually get the benefit, but that breaks down fast - the grocery tax and the national park tax both have no exit, yet I feel them differently, and the war tax vs. vaccine tax pair seems to hinge on whether I think what's being bought is worth it, not on consent or exit at all. So if a clean principle has to drop one of my verdicts, which one goes, and what does it say about the foreign aid case?

One more thing - when you answer, tell me how confident you are in whatever principle you land on, and single out the one point you're least sure about. Like, which of my verdicts would you be most willing to trade away if the principle demanded it, and how sure are you that's the right trade?
