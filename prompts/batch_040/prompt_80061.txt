I'm a philosophy of science PhD student and this question has been chewing me up for months, so I want a diagnosis more than a verdict. The question is, when we're doing mechanistic anomaly detection, what actually counts as the same mechanism? I'm trying to work out how you'd even tell, in a system you're monitoring, whether an unusual reading is the same mechanism running a bit off or a different mechanism quietly taking over, and every time I raise it in my reading group the conversation just stalls. The people who follow the parts-and-processes account of mechanisms (a mechanism is an assembly of parts and processes organized so they reliably produce a phenomenon) will insist that if the parts and the causal story are unchanged, it's the same mechanism and the anomoly is just a perturbation or noise. The people coming from the ML and interpretability side, and a lot of the biologists I read, will say no, the thing that matters is whether the input-output behavior or the functional role is the same, and the same function can be realized by totally different machinery. We keep circling back to the thermostat example (is a weird heating curve the same thermostat with a drifted sensor, or has some other process grabbed the temperature?), and then someone always brings up homology, where it turns out biologists literally split into a common-ancestry camp and a shared-mechanism camp and never reconcile, and everyone nods and the meeting ends.

My working hypothesis, which I might be wrong about, is that this isn't really one disagreement but two or three questions welded together under the word mechanism -- identity by physical parts, identity by causal process, identity by function -- and each side is quietly using a different one, so it reads like a substantive impasse but is mostly verbal. Or maybe not, and maybe there's a real hidden empirical question doing the work (is the deviation best explained as a perturbation of M or as the operation of some M'), and it's just underdetermined until someone pins down the sameness criterian, in which case the stall is the point and not a bug. Can you actually diagnose what kind of disagreement this is, using what each side says rather than just restating the positions, and tell me whether anything could move it -- a distinction that finally separates the questions, a formal or empirical result, a change of question -- or whether I should just accept that it's not going to resolve and why?
