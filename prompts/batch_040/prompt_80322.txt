I'm an auditor — I do statutory audits under IFRS, so going concern, impairment, off-balance-sheet items, related-party transactions, and materiality are the language I actually think in — and a few weeks ago I was stuck between two friends at a dinner who had started arguing about whether the British Raj was a net good for India. One was making the Niall Ferguson case: that the Empire was a net positive, that it spread markets, rule of law, and infrastructure like railways and the telegraph, and that the real question is the counterfactual — what India would have looked like without it, which he thinks would have meant more war, less trade, and less modernization. The other was making the Chandan Tharoor case: that the Raj was a net catastrophe for India — the famines that killed millions while grain was still being shipped out, the deindustrialization of the textile sector, the systematic drain of wealth back to the metropole, and the violence of conquest — and that a non-imperial India would have done better. I kept thinking the whole fight is really a fight about how to do the accounting on a very long-lived, very diffuse reporting entity, and that if I translated it into the vocabulary I use every day, a lot of the apparent disagreement would either collapse into a technical problem or would turn out to be something the vocabulary simply can't host. So: restate the Ferguson case and the Tharoor case on the British Raj in audit and accounting vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report.

For the setup, treat the Raj as a single reporting entity that spans both the East India Company period and the Crown period, with a de facto period-end and breakup at 1947 — so be explicit about which basis, going concern or liquidation, is the honest lens for the final stretch, and about the fact that the owners are a moving target (the Company's shareholders, then the Crown and the British public), which I read as a real attribution problem rather than a footnote. Where it helps, anchor the translation in concepts I'd recognize: impairment as recoverable amount versus carrying amount, off-balance-sheet items, related-party transactions, and performance attribution in the Brinson sense — allocation versus selection versus interaction — for the question of how much of the post-imperial legacy is attributable to imperial policy versus to secular trends like the Industrial Revolution. And I have a hunch the framework has no home for a counterfactual baseline, which is exactly where Ferguson and Tharoor actually disagree, so test whether that hunch is right and, if it is, name the nearest accounting concept and say why it still falls short.

Deliver the translation as a table with these columns: claim (the Ferguson or Tharoor point in plain English, one or two sentences); accounting rendering (the same point in audit vocabulary); fidelity (exactly one of: clean, distorted, no expression); and residue note (what had to be fudged to fit, or what's missing, in one sentence). Cover at least four distinct claims from each side — the specific pillars, not just the headline net-positive-versus-net-negative. After the table, give me a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: clean_translations (a list of the claims that carry over without distortion), distorted_translations (a list, each entry a claim plus a one-line note on what got warped), inexpressible (a list of the points that have no accounting counterpart at all), and verdict (a short string saying whether the residue looks like a defect of the accounting framework or a sign that the original debate is less clear than it looks, and which one if you think the latter). Skip any reading list or citations — I want the translation and the residue, not a bibliography.
