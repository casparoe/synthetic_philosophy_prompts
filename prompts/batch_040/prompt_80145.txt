I'm making a handout for a class on ethics of nonhuman animals and I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe about the moral status of insects, not by the label they'd pick for themselves. Start everyone at the shared premise that insects show pain-like behavior and have preferences, then make each branch node one of the claims that really separates positions: whether sentience admits of degrees or is binary, whether moral weight tracks how sentient an insect is proportionally or is all-or-nothing once you clear a threshold, whether you treat your uncertainty about sentience as multiplying moral weight (expected-value style) or just as a reason to be cautious, and whether the sheer number of insects changes what's obligatory even when each individual's weight is tiny. Each leaf should carry the position (named if anyone actually defends it, "unnamed" if not) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then mark which leaves the scale argument can actually reach — the idea that even if the chance or degree of sentience per insect is low, there are so many of them that the total moral weight is large — and which it's blocked from, in terms of the branch answers, and flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate plus any empty cell and why it's empty (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).
