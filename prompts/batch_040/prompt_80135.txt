I'm a retired geophysicist (thirty years in the field, so centers of mass are basically secon nature to me) and I've only recently gotten into philosophy. I keep circling back to Dennett's idea that the self is a "center of narrative gravity," because the physics analogy clicks for me: it's a derived point, a useful fiction, not a physical object, the way a center of mass isn't an atom. But the more I poke at it the more I realize I don't actually know what a theory of this thing is supposed to deliver. Before I read any more of the literature I want the spec first.

So write the desiderata. What does an adequate account of the self-as-center-of-narrative-gravity have to get right? I want the full requirements list -- the cases it must handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results from neuroscience it can't contradict -- and the reason each one is a requirement rather than just a preference. Put it in a table with these columns: "desideratum" (a short name for the requirement), "the case it must handle" (a concrete case or puzzle), "why it's a requirement" (the consideration behind it), and "hard or soft" (non-negotiable, or a preference of one camp?).

A few cases I specifically want on the list: the doughnut problem (a center of mass can sit outside the physical object, out in the hole -- so what does it even mean if the self, being a center, can be "nowhere in the body," and does the theory have to say something about that, or is that a reductio); the bootstrapping problem (if the self is a product of the narration, who or what is doing the narrating, and how does the account avoid smuggling back in the very thing it's trying to eliminate); the split-brain and dissociative-identity cases (the center seems to fragment or multiply, so the theory can't just assume one stable center for free); and the group case (a committee, a corporation, a hive, a "we" also seems to have a center of narrative gravity, so the account shouldn't be rigged to onlu work for individuals). And I want the explanatory-indispensability requirement handled properly: the fiction has to do real work, the way the center of mass lets you treat a whole distributed system as a point -- so what is the self-as-fiction actually buying us that we couldn't get without it?

After the table, in prose: flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied -- if the list itself forces a tension (say, between "the self must be causally and explanatorily indispensable" and "the self must not be a real entity in the ontology"), I want that surfaced, because that's the part I most want to see. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out candidates, with a one-line note on how the obvious contenders -- a homunculus/inner-observer view, a simple brain-localized self, and a pure social-constructivist view -- fare against the whole list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not the scoring; I'll do the scorecard myself.
