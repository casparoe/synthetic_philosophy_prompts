I am fluent in that field-science and engineering vocabulary, but not in the philosophical literature, so please begin with a short neutral statement of the positions and then translate them systematically. Pay particular attention to the minimal-device case: a simple system with two output states whose apparent representational content depends entirely on an interpreter's assignment.

For each major claim, say whether it maps cleanly onto my vocabulary, whether it survives only with distortion, or whether it has no expression in that vocabulary at all. In the translation, keep separate three notions that I tend to conflate: a signal being physically produced by a source, being statistically correlated with that source, and being used by a downstream consumer to predict or act. I suspect the philosophical dispute lives in the gap between those notions, but I want to know whether that suspicion is correct.

I am especially interested in the residue. Does the dispute become, in my terms, a disagreement about when a sensor's output can be treated as a representation: when the coupling is reliable, when there is a consumer, when there is a stable error-correction loop, or when the system can be calibrated against some external standard? If the translation suggests that the philosophical debate is less substantive than it looks, say so. If it suggests that my vocabulary is missing something — a normative, first-person, or truth-conditional dimension — identify what that missing dimension is and explain why it cannot be captured by signal-processing concepts alone. Please do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the conceptual work done in the translation and the residue report.
