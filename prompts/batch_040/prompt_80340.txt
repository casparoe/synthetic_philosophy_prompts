I'm a software engineer who now offloads a lot of my design and debugging to an LLM assistant — I regularly ship solutions I couldn't have derived or fully explained on my own. I keep returning to Clark and Chalmers' extended mind thesis, which holds that an external resource like a notebook counts as part of your mind when it is constantly available, automatically accessed, and immediately trusted. My assistant is constantly available and automatically consulted, but it lacks the reliability a notebook has: a notebook merely preserves what you wrote, whereas my assistant actively generates plausible-sounding claims it is sometimes wrong about, and it quietly changes when the provider updates the model. What are the most important implications for my own understanding of my codebase, if, on this view, my understanding is now partly located in the hybrid system (me plus the assistant) rather than entirely in my brain — in particular, is my understanding being extended, eroded, or something neither word captures, and what happens to it when the assistant is deprecated or swapped for a different model?
