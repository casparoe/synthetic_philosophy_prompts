My friend just waved me off with the old line "history is written by the victors" (often attributed to Churchill, probably wrongly — I've never seen a source), as if that settled it, and it really did in the conversation, which is why I want to seperate out what the line is actually doing.

The problem: the slogan seems to predict that the defeated side's story would be bleak, suppressed, or just the victors' version, but the pattern I'm describing is the opposite — the losers are the ones producing the most dramatic version in circulation. And Dunkirk is the odd one out: Britain won the war, so by the slogan the British account should be the trustworthy one, but the "miracle of Dunkirk" framing is itself a spin, an evacuation reframed as a victory. I also suspect "victors" is doing ambiguous work — victor of the battle? of the war? of the century? — but I can't pin it down.
