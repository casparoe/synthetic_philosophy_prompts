In the many-worlds interpretation, the Born rule problem is the question of how probability makes sense in a deterministic theory in which every possible outcome occurs. I want a number, not a survey of the debate: your credence, between 0 and 1, that the decision-theoretic derivation of the Born rule (the Deutsch–Wallace line of argument) is a genuine, non-circular derivation. For the purposes of this estimate, count the proposition as true if a rational agent who knows only the unitary quantum dynamics and a small set of rational-agent axioms — and who does not already know the Born rule — would be rationally compelled to assign credences to the branches in proportion to the squared amplitudes.

I want the number and the decomposition: the sub-questions the credence depends on, a rough probability for each, and how they combine. At minimum, decompose along these lines: (a) whether the rational-agent axioms the derivation relies on are acceptable, or whether they are themselves loaded with Born-rule content (which would make the derivation circular); (b) whether "self-locating uncertainty" — the idea that before the measurement the agent is genuinely uncertain about which branch they will find themselves on, even though every branch occurs — is a coherent and well-defined notion in a deterministic multiverse, or a confused borrowing of single-world probability language; (c) whether the "indifference" or "symmetry" assumption in the derivation is quietly doing the work that the Born weights should do; (d) whether the agent's "payoffs" or "utilities" across branches are already, in effect, Born-weighted (another route to circularity); and (e) whether the derivation generalizes beyond the simple, symmetric cases it is usually presented in. Assign a rough probability to each, combine them into a single number, and tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move it most.

For context, in case the details aren't fresh: the Deutsch–Wallace argument is roughly that a rational agent about to undergo a measurement (who will, in the Everett picture, split into one copy per branch) should bet on the outcomes as if the Born rule gives the probabilities, because the standard axioms of rational choice, modified for branching, force the weighting of branches by squared amplitudes. The main lines of objection are: the circularity objection (the axioms or setup already presuppose the Born rule); the self-locating-uncertainty objection (it's unclear what "uncertain" means when you'll be in every branch); the indifference objection (an indifference principle is doing the real work); and the "typicality" or "measure-based" alternative (the Born rule should be understood via typicality or measure over the branches, not via rational betting). You don't need to resolve these; I want your credence that the derivation is non-circular and genuine, with the decomposition.
