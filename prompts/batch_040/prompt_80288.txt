Write a villanelle in which the two refrains are, respectively, the transworld-identity line — that the very same individual exists in many possible worlds — and the counterpart-theory line — that a modal claim about me is really a claim about a distinct but similar individual in another world. Let the returning lines do the work of two positions that keep passing each other: each refrain restates its case and slides past the other without settling the dispute. The volta should be the moment the reader realizes the argument is not only about other worlds. The very problem that pushes one to deny transworld identity — the problem of accidental intrinsics, the thought that the same individual could not both be tall and not-tall — also seems to threaten identity through time, so that the self I was five minutes ago is a counterpart of me rather than identical to me. The closing couplet should set the two refrains side by side and let that diachronic consequence land. Someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which line is Kripkean and which is Lewisian and why they end up together at the end, and the metaphysics should be accurate enough that a philosopher of modality would not wince.
