I am mediating an adversarial collaboration between two of my colleagues, one a defender of the European practice of banning commercial surrogacy (allowing, at most, altruistic arrangements) and the other a defender of the American practice of a regulated commercial market, and I would like you to draft the joint statement the two of them could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. Give me first the claims both camps can genuinely accept — about what the two regimes actually do in practice and about the values each invokes (autonomy, protection from exploitation, the child's welfare) and how each reads them — then the precise points of residual disagreement, each phrased so its own camp would accept the wording, and then the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect two cruxes are whether a prohibition that drives the practice underground increases or decreases the very harm it purports to prevent, and whether the fact that even the ban jurisdictions permit paid egg donation reveals an incoherence in the ban's own rationale; for each crux, supply a concrete case or piece of evidence both camps would agree bears on it and record each camp's prediction in advance. Write the whole thing as one continuous prose memorandum addressed to both scholars — no headings, no bullet points, no tables, no bold — the way one would write a letter. Close with a brief note on where your own drafting is most likely to fail: which of the agreed claims is more contested than it looks, which crux you may have misidentified, and what evidence or argument would lead you to rephrase a crux or to doubt that the two could sign at all.
