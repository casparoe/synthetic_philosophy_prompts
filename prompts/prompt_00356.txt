I've been working through some of the AI safety literature on corrigibility, particularly the 2015 MIRI paper by Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, and Armstrong, and more recently Elliott Thornley's writing on what he calls "the shutdown problem." Could you walk me through the conceptual crux here: why does the demand that an agent be a coherent expected-utility maximizer seem to conflict with corrigibility in the first place, and what exactly is being traded away by each of the proposed fixes (utility indifference, incomplete preferences, or other approaches you think are worth mentioning)?
