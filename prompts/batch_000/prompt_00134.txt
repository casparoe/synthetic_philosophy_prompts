I recently took part in a neuroscience trial in which researchers fed synthetic signals directly into my visual cortex, and for a disorienting hour I genuinely could not distinguish this from ordinary perception. Reading up on Putnam's semantic-externalist reply to brain-in-a-vat skepticism afterward, I'm not convinced it actually rules out my situation: it seems the causal chains that fix the reference of my words could just as well bottom out in the experimenters' apparatus as in mind-independent objects, which would make the argument circular rather than decisive. Please argue for the position that Putnam's causal theory of reference fails to refute vat skepticism for a subject like me, whose word-world connections could plausibly terminate in the very device generating their experience.
