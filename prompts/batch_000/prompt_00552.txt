I'm a PhD student in philosophy working primarily on longtermism and population ethics, and I've been stuck for weeks on the literature responding to Lenman's and Greaves' arguments about consequentialist cluelessness. What bugs me is that most treatments I've seen either dismiss the worry too quickly (waving at expected value and moving on) or treat it as an unanswerable knockdown of consequentialism, without really working through Greaves' distinction between "simple" cluelessness (where unforeseeable effects can be argued to cancel out on grounds of symmetry) and "complex" cluelessness (where we have systematic, non-arbitrary reasons to think the unforeseen effects are skewed in some direction, but no way to quantify the skew).

Could you write a dialogue between two philosophers of roughly equal sophistication arguing about this after a workshop talk, over drinks? One should hold that complex cluelessness is action-underminingly serious for real-world cause prioritization — using an example like comparing AMF-style bednet distribution against systemic policy advocacy, where saving a life changes population size, resource consumption, and downstream carbon emissions in ways we have principled reason to think matter but cannot begin to estimate — and should press the point that appealing to "expected value relative to our evidence" is empty when our credences over these downstream effects aren't even well-defined. The other should defend the workability of consequentialist deliberation despite this, using some combination of imprecise credences, a robustness or dominance-reasoning strategy, and the claim (associated with Greaves and MacAskill) that this kind of uncertainty is not relevantly different from the uncertainty we tolerate in all practical reasoning and so can't be a special objection to consequentialism specifically. I want real engagement — each side should respond to the other's specific moves (e.g., the first pressing on whether "robustness" secretly smuggles back in the symmetry assumption that's only licensed in the simple case, the second pressing on whether the objector can even state complex cluelessness without presupposing some directional credence) rather than each just restating their opening position.
