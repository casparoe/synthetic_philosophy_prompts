So I've been going down a rabbit hole researching AI safety/game theory stuff for a piece I'm writing, and I keep running into this idea of "commitment races" -- I think it's originally from Wei Dai on LessWrong, in the context of superintelligent agents bargaining with each other. The basic gist I'm getting is that agents have an incentive to lock in commitments (like "I will never give in to threats" or some Dr. Strangelove-esque doomsday-device type pledge) as early as possible, before they've fully reasoned things through, because being committed first gives you leverage over agents who commit later or who stay flexible. But that seems to create this weird race to the bottom where everyone wants to bind themselves irrevocably ASAP, sometimes based on very little reasoning, which seems like a recipe for disaster if two agents lock in incompatible commitments.

Can you help me get an actual intuitive handle on this? Like why can't agents just reason their way out of the race by anticipating it? What actually breaks down? I don't need the full formal treatment with all the decision-theory jargon (updatelessness, logical time, etc.) unless it's load-bearing for the explanation -- I mostly want the gut-level "aha" of why smart, careful reasoners would still end up racing to commit rather than just... not doing that.
