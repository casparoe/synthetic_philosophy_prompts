So this weekend I was at a music festival about 40 min outside the city, and my phone died before I could order a Lyft home. This random guy offered to drive me back, but said upfront "look, I've been burned before, so I'll only do this if you swear you'll venmo me $25 for gas once we get to your place." I obviously said yes, we start driving, and he mentions almost casually that he's pretty good at telling when people are bullshitting him and has turned down way more people than he's helped. We get to my apartment and I paid him, but afterward I couldn't stop thinking about whether I "had" to. Like, once I'm home safe, what's actually in it for me to pay up? The ride already happened either way.

I brought this up with my decision theory seminar group (we've been going through classical causal decision theory vs some of the newer updateless approaches) and it turned into a whole argument. A couple people said obviously once you're home the rational move is to just keep your money since the drive is a sunk cost at that point, but that felt off to me given that this guy's whole business model depends on being able to spot people who'd think that way and just not pick them up in the first place. Is there an actual principled reason I should've paid him beyond just "guilt" or "being a good person," or does standard rationality really say I got a free ride (pun intended) once the car was already in my driveway?
