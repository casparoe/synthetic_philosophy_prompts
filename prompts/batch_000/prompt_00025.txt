David Hume died in 1776, decades before the mature statistical mechanics of Boltzmann and the discovery of genuinely probabilistic laws in quantum mechanics. Given his regularity-based account of causation and skepticism about necessary connections, how do you think Hume would have responded to irreducibly probabilistic laws and to the modern Humean project (à la David Lewis's "best system" analysis) of grounding laws of nature in patterns of particular fact? Would he have embraced Humean supervenience as a natural extension of his own views, or found something in it to resist?
