Last night a friend and I spent hours arguing about evidential cooperation in large worlds—specifically, whether the sheer likelihood that our universe contains countless causally isolated but psychologically similar agents gives us genuine reason to act as if we'd struck a bargain with them, even though no actual causal interaction (or even acausal decision-theoretic mechanism beyond correlation) is possible. I suspect this collapses into wishful cosmic reasoning that smuggles in obligations we haven't earned; my friend insists it's a hard-nosed implication of taking decision theory and a large or infinite universe seriously. Write a dialogue between a proponent of evidential cooperation in large worlds and a skeptical decision theorist, in which they work through a concrete case—say, whether an altruist should redirect part of a limited charitable budget toward causes favored by hypothetical distant agents whose values they don't share—with each pressing the other on the weakest points of their position rather than talking past them.
