Write an essay on the controversy over whether instrumental convergence genuinely supports the orthogonality thesis or subtly undermines it. Bostrom and others argue that nearly any final goal, pursued by a sufficiently capable agent, will tend to produce convergent instrumental subgoals (self-preservation, resource acquisition, goal-content integrity). Some critics contend that this convergence itself implies constraints on what "intelligence" and "goals" can coherently combine, suggesting the space of realistic agents is less orthogonal than the thesis claims. Weigh whether instrumental convergence is a truth compatible with -- and even explanatory of -- orthogonality, or whether it reveals hidden dependencies between intelligence and terminal values that the orthogonality thesis denies. Defend a clear verdict.
