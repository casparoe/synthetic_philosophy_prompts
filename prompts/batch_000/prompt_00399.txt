I mentioned this interpretation to a friend who's more into decision theory as a hobby, and they pushed back, saying that Death in Damascus is actually supposed to be a serious problem for CDT as well, not just for EDT, and that this is precisely why it's considered a harder case than something like Newcomb's problem. That surprised me, since I thought the whole appeal of causal decision theory was that it doesn't get tripped up by these prediction-correlation setups. Could you explain why CDT would still run into difficulty in this scenario, and what my reasoning above is missing?
