I teach a seminar on Sartre's existentialism, and I'm weary of student fiction that leans on his own stock illustrations of bad faith — the waiter performing "waiterness," or the woman ignoring the hand on her knee. Write an original short story, set in a milieu of your choosing, that dramatizes a character's descent into bad faith as Sartre defines it: the flight from the anguish of radical freedom into a false self-conception as a fixed thing with a determinate nature, rather than as a being condemned to continually choose. The mechanism of this self-deception should be legible through the character's choices and interior monologue alone — do not name Sartre, bad faith, or existentialism anywhere in the text.
