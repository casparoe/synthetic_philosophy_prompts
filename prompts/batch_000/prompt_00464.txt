I'm drafting the root-cause section of an incident report after an accident at the warehouse I manage, and two colleagues on the review committee keep clashing over how to identify the "true" cause. One insists we should ask, for each candidate factor, whether the injury would still have occurred had that factor been absent, and build our account from whichever factors pass that test. The other argues this method sweeps in too many irrelevant coincidences, and that we should instead ask which factors we could have deliberately changed, holding everything else fixed, to alter the outcome. Their disagreement isn't just semantic — it's leading them to name different people and procedures as responsible. Which approach is more defensible for pinning down what actually caused the accident, and how should I explain the difference to the committee?
