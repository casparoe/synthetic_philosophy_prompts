So I've got a job offer that's been messing with my head for weeks. It's a really well-paid data science role at a defense contractor, basically building targeting software for drones. Part of me thinks: look, someone's gonna build this stuff whether I'm involved or not, and if I take the job maybe I can push for more accurate targeting that kills fewer civilians than if some less careful engineer does it instead - so net-net it's probably better if I'm the one in the seat. But another part of me feels like there's just something wrong with helping build a system whose whole purpose is to kill people, full stop, regardless of how the math on outcomes shakes out - like some lines shouldn't be crossed even if crossing them leads to marginally better consequences. The annoying part is I genuinely don't know which way of thinking about this I actually buy. Some days the outcomes-focused reasoning feels obviously right and the other view feels like squeamishness dressed up as principle. Other days it flips completely and the outcomes reasoning feels like a rationalization. I brought this up with a couple friends over drinks and they just said "go with your gut," which isn't super helpful since my gut keeps changing its mind depending on the day. My partner thinks I'm way overanalyzing what should be a simple decision, but it feels to me like the fact that I can't settle on which framework is correct is itself important information for how I should decide, not just a personal quirk I need to get over. How should I actually go about making this call given that I can't resolve which ethical theory is the right one to be using?
