My colleague and I run a small consulting partnership and split the profits from our joint contracts according to an informal agreement anchored to what each of us would earn if we worked entirely on our own. Recently, my colleague received an extremely lucrative outside job offer that they have no real intention of accepting, but they argue that this offer raises their theoretical "ceiling" in our partnership and therefore entitles them to a larger share of our joint profits, even though neither of our actual fallback positions has changed. It strikes me as strange that a hypothetical outcome neither of us will ever realize should affect how we divide real, current earnings, but I can't quite articulate why this bothers me. Is my colleague's reasoning defensible, or am I right to resist it?
