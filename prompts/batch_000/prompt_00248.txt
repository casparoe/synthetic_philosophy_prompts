During a recent incident review, I noticed something that's been bothering me. Our internal status dashboard had actually frozen and stopped pulling live data about three hours before an outage was detected, but the last state it displayed before freezing happened to read "All systems operational." Coincidentally, at the exact moment our on-call engineer checked it, all systems genuinely were operational (the outage hadn't started yet). She told leadership, "I know everything's fine, I just verified it on the dashboard," and technically her belief was both true and reasonably formed given the information she had. But once we discovered the dashboard had been frozen the whole time and wasn't actually monitoring anything, it felt wrong to say she really "knew" the systems were healthy at that moment, even though nothing in her reasoning was careless. I can't quite articulate why this bothers me. Was she justified in claiming she knew, or is there something conceptually broken about calling this knowledge at all?
