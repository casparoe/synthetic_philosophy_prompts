I've been mulling over Hofstadter's notion of superrationality, and it seems like most explanations insist it only applies when two players are exact, deterministic copies of one another -- otherwise there's supposedly no guarantee their reasoning converges on the same output. I suspect this requirement is too strict, and that mere similarity in how two agents reason, short of literal duplication, is enough to justify mutual cooperation in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma. Please make the strongest case you can for this looser reading of superrationality, and address the obvious worry that without exact identity there's no guarantee the other party actually arrives at the same conclusion you do.
