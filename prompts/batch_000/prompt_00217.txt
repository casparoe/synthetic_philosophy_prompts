Hey, so I've been down a rabbit hole on Molinism ever since a debate with a religious friend about free will and divine foreknowledge got kind of heated. The concept of "middle knowledge" - that God supposedly knows all these counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (like "if Sarah were placed in situation S, she would freely choose X") even *before* deciding which world to actualize - reminded me wierdly of branch prediction in CPUs, where the processor speculatively computes down a path before it's confirmed the branch will actually be taken. But my engineer brain keeps snagging on the same objection every time: what's the truthmaker for these counterfactuals? In a CPU, the "ground truth" for a prediction eventually resolves when the branch actually executes. For Molinism's counterfactuals of freedom though, there's allegedly no fact of the matter grounding them independent of an actual free choice being made - this is basically Robert Adams' "grounding objection," which I gather is a pretty big deal in this literature.

I want you to actually argue for a position here rather than just summarizing the debate: does the grounding objection succeed in sinking Molinism, or can Molinists give an adequate response to it (e.g. some kind of primitivism about counterfactual truth, or just denying that all contingent truths need external grounds)? Pick a side and defend it with real arguments, don't hedge or land on "it depends."
