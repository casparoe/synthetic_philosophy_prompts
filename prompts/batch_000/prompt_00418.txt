So I'm taking a set theory elective and we just covered Godel and Cohen's results on the continuum hypothesis, and honestly it kind of broke my brain a little. Our professor described CH as "independent of ZFC" and then in the next breath compared it to the parallel postulate in geometry -- like, once you know Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometries are both consistent, the question "is the parallel postulate actually true" just dissolves into "true relative to which axioms you pick." So doesn't the same thing happen with CH? Once Cohen showed you can have models of ZFC where CH holds and models where it fails, isn't asking whether CH is "really" true or false a kind of category error, like asking whether Euclidean geometry is the "real" geometry?

I brought this up with a friend and they said actual set theorists (like Woodin apparently) still argue about whether CH is true and spend careers looking for new axioms to settle it, which seems bizzare to me if the independence result already showed the question is meaningless. Am I missing something here, or is this really just a matter of taste in which axioms you like?
