Aumann's agreement theorem is a staple of rationalist discourse: two Bayesians with common priors who achieve common knowledge of each other's posteriors cannot rationally continue to disagree. Please argue that this result loses much of its normative force once computational complexity is taken seriously. Specifically, defend the claim that the theorem's reliance on unbounded common-knowledge iteration obscures the fact that reaching, or even verifying, common knowledge may be computationally intractable for bounded reasoners, and that persistent disagreement between otherwise epistemically virtuous but computationally limited agents is therefore not, by itself, evidence of irrationality.
