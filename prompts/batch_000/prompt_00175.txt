A friend and I just spent an hour arguing about this: my sister was diagnosed with ADHD last year, and now attributes most of her inconsiderate behavior—forgetting plans, interrupting people, being late to everything—to her condition. My friend thinks that once you know someone's behavior has a neurological cause, it's basically irrational to keep feeling resentment or anger toward them; the only reasonable response is to adjust your expectations and manage the relationship accordingly, the way you would with someone who has a physical disability. I pushed back, arguing that if we go too far down that road, we stop relating to people as people and start treating them like problems to be managed. Who has the better argument here, and is there a principled way to decide when it's still appropriate to hold someone responsible versus when we should just adapt around their condition?
