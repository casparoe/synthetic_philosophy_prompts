So I've been building autonomous trading agents for a small crypto startup, and my co-founder keeps bringing up this "program equilibrium" idea from game theory to convince me we don't need a ton of defensive logic against other bots gaming our system. His pitch is basically: once two agents can read each others source code (which they can, since ours are open-source contracts on chain), they're mathematically stuck in a cooperative equilibrium, because neither one benefits from defecting once the other can literally verify what you're going to do. He keeps citing the Löbian cooperation stuff and the Tennenholtz paper like it settles the matter.

This feels almost too good to be true to me, like we could just publish our bot's source and rival firms' bots would be rationally forced into cooperating with us instead of exploiting us. Is that actually how it works? Should I be pushing our team to make everything source-visible so we get this equilibrium for free, or am I missing something about what program equilibrium actually guarantees here? Trying to figure out how much engineering effort to actually put into anti-exploitation safeguards vs just leaning on the theory.
