So I've been reading about Newcomb's problem again after seeing it come up on LessWrong for the millionth time, and I still don't get why one-boxing is considered rational by so many smart people. Like, the predictor already made its prediction and put the money in the boxes before you even choose right? So causally your decision now can't reach back in time and change what's already there. Doesn't that just settle it in favor of two-boxing, since backward causation is impossible? Why do people keep treating this as some deep unresolved paradox instead of just admitting two-boxers are right and moving on?
