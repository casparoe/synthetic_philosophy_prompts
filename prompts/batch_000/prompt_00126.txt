So last night my friend Deshaun and I got into this whole thing about how likely it is that our buddy's new food truck is gonna still be running a year from now. We started off pretty far apart—I said like 70%, he said 35%—and then we just kept going back and forth for almost an hour, each telling the other our updated number after hearing theirs. Like I'd hear his reasoning, nudge my number down a bit, tell him my new number, he'd nudge his up a bit, etc. We both know each other pretty well and genuinely think the other person is a resonable, non-biased thinker who has access to basically the same info about the food truck (menu, location, the guy's work ethic, startup capital, all of it). But after like five or six rounds of trading numbers we plateaued at 55% vs 40% and just kinda threw up our hands and agreed to disagree.

It's bugging me though. If we both trust each other to be honest and rational, and we're not withholding evidence from each other, shouldn't our numbers have actually converged instead of just stalling out with a gap? Deshaun's take is that "agreeing to disagree" is a totally normal, healthy endpoint for two reasonable people. My gut says something is off about that—like if we really did have the same info and really did trust each other's rationality, ending up at different numbers means one of us is quietly doing something wrong, even if neither of us can point to what. Who's actually right here, and is there something to the idea that persistent disagreement between two people who consider each other rational is even possible in principle?
