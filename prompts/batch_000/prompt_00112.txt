For my philosophy of physics seminar, I've been writing about the quantum suicide thought experiment, and I think it actually gives us a way to empirically favor Everett's many-worlds interpretation over collapse theories like Copenhagen. My reasoning: if I ran the apparatus over many trials, a collapse theorist should expect that I'd almost certainly end up dead, whereas under Everett I'm guaranteed to find myself alive in some branch no matter how many rounds are run. So if I actually survived dozens of trials, wouldn't that constitute genuine first-person evidence in favor of many-worlds? Or is there a flaw in treating this asymmetry as an epistemic advantage for the Everettian?
