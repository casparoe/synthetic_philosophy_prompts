So I've been going down a rabbit hole of old LessWrong posts on coherence theorems and money-pump arguments (the Eliezer "coherent decisions imply consistent utilities" post, plus some of the discussion it spawned), and I think I finally get the basic argument: if your preferences are intransitive or otherwise incomplete, someone can construct a sequence of trades that pumps money out of you, so "rational" agents get forced into having a consistent utility function they maximize. My roommate and I were arguing about what this means for AI safety, and he made a claim that's been bugging me since: he says this basically proves corrigibility is a lost cause, because any sufficiently capable agent will inevitably get money-pumped out of any "let humans shut me down" preference that isn't already baked into a fixed utility function, so it'll self-modify (or just behave, over time) into a ruthless EU-maximizer with some goal that has nothing to do with corrigibility, no matter how we train it initially. Basically his view is that coherence arguments are a mathematical proof that incomplete/corrigible preferences can't survive contact with optimization pressure, full stop. Is this actually what the theorems show, or is he overstating what money-pump arguments can prove here?
