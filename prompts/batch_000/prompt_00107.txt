Argue that in program equilibrium settings, a mutual cooperation outcome sustained by agents submitting transparent source code (e.g., via variants of Löbian cooperation à la modal fixed-point programs) does not actually vindicate anything like rational cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma, since the "cooperation" achieved is an artifact of proof-theoretic coincidence rather than of either party's practical reasoning tracking the other's expected behavior in a genuinely game-theoretic sense.
