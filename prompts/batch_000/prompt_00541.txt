In my physics elective, we've been discussing the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, which got me curious about the quantum suicide thought experiment. Proponents of the resulting "quantum immortality" conclusion argue that if many-worlds is true, each of us should subjectively expect to go on surviving indefinitely, since our future first-person experience can only ever be continuous with branches in which we survive. Critics counter that this reasoning either misapplies anthropic probability or smuggles in a confused notion of personal identity across branching worlds. Write an essay assessing whether the quantum immortality argument succeeds on its own terms, assuming the many-worlds interpretation is correct. Weigh the strongest considerations on both sides, but end with a definite conclusion rather than a noncommittal one.
