For our school's math club fundraiser, a classmate proposed a game: he'll flip a coin repeatedly until it lands tails, paying $2 if tails comes up on the first flip, $4 on the second, $8 on the third, and so on, doubling with each additional flip. He calculated that the average payout across all possible outcomes is technically infinite, and argued we should therefore charge a very high entry fee, since supposedly no fee would be "unfair" to charge. This doesn't sit right with me, since I can't imagine any reasonable person paying, say, $100 to play, but I can't quite explain why my intuition and his math seem to disagree. How should I think about what a fair price to charge actually is, and what's going wrong with his reasoning?
