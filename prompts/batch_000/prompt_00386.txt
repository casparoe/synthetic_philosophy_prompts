So I've been chewing on the problem of old evidence for a while now (the classic Glymour example: Mercury's perihelion precession was old news by the time GR explained it, yet everyone treated it as huge confirmation for Einstein). Standard Bayesian confirmation theory seems to choke on this, because if P(e) = 1 already, then P(H|e) = P(H), so technically no updating can happen -- but that clashes hard with how science actually works. I've seen two big camps trying to patch this up: Garber and Jeffrey's move of relaxing logical omniscience (so the "new evidence" is really the discovery of the entailment between H and e, not e itself), versus the counterfactualist approach (a la Howson) where you ask what your credence in e would've been had you not already known it for certain.

I was arguing about this in a rationalist discord last week and kind of took the counterfactualist side by default, but now I'm second-guessing myself. Can you make the strongest case for the Garber-Jeffrey style resolution (dropping logical omniscience) as the better fix for Bayesian confirmation theory, over the counterfactual credence approach? I'd like you to get into the weeds on what "logical omniscience" even cashes out to for a bounded, non-ideal reasoner, since that part still feels pretty hand-wavey to me, and explain concretely why the counterfactualist move ends up being less principled or running into its own problems.
