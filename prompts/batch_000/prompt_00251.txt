I've been following the rationalist blogosphere since the old Overcoming Bias days, and Aumann's agreement theorem comes up constantly in discussions about disagreement and epistemic humility. Last week a friend and I had a long, genuinely good-faith argument about whether a mutual acquaintance's startup is going to succeed, and after three hours we still landed on opposite conclusions despite laying out all our evidence and reasoning for each other as clearly as we could. Since neither of us think the other is stupid or dishonest, I've been telling him that Aumann's theorem basically proves one of us *must* be making some kind of reasoning error, because two rational people with access to the same information aren't supposed to be able to agree to disagree once they know each other's actual views. He's skeptical that a math theorem can say anything about a messy real-world disagreement like ours. Assuming I'm remembering the theorem correctly, is there a practical method we could use to figure out which of us is the one being irrational, or at least to force our beliefs to converge the way the theorem says they should?
