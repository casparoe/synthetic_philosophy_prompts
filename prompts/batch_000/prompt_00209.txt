Longtime readers of the acausal trade literature will know Caspar Oesterheld's proposal that agents can defang blackmail by adopting a "surrogate goal": some minor value they precommit to caring about intensely, so that a rational blackmailer, indifferent between targets, extorts the surrogate instead of whatever the agent actually holds dear. What I've never seen clearly addressed is the regress this seems to invite: if a sufficiently sophisticated blackmailer can infer that the agent's willingness to pay out on the surrogate is itself just a policy adopted for its acausal payoffs, why can't they threaten that underlying policy directly, or threaten the agent's "true" values in a way that routes around the redirection entirely? Please explain how the surrogate-goal proposal is meant to block this regress, and clarify whether proponents think it succeeds against arbitrarily sophisticated threateners or only against some more limited class of them.
