For the past few months I have been dealing with something strange that I would like an outside perspective on. I have a demanding job that requires handling several channels of communication simultaneously, and on a handful of occasions I have apparently carried on a complete, coherent text exchange with a colleague—complete with specific project details and appropriate jokes—that I have no memory of afterward, even though at the time I was fully absorbed in an unrelated, equally complex phone conversation. My colleagues insist that I was lucid and responsive throughout the texting; it was not gibberish or out of character, and it referenced information I could only have known if I had actually been attending to it. A therapist suggested this might be a mild dissociative response to stress, but what unsettles me more than any diagnosis is a conceptual puzzle it raises. If there were two coherent, contextually appropriate streams of thought and action running at once, and I can only consciously recall one of them, does it even make sense to say that "I" was the one who sent those texts? Or would it be more accurate to say that some other, semi-independent center of agency in my brain was responsible, with "me" being nothing more than whichever stream happens to retain the memory afterward? I am not looking for a clinical opinion—I have that covered—but I would appreciate help thinking through what a situation like this actually implies about whether I am one continuous subject of experience or something more fragmented, and how that should bear on how I assign responsibility for things "I" apparently did but cannot remember doing.
