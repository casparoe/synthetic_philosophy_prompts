As a retired physicist who has lately been reading through the metaphysics of science literature, I find myself stuck on the "explanatory circularity" objection to Humean accounts of laws, especially Lewis's Best System Analysis. If a law is nothing more than an axiom or theorem in the simplest, strongest systematization of the entire Humean mosaic, it seems laws cannot also explain the very regularities they merely summarize, since explanantia are ordinarily thought to be distinct from and prior to what they explain. Yet Humeans such as Loewer insist this worry trades on a question-begging, anti-Humean picture of explanation. Could you lay out clearly why the circularity charge is thought to have force, and how sophisticated Humeans attempt to defuse it?
