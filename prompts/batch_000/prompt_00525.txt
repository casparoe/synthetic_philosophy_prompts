After forty years as a physicist, I've spent my retirement working through the literature on infinite ethics, and one result keeps nagging at me: apparently no social welfare ordering on infinite utility streams can satisfy both Strong Pareto and Finite Anonymity while remaining complete, unless one invokes non-constructive tools like free ultrafilters. Write a dialogue between two philosophers debating how to respond to this impossibility result. One should defend the overtaking (or catching-up) criterion, accepting that many streams will simply be incomparable in exchange for staying fully constructive. The other should accept an ultrafilter-based extension that delivers a complete ordering, even though it settles comparisons using a choice principle that cannot be explicitly described or justified case by case. Have them engage directly with each other's strongest objections -- on whether ethical silence is more defensible than ethical arbitrariness, and whether completeness is worth purchasing with indeterminacy of a different kind -- rather than simply restating their own positions.
