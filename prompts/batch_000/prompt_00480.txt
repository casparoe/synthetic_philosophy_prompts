Having spent my career studying signal detection in the visual systems of anurans, I've recently been drawn into the philosophical literature on teleosemantics, and I keep getting stuck on the frog's fly-detector. Millikan and her allies want to say that natural selection can fix determinate content for such states, silencing the disjunction problem; their critics reply that the very same selection history is equally well explained by "small dark moving thing" as by "fly," so teleology can't do the disambiguating work it's advertised to do. Write a dialogue between a committed teleosemanticist and a causal-informational semanticist skeptical of teleosemantics's ability to fix determinate content, using the frog case as their running example. Have each press the other on their strongest point rather than a caricature of it, and let the exchange end without one side simply conceding.
