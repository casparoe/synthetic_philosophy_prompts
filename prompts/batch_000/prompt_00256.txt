But a classmate pointed out that this equivalence, due to Pearce and Bernheim, is clean only in two-player games, and that once you move to games with three or more players, using mixed-strategy dominance to eliminate strategies quietly assumes that each player believes their opponents randomize independently of one another — an assumption that isn't obviously forced by common knowledge of rationality alone, especially if you allow for correlated beliefs across opponents.

Write a dialogue between two game theorists hashing this out: one who defends iterated elimination via mixed-strategy dominance as the correct operationalization of common knowledge of rationality, and one who is skeptical, pressing the independence worry and wondering whether "correlated rationalizability" rather than the more restrictive "independent" notion is the more defensible epistemic benchmark. Have them work through a small, concrete three-player example to make the disagreement vivid, let each side genuinely respond to the other's objections rather than just restating their own position, and don't let either side come off as obviously right from the outset.
