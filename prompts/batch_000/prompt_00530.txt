In teaching Bayesian epistemology, I want to sharpen my students' grasp of a distinction that's often glossed over too quickly. The standard (synchronic) Dutch book argument is meant to establish probabilism: that an agent's credences at a single time should conform to the probability axioms on pain of guaranteed loss. But a further, diachronic version of the argument is sometimes invoked to justify updating by strict conditionalization over time. Please explain how the diachronic argument differs in structure and underlying assumptions from the synchronic one, and why philosophers such as Levi have contended that the diachronic version fails to establish a genuine rationality requirement even granting that the synchronic argument succeeds.
