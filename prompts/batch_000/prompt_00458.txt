Having spent forty years running scattering experiments rather than reading Parfit, I've lately become preoccupied with quantum immortality -- not the crude version where you point a gun at your head hooked to a Geiger counter, but the subtler anthropic claim that any observer, conditioned on their own continued existence across enough branching events, should expect from the inside to go on and on, however astronomically improbable that looks from the outside. I'd like to see this dramatized with the philosophical bite left intact rather than sanded down into a survival-horror trope. Write a short story following a single character through a series of near-fatal branchings in an Everettian universe, one that takes seriously the asymmetry between first-person anticipation and third-person probability, and follows the idea to its uncomfortable end: what such a person would notice about their own life, and what, if anything, it should change about how they act once they do.
