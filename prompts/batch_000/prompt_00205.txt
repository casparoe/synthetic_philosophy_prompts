I've spent a lot of time thinking about Aumann's agreement theorem and why it's supposed to be irrational for two honest people who trust each other's judgment to keep disagreeing once they've shared their views. Write a dialogue between two old friends, both amateur poker players, who hold sharply different opinions about whether a particular hand was misplayed. One of them has read about the theorem and insists that their continued disagreement, now that they've each heard the other's reasoning in full, means one of them must be fooling themselves or holding something back. The other pushes back, arguing that the theorem only works under conditions, like common priors and full mutual transparency, that never really hold between two actual people with different histories and blind spots. Have them go back and forth seriously, each responding to the other's strongest points, without either one simply conceding or being made to look foolish.
