I'm a novelist, and my longtime co-author and I are in the middle of adapting our jointly written trilogy into a television series. The studio's deal with "us" as a unit is already signed and fair, splitting overall rights and residuals evenly, but now we're privately negotiating how the two of us will divide showrunner credit and back-end bonuses between ourselves. My co-author has proposed something that unsettles me: that we each, independently and without telling the other beforehand, send the studio's business affairs office a memo staking out our preferred terms (e.g., "I should be sole showrunner, she consulting producer"), on the theory that whichever memo arrives first will anchor the studio's expectations in that person's favor. The trouble is obvious to me even if I can't quite name why: if we both do this and our memos contradict each other, the studio could get nervous about working with two people who can't agree internally, and shelve the adaptation altogether, leaving us both worse off than if we'd just split things down the middle from the start.

I keep circling back to the sense that there ought to be some way to preserve each of our incentives to advocate for a good outcome without exposing the whole project to this kind of self-inflicted collapse — some arrangement that captures whatever gains either of us might get from staking out a strong position, but without the risk that comes from doing it unilaterally and in secret. I'm not a game theorist, just someone who's watched enough business deals in Hollywood implode over pride and bad timing, and this feels like a more general problem than just our particular contract. What should I actually propose to my co-author instead of the dueling-memos plan, and is my instinct that "there's a smarter, safer way to get the same benefits" actually well-founded, or am I just being naive about how negotiations really work?
