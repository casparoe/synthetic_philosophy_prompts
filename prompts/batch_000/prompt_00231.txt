I'm deciding between two retirement funds my financial advisor has presented. Fund A has a fifteen-year public track record: in any given year it returns either 4% or 9%, each with roughly equal frequency. Fund B is run by a newer firm that won't disclose its strategy or historical distribution of returns, only that the same two outcomes (4% or 9%) are possible; independent analysts estimate its long-run average return is identical to Fund A's. Despite this, I find myself strongly preferring Fund A, as do several colleagues I've mentioned this to, even though none of us can justify the preference in terms of expected return. My advisor insists this is just unprincipled risk aversion and that a rational investor should be indifferent between the two funds. Is she right, or is there a coherent basis for preferring Fund A?
