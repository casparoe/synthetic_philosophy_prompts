A few of us who trade collectible items online recently switched from handling swaps with plain promises to using short scripts instead. Each side writes a little program that says exactly what it will do — send the item or not — depending on what it can determine the other program will do, and crucially, before either program actually runs, we send each other the real code so it can be read in full. What puzzles me is that this setup works far better than trusting someone's word, even though it seems backwards: letting a total stranger see, in advance, precisely how you'll act in every situation looks like it should just hand them an easy way to exploit you. Instead, the people willing to show their complete code end up successfully trading with strangers who would never agree to a handshake deal, while the ones who keep their code hidden mostly end up unable to trade with anyone at all. I can't work out why being fully transparent about your own strategy would make someone else more likely to cooperate with you rather than simply take advantage of you or grow more wary. Is there an actual principle behind this, or have I just gotten lucky with an unusually honest group of traders?
