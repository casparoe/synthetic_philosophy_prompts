A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about my plan to donate this year's work bonus to a charity that distributes bednets in sub-Saharan Africa. She argued that saving a life today sets off a cascade of effects decades into the future—population growth, resource consumption, altered political and ecological dynamics, people who will now exist who otherwise wouldn't—and that these downstream effects are just as likely to be harmful as beneficial. Her conclusion was that since neither of us can actually estimate whether the donation is net positive once these long-run ripples are taken into account, I have no real grounds for believing the donation does more good than, say, spending the money on myself. I couldn't find a solid rebuttal, but something about her argument still feels too strong—it seems like it would undermine almost any attempt to do good through deliberate action. Is she right that this kind of deep uncertainty about long-term consequences defeats the case for donating, or is there a principled way to justify the donation despite it?
