I'm teaching a seminar on philosophy of language this term, and something that happened at my own family's Thanksgiving planning has been bugging me ever since, it feels like it's getting at something we've been discussing in class but I can't pin down the principle. In the family group chat, my sister-in-law asked "does everyone still eat meat, want to make sure I plan the menu right?" My nephew, who's been quietly cutting back on meat but hasn't told anyone, replied "I've been really into vegetables lately, the farmers market near my place has amazing stuff this time of year." Everyone read this as him going vegetarian and my sister-in-law spent hours making a seperate tofu dish and adjusting the sides. Turns out he still eats meat, he just genuinely likes vegetables, and now there's low-key family drama about whether he mislead everyone even though, as my brother keeps pointing out, he never actually said he'd stopped eating meat or asked for a special dish. My sister-in-law feels like she was basically lied to and put in a bunch of extra work for nothing, while my nephew insists he's completely in the clear since every word out of his mouth was true. I keep sensing there's a real distinction here between what he literally said and what he communicated, and maybe some norm he's on the hook for even without saying anything false, but I'm having trouble articulating it in a way that would settle the family argument. Can you help me work out who's actually responsible here and whether there's a principled way to say the nephew did something wrong, short of accusing him of lying?
