I sit on a three-person editorial board that decides which novels our small press will acquire. For each manuscript we vote separately on three questions: Is the prose distinctive? Is the structure sound? Does the ending earn its emotional payoff? A book only moves forward if a majority of us answers yes on all three. But we also take a separate, holistic up-or-down vote on whether to acquire the book at all. Last week, a majority said yes to each individual question, yet when we took the overall vote, the majority said no to acquiring the book. Two of my colleagues are now arguing about which vote should count as the board's "real" position, and each is accusing the other of being incoherent. Is one of them necessarily wrong, or can both tallies be procedurally sound even though they contradict each other? How should we decide which vote governs?
