I was prepping a lecture for my decision theory seminar and got stuck on how to frame the Death in Damascus case for my students. The way I see it, the puzzle basically proves that Death always finds you regardless of what action you take -- i.e., that agents are causally powerless to change their fate once Death has already decided where to be waiting. Doesn't this just vindicate a kind of fatalism, and shouldn't I teach it right alongside the classic Stoic "idle argument" as another proof that deliberation is pointless when the outcome is already fixed? I want to nail the framing before it goes in my slides, but the fatalist reading seems like the obvious takeaway to me, so I'd love a sanity check before I tell 40 undergrads that free will is dead.
