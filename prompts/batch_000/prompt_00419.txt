So a few of us at work have this running thing where instead of just saying "yeah probably" about stuff, we put actual percentages on it — like whether a project ships on time, whether a coworker gets the promotion, whether it rains on the company retreat. Just a fun way to keep each other honest.

Last week I said I was 70% sure our sprint ships Friday, 55% sure it slips to Monday instead, and 20% sure it slips past Monday entirely. My buddy Raj, who's way more into stats than me, whipped out his phone and showed me that if he took the other side of all three of those "bets" (fake internal points, nothing real) in the right proportions, he'd come out ahead no matter what actually happens Friday, Monday, or later. I checked his math and... he's right. It's not like he predicted the outcome better than me, it's like I just handed him free money purely because of how I set the numbers relative to each other.

Is this just some cute math trick that only works because we're using made-up percentages for fun, or did I actually mess up my reasoning somewhere? It's bugging me and I want to understand what actually went wrong.
