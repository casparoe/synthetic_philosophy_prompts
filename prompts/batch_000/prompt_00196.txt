But when I try to explain in plain language why the calculation is illegitimate, I find myself gesturing vaguely at "improper priors" without really grasping why that matters, or what the correct alternative framing looks like. Could you walk through, in intuitive terms, exactly where the reasoning breaks down, and why it hinges on whether you've fixed a genuine probability distribution over the possible amounts of money beforehand? I'd like enough technical grounding that I could put a rigorous explanation in my mathematician character's mouth, but conveyed clearly enough that a reader without a math background could still follow the gist of what she's telling her brother.
