I'm drafting a novel in which two academic characters—a philosopher of causation and her former student—clash over how to explain, to a skeptical detective, why "if you hadn't been at the scene, the victim wouldn't have died" doesn't settle the question of causal responsibility. I want their argument to hinge on a real distinction, so I've been reading about interventionism as an alternative to the classic Lewisian counterfactual analysis. My understanding is that interventionists sidestep the whole apparatus of possible worlds and similarity metrics that trips up counterfactual theories, since they ground causal claims in what would actually happen under a real, physical manipulation rather than in a counterfactual supposition about some other possible world. Is that the right way to frame the contrast for my scene, or am I collapsing a distinction that doesn't quite work that way?
