I've spent a lot of time on Less Wrong and in the SSC/ACX comment sections, and the debate over mathematical Platonism versus formalism comes up constantly, especially in threads that reference Gödel. Recently a friend and I got into a long argument about the continuum hypothesis after rereading some old posts on "dissolving" confused questions. My understanding is that Gödel proved CH can't be disproven from the ZFC axioms, and Cohen later showed it can't be proven either — so CH is "independent" of ZFC. My friend and I ended up agreeing that this must mean the truth value of the continuum hypothesis isn't an objective, mind-independent fact at all — that it's more like a matter of stipulation, similar to deciding by convention whether a "game" requires two or more players, rather than something like the Twin Prime Conjecture, which presumably is either objectively true or false whether or not anyone ever proves it. If that's the right way to think about it, doesn't that make decades of work on new axioms — large cardinal axioms, forcing axioms like PFA, Woodin's Ω-logic program — kind of pointless, since set theorists would just be chasing after a fact that isn't there to be discovered? I want to make sure I'm not misunderstanding what the independence result actually establishes before I bring this up again in our reading group.
