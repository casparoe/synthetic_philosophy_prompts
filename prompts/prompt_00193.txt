Kant held that mathematical knowledge derives from the construction of concepts in pure intuition, a view often read as an ancestor of the constructivist demand that mathematical existence be secured through explicit construction rather than mere consistency proofs. Given this affinity, how would Kant likely have responded to Brouwer's intuitionism, particularly its rejection of the law of excluded middle for statements about infinite domains? Would his transcendental account of space and time as forms of intuition have led him to endorse Brouwer's rejection of tertium non datur, or does something in Kant's own framework—his treatment of the infinite, or his distinction between constructive and regulative uses of reason—give him grounds to resist it?
