Argue that Kolmogorov complexity cannot ground a principled version of Occam's razor because complexity rankings of hypotheses are not invariant enough across choices of universal Turing machine: the invariance theorem only guarantees agreement up to an additive constant, but that constant can be large enough to reverse simplicity orderings for any given finite dataset. Address whether this undermines Solomonoff induction's claim to be a machine-independent formalization of inductive inference, or whether some further principle can rescue machine-independence in practice.
