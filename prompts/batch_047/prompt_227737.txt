After a long and unresolved argument with a friend about split-brain patients, I want an audit of where I've ended up rather than another verdict on the cases. My commitments: (1) following complete corpus callosotomy, there are two distinct subjects of experience associated with one body, not merely one subject with fragmented access to information; (2) which and how many subjects exist cannot depend on what an experimenter happens to ask, so whatever the right count is, it held before anyone ran the classic lateralized experiments; (3) a surgical cut of this kind cannot create a new person — at most it can reorganize the psychological life of the one who went in; (4) each subject's introspective sense that its own experience is unified is to be taken at face value, since there is no higher vantage from which to correct it. Are these jointly tenable? Please identify the genuine tensions, in order of severity, stating precisely which commitment has to go in each case and what abandoning it costs; and distinguish these from any pairings that look inconsistent but are not, since my friend flagged nearly every combination and I suspect some of his objections dissolve on inspection.
