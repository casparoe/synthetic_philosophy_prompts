So I'm in my school's philosophy club (we meet thursdays, it's pretty informal) and last week we did infinity stuff -- Zeno's paradoxes and then this thing called Thomson's lamp, where a lamp gets switched on after half a minute, off after another quarter minute, on after an eighth, and so on, so infinitely many switches happen in one minute total, and the question is whether the lamp is on or off at the end. I got kinda obsessed with it because the whole setup is so physically ridiculous. Like, what is the switch even made of. Also apparently some philosophers respond by saying the description just doesn't tell you the final state but nothing contradictory happened, which feels like cheating to me but I can sort of see it.

Anyway our club is doing a "funny pieces" night and I want to write a parody of a one-star online product review (like an Amazon review) for the Thomson Lamp, written by an extremely angry customer who bought it as a normal desk lamp. The joke should be that all his complaints are just... the actual philosophy, but filtered through consumer rage. So he's furious that the manual refuses to say whether the lamp is on or off at the end of the demonstration mode ("final state not covered under warranty"), the switch mechanism wears out after finitely many flicks even though the demo requires infinitely many, customer service keeps telling him the instruction booklet is "complete and consistent" and his question about the end state is "ill-posed," his roommate tried to add a wire that turns it on at the one-minute mark and now the lamp "works fine" which somehow makes everything worse. Maybe also a bit about the electricity bill, or the light visibly vibrating, whatever fits.

About 600-700 words, keep it deadpan the whole time -- the reviewer never acknowledges he's stumbled into a metaphysics debate, he just wants a refund. It should be funny to people who've never heard of supertasks (my friend group chat) but with enough specific details that my club advisor can tell I actually understood the paradox and the standard replies. Don't break the fourth wall or explain the joke in a note at the end, trust the reader.
