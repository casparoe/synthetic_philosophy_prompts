Draft the joint statement they should have produced if they had run an adversarial collaboration instead. The statement needs four parts.

This section must not presuppose either party's ontology.

Second, the disagreements, stated in wording each would accept as a fair description of their own position. Distinguish at least two separate questions they have been conflating: whether models related by a hole diffeomorphism represent two genuinely distinct physical possibilities or one possibility described twice, and whether the argument is fundamentally an indictment of substantivalism, a lesson about determinism, or a general point about how symmetries should be interpreted in physical theory.

Third, the cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, each paired with a concrete consideration both agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Test these three candidates: whether differences that are purely haecceitistic (models agreeing on every qualitative fact but differing over which spacetime point is which) count as real differences in possibility; whether a substantivalist who accepts that diffeomorphic models are one possibility rather than many has thereby ceased to be a substantivalist in any interesting sense; and whether whatever one says about general covariance must also be said about gauge symmetry in electromagnetism, or whether there is a principled disanalogy (for instance, that only the spacetime symmetry concerns the arena in which physics happens) that both sides can state without slanting it.

Fourth, a verdict on which parts of their year-long dispute were verbal: identify any disagreement that dissolves once the terms are fixed, and explain why.
