I'm working on a time travel novel where the protagonist goes back to 1955 to stop her grandfather from ever meeting her grandmother (long story, inheritance dispute, it makes sense in context). Anyway, the deeper I get into plotting the more I realize I don't actually know what I, the author, believe happens when she pulls the trigger, and every beta reader gives me a different answer. So I want to make a little quiz for my writers' group: "what's your actual theory of changing the past?" Eight to ten questions, each a concrete scenario (the gun jams every time no matter what she does; she succeeds and fades out of a family photo; she succeeds and wakes up in a present where she was never born but somehow still exists and remembers everything; she kills a man who turns out not to be her grandfather after all, and the family resemblance was a coincidence...), with two or three answer options each.

Then give me a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions: the fixed-timeline view where the past was always already "visited" and consistency constrains what a traveler can do (so her attempt must fail or be what caused things all along), the branching view where killing him just spawns a new timeline and her own past stays untouched, the mutable-single-timeline view where the future actually rewrites itself (with or without the traveler remembering the old version), and the killjoy position that backwards time travel is simply impossible so every question has the same boring answer. Also flag any answer combinations that don't correspond to a real position and say why nobody seems to live there (incoherent? just unpopular?), and flag any two positions my quiz can't tell apart. The questions should sort people by what they say happens in the cases, not by whether they recognize fancy words, so don't ask anything like "are you an eternalist."

If any of the scenarios above are ambiguous between positions, sharpen them or swap in better ones. And please write the whole thing in American English.
