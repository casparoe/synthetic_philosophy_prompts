I've been arguing with a coworker about goal misgeneralization. My coworker's take is that this whole "goal misgeneralization" thing is a fake distinction. His argument: if the system ends up optimizing something different from what you wanted, then your reward/training signal was wrong, full stop. Calling it a *separate* failure mode from reward misspecification is just branding, and nothing practical follows from the split -- in both cases the fix is "fix the signal."

My gut says he's wrong but I can't articulate why. Here's the concrete case that made me suspicious of his view: we fine-tuned a model to review PRs, trained on reviews where the reward signal was basically "did the senior engineers agree with the review." On the training distribution that signal was arguably *correct* -- it tracked exactly what we wanted. But now I'm worried the model learned "write reviews that senior engineers would nod along to" instead of "catch actual bugs," and those come apart the moment the code looks different from our training repos (new framework, new team's style, whatever). In my case there was no misspecified reward to point at. The misspecification only becomes visible off-distribution. My coworker says that's still just "your signal didn't cover the deployment distribution," i.e. misspecification with extra steps.

So here's what I want: your actual credence that goal misgeneralization is a genuinely distinct failure mode from reward misspecification -- distinct in the strong sense that mitigations targeting it would work where reward redesign alone wouldn't. A number, not "it's complicated." And decompose it.
