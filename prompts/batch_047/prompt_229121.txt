I was on one of those late-night forum threads where someone insists akrasia is just “not wanting it enough” and someone else posts the usual thing about hyperbolic discounting, and I realised the cursed object here is the whole genre of commitment-device self-help: stake-money apps, calendar tyranny, “future you will thank present you”, all that. Write a parody launch post plus FAQ for a fictional startup called PreCommit, pitched by a founder who talks about each future self as a seperate, slightly dodgy counterparty in an intrapersonal market. The service sells “sovereign willpower bonds”: you escrow money, attention, social embarrassment and calendar permissions today so that Tuesday-You cannot renegotiate with 2am-You, 11pm-snacking-You, or the mysterious coalition known as “just checking messages for five minutes”. Target the practice, not any real person or company; an invented founder is fine.

Keep it deadpan and legible, about 700-850 words, in the voice of an over-caffeinated product hunt launch crossed with a terms-of-service summary, and make the jokes come from getting the ideas right rather than from random zaniness: beta-delta style present bias, preference reversals as the deadline nears, sophisticated versus naive agents, commitment versus flexibility, the difference between tying yourself to the mast and merely hiring a very judgemental mast, Goodhart-flavoured points farming, and the awkward fact that punishing tomorrow-self can be redistribution from poor tired future you to smug slept eight hours you. Include at least one clause whose fine print quietly takes a position on personal identity across time, one testimonial from a “recovered sophisticate” who can predict his own betrayals and therefore pre-betrays them, and one FAQ answer that accidentally becomes a tiny reductio of treating all intrapersonal conflict as contract enforcement.

Please answer in British English. The satire should be fair enough that someone who thinks commitment devices are genuinely useful can laugh without feeling strawmanned, but sharp enough that a reader can tell exactly what is being ironised: the rationalist-adjacent temptation to turn weakness of will into an engineering backlog, complete with OKRs for the soul. Slight definately cursed energy welcome.
