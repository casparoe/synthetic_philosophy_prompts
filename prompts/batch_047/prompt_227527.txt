I am a novelist in the early planning stages of a literary novel, and I should like some philosophical counsel before I commit myself to a structure.

The book is to be organised around a single uneasy question: how much of what we blame people for turns on factors beyond their control. In the course of my reading I have encountered the customary fourfold taxonomy of moral luck — resultant luck (luck in how one's actions turn out), circumstantial luck (luck in the situations in which one finds oneself), constitutive luck (luck in the sort of person one happens to be), and causal luck (luck in being the product of antecedents one did not choose). It strikes me that these four varieties might behave very differently as material for fiction, and I want to get the philosophy right before I build anything on it.

Please rank the four categories from most to least promising as the load-bearing structure of a serious novel. By 'promising' I mean a compound of three things: the capacity to sustain narrative tension without contrivance; the tendency to resist tidy moral resolution on the reader's part; and the likelihood of surviving the scrutiny of a philosophically literate reader without collapsing into melodrama or incoherence. For each category, give me its placement, a few sentences of justification, the kind of plot it most naturally generates, and the characteristic failure mode a novelist should fear when working with it — the way, for instance, a story built upon resultant luck might stand accused of rigging its own dice.

Two further requests. First, my own instinct is that circumstantial luck is the richest vein, but I suspect this may be only because it is the easiest to dramatise; if my instinct is mistaken, or correct for the wrong reasons, please say so plainly. Second, having produced the ranking with a tragedy in mind, tell me briefly whether it would be rearranged for a comic novel, and if so, how and why.

Please do not supply reading lists, references, or citations; I am after your judgement rather than a bibliography. And I should be grateful if you would reply in British English.
