So I run our uni's philosophy society and next week I'm doing a session on the Lucas–Penrose argument, i.e. the claim that Gödel's incompleteness theorems somehow show the human mind can't be a machine. Crowd will be a mix: some mathmos who know the theorems but not the philosophy, some philosophy students who've heard of Gödel but only as a vibe, and at least one guy who has definately read a pop summary of The Emperor's New Mind and wants to talk quantum consciousness.

Don't give me a rundown of the argument itself -- I know it. Plan how I should teach it in about an hour. Specifically:

- The order the pieces have to go in. What do they need before the Lucas move even parses? (I'm guessing: unformal provability vs truth, what a Gödel sentence actually says, the consistency assumption -- but tell me what depends on what and what can be skipped for this audience.)
- The one example or device that carries the session. I was thinking of just writing a plain-language "this sentence can't be proved in system S" on the board and building everything around it, but if there's something better that doesn't require them to follow any actual Gödel numbering, say so and say what it needs to have and avoid.
- The misconceptions, in the order they'll actually show up, and the exact point in the sequence where I should head each one off. The ones I expect: "Gödel proved some truths are unprovable, full stop", "humans can just *see* the Gödel sentence is true and that's the whole argument", mixing up the first and second theorems, and "this proves consciousness is non-physical" arriving way too early. There are probably others I'm not expecting.
- Where the argument is actually weakest -- I want to teach it steelmanned, so tell me the point in the lesson where I should let them attack it themselves and what the best objection for them to find is (e.g. the bit about how we only 'see' the Gödel sentence's truth if we assume the system is consistent).
- One question I can pose at the end that tells me whether they got the actual logical structure, as opposed to just having enjoyed the vibes.
- What I should openly refuse to go into at this level (Gödel numbering details? Penrose's quantum stuff?) and the one-line way to park each without seeming like I'm dodging.
