I'm on the five-member policy committee of a mid-sized professional association (about 4,000 members -- think a regional society of actuaries), and we've been asked to recommend whether the association should run its next big decision as a "futarchy"-style trial. The concrete case: we have to choose between keeping our annual conference always in Chicago or rotating it among three cities, and one very enthusiastic member has proposed that instead of the board voting, we set up a conditional prediction market. The idea is that members trade contracts that pay out based on what total membership revenue and member-satisfaction survey scores actually turn out to be over the following three years, conditional on each location policy being adopted -- and the board commits in advance to picking whichever policy the market prices as better on that metric. He keeps saying this "separates values from beliefs" and that the board's job should only be to define the success metric, not to predict anything.

I'm skeptical but not dismissive, and honestly the rest of the committee is split two-two, so my memo will probably be the swing document. A few things are bugging me. First, the metric: bundling revenue and satisfaction scores into one number already smuggles in a value judgment about tradeoffs (how much is a satisfaction point worth in dollars?), so it's not clear values and beliefs actually got separated -- they just got moved. Second, maybe a hundred members would realistically trade, and they're mostly the same people who attend board meetings; I'm worried about both thin markets and about one wealthy member with a strong preference buying the outcome she wants -- though my economist friend says manipulation attempts can actually make such markets *more* accurate by subsidizing information, which I find counterintuitive and haven't fully digested. Third, if the market says rotate and the board overrides it, the whole trial looks pointless; if we promise not to override, haven't we basically abdicated?

Write the memo I'll circulate to the committee: a clear recommendation (adopt, reject, or adopt-in-modified-form -- I'd honestly consider a pilot where the market is advisory only for one cycle), with reasoning the other four members can argue against point by point. Please engage seriously with the strongest case *for* the proposal, including the idea that markets aggregate dispersed information better than deliberation, rather than just dunking on it, and address the three worries above specifically. Also say what would have to be true about the metric-design process for the "we only choose values" framing to be honest. Around 800-1000 words is fine.
