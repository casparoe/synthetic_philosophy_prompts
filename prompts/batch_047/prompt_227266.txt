Here is a philosophical concept I keep circling back to, and I'd like your honest probability on it rather than a literature summary. The idea is sometimes discussed under the label evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I'll state it in my own words to be clear about what I'm asking: if the universe (or multiverse) is sufficiently large, then there exist very many agents whose deliberation is correlated with mine -- agents running relevantly similar reasoning, facing relevantly similar decisions -- even though I can never causally interact with most of them. On some decision theories, when I choose cooperatively, this is evidence that those correlated agents are choosing cooperatively too, and so an apparently small one-person decision can, in effect, carry the weight of a vast number of mirrored decisions. The strong version of the claim says that this effect should substantially change how a rational agent weighs actions with seemingly negligible causal footprints (one vote, one purchase, one donation), because the agent's choice is entangled with a huge population of similar choosers.

My credence that the strong version of this claim is correct -- that such evidential considerations genuinely and significantly shift the rational weighting of everyday small-footprint decisions -- is about 0.15. Assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each term, show how they combine into your final number, and tell me which term your credence is most sensitive to.
