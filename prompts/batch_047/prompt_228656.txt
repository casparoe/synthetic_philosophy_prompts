Okay, dumb question from someone who's read a decade of LessWrong but zero actual physics. The grandfather paradox has always struck me as basically a reductio: backwards time travel lets you generate a contradiction (kill grandpa -> you're never born -> nobody kills grandpa -> grandpa lives), and contradictions can't happen, so time travel to the past is logically impossible, full stop. Not engineering-impossible like warp drives, but impossible like married bachelors. Even HPMOR kind of concedes this, right? The Time-Turner rules are written so you can never change anything, which reads to me like Rowling-but-rational accepting that the paradox wins and routing around it.

But then I see actual physicists (not cranks -- people on Physics StackExchange citing real papers) happily working on closed timelike curves, wormhole time machines, the Novikov self-consistency principle, chronology protection, etc. If the logic is airtight, what are these people even doing? Are physicists just bad at philosophy? Or am I wrong about what the paradox actually proves?

Answer in American English, please.
