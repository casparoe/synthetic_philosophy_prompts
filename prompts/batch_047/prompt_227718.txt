A colleague of mine defends a picture of the mind built on Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment, and I want the picture stress-tested. The thought experiment, as she presents it, runs as follows. Imagine a system whose substrate is an enormous number of tiny interacting marbles -- simmballs -- colliding and jostling according to purely local mechanical rules, with nothing at that level but pushes and trajectories. At a much coarser grain of description, certain large-scale configurations of simmballs amount to symbols, and these symbols figure in stable, law-like patterns at their own level: one symbol-configuration reliably gives rise to another, and the symbol-level narrative is compact, intelligible, and predictively excellent, whereas the full simmball-level narrative of the same process would be unmanageably vast and would obscure the very regularities that matter. On this basis she draws a conclusion about brains: thoughts genuinely push neurons around. The symbol level, she says, is where the causal action is; the neural level merely implements what the symbols settle. She takes the careenium to show that this kind of downward causation is entirely unmysterious, because in the careenium itself the symbol-level story is plainly the right causal story to tell, even though the simmballs are doing all the actual colliding.

Please audit this analogy as an argument. First, give the correspondence table explicitly: what in the brain is supposed to play the role of the simmballs, of their collision rules, of the symbols, of the symbol-level regularities, and of the observer who finds the coarse-grained description indispensable. Second, identify where the mapping breaks or strains. Some candidate pressure points you should adjudicate rather than merely list: in the careenium, any difference a symbol makes is exhausted by the collisions of its constituent simmballs, so the macro level appears to have no causal levers of its own -- is there anything in the neural case that would break this parallel, and if not, does the analogy support downward causation at all, or only the indispensability of symbol-level talk? Does the autonomy the careenium grants the symbol level -- which looks epistemic, a matter of which description is tractable and illuminating for us -- license the causal conclusion my colleague draws from it? And does the analogy quietly presuppose things brains may not deliver, such as a clean, well-defined mapping from symbol-tokens to substrate-states, or a single privileged level of description sitting neatly above the physics? Finally: once the disanalogies are on the table, state plainly what my colleague's conclusion is entitled to -- whether thoughts-pushing-neurons survives in any defensible form, whether the careenium at best establishes something weaker (and if so, exactly what), and whether a different analogy would serve her better.
