I'm in a philosophy of psychiatry seminar and we've just finished Hacking on looping effects and "making up people" -- the idea that classifying people changes the people classified, who then change in ways that feed back into the classification itself. For my term paper I want to run the multiple personality disorder epidemic of the 80s and early 90s through this framework. Quick recap of the facts as I understand them: MPD went from a vanishingly rare diagnosis to tens of thousands of cases after it got into the DSM-III in 1980 (Sybil had come out in 73), specialist clinics and conferences sprung up, patients reported more and more alters and recovered memories of ritual abuse, and then the whole thing more or less collapsed under skeptical backlash, lawsuits against therapists, and the false memory wars, with DSM-IV renaming it dissociative identity disorder.

Analyze the episode as a test case for the looping effect model. Map each stage onto the framework: what plays the role of the classification, what's the feedback loop, where do "the people classified" actively participate vs. get pushed around by clinicians. Then tell me where the model strains -- the skeptical story here is usually iatrogenesis (therapists implanting the thing wholesale), and I'm not sure whether that's a version of looping or a rival to it. Finally, the reverse direction: does the epidemic confirm Hacking, or does the way it ended (the diagnosis receding, patients recanting) show something his framework doesn't predict or can't easily accomodate? Keep it grounded in the case, not a general summary of the book.
