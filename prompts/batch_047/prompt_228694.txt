I'm on the grants committee of a small foundation that funds conceptual work in biology -- philosophy and theory, not fieldwork or sequencing. We've got money for about six researchers over three years, and this cycle's theme is the species problem and the conceptual mess around taxonomy more generally. I've been asked to draft the call for proposals, so I need to figure out what the actually important open questions are before I accidentally fund three more papers proposing yet another species concept.

So: which open questions in this area should the call prioritize, and in what order? Some candidates I keep hearing about: whether pluralism about species concepts is a genuine resolution or just a shrug that leaves each subfield doing its own thing; whether "species" picks out a natural kind or is just a bookkeeping unit; whether the ~95-97% average nucleotide identity cutoffs used to define microbial "species" in metagenomics are tracking anything real or are pure convention; how much hybridization and horizontal gene transfer have to happen before the tree-of-life picture stops being even approximately right; whether lumping vs. splitting decisions under phylogenetic species concepts have real stakes for conservation triage (since listed "species" get protection and management units often don't); and whether paleontology's morphospecies problem is the same problem or a different one wearing a hat.

For each question you prioritize, tell me: is it upstream of other questions (does resolving it reorganize the rest), is it tractable for philosophers rather than requiring wet-lab work, and who outside the seminar room should care. Also tell me which of my candidate questions you'd strike off the list as overrated or already as-resolved-as-they're-going-to-get -- I suspect at least one of them is mostly a verbal dispute and I'd rather hear that now.

One constraint: I don't want the deliverable in any case to be "another unified species concept," so if you think the most fundable questions are ones where that's not the goal, say which ones.

Finally, flag where your ordering is most fragile: what argument or piece of evidence -- say, from microbiology or from how conservation law actually handles taxonomic revisions -- would make you reorder the list, and where is your reasoning most likely to be wrong?
