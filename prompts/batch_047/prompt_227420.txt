I've been losing sleep over something at work and could really use an outside perspective.

I work in the accounts department of a mid-sized manufacturing firm. Nothing glamorous, I mostly process invoices and chase payments. A few months back I realised that our single biggest client, nearly 40% of our revenue, is a company whose whole business model I'd honestly call predatory. I don't want to give too much away, but think along the lines of payday lending: legal, but it makes its money off desperate people in a way I find hard to defend.

So I started thinking about quitting. When I mentioned it to my brother he said it was the daftest thing he'd ever heard, and honestly his arguement has been rattling round my head ever since. It goes like this:

1. The client isn't going anywhere. Whether I stay or go, they keep paying us and we keep supplying them.
2. My job would be readvertised and filled within a fortnight. Whoever replaces me processes the exact same invoices.
3. So nothing in the world actually changes if I leave, except that I'm unemployed, my family is worse off, and some stranger is doing the thing I felt too squeamish to do.

And here's the bit I can't get past: I think he's right on all three points. Me leaving genuinely would change nothing. The harm carries on at exactly the same level either way. By his logic, staying isn't just excusable, it's arguably the better outcome all round, since at least the paycheque goes to someone who loses a bit of sleep over it.

And yet. When I sign off those invoices on a Friday afternoon it feels like I'm doing the thing, not just watching it happen. If a mate told me he did the books for a loan shark I'd think a bit less of him, so why do I get a pass just because the loan shark is a plc?

So my questions I suppose are these. Is there actually a flaw in my brother's reasoning somewhere, or is the uncomfortable truth that it simply doesn't matter what I do? If my contribution is fully replaceable and the total harm is identical either way, is "but then I'd be part of it" a real reason or just a squeamish feeling I ought to get over? And does it matter that I'm several steps removed from the sharp end, seeing as I'm not the one setting the interest rates, just the one doing the paperwork?

I've tried the line of thinking where if everyone refused jobs like mine these companies would be starved of staff, but honestly they pay well enough that there'd always be takers, so that doesn't seem to get me very far either.

Any clarity much appreciated. I keep going round in circles on this one.
