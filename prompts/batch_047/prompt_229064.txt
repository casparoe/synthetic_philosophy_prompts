Just had a three-hour argument with my mate down the pub about whether comparing historical atrocities is legitimate or plain obscene, and neither of us budged. He reckons any ranking of evils -- the Holocaust versus the Holodomor versus the transatlantic slave trade -- is inherently degrading because it turns suffering into a leaderboard. I reckoned we rank atrocities constantly anyway (school curriculums, memorial funding, which genocides get international tribunals) so we might as well do it openly and defend the criteria.

Write me a dialogue between two equally sharp characters who take these two sides, but push them past the obvious talking points. Some things I'd like them to actually grapple with: whether refusing to compare is itself a covert comparison (since silence allocates attention too); whether the obscenity charge proves too much, given we happily distinguish murder from genocide in law; whether comparison commits some kind of category error by treating different forms of suffering as measurable in one currency; and whether the real problem is comparison per se or who's doing the comparing and for what audience. Neither character should be a strawman and they should respond to each other's actual arguments, not just restate their opening postions.

Format: exactly five exchanges (one exchange = one character speaks, then the other replies), each exchange a couple of sentences per speaker, no introduction or conclusion around the dialogue. And write it in British English please.
