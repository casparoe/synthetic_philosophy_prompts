I'm a third-year philosophy undergraduate taking a course on situated cognition (the "4E" literature: embodied, embedded, enactive, extended). For the revision week, I've volunteered to make a handout for my seminar group: a decision tree that tells a student which position on the extended mind thesis they actually hold, based on what they think rather than which label they find appealing. I'd like you to write the tree.

Here is what I need:

The tree should start from a shared minimal commitment that everyone in the seminar accepts (roughly: some broadly functionalist story about what makes something a memory or a belief is on the table, and the Otto-and-his-notebook case is at least intelligible as a challenge). From there, each branch node should be a single diagnostic question on which the standard positions genuinely come apart, phrased so that someone can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. Candidates for splitting questions I have in mind include: whether an external resource must replicate the functional profile of an internal one to count as cognitive (or whether playing a complementary, transformative role is enough); whether there is a "mark of the cognitive" (e.g. non-derived content) that external vehicles necessarily lack; whether inferring constitution from causal coupling is always a fallacy or sometimes legitimate; whether conditions like constant availability, automatic endorsement, and fluent access do real work or are ad hoc; and whether the skull-and-skin boundary has explanatory significance or is just where our intuitions happen to stop. Please use whichever of these earn their place, add others if the tree needs them, and order them so that the most fundamental disagreements come first.

The leaves should be named positions, and I want at least: first-wave extended mind built on the parity principle; second-wave "complementarity" externalism; the embedded-but-internalist view on which external resources are merely causal scaffolds for a brain-bound cognitive process; an enactivist reading on which cognition is constituted by the organism–environment dynamic itself; and a robust internalism that denies even the embedded concession. Each leaf should carry one sentence stating the position's most awkward commitment (for example, the first-wave view's exposure to cognitive bloat — my phone's contact list becoming part of my memory).

Two further requests. First, build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of names: lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space, and for any unoccupied combination, add a one-line diagnosis of whether the gap exists because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. Second, flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate — I suspect embedded internalism and the enactivist view may collapse into each other at the level of what my classmates will actually answer, and if so I'd rather the handout said so than pretend otherwise.

Seven to nine branch questions and five or six leaves feels right for a two-page handout. Please present it as a numbered flowchart (if YES go to node n, if NO go to node m), with the diagnostic questions worded as plainly as possible — my seminar group includes people who haven't done the reading, and the tree will be useless if answering a question already requires knowing the jargon it sorts you into.
