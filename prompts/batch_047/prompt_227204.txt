So I work as a software engineer at a mid-size infra company, and somehow I got voluntold onto our "engineering council," which is basically the body that gets asked to settle stuff when teams cant agree. Heres the situation. We have two staff engineers, Maya and Dmitri, who are both genuinely excellent -- like, if I had to bet on whos right about a technical call, Id honestly struggle to pick between them. For the last eight months they've been in a running disagreement about whether our main database migration will survive contact with peak traffic. They sit in the same meetings, they read each others design docs, they share all their load test data, they've both explained their reasoning to each other a dozen times. And Maya still says roughly 80% chance the migration is fine as planned, Dmitri still says roughly 30%. Neither of them is being stubborn in an obvious way -- they've each adjusted small details, but the headline numbers never converge. Leadership has lost patience and told the council to design a "disagreement resolution protocol" for cases like this, and I got assigned to draft the memo recommending what the protocol should actually say.

Here's the wrinkle thats been bugging me. I was nerding out with a friend about this and he told me about Aumann's agreement theorem, which (as I understand it, correct me if Im butchering it) says that rational agents with common priors who share their honest beliefs back and forth can't just keep "agreeing to disagree" -- eventually their estimates should converge. Which suggests that Maya and Dmitri's situation shouldn't be possible if they're both reasoning well, and something has gone wrong. But then again, the theorem seems to assume things like shared priors, and maybe real engineers bring different tacit experience that counts as different priors, in which case persistent disagreement is fine and even healthy and we should just... pick someone and move on? I genuinely can't tell whether the right protocol is "keep forcing them to exchange evidence until the numbers converge," or "treat the disagreement itself as a signal and just go with the more conservative estimate," or "have a designated decider overrule both of them," or something else.

Please draft the memo I'd circulate to the council. It needs a concrete recommendation (an actual protocol with steps, not just "more discussion"), a justification for it, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument against whatever you recommend -- including whether Aumann's theorem is even applicable here or whether I'm overapplying a result whose assumptions the real world violates. Keep it under a page, since nobody on the council reads anything longer.
