In most developed welfare states, entitlement to the core social safety net -- unemployment insurance, contributory pensions, often even disability benefits -- is indexed to a person's history of paid employment, and continued receipt of unemployment benefits is typically conditional on documented, ongoing search for a paid job. Caring for one's own children or elderly parents full-time, volunteering for the local fire brigade, organizing a tenants' association, or writing a novel no one buys counts for none of this: it builds no contribution record and, if you receive benefits while doing it, may count as failing to seek work. At the same time, the public culture of these same societies routinely describes full-time parenting as the hardest and most valuable job there is, celebrates volunteers as the backbone of the community, and laments that essential care work is undervalued -- without that valuation showing up anywhere in the contribution ledger.

What conception of work does this institutional arrangement presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: what, on the view the system seems to embody, makes an activity work rather than leisure or private life -- that it is paid, that it produces something exchangeable, that it is performed under another's direction, that it is burdensome in a specific way? Which of the rival conceptions (work as market contribution, work as socially necessary labor whatever its form, work as a duty owed to one's fellow citizens, work as merely a proxy for need) does the practice actually require, and which is it merely consistent with? Then address the apparent inconsistency: can a society coherently hold that caregiving is supremely valuable labor while building a benefits architecture on which it is not labor at all -- or is the public praise just sentiment the institutions quietly disown? Finally, say what would concretely have to change in the design of benefits, contribution records, and conditionality rules if the system were rebuilt on a broader conception of contribution, and whether any known objections to such a redesign (free-riding, unverifiability, cost) are objections to the theory or only to its implementation.
