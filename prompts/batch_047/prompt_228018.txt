Is there a name for this? I keep running into the same pattern when my friends and I argue about blame, and I can't find the right words for it. Start with an ordinary case: someone's boss leans on them hard to sack an employee, they do it, and afterwards they defend themselves by saying "I had no choice, the boss made me." Fine. But now compare two versions. In the first, the boss's pressure was real but, as it happens, completely unnecessary — the person already wanted the employee gone and would have done the sacking on their own initiative even if the boss had stayed silent. In the second, nobody applies any pressure at all: the person makes up their own mind to do the sacking, goes through the normal deliberation, and meanwhile the boss is quietly monitoring them, fully prepared to force their hand the moment they waver — except they never waver, so the boss never lifts a finger. In both cases, in some sense, the person "couldn't have done otherwise". But the way they end up unable to do otherwise is totally different, and in the second case the inability seems to have played no role whatsoever in what actually happened.

My gut says the person is blameworthy in both cases, and that the "I had no choice" defence should fail in both — but it clearly fails for different reasons, and I'm not sure the second case even counts as being pressured at all. Is there an established concept for that second kind of situation, where someone is responsible despite having had no alternative, because the thing that would have removed the alternative never actually intervened? I'd like more than a bare name: tell me how it differs from ordinary duress or coercion, and from the related case where two fully sufficient causes both actually operate (two people simultaneously pushing the same boulder, say). And give me a committed verdict, not a menu — does moral responsibility survive the absence of alternatives in the monitoring-boss case, yes or no, and what follows for how we should treat "I had no choice" excuses generally? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning; please don't restate my examples back at me.
