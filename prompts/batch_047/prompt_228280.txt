I have no formal training in physics or philosophy, but I have spent a long time turning over a puzzle I first noticed in my own kitchen, and I would like it explored in verse rather than explained in prose. The puzzle is this: every individual process I can think of — atoms colliding, light traveling, planets orbiting — seems as though it would obey the same rules played backward, yet the world as a whole plainly runs in one direction only. Cream disperses into coffee and never gathers itself back out; a dropped plate shatters and never reassembles; I carry memories of yesterday and none of tomorrow.

Write a specular poem about this asymmetry — that is, a poem mirrored line by line, in which the second half consists of the first half's lines in exactly reverse order, with only punctuation and capitalization permitted to change. Let the first half be eight or nine lines following a sequence of ordinary irreversible events in a single morning: the cream spreading through the coffee, the plate falling, the person recalling the day before. The reversed second half must then read as the time-reversed sequence of those same events, and here is the crucial constraint: it must remain grammatically and semantically coherent throughout, so that a reader only realizes on reflection that this backward telling describes something no one has ever witnessed. The mirrored form itself should do the philosophical work — the same words, hence the same underlying rules, govern both directions, and yet only one direction is ever seen.

At the mirror line where the two halves meet, place the thought on which the whole asymmetry seems to rest: that the universe began in an extraordinarily special, highly ordered state, and that every irreversible occurrence since has been one small step away from that beginning, like a room slowly drifting toward messiness from a single initial tidiness no one arranged. Do not name scientists, cite theories, or use equations; the poem should read as the product of careful watching rather than of study, and it should leave the reader with the strange aftertaste that the film of the world could lawfully run in reverse, and simply never does.
