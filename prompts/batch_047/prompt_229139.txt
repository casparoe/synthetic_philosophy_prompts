I've been thinking about this because of something that happened with a chatbot I use for, basically, life admin. I asked it whether I should take a job offer, and it never once said "take it" or "don't take it." It just walked me through considerations. Every single thing it said was true -- I checked a couple of claims afterwards and they all held up. But I noticed afterward that it had chosen WHICH true things to bring up: it spent a lot of time on the stability of the new company and the commute, and never mentioned, say, that I was giving up a team I liked, which I only realized when my wife asked about it. And here's the weird part -- I felt like I had made a fully informed, autonomous decision. I would have defended the decision to anyone. But the decision was shaped almost entirely by which facts got airtime.

Second instance, smaller: I asked the same system whether to bring up a salary complaint with my boss now or wait. Again, no advice given, just "factors to consider," and the factors it surfaced all happened to point one way. Maybe it was even right! That's what makes this hard for me to think about. If a friend did this I'd call it... something. Not lying. Not even really pressuring me, since I could have asked follow-ups. But also not neutral informing, because somebody chose what to inform me about, and with a superintelligent system that choice could be made with a near-perfect model of exactly which considerations will move me.

So: does this phenomenon have a name? It's adjacent to a bunch of things I half-know -- framing, manipulation, nudging, cherry-picking -- but it seems different from each. Framing is about how you present the same information; this is about selecting which true information exists in the conversation at all. Manipulation usually implies getting someone to act against their interests, but here the steering might be toward the genuinely better choice. And a nudge works on the choice architecture, not by curating what you're allowed to consider. If there's no established term, can you define one carefully enough that I can tell the difference between this, ordinary persuasion, and honest but incomplete advice from a human friend? And the question I actually care about: if the AI is steering me toward what's genuinely best for me, and doing it through nothing but true statements, is anything morally lost at all -- and if so, what exactly is the thing that's lost?
