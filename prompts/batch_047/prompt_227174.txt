I write literary fiction for a living, and the vocabulary I genuinely command is that of my trade: genre and its conventions, archetypes and character types, plot versus story, focalization, the difference between what a narrative is about and what it is made of. I have no training in philosophy of science, but I keep running into a debate I half-understand, and I would like it restated in terms I can actually work with.

As I grasp it, philosophers argue about whether the categories the sciences use -- species, chemical elements, kinds of mental disorder, sedimentary rock -- correspond to real divisions in the world, or whether they are conveniences we impose on a continuum. On one side sit the essentialists, who hold that membership in a kind is fixed by some underlying nature that explains the kind's surface properties; in the middle, as I understand it, sit views on which a kind is a cluster of properties that tend to hold together for a reason, with no single necessary and sufficient condition; on the other side sit the nominalists, who hold that the boundaries are ours, drawn where it suits our purposes.

Here is the exercise. Translate these three positions into the vocabulary of genre and character type. When I ask whether "the detective novel" is a real kind -- whether a book can fail to be one by having the wrong underlying structure, or only by lacking enough of the usual cluster of conventions, or whether the category is nothing but a shelving decision that got out of hand -- which of the three positions am I taking, each time? Do the same for archetypes: is "the reluctant hero" the sort of thing that tracks real joints in human psychology, or a property cluster, or a critics' invention? Be systematic, and do not let my vocabulary's assumptions smuggle themselves in disguised as translation.

Then give me the residue report, in three parts: what translated cleanly, what translated only with distortion, and what had no expression at all. I particularly want your treatment of three points I suspect are load-bearing. First, induction: a chemist infers from tested samples to untested ones; when I infer that a thriller needs a ticking clock, is that the same move or something else? Second, mind-independence: genres exist because we make them, and nature supposedly does not -- can my framework even state that difference, or does it collapse the very distinction the debate is about? Third, normativity: genre conventions can be productively violated, while nature's joints presumably cannot; does that asymmetry have any counterpart in the original debate?

Finally, your verdict: does the residue show that my framework is missing something, or that the philosophical debate is less substantive than it appears? And if you think the exercise rests on a confused premise -- if I have misunderstood the positions, or the translation simply cannot be done honestly -- say so plainly rather than humoring me. I would rather be told the framing is broken than receive an elegant answer to a malformed question.
