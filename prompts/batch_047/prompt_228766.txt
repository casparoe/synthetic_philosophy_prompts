Two colleagues of mine, both archaeologists, have been arguing about a dig neither of them will drop, and I've been asked to weigh in. The site is a prehistoric settlement with no writing, occupied for roughly two centuries. Adults were buried in a cemetery outside the settlement, mostly without grave goods. Children under about two were buried beneath house floors, each with a few objects — beads, a small vessel — and with a consistent body orientation; the pattern holds across the entire occupation.

Anna argues that the infant burials support an inference to belief: the effort invested, the goods, and the stable orientation indicate that the community attributed some form of continued existence or personhood to dead infants. Mark replies that the deposit is equifinal — the same pattern could have been produced by belief, by families displaying status at a funeral, or by mere spatial convention — so the belief inference is underdetermined and should be withheld. Anna counters that underdetermination infects every inference, including Mark's own talk of status display and convention; refusing belief inferences specifically, rather than inference generally, is arbitrary. Mark says there is a genuine asymmetry: economic or technological readings can be anchored by ethnographic analogy and replication experiments, whereas a claim about what these people believed about the dead admits no independent check whatsoever. Anna then appeals to convergence: two centuries of consistent orientation, age-appropriate goods, and real cost would be a strange coincidence if no belief were behind them. Mark's last move is that consistency across centuries is precisely what institutions do — practices routinely outlive the beliefs that launched them — so durability is evidence of tradition, not of belief.

Can you go through the exchange point by point and say who is right at each step — not just who wins overall — and whether both are missing something, for instance about what could count as independent evidence for a past belief in a case like this, or whether 'belief' is even the right unit of analysis?
