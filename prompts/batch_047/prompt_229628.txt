David Hume never confronted modern population ethics, but his writings supply the raw materials for a position, and I want to know what that position would be. In his essay on the populousness of ancient nations he treats the size of a society's population as one of the strongest outward signs of its internal happiness, on the grounds that people multiply where life is tolerable and government mild. Elsewhere he grounds all value in human sentiment rather than in any property that states of affairs possess independently of us; his moral sentiments respond to the utility and agreeableness of traits toward persons who actually exist and can be objects of sympathy; and he is notably suspicious of chains of abstract reasoning that carry us far beyond the sentiments they are supposed to summarize.

Suppose Hume were presented with the repugnant conclusion in its modern form: for any population of people with very high welfare, there is a better population, vastly larger, in which every life is barely worth living. Lay out the most defensible Humean response. Work out, in particular: whether his populousness-as-sign thesis commits him to anything like aggregation of welfare across persons, or whether a sign relation is too loose to generate an impersonal ranking of populations; whether his sentimentalism implies that an agent-neutral betterness ordering of populations is even the right format for the question, given that merely possible people cannot be objects of sympathy; whether his distrust of abstraction licenses dismissing the barely-worth-living population as a stipulative artifact rather than engaging with it; and where, if anywhere, a Humean would locate the mistake in the reasoning that generates the conclusion — or whether the honest Humean verdict is to accept it.

Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: the first item stating the predicted verdict on the conclusion itself, and the remaining four giving the grounds drawn respectively from the populousness thesis, the sentimentalism, the epistemology of far-fetched cases, and one point where the Humean materials genuinely run out and any attribution would be a guess.
