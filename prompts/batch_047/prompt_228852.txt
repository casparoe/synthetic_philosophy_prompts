Is there a name for this? I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and over the past few years I've ended up writing about a couple of forgery scandals -- one in the art market, one in rare wine. What stuck with me isn't the fraud itself but what people said afterward. Collectors and critics who had loved a painting for a decade would, once it was exposed, suddenly report that the hands were always clumsy, the light was off, you could tell all along. The wine people were the same: sommeliers who'd raved about a bottle at dinners swore that, thinking back, it had never actually tasted right. So the pattern I'm after is this: information about where an object came from doesn't just change what people believe about it, it seems to reach backwards and change the experience they remember having had, and maybe even the experience they're having right now when they look at the thing again. Nobody touched the canvas; the wine is chemically identical; only the story changed.

I want to be careful about what I'm not asking. I know there's literature on why originals are valued more than perfect copies, and on whether you could in principle see a forgery as different just by looking -- that's all downstream of this, and it's about value and knowledge, not about the weird temporal part, the re-coloring of an experience that's already over. It's also not plain hindsight bias, because these people aren't just misremembering what they predicted, they're reporting (as far as I can tell sincerely) that the thing looks or tastes different now.

So: does this specific phenomenon have an established name in philosophy or psychology, and if so what are the nearest concepts and how does this differ from each? If nothing quite fits, coin a term, define it properly, and give me some boundary cases so I can see the edges -- does it apply when the new information is about the artist's crimes rather than the work's origin? Does it apply to music, where there's no unique physical object? Does it ever run in reverse, where learning something good about provenance improves a memory? I won't be satisfied with just a label; I need to know where the carving stops.
