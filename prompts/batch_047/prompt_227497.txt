I'm in 11th grade and I got into transformative experiences after an argument with my friend about whether someone can rationally choose to become religious, have a kid, transition, join the military, etc., if the choice might change what they value. I definately don't understand all the philosophy here, but I've started defending this position:

When a decision would transform your personality or standards of evaluation, you cannot justify it by imagining how happy your future self will be, because that future self is partly created by the choice. Instead, the decision is rational only if it passes what I call the Revisability Test: it preserves enough agency for your later selves to keep questioning the choice, it doesn't trap you in a life where criticism becomes impossible, and it leaves open a reasonably wide range of worthwhile futures. So choosing to explore a religion could be rational even if conversion is unpredictable, while joining a cult that trains you never to question it would fail. Same with becoming a parent, maybe: the unknown love isn't evidence before the choice, but the choice can still be rational if it expands your capacity to care rather than destroying your capacity to reflect.

What would count against this? Please don't just give obvious examples like some people are happy in cults. I want the strongest philosophical defeaters: cases where giving up future revisability might be rational, cases where preserving too many options prevents real transformation, problems with comparing future selves, or arguments that my test secretly assumes the current self gets to rule over every later self. Also say whether the view predicts anything different from just choosing the most reversible option or maximizing expected welfare, because maybe it collapses into one of those.

Rank the objections from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish objections my view would have to accept from ones I could reasonably answer. At the end, say which single argument would most likely change your verdict about whether the Revisability Test works, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
