Since retiring from a career in chemistry, I have been reading the metaphysics of dispositions, and before committing my current views to an essay I would like to know whether the package actually holds together. I hold the following: (1) dispositions are intrinsic to their bearers and are, strictly, identical with microphysical structures — fragility just is a certain pattern of molecular bonding, charge a feature of the particle itself; (2) the laws of nature are contingent, so that very same bonding pattern could, under different laws, have grounded a different dispositional profile; (3) a disposition is individuated by its characteristic stimulus and manifestation — fragility is the disposition to break when struck, and nothing with a wholly different manifestation-type could be fragility; (4) the standard fink and mask cases (the live wire connected to a device that would instantly drain it the moment it is touched, the fragile glass so well packed that it survives the blow) refute the simple conditional analysis, and the usual repairs, whether ceteris paribus clauses or talk of ideal conditions, are either vacuous or false; (5) a masked object retains its disposition throughout — the wrapped glass is fragile still, and the poison remains poisonous while the antidote does its work; (6) disposition ascriptions are objectively true or false, not interest-relative or a matter of how we choose to describe things. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity and say, for each, precisely what has to give; I have no advance preference about which thesis survives.
