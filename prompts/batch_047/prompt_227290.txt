I am a doctoral student in medieval studies, and I keep running into a pattern in my secondary reading that I cannot find a proper term for, though I suspect historiographers must have named it at some point. The pattern is this. A period seems to acquire its identity largely through a label coined by the period that comes after it, and the label is rarely neutral -- it is coined as a foil, often as an outright insult, so that the later period can define itself against it. So, to take the cases closest to my work: the humanists describe the centuries between themselves and antiquity as an interval of darkness precisely because they need such an interval for their own story of rebirth to make sense; much later, the very word for that rebirth gets elevated from a metaphor into the name of a period; and something similar seems to happen when the dramatic changes in seventeenth-century natural philosophy get packaged, retrospectively, as a single Revolution. In each case the christening smuggles in a value judgment, and then -- this is the part I find really interesting -- later scholars reject the value judgment but keep the label. Generations of medievalists have demonstrated that the so-called dark centuries were anything but dark, yet the field is still organized around a name that encodes the darkness claim, and the rehabilitators end up asking questions the label dictates (was there really a Renaissance? did the Scientific Revolution happen?) rather than ones they might have chosen themselves.

I am aware of some nearby concepts, but none of them seems to be exactly this. Whig history is about writing the past as a march toward the present, which is related but broader, and it does not capture the specific mechanism of a successor period baptizing its predecessor as a foil. Presentism is about judging the past by current standards, which is not it either. Invented tradition is about fabricated practices, not fabricated epochs. What I want to know is whether there is an established term for this narrower phenomenon: a period's identity being constituted by a retrospective name coined as a polemic, where the name then outlives the polemic and quietly constrains what later historians think needs explaining. If such a term exists, I would like to know what it is and how its users have handled the persistence of the label after the value judgment is abandoned. If it does not, I would ask you to say so plainly, propose a name with a precise definition, and mark out the boundary cases -- for instance, does the phenomenon require that the label originate with the successor period itself, or do labels imposed by much later scholarship count, and what should we say about labels like "Late Antiquity" that were coined specifically to escape an older foil-naming? I am less interested in the word than in the carving: I want to know exactly where this phenomenon ends and ordinary period-naming begins, so a bare label without that analysis would not help me.
