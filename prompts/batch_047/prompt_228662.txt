I have no academic training, but over the past year I have been reading popular books about the "Great Divergence" — the question of why industrialisation took off in north-western Europe, and Britain in particular, rather than in China or India, which looked at least as prosperous a few centuries earlier. I keep running into a meta-dispute that interests me more than any particular answer: some writers treat "why Britain and not China?" as a perfectly good explanatory question, while others argue it is badly posed — that it smuggles in the assumption that Europe was destined to industrialise and that China somehow "failed", or that it invites a futile hunt for a single decisive factor (coal, colonies, institutions, culture) when the outcome may have been contingent. I have decided to spend the next year thinking this through properly, but I cannot read everything. Which open questions in this debate should I prioritise, and in what order? Please defend the ordering: I want to know which questions are upstream of which (for instance, whether one must first settle when Europe actually pulled ahead before asking why, or whether the contrastive question itself needs reformulating before any causal inquiry can begin), which alleged causes are overrated enough to be set aside early, and what findings would move a question up or down the list. My aim is a defended agenda for my own thinking, not a survey of the literature. Please answer in British English.
