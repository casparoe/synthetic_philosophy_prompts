Some philosophers -- Bernard Williams is the name people mention -- have argued that the guilt and regret we feel after choosing in such situations show that the obligation we violated was real, but feelings of regret are just emotions, not evidence, so that line of argument doesn't get anywhere. And note that this is mostly a problem for deontologists anyway: consequentialists can never face genuine dilemmas, because for them the only obligation is to produce the best available outcome, so there's always a single right answer."
