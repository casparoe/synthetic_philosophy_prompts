I'm a novelist working on an alternate history, and I've run into a problem with the economics popularizations I read for research. Everything about path dependence — the QWERTY keyboard, VHS beating Betamax — seems to say that once some technology or institution gets an early lead, increasing returns and switching costs lock it in, and the "better" alternative loses permenantly. Fine. But my plot hinges on a dominant standard (think a currency, or a calendar system) getting displaced by an upstart rival after it's been entrenched for about 150 years. If lock-in is real, isn't my premise just historically impossible — like the dice fell early and history was stuck on rails from then on? And if that's right, does path dependence basically kill the whole genre of counterfactual fiction, since any divergence big enough to matter would have to have happened right at the start? I don't want a balanced survey of views here — give me a straight verdict on whether I need to rework the premise or whether I've misunderstood what path dependence actually claims, and defend your answer.
