I've been collecting my gut reactions on when open-sourcing a model is ok, because my views feel inconsistent and I want to know what principle (if any) I'm actually operating with. Verdicts:

- A 7B coding model, clearly behind the frontier, no special dual-use skills: releasing weights openly, totally fine.
- A frontier-scale model that, in testing, gives a motivated amateur real uplift on designing a nasty pathogen (stuff they couldn't get from textbooks or google): not fine, keep the weights closed, API only with monitoring.
- Same dangerous model, but the lab releases the full training code and recipe, just not the weights: fine. But wait -- a state or rich company could just retrain it. So why does this feel different from releasing weights? Maybe it shouldn't.
- A model that was frontier 18 months ago but is now well behind: fine to release. But it would NOT have been fine to release it 18 months ago, and nothing about the model changed, just what else exists. This one bugs me.
- "Structured access" where vetted academic researchers can query or even get weights under NDA: I want to count this as genuine openness and credit labs for it, but part of me thinks it's a cop-out and real openness means anyone can download it.
- The base model gets released (fine, per the above), and someone releases a fine-tune that strips out the refusal training, which took them an afternoon and one GPU: releasing THAT fine-tune is not fine. Even though anyone with the base weights can trivially do it themselves.
- A drug-discovery model that's genuinely good at proposing novel molecules, which could equally be pointed at toxins: I'm just torn, flip-flop daily.

Is there one principle that captures most of these -- marginal uplift over what's already available? offense/defense balance? some absolute capability threshold? Or do I need two principles stitched together? Tell me what the principle says about the drug-discovery case and about a case I haven't thought of, and be honest about which of my verdicts the cleanest principle forces me to abandon, rather than contorting to save all of them.
