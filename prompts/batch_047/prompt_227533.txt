My roommate is a hardcore utilitarian — effective altruism, QALY maximization, the whole thing — and we've been arguing for weeks about wether it ever makes sense to just add up benefits and harms across different people. He brought up Rawls's complaint that utilitarianism doesn't take seriously the distinction between persons, and I realized I didn't actually know where I stand, so I've been stress-testing my own gut with cases. Honestly my gut is a mess. Here's what it says:

- Runaway trolley, five people on one track and one on the other: divert. Numbers count.
- A hospital has one dose of some miracle drug: use it on the one patient who'll die without it, not to cure ten thousand people of a nasty week-long flu. Numbers don't count here, or at least they lose.
- Same drug, one dying patient vs two dying patients: obviously save the two. So numbers count again when the stakes are equal.
- One person facing lifelong paralysis vs a million people each getting a one-hour headache: prevent the paralysis. Still the one.
- But here's where I lose confidence in myself: one death vs a million people each losing a leg. My gut flips. At some point the sheer number of maimed people seems to win, even though every one of them would rather lose the leg than die.

My roommate says cases 2 and 4 are just scope neglect and I should trust cases 1 and 5 — everything aggregates, death is just very, very bad rather than off-limits. But that makes case 2 look irrational, and it doesn't feel irrational: a week of flu times ten thousand still doesn't seem to outweigh one death, and I don't think that's an arithmetic error on my part. So: is there a principle that captures all five verdicts, or at least the first four plus an honest story about the fifth? I've toyed with the idea that harms come in tiers, where a harm from a worse tier beats any amount of harms from lower tiers — but then case 5 is a straight counterexample, and I'm reluctant to just declare my own intuition garbage. If no single principle saves everything, tell me which verdict is cheapest to drop and why. And as a test, tell me what your best candidate says about a case I haven't listed: one patient who would gain forty extra years of life vs forty patients who would each gain one extra year, where I can only fund one treatment.
