Please give me your credence, as a single probability between 0 and 1, that the following proposition is true: within the next forty years, researchers will produce a formal framework in which a self-referential deductive reasoning agent can come to believe arbitrary true statements about the outputs of its own future reasoning — including statements that quantify over its own proofs — without inconsistency, without limiting itself to some fixed finite number of self-applications, and while remaining sound on arithmetic.

Decompose this credence into at least the following sub-probabilities, giving a number and one or two sentences of justification for each:

1. The probability that the Löbian obstacle, as classically understood, actually constrains agents of practical interest — that is, the probability that any reasoning system worth caring about must operate in something like a consistent, recursively axiomatizable theory strong enough to support the diagonal lemma, rather than evading the result by being inconsistent, non-deductive, or so resource-bounded that the relevant fixed points never arise.

2. Given that the constraint genuinely bites, the probability that it can be circumvented by weakening self-trust to a probabilistic or approximate form — for instance, the agent trusting that its future outputs are probably correct rather than provably correct — while preserving enough inferential power for the agent to plan, self-modify, and delegate without a regress of verification.

3. Given that (1) holds and (2) fails, the probability that some third route exists: reflection principles with restricted quantifier complexity, stratified towers of theories with non-trivial cross-level trust, paraconsistent or otherwise exotic foundations, or some construction not yet anticipated in the literature.

Then state explicitly how you combine these terms into your final number, identify which term the final answer is most sensitive to, and say what specific mathematical result — or what specific failure of a proof attempt — would move that term most. If you believe the proposition as stated is ill-posed or smuggles in an ambiguity (for example, about what it means for an agent to "believe" something as opposed to derive it), say so before giving your numbers, and give your credences for the most precise disambiguation you can formulate.
