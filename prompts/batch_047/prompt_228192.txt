I’m a novelist plotting an alternate-history book, and at dinner two writer friends got into an argument I want refereed. The question was whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler.

Mara said almost certainly yes, at least in the relevant sense. Germany had the humiliation and resentment after World War I, reparations, economic chaos, intense nationalism, widespread antisemitism, a weak democratic system, and powerful conservative elites who thought they could use an authoritarian movement for their own purposes. Once the Great Depression hit, she argued, some radical nationalist regime was very likely. Hitler was the person who happened to occupy the role, but if he hadn’t, another demagogue or military leader would have supplied the missing face.

Jonah said that proves too much. Those conditions might explain an authoritarian Germany, a military dictatorship, or even a major European war, but not specifically Nazi Germany. The Nazi Party could have remained one faction among several without Hitler’s speaking ability, tactical choices, absolute refusal to share power, and talent for making himself seem indispensable. Jonah also said the Holocaust was not already sitting inside the circumstances waiting to happen: antisemitism was widespread, but turning it into bureaucratic genocide required a particular ideological movement and leaders who kept choosing escalation.

Mara replied that Jonah was making the mistake of comparing every alternate Germany against actual Nazi Germany detail for detail, so of course no alternative looks exactly like it. A different leader wouldn’t necessarily use the same symbols, follow the same timetable, or launch the Holocaust in precisely the same way. But if the counterfactual Germany had ultranationalism, dictatorship, racial scapegoating, territorial expansion, and mass persecution, calling it “not Nazi” would be treating the name and biography as more important than the substance.

Jonah answered that Mara had drifted from “Nazi Germany” to “some very bad right-wing Germany.” He agreed that without Hitler there probably still would have been severe instability and perhaps dictatorship. He denied, though, that the specific fusion of Hitler-worship, racial ideology, totalitarian party rule, aggressive expansion, and genocide had a high probability of emerging from the structure alone. Structural forces can make a range of outcomes possible, he said, but they don’t automatically select the worst member of the range.

Mara’s last move was to say Jonah was smuggling in hindsight: because Hitler was the actual vehicle, every contingent feature of Nazism now seems inseparable from the man. If history had produced a different radical movement, historians might now be explaining why that leader and that movement were structurally inevitable too. Jonah’s final response was that this cuts both ways: because the Depression and authoritarian turn feel inevitable in hindsight, Mara may be treating one possible path as if the structure had guaranteed it.

For my novel, I don’t need a bibliography or a survey of every historian’s view. I need a careful, counterfactualy disciplined adjudication of this exact exchange. Say who is right about each major point, where either person changes the question or leans on a questionable inference, and then give a committed overall answer to the original question. Please distinguish separately the odds of (1) a literal Nazi Germany substantially resembling ours, (2) some Nazi-like dictatorship, (3) another European war, and (4) a genocide comparable to the Holocaust. Take a definite position rather than just listing possibilities, and make it clear how confident you are in each judgment and why.
