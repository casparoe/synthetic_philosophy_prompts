Please explain a tension I keep running into in Schopenhauer's aesthetics. On his view, the other arts afford temporary release from suffering because in contemplating the Platonic Ideas, perception escapes the service of the Will and we become, briefly, will-less subjects of knowing. Yet music is the exception: he takes it to be a copy not of the Ideas but of the Will itself, which is supposed to explain its unique depth and power. Two problems seem to follow, and I'd like you to work through both. First, what notion of "copy" can he be using, given that music represents nothing in the phenomenal world -- and does that notion survive his own insistence that music is not a depiction of events or feelings? Second, and more troubling: if the Ideas bring peace precisely because they stand at a remove from the Will, why should the art form in most direct contact with the Will -- the engine of all striving and suffering -- yield the deepest aesthetic satisfaction rather than the most acute distress? Please explain how Schopenhauer's framework can resolve this, or, if you think it cannot, locate exactly where it breaks down.
