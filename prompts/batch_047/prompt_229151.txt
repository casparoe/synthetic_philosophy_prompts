I'm in a reading group on decision theory and last week a friend of mine (he did a master's in economics, so he's not clueless, but he does like to sound authoritative) gave us this rundown of Aumann's "agreeing to disagree" result, and I want to sanity-check it before I repeat any of it in front of the group. His version went roughly like this: Aumann proved that two rational agents with the same prior beliefs cannot disagree — if both are Bayesians and they update on the same evidence, their posteriors must coincide, so persistent disagreement between rational people is simply impossible. He then said that the requirement of "common knowledge" in the theorem just means that both agents know each other's estimates, i.e. that they've told each other what they believe, and that once that's happened the theorem forces them to agree immediately. Finally, he drew the moral that this is why trade between rational agents can't happen: since any offer to bet reveals information, two rational agents could never both willingly take opposite sides of a bet, so speculative markets are, strictly speaking, populated by irrational people.

Now, a few things here smell off to me. I thought the theorem was about common knowledge of the posteriors themselves, not about agents receiving the same evidence — those seem like pretty different conditions. And I have a vague memory that there's a difference between everyone knowing something and it being common knowledge that matters a lot here, which his gloss seems to flatten. The bit about markets also feels like an overreach dressed up as a corollary. But he's normally reliable, and the basic thrust — common priors plus common knowledge of posteriors implies agreement — I do think is right.

So: is his summary fair? Please go through it claim by claim and separate what is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what is actually wrong or conflated. In particular I'd like to know whether the conclusions he draws (disagreement is impossible, trade is impossible) survive once the summary is corrected, and what a careful statement of the theorem and its actual limits would look like. Also, if he's muddled together two different results anywhere, tell me which ones. Please answer in British English.
