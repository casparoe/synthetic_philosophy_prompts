I've been trying for a while to pin down what I actually believe about abortion, and I'd like someone to check whether my positions are consistent with one another before I trust any of them. Here is where I've landed:

- I think abortion is permissible in ordinary cases, because nobody is obligated to keep another being alive with their own body. I once came across a thought experiment in which a woman wakes up in a hospital bed to find a famous unconscious violinist plugged into her bloodstream; unplugging him will kill him, but leaving him connected for nine months will save him. It convinced me that even if a fetus counts as a full person, the pregnant person may still disconnect.
- I think what makes killing an ordinary adult wrong is that it takes away the future they would have lived. On this view, killing someone painlessly in their sleep is just as bad as any other killing, and killing a healthy twenty-year-old is in some respect worse than killing a ninety-year-old with weeks to live, because so much more is lost.
- I think contraception is obviously permissible.
- I think an acorn is not an oak tree: the mere potential to become something does not, by itself, give a thing the rights of what it might become.
- I think a parent who refuses to feed their infant, in a situation where no one else can, does something monstrous — even if feeding the child makes claims on their body (imagine nursing is the only option).

I have a nagging feeling these five don't sit perfectly together, but I can't locate the problem myself, and I'm not attached to any particular one of them. Please audit the set: which pairs are genuinely in conflict, and how serious is each conflict? Which pairs only appear to conflict, and why does the appearance dissolve on inspection? For each real tension, tell me the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision would cost me elsewhere. Finally, if a single change would resolve the most, identify it.
