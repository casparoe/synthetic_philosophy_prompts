You've probably run into the torture vs. dust specks setup before: would you prefer that one person be tortured horribly for 50 years, or that some astronomically large number of people each get a single dust speck in the eye? I'm bored of the standard framing, so here are two variants I want you to compare and then actually take a stand on.

Variant A (interpersonal, the classic): 3^^^3 distinct people each suffer one dust speck in the eye, versus one person being tortured for 50 years.

Variant B (intrapersonal): one person lives for an extremely long time — say a billion years — and once a day, every day, for that entire span, they get a dust speck in the eye. Nothing else bad ever happens to them; the rest of their life is fine. The alternative is that a different person gets tortured for 50 years. The total structure of the aggregation is the same as in A, but now all the tiny harms land inside a single life.

Here's what I want you to think through. Defenders of lexical thresholds (torture is worse in kind, and no number of specks ever adds up to it) often rely, explicitly or not, on a move that's only available in the interpersonal case: each speck-sufferer has only a trivial complaint, and you don't get to pool trivial complaints across different people into one big one. That move is unavailable in Variant B, since there's just one sufferer. Does that make B harder for the threshold view, or is the threshold view really about the *kind* of harm and therefore indifferent to how the harms are distributed across lives? Conversely, if you bite the bullet in B and say the speck-person's billion-year life is the worse outcome, are you then committed to the same answer in A, or is there a principled reason aggregation works within a life but not across lives?

Write an essay. Weigh the main positions — lexical thresholds, vague or incommensurable value, straightforward additive aggregation — but land on a verdict for both variants and justify it. What I care about most is whether your verdict is the same for A and B or different, and if it's different, which morally relevant feature is doing the work.
