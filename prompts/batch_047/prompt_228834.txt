I'm in a translation seminar and we've just started talking about Le Ton beau de Marot and translation under formal constraints. I keep getting stuck on what it would even mean for a translation to "preserve the form," since that could mean a dozen different things.

Suppose we had this little made-up English poem:

A red thread ties the room  
Room after room to rain  
Rain makes the window bloom  
Bloom is my word for pain

The constraints are that the last word of each line becomes the first word of the next, lines 1 and 3 rhyme, lines 2 and 4 rhyme, and the repeated words are also supposed to create a movement from ordinary domestic space to weather to emotion. In many languages you probably cannot preserve all of that at once while also keeping the literal meaning, naturalness, tone, etc.

So before we compare actual translations, I want the desiderata for an adequate theory of form-preserving translation. What would such a theory have to deliver? Please give me a requirements list covering things like:

- which formal features it must be able to identify and rank,
- which distinctions it cannot collapse, like rhyme vs. sound-pattern more generally, or reproducing a constraint vs. producing the same effect on a reader,
- how it should handle cases where form and semantic content are doing the same work,
- what it should say about deliberate deviations, compensation elsewhere in the text, and forms that only become visible across the whole poem,
- whether preserving a constraint means preserving the constraint itself, the difficulty it creates, or the experience it produces,
- which requirements seem genuinely necessary and which might just be prejudices from one school of translation.

Also flag any desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable, exspecially in a case like the poem above, and say which requirement would eliminate the most common approaches. Then briefly score literal translation, formal equivalence, free/adaptive translation, and a constraint-satisfaction approach against the list. I care more about getting the criteria right than about picking a winner, and I don't need citations or a bibliography.
