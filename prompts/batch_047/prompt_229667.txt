Im a retired atmospheric chemist who has wandered into political philosophy late in life, and next month Im giving a brown-bag in a politics department defending a view I call the compulsory exposure account of compatriot priority. Roughly: borders arent just club memberships or cultural containers, they put people under the same coercive machinery and make their risks correlated through tax law, courts, policing, conscription capacity, pandemic rules, environmental regulation, pension promises and so on. So compatriots can have special claims only where priority helps keep that coercive risk-pooling legitimate and reciprocal: emergency care, basic schooling, due process, disaster response, maybe wage insurance during trade shocks. But no automatic priority for status goods, heritage vibes, or policies that quietly export the costs onto outsiders. I like the view because it is not globalist mush and not blood-and-soil either, but I definately worry it is too clever by half.

The audience is not a general public lecture: two Kantian cosmopolitans who think associative duties are mostly rationalization, one civic republican who keeps talking about domination and non-domination, a migration economist who cares about fiscal externalities and labor-market absorption, a legal theorist who will pounce on legitimacy and standing, and one older political sociologist with mild nationalist sympathies who thinks cosmopolitan talk dissolves the motivation needed for welfare states. Prepare my Q and A prep sheet, not a mini essay: list the objections this particular crowd is most likely to raise, ordered by probability times damage, phrased in the vocabulary each person would actually use, with what I should concede fast, what I should hold onto, a two minute reply for each, and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the economist-style objection even if it sounds empirical, the legitimacy objection even if it feels like wordplay, the objection that my view collapses into either Rawlsian luck egalitarianism or ordinary nationalism, and the nasty border case about refugees who are exposed to our coercion without membership. Finish with the question Im hoping nobody asks and the most honest answer I could give without torching the whole account.
