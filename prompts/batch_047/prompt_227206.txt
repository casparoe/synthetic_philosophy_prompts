For an upper-level undergraduate philosophy-of-physics seminar, construct a decision tree that classifies responses to Newton’s rotating-bucket argument. Begin with the observation that the water’s surface can be concave even when the water is not rotating relative to the bucket, and end at a named position on the ontology of space, motion, and inertial structure.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of historical labels:

- Use 10–14 branching nodes and 6–8 leaves.
- Phrase every node as a question that could be answered without knowing which position it leads to. Where possible, formulate the question through a concrete counterfactual rather than abstract terminology.
- The diagnostic cases must include:
  1. a bucket in an otherwise empty universe;
  2. a stationary bucket surrounded by a rotating shell containing all other matter;
  3. two worlds with the same material relations but different patterns of absolute acceleration;
  4. two worlds differing only in which manifold locations are occupied by matter;
  5. a sealed inertial compass that registers rotation without observing external objects;
  6. a world containing matter but no fact determining which trajectories are inertial.
- The leaves must include, though you may subdivide or merge them if necessary:
  - traditional manifold substantivalism;
  - sophisticated substantivalism;
  - Machian relationism;
  - a broader form of modal or Leibnizian relationism;
  - point-free inertial structuralism, on which affine or inertial structure is physically real but individual manifold points are not;
  - dynamicalism, on which inertial geometry encodes features of the laws and material dynamics rather than constituting an independent physical background;
  - a conventionalist or deflationary position, if it occupies a genuinely distinct region of the space.
- Before naming the leaves, briefly map the possible combinations of answers. Identify any plausible combination that is unoccupied and say whether it seems incoherent, technically unstable, merely unattractive, or simply underexplored.
- At each leaf, provide the position’s name, a two-sentence characterization, and its most awkward commitment.
- Flag any positions that the bucket experiment by itself cannot distinguish. In particular, consider whether sophisticated substantivalism and point-free inertial structuralism differ observationally, and whether Machian and other forms of relationism make the same prediction about the rotating shell.
- Keep the main tree within an idealized Newtonian setting. Add a brief note only where switching to general relativity would change which branches remain available.
- Do not provide citations, quotations, or a reading list; the classification should be intelligible from the distinctions and cases stated in the tree itself.
