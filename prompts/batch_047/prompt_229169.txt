Write an essay on the problem of probability in the Everett (many-worlds) interpretation of quantum mechanics. The essay should clearly distinguish the two standard difficulties: the incoherence problem — how talk of probability can even make sense if every possible outcome of a measurement occurs on some branch — and the quantitative problem — why a rational agent's credences should track the squared amplitudes given by the Born rule rather than, say, a simple count of branches. In addressing these, consider the following hypothetical: suppose we discovered that nature only ever produces equal-amplitude splittings, so that branch counting and the Born rule always agree. Would the Everettian's probability problem then be solved, or would something problematic remain? Use your answer to diagnose what the problem really consists in. Then weigh the main strategies available to the Everettian — for instance, decision-theoretic derivations of the Born rule, the proposal to treat amplitude-squared as a primitive measure of how much one should care about each branch, or the denial that ordinary uncertainty requires unique outcomes — against the strongest objections each faces. The essay must not end in agnosticism: reach and defend a verdict on whether coherent, non-circular sense can be made of probability in a branching universe.
