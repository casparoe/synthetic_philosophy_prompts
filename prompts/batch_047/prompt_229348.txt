My roommate is a prosecutor in training, and last week she explained to me how drunk-driving law actually works in practice, and it's been bothering me ever since. Nearly every jurisdiction fixes a bright-line blood alcohol concentration — 0.08% in much of the United States and the UK, 0.05% in most of continental Europe, 0.02% or even 0.00% in parts of Scandinavia — at which a driver is legally impaired, full stop. One hundredth of a percent below, and the conduct is lawful; one hundredth above, and it's a crime. Yet the same legal systems seem remarkably comfortable with vagueness elsewhere: most jurisdictions also criminalize "driving while impaired" or "driving recklessly" with no quantitative threshold at all, leave the determination to officer testimony and jury judgment, and treat the bright-line number and the fuzzy standard as complementary rather than competing tools. On top of that, enforcement practice quietly builds in tolerances — breathalyzers have known error margins, prosecutors rarely charge borderline cases, and the number on the machine isn't quite the number in the blood.

Here's what I want to understand. What picture of vagueness does a sharp statutory threshold like the 0.08 limit presuppose? Does enacting a precise cutoff implicitly commit the legislator to the view that there really is a fact of the matter about where sober ends and impaired begins — a hidden boundary the law aims to track — or is it closer to a stipulation, a deliberate decision to draw a line where nature provides none, on something like the model of how a dictionary settles borderline cases of "heap"? And is the coexistence of the precise BAC rule with the fuzzy "impaired driving" rule a coherent division of labor, or does it reveal that the legal system hasn't decided which theory of vagueness it believes in?

Please reconstruct the implicit theory (or theories) as carefully as you can. In particular, I'd like you to distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits well with — for instance, does the enforcement tolerance for measurement error presuppose anything about vagueness at all, or is it just epistemic humility about instruments, and does the difference between those matter? Finally, tell me what would have to change in this area of law if we took seriously the view that predicates like "impaired" are essentially and irreducibly vague: would the bright-line limit have to be abandoned, re-described, or could it be retained on different foundations?
