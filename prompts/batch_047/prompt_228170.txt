A friend who's really into Bayesian epistemology explained van Fraassen's Reflection principle to me like this: "The idea is that you should defer to your future self. If you know that tomorrow your credence in something will be 0.9, then rationality requires you to set your credence to 0.9 today, because your future self will know more than you do now, so refusing to defer is like betting against the better-informed person. Van Fraassen backs this up with a Dutch book: if your future credences are predictable and different from your current ones, someone can construct a set of bets you're guaranteed to lose money on, which shows that changing your mind without getting new evidence is incoherent. Basically Reflection plus conditionalization forces rational agents to only ever update on new evidence."

I mostly buy the betting part but something about the "your future self knows more" rationale bugs me -- like, what if I know I'm about to get drunk and my credences will be all over the place? Anyway: which parts of his summary would a van Fraassen fan accept, which parts get the principle or its motivation subtly wrong, and does the punchline about conditionalization actually follow from Reflection? If some of what he said is contested rather than settled, flag that too.
