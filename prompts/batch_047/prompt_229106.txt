I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, but I've gotten into this argument with two friends after one of them mentioned on a podcast (some philosophy podcast, forget which) that hedonism has a problem because pleasures supposedly have nothing in common with each other. My first reaction was that this is obviously wrong -- pleasure is just... the good feeling? But the more cases I run through my head the less sure I am, so let me just give you my gut verdicts and you tell me if there's a principle hiding in there or if I'm secretly tracking two or three different things.

My verdicts: first sip of good coffee in the morning, definitely pleasure. Finally merging a PR after two weeks of review hell, pleasure. Scratching an itch, pleasure, which is weird because it's just fixing something bad. The relief when an incident turns out not to be my code -- that one I'd also call a pleasure, but it's literally just a bad thing going away, nothing positive added. A sad movie that makes me cry and that I love: pleasure, even though the crying part is not fun. Anticipating a trip the night before: pleasure, even though nothing good is even happening yet. Now the harder ones. I get into flow states coding where I'm honestly not feeling *anything* for two hours, no good feeling I can point at, and yet afterwards I'd say that was one of the most enjoyable parts of my week -- verdict: not a pleasure while it's happening, but somehow it counts? And the smug little glow when a rival team's launch flops -- it feels good, I'm not proud of that, but my gut says it doesn't really count as a pleasure, or at least not the same kind. Last one, and this is the case I keep flip-flopping on: the tiny hit when I check my phone and there's a notification. It feels like the scratching-an-itch case, but I'd hesitate to call it a pleasure at all, it feels more like being pulled than enjoying something.

So: is there one property all my "pleasure" verdicts share, and what does it do to the flow case, the notification case, and the schadenfreude case? Because from an engineering perspective this looks like someone tried to define a single utility function over things that share no common type, and if pleasures genuinely have nothing in common then "maximize pleasure" seems as ill-typed as "maximize the average of a song, a sandwich, and a nap." Which of my verdicts would I have to give up to get a clean principle, and if instead I keep them all, what's the most principled way to split pleasure into kinds without it becoming a definately ad hoc list?
