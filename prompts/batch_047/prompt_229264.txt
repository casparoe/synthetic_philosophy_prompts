I am in my final year of secondary school and co-host our philosophy society's podcast. Our next guest is a philosopher who defends teleosemantics: roughly, the view that what a mental state represents is fixed by the biological function of the mechanisms producing it, where function is cashed out in terms of selection history rather than current disposition. I have done my background reading -- the usual frog-snapping example, Millikan's consumer-side proposals, Dretske's indicator-based precursor -- and in past episodes I made the mistake of letting guests spend half the hour simply stating their position, which our listeners already know. This time I want to press on the view itself, and I would like your help drafting the questions.

Please write eight to ten interview questions, each followed by one follow-up tailored to the reply our guest is most likely to give. At least one question each should cover: the indeterminacy worry (what, on the selection story, settles that the frog's inner state represents fly rather than small dark moving speck, and whether appeal to what the mechanism was selected for doing genuinely resolves this or merely relocates it); the Swampman objection (the molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person formed by a freak accident, who on this view would apparently have no contentful thoughts whatsoever, and whether the usual dismissals of this case are anything more than appeals to intuition pumping in the other direction); contents that seem to lack any relevant selection history, such as thoughts about things never encountered by one's ancestors or abstract subject matter; and the asymmetry the account needs between correct representation and misrepresentation, including whether the consumer of a representation can supply what the producer cannot. Avoid anything biographical or career-related. For one question of your choosing, also note in a sentence what a genuine concession from the guest would sound like, so I know when I have actually won a point rather than received a deflection.

Number the questions and indent the follow-ups beneath them. The whole response should come to no more than 600 words, and please write in British English.
