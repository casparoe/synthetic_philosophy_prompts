I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long piece about the epiphenomenalist option in the consciousness debate: the view that phenomenal properties are real, nonphysical, and causally inert with respect to the physical world, including the neural events that produce our speech, writing, and introspective judgments about experience. My own credence that epiphenomenalism is true sits around 0.15. A neuroscientist I interviewed for the piece put hers at 0.45, and before I write the section I want to locate where that disagreement actually lives rather than paper over it.

So: give me your credence that epiphenomenalism, as just defined, is true -- but what I really need is the decomposition. Here is my proposed carving, though tell me if you think the joints are wrong: (a) the probability that phenomenal properties are not identical to, or fully realized by, physical properties; (b) the probability that the physical domain is causally closed at the level relevant to human behavior; (c) the probability, conditional on (a) and (b), that causal exclusion forces phenomenal inertness rather than permitting interaction, downward causation, or systematic overdetermination; and (d) the probability that the standard self-undermining worry is survivable. On that last one, please take seriously the strongest version of the reply available to the epiphenomenalist: that my judgment that I have experience could be reliably produced by the neural basis of the experience itself, so that the judgment tracks reality without the experience doing any causing -- and assess whether that machinery is enough to secure knowledge, or whether it leaves the epiphenomenalist in the position of a thermometer whose readings never influence what anyone says about the temperature.

Assign a rough probability to each term with one line of justification, show how they combine into your final number, and identify which term the final number is most sensitive to. Then two closing questions: first, which sub-question most plausibly accounts for the gap between my 0.15 and her 0.45; and second, what empirical or conceptual development over the next decade would most move your own credence, and in which direction.
