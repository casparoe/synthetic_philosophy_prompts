I cover science and ideas, and I’m writing a magazine essay on the Anthropocene that I don’t want to turn into another routine tour of lake sediments, plastics, chicken bones, and radioactive fallout. My hunch is that people keep arguing past each other because they’re asking one label to do three different jobs. Please test that hunch and then take a definite stand.

Use this framework:

1. The stratigraphic question: when did human activity leave a clear, globally detectable mark that future geologists could identify without knowing anything about us?
2. The historical-causal question: when did human societies become a genuinely planet-shaping force, as opposed to merely having important local effects?
3. The political-standing question: whose knowledge, interests, and experiences should count when we decide what the Anthropocene means and who is responsible for it?

Write an essay of roughly 1,200–1,500 words that compares at least four candidate starting points: early farming and deforestation, the Columbian exchange around 1600, the Industrial Revolution, and the mid-twentieth-century Great Acceleration. You can add another candidate if it helps. Don’t just compare the physical evidence for each one. Also examine what each starting point implies about responsibility. An early date risks spreading blame across humanity in general; a very recent date risks letting centuries of imperial extraction disappear; a colonial date can turn a political catastrophe into what looks like a clean natural boundary.

The essay should also address whether a globally legible geological signal should outrank a morally and historically better origin story. If future geologists would most easily detect the mid-twentieth century, but that date badly obscures the forces that made fossil-fuel civilization possible, should the Anthropocene still start there?

Reach a definite verdict on all of these:

- Should the Anthropocene be treated as a formal geological interval, a historical period, or deliberately as two related concepts with different purposes?
- What start date or narrow window should be used for your preferred interpretation?
- If geologists, historians, Indigenous and frontline communities, and climate diplomats disagree, whose judgment should govern which use of the term, and why?

Please don’t end with the usual claim that every perspective is partly right. Make definite choices and defend them, while acknowledging the strongest objection to your own position. No citations or bibliography. Write in American English.
