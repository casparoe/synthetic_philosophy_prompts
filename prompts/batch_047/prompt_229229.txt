In my philosophy of physics seminar, debate over the Rietdijk–Putnam argument has stalled. Everyone accepts that observers in relative motion at a shared here-and-now slice Minkowski spacetime into different simultaneity hyperplanes, so a distant event can be assigned a later time by one observer while being treated as present by another. Block-universe proponents infer that, unless one simultaneity plane has privileged ontological status, that event already belongs to reality. Critics reply that the inference equivocates among being simultaneous relative to a frame, being determinate relative to an event, and being predictable; they also insist that eternalism does not entail determinism. My hypothesis is that the dispute concerns what may count as ontology under relativistic constraints, not any unresolved physics. Diagnose the impasse from the argument’s actual steps: is it verbal, modal, metaphysical, or secretly physical? What distinction or formal result would constitute progress, and why has neither emerged?
