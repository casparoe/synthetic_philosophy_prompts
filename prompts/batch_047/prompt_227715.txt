So a friend of mine who did a philosophy module last year sent me her summary of Nyāya epistemology, because she knows I've been reading about the sorites paradox and thought the Indian stuff on knowledge-sources might interest me. Here's what she wrote:

"In the Nyāya school, knowledge comes from four sources (pramāṇas): perception, inference, comparison, and testimony. Inference basically means induction -- you observe a bunch of cases where smoke goes with fire, generalise, and then you can infer fire from smoke the next time. Comparison is reasoning by analogy, like arguing that because two things are similar in some respects they'll be similar in others. Testimony is just trusting what reliable people or scriptures tell you, which shows the school wasn't really doing epistemology in the Western sense, since it builds authority in from the start. All the classical Indian schools accepted these four sources, they just disagreed about how to rank them."

Now, I mostly believe her because she got a good mark in the module, but a couple of things niggle at me. The bit about testimony making it "not real epistemology" seems like a weirdly confident verdict, and I have a vague memory that the Indian schools actually disagreed quite sharply about how many pramāṇas there even are. Also, is inference really just induction? That seems too simple.

Could you audit the summary for me -- tell me which parts are accurate, which are plausible-sounding but subtly wrong or oversimplified, and what a defender of the Nyāya view would push back on hardest? I'd rather you commit to a verdict on each point with reasons than just lay out possibilities and leave me to decide. And please answer in British English.
