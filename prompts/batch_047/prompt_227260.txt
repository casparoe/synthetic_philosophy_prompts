Ok so I'm in a philosophy of well-being seminar and we've been going over the heterogeneity problem for hedonism -- the worry that pleasures (eating, solving a puzzle, orgasm, quiet contentment, schadenfreude lol) have nothing introspectively in common, so "pleasure" can't be a single good-making property. I've pretty much landed on the attitudinal hedonist response: what all pleasures share isn't a felt quality, it's that the subject takes a pro-attitude toward the experience (wanting it to continue, being glad it's happening, whatever). The felt qualities can be as diverse as they like; the attitude is the common thread.

What I want to know is: what would actually count against this? Like, concretely. Which cases or intuitions should make me give it up -- I'm thinking stuff like pleasures you're indifferent or even averse to while having them, or whether "taking a pro-attitude" can be identified without circularly defining it in terms of pleasure, or whether the view quietly collapses into a desire theory and inherits all its problems. Which of these would a committed attitudinal hedonist concede as a real threat vs. brush off? And is there anything the view risks at all, or has it been qualified into something unfalsifiable -- and if so, is that fatal for a theory of well-being or just par for the course? Rank the defeaters from "this sinks it" to "awkward but livable" if you can.
