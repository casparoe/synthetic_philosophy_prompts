i cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine and i have a running irritation i want to turn into a column: every third pitch i recieve invokes plato's cave. the feed is the cave and the posts are the shadows. QAnon is the cave. VR is the cave, AI-generated content is the cave, "the mainstream media" is the cave depending on who's emailing me. and the analogy is always doing the same job: most people are chained up staring at projections, the pitcher (or their subject, or their book) has slipped the chains and seen the real thing, and anyone who disagrees just proves how deep in the shadow-show they are. unfalsifiable, flattering, and apparently immune to overuse.

rather than just roll my eyes in print i want to audit the thing properly, because plato presumably knew what his own allegory was for. so:

1. the correspondence table. in the actual allegory (Republic book VII: the lifelong chained prisoners, the fire, the raised way behind them with people carrying artifacts along a screen, the forced ascent, the sun, the return) -- what is each element doing in plato's own setup, and what does each get mapped onto in the modern "your feed is the cave" usage? i suspect the modern version quietly drops some load-bearing details: that plato's shadows are shadows *of artifacts*, i.e. copies of copies, that the prisoners give each other honors and prizes for who's best at predicting which shadow comes next (if i'm remembering that right), and that the fire stands in for something specific rather than just "bad vibes."

2. where the mapping breaks in ways that actually matter for the conclusion it's carrying. a few i want pressure-tested: plato's prisoners don't know they're prisoners and are genuinely skilled competitors at the shadow game, whereas modern feed users mostly know it's a feed and are bored by their own cynicism. plato's puppeteers seem to be inside the cave themselves, not masterminds who've been outside -- does he even give them a motive, and if not, where does the modern version's villain (the algorithm, the media) come from? the escape in plato is forced, painful, and slow, with the freed prisoner at first seeing *worse* than the people still chained, not a matter of deleting an app. and the one that seems fatal to me: for plato the whole sensible world is the image realm, so "log off and go touch grass" is still inside the cave by his own mapping -- the columnists put the sun where plato put more shadows.

3. does the conclusion survive? the pitch version concludes roughly: distrust everything mediated, trust the ones who got out. plato's version seems to conclude something weirder: the person who comes back down is half-blind in the dark, loses the shadow-guessing contests to people who never left, looks ridiculous, and the cave-dwellers would kill him if they could (which i take it is about socrates). and plato still thinks the philosopher has to go back down and rule anyway. so does the allegory actually support the distrust-the-masses moral it's used for, or does it undercut it -- the shadow experts really are good at shadows, the escapee can barely function, and getting out isn't a credential for anything except a long painful re-entry? if there's a legitimate core the analogy can carry about recommendation systems and curated feeds, tell me what it is and what a more honest use would look like; if the modern usage is basically laundering "everyone but me is a sheep" through a canonical text, say that instead.

casual register is fine but do the philosophy like you mean it.
