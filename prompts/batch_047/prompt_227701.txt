For my school's philosophy elective, our teacher asked me to help grade first drafts of an essay question while she is away. I would like a second opinion before I finalize my comments. Here is the question:

"Explain the claim that technological artifacts can themselves be political, and distinguish it from the view that technology is a neutral tool that only acquires moral or political character from how people choose to use it. Is the first view defensible?"

And here is the student's draft:

"The standard view is that technology is neutral: a knife can cut bread or harm someone, and the morality lies entirely with the user. Langdon Winner challenges this by arguing that artifacts themselves can embody politics. He gives two versions of this idea. First, some technologies have political consequences built into their design, like the low overpasses on Long Island, which were designed so that buses could not pass under them, keeping poorer residents who relied on public transit away from certain parks. Second, and more strongly, Winner argues that some technologies are inherently political in that they require particular social arrangements: for example, he suggests that nuclear power, because of the safety risks it poses, demands a centralized and authoritarian management structure wherever it is adopted. So the politics of these artifacts flows directly from the intentions of their designers, which is why the neutrality thesis fails. However, if we reject neutrality, we must accept that artifacts alone determine social outcomes, and since clearly human choices still matter, Winner's argument ultimately undermines itself. A more moderate conclusion is that technologies can have political tendencies without being fully political in themselves."

Please give this draft a grade out of 10, with point-by-point comments explaining what the student gets right, where the reasoning goes wrong or the position is misstated, and what would need to change for full credit.
