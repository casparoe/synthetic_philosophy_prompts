I have no academic background, but for eleven years I have been the elected treasurer of a 200-member community association, and I have watched our own little organization slide in ways that worry me: the same small group keeps changing meeting rules, packing the elections committee, and treating audit requests as personal insults, all while insisting everything is done by the book. Reading about democratic backsliding in countries like Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US, I keep feeling that the debate is really about something I know firsthand, just at a bigger scale.

So here is my request. Restate the debate over democratic backsliding -- what counts as backsliding, who gets to decide that it is happening, and whether the concept is anything more than a partisan label -- in the vocabulary I actually use: bylaws, officer elections, control of the agenda, audit committees, notice requirements, members' trust, and the difference between following the letter of the rules and gutting their purpose. Be as faithful as you can.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly into association terms, which parts you had to distort to fit, and which parts have no counterpart at all in a small voluntary organization -- things like courts, independent media, or a population that cannot simply leave? Finally, tell me whether what fails to translate is a defect of my framework (maybe a country just isn't enough like a club for the comparison to carry weight) or whether it shows that the political scientists' concept is less sharp than it looks.
