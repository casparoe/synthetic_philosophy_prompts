I'm on an animal ethics committee for a local wildlife charity, and at our last meeting I proposed a modest pilot program -- supplemental winter feeding on a reserve where young deer routinely starve in late-season cold snaps. A senior member shut the discussion down with a single sentence: "nature knows best." I've heard the same phrase from friends when I mention broader ideas like vaccinating wild populations against painful diseases or managing parasites, and it seems to function as a conversation-ender every time, as though intervening in ecosystems to reduce suffering were not merely impractical but somehow conceptually confused.

The problem is that I genuinely can't tell what claim is being made. Sometimes it sounds like a cautious empirical point -- ecosystems are complex, interventions backfire, so we should usually abstain. Sometimes it sounds like something much stronger, almost an appeal to nature in the strict sense, where what's natural is being treated as good or beyond moral criticism. And sometimes it seems to be a point about moral standing, as if wild processes were owed a kind of respect that human suffering would never be owed. I'd like the different precise theses this slogan could be expressing laid out as a menu: for each reading, state it carefully, say whether it's a platitude, a plausible but contested empirical claim, a substantive moral thesis, or simply false, and note what would follow for intervention cases if it were true. Most importantly, tell me which readings could actually justify the dismissive use it was put to in my meeting -- i.e., which ones would rule out my feeding program without further argument -- and whether those readings survive scrutiny.

I'd also like the answer to be honest about the state of disagreement here: separate what is genuinely settled -- say, the basic ecology of how interventions can misfire, or the uncontroversial fact that wild animals experience very large amounts of hardship -- from what is legitimately contested among people who work on wild animal welfare, and don't present a contested position (in either direction) as the consensus view.
