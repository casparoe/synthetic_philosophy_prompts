I am a PhD student in epistemology, and my dissertation chapter is on hinge epistemology — specifically on what I am calling the "membership problem": given Wittgenstein's remarks in On Certainty, what settles whether a particular proposition belongs to the riverbed or to the water flowing in it? My supervisor thinks I have been treating hinge status as a binary, all-or-nothing property, whereas the text supports something more graded. To test this, I have been building rankings of candidate propositions and checking whether my intuitions about their relative hinge-status survive contact with the actual text. I would like you to do the same exercise, independently, so I can compare.

The passage I keep returning to is On Certainty, §94 (Anscombe and Paul translation): "But I did not get my picture of the world by satisfying myself of its correctness; nor do I have it because I am satisfied of its correctness. No: it is the inherited background against which I distinguish between true and false."

Here are the ten candidate propositions. Please assign each a hinge-score from 0 (clearly water, an ordinary empirical belief open to testing in the usual way) to 10 (clearly riverbed, part of the inherited background in Wittgenstein's sense), and sort them accordingly:

(a) I have two hands.
(b) The earth existed long before I was born.
(c) Water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.
(d) 12 × 12 = 144.
(e) My name is [the speaker's own name].
(f) Objects do not cease to exist when unobserved.
(g) The theory of plate tectonics is broadly correct.
(h) Other people have inner lives.
(i) The sun will rise tomorrow.
(j) The universe is a computer simulation run by future humans.

Three constraints on your scoring. First, each score must be justified from §94 specifically, not from the famous hinge metaphor elsewhere in the book: say which feature of the passage — the absence of verification ("satisfying myself of its correctness"), the inherited character of the background, or its functional role as that against which true and false are distinguished — is doing the work in your verdict, and be honest where the three features pull apart. Second, at least one pair of propositions on the list should end up in an order that would surprise someone working from the standard Moorean reading of On Certainty; identify that pair and defend the ordering. Third, flag any proposition whose score you think is genuinely indeterminate under §94 alone, and say what further remark or doctrine from the book would have to be brought in to settle it.

Finally, a meta-question my supervisor insists I answer: is hinge-status, on your analysis, a property a proposition has simpliciter, or only relative to a person, community, or historical moment — and does §94's talk of inheritance settle this, or merely suggest an answer? Please keep the whole response, excluding the quoted passage, under 900 words.
