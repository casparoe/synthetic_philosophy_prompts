I help run a small moral philosophy reading group and we're deep in contractualist rescue cases at the moment -- the framework where an act is wrong if it would be ruled out by a principle that someone affected could reasonably reject, where each person gets to complain only on their own behalf, so you can't just pile up millions of tiny burdens on seperate people and say they outweigh one person's huge burden. Our convenor has been giving verdicts case by case and I want to figure out the rule she's actually running before the next session. Her calls so far:

1. A technician is trapped in the works at a power station and will be badly burned unless we shut the grid down for an hour, which would cut the live broadcast of a cup final and mildly spoil the evening for ten million viewers. She said: shut it down, burn the technician's evening plans are not the issue, he gets saved.
2. A lifeboat can reach one drowning swimmer in one direction or five drowning swimmers in the other, same effort, all strangers, all will die without rescue. She said: go for the five.
3. One dose of a drug left. Patient A dies without it. Patient B dies without it and the disease will also blind her if she somehow survives. She said: give it to B.
4. A runaway tram will either kill one track worker or sever one arm each from twenty workers on the other branch. She said: send it towards the twenty -- the one keeps his life, the twenty lose arms.

Here's the new case she's promised us for next week. The tram will either leave one worker permanently blind in both eyes, or leave forty workers each permanently blind in one eye, and the driver has to pick a branch.

So: what does she say, and why? The bit I really care about is that I can see at least two different rules that fit all four of her earlier verdicts -- one where only pairwise comparisons between individuals ever matter and numbers only break exact ties, another where numbers count whenever the harms on each side are close enough in kind and seriousness -- and they seem to pull apart on this new case depending on whether one-eye blindness and both-eyes blindness count as the same tier of harm. Lay out the candidate rules properly, say what each predicts, give me your best guess at her actual verdict with the reasons, and tell me which further case I should propose to the group to force her rule into the open. If you think her four verdicts are actually in tension with each other rather than rule-governed at all, say that instead and defend it. Please answer in British English.
