I run a fortnightly interdisciplinary reading group at my university (philosophers, physicists, a few economists), and we are spending a session on the Fermi paradox. I want to devote the meeting to the dark forest hypothesis, roughly as developed in Liu Cixin's novel: the universe may be crowded with civilisations that stay silent because revealing your location invites pre-emptive destruction, given that survival is every civilisation's first need, that civilisations grow while the total stock of matter does not, that you cannot verify another civilisation's intentions across interstellar distances (the chain of suspicion), and that a technological explosion can let a presently weaker civilisation overtake you during the centuries your message is in transit.

Rather than discuss all this in the abstract, I want a game or structured simulation, playable in about 100 minutes with 12–16 people seated around tables with pen and paper (no laptops), that makes the group *feel* the logic before it is named. Specifically, I want the rules arranged so that most players begin the game intending to cooperate and trade, and discover over successive rounds that striking first against a newly detected civilisation is the rational move even when they harbour no hostility — that the suspicion does the work by itself, because intentions cannot be verified and technological overtake cannot be ruled out. If at all possible, at least some players should be given secret constraints or incentives (e.g. genuinely pacifist civilisations that can never strike, or factions that gain from trade) so that the group also feels the asymmetry between being trustworthy and being unable to *prove* it.

Please include: the rules and setup (detection mechanics, communication delay, how technological growth is tracked, what a strike costs and achieves), the payoff scheme with some small real stake that doesn't require money, how eliminated players are occupied, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps what actually happened in the room onto the components of the hypothesis (survival as primary motive, growth against finite resources, chains of suspicion, technological explosion, exposure as fatal). Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — players treating it as a party game and striking at random, metagaming through table talk, the strongest personality dominating — and how to handle each. Be honest about which feature of the hypothesis the game cannot reproduce: real players can read each other's faces and have reputations, whereas actual civilisations cannot, so tell me where the anonymity mechanic leaks and what I must supply verbally instead. The participants should not hear the phrase "dark forest" until the debrief.

After the full design, give me a summary table with the columns: Game element (rule or phase) | Dark forest concept it models | What participants should feel at that moment | Predictable failure mode and fix.

Please write in British English.
