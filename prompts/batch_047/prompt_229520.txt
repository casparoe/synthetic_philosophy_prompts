Write a sestina about Erwin Schrödinger's late conviction, formed through his reading of the Upanishads, that consciousness is numerically one: that the sense of being an "I" is the very same inwardness in every being that has it, that the plurality of minds belongs to the realm of appearance rather than to what is ultimately real, and that each subject's inner life is not a fragment or spark of the absolute but, as the Upanishadic formula has it, identical with it entire.

Use these six end-words in the standard sestina permutation: eye, glass, window, name, one, sleep. Let each of the six stanzas be spoken from a different embodied position -- a child, an old person facing death, a soldier, a reader alone at night, and so on, your choice -- so that the recurring end-words land in a different mouth each time. The rotation of the same six words through six voices should itself do philosophical work: it is the closest a poem can come to showing, rather than asserting, one consciousness looking out of many windows.

One stanza should be spoken by, or addressed to, the cat in the box. Handle this carefully: the point is not the common misreading that Schrödinger thought consciousness collapses the wave function. The cat matters here because its unseen inner life, sealed off from every observer, is the same inwardness as the observer's -- not because it is a measuring device.

The three-line envoi must gather all six end-words into the mouth of a single speaker, so that the reader belatedly understands that the six voices were one voice all along. In doing so, thread the needle Schrödinger himself threads: the closing claim must not sound like one ego grandiosely proclaiming itself to be God, which would be madness, but like the quiet dissolution of the picture on which there were ever many egos to begin with.

The result should be accurate enough that a reader who knows the doctrine of maya and Schrödinger's singulare tantum argument finds the poem right about them, and self-contained enough that a reader who knows neither still finds it a good sestina.
