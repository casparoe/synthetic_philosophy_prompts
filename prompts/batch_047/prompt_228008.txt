I find myself persuaded by the view that culture can be a genuine cause in social explanation -- specifically, by a Weber-style claim that the values he associated with ascetic Protestantism (methodical work as a calling, systematic reinvestment, worldly asceticism) did real causal work in the emergence of modern capitalism, rather than merely rationalizing economic changes that would have happened anyway. I do not mean the strong claim that the ethic was necessary or sufficient, only that it was a contributing cause of the kind that could have made a difference. Tell me what would count against that position. I want several things distinguished. First, the empirical defeaters: which historical patterns would genuinely undermine it (capitalism arising where the relevant values were absent, the values flourishing for centuries where capitalism did not arise, the ethic appearing in the record only after the economic practices it is supposed to explain), and for each, how a defender would respond and whether the response is legitimate or merely protective. Second, the epistemological defeaters: can the presence and strength of a value be measured independently of the outcome it is meant to explain, or does the thesis inherit a confirmation problem that no amount of archival evidence can fix? Third, the rival-mechanism defeaters: confounders such as literacy, urban institutions, or church-state arrangements that might account for both the values and the economic takeoff, and what it would take to separate these channels in a domain where experiments are impossible. Fourth, whether the moderate version I hold has been qualified into something no evidence could touch, and if so whether that vagueness is a defect or just the correct epistemic posture for causal claims about whole civilizations. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which of them a committed Weberian would themselves concede as counting, and identify what the position predicts that a purely materialist rival does not -- so that I can tell whether anything is actually at stake between us.
