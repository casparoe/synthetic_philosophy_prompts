Something I keep noticing in arguments and I want to know if there's an actual name for it, because "circular reasoning" doesn't seem to quite cover it.

My uncle is convinced that his favourite YouTube news channel is trustworthy. I asked how he knows, and he said: because he's checked its claims plenty of times and they always turned out to be true. So I asked how he checked, and it turns out he checks by watching later videos on the same channel, or seeing whether the channel's follow-up reporting confirms its earlier stories. Each individual check looks fine on the surface -- the channel said X, later the channel confirmed X, check passes. But the whole procedure somehow adds up to nothing, and I can't articulate exactly why.

Same shape with my brother's car. He trusts his fuel gauge because, he says, every time it's said half a tank he's checked and the tank really was half full. Where "checked" meant... looking at the gauge again, maybe at a different angle. He was half joking, but half not.

And honestly I do the same thing with my own memory. If you ask me why I think my memory is reliable, all I can really do is recall a bunch of occasions where my memory served me well. But those are themselves memories. There doesn't seem to be anywhere else to stand.

What gets me is that none of this looks like the textbook fallacy where you just assume the thing you're trying to prove. Nobody's slipped the conclusion in as a premise. Every single step, taken alone, seems legitimate -- it's the loop as a whole that's rotten. Is there an established concept for this specific pattern, where a method or source gets vouched for entirely by its own outputs? And if so, how is it supposed to differ from ordinary circular reasoning, assuming it does? Also curious whether the memory case is even the same problem as the fuel gauge case, since with the gauge you could at least in principle check some other way (stick a dipstick in the tank or whatever), whereas with memory there seems to be no outside vantage point at all. If there isn't a clean name for exactly this, feel free to coin one, but I'd want the definition to say something about why these cases feel broken even though no individual step is.
