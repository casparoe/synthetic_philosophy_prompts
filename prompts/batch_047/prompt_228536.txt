I've been thinking about killing vs. letting die and I want to know what principle, if any, is underneath my gut reactions. For background, I keep coming back to the bit in Aquinas's Summa Theologiae (II-II, q.64, a.7) where he defends lethal self-defense by saying, in effect, that one act can have two effects and what matters morally is the effect you intend, not the one you merely foresee as a side effect. Feel free to lean on that or tell me it can't do the job.

My verdicts:
- Bystander trolley: divert the runaway car onto a siding, killing one worker to save five -- permissible.
- Transplant: surgeon kills one healthy patient to harvest organs for five -- clearly not permissible, even though the arithmetic is identical.
- I skip a $200 donation to an effective charity and spend it on a ski weekend instead -- a bit shabby of me, but nowhere near murder, and I don't think I'm obligated to donate.
- Drowning toddler in a shallow pond, I'm the only one around, rescue costs me wet shoes: not rescuing is monstrous, close to as bad as holding the kid under. I realise this sits badly with the donation verdict.
- A doctor unplugs a ventilator at a competent patient's insistent request -- permissible; the same doctor giving the same patient a lethal injection, same request, same outcome -- not permissible (or at least much worse).
- I notice a coworker about to drink from a water bottle I know someone else poisoned, and I just say nothing -- that feels like killing, not letting die, even though I did literally nothing.

What I want, in this order: The principle (about a paragraph -- one principle, stated precisely, or tell me no single principle survives and give me the best candidate anyway). Case by case (roughly half a page -- how it rules on each of my six, flagging any case where it needs an extra clause and why that clause isn't just ad hoc). The verdict I'd have to drop (a short paragraph -- if the principle can't get all six, say which one goes and talk me into it). And a stress test (a few sentences -- give me one new case my principle and Aquinas's intended/foreseen idea disagree on, so I can see which one I actually believe).
