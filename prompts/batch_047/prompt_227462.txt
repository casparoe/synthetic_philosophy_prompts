Suppose a small, present-day republican city resembling Rousseau’s Geneva proposes replacing its traditional civic festival with an “adaptive republic pageant”: residents wear virtual-reality headsets, receive speaking roles shaped by generative systems, and collectively influence the plot, while a professional studio designs the scenarios and an algorithm personalizes each participant’s emotional arc to maximize engagement. Taking Rousseau’s critique of professional theater, actors, imitation, and passive spectatorship together with his praise of participatory civic festivals, predict whether he would approve the replacement, reject it, or endorse it only after substantial redesign. Do not treat interactivity as automatically decisive: consider whether role-playing remains corrupting when it is responsive rather than conventionally scripted, whether technologically mediated participation creates genuine common presence or merely synchronized isolation, whether emotional identification with fictional characters preserves the moral danger Rousseau found in theatrical representation, and whether algorithmic personalization reproduces commercial theater’s cultivation of appetite and self-display. Give a committed verdict and defend it against the strongest alternative reading, rather than presenting several possibilities without choosing. Answer in exactly five numbered points of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion.
