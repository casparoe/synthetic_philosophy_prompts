Write a dialogue between two equally sharp philosophers of mind after seeing the same lab demo: volunteers watch a video where 80 ms slices are secretly removed, yet most swear the clip was seamless, and a few can later describe hidden objects they insist they never noticed. One defends a modest Cartesian theater: not a little homunculus, but a single neural stage where contents are bound, timestamped, and only then count as experienced. The other defends Dennett-style multiple drafts: there are lots of parallel editorial processes, and asking exactly when the finished version entered consciousness is a confused question. Have them actually argue, not trade slogans: they should fight over whether the gap case shows postdiction or genuine filling-in, whether reportability is being confused with phenomenality, whether a theater can be distributed in space but still unified in time, and what would count as evidence against each view. Neither side should be made stupid, and end with one of them conceding a real cost of their position, tho not necessarily giving it up.
