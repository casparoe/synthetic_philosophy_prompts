I've been arguing with my flatmate about mind uploading again (he's doing a conversion masters in cognitive science and won't shut up about it). His favourite move is an analogy he's certain settles everything: your body is already constantly swapping out its matter -- cells die and get replaced, atoms come and go, and supposedly every cell in your body is renewed every seven years or so -- and yet nobody thinks you stop being you because of it. So, he says, replacing your brain neuron by neuron with silicon parts that do exactly the same functional job is just the same process with fancier materials. Continuity of self is preserved in the natural case, so it's preserved in the artificial case, and gradual uploading is no more threatening than growing older. He concedes that instantaneous scanning-and-copying might be different, but thinks gradual replacement is as safe as metabolism.

I'm not convinced the analogy earns what he wants it to earn, but I can't quite pin down where it goes wrong. Can you audit it properly? Lay out the explicit mapping -- what in natural bodily turnover is supposed to correspond to what in neuron-by-neuron silicon replacement -- and then go through the disanalogies one by one. In particular I'd like to know which of these actually matter for the identity question and which are just incidental: the rate of replacement (slow trickle vs chosen schedule), the fact that in the uploading case the original material is discarded rather than recycled by the same ongoing process, whether the 'everything is replaced every seven years' claim is even true of the brain in the first place, and whether it makes a difference that natural turnover is something that happens *to* you while uploading is something done *to order*.

And the payoff question: if the analogy does break down somewhere, does my flatmate's conclusion break down with it -- i.e. is gradual replacement actually any safer than the teleporter-style copy he concedes is dicey? Or can the conclusion survive on other grounds even if his favourite analogy can't carry it? If there's a better analogy for what's going on in gradual uploading, I'd like to hear that too. Please answer in British English, and feel free to use a table or structured list for the correspondences if that helps.
