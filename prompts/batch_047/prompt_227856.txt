I am a first-time teaching assistant for an undergraduate course in historical sociology, and I am grading short-answer responses to the following exam question:

"Max Weber's The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism is often described as showing that culture can be a cause of economic change. Explain the causal mechanism Weber proposes connecting Calvinist doctrine to modern capitalism, and state one way in which Weber qualifies or limits this claim."

One of my students answered as follows:

"Weber argues that the Calvinist doctrine of predestination had unintended psychological consequences. Because no one could know whether they were among the elect, believers lived in anxiety about their salvation, and they came to treat success in their worldly calling as a way of demonstrating and thereby securing their election. Combined with the Lutheran idea of the calling — the notion that diligent work in one's worldly station is a religious duty — this produced what Weber calls inner-worldly asceticism: systematic, disciplined labor, frugality, and the reinvestment of profits rather than their consumption. These habits, Weber claims, generated the spirit of modern capitalism, an ethos in which accumulation is pursued as an end in itself. Weber presents this as a necessary-condition explanation: without the Protestant ethic, modern rational capitalism could not have arisen, and in this respect his account stands as a direct refutation of historical materialism, which takes economic conditions to determine ideas rather than the reverse. He thus shows that values are not mere reflections of material interests but can independently drive economic transformation."

Please grade this response out of 10 and give me the specific comments I should write on it, organized point by point: what the answer gets right, what specific corrections it needs (with the correct statement of Weber's position in each case), and where, if anywhere, partial credit is appropriate for a claim that is defensible only in a weaker form. I would like the comments to be suitable for returning to the student, so please phrase them accordingly.
