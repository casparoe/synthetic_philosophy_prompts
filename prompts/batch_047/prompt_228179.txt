Please explain what exactly Descartes' cogito establishes, addressing the following in turn. First, the dispute between reading it as an inference — I think, therefore I exist, which appears to smuggle in a suppressed premise such as "whatever thinks, exists," and which Descartes would presumably need to doubt — and reading it as a performance, in which the act of doubting enacts the very existence it reports rather than deducing it. Second, Lichtenberg's objection that the cogito at most licenses "thinking is occurring" rather than "I think": what work is the "I" doing, and can a subjectless reformulation still survive radical doubt, or does it lose the certainty that made the argument attractive? Third, apply your analysis to this case: suppose a fleeting episode of thought occurs in a Boltzmann-brain-like fluctuation lasting a millisecond, with the content "I am doubting whether I exist," after which nothing continues. Does the cogito succeed for that episode? Who, if anyone, could affirm it, given that affirmation seems to require a temporally extended subject the scenario excludes? Conclude by stating the strongest minimal claim the cogito can support without presupposing the persistence, unity, or substantiality of a self. Please write your answer in British English.
