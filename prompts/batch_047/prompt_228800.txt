I am preparing a position statement on the ethics of humanitarian intervention for a policy institute's working group, and before circulating it I want a rigorous consistency audit of the commitments I intend to defend. Here is the full set:

1. NATO's 1999 intervention in Kosovo was justified, even though it proceeded without Security Council authorization.
2. The 2003 invasion of Iraq was not justified, and its failure was not merely a failure of execution or intelligence; it was illegitimate in principle.
3. What ultimately distinguishes legitimate from illegitimate interventions is the genuinely humanitarian motive of the interveners, not the presence or absence of a legal mandate. Law tracks legitimacy imperfectly at best.
4. The 2011 intervention in Libya was justified when it began, but NATO's expansion of the mission from protecting civilians to pursuing regime change was a betrayal of the authorizing resolution's terms, and that overreach is precisely what made the campaign, taken as a whole, illegitimate.
5. State sovereignty is a genuine moral value, not merely a legal convenience, and unauthorized intervention damages the international order in ways that must be counted as real costs, even when the intervention succeeds.
6. A Security Council veto (or threatened veto) by an interested power cannot function as a moral veto on humanitarian rescue; if it did, the most atrocity-prone situations would be systematically unaddressable.

My questions are these. First, are all six commitments jointly tenable, or do some of them rest on incompatible pictures of where the legitimacy of an intervention comes from? I am especially interested in whether the standard I use to sort the cases is one standard or two different standards applied opportunistically. Second, identify any tensions that are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, so that I do not waste the group's time defending against objections that miss. Third, for each genuine tension, state exactly which commitment would have to be revised or abandoned to resolve it, and what that revision would cost me in terms of the case verdicts I want to keep. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious.

I am not asking for a survey of positions in the literature. I want a committed verdict: tell me which single revision to my set restores the most consistency at the lowest cost, defend that choice against the next-best alternative, and be explicit about where you are confident and where your reasoning depends on a contestable premise.
