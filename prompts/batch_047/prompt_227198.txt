Is there an established name for the following pattern in how competent speakers treat borderline cases? Consider a man who is genuinely on the border between tall and not tall. The same speakers who refuse to call him tall, and equally refuse to call him not tall, will happily grant that he is either one or the other, and will flatly reject the suggestion that he is somehow both. Nor do speakers who give opposite verdicts about him correct one another: each verdict seems permitted, rather than one being a mistake. I am aware of two rival accounts of borderline cases — one on which such statements are neither true nor false, and one on which they are true or false but we cannot know which. Does the pattern of use I have described have a name of its own? Does it fit one account better than the other, or is it exactly what both accounts predict? If it has no established label, coin one, define it, and mark its edges; and in any case say what observable behaviour, if any, would actually discriminate between the two accounts, since I suspect this pattern cannot. Please answer in British English.
