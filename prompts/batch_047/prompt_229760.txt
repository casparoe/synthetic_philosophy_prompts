Two colleagues in my philosophy of religion reading group have been arguing past each other for two years, and I want to force them to produce something constructive. One is a committed Molinist: she holds that God, prior to any creative decree, knows all true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, and that these truths are not up to God and not grounded in anything actual. The other presses the grounding objection and favors a broadly Thomist picture on which God knows what free creatures will do by willing and effecting it, since on his view there is nothing in virtue of which a never-actualized free choice would have gone one way rather than the other. Draft the joint statement they should be able to co-author after a genuine adversarial collaboration. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both can sign, which I expect to include the existence of libertarian-free creatures, the infallibility of divine foreknowledge, and the inadequacy of purely compatibilist accounts of providence, but which should also state precisely what each side owes by way of explanation; (2) a disagreements section in table form, where each disputed thesis is stated once in wording both accept as neutral and then glossed in each party's own preferred terms; (3) a cruxes section. At minimum, the cruxes should cover whether truth requires a ground in actuality or whether some contingent truths are simply ungrounded; whether counterfactuals about the free acts of creatures God never creates (not merely never actualizes in these circumstances, but never brings into existence at all) are true, false, or truth-valueless, and what each side's answer costs it; and whether the Thomist alternative can distinguish divine concurrence from causal determination without covertly denying the libertarian freedom both parties affirm. For each crux, specify an argument, thought experiment, or theoretical commitment that both parties agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts or concedes about it. Finally, include a short closing section identifying any part of their dispute the drafters conclude is merely verbal, and explain the diagnosis. Format matters: numbered agreed-claims first, then the table, then the cruxes, then the verbal-dispute section.
