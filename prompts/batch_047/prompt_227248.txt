So I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a big feature about whether the sciences actually carve nature at its joints or just sort of saw it into convenient chunks. I've already done the Pluto thing to death (every editor wants Pluto, I'm sick of Pluto), and I've got interviews lined up with a philosopher, a taxonomist, and a guy who runs protein-structure ML models. Before I talk to any of them I want a spec sheet: what would a theory of natural kinds actually have to deliver before I'd let it drive the framing of the piece? Give me the desiderata -- the cases it has to get right (chemical elements, obviously, since that's everyone's anchor example, but also species given hybridization and ring species, psychiatric diagnostic categories, whether a cluster a neural net finds in protein-fold space counts as a discovery or a filing choice, maybe synthetic minimal cells if you're feeling brave), the distinctions it can't collapse (real vs. useful, kind-hood vs. membership in a kind, whether kinds come in degrees or are binary), and the facts from actual science it's not allowed to contradict, like species being historical entities and categories being messy at the edges.

For each requirement give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which ones are genuinely non-negotiable vs. which are just the hobbyhorse of one camp. The part I care most about: tell me if some pair of plausible desiderata can't be jointly satisfied -- like if demanding that kinds ground induction fights with demanding that biology gets real kinds, or whatever the actual fault lines are -- because a requirements list that no theory can pass is itself a story. Then briefly, and I mean briefly, score the main contenders against the list: microstructural essentialism, the homeostatic property cluster picture, promiscuous/pluralist realism, and flat-out eliminativism. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than the scorecard; I can do the scoring color-coding myself. Also, the feature is for a general audience, so wherever a desideratum only makes sense if you already did a philosophy PhD, say so -- that's useful information for me too, since it tells me which debates are really about nature and which are about the philosphy seminar room.
