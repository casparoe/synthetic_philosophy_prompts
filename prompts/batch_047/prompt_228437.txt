Hosting a philosophy-of-film night with some friends and we're doing Everything Everywhere All at Once. I want to read it through Lewis vs. Kripke on cross-world identity. The setup honestly looks designed for counterpart theory: concrete branching worlds, and verse-jumping is basically a dramatization of Lewis's semantics -- "Evelyn could have been a kung fu master" is true because some concrete other-Evelyn is one, and our Evelyn can literally borrow what that counterpart has. But the emotional payoffs seem to need transworld identity: the laundry-and-taxes moment in the movie-star universe only lands if that man is Waymond, and on Lewis the strict metaphysical truth is he's a similar stranger, which makes the scene basically the Humphrey objection with better lighting. Does the film have an answer to that objection -- does Evelyn's arc show that we can care about counterparts the way we care about our own future selves, which is roughly what Lewis always said in reply? Two test cases I'd want a reading to handle honestly rather than skip: the rock universe, where the supposed counterparts share almost no similarity with Evelyn and Joy at all (is the film exploiting the context-sensitivity of counterpart relations, or just cheating for the bit?), and Jobu Tupaki experiencing every universe at once, which as far as I can tell neither theory has any room for. Tell me which side the film ultimately comes down on, or whether its productively inconsistent, and what it adds to the debate that the journal articles don't.
