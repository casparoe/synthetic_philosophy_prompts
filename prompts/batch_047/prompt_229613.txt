I serve on the allocation committee of a mid-sized donor-advised fund, and at our last meeting we got into an argument that has stuck with me. We had been informally using the standard importance-tractability-neglectedness framework to compare causes, and I realized that my actual verdicts on the cases in front of us did not line up with the scores I had assigned. I want to know whether there is a coherent principle behind my judgments, or whether I am simply inconsistent. Here are the verdicts, with my rough scores. First, malaria prevention: very high importance, very high tractability, moderate neglectedness — fund it without hesitation. Second, research into catastrophic risks from advanced AI: astronomically high importance by my lights, low tractability, quite neglected — fund it, and this is the verdict I am most confident about. Third, interventions aimed at wild animal suffering: even more astronomical importance, almost total neglectedness, extremely low tractability — I do not fund it, even though the ITN profile looks structurally identical to the AI case, just more extreme on every dimension. Fourth, a well-run local animal shelter: low importance, high tractability, not neglected — do not fund, easy call. Fifth, a global health charity that was underfunded three years ago but has since attracted large inflows from major funders: I still fund it, but at a reduced level, and the reduction tracks the decline in neglectedness rather than any change in the underlying problem. Sixth, and this is the case that provoked the argument: a speculative pandemic-preparedness intervention whose cost-effectiveness estimate, on paper, beats everything else on our list by two orders of magnitude, but where I know the entire estimate rests on one analyst's model with parameters nobody else has checked. I refused to fund it, and my colleague pointed out that its ITN scores were excellent.

So here is what I want from you. Is there a single principle, or a small set of them, that delivers all six verdicts? Candidates I have considered and found wanting: pure expected-value maximization seems to force me to fund the wild animal interventions and the unaudited pandemic estimate, so it overturns my two most interesting verdicts; a flat credibility discount on speculative estimates saves those two but I cannot see how to state it without also discounting the AI risk work, which is speculative in a similar way; and treating neglectedness as merely instrumental — evidence about marginal rather than total impact — handles case five nicely but feels like it abandons the framework rather than interpreting it. If you can state a principle precisely enough that I can see which clause each of my cases motivates, please do, and tell me which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, since I assume at least one has to go. I would also like to know what your best candidate predicts for two cases I have not decided: a cause where tractability is high but only because the measurable part of the problem is small, and a cause where the importance ranking between two options flips depending on which of two plausible moral views I hold. Please do not just re-describe the ITN framework back to me; I know what it says. I want to know what my verdicts say.
