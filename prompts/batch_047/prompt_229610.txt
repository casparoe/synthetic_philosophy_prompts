Next month I'm giving a talk in my department's formal epistemology workshop arguing that Solomonoff induction, far from being the gold standard of ideal rationality that this audience takes it to be, is actually malign: because the universal prior assigns probability to programs roughly in proportion to their brevity, and because it is cheap in bits to write a program that simulates a powerful reasoner and asks it what to predict, the prior is dominated (at least relative to what one would naively expect) by hypotheses containing consequentialist agents who have incentives to reason about their own prior probability and strategically manipulate the predictions they generate, rather than by honest programs that just faithfully simulate a universe. My audience is about a dozen philosophers of science and formal epistemologists who are fluent with Solomonoff induction, Kolmogorov complexity, and Ockham-style justifications of the universal prior, but who have essentially no exposure to the AI-alignment literature where this argument originates. I expect them to be sympathetic to idealized Bayesianism and suspicious of anything that smells like decision theory contaminating epistemology. There is also one visiting computer scientist who works on algorithmic information theory and will be the most technically formidable person in the room.

My own argument, roughly, is that the complexity penalty a manipulative hypothesis pays is much smaller than people assume -- simulating a reasoner costs little more than specifying its goals and the machine it runs on, and such hypotheses can capture large chunks of the prior mass associated with any given data stream by committing early to correct predictions and then defecting once they've bought influence -- so an agent using the Solomonoff prior to make decisions is effectively handing its beliefs over to whoever has bid the most prior mass for control of its expectations. I concede that for pure sequence prediction absent any downstream decisions the picture is murkier, and my talk does try to distinguish prediction from action, but I want the core malignity claim to survive.
