Two philosophers of physics in my department — a psi-ontic realist who holds that the quantum state represents physical reality, and a psi-epistemic theorist who holds that it represents an agent's information or degrees of belief about an underlying ontic state — have been arguing past each other for years, mostly trading slogans ("collapse is physical!" vs. "collapse is just updating!"). Draft the joint statement they should produce if forced into an adversarial collaboration.

The statement should include: (1) a section of claims both can sign — about what the formalism of quantum mechanics does and does not settle, what the PBR theorem and related no-go results actually assume (e.g., preparation independence) and therefore do and do not rule out, and why the existence of the quantum Bayesian or statistical-interpretation traditions does not by itself refute psi-ontology; (2) a section stating the residual disagreements in wording each party would accept for its own position — including whether the wavefunction's apparent collapse and the exponential growth of entangled states are problems for the epistemic view, and whether interference phenomena and the impossibility of distinguishing non-orthogonal states are problems for the ontic view; (3) a list of cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — and for each crux, an argument, thought experiment, or conceivable empirical or formal result that both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction or concession recorded in advance. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal (for instance, disagreement over what "represents reality" requires), the statement should say so explicitly and identify which part.
