I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent longtermist writing for years, and one argument that keeps resurfacing in my corner of the community is the dispute between people who think extinction risk should dominate longtermist prioritization and people who think suffering risks -- futures that contain astronomical amounts of intense suffering rather than no future at all -- deserve comparable or greater attention. I've watched this debate cycle through the same moves many times: one side invokes the sheer scale of lost value if humanity goes extinct, the other side counters that some futures are worse than no future, someone brings up population ethics, someone else brings up cluelessness about whether our interventions backfire, and the threads usually end with both camps agreeing the other has a point and then changing nothing about their actual prioritization. I'd like to see what this debate looks like when it's forced into the discipline of an adversarial collaboration.

Please draft the joint statement that a committed x-risk prioritizer and a committed s-risk prioritizer could both sign after an extended good-faith collaboration. I'd like: (1) a numbered list of claims both can endorse -- these should be substantive rather than diplomatic mush, and I'd expect them to include agreements about the astronomical stakes of the future, about whether "worse than extinction" outcomes are coherent, about the dependence of the dispute on population axiology, and about the ways s-risk reduction and x-risk reduction can either reinforce or trade off against each other in practice; (2) a disagreements table where each remaining disagreement is stated in neutral wording that each side would accept as a fair characterization of its own position -- I suspect the real fault lines include whether extreme suffering can outweigh vast amounts of positive welfare, how much weight to give suffering-focused moral views under moral uncertainty, and whether s-risk-targeted interventions are tractable at all or mostly risk backfiring; and (3) for each disagreement, the crux: the sub-question whose resolution would actually move at least one side, plus the argument, observation, or class of evidence both parties would accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction stated in advance. Where you find that part of the dispute is verbal -- for instance, both camps sometimes mean different things by "worse than extinction" depending on their implicit population ethics -- please say so explicitly and identify which part.

I'm more interested in the precision of the residual disagreements than in a verdict, so please resist adjudicating who is right. At the end, include a short note on your own confidence: how confident are you that you've represented each camp's actual position rather than a caricature, and which single claim in your joint statement are you least sure both sides would genuinely sign -- and why that one?
