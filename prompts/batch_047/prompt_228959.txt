A woman with terminal pancreatic cancer, weeks to live, in constant pain, asks her doctor for the pills: fine, obviously. A 24-year-old with treatment-resistant depression who's tried everything for six years: not fine. A man with locked-in syndrome after a stroke, fully aware, who communicates by blinking that he wants to die: fine. An 82-year-old in okay health who says she's simply done, her friends are dead, she's tired: not fine, and I feel a bit bad about that one. A guy with early Alzheimers who signed a directive years ago asking to be euthanized once he no longer recognizes his kids, but who now, at that stage, seems genuinely content: not fine to follow the directive, even though the version of him who wrote it would be horrified. And here's the pair that messes me up: the pancreatic cancer patient could just refuse treatment or stop eating and I'd fully respect that, but somehow her asking the doctor for the pills feels different, and I can't say why since both end the same way.
