ok so bear with me for a sec. I've been reading rationalist blogs and the adjacent internet since like 2011, which means I've now seen the taxation-is-theft fight play out roughly five thousand times, in comment sections, on SSC open threads, on twitter, at one memorable house party. And it's the exact same arguement every time. Libertarian says: taxation is theft, it's taking your property by threat of force, if I did it to you it'd be robbery so it's robbery when the state does it. Response: "your property" already presupposes the whole legal-institutional apparatus, there's no morally loaded pretax distribution that the state then grabs a chunk of, the tax is part of what defines what was yours. Counter-response: no, there are natural pre-institutional entitlements, self-ownership, Lockean provisos, etc. And then everyone just runs in circles on that last step forever. Fifteen years, zero movement, and these are often smart people who agree on a ton of empirical stuff, so it's not obviously a facts disagreement.

My working hypothesis: this is a verbal disagreement wearing a substantive costume. "Theft" just means "unjustified taking," so the entire fight is really about whether the property entitlements are justified, and the word "theft" does no work except raising everyone's blood pressure. Dissolve the word, get a real debate about property. But I suspect that's too easy, because when people are being careful the fight just relocates to whether moral entitlements can be pre-institutional at all, and that has the feel of something no argument can settle — like both sides are standing on bedrock intuitions and kicking.

So what I actually want is a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this, really? Verbal? A substantive dispute about the metaphysics of property? Two groups answering different questions under one label (one about what the state may permissibly do, one about what my payslip morally means)? Diagnose it using how each side actually argues when they're being careful rather than dunking. And given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like — a distinction that dissolves part of it, an argument that settles a sub-question, or should I just accept this is bedrock-intuition combat and the thread will outlive us all? Hard constraint: do NOT relitigate the first-order question, I don't need your take on whether taxation is theft. I want the map of the territory-war, not another soldier.
