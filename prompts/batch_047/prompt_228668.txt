I am a third-year PhD student in philosophy, coming to metaphysics from philosophy of physics, and I need to settle on a dissertation cluster within the literature on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality. My committee has imposed one unusual constraint, which I have come to think is the genuinely interesting one: at least half the dissertation must consist of questions that pass what I call the export test. A question passes the export test if it can be posed, and its answer would still matter, to someone who rejects the grounding idiom entirely — someone who thinks that talk of what grounds what is at best a notational convenience over fine-grained ideology and at worst confused. So, for instance, a question about the formal profile of grounding itself (whether it is transitive, whether it is monotonic, whether grounding facts are themselves grounded and what regresses that generates) presumably fails the test unless it can be repackaged as a question about explanation or structure that the skeptic still needs answered, whereas questions about whether fundamental physics supplies a minimal complete base for everything else, or whether an infinite descent of dependence relations is coherent, arguably survive translation intact.

With that constraint in mind: what do you take to be the main open questions in this area right now, and which two or three should I prioritize for roughly three years of work, given that I am strong on formal methods and physics but thin on the history of metaphysics? I am less interested in the list than in the ordering and its defense. Please make your criteria explicit: I care about which questions are upstream of others (in the sense that their answers constrain what can be said elsewhere), which are tractable with my toolkit rather than requiring close engagement with scholastic or phenomenological traditions, which pass the export test and why, and which are comparatively neglected. Where these criteria pull apart, tell me how you weigh them against each other. I would also like you to name one or two currently fashionable questions that you think I should avoid, and to say whether they fail on exportability, on tractability, or because settling them would not constrain anything downstream. Finally, tell me what would change your ranking: what result or development over the next few years, whether from within metaphysics or from physics, would move a question up or down your list, and in which direction? What I want is a defended agenda, not a survey of the field — and no bibliography.
