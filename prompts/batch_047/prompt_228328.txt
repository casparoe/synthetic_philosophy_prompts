so a couple months ago I had to decide whether to visit my dad while he's on chemo after getting a kind of ambiguous negative result on a home test, and it got me thinking about whether I actually *knew* I wasn't sick or just had decent evidence. I fell down a rabbit hole on pragmatic encroachment and contextualism and now I've sketched out where I've landed. before I embarrass myself at our reading group, can you tell me if this hangs together:

1. In the classic bank cases, the person with a lot riding on it doesn't know the bank will be open saturday, even though someone with identical evidence and nothing at stake does know. Whether you know genuinely depends on your practical situation, not just your evidence.

2. You should only treat something as a reason for acting if you know it. So the high-stakes guy has to go double-check, full stop.

3. But also: sometimes it's perfectly fine to just act on a really well-supported belief without checking further, even when you wouldn't quite say you know. If I'm 99% sure and checking costs me an hour, sometimes the rational move is to just go.

4. Whether a sentence like "she knows the test was negative" is true depends on the standards in play in the conversation of whoever's saying it. My mom in her low-stakes context can truly say of me "he knew he wasn't contagious" while in my context, standing outside dad's hospital room, "I knew" would have been false.

5. Testimony transfers knowledge: if my doctor knows the result and tells me, and I take her word for it, I now know it too, even if her situation is way lower-stakes than mine.

6. I'm not a skeptic. Ordinary people know tons of ordinary stuff, and any view that ends with "actually nobody knows anything" has gone wrong somewhere.

Which of these are actually in tension and which just look like they are? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest fix. And if there's one single revision that would clean up the most, tell me which it is -- I genuinely don't know where the problem is, if there is one.
