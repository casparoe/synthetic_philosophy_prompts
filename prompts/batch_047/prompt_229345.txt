I am preparing a position statement for my doctoral qualifying exams in epistemology, and before I circulate it to my committee I would like a consistency audit of the view it describes. Here is what I currently hold about the analysis of knowledge:

(a) The original Gettier cases refute the justified-true-belief analysis, and the failure is structural: no supplement of a further condition on the same template — no false lemmas, no undefeated defeaters, appropriate causal connection, and so on — yields non-circular necessary and sufficient conditions for knowledge.

(b) Justification is a matter of reliable production: a belief is justified if and only if it is produced by a cognitive process that maintains a suitably high truth ratio in the circumstances in which it actually operates.

(c) Knowledge excludes veritic luck: a subject knows p only if her belief that p is safe — that is, given the method she actually used, she could not easily have been wrong about p.

(d) In the fake barn county case, Henry, who happens to look at the one real barn in a district full of facades, knows that he is looking at a barn. Ordinary unaided vision is a reliable process, his belief is true and justified, and nothing further is required.

(e) Fallibilism is true: a subject can know p on the basis of evidence that does not entail p.

(f) Knowledge is the norm of assertion: one is in a position to assert p outright only if one knows p.

Please audit this set rather than summarize it. Identify which pairs (or triads) of commitments are in genuine tension, state precisely what the conflict is, and rank the tensions by severity; for each, say what would have to give. Where two commitments might appear to conflict but do not, explain briefly why the appearance dissolves rather than counting it as a cost. In particular, check whether the verdict in (d) is actually delivered by the principle in (b), or whether extracting it requires an assumption I have not stated — and whether that assumption is one (c) permits me to make. Then identify the single revision that restores consistency at the lowest overall cost, and say explicitly what that revision forfeits. Conclude with a final line of the form \answer{<letter>} naming the one commitment that should be revised or withdrawn first.
