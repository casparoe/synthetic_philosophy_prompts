I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy and in about six weeks I'm giving a talk in our department's formal epistemology series defending a position on the Sleeping Beauty problem that my advisor keeps warning me is going to get me in trouble. The view, roughly, is that Beauty should be a halfer, and that every serious thirder argument — the Dutch book considerations, the long-run betting frequency arguments, all of that — only gets going because it quietly assumes an agent's credences are pinned to her betting dispositions. My claim is that the memory-erasure built into the case severs exactly that link: Beauty on Monday can't use her credence to guide her bets the way a normal agent can, because she knows any bet she accepts may be offered to her again (or already has been) in a state subjectively indistinguishable from her current one, so her betting behavior floats free of her credence. Once you see that, the thirder arguments lose their grip and what's left standing is the halfer thought that she learns nothing new when she wakes up. I've been calling it the two-currency view: credence and betting quotients are different currencies, and Sleeping Beauty is a case where the exchange rate breaks down. A friend in the cog sci program keeps telling me the whole puzzle is just about how you define probability, which I think is way too quick, but it has made me paranoid that someone in the room will try a version of that move.

The audience is the part I'm actually worried about. Two of the faculty have published on self-locating belief and are committed thirders, and one of them I happen to know thinks the double-halfer literature is a cautionary tale. There's also a decision theorist who takes a hard functionalist line on credence — if it doesn't guide action, it isn't a credence — which cuts right against my whole strategy, and a philosopher of probability who I'm fairly sure thinks the case is too underdescribed to have a determinate answer and might just announce that in Q&A. Plus the usual grad students, who will ask whatever makes them sound clever.
