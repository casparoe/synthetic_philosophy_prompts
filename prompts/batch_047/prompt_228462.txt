Software dev here, no philosophy training, trying to figure out when I'm actually being weak-willed vs just... changing my mind like a reasonable person. My gut verdicts:

1. Installing a website blocker at night so I can't browse Reddit during work the next day, then at 2pm trying to find a workaround: the workaround attempt is weakness, installing it was smart.
2. Telling myself I'll get up at 6 to run, then hitting snooze at 6: weakness, clearly.
3. Planning to finish a side project this weekend, then a friend invites me hiking and I ditch the project: not weakness, plans can change, that's just life?
4. Agreeing to a deadline estimate I know is too aggressive because saying no in the meeting felt awkward, then missing it: not really weakness, more like a social failure?
5. Buying a year gym membership in January as a "commitment device," never going after March: the buying was fine, but this feels different from case 1 somehow and I cant articulate why.
6. Deciding to stop drinking coffee after 2pm for sleep reasons, then having one at 4pm on a day I'm exhausted and need to push a deploy: honestly feels justified, not weak, even though structurally it's identical to the snooze case.

The pair that bugs me is 2 vs 6 -- same shape (evening-me makes a rule, tired-me breaks it), opposite verdicts. Is there a principle that captures all of these, or do I have to give one up? And does that "hyperbolic discounting" thing I've read about actually explain the pattern, or does it explain too much and make every preference flip look rational?
