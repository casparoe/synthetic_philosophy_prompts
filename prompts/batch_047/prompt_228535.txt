I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I read ethics on the side, and lately I've been thinking about credit assignment -- in reinforcement learning we obsess over whether the agent got the reward for the right reason rather than by accident, and it occurred to me that my own intuitive views about moral credit might not survive the same scrutiny. I'm putting together a short write-up of my positions for a reading group, and before I circulate it I want a consistency check. Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) An action has moral worth only if the agent performed it for the right reasons -- it's not enough that the action was in fact right or that the agent happened to want to do it. (2) When a friend visits me in the hospital out of spontaneous affection, without any thought of duty or obligation, that visit is morally worthy, and I'd flatly reject the claim that a reluctant visitor who came purely from duty deserves more credit. (3) Moral worth should not depend on factors outside the agent's control -- not on whether the visit actually cheered me up, and not on whether the agent happened to be raised in a way that gave them good values in the first place. (4) What confers worth on an action is the agent's sensitivity to the actual moral reasons at stake -- their grasp of why the action is right, as opposed to merely happening to desire the right outcome. (5) A person of settled, habitual virtue who helps others without any internal struggle is morally superior to someone who has to wrestle down selfish impulses before doing the same thing.

What I'd like is an audit of this set, not a lecture on the literature. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, and order the tensions by severity -- I'm sure some pairs only look like they conflict and dissolve once you're careful about what "the right reasons" requires, so please distinguish those explicitly rather than flagging everything. For each genuine tension, identify exactly what commits me to what, and what the cheapest available fix would be and what it costs me. Finally, if you had to pick a single revision that restores the most coherence while sacrificing the least of what I obviously care about, which would it be? I'm not attached to any particular one of the five, so don't assume you know which one I'd rather keep.
