I'm a novelist, mostly literary fiction but right now I'm deep in research for a book set during a cold-war-style standoff between two nuclear powers, and I keep running into the same weird dynamic in everything I read and I don't have a clean word for it. Here's the shape of it. Both sides, privately, desperately want to avoid the big war. Nobody is a maniac. But each side keeps taking these little steps -- moving submarines closer, running a test, putting some unit on higher alert -- and each step, taken on its own, is small and totally defensible, because the alternative (doing nothing after the other guy's last step) reads as weakness, and showing weakness is supposed to be the actually dangerous thing. And here's the part that really gets me: the steps only *work* as threats because everyone involved knows that at some point control gets wobbly. Like, the threat isn't "I will choose to end the world," it's "at this tempo, something might slip -- a radar glitch, some lieutenant reading an order wrong -- and then we're all dead, so you'd better back off first." The danger is doing the persuading. The accident is load-bearing, if that makes sense.

So my question: does this specific configuration have a name? I know the word brinkmanship gets thrown around, but from what I can tell that usually means one guy steering toward the edge on purpose to scare the other, and that's not quite it -- in my scenario neither side really chose the situation, they both kind of backed into it one reasonable step at a time, and each would genuinely prefer the machine be switched off, but switching it off unilaterally is the one move they can't make. I've also seen people talk about it like a game of chicken, but in chicken the danger is just that both drivers are stubborn; the steering wheels work fine. In my thing the cars are *mechanically unreliable on purpose* and that's the whole strategy, which seems like a different animal.

What I want isn't just a label I can drop into a sentence, it's the carving. Is this a known subtype of something bigger, or a genuinely distinct thing that the usual terms don't quite cover? How is it different from (a) one-sided brinkmanship, (b) a pure accidental-war scenario where nobody is using the risk as leverage, and (c) a commitment device, which I half-understand to be when you tie your own hands on purpose? If the closest real concept only fits imperfectly, tell me where it fits and where it strains. And if nobody has cleanly named the both-sides-stumbled-into-it, accident-as-message version, feel free to coin something -- I'll steal it for the book, with gratitude and definately no royalties.
