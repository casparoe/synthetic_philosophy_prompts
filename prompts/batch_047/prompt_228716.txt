Standard discussions of the Liar treat it as a pathology of static sentences, but the case I actually care about is a system whose truth predicate shows up *inside* the claims the system itself generates and acts on — e.g. an agent checking statements like "my next output will be true" before deciding what to say or do. So: please sort the main families of responses to the Liar into tiers by how well they'd serve as the working truth-predicate for a system doing live self-reference like that, not just how elegantly they dissolve the classic paradox. The candidates: Tarski-style hierarchical/typed truth, Kripke's fixed-point approach, revision theory (Gupta/Belnap), dialetheist/paraconsistent approaches, contextualism about "true", and deflationism. Give each a tier and a sentence or two on why it lands there — in particular, does it let the system keep something close to naive T-schema reasoning in day-to-day operation, or does it force crippling gaps, hierarchies, or tolerated contradictions at exactly the wrong moments? If you think a hybrid beats all of them, say so and rank it too. British English please, and don't worry about citing anyone, I just want the actual assesment.
