Give me your actual credence in the following claim about consciousness, with the reasoning decomposed rather than a tour of both sides: if the meta-problem were fully solved, the hard problem would largely dissolve.

To make the claim precise, imagine a completed cognitive theory that passes what I'll call the counterfactual invariance test. It explains, in purely physical and functional terms, not only our verbal reports of consciousness but also our private judgments, philosophical intuitions, sense of obviousness, disagreements about zombies, and felt resistance to deflationary theories. And it predicts that all of these would remain basically unchanged in the nearest possible world with the same cognitive machinery but no phenomenal consciousness. If such a theory were established, what is the probability that the remaining hard problem becomes mostly a ordinary scientific question about which physical or functional states are accompanied by consciousness, rather than a distinct explanatory-gap problem?

Please give:

1. your final credence as a percentage;
2. separate rough credences for the key subclaims it depends on, such as whether the counterfactual invariance test is coherent, whether any plausible theory could pass it, and whether passing it would explain away the intuition rather than merely predict that we have it;
3. how those subclaims combine into the final number;
4. the subclaim your answer is most sensitive too;
5. what argument or evidence would move your credence upward or downward by at least twenty points.

Don't substitute a survey of illusionism versus phenomenal realism, and don't hide behind it depends what you mean by dissolve. Use my definition above, say where it might be underspecified, and then commit to a number anyway.
