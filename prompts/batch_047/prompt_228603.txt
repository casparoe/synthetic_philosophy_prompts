In a philosophy of physics seminar, our instructor concluded a discussion of the hole argument with the slogan “spacetime points have no identity.” She used it to reject the suggestion that a diffeomorphic rearrangement of matter inside an otherwise empty region represents a distinct physical possibility: if points are not independently identifiable, she said, the allegedly different arrangements are merely different presentations of the same possibility.

Treat the slogan as an ambiguous thesis rather than as an explanation. Distinguish the precise claims it might be making—for example, claims about identity across mathematical models, identity across possible worlds, the observability of which point an event occupies, individuation by spatiotemporal relations, haecceitistic differences, or the contrast between a change of coordinates and a permutation of independently existing points. For each interpretation, explain its implications for spacetime substantivalism, gauge redundancy, determinism in generally covariant theories, and the hole argument itself.

Conclude by identifying which interpretation, if any, actually supports the instructor’s dismissal and which merely redescribe the problem, presuppose relationalism, or conflate mathematical representational freedom with physical equivalence. Organize the answer as a numbered menu of readings, with a clear verdict on each.
