So I recently got voluntold onto the "responsible AI" working group at my job (we build a chatbot used by a big insurance company's customers) and within one meeting it became obvious that when people say an assistant "shouldn't do harmful things," they mean at least five different things by it, and nobody can tell me which ones they actually hold. It got personal last week when my sister asked me, half-joking, whether our bot would help her write a breakup text, and I realised I had no idea what the principled answer is or even what the question turns on.

So: build me a decision tree for the question "when should an AI assistant refuse, deflect, or override what a user is asking for?" I want the tree to sort *me*, not to recite policy.

Requirements:

- Start from a neutral node like "a user has asked the assistant to do X" and branch from there. Each branch should be labeled with a question I can answer about a concrete request without already knowing which position it leads to. Good candidates for splitting questions might be things like: does the harm fall on the requester or on a third party? would the user plausibly endorse this request on calm reflection, or is it a momentary impulse? is the action reversible? is the assistant refusing outright, or quietly steering (answering a slightly different question, adding friction, moralising)? does refusal require the assistant to be confident it's right about what's good for the user? You can use some of these and add your own, but every split has to be a genuine fork where reasonable people diverge, not a rhetorical question.
- Aim for somewhere between 8 and 12 leaves. Name each leaf (make up a memorable name if the position has no standard one) and give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Include at minimum: a hard anti-paternalist leaf (assistants may protect third parties but never protect adults from themselves), a leaf where the assistant defers to the user's settled values over their in-the-moment requests, and a leaf where transparent paternalism is fine but covert steering never is. If the natural structure of the tree produces those, great; if not, say why.
- Note any combination of answers that's logically available but empty, and say briefly why: is it incoherent, or just a position nobody wants to defend in public?
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate in practice, i.e. positions that would handle every realistic request identically even though they disagree in theory.
- Here's where I want commitment, not a menu: at the end, tell me which single leaf *you* would operate under if you were our bot, give the actual reason (not "it balances competing concerns"), and then pick one everyday request -- not bioweapons, not self-harm, something mundane like the breakup text, or "help me write a glassdoor review trashing my old boss," or "summarize my friend's private journal so I don't have to read it" -- where following your leaf makes you do something a reasonable user would be annoyed or unnerved by. If you can't find such a case, that's a sign your leaf is vacuous, so try again.

Don't structure it around famous philosophers or named theories unless one genuinely earns its place -- I'd rather the tree be built from the questions than from a textbook's table of contents. ASCII or indented outline format is fine, whatever's clearest.
