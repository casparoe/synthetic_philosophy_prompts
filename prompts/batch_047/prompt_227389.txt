I am preparing a handout for my undergraduate seminar in political philosophy on the franchise, and I want a decision tree that takes a student from the starting commitment that a democratic government should be accountable to the people to a named position on who may vote and whether they must vote. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of authors: first the diagnostic questions, then the positions occupying each combination of answers. I need four to five branch nodes and six to eight leaves. Each node must be phrased as a question a student could answer without knowing where it leads, and at least two nodes must be anchored in a concrete case (a seventeen-year-old who pays income tax, a non-citizen who has resided in the country for a decade, the analogy between voting and jury service). Crucially, the tree must treat the extent of the franchise and the obligation to exercise it as separate axes, so that compulsory voting for citizens only and voluntary voting for all residents do not get forced onto the same branch. The leaves must include: the liberal status quo (adult citizens, voluntary), compulsory voting on the jury-duty model, residence-based enfranchisement of non-citizens, a lowered voting age defended on the ground that age is only a proxy for competence, epistocracy, and at least one logically available but largely undefended combination (say, mandatory voting for non-citizen residents), with a sentence diagnosing whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Attach one sentence to each leaf stating its most awkward commitment, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and write the handout in American English.
