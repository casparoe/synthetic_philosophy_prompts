I teach an undergrad political philosophy course, seminar-style, about 16 students, and I have one 75-minute session for the liberty unit before we read anything on republicanism. Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the difference between negative and republican freedom in their gut -- specifically the claim that you can be unfree even when nobody ever interferes with you, just because someone *could* interfere at will and you know it. So the setup should put some participants under another player's unchecked discretionary power while making sure that power barely gets used, so what they experience is non-interference under domination, not actual meddling. Give me the rules, roles, materials, and how to keep the power-holder restrained without just telling them to be nice (some incentive structure?), plus a timeline. Then a debrief that maps specific moments onto the interference vs. arbitrary-power distinction and onto stuff like having to watch what you say around the powerful person. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (I'm guessing students just say "that's unfair" and miss that nothing actually happened to them, or the powerful player goes rogue in round one and ruins it), and be honest about what the activity can't reproduce -- like that real domination isn't a game you get to walk out of after an hour.
