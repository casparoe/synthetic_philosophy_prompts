I've come to hold the following position: program equilibrium results (in the style of Tennenholtz's construction, and the later literature on agents that condition their behavior on proofs about each other's source code) genuinely dissolve the one-shot prisoner's dilemma for idealized agents. That is, once players can inspect each other's code, mutual cooperation becomes the rational outcome in a way that is not merely the old cooperative equilibrium of a repeated or reputational game wearing new clothes. What compels me is the self-auditing structure: each agent's program verifiably cooperates exactly with programs that verifiably cooperate back, so defection becomes provably self-undermining in a way no commitment device available in the standard game can achieve. The critics I encounter say either that this trivializes (it is just a Nash equilibrium of a metagame, and mutual defection remains one too, so nothing has been dissolved) or that it fails to generalize (real agents cannot read each other's source, the construction leans on a shared proof system, and it is brittle in precisely the ways Löb's theorem predicts).

Tell me what would count against my position. In particular:

1. Which formal results or possible constructions would most damage it? For instance: a demonstration that for every cooperative program equilibrium there is a payoff-equivalent defection-flavored one, so the construction selects nothing; a fragility result showing cooperation evaporates under any relaxation of perfect mutual verification or unbounded proof search; or a reduction showing the whole apparatus collapses into an ordinary pre-game commitment stage that standard game theory already handles.
2. Which empirical or engineering developments would count? For instance, agent systems that can verifiably inspect one another's code and yet systematically fail to cooperate, or formal-verification practice showing the required self-referential proofs are infeasible at any realistic scale.
3. Which objections would the people who actually work on this concede as real limitations, and which would they dismiss as missing the point? I want the concession map from the inside, not a critic's caricature.
4. Is there a weak version of the position that is trivially safe, and has the discussion quietly retreated to it? I want an honest verdict on whether the strong claim risks anything at all, or has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if so, whether that matters for a claim of this kind.
