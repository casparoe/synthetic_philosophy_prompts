so i'm a software engineer at a regional hospital network, no philosophy training at all, and i'm on the team that maintains our patient triage and scheduling tool. anytime we want to change how it trades things off (who gets bumped when a surgical slot opens, how ambulances get routed between wards, etc) we have to run it past an internal ethics comittee -- two doctors, a lawyer, and a patient advocate -- and after two years of watching them rule on stuff i honestly cannot figure out what rule they're applying. this matters to me because i have to actually encode whatever their principle is into the constraint layer of the model, and vibes dont compile.

their verdicts so far: they approved ranking patients purely by predicted benefit of treatment even though it systematically deprioritizes older patients; they rejected a feature that would slightly fudge the displayed wait-time estimates to spread patients more evenly across wards, even though the model showed it improved overall outcomes; they rejected a mode that overrode patients' stated refusals when the model was very confident the treatment would help them; and they approved using likelihood-of-showing-up as a scheduling feature even though it deprioritizes patients who miss appointments for reasons totally outside their control, like transit or childcare.

now the new proposal: in a specific clinic, patients get an automated message telling them they have the next opening. the model found that bumping one of these patients for a higher-need arrival improves expected outcomes, and the bumped patient would be told openly why and rescheduled -- no deception, nobody's refusal is being overridden.

what do you think the committee says? more importantly: what are the two or three different rules that fit all four of their past verdicts but disagree on this one, which past case is the closest analog, and what principle are they committing to with each possible verdict? also what's the next hypothetical case that would most cleanly tell the candidate rules apart, since i'd rather ask them about that before i build the wrong constraint into teh system.
