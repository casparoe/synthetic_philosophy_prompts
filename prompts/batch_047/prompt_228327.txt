Here is my opening move, which I want you to answer directly. When chemists abandoned phlogiston, they did not thereby abandon the tools they used to argue. Your elimination is different. You wrote arguments intending that I understand them and, on their basis, come to believe eliminativism. But if your theory is true, there are no beliefs: there is nothing for me to acquire, no such state as my accepting your conclusion, and your project of persuading me is unintelligible on your own terms. So either eliminativism is true and no one can believe it, or people can believe things and it is false. The phlogiston analogy fails at exactly the point where you need it. What is wrong with this argument?
