i run a small reading group (mostly grad students, one very patient retired lawyer) and we've spent the fall on nietzsche's genealogy of morals. for the last meeting i made everyone read the preface, which people usually skip, and we got properly stuck on section 2, where nietzsche insists his ideas weren't a pile of disconnected hunches but grew out of each other from a single root. he writes: "Rather with the necessity with which a tree bears its fruit, so do our thoughts, our values, our Yes's and No's and If's and Whether's, grow connected and interrelated, mutual witnesses of one will, one health, one kingdom, one sun—as to whether they are to your taste, these fruits of ours?—But what matters that to the trees? What matters that to us, us the philosophers?" (this is the old public-domain Levy-edition translation on project gutenberg). the retired lawyer immediately pounced: if a thinker's values are fruits of one root, one physiology, one will, rather than answers to reasons or trackings of truth, then isn't the genealogy itself just another fruit, nietzsche's own symptom, no more entitled to our assent than the priest's? does the critique eat its own tail, or does he actually have a way out, and is "health" or "taste" doing any real work there or is it a dodge?

so here's what i want: a poem to read aloud at the end of that last session, written in terza rima, because i want the interlocking rhyme scheme to do the genealogical work formally, each tercet literally growing its opening rhyme out of the previous stanza's middle sound the way the fruit grows out of the root. maybe 7 to 9 tercets plus a single closing line. the poem should take the tree passage seriously as a claim about where thoughts come from, then turn on the self-swallowing problem: the poem's own speaker, praising or attacking the tree, is also hanging from a branch, and the final line should leave genuinely open whether thats a refutation or just what thinking is. stay in the imagery of roots, grafts, fruit, taste, ripeness, blight, soil, no name-dropping of nietzsche or the will to power or anything that sounds like a seminar paper. it should work as a poem for someone who's never read him, and feel exactly right to someone who has. definately no neat moral at the end. and write it in American English please.
