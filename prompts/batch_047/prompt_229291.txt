i'm writing a paper on responsibility gaps for autonomous systems and before i read one more proposed "solution" i want the spec first. write the desiderata: what does an account of responsibility for harms caused by highly autonomous learning systems actually have to deliver to count as solving the problem rather than renaming it?

some things i know i want on the list:

- it has to handle my running case and say something non-hand-wavy about it: a hospital's triage model gets retrained nightly on new patient data, and over a few months it quietly shifts how it weights one symptom. a patient who would've been flagged under the old version gets sent home and dies. nobody designed the change, the validation suite still passes, and the model outperforms human triage overall. so: who, if anyone, is responsible here, and does the account's answer feel like an answer or like a shrug
- the control and epistemic conditions: any real account has to confront the fact that no single person had both control and knowledge of the failure. it can't just weaken those conditions whenever convenient -- or if it does, it owes a story about why that isn't just laundering the gap through redefinition
- it has to distinguish the different gaps (backward-looking blame, forward-looking accountability, who-compensates-the-victim) instead of pretending that closing one closes all of them
- it can't lean on "the manufacturer should have foreseen it" in a way that smuggles back in foresight of the *specific* failure, since that just denies the premise that the system is autonomous
- it has to say whether the gap is a bug of current tech or a permanent feature as systems get more autonomous, and it has to be consistent with whatever it says about older automation (autopilots, algorithmic trading crashes) -- no special pleading for whatever happens to be newest

for each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a genuine requirement and not just my taste, and mark which ones are non-negotiable vs which are really the preferences of one camp (retributivists, engineers, insurers, liability lawyers, whoever).

then the part i actually care most about: tell me which subset of these desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. my hunch is that the list itself contains the gap -- that "someone must answer for serious harms", "responsibility requires control plus epistemic access", and "a machine can't be the someone" can't all hold at once for genuinely autonomous systems, so at least one has to go. if you agree, say which one you'd sacrifice and why; if you don't, show me the consistent set.

at the end, a brief note on how the main families of fixes do against the list (strict liability schemes, tracing back to deployers or procurers, assigning the system itself some kind of quasi-responsibility) -- but keep that short. the spec is the deliverable, not the scorecard.
