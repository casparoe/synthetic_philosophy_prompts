So I've been going down a rabbit hole on Thomas Hobbes lately (just wikipedia and podcasts, I have zero academic background, so bear with me). From what I gather, his whole thing in Leviathan is that life without a strong government is nasty brutish and short, and the way out is everyone handing over power to one sovereign who monopolizes force. Fine. But here's what's bugging me.

There's this popular argument going around that the world has actually gotten way LESS violent over the centuries -- fewer wars, fewer murders, people living longer than ever, all that. And I keep thinking: what would Hobbes make of that claim if someone could explain it to him today?

A few specific things I can't figure out on my own:

1. I read that for Hobbes, "war" doesn't just mean actual fighting -- it also includes long stretches where there's no battle but everyone knows both sides are ready and willing to fight. If that's right, wouldn't he say something like the Cold War was straight-up war, even though the US and USSR never directly went at it? And does that mean the whole "peaceful modern era" people brag about is, by his definition, still wartime, because of nuclear deterrence and everyone pointing missiles at each other? That seems to flip the whole "less violent" story on its head.

2. Does it even matter whether you count violence per person or in total? Like, WWII killed more people than basically anything, but there were also way more people alive. I feel like Hobbes might have an opinion on which measure counts, given his focus on each individual's fear of violent death, but I can't tell which way he'd go.

3. The tricky one: Hobbes says the sovereign's force is what SAVES us from violence. But modern states do a ton of violence themselves -- policing, prisons, executions, bombing other countries. Would he count that as violence at all, or as the thing that makes non-violence possible? And if he doesn't count it, is the "world is less violent" claim basically rigged in favor of whatever the state does?

So my question is: if Hobbes were around today and heard the pitch that violence has declined and we should be optimistic, what would he actually say? Would he see it as vindication (strong states did their job), as statistical trickery, or as naive peacetime complacency? Try to reason from his actual views rather than just guessing what a grumpy old philosopher would say.
