I'm trying to pin down what makes something we do together rather than just two things happening at the same time, so here are my gut verdicts and I want to know what theory they add up to. Two strangers both push the same stalled car at the same time without noticing each other -- not a joint action. Two people walking together, where if one suddenly speeds off the other could legitimatly say "hey, I thought we were walking together" -- joint, and the fact that the rebuke makes sense seems like the whole thing. A jazz duo improvising with no plan and no talking, just listening and reacting -- joint. A crowd that turns into a riot, people egging each other on and smashing windows, nobody aiming at anything shared -- not joint. A flash mob where the participants never communicate, they all just saw the same viral post and showed up -- joint, I think? And a committee that passes a resolution where every member voted for it purely out of self-interest -- the committee did it intentionally even though no individual cared about the resolution itself. Is there one principle that captures all of these? The jazz duo and the riot are what keep tripping me up, since both involve mutual responsiveness with no explicit agreement, and I can't cleanly say what distinguishes them without either letting the riot count or kicking the jazz duo out. If your principle has to overturn one of my verdicts, tell me which and why. Also, what does it predict about a case I didn't list -- a couple whose habits and finances have slowly converged over decades into a shared way of life that neither of them ever actually chose?
