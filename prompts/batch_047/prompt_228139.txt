A decade of reading rationalist blogs and forums has left me thoroughly marinated in the standard thought experiments on personal identity — teleporters, uploads, brain bisection, the usual furniture — and with a set of gut verdicts that I suspect do not cohere. I would like you to perform reflective equilibrium on them. I am asking from a broadly Buddhist direction: I take anattā seriously, and I suspect my confusion comes from treating "me" as tracking a metaphysical fact rather than a conventional designation, but I cannot see how to make the conversion cleanly.

My verdicts:

1. A teleporter that scans me, destroys the original, and rebuilds me atom-for-atom on Mars: I would step in without hesitation. The person who arrives is me.
2. The same machine, except the scan leaves the original intact, and the original is told he will be painlessly destroyed within the hour: the man on Mars is me, and the man left behind is about to die a real death. I recognise that 1 and 2 are in tension; that is rather the point.
3. Gradual replacement of my neurons by functionally identical chips over ten years: unambiguously still me throughout.
4. Sudden, total, irreversible amnesia, with personality and skills intact: still me, though a diminished me.
5. Corpus callosotomy producing two diverging streams of experience: I do not think the pre-operative person survives in either hemisphere alone, but I refuse to describe the operation as a death followed by two births. Something survives in both, and I cannot say what.
6. Cryonic revival after marginal preservation: probably me, conditional on enough structure surviving — yet this feels shakier than the teleporter, and I cannot justify the difference, since the teleporter also transmits nothing but information across a gap.
7. Deep meditative absorption in which the sense of self lapses entirely for an hour: no one died and no one was born during that hour; the question of identity simply did not arise. This verdict feels to me like the seed of the right account, but it sits oddly beside 1 and 2, where I am perfectly happy to speak of me arriving on Mars.

What I want from you:

- A principle — or at most two competing candidates — stated precisely, that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, with each clause of the principle tied explicitly to the case that motivates it.
- An honest statement of which verdict or verdicts it cannot accommodate. I expect 1 and 2 to be the casualties, or possibly 5; tell me which, and why, rather than rescuing everything with ad hoc machinery.
- The principle's verdicts on two cases I have not listed: (a) a reconstruction made from a scan taken ten years ago meets the present me, and both lay claim to the same friendships, debts, and property; (b) two successors with identical psychology at the moment of branching, one of whom is immediately informed that he is "the original" and behaves accordingly.
- Finally, a verdict on whether anattā actually does work here or merely renames the problem. If the Buddhist claim is that "is it me?" has no answer beyond conventional designation, does that dissolve the fission and teleporter puzzles, or does it simply relocate the question to which conventions we ought to adopt — and if the latter, what should govern that choice?

I am after the strongest principle you can construct, not a survey of named positions, so please keep the discussion general and argue for your preferred resolution rather than touring the literature.
