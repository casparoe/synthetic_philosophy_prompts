I spent forty years as an experimental chemist and in all that time it never once occured to me to worry that the ground might give way under the maths itself, so now that I'm retired and reading philosophy I want this thought through properly. Build me a thought experiment in which the mathematical community discovers that a standard axiom system (the usual set-theoretic foundations, say) is inconsistent. Constraints: the derivation of the contradiction should be too long for any one person to check by hand (computer-verified is fine), the inconsistency must sit in the axioms themselves rather than in some dodgy lemma people happen to lean on, and the case has to be engineered so that the rational response is neither "burn mathematics down and start again" nor "carry on exactly as before" -- I want the interesting middle forced, not just gestured at. After the scenario, do the check: why each extreme response fails in this case, which feature of the setup is doing the real work, and whether removing that feature collapses the thing back into one of the boring verdicts. Then say what a "carry on regardless" person would throw at it. And humour a Brit: answer in British English please.
