Consider the following proposition, which I'll try to state precisely enough to have a truth value: the end of Roman political authority in the Latin West between roughly 400 and 600 CE constitutes a genuine civilizational collapse -- meaning a large, durable decline in material living standards, institutional capacity, and the stock of transmissible knowledge for the median inhabitant of the former Western provinces -- rather than a transformation in which one institutional order was largely replaced by successors of comparable capability, with the losses concentrated among an elite stratum while continuity predominated elsewhere.

I hold this proposition at roughly 0.75, but I'm aware that my evidence is a pile of heuristics rather than a model, and I'd like to see the reasoning made explicit. Please give your own credence as a number, but the number is the least important part. What I want is the decomposition:

1. Identify the sub-questions your credence actually depends on. Candidates might include: what the archaeological record can and cannot tell us about median welfare (as opposed to elite consumption) across that interval; how much weight to give written sources that mostly report the experience of literate landholders; whether "collapse" should be evaluated against the counterfactual of continued imperial rule or against the actual trajectory of the East; whether population decline, where it occurred, was a cause, an effect, or a confound; and how much the answer varies by region, such that the proposition is really several propositions averaged together.

2. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question resolving in the direction that favors the collapse reading, and state how these combine into your final number -- multiplicatively, as a weighted sum, or in some other structure. If two sub-questions are correlated, say so rather than pretending independence.

3. Tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to: the one where a modest shift in your assessment would move the headline credence the most, and in which direction.

4. Finally, name the single piece of evidence or argument -- something that could in principle exist, whether or not it does -- that would move your credence the largest distance from where it now sits. If someone at 0.75 and someone at 0.30 disagree about this proposition, where does their disagreement most likely live in your decomposition?

I'd rather see an honest 0.5 with a well-specified dependency structure than a confident number resting on an unstated one. If at any point the decomposition forces you to conclude that the proposition as I've stated it is not well-defined -- for instance, because "collapse" fails to commute across regions or social strata -- say so explicitly, propose the nearest well-defined variant, and give your credence for that instead.
