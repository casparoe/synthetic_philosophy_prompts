Here are my verdicts.

Case 1. The standard experiment: a fair coin is flipped on Sunday; I am put to sleep; if heads, I am awakened once (Monday) and my memory of the awakening is erased before Tuesday; if tails, I am awakened twice (Monday and Tuesday) with memory erasure in between. On awakening, with no information about the day, my credence that the coin landed heads should be 1/3, not 1/2.

Case 2. Same experiment, but on awakening I am told it is Monday. My credence in heads should now rise to 2/3.

Case 3. Dr. Evil learns that a perfect duplicate of him has been created in a distant lab, and that a fair coin was flipped to decide which of the two -- original or duplicate -- will be mildly shocked. Dr. Evil's credence that he is the original stays at 1/2; the mere existence of the duplicate does not, in my view, make him more likely to be one or the other, and I see no shift at all from the duplication itself.

Case 4. A physicist is choosing between two cosmologies, empirically indistinguishable to us, one containing a trillion observers in our situation and one containing a trillion trillion. I flatly refuse the conclusion that she should be virtually certain of the larger universe on grounds of observer frequency. If that is where the reasoning leads, the reasoning is broken; the evidence of our senses is what it is, and the head-counting prior should not swamp it.

Case 5. Same physicist, different question: I accept that if she learns her theory predicts observers like us arise overwhelmingly at one point in cosmic history, then conditional on that theory, locating herself at that point is fine and unremarkable. Ordinary inference about where observers are concentrated within a single world strikes me as legitimate; it is the choice between worlds by observer count that I resist.

Case 6. I decline to assign any serious credence to the hypothesis that I am a short-lived brain fluctuating out of thermal chaos with fabricated memories, even if some cosmology predicts that such brains vastly outnumber ordinarily embodied observers like me. Whatever account of self-location I end up with, it had better deliver this verdict as a theorem rather than requiring an ad hoc patch.
