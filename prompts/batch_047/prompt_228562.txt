In discussions of prosaic AI alignment, I keep seeing the same standoff. The “behavior-first” people think scalable oversight, RLHF, adversarial testing, and careful deployment can align systems built from current methods without a new conceptual breakthrough. The “internals-first” people answer that this mostly produces compliant behavior while leaving the learned goals opaque, so a sufficiently capable system could remain aligned only until the incentives or distribution change. Both sides know that current evaluations are imperfect and that internal interpretability is hard, yet neither seems to budge.
