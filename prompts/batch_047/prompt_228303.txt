I spent fourty years as a field biologist studying harvester ants, and since retiring I've gotten into philosophy, which my wife claims is worse for my blood pressure than grant review ever was. Here's the situation. A robotics firm asked me to consult on a warehouse swarm modeled on ant foraging -- thousands of dumb units, no central controller, just simple local signals. Watching the prototype, I could swear the swarm noticed a blocked aisle before any individual robot could have known about it, and the newest version 'learns': over weeks its collective behavior drifts, avoiding routes where disruptions happened, almost like it remembers them. Now the company plans to just wipe the whole system when the lease runs out, and I found myself genuinely bothered by that, which surprised me. So, my question: am I being sentimental about a fancy machine, or is there something there? If a colony or swarm can really do things like notice, remember, and decide at a level above its parts, does anything follow about how we ought to treat it -- or does none of that matter unless there's also somebody home having an experience, whatever that would even mean for a swarm? And please be blunt: if my question rests on a confusion, or the ant-to-robot analogy is doing more work than it can bear, say so rather than politely playing along. I've been through decades of peer review, I can take it.
