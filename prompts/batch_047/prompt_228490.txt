I'm in a reading group on formal epistemology and last week we got completely stuck on a puzzle our organizer gave us, and two members ended up in a real argument about it. Here's the setup as it was given to us. A soldier, Judy, is dropped by parachute into a region split into two territories, Red and Blue, and each territory has a headquarters somewhere in it. She has no clue where she landed, so her credences are: 1/2 Red, 1/2 Blue, and within each territory a 1/4 chance she's near the HQ (so four equal cells of 1/4 each: Red+HQ, Red+not, Blue+HQ, Blue+not). She gets one radio message that tells her exactly one thing: if she's in Blue territory, the chance she's near HQ is 3/4. The question is whether her credence that she's in Red territory should move off of 1/2.

Marta said obviously not. The message is entirely about what things are like inside Blue territory -- it says nothing about Red at all. If you got a tip about the layout of one neighborhood in a city, your credence that you're in a different neighborhood shouldn't budge. She said any update rule that moves P(Red) here is thereby broken, and she sketched an alternative way of updating (she said you should change your beliefs as little as possible overall, not just jam the new conditional in) under which P(Red) stays exactly 1/2. Dev said she'd done the math wrong. His update: the message means the Blue+not cell shrinks by a factor of three relative to Blue+HQ, so the four cells become proportional to 1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/12, which renormalizes to 3/10, 3/10, 3/10, 1/10 -- so P(Red) rises to 3/5. Marta said that's a reductio of his method, not an answer, since it has Judy becoming more confident she's in Red for purely Blue-flavored reasons. Dev replied that there's no paradox, because the new conditional is correlated with Red in her overall distribution once you renormalize, and that her intuition is just what it feels like to resist the correct arithmetic. Then Marta said his arithmetic assumes a particular updating convention (she called it "conditioning on the conditional") that isn't sacred, and Dev said her convention lets you learn something and leave your marginals untouched in a way that would violate how you'd update if the same info arrived gradually.

I'm honestly torn and they asked me to settle it next session. Can you go through the dispute point by point -- is Dev's arithmetic right as far as it goes, is Marta right that a correct update can't move P(Red), does the answer depend on which update rule you adopt, and if so is there any principled way to pick one? Who's right about what, and is there something both of them are missing?
