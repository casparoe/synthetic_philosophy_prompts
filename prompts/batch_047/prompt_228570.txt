So I was rereading Einstein's little essay "Why Socialism?" (the one he wrote for the first issue of Monthly Review back in 1949) and there's a bit near the end I keep chewing on. He basically says: look, a planned economy is not yet socialism, and planning as such is perfectly compatible with the complete enslavement of the individual. The actually hard problems, on his telling, are socio-political, not technical — how do you keep the bureaucracy from becoming all-powerful once economic and political power are that centralized, and how do you protect the rights of the individual with some kind of democratic counterweight against it.

Now fast forward to today. There's a whole discourse (some of it online-lefty, some of it from genuinely serious economists and CS people) arguing that the old anti-planning objection has expired. The pitch goes: Mises and Hayek said no planning board could ever gather and process the information that prices aggregate, but that was before machine learning, realtime demand data, and firms like Amazon or Walmart, which are already internally planned economies the size of small countries and seem to work fine. So, the argument concludes, we could now run a democratically planned economy with algorithms doing the allocating, and it would beat markets on efficiency and equality both.

What I want from you: what do you think Einstein would say to this if he dropped into the debate? Don't just tell me whether he'd cheer or boo in general — I want to know whether, given that passage, he'd think the AI-planning pitch even touches his worry. He never says planning is technically impossible; he says it isn't socialism yet, and that the hard part is power. Does swapping Gosplan for a recommender system solve that, or does it just move the politburo somewhere harder to see? Would he find an algorithmic allocator more accountable than a ministry because its rules are inspectable code, or less accountable because nobody, including its designers, can actually say why it allocated what it allocated? And is there anything a planner would have to bolt on — transparency requirements, veto rights, decentralization, whatever — before he'd upgrade it from "planned economy" to "socialism" in his sense?

Take a definite stance on what his verdict would be, definately don't just split the difference, but make the reasoning track the actual text rather than a vibes-level "Einstein liked socialism."
