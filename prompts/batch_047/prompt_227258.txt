I'm writing up my views on animal ethics for a working group on global priorities, and before I circulate them I want a consistency audit. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Hedonic welfare — positively and negatively valenced experience — is the only thing of intrinsic moral value; everything else matters instrumentally.

(2) Insects are quite unlikely to be sentient. My credence is around 5% for bees and under 1% for fruit flies, on the grounds that sentience probably depends on particular kinds of integrated neural processing that insect brains may or may not instantiate.

(3) Because farmed and wild insect populations are astronomically large, expected-welfare reasoning makes insect welfare one of my top moral priorities, and I donate and advocate accordingly — including for interventions aimed at reducing wild insect suffering.

(4) I reject fanatical reasoning: a tiny probability of an enormous payoff cannot by itself settle a decision. That is why I don't pay Pascal's mugger, and why I give no weight to the possibility that my houseplant suffers.

(5) Behavioral evidence — flexible motivational trade-offs, self-protective behavior, learned avoidance — is the right kind of evidence for updating credences about sentience, for insects as for any other system.

(6) Under moral uncertainty, the right procedure is to maximize expected value across the normative theories in which I have nonzero credence.

Please audit this set rather than lecture me on insect minds. Begin your response with your verdict in the first sentence — whether these six commitments are jointly tenable — and then give the audit, with no preamble and no restating of my list. For each pair of commitments you take to be in tension, state the tension precisely, say how severe it is and why, and identify the cheapest revision that resolves it and what that revision costs. Distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones that dissolve on inspection, and rank the genuine ones by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most tensions at once. Do not assume I already know where the problem is, and do not treat the low credences in (2) and the high priority in (3) as automatically contradictory — that conjunction is exactly the position under audit.
