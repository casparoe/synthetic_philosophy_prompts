I am a graduate student in international relations, and I have followed my supervisor's public positions on military intervention for years. Her track record:

1. Kosovo, 1999 — she supported NATO's bombing campaign despite the absence of Security Council authorization, describing it at the time as unlawful but justified.
2. Iraq, 2003 — she opposed the invasion from the outset, arguing that the humanitarian case was retrospective: Saddam Hussein's worst crimes were years in the past, and there was no acute emergency in 2003 that war could halt.
3. Libya, 2011 — she supported the initial intervention to protect Benghazi under the Security Council's mandate, but publicly broke with the operation once it became a campaign for regime change, which she argued exceeded and discredited the mandate's logic.
4. Rwanda, 1994 — she maintains, retrospectively, that outside powers were obligated to intervene, authorization or not.
5. Syria, 2013 — she opposed punitive airstrikes after the chemical weapons attacks, on the grounds that the attacks were over and the proposed strikes would neither protect civilians nor remain limited.

A colleague and I are drafting a briefing note on a new case and want to predict where she will land. The Republic of Kessovia's army and allied militias have killed several thousand members of the Hadri minority and driven roughly 400,000 across the border; whether this amounts to genocide is contested. The Security Council is deadlocked by a certain veto. The regional organization has voted to intervene and can field a capable force. Military planners judge that protecting the border enclaves where most Hadri have fled is feasible at low cost, but that halting the killing throughout Kessovia would very likely require removing the regime — and the regime's patron, a nuclear-armed neighbor, has warned that regime change would bring it into the war.

Please do the following. First, identify at least two distinct decision rules that are each consistent with all five of her past verdicts, and state plainly what each rule predicts for both the limited enclave-protection operation and the expanded operation that would remove the regime. Second, give your best prediction of her verdict on each operation, and explain which rule you take her actually to be running and why her record favors it. Third, if you find that her past verdicts genuinely underdetermine either prediction, say which single feature of the Kessovia case is doing the most work. Finally, describe the further hypothetical case — one that could plausibly arise — that would best discriminate between the two leading candidate rules.
