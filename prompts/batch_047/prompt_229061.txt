I'm taking phil of mind this semester and we just did the extended mind unit (Otto and his notebook vs. Inga and her memory). My roommate, who's in the class with me, put together this argument and it seems to prove way too much, but neither of us can find the hole.

(1) A belief is just whatever plays the belief-role for you: information that guides your reasoning and your actions. Where it's physically stored doesn't matter.
(2) That role is played when the info is reliably there when you need it, you go to it without deliberating, and you automatically endorse what it tells you.
(3) Otto's notebook hits all three for the museum address, so the notebook entry is one of Otto's beliefs. (This is the extended mind bit and I'm totally fine with it.)
(4) Now take me and my phone: it's in my pocket all day (reliably available), I look things up without deliberating, and I just go with whatever comes up (automatic endorsement).
(5) So the stuff accessible through my phone is among my beliefs -- literally the contents of the internet, or at least everything it would pull up on any topic.
(6) Those contents contradict each other all over the place, and tons of it is stuff I'd flat-out deny if you asked me cold. So on this view I have massively contradictory beliefs and "believe" things I'd sincerely disavow. That cant be right.
(7) So something in (1)-(2) has to go -- but (3) was built entirely out of (1)-(2), so it looks like saving Otto costs the bloat, and killing the bloat costs Otto.

Which step do I actually reject? If I have to choose I'd rather keep (3) and lose (5), but I can't see what separates them, since everything I said about my phone seems true in exactly the way the notebook stuff is true of Otto. And if the standard move is to tighten the criteria so my phone fails, why doesn't the notebook fail too?
