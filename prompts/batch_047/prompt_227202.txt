So I'm a novelist (two literary novels out, currently stuck on the third) and the new book is about death — specifically about an immortal man who slowly comes to see his condition as a curse while everyone he loves keeps dying around him. Writing it has forced me to figure out what I actually believe about death, and I've ended up with a pile of views that each feel right to me on their own, but a philospher friend of mine skimmed them over coffee and said they "don't all fit in the same suitcase" and then refused to elaborate, which has been bugging me for weeks. So here's where I've landed:

1. The Epicurean point seems basically right: once I'm dead there's no me around to be deprived, harmed, or to have experiences at all, so being dead can't be bad for me. Death is nothing to us.
2. Still, it's clearly worse for someone to die at 30 than at 80. The thirty-year-old misses out on decades of good things — love, projects, watching kids grow up, finishing the damn book — and that's exactly what makes early death tragic. The badness lives in the goods they lose out on.
3. I also find Lucretius's symmetry argument convincing: the infinite stretch of time before I was born doesn't upset me or seem bad in retrospect, so the infinite stretch after I die shouldn't either.
4. It's perfectly rational to dread a painful or drawn-out dying process — that's a real experience somebody actually has to live through — but dreading being dead itself is confused, and I'd want that distinction to come through in the book.
5. Immortality in my novel is genuinely awful for the character. Endless time flattens everything: pleasures repeat, ambition curdles, he eventually can't want anything new. I want the reader to really feel that never dying would be a curse rather than a prize.

Before I build the whole arc of the novel on these, could you audit them for me? Which pairs are actually in conflict and which only look like they are at first glance? Please rank any real tensions by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would cost me — ideally I'd like to keep as much of the list intact as possible, since some of these are load baring for the plot.
