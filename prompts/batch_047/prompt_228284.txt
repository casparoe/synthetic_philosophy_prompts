The other day I skipped a local election, then complained about who won, and a friend hit me with "if you don't vote, you can't complain" and considered the argument settled. Break down the precise claims that slogan could actually be making: a causal claim (non-voters helped cause the outcome, so it's partly their fault), a moral claim (they forfeited the standing to object), an expressive claim (complaining without voting is hypocritical), or what? Say which readings are platitudes, which are substantive but defensible, and which are just false. Then do the work my friend needed it to do: which reading, if any, would actually support compulsory voting, and which readings would make compulsory voting pointless or even counterproductive? Finally, the slogan seems to imply that people who cant vote -- non-citizens, sixteen-year-olds -- also can't complain. Does it? And if not, what does that tell us about which reading is the right one?
