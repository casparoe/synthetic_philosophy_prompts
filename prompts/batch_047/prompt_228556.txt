For a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my position before we discuss imprecise probabilities, and I would like a consistency check before the others start objecting. My view is roughly this: (1) rational credence can be imprecise, so that an agent with thin or conflicting evidence may rationally be represented by a range or set of probability functions rather than by one exact number; (2) suspension of judgment is sometimes the uniquely rational response, not merely a temporary failure to make up one’s mind; (3) when imprecise credences guide action, the right rule is to choose an option that is best, or at least not worse, under every probability function in the set, while otherwise permitting more than one choice; (4) learning new evidence should proceed by conditioning each probability function in the set on that evidence; (5) a rational agent must never be exposed to a sure loss merely in virtue of having the credences they do; and (6) genuinely relevant evidence should normally make a rational credal state more accurate or more useful, and in the central cases should narrow uncertainty rather than dilate it. I also accept that rationality is not always decisive: there can be ties, permissible pluralities, and cases in which several incompatible attitudes are all reasonable.

Please audit this package rather than give a general introduction to imprecise probability. Identify which commitments genuinely pull against one another and which apparent conflicts dissolve once the relevant distinctions are made; rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one state exactly what would have to be given up and what that revision would cost. In particular, consider whether my action rule can produce trouble across sequences of choices even when each individual choice looks permissible, and whether my updating rule can conflict with my claim about relevant evidence narrowing uncertainty. Do not assume that every pair involving both imprecision and action is automatically inconsistent. End by naming the single revision that would restore the most coherence while preserving the spirit of the view. Please write the answer in American English.
