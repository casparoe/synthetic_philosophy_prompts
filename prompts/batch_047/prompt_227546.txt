Write a parody FAQ published by the Bureau of Moral Worth, an imaginary Kantian licensing agency that audits good deeds to certify whether they were performed from duty and therefore genuinely count. It should present, in numbered entries, the Bureau's replies to at least the following public inquiries: the shopkeeper who deals honestly with customers because honesty is good for business; the cheerful donor who gives because giving makes her happy; the formerly compassionate man whose fellow-feeling has been worn away by his own grief and who helps others anyway; the passerby who dove in to save a drowning child on pure impulse, without a moment's deliberation; and the anxious citizen who worries that the pleasure he took in having done his duty retroactively contaminates the deed. The replies should be technically competent Kantiana — true to the distinction between acting from duty and merely acting in conformity with it, to the claim that moral worth resides in the maxim rather than the outcome, and to Kant's own skepticism about whether anyone can be certain of her deepest motives — delivered with absolute procedural confidence. About 700 words, in a deadpan civil-service voice. The comedy must come from the doctrine being applied correctly and taken completely seriously, not from caricaturing it.
