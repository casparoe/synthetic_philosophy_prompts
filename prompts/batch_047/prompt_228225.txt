At the close of my seminar on the ethics of war, I put my own credence that maintaining a nuclear deterrent posture is morally permissible at roughly 0.35; a colleague put hers at 0.9. I would like your number, with the derivation made explicit rather than gestured at. Please decompose the question into at least the following sub-questions, adding any you judge to be load-bearing: (i) whether a rational state can credibly threaten a retaliation it would be irrational to execute, or whether deterrence instead requires cultivating genuine unpredictability in an adversary's eyes; (ii) whether it can be permissible to form and maintain a conditional intention to do what would be impermissible to do, and what hangs on the answer; (iii) whether the absence of great-power war since 1945 is properly attributed to deterrence rather than to rival explanations; and (iv) how a small probability of civilizational catastrophe should be weighted against decades of stability. Give each term a probability and one line of justification, state how the terms combine into your final credence, and identify the term to which the final number is most sensitive. Finally, say where the disagreement between my 0.35 and my colleague's 0.9 most likely lives.
