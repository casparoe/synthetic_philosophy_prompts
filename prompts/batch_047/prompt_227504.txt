Construct a thought experiment that separates four claims often run together: a practice has a cause; it persists because it is useful; there is a reason for it; and someone has that reason. The centerpiece should be a free-floating rationale—an advantage that genuinely explains why a practice is maintained even though no designer, participant, observer, or collective agent has ever represented or endorsed that advantage.

Set the case in a realistic human institution rather than in biological evolution. The practice must arise through accident or drift and be copied for a stated reason that is false or irrelevant. Its actual rationale must depend on an unobvious statistical or environmental structure, not on a hidden expert, secret planner, collective mind, or the theorist’s purposes. Include two closely matched versions of the institution in which the participants’ beliefs, intentions, and local behavior are the same, while the crucial background condition obtains in only one. The practice should therefore have the relevant rationale in one version but lack it in the other. Include a non-miraculous intervention or comparison that could reveal this difference while holding the participants’ stated beliefs fixed.

After presenting the scenario in no more than 600 words, demonstrate that it works. Identify the practice, the bearer and beneficiary of the rationale, the relevant counterfactuals, and the evidence that the rationale is explanatory rather than merely projected by an outside analyst. Distinguish the practice’s causal origin, its selection or maintenance explanation, its justification, and the reasons the participants take themselves to have. Explain why reliability alone would not suffice, and why a widely accepted false rationale is not simply an unknown true one.

Then address the conceptual fork: when someone finally discovers the advantage, does an existing rationale become represented, or does the practice only then acquire a reason in the full sense? Give the strongest objection that free-floating rationale is just metaphorical shorthand for a complex causal process, and reply without defining the objection away. Finally, identify the single feature of your case doing the most work and explain what collapses if that feature is removed. The aim is to determine whether reasons without reasoners constitute a genuine explanatory category, so do not merely stipulate that the rationale is real.
