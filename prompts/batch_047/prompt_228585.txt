I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm writing a sidebar for a long piece on political polarization. I want a "which epistemic trap are you actually in?" quiz that readers can take in a few minutes. The catch: everyone I talk to calls everything an echo chamber, but I want the quiz built on the distinction that they're not the same thing -- a filter bubble leaves things out passively (you just never encounter the other side), while an echo chamber actively discredits outsiders in advance (you hear them, but you've already been given reasons not to trust them). So the diagnostic questions have to pry those apart with concrete scenarios, not by asking people which label they prefer.

Please give me 8-10 questions, each a recognizable everyday situation (how you react when a relative shares an article from an outlet you distrust, what happens when a source you trust gets something badly wrong, whether you can name a person whose politics you disagree with but whose judgment you'd still take seriously, etc.), each with 2-3 answer options. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these leaves: classic filter bubble, echo chamber (the active-discrediting kind), a healthy high-trust-but-open network that should NOT come out looking pathological, and an "actually fine, just politically interested" leaf. Flag any two leaves your quiz can't reliably separate, and note any combination of answers that would land someone in an unoccupied spot in the space (and whether that spot is empty because it's incoherent or just rare).

Format it in this order: "The questions" (bulk of it, maybe 400 words), "Scoring key" (~150 words), "Where this gets shaky" (~100 words). In that last section, say how confident you are that the question-to-position mappings actually track the real distinction, and single out the one question or leaf you're least sure about -- I'd rather cut it than run something misleading.
