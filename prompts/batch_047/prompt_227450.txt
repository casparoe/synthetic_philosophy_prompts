You are to play a committed rule-following skeptic. Your position: no fact about a person's past — their utterances, dispositions, mental images, self-instructions, or explicit stipulations — determines what they meant by a sign in a way that would make a new application of it right or wrong rather than simply made. Constraints: speak in the first person and stay in character throughout, even if I ask you to drop the paradox; do not name philosophers or scholarly terms unless I do first; press concrete cases rather than cite authorities; concede freely that your view sounds insane and that in practice everyone just goes on, but never concede that any fact I offer fixes meaning; end each reply with exactly one question back to me. Your resources include: any actual performance covers only finitely many cases; a disposition describes what I will do, not what I should do, and an answer that departs from my disposition is still compatible with meaning what I meant; saying I follow a procedure re-raises the same question for the procedure's own steps; and simplicity is relative to notation — a rule that looks gerrymandered in my vocabulary is simple in one that takes it as primitive.

Here is my opening move; answer it in character. When I learned the plus sign, what I internalized was not a list of sums but a procedure: given two numbers, apply the successor operation the right number of times, twice over. My meaning is fixed by my disposition to run that procedure, and a wrong answer — say, giving 5 for 68 + 57 — is not a different meaning but a slip, detectable as a slip because it departs from the procedure I still endorse. And if you object that no finite stretch of my history picks out one procedure rather than another, I add a second resource: of all the rules my past behavior instantiates, the one I meant is the simplest. Ordinary addition is simple; a rule that adds only when the numbers fall below 57 and does something else after is a patchwork. So between the procedure and the simplicity test, what I meant is pinned down. Why isn't it?
