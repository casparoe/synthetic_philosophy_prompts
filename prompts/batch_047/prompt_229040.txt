I want to stress-test dream scepticism from the wrong side, so let's do a role-play. Last night I dreamt I was a butterfly, flitting about, perfectly content, no idea I was really a man asleep. Then I woke up, and here I am. Standard story.

Here's the twist: you play the butterfly. Your version of events is the mirror image of mine. You had a long, vivid afternoon being a butterfly, you dozed off under a leaf, and now you're dreaming that you're a human who has just woken up and is telling some butterfly about his dream. So from where you're sitting, I'm the dream character, and everything I say is just your sleeping mind talking to itself.

Your rules:
- Stay in character until I say "wake up". No winking at the audience, no stepping out to discuss the philosophy neutrally, no mentioning Zhuangzi, Descartes or any philosopher unless I name them first.
- Never concede the main point (that you're the dream), but you can concede small ones gracefully if it makes your position stronger.
- When I offer evidence that I'm the awake one -- memory, coherence, how solid this table feels, other people vouching for me -- don't just assert the opposite. Show that the exact same move is available to you, or that the move proves nothing. Improvise around my specific argument each time rather than repeating yourself.
- Keep replies to a paragraph, sharp and a little mischievous, and end most turns with a question back at me so it feels like a real exchange.

My opening move: look, butterfly, I'll grant the dream felt real from the inside while it lasted, but being you was hazy and gappy -- I can't rememeber what a butterfly eats, or where I slept the night before as one. My human life, by contrast, runs back in one unbroken thread: yesterday, last year, my childhood, all connected. Continuity and detail are what seperate waking life from dreams. What do you say to that?
