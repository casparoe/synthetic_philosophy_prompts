So I'm writing a novel where one of the side characters is this libertarian econ guy who won't shut up about Nozick at dinner parties, and for his big speech I basically paraphrased a bit from a pop philosophy book I got at an airport. My beta reader (who did a phil minor twenty years ago) flagged it and said I've made Nozick say stuff he didnt say, but she couldn't tell me exactly what, and now I'm paranoid the whole scene is built on sand. Here's the passage I lifted, roughly:

"Nozick's famous Wilt Chamberlain argument shows that government redistribution of wealth is unjust. Imagine everyone in a society starts out with equal shares, and then a million people each freely pay 25 cents to watch Wilt Chamberlain play basketball. He ends up rich, everyone else ends up slightly poorer, and nobody was cheated -- so the resulting inequality is perfectly just. The lesson is that any distribution is just as long as it results from people freely choosing to spend their money, which means taxing the rich to fund welfare programs is morally on a par with forced labor. Egalitarians can only maintain equality by constantly meddling in people's private choices, and Nozick takes this to refute the idea that justice is about matching some pattern, like equality or need or merit."

The bits I most need for the scene are (a) the forced labor line, because my character delivers it like a mic drop, and (b) the idea that whatever emerges from free choices is automatically just, because the plot later hinges on him getting conned and having to square that with his principles. So: how fair is that summary actually? Which parts would a Nozick reader sign off on, which are common oversimplifications I could get away with in fiction, and is there anything in there that's just wrong -- like, wrong enough that a philosophy professor reading my novel would throw it across the room? Also curious whether the fix breaks my plot point about the con, because if Nozick has some built-in way to handle dodgy transactions that would actually make the scene better.
