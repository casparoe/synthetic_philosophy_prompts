I'm prepping for my phil of science seminar and I keep hitting a wall with Occam's razor. Students wheel it out constantly -- last week someone used it to dismiss an argument about fine-tuning, the week before it was conspiracy theories -- always in the form "the simplest explanation is probably true," as if that's obvious. But when I push, nobody can tell me what "simple" means or why simplicity should track truth at all.

So: what are the distinct theses this slogan could be? I assume there's a spread from near-trivial (prefer fewer assumptions when evidence is equal, which sounds like a tiebreaker, not a truth-detector) to something metaphysical (nature itself is simple, which seems empirically dubious) to something epistemic (simplicity is actual evidence). Which of these are defensible and which are just vibes?

Also -- I've heard computer science people talk about measuring simplicity as shortness of description, and even building a whole theory of prediction on preferring short descriptions. Does that actually justify the razor, or does it just bake a simplicity-preference in as a prior and call it a derivation? Be honest about which reading you land on, what argument would change your mind, and where your reasoning is most likely to fall apart.
