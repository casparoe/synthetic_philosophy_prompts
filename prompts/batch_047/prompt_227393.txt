So I'm a PhD student working on phenomenology / existentialism, and my dissertation is on bad faith in Sartre, and I keep running into a thought experiment I can't settle myself. I want to hear what you think Sartre would say about it.

Imagine a near-future app, call it something like an "authenticity tracker." It watches your behavior -- purchase history, messages, location data, how your face looks on camera, voice stress patterns, whatever -- and it gives you a daily score for how authentic your choices are. Like, it flags when you agreed to plans you didn't want, when your stated values diverge from what you actually do, that kind of thing. And people genuinely use it: they check their score in the morning, they feel bad when it drops, they make decisions partly to keep the number up. Some users say it helps them catch themselves sliding into self-deception. Critics (in my fictional scenario) say it's just a new way of hiding from freedom.

So: what would Sartre's actual position on this thing be? I can see the obvious reading -- an authenticity score seems almost designed to be incoherent on his view, since it treats authenticity as a measurable property of a person, a fact about you that exists whether or not you choose it, which smells like the spirit of seriousness or turning the for-itself into an in-itself. But I'm less sure the obvious reading is right. Sartre thinks bad faith is self-deception about your own freedom, and isn't a tool that catches you lying to yourself at least a step toward lucidity? He also lets the Other's knowledge of your body play some role in self-knowledge, and the tracker is sort of a mechanical Other.

Please reason through it the way Sartre would, using his actual conceptual machinery (facticity vs transcendence, the look of the Other, bad faith's structure of lying-to-oneself, etc.) rather than just giving me "he'd hate it" or "he'd love it." And tell me where you think he'd land on net, and whether the answer changes depending on how the person relates to the score (checking it anxiously vs. treating it as one input among others). Don't just summarize Being and Nothingness at me -- I want the prediction and the argument for it.
