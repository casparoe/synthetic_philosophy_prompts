I'm on the editorial board of our grad student union's newsletter and somehow I've become the de facto "free speech person" whenever disputes come up, so I've been trying to write down what I actually believe about speech restrictions to check I'm not just ruling case-by-case on vibes. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The only legitimate reason for the state to restrict what someone says is to prevent harm to other people -- never because the speech is offensive, and never for the speaker's own good.
(2) "Harm" has to mean something concrete -- physical injury, financial loss, credible threats -- and not hurt feelings, offense, or vague stuff like damage to someone's dignity or to the "social climate," because once you let those in the principle can justify banning anything.
(3) Even so, sustained targeted harassment of one specific person -- like someone repeatedly directing slurs at a particular coworker over weeks -- is something an employer or the law can legitimately shut down.
(4) For speech addressed to the public at large, it should be punishable only when it's both intended and likely to produce imminent lawless action.
(5) A university punishing a student for turning in a plagiarized essay is obviously not a free speech issue at all, even though it's literally punishing someone for words they produced.
(6) Private platforms banning users isn't censorship in any sense that matters philosophically -- only the state can violate free speech.

My question is whether this package actually hangs together or whether I'm papering over something. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and for each real tension what's the cheapest fix and what would it cost me? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally fatal, and be specific about which commitment has to move rather than just gesturing at "tradeoffs." Also, where the answer depends on a position that's genuinely contested among people who work on this stuff -- in law or in philosophy -- flag it as contested rather than presenting one side as the settled view, since I want to know how much of my package rests on ground that's still disputed.
