I am a doctoral student in public policy studying prostitution law, and I keep running into the same impasse in the literature. Three regimes dominate the debate: the Nordic model, which decriminalizes selling sex but criminalizes buying it; legalization with licensing and regulation, as in Germany and the Netherlands; and full decriminalization, as in New Zealand. The empirical question -- which regime best protects the safety and welfare of sex workers -- has been studied for two decades, and yet the scholarly and advocacy communities seem no closer to agreement than they were in 2005. Both camps cite studies showing reduced violence under their preferred regime and increased trafficking or harm under the other; both dismiss the other's evidence as methodologically compromised, and the same country-level cases get recruited as success stories by one side and cautionary tales by the other.

My working hypothesis is that the empirical dispute is real but not load-bearing: underneath it sits a disagreement about the appropriate counterfactual baseline (should a regime be compared to an unregulated criminalized market, or to an ideally regulated one?), plus a prior normative disagreement about whether selling sex is labor to be made safe or a harm to be reduced in volume, which determines in advance which outcomes count as success. If that is right, then more and better studies will not settle anything, because the sides are not measuring against the same standard.

Is that diagnosis correct? Please assess what kind of disagreement this actually is: a genuine empirical dispute that better data could resolve, a disguised normative dispute that no data could resolve, a disagreement about standards of evidence, or several of these operating at different levels. I want reasons grounded in what the two sides actually claim and concede, not another survey of the policy options. And given your diagnosis: what, concretely, would count as progress -- a particular study design, a conceptual distinction, an agreed-upon metric -- or should we expect the debate to stay stalled regardless, and why?
