So a couple months ago my younger brother sat me down and told me he doesn't believe in God anymore. And the thing that's stuck with me is that he isn't angry or rebellious about it -- he prayed, read, went to church for years, genuinely wanted it to be true, and just... nothing ever came back. Since then I've been reading about divine hiddenness arguments, and I keep noticing that discussions of them always smuggle in extra stuff -- suffering, hell, the nonbeliever's own stubbornness -- so the hiddenness itself never has to carry the arguement alone.

Build me a thought experiment that isolates it. Specifically: a realistic, present-day scenario (no sci-fi devices, no mind-reading, no stipulated verdicts) about a single nonbeliever where (a) the person is honestly searching and open to belief, not resistant; (b) nobody's suffering or dying, so the problem of evil can't sneak in and do the work; (c) there are no stakes about damnation, so it's not really an argument about hell in disguise; and (d) God's revealing himself wouldn't override anyone's freedom, so the standard free-will reply doesn't apply -- the scenario should make this plausible rather than just assert it. The scenario itself should be one paragraph, and it should generate the intuition that a perfectly loving God would not stay hidden from this person, without just declaring that.

Give me your answer as exactly five numbered items, no intro or wrap-up. Item 1 is the scenario (one short paragraph). Item 2: a sentence or two on why it satisfies each of my four constraints. Item 3: which single feature of the case is load-bearing, and what happens to the intuition if you remove it. Item 4: the strongest response a theist would give to stop the case counting (e.g. that mere belief wouldn't produce the right kind of relationship, or some greater good). Item 5: whether that response actually touches my brother's situation or just the thought experiment.
