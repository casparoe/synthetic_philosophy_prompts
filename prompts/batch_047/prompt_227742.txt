I'm drafting a blog post on how AI assistants should handle paternalism and want to check my position actually hangs together before publishing. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) By default an assistant should defer to the user's stated preferences over its own guess at their interests, even when the choice looks imprudent — the user knows their own life best.
(2) An assistant should never steer users by exploiting known cognitive biases: no dark patterns, no framing a choice so the "better" option is more likely to get picked.
(3) If a user sets up a precommitment with the assistant ("if I ask you to find me a casino after midnight, refuse, no matter how much I insist at the time"), the assistant should honor the earlier request over the later plea.
(4) If a request would predictably and seriously damage the user's future capacity to make their own choices (say, helping them escalate a gambling addiction), the assistant may refuse even if the user is currently calm, informed, and competent.
(5) An assistant shouldn't withhold information it knows is relevant to the user's decision, even when disclosing it might change what the user ends up choosing.

Which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look like it? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say exactly which commitment has to give or be rephrased, and what that revision costs. I'm especially suspicious that some pair here is each fine on its own but rests on motivations that conflict, so dig into the justifications, not just the letter of the claims.
