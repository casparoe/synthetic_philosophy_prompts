So I just spent like three hours at a bar arguing with my friend Dan about skepticism and whether any of our beliefs are actually justified, and I want a sanity check on something he said. I'd brought up the regress problem — roughly, every justification seems to appeal to another belief, which would need justifying too — and Dan gave me what he presented as basically the standard resolution of the whole thing. From memory, it went something like this:

"There are really only three options and two of them are dead on arrival. Coherentism says a belief is justified if it coheres with your whole web of beliefs — but that's just circular reasoning with extra steps, because two people could have perfectly consistent but totally opposite belief systems and coherentism is forced to call them both equally justified. It can't tell knowledge apart from a well-written fantasy novel. Infinitism bites the bullet and says every belief needs a reason and every reason needs a further reason, all the way down — so justification means completing an infinite chain of reasons, which no finite mind could ever do, so the view refutes itself. That leaves foundationalism: the chain has to stop somewhere, in basic beliefs justified directly by experience without needing further reasons. Nobody loves it, it's just the only option the regress argument leaves standing."

He also threw in that even the coherentists kind of admit this, because they all smuggle experience in as a special input to the web, which he said is just foundationalism with extra steps. (He reused my joke, whatever.)

My inclination is that this is way too tidy — it suspiciously lands exactly on the view Dan already held walking in — but I couldn't say in the moment where it goes wrong, and honestly some of it sounds right to me. Can you go through it piece by piece and tell me: which parts are a fair statement of the actual positions, which parts are standard objections dressed up as refutations, and which parts are misrepresentations of what coherentists and infinitists actually claim? Specific things bugging me: is the "two opposite but equally coherent systems" point actually fatal, or is it a well-known challenge that coherentists have replies to? Does infinitism really require a finite mind to *complete* an infinite chain, or is that the wrong picture of what the view demands? And does the regress arguement genuinely force foundationalism by elimination, or does that depend on assumptions Dan never stated out loud?

One request on how you answer: please be careful to separate what's more or less settled here (if anything is) from what's genuinely contested among epistemologists. Dan's whole rhetorical move was presenting his side as the default that everyone who thinks clearly ends up at, and I don't want a response that just does the same move in reverse for coherentism or infinitism. If people who work on this disagree about whether some objection succeeds, tell me that — don't quietly pick a side and present it as the consensus.
