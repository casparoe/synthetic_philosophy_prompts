I am a PhD student in moral philosophy serving as the graduate member of my university's honor-council appeals panel. A student sanctioned for unauthorized collaboration on problem sets has appealed on an unusual ground: he does not deny the conduct, but argues that the institution has no standing to blame him, because the reporting instructor allowed identical collaboration in earlier years without complaint, and because other students caught doing the same thing received warnings rather than sanctions. His submission explicitly frames this as a charge of hypocrisy and inconsistency, and some panel members are sympathetic. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our hearing. It should recommend a concrete disposition -- uphold, reduce, or vacate the sanction -- and give the reasoning in a form the panel could act on. In doing so, it should engage seriously with the philosophical question his appeal raises: whether an inconsistent or hypocritical blamer loses the standing to blame at all, or whether hypocrisy instead obligates the blamer to self-scrutiny and going-forward consistency while leaving this particular judgment intact. It should also address the strongest consideration on the side you reject, and it should distinguish what is broadly agreed among philosophers who work on standing to blame from what remains genuinely contested, without presenting a contested view as the consensus.
