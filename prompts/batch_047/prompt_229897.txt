I got into an argument with a friend about mind uploading. I said that even a perfect simulation of a brain might not be conscious, and he ended the discussion by declaring that the brain is a computer -- so simulating one on different hardware changes nothing, the way a program runs the same on any machine. I've been turning this slogan over since and can't pin down what it could even mean, since brains don't seem to have programs, instruction sets, or a clean split between hardware and software. What are the distinct precise claims the brain is a computer could be making -- from the trivial (brains process information in some loose sense) through the substantive (cognition is computation over representations; what matters is the abstract causal organization, not the biological material) to the radical (the brain literally executes software, so the right program on silicon would be a mind)? For each reading, say whether it's a platitude, genuinely contested, or likely false, and which readings my friend would actually need to be true for his uploading conclusion to follow. I suspect he was trading on the tame readings to smuggle in the radical one, but I want to see it laid out.
