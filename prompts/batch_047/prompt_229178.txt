I help run our neighbourhood allotment association, and every spring we get a small grant from the council, about £800, which the two of us on the grants committee have to allocate. I care deeply about the wildlife pond project — newts, dragonflies, the lot. My co-committee member, Priya, is just as passionate about the children's gardening patch. Neither of us thinks the other project is worthless exactly, we just genuinely rank them differently, and we've both put in the volunteer hours to back it up.

Here's the snag. Both projects have a kind of threshold: the pond needs a proper liner and pump or it just silts up again (we learnt this the hard way), and the kids' patch needs raised beds and fencing or the foxes destroy everything. Half-measures are worthless for both. So the "fair" option — split the money down the middle every year — means neither project ever actually happens. Priya has suggested we alternate: this year the whole lot goes to her patch, next year everything goes to my pond. By the numbers this clearly gets more total good done, and yet something about it bothers me. It feels like I'm treating the newts as a bargaining chip, agreeing to let them go without for a whole year as part of some deal, when my support for the pond isn't supposed to be a preference I can trade away — it's what I think is right. If I really believed in the pond, shouldn't I fight for it every single year rather than selling it out half the time?

Am I being silly here? Is alternating actually the obviously sensible arrangement and I'm just being precious, or is there something genuinely dodgy about horsetrading when the thing being traded is a matter of principle rather than taste? Also, if we do alternate, does it matter who goes first — Priya says it doesn't but I notice she's volunteered to go first, which makes me suspicious.
