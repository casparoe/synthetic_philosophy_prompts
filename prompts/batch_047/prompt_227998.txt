I'm in my final year of a philosophy degree and this semester I'm taking a seminar on normative ethics, which has just finished a three-week unit on negative utilitarianism. For the last session, our instructor has asked each of us to submit a one-page statement of our settled commitments in the vicinity, which will be anonymized, circulated, and then criticized by the group. Before I hand mine in, I'd like an audit of whether it actually hangs together. Here it is, more or less verbatim:

(1) I'm a negative utilitarian with a lexical threshold: suffering above a certain intensity cannot be morally outweighed by any amount of pleasure, nor by any quantity of milder suffering; below that threshold, pleasure and pain are commensurable and trade off against each other at constant rates.

(2) Value aggregates impartially: the overall badness of an outcome is the sum of the suffering it contains, with no priority weighting for the worse off and no discounting based on how many people are affected.

(3) The badness of torture is finite in the way that matters: a large enough number of distinct people each enduring one mild headache would constitute a worse outcome than a single person being tortured for an hour.

(4) It would be impermissible to torture one person in order to prevent chronic, moderate, but non-debilitating back pain in a million others.

(5) It would not be an improvement -- and would, if anything, be wrong -- to painlessly annihilate a universe whose entire contents were one person living an otherwise neutral life punctuated by a single pinprick.

(6) There is nothing objectionable about bringing into existence a life that contains some genuine but mild suffering, provided the life also contains enough pleasure.

What I want is a consistency check, not a lecture on the pros and cons of negative utilitarianism generally. Tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with each other and which only appear to be at first glance; for the genuine ones, rank them by severity and, for each, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency along with what that revision costs me in plausibility. I'd also like to know, if more than one tension is real, which single change would clean up the most of the set. Please don't just flag every pair as problematic -- part of what I'm testing is whether the view can discriminate between real and merely apparent problems, and I'd rather find out now than in front of the seminar.
