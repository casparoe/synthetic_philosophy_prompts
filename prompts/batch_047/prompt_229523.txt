Last night a friend and I spent several hours arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem, and we reached an impasse that I would like you to help me break. Let me lay out the case precisely, in my own preferred parametrization, so that we are arguing about the same thing.

A paratrooper drops into territory divided into four quadrants: two lie in Red Company territory (call them Red-Headquarters and Red-Outskirts) and two in Blue Company territory (Blue-Headquarters and Blue-Outskirts). She has no information favoring any quadrant, so her credences are uniform: 1/4 each, and hence credence 1/2 that she is in Red territory. She then radios headquarters, and the only message that comes through is this: if you are in Red territory, the probability that you are in Red-Headquarters is 3/4. The message says nothing at all about Blue territory, and nothing about how the message was selected from among possible messages.

My position is that the only rational response is to reallocate her credences within Red territory to 3/8 and 1/8, while leaving her credence that she is in Red territory unchanged at 1/2. My friend insists this is irrational and that her credence in Red must rise above 1/2, on the grounds that any update minimizing divergence from the prior subject to the learned conditional as a constraint — or any update treating the conditional as evidence to be imaged on — shifts probability mass toward Red. My friend pressed three objections I struggled to answer. First, there is no proposition she has straightforwardly learned, so my preferred update cannot be represented as conditionalization and therefore lacks the standard justifications (Dutch book immunity, expected accuracy) that conditionalization enjoys. Second, a reflection-style argument: had she anticipated receiving exactly this message, she would seemingly have been required to adjust her credence in Red in advance, and it is incoherent to plan an update one regards as irrational. Third, an order-dependence worry: if conditional messages could arrive in sequence, my conservative rule makes the final credences depend on the order of arrival, which looks like a symptom of a rule that is not tracking truth.

Please argue for my side: that keeping the marginal credence over the Red/Blue partition fixed at 1/2, while revising the conditional credences within Red, is the rationally correct response to this evidence. Address each of my friend's three objections head-on rather than merely restating intuitions about what the message is "about." If part of the defense requires conceding something — for instance, that the update rule is more restricted in scope than conditionalization, or that the diachronic argument must be rejected in a way that has costs elsewhere — say so explicitly and argue the concession is worth making. I am not interested in references to the literature; I want the strongest version of the argument itself, and an honest accounting of where it is forced to bite a bullet.
