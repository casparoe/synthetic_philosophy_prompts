We wrote "the forgotten migration caused the outage" and nobody blinked. Case 2: a library update broke the checkout flow on an old browser we technically still support, and when someone suggested the root cause was that nobody had tested on that browser, the room pushed back hard and said no, the cause was the incompatible library, the untested browser was just background. Case 3: the on-call engineer didn't notice a paging alert for forty minutes because they were at dinner, and we listed that as a contributing cause in the report. Case 4: a junior dev didn't trigger the failover runbook during an incident because nobody had ever trained them on it, and we explicitly said their not acting was NOT a cause, since you can't omit something you never knew to do. Checking the warmup is on no checklist, appears in no runbook, and is nobodys assigned duty, but every senior engineer on the team has done it as a habit for years and it's kind of just "how things are done here." Half the team wants to name the skipped check as the cause, half says you can't cause something by skipping a step nobody ever told you to do.

So: what verdict is most consistent with our own precedents, and which earlier case is the closest analog?
