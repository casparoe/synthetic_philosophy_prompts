I've been sitting in on a metaphysics reading group that's been going through the Lewis vs. Kripke fight over how objects show up across possible worlds -- counterpart theory vs. transworld identity -- and I swear the debate has been frozen in the exact same configuration since the 70s. The Lewis people keep saying: of course Nixon himself doesn't literally exist at other worlds, nothing can be in two places at once, all we need is sufficiently similar counterparts and the counterpart relation can be context-sensitive. The Kripke people keep saying: we don't need some similarity criterion to make sense of what Nixon might have done, we just stipulate that we're talking about Nixon, and anyway nobody worries that their counterpart at another world got rich -- they worry whether *they* might have. And then it just... repeats. Everyone grants the formal machinery of both frameworks works fine, nobody's disputing any logic.

My working hypothesis, which I want stress-tested: the two camps were never actually competing theories. Lewis is trying to *reduce* modality to something non-modal (concrete worlds, similarity), so he needs counterparts to be picked out by qualitative facts. Kripke is just describing how modal talk functions for people who already accept modality, so stipulation is fine for him. Under this reading, the real disagreement is about whether de re modal claims need grounding at all, and "counterparts vs transworld identity" is a proxy war that mislabels the actual fault line.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Verbal, metaphysical, meta-metaphysical? Is my hypothesis right that the sides are answering different questions, or is there a single question they genuinely answer differently? And whatever your diagnosis: what would actually count as progress here? A formal result showing one framework can't express something the other can? A argument about which view better serves some job (semantics, physics, ethics)? Or is this the kind of dispute where nothing could move it, and if so is that a defect in the question or just what bedrock looks like?
