I'm TAing an undergrad metaphysics course and building a "which view of persistence do you actually hold?" flowchart for a handout, one that sorts students into endurantism, perdurantism (worm theory), stage theory, and maybe a Humean dissolution of the whole question, without them needing to know any of the labels beforehand. I want it anchored in this passage from Hume's Treatise (I.iv.6, Of personal identity):

"We have a distinct idea of an object, that remains invariable and uninterrupted thro' a suppos'd variation of time; and this idea we call that of identity or sameness. We have also a distinct idea of several different objects existing in succession, and connected together by a close relation; and this to an accurate view affords as perfect a notion of diversity, as if there was no manner of relation among the objects. But tho' these two ideas of identity, and a succession of related objects be in themselves perfectly distinct, and even contrary, yet 'tis certain, that in our common way of thinking they are generally confounded with each other."

First node should turn on the passage: does the student agree with Hume that our idea of an object persisting through change is just these two ideas confounded, so persistence is something to explain away rather than explain? Agreeing should route them toward the Humean/deflationist leaf; disagreeing sends them into the standard cases. Build the tree from the logical space rather than a list of names, 8 questions max, each a concrete case a student can answer cold (the statue squashed along with its lump of clay, the ship of Theseus, someone sitting at noon and standing at one, a lump that loses a small chip, a road that's straight in one place and bent in another). Each leaf gets its name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate (I suspect worm theory and stage theory will be a problem) and note any answer combination that looks coherent but unoccupied, with a guess at why. Finally: tell me which leaf Hume himself lands in given the passage, and whether his calling the succession idea "as perfect a notion of diversity" makes him a closet perdurantist. Last line should be a one-sentence summary of the whole instrument.
