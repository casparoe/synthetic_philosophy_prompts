So my team lead read some pop-science article about the "extended mind" idea -- like, your notebook or phone can literally be part of your mind, not just a tool -- and he's been using it to argue in our retro that documentation is pointless because "the wiki is already in our heads." Which is obviously convenient for him because he hates writing docs. But he actually wrote out his reasoning and I can't find the hole, even though the conclusion seems insane. Here's his argument, cleaned up a bit:

(1) If some process outside your skull does the exact same job for you that a memory inside your skull would do, there's no principled reason to call the brain one "part of your mind" and the outside one "just a tool." Same function, same status.

(2) My notes app, my IDE's autocomplete, and my browser history do the same job biological memory does: it's stored info that I pull up fluently when I need it. Functionally it's just slower RAM.

(3) So the contents of my notes app are literally part of my mind. I believe that stuff right now, the same way I believe my own phone number, even when I'm not looking at it.

(4) My notes app syncs from our team wiki -- most of my "notes" are just copies of wiki pages.

(5) So the wiki's contents are part of my mind too.

(6) The wiki is mostly auto-generated docs covering our whole codebase, plus links out to public docs and Stack Overflow threads, which link out further.

(7) So basically the whole reachable public internet is part of my mind, give or take.

(8) The exact same argument works for everyone on the team, since we all sync from the same wiki. So we all literally share one giant mind.

He's concluded from this that (a) I can't disagree with him about anything on the wiki because "it's the same mind," and (b) writing docs is redundant. I mostly want to keep the intuition in (1)--(3), honestly -- it does seem like my notes app is part of how I think in some real sense, and I don't want to say my brain is special just because it's wet and inside my head. But I refuse to accept that I share a mind with a guy who thinks tabs vs spaces is a personality trait.

Which step do I actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? Like, is the problem in (2) -- are my notes not actually doing the same job as memory, and what would the original thesis say the extra requirements are? Or is the problem the chaining move in (4)--(7), where "part of" seems to get treated as transitive when it obviously isn't? Walk me through what a defender of the extended mind view would say is the fine print, which of my examples actually pass the fine print and which fail, and what each way of blocking the argument forces you to give up.
