The principle of natural selection boils down to 'survival of the fittest', but fitness is defined in terms of survival, so the theory is a tautology: it predicts that whatever survives was the fittest, and no observation could ever count against it. Popper therefore classified Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme rather than a scientific theory, which in his framework amounts to saying it isn't really knowledge at all. He never retracted this verdict, and late in life he went further, suggesting that evolution is actually driven by the purposive, goal-directed activity of organisms rather than by blind selection -- a view closer to Lamarck than to Darwin, and one that biologists largely ignored because Popper never properly understood the Modern Synthesis."
