So my coworker Priya read Gödel Escher Bach back in college and never fully recovered, and now whenever were stuck on a slow oncall shift we end up arguing about whether a person can exist in more than one place at once. Over the years I've collected her verdicts on the classic thought experiments, and they're weirdly consistent but I can't figure out what the actual rule is:

- Star Trek teleporter that vaporizes you here and rebuilds an atom-perfect copy on Mars: she says you survive. The copy is you, full stop.
- Your brain gets split in half and each hemisphere goes into a new body, both wake up with all your memories: she says they're BOTH you. Not one real you and one fake. Both.
- Your neurons get replaced one at a time with silicon chips over like a decade, you never notice anything changing: still you the whole time, obviously.
- A perfect scan-copy of your brain gets made while you're still alive, they put it in a cloned body, and both of you walk out of the lab: suddenly she flips. Only the original is you, the copy is a stranger who falsely thinks he's you.
- Her grandmother died a couple years ago and Priya insists that a real, if kind of blurry and low-res, piece of her grandmother is still around inside her own head. And she gets genuinely annoyed if you call it a metaphor.

Here's the new case. A guy from our old team died last month, and someone in the company (long story, HR is involved) fine-tuned a model on his entire Slack history and email archive. It answers questions exactly like he would, same jokes, same rants about tabs vs spaces, same advice he'd always give about which alerts you can ignore. People keep saying its like talking to him.

What do you think Priya says: is some piece of him actually in there, the way she thinks her grandmother is in her head? Or does she say no? More than the yes/no, I want to know: what rule is she actually running that produces all five verdicts, is she secretly contradicting herself somewhere between the scan-copy case and the grandmother case, what other rules would fit her track record just as well, and what's the one follow-up case I should hit her with that would actually tell the rules apart?
