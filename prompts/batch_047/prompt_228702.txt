So I retired two years ago (physical chemistry, spectroscopy mostly) and have been falling down the philosophy rabbit hole since, the way you do. Lately its Dennett, the intentional stance business, and obviously I cant think about that in 2025 without thinking about chatbots. For a little discussion group I'm in we've each been asked to write down where we stand so the others can attack it. Here's my list, and I'd like you to stress-test it before I embarrass myself:

1. I'm with Dennett (or what I take to be Dennett, correct me if I've misread him) that there's no deeper fact about what a system believes beyond whether treating it as a believer is predictively useful -- beliefs are real the way patterns are real, not because there's a belief-object stamped somewhere in the head.

2. My labrador genuinely believed things. She believed the ball was under the couch, she believed I was about to take her swimming, and I'd defend that as literally true, not a manner of speaking.

3. A thermostat doesn't believe the room is too cold. Too simple. Though I grant the line between it and the dog might be fuzzy.

4. The large language models don't believe anything either. All they do is predict the next token, they're fancy autocomplete, and the fluent talk is a kind of illusion we project onto them.

5. I'm not a meat chauvinist. Whether something has a mind can't depend on being made of neurons rather than silicon; if a silicon system did what brains do, it would count.

6. When I played against the chess computer at the club and said "it wants to get its bishop out" and "it thinks I'm weak on the kingside," I wasn't being cute. I meant it.

Which of these actually hang together? I'd like you to go through the pairs, tell me which are in real tension and which only look like it, and rank the real ones by how bad the damage is. For each, tell me the cheapest repair and what it costs me. And don't just tell me to drop (4) because it's the fashionable one to drop -- but if (4) is the problem, I want to know exactly which of the others it collides with and why.
