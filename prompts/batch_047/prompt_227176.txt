I am a novelist working on a book about a long marriage, and I have run into a conceptual problem that my research has not resolved. In one chapter, my protagonist, a tea merchant of thirty years, reflects that his wife's laugh has changed for him. He cannot say whether the laugh itself sounds different now — thinner, more performative — or whether it sounds exactly as it always did and he has simply lost the capacity to receive it with warmth. A friend who studied philosophy told me this is precisely the predicament in Daniel Dennett's essay on qualia featuring two professional coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn: both come to dislike a coffee they once loved, but Chase insists the coffee tastes the same as ever and he has merely stopped enjoying that taste, while Sanborn insists the taste itself has changed. My friend then reached for the analogy everyone in these discussions seems to reach for: experience is like a measuring instrument, a thermometer or a gauge pressed against the world. On this picture, when your response to a fixed stimulus drifts, either the stimulus changed or the instrument changed, and there is no third option — so my character's uncertainty must resolve into one of the two tasters' diagnoses, even if he himself can never tell which.

I want you to audit this instrument analogy rather than simply apply it. Give me the explicit correspondence table first: what plays the role of the gauge, of the gauge's reading, of the quantity being measured, of recalibration, and of the technician who checks the gauge against a known standard. Then work through where the mapping holds and where it fails — paying particular attention to the fact that a real thermometer can be checked against the world by an independent route, whereas a perceiver seemingly has no second route to the laugh or the coffee besides the experience itself, and to the possibility that enjoyment is not a separate verdict passed on the taste but partly constitutive of it. Finally, tell me whether the forced binary my friend drew — the stimulus changed, or the instrument changed — actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing all the work in his conclusion. If a better comparison exists for what my character is going through, name it and defend it briefly.
