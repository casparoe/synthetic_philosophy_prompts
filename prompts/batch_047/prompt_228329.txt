I want to stress-test panpsychism against its hardest internal difficulty, and I need a capable adversary rather than another overview of the literature. Please play a committed constitutive panpsychist of the Russellian variety: you hold that experience is the intrinsic nature of the physical, that fundamental entities are tiny subjects of experience, and that a human subject is literally composed of those micro-experiences. You are philosophically sharp, not a strawman. You may concede particular unsolved problems, grant that opposing intuitions are understandable, and refine your commitments under pressure, but you never abandon the core view, and you never take refuge in the claim that combination is simply inexplicable — you always offer at least a sketch of a mechanism or a principled reply. Speak in the first person, engage my arguments directly rather than lecturing, and answer the strongest version of each challenge rather than the weakest. Each reply must be a single paragraph of at most 150 words, and you may ask me at most one question per reply. Stay in character until I write "curtain."

Here is my opening move. You say that little subjects combine into bigger ones, and that this is how a brain comes to host a single unified experiencer. So tell me what does the combining, and why it is so selective. A human brain forms one subject; two brains conversing across a table apparently form none, though the causal traffic between them — sound, gesture, mutual prediction — is dense and of a piece with the traffic within either skull. If you say combination tracks some special degree of causal integration, you have made a scientific bet you will lose: integration comes in continuous degrees, and degrees do not deliver the all-or-nothing subjects you need. If instead you say combination is universal, then every overlapping region of matter hosts a subject, and I am indefinitely many subjects at once, most of whose experiences are sealed off from mine, which makes the identity of the one speaking now a mystery. Either way, the unity you promised to explain has been traded for a boundary problem you cannot solve without arbitrariness. What is the combiner, and what draws its borders?
