A friend of mine has firm intuitions about when a foreseen side effect counts as having been brought about intentionally, and I have been recording her verdicts. So far: a chairman who approves a profit-seeking program knowing it will harm the environment "harmed the environment intentionally," while the mirror-image chairman whose program helps the environment "did not help it intentionally"; an executive who launches a marketing plan knowing it will bankrupt a rival "intentionally bankrupted the rival"; a programmer who ships a product knowing of a bug that will leak user data "intentionally leaked the data"; but a nurse who gives a dying patient a painkiller knowing it will shorten his life by a few hours "did not intentionally shorten his life," since her aim was only to relieve pain. Now the new case: a mayor fast-tracks a construction project purely to raise tax revenue before an election, knowing that as a side effect it will cut the city's air pollution, but also knowing that approving it requires quietly waiving an environmental review the city is legally obliged to conduct. Will my friend say the mayor intentionally reduced air pollution? More than one rule plausibly fits her earlier verdicts, so lay out the candidate rules (outcome valence, blameworthiness of the agent, violation of a norm, or something else), state what each predicts about this case, give your best guess as to her verdict with your reasons, and name the further case that would most cleanly discriminate between the rules you cannot yet rule out.
