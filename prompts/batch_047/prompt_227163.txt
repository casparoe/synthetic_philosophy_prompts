I run compliance for a mid-sized charity that funds health and education projects in twelve countries, and my staff keep bringing me borderline cases that our anti-bribery policy doesn't settle: a village committee gives our country director a goat at a farewell ceremony; a ministry clerk hints that our registration will sit in a drawer unless we pay a 'processing fee' that appears on no schedule; a local partner proposes hiring the regional governor's niece, who is genuinely the best-qualified candidate; a mayor we work with has steered a sanitation project towards wards that voted for him, which he describes as keeping faith with his supporters. Write me a decision procedure my field officers can actually run -- a sequence of questions with stopping points -- that distinguishes gifts from bribes, facilitation payments from legitimate fees, and patronage from ordinary political responsiveness. The procedure should be philosophically literate about the debate over whether corruption is a universal notion or a Western export: I want it to take seriously the possibility that some judgements are culturally relative (hospitality norms, obligations to kin and clan, the expectation that officials look after their own) while identifying where, if anywhere, the relativity runs out and a practice is corrupt on any reasonable account -- and it should say what the procedure should do with cases where those two deliver conflicting verdicts. Specify which distinctions the procedure treats as doing the real work (e.g., whether the benefit targets the person or the office, whether a specific quid pro quo is understood, whether the arrangement could survive publicity) and which familiar rules of thumb it should discard as culturally parochial. Then run the procedure on the four cases above, including a clear verdict on each, and identify at least one verdict my staff will find uncomfortable in each direction -- one clearance that will look bad to our European donors and one prohibition that local staff will find obtuse. Finally, mark the points where the procedure has to hand the decision up to me because the question is one of values rather than of classifiable fact. Please write in British English.
