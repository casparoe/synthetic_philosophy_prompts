I manage a network of six physiotherapy clinics, and I must decide which of two rehabilitation programmes for lower-back injuries — call them Programme A and Programme B — to adopt across the whole network next year. We have run both for the past eighteen months, with patients free to choose between them, and our analysts have produced recovery figures that have left the board divided.

In five of our six clinics, Programme B produced a higher proportion of patients reporting substantial recovery within twelve weeks. Yet when the figures are pooled across the entire network, Programme A comes out ahead by a comfortable margin. The explanation, so far as we can tell, is that Programme A was chosen disproportionately at our two specialist sports-injury clinics, which see younger patients with acute injuries who tend to recover quickly under almost any regime, whereas Programme B was chosen mostly at our general clinics, which serve older patients with chronic conditions.

Half the board insists the pooled figure is the honest one, because it reflects the network's actual overall results. The other half insists we should compare like with like within each clinic, and that the pooled figure is merely an artefact of where each programme happened to be offered. Before I commission a further study, I would like to settle the matter on the data we already have.

My question is therefore twofold. First, on the figures as described, which programme should we adopt — and does your answer change depending on whether patients will continue to self-select into programmes, as they do now, or whether we begin assigning them centrally at intake? Second, a colleague of mine claims there must be a purely statistical rule for deciding whether to trust the pooled figure or the clinic-by-clinic breakdown — some quantity one could compute from the numbers themselves that would tell us which comparison is the right one. Is she correct, or does the right choice depend on something the spreadsheet cannot show? Please give your recommendation and your reasoning, and write your answer in British English.
