I head the communications office of a national electoral commission, and part of my job is reviewing speeches, press releases, and official statements -- ours and, in an advisory capacity, ministers' -- for invocations of 'the will of the people' that overclaim what any democratic process can deliver: a 52–48 referendum presented as permanently settling an issue, a party with 37% of the vote claiming a mandate for a specific manifesto pledge, a prime minister citing polls as proof of what the people want. Write a decision procedure my reviewers can apply to any such statement: the questions to ask in order, what counts as passing each, and where the procedure stops with a flag rather than a verdict. The procedure should preserve, in usable form, the distinctions the literature on populism and democratic legitimacy actually turns on -- a majority of voters versus a majority of the electorate versus a plurality; a mandate to govern versus authorization for one policy; the people as an empirically aggregated electorate versus the moralized, unified 'people' of populist rhetoric -- and it should say explicitly which distinctions had to be simplified or dropped to keep the checklist runnable in under an hour, and where judgment cannot be eliminated. Then run the procedure on the three cases above, including one where it yields an answer my office will find politically uncomfortable.
