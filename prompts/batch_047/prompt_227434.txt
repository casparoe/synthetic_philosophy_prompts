Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two historians of the French Revolution: one holding the classic social interpretation (1789 was a bourgeois revolution, driven by class struggle between a rising capitalist bourgeoisie and a privileged feudal aristocracy) and one holding the revisionist interpretation (the Revolution was primarily a political crisis of the state, driven by ideology and contingency, not by a coherent class project). The statement should have three parts: (1) a numbered list of claims BOTH can actually sign -- things like what the revisionist economic history did and didn't show about the bourgeoisie, what everyone agrees happened in 1789-94 at the level of events, and what each side concedes the other got right; (2) the remaining disagreements, stated so precisely that each side would accept your wording of its own position (this is where you have to separate genuine disagreement from the stuff that's just two different uses of words like 'bourgeois' or 'cause'); (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions, like whether the Terror was a product of circumstances or of the revolutionary discourse itself, where settling the question would actually force one side to give ground, plus whatever evidence both agree would bear on them. Neither side gets to strawman the other, and if you think part of the debate is really about what a 'cause' of a revolution even is, say so. Max 750 words.
