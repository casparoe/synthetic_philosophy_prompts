Cosmologists do something kind of funny. When a theory of the universe — a particular inflationary model, say, or a picture of an eternally lingering vacuum — predicts that the overwheming majority of observers with my exact current experiences are Boltzmann brains (freak thermal fluctuations that pop into existence for a split second with a full set of false memories, rather than ordinary evolved creatures on planets), they treat that as a near-knockdown reason to throw the theory out, or at least to throw out the prescription it uses to compute probabilities. Models get called pathological, measures get ruled out, papers treat avoiding a Boltzmann-brain-dominated cosmology as a basic adequacy condition. But the same physicists would roll their eyes at the suggestion that we should reject a physical theory because it's compatible with my being a brain in a vat or the plaything of Descartes' demon. In one case "your theory predicts I'm probably a deluded fluctuation" ends careers; in the other it's dorm-room philosophy.

What does the Boltzmann-brain veto actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology: what has to be true about self-locating belief, typicality assumptions, and confirmation for the inference "the theory says most observers like me are BBs, so if it were true I'd probably be one, so it's probably false" to be legitimate evidence against a theory? Is the practice consistent with the profession's shrug at classic skeptical hypotheses, and if not, is there a principled difference — maybe that the BB scenario is a positive high-measure prediction rather than a bare logical possibility, or that a BB-dominated theory undermines its own empirical support in a way vat-scenarios don't? Also, is the veto really forced on us, or could a cosmologist keep the theory and just conditionalize on not being a BB, and what would concretely have to change in the practice (which models count as viable, how the measure problem gets handled) if that stance were adopted instead? And say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: which parts strictly require something like a typicality principle, and which parts just sit well with one while being licensable by several rival views of anthropics.
