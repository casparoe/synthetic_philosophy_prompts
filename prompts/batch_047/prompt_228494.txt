My friend and I got into an argument a few weeks ago that we couldn't resolve, and I want to know whether philosophers already have a name for the thing we were stuck on.

Here's the setup. We both used to love the same brand of ginger soda, and now neither of us drinks it. She says the soda itself tastes different to her than it used to — that the actual flavor experience changed, that the sweetness turned harsh. I say the flavor is exactly what it always was, and what changed is me: the same taste that used to please me now strikes me as cloying. So far that's just two competing explanations, and either could be true.

But then it got strange. She admitted she couldn't actually tell, from the inside, that the taste itself changed rather than her reaction to it. And I had to admit the mirror image: when I take a sip and dislike it, I can't point to some pure, fixed flavor that has stayed constant while my attitude toward it shifted. The taste and the disliking arrive as one package, and neither of us can peel them apart, not even for our own case. We have memories, but comparing a present sip to a remembered sip is just comparing two experiences, and the memory might be the thing that drifted.

A teacher suggested this was close to the inverted spectrum, but I don't think that's right. The inverted spectrum is about whether your red is my green — a possible difference between two people that is at least stable. Our problem is within one person over time, and it's worse than disagreement: we can't separate the experience from our verdict on it, so we can't even say what evidence would settle which of us is right.

So my questions. First, does this phenomenon — the impossibility, even for the person having the experience, of distinguishing a change in the experience itself from a change in the standards or reactions brought to it — have an established name? I don't just want a label. I want to know how it's distinguished from three nearby things: (a) the inverted spectrum, (b) ordinary acquired tastes, where surely something real changes and nobody is confused about what, and (c) cases like wine experts who say their palate became more refined, where we normally assume the expert detects a difference the novice genuinely misses — which seems to presuppose that experience and judgment come apart after all. Second, if there is no established name, propose one, define it precisely, and give me its edges: which everyday cases fall inside it and which fall outside. Third, and this is the part I care most about: is this merely a practical measurement problem — we lack good introspective instruments — or does it show there is no fact of the matter about which story is true? My friend insists her taste really changed, and I realized I couldn't even tell her what she'd be wrong about if she were mistaken. I'd like to know whether that failure is mine or the world's.
