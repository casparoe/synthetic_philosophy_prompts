Two things I keep noticing and I can't tell if they're the same phenomenon. First: last week a friend visiting from Europe refused to tip at dinner, on principle -- he thinks tipping is a rotten system and employers should just pay properly. The weird part is everyone else at the table agreed with him about the system, including the guy who then angrily threw down extra cash to cover it and called him a jerk. When my friend said "you literally just said the system shouldn't exist," the guy goes "that's not the point." Second: a coworker thinks our local escalator rule (stand right, walk left) is dumb because it halves capacity, and she's probably right, but she still gets visibly annoyed when tourists stand on the left. She'll rant about the rule at lunch and then enforce it with her eyes the same afternoon.

So the pattern is: getting sincerely angry at someone for breaking a convention you yourself think shouldn't exist. It's not hypocrisy, because they'll cheerfully admit the rule is bad if you ask. It's also not that they've been fooled into thinking everyone else loves the rule -- my tablemate knew the whole table agreed with the European guy. But the anger doesn't feel fake or conflicted to them, it feels completely appropriate, and the "that's not the point" seems to be doing real work.

Is there a name for this? People keep telling me it's "just how norms work," which isn't a name. If there's something established, how is it different from plain hypocrisy and from just being confused? And if there isn't, what would you call it -- and does it make a difference that my friend is open about his reasons and would happily see the whole system burn, versus someone who just doesn't feel like tipping that night?
