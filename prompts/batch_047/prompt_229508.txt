I run a tiny podcast where I talk to researchers about social science findings that went viral, mostly because I get annoyed at how the pop coverage flattens everything. Next week's guest is a psychologist whose new paper argues that the gender-equality paradox (the thing where countries that score highest on gender equality have MORE women-avoiding-STEM, men-dominated trades, etc., not less) is best explained by evolved, on-average sex differences in interests that get expressed more freely once money pressure and discrimination ease off -- basically the "in rich equal societies people can afford to be themselves" story.

She's media-trained and very slippery, so I need help. Draft 8-10 interview questions that don't let her just restate the finding. Specifically I want:

- at least one question on the obvious tension: if the differences are built in and stable, why do they shrink and grow across countries and across decades at all -- what exactly does her theory predict about cultural change speed vs. biology speed
- one on whether "free choice" is doing too much work -- how do you tell a choice that's free because constraints are gone from a choice that's shaped by subtler stereotypes that wealthy countries might be *better* at installing
- one on what would actually falsify her view, since "people express their true preferences when they can" sounds unfalsifiable the way she usually phrases it
- and one on the policy upshot, because I suspect her answer commits her to saying current gaps in egalitarian countries are basically fine, and I want her to either say that out loud or squirm

For each question give me the follow-up I'd ask when she gives the answer she's most likely to give. Then end with a last line starting "Verdict:" naming the number of the single question you think is hardest for her to dodge and why in a few words.
