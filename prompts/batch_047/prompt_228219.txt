Two friends of mine got into a surprisingly heated argument last weekend and I want a verdict. Our mutual friend Dana donated a kidney to a stranger through a non-directed donation program. Over drinks, Marcus said what she did wasn't just good, it was morally required: she had two working kidneys, the risk to her was low, someone was dying, and if it's the best thing she could do then calling it "above and beyond" is just a way of letting the rest of us off the hook. His line was basically that the category of supererogation is incoherent -- either she had most reason to do it, in which case it was obligatory, or she didn't, in which case donating wasn't actually the right thing.

Jess pushed back that this makes morality insanely demanding, since on Marcus's view we should all be donating kidneys and giving away most of our money, and nobody can live like that. Marcus said that confuses what's wrong with what's blameworthy -- maybe morality requires the kidney but we're not justified in condemning people who don't, so the demandingness is real and we should just be honest about it. Jess conceded that was a better distinction than she'd expected, but said there's still something off: morality has to leave room for people to weigh their own projects and commitments, and Dana's donation is admirable precisely because it was optional -- she could have permissibly declined, which is why we praise her rather than just noting she did her duty. Marcus said "agent-centered prerogative" talk is just a polite name for permission to be selfish, and that Jess is confusing what we can get away with socially with what's actually permitted. Jess's last move was that if everything best is obligatory, the concept of a moral hero collapses, and any theory that can't distinguish Dana from someone who merely pays their taxes has lost something important. Then they both looked at me.

Please go through the exchange move by move: who's right about what, which concessions were too quick or too slow, what both of them are missing, and who's more right overall about whether supererogation is a coherent category.
