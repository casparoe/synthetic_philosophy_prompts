My opening move, which your first reply must answer in character: the paradox strikes me as a technical artifact rather than a dilemma for liberalism. It arises only because the framework counts every preference anyone has about anything -- including what I wear, read, or do in private -- as a legitimate input to the social ordering. Once you launder preferences and exclude the meddlesome ones, or simply restrict the domain of social choice to matters of genuine public concern, the conflict between the Pareto condition and minimal liberty disappears. A liberalism that ignores nosy preferences was never at risk, so the result shows at most that we must be careful about what we feed into a social welfare function, not that there is any deep clash between rights and unanimity. Why isn't that the correct moral?
