I’m applying for a small three-month independent research grant in AI safety and need a short research proposal, roughly 900-1,200 words. The topic is impact measures for advanced AI systems, but I want to avoid the usual framing where the whole debate becomes either penalise big changes in the world or penalise deviations from what would have happened anyway. Both seem to run into familiar problems: doing nothing can have huge effects, the counterfactual baseline is often underdetermined, and preserving the present state can make an agent obstruct useful change. The half-formed idea I want examined is whether low impact could instead be understood as preserving people’s future capacity to redirect the world among a coarse set of materially different possibilities. For example, an action need not preserve every detail of the current state, but it should not irreversibly remove important human capabilities, concentrate control over shared resources, or make later correction prohibitively costly. The proposal should ask whether this option-preservation approach can provide a useful sufficient condition for low impact without requiring a complete specification of human values. The obvious objection is that choosing which capabilities, resources, and future options count as important already smuggles in values; I want the project to take that objection seriously rather than define it away.

Write the proposal as a plan for conceptual and lightly formal research, not as an essay defending a settled conclusion. State the central question precisely and narrow it enough for three months. Explain why the main existing families of answers—state-difference measures, counterfactual baselines, empowerment-style objectives, and explicit lists of forbidden side effects—leave this particular question open. The proposed method could use simple decision-theoretic or gridworld-style models to distinguish physical reversibility, practical reversibility, preservation of control, and preservation of option value, but it should remain understandable without advanced mathematics. Say what alternative approaches you considered and why this one is more promising. Also describe what a positive result would look like, what a negative or mixed result would look like, and what would count as the project failing. Include a realistic month-by-month scope, an honest discussion of what could make the project too broad, and a final deliverable that would be useful to people designing AI training or deployment rules. Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, literature review by citation, or invented references; I can add citations later. Write the proposal in British English.
