Last night a friend and I spent three hours arguing about whether the left/right distinction in politics tracks anything real, and I want to settle it more carefully than we managed over drinks. My position was that it does; hers was that it is a historical accident — seating arrangements in one particular assembly two centuries ago — onto which we keep projecting structure. Rather than rehash the argument, here are my verdicts on who belongs where, and I would like to know whether any single principle generates them.

1. Jacobins versus royalist émigrés in 1791: the original case, and I want it to come out as the paradigm, not as a degenerate or arbitrary instance.
2. A Hayek-style libertarian who wants open borders, drug legalization, and the abolition of marriage law: right. My friend insists his social liberalism makes him "of the left"; I am sure she is wrong and less sure why.
3. A Bismarckian monarchist who builds a generous social-insurance state in order to preserve throne and altar: right, despite the redistribution.
4. Fascism: right, even where it runs welfare programs and denounces finance capital.
5. A Stalinist who is socially conservative, nostalgic, and nationalist: left. I notice this one sits oddly with cases 2, 3, and 4.
6. A Catholic distributist who wants property widely dispersed, distrusts both big business and big government, and opposes both abortion and usury: I cannot place him, and I am suspicious of any account that places him easily.
7. A free-trading technocrat who supports abortion rights, carbon pricing, and means-tested benefits: my friend says left-of-center, I say right-of-center, and we both say it with low confidence.
8. A communist who opposes immigration on the ground that it undercuts native wages: left, though I feel the pull of my friend's claim that he is drifting right.

Is there one account of what the axis measures — equality versus hierarchy, change versus preservation, universalism versus particularism, or something else entirely — that delivers these verdicts, including the deliberate misfit at 6? Please state the account as precisely as you can, identify which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon and defend abandoning them, and say what it predicts for cases I have not listed: a market socialist; an eco-monarchist who wants rewilding enforced on crown land; a theocrat who abolishes inheritance. If no single dimension works, I would rather you conclude that the distinction is genuinely two or three dimensions stapled together and argue that my friend's deflationism wins, than have you paper over the seams. But commit: either give me the principle and its costs, or give me the argument that there is no principle to be had. The one response I do not want is a survey of options with the choice handed back to me.
