Alright, background: I retired two years ago after about forty years as an experimental physicist (condensed matter, lots of precision measurement), and since then I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole, as one does. Recently I've been chewing on so-called spectrum arguments and I keep going around in circles. I'd like someone to explain the conceptual issue properly, deeper than the summary-level version I keep finding.

Here's the version that bothers me. Take a cup of coffee with zero grains of sugar, and a cup with ten thousand grains. Adjacent cups in the series differ by one grain, which is way below my threshold of taste -- for any two neighbors I'm genuinely indifferent, couldn't tell them apart blind. So: cup 0 and cup 1, indifferent; cup 1 and cup 2, indifferent; and so on. Yet I'm obviously not indifferent between the endpoints; one is coffee and the other is syrup. Chain it together with transitivity and you get a contradiction. Fine. But where exactly is the fault?

I can think of several places to put the blame and I can't tell which one the philosophers are actually arguing about. (a) Maybe "better than" (or "at least as good as") just isn't transitive, full stop. (b) Maybe transitivity is fine but indifference-based-on-indistinguishibility was never a value relation to begin with, so chaining it tells you nothing about betterness. (c) Maybe imperceptible differences can be evaluatively relevant after all, so the premise that each adjacent pair is equally good is simply false. Is there actually a live disagreement between these, or are they notational variants of each other? And what does it cost to hold each one?

Two more things I'd like explained, because they connect to my day-job instincts. First: does anything change if you construct the spectrum so each step *is* perceptible but tiny in value (say, one extra minute of mild headache somewhere down the line, or the population-ethics versions where you trade a bit of average quality for a lot of quantity)? Is that the same problem wearing different clothes, or does the diagnosis have to change once perception drops out of it? Second: in physics we'd say the measurement noise is just noise around a real value -- the true mass exists, my instrument's resolution is my problem. Is the analogous move available here, i.e. there's a true ranking and my discriminative capacities are just a lousy instrument? Or does that analogy break down for value, and if so, why exactly?
