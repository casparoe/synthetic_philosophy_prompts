I read a piece in a science magazine about the psi-ontic vs psi-epistemic debate and I want to know how much of it to trust. It summarizes the debate like this: "Physicists disagree about whether the wavefunction is a real thing. On one side, psi-ontic folks think the wavefunction is a physical object out there in the world, like the electromagnetic field, which literally collapses when you make a measurement. On the other side, psi-epistemic folks think the wavefunction is just a summary of our ignorance about what the particle is really doing, the same way saying a coin flip is 50/50 just describes our ignorance of which way it will land. The Pusey-Barrett-Rudolph theorem from 2012 settled the argument by proving the wavefunction has to be real, which is why psi-epistemic interpretations like QBism have been abandoned." Could you go through this and tell me which parts a foundations person would accept and which they'd push back on? I'm especially unsure about the coin-flip analogy -- if the wavefunction were just ignorance like that, wouldn't quantum mechanics be basically classical probability? -- and about whether PBR really settled things as cleanly as the article claims. Also, if parts of the summary lump together positions that should be kept apart under the same label, point that out.
