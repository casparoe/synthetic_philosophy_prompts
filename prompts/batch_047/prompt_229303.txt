A friend who recently took a sociology course summarized Weber's argument in The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism for me like this: 'Weber's claim was that capitalism arose because Protestantism taught hard work, frugality, and saving as religious duties, so Protestants simply invented the capitalist spirit that hadn't existed before. His main evidence was that Protestant regions industrialized faster than Catholic ones, from which he concluded that a Catholic could never develop the capitalist mindset. The book is essentially a refutation of Marx: where Marx thought material conditions determine ideas, Weber showed that ideas determine material conditions. And although capitalism eventually stopped needing religion, the Protestant ethic itself survives today as a secular habit of diligence.' I have a vague sense that Weber's actual thesis was more hedged than this, but I can't say where. Please go through the summary claim by claim: which parts would a careful reader of Weber accept, which conflate his actual mechanism with something cruder, which overstate a probabilistic claim as a categorical one, and which misdescribe what he took modern capitalism to have become? And once the summary is corrected, does the 'refutation of Marx' framing survive at all, or does Weber's argument turn out to be compatible with most of what a materialist wants to say?
