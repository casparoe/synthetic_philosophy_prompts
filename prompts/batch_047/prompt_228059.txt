I'm a software engineer (no formal physics or philosophy training) trying to get a grip on what, exactly, a satisfactory account of the thermodynamics of information would have to accomplish. I've read enough popular and semi-technical material to know the standard pieces -- Szilárd's single-molecule engine, Maxwell's demon, Landauer's claim that erasing a bit has a minimum heat cost, and Bennett's well-known resolution in which the demon's memory, rather than its measurement, carries the entropy bill. What I don't have is a way to tell whether these pieces actually add up to a theory, or merely to a family of suggestive thought experiments plus one disputed inequality.

So, before I read any more: write the desiderata. What must any adequate theory of the physics of information-processing deliver in order to count as adequate for the purpose of telling me, as an engineer, which real or idealized computational devices have unavoidable thermodynamic costs and which don't? For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Some desiderata I suspect belong on the list, and I'd like you to confirm, refine, or reject each:

- It must say which operations are charged and which are free, in a way that lines up with the logical structure of the operation rather than its physical implementation -- or explain why the logical/physical distinction can't do that work.
- It must handle erasure of redundant information: if I erase a bit that is a copy of a bit I keep, intuition says the cost should differ from erasing the only record of something. A theory that prices these identically owes an explanation.
- It must handle partial and probabilistic knowledge: a demon that knows the molecule is on the left with probability 0.7 should have a well-defined work-extraction story, not just the all-or-nothing cases the classic thought experiments use.
- It must handle memory compression as distinct from erasure: a demon that defragments its memory -- keeps the information but stores it more compactly -- seems to do something logically different from resetting, and the theory should say whether that something is free, costly, or gains heat.
- It must say something about correlated records: if the demon's memory ends up correlated with the system rather than containing a clean record of it, where does the bookkeeping close?
- It must resolve the demon in a way that doesn't depend on the demon being made of any particular stuff (silicon, neurons, gears, photons), or admit that the resolution is substrate-dependent and live with the consequences.

Beyond adjudicating that list, I want three things. First, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which one you'd drop and why -- a list that is itself inconsistent is a finding, not a failure of the exercise. Second, mark each desideratum as non-negotiable, negotiable, or a prejudice of one camp dressed up as a requirement. Third, and only briefly, score the main existing stances against your final list: the orthodox Landauer-Bennett picture, skeptical views holding that the alleged erasure cost is an artifact of idealization rather than a law, and purely statistical-mechanical views that treat information talk as a redescription of entropy talk with no independent content. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scoring is secondary, and I'd rather have six well-argued requirements than a leaderboard.
