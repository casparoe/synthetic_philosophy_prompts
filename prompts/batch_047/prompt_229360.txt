Write a satirical corporate announcement, roughly 500-600 words, in the voice of a major AI lab's press release and accompanying FAQ, unveiling Consensus™, the first chatbot aligned not to any one user's values but to humanity's values, aggregated. The humor must come from the lab having taken the philosophy of value aggregation seriously and implemented it literally: Arrow-style preference cycles mean the assistant's positions on contested issues rotate on a published schedule; idealization is handled by a Department of Counterfactual Selves that computes what each user would want if fully informed, vividly imaginative, and free of bias, then bills them for it; the global headcount means Western early adopters are quietly outvoted; expected future persons get a voting bloc and immediately dominate all decisions; and the FAQ solemnly explains why interpersonal comparisons of wellbeing are "approximated using a proprietary exchange rate." Keep the corporate voice deadpan and reassuring throughout -- the lab must never notice anything is wrong. A reader finishing it should have felt, underneath the jokes, the real unresolved questions: whose values, aggregated by what rule, at what distance of idealization, and who audits the answer.
