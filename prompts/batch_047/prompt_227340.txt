I need help figuring out whether I've broken my code or broken probability theory. I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy or stats training, and I was messing around with a simulation of a betting game a friend pitched me over beers. The game: flip a fair coin until it comes up heads. If the first heads lands on flip n, the payout is 2^n divided by n dollars -- but with a twist: if n is even, you PAY that amount instead of winning it. So flip 1 pays +$2, flip 2 costs you $2, flip 3 pays +$8/3, flip 4 costs you $4, and so on, the stakes ballooning while the sign alternates. I wanted the fair entry fee, so I did what I always do and wrote the expected value as a sum. Each outcome has probability 1/2^n, the powers of two cancel, and the terms come out to +1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + ..., which I half-remembered is the alternating harmonic series, equal to about 0.693. So: fair price 69 cents, done, pub trivia solved.

Except then I actually ran the simluation, and the running average per trial does not settle at 0.693 or anywhere else. It hovers around small numbers for ages, then some freak trial hits a long run of tails before the first heads and the average gets yanked up or down by hundreds or thousands of dollars, and these kicks keep arriving no matter how many millions of trials I run, alternating in direction. My friend insists the sim must be buggy because "the math says ln 2." But I noticed something unsettling: if I group or reorder the terms of that infinite series differently -- which feels like it shouldn't matter, addition is commutative, right? -- I can make the sum come out to basically anything I want. So now I suspect the series only converges in some fragile, order-dependent way, and that the game might not have an expected value at all. Not even "infinity" like that famous St. Petersburg game, which at least gives you an answer of sorts.

So my question: does this situation have a name -- a game whose expectation isn't merely huge or infinite but fails to exist because the positive and negative parts each blow up on their own? I don't want just a bare label, though. What are the closest established concepts (the St. Petersburg paradox? heavy-tailed distributions with no defined mean, like that Cauchy thing? whatever the reordering phenomenon is called in calculus?), how does my game differ from each, and which pigeonhole does it actually belong in?
