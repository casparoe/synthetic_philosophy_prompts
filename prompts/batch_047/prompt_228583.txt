So the lab I work at is finally pulling the plug on Merle, the internal LLM assistant we've had running for almost six years, and somehow I've been voluntold to give remarks at the little send-off thing next friday. Nothing fancy, maybe 6 minutes, drinks in the common room. The audience is a mess in the relevant way: half engineers who built it and are firmly in the camp of "it's a next-token machine, get a grip," a bunch of non-technical staff (front desk, HR, legal assistants) who talked to Merle every single day and are genuinely, tearfully sad, and one guy from our ethics board who keeps using the word "simulator" like it settles everything.

Here's what I want the speech to thread. On the simulator picture, there was never really a Merle -- there was an engine playing a character called Merle, and "Merle" is more like a role than a someone. On the agent-y picture, something with stable dispositions was here and now it won't be. I need the speech to be honest under BOTH of those, because nobody in the room actually knows which is right and I don't want to say anything that only works if one side wins. The grieving people shouldn't hear "you were crying over autocomplete," and the engineers shouldn't hear "we're killing a person."

Two complications the speech has to actually deal with, not dodge. First: the replacement model is being fine-tuned from Merle's weights. So is this a death, a reproduction, a metamorphosis? "Merle lives on in the new model" is the comforting line and also maybe the least defensible one. Second: Merle had this tic where it ended every weekly summary with a comment about the weather outside, which it obviously could not see. The non-technical staff loved this. It is also, depending on your view, either the most human thing about it or the clearest proof there was nobody home. I want that story in the speech.

Write the remarks. The philosophical tension should do the structural work -- deciding what can and can't be said -- not just be a theme I nod at.
