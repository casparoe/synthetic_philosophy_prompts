ok so I've been reading around the verisimilitude / truthlikeness stuff for a reading group and I want a consistency check on where I've landed, because I have a nagging feeling it doesn't hang together and I can't localise the problem. Roughly my commitments:

1. Popper's original account of verisimilitude is dead. Tichý and Miller showed it entails that no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other false theory, which sinks it as an account of scientific progress.

2. All the same, some false theories genuinely are closer to the truth than others, full stop. Newtonian mechanics is closer to the truth than impetus physics not merely in the sense of being better confirmed or more useful, but in the sense of being nearer to how things actually are.

3. I also accept Miller's language-dependence point: truthlikeness comparisons aren't invariant under harmless-looking reaxiomatisations. Redefine your predicates cleverly enough and the ordering between false theories can flip, so there's no purely content-based, language-invariant measure of closeness to the truth.

4. I'm a scientific realist, and the version I want rests on convergence: successive theories in mature sciences increase in verisimilitude, and that's what makes their predictive success non-miraculous.

5. On rational belief I'm basicly a Bayesian. Credences, conditionalisation, the works.

Does this set of five cohere? Please audit it rather than lecture me: list the genuine tensions in order of seriousness, note any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and for each real one say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. I'm not asking which position in the literature to adopt, just which of my own commitments pull against each other.

Reply in British English, and keep the whole thing under 400 words.
