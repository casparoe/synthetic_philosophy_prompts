Consider a sharpened version of the evolutionary debunking argument against moral realism. Grant the realist both that there are stance-independent moral truths and that natural selection pushed human moral intuitions toward cooperation-promoting content. The debunker's residual claim is then narrower than the usual one: not that our moral beliefs are false, but that the realist cannot explain why the evolutionary forces shaping our intuitions would correlate with the moral truth without positing a connection -- a 'third factor' in Enoch's sense -- that is either a brute coincidence or ad hoc. Call this the correlation-explanation claim.

Give me your credence that the correlation-explanation claim is correct: that no non-coincidental, independently motivated explanation of the correlation is available to the realist. Decompose the estimate: the probability that evolutionary influence on our core moral intuitions is as pervasive as the argument requires; the probability that third-factor accounts genuinely dissolve the coincidence rather than rename it; the probability that the debunker faces a companions-in-guilt problem with our epistemic or logical beliefs serious enough to undercut the argument; and any other term you think belongs. A number and one or two lines of justification for each, then how they combine.

Finally, say how confident you are in the overall estimate, and identify the single sub-question whose resolution would shift your credence the most.
