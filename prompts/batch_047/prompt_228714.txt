Here's my take on when we count something as done intentionally, and I want to know what principle (if any) unifies my verdicts. My hunch going in is something like "foreseen + wanted, or at least not-wanted-but-accepted," but I think my own intuitions might already refute that.

Case 1: the chairman is told a new program will boost profits but harm the environment, says he doesn't care about the environment, greenlights it, profits go up, environment gets harmed. Verdict: he harmed the environment intentionally. Pretty confident.

Case 2: same chairman, same indifference, but the program is projected to *help* the environment, it does, and he couldn't care less. Verdict: he did NOT help the environment intentionally. Also pretty confident, even though his mental state is identical to case 1.

Case 3: company policy says nobody works weekends. The chairman is told a program will boost profits but will require weekend shifts, says he doesn't care about the policy, approves it. Verdict: he intentionally got people working weekends.

Case 4: company policy *requires* weekend shifts. Same chairman, same indifference, approves a profit program that happens to involve weekend work. Verdict: he did NOT intentionally get people working weekends. And yet case 3 and 4 have him in the same mental state with the same causal outcome.

Case 5: a skilled archer aims at a bullseye in a competition and hits it. Verdict: intentional. A total novice aims, the arrow ricochets off a tree branch and lands dead center. Verdict: she did NOT intentionally hit the bullseye, even though she aimed at it. So wanting plus foreseeing plus causing isn't enough either?? The novice wanted it and caused it.

Case 6: a kid deliberately throws a rock to break a window but aims badly; a gust of wind carries it in. Verdict: honestly torn. It feels more intentional than the novice archer but less than the skilled one, which makes me suspicious that "intentional" is secretly a graded notion and the whole exercise is doomed.

So: is there one principle that delivers 1-5, and what does it do with 6? My candidates are (a) the "side effect counts as intentional iff it violates a norm" story, which handles 1-4 but I'm not sure about 5; (b) a competence/skill clause bolted onto a desire-based account, which handles 5 but seems to whiff 1 and 3; (c) something pragmaticky where "intentionally" just means "attributable to the agent in a way relevant for praise/blame," which feels like giving up but maybe isn't. Tell me which of my verdicts has to go under your preferred principle, and what the principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: a CEO indifferent to a side effect that harms nobody but slightly embarrasses a rival, and a dog owner who lets his dog off leash knowing it will probably knock over a kid's sandcastle. End your answer with \answer{...} containing your final one-sentence statement of the principle.
