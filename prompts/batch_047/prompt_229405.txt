I read an ethics explainer that summarizes prioritarianism this way: “Prioritarianism is a compromise between utilitarianism and egalitarianism. It ranks outcomes by adding everyone’s well-being, but gives extra moral weight to gains and losses affecting people at lower absolute welfare levels. Unlike egalitarianism, it does not value equality for its own sake, so a change that lowers everyone’s welfare in exactly the same proportion leaves the situation equally good. The leveling-down objection is that, because prioritarianism cares only about the worse-off, it must favor making everyone equally miserable whenever that increases the priority assigned to the worst-off.” Which parts would a careful prioritarian accept, which are conflations or overstatements, and does the leveling-down objection actually work against the view as summarized?
