I'm a third-year undergraduate applying for an eight-week summer research fellowship in philosophy, and I need a two-page proposal to submit with the application. My project would be on the so-called paradox of deontology: constraints tell me not to kill even when killing one would prevent five other killings, and that seems paradoxical -- if killings are bad, fewer of them should be better, so the constraint appears to forbid the outcome the constraint's own rationale ranks highest. I've spent the term reading around the standard responses -- agent-relative values, the inviolability or status of persons, second-personal reasons -- and in our reading group I kept having the same half-formed thought: the paradox only gets going if we individuate the relevant states of affairs without reference to agency. A world in which I kill one and a world in which five strangers kill are, from the deliberative standpoint of the agent deciding, not two quantities of one bad stuff; they differ in kind, because one contains something done by me and the other contains things that merely happen. My suspicion is that the paradox is less a contradiction inside deontology than the symptom of smuggling an outcome-ranking picture into a theory whose basic unit is the agent's deed -- and that once that's made explicit, the interesting question shifts to whether the smuggled picture can be independently motivated or is just consequentialism assumed in the premises. I want this turned into a proper proposal rather than a hunch.

Act as a professional philosopher in normative ethics who has supervised dissertations and sat on fellowship committees -- you know what makes a proposal sound fundable versus half-baked.
