I'm a PhD student in epistemology and I'm TAing a course where we do the regress problem. I want to give the undergrads a one-page flowchart that walks them from "every justified belief is either justified by another belief or it isn't" to a position on the regress -- foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism, or skepticism about justification. The problem is every textbook tree I've seen just asks things like "do you think justification can be circular?", which sorts people by which label sounds nice rather than by what they actually think.

So please build me a decision tree where each node is a concrete question or mini-case that someone can answer without knowing the jargon -- e.g., something like "could a belief you have no reason for still make your other beliefs rational, or does that just spread the unreason?" rather than "are you a foundationalist?". I'd like the tree to include:

- a node that splits modest/fallibilist foundationalism (basic beliefs can be merely probable) from the classical indubitability version, since my students always conflate them
- a node that separates holistic coherentism from just... a circle with extra steps, because that's the objection they all raise
- infinitism as a real leaf, with the question that makes it seem live rather than a joke option
- skepticism as the leaf where you refuse all the escape hatches, not as a node

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't cleanly separate, and note any combination of answers that's logically available but that nobody seems to actually hold (and your best guess why -- incoherent, or just unattractive?). Keep it to maybe 6-8 nodes so it fits on a handout.
