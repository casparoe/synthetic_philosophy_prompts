Pub-quiz philosopher here (the annoying kind, apparently). There's a pattern I've noticed in puzzles about doing infinitely many things in a finite stretch of time, and I want to know if anyone has actually given it a name.

Three versions I know of. One: the famous lamp that gets flicked on and off, each flick taking half as long as the one before, so infinitely many flicks are done by the two-minute mark — and then everyone argues about whether the lamp is on or off AT two minutes. Two: a bouncing ball where each bounce takes half the time of the previous one, and apparently it just comes to rest at the end, nobody much fussed. Three: a machine that prints the digits of pi, each digit twice as fast as the last, and people tie themselves in knots asking what's on the tape "when it's finished".

Here's the pattern as I see it. In every case the story painstakingly describes all the infinitely many steps and then just... stops, and the entire arguement takes place in the gap between "everything up to the final moment" and "the final moment itself". The lamp debaters argue as if the end state must somehow be squeezed out of the trend of the flicking. The ball people cheerfully let the end state come from somewhere else entirely (gravity, friction, whatever). The pi machine people get stuck because the trend (digits, forever) seems to positively forbid any final state at all. So the real action looks to me to be the same every time: whether you're allowed, or maybe required, to treat the state at the end as something the process has to settle into, versus something the story simply hasn't told you.

Is there a name for that gap, or for the assumption that there can't be one? I don't want a general tour of infinity puzzles — I want to know whether this specific thing (the final instant left unspecified while everyone argues as though it weren't) has been carved out as its own concept, and how it differs from nearby ideas. Note it can't just be "the sequence doesn't converge": the ball's positions DO converge and the lamp's on-off states don't, yet the ball case feels unpuzzling and the lamp case feels impossible, so convergence alone isn't doing the work. If there genuinely is no name, coin one, define it properly, and give me a couple of borderline cases it should and shouldn't cover so I can see the edges.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — either "yes, it's called X" or "no name exists, here's my coinage" — then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back at me.
