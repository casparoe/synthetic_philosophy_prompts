After a long argument with a friend, we are stuck on whether Wittgenstein’s On Certainty supports a substantive hinge epistemology or merely exposes a misunderstanding in skeptical and anti-skeptical arguments. For the purpose of settling that dispute, write the desiderata an adequate theory of hinges must meet: what hinges are if they are neither ordinary beliefs nor mere behavioral dispositions; how they can make doubt and inquiry possible without themselves being epistemically justified; how they relate to certainty, knowledge, evidence, and reasons; and how apparently fixed commitments can nevertheless change without being revised one proposition at a time.

For each requirement, give the motivating case or problem—radical skepticism, Moorean certainty, inductive practice, deep disagreement, conversion, and gradual conceptual change would all be useful. Include a requirement that the theory model both hinge inheritance, where later beliefs acquire their standing from an inherited background, and hinge drift, where a commitment’s role changes gradually from unquestioned scaffolding to contestable claim, or vice versa. Distinguish non-negotiable requirements from preferences characteristic of epistemic, therapeutic, naturalistic, or relativist readings of Wittgenstein. Most importantly, identify any desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—for example, explaining hinges’ exemption from doubt without making them arbitrary, or allowing rational criticism across hinge systems without assuming a neutral standpoint.
