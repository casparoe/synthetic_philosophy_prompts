My science & tech studies professor keeps issuing verdicts about whether technologies are "political" in lecture and I can't figure out the rule she's running. Her calls so far: Robert Moses's low overpasses over the Long Island parkways — political. The mechanical tomato harvester developed at UC Davis — political, and she stressed this was true even though the engineers just wanted a machine that picks tomatoes, no agenda. Nuclear power — political no matter what, even in a society that's otherwise totally egalitarian. But a ballpoint pen and an ordinary wooden chair — not political, just tools, end of story. Next week the case is the standardized shipping container. I say she'll call it not political, since it was designed to cut freight costs, not to do anything to anyone. My roommate insists she'll say political, because containerization gutted dockworker unions and decided which cities became major ports and which died. Who's right? Honestly I care less about the verdict than about the reconstruction: give me the rules that fit her earlier calls (I'm pretty sure there's more than one that fits), say what each predicts for the container, and tell me what other example would settle which rule she's actually using.
