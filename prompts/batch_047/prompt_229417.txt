I've been reading and occasionally commenting on rationalist blogs for well over a decade, and one distinction I keep reaching for is Dennett's cranes-versus-skyhooks one, along with his charge of greedy reductionism. But when I try to state the underlying principle precisely, my own case judgments don't line up cleanly. Here are my verdicts, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures them.

- Explaining the tides by lunar gravity: an unproblematic crane. Every link in the chain is independently understood.
- Explaining antibiotic resistance by random mutation and selection: a crane, and a good one — partly because the same machinery also predicts, in advance, why resistance was bound to appear wherever antibiotics are used.
- Explaining human altruism toward strangers by an innate moral sense: a skyhook. The mystery is just relocated into the genome, which now has to explain how the sense got there and why it has the content it does.
- Explaining the origin of life on Earth by panspermia from an older alien biosphere: also a skyhook, even though everything posited is perfectly physical. It doesn't explain life; it exports it.
- Explaining why anything exists at all, or why the laws are as they are, by terminating in fundamental laws that have no further explanation: not a skyhook. Every chain of explanation has to bottom out somewhere, and simple brute regularities seem like an acceptable floor.

The third and fourth verdicts against the fifth are the pair I can't reconcile. Both panspermia and brute-laws-termination respond to a why-question by deferring it; my gut says one is legitimate and the other isn't, and I can't say why without circularity. A further set:

- Explaining a grandmaster's move by her recognition of a familiar tactical motif: a fine explanation at its level. Explaining the same move by patterns of neural firing: technically true, but greedy — it skips the levels where the explanatory work lives.
- Explaining conscious experience by quantum effects in microtubules: a skyhook wearing a crane's clothing. The quantum mechanics is real physics, but nothing in the account connects it to experience, so the hard part is handed off to a mechanism precisely because that mechanism is murky.
- Explaining a market boom by the animal spirits of investors: I genuinely can't place this one. It could be lazy label-mongering, or legitimate shorthand for a real, if hard-to-formalize, psychological process.

My tentative principle: an explanation is a crane when it decomposes the explanandum into parts that are simpler than it, independently attested, and sufficient to generate it; an explainer is a skyhook when it is at least as complex or at least as improbable as what it explains, so that the explanation trades one mystery for a bigger one; and reductionism is greedy when it reaches for a lower level before the intermediate levels have been given their due — when the skipped levels are where the generalizations with predictive power actually live. Test this against my cases and repair it as needed. In particular: does it get the panspermia-versus-brute-laws asymmetry right, and can it say anything principled about the animal-spirits case? I'd also like its verdict on cases I haven't listed — for instance, explaining the apparent fine-tuning of physical constants by a multiverse with varied constants across regions, or explaining the success of science by a correspondence between the world and the structure of human reason. And tell me honestly which of my verdicts the best version of the principle cannot accommodate.
