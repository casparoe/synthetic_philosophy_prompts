I'm giving a 20-minute talk at an interdisciplinary workshop and want to prep for the Q&A. My claim: the absent-minded driver problem isn't just a cute paradox about imperfect recall, it's the right formal model for a big chunk of deployed AI systems -- think a content moderation model or a trading agent that gets spun up fresh millions of times with no memory of its past runs, facing the "same" decision over and over. I'll argue that the standard conflict in the literature between what you'd commit to ex ante and what seems rational at the moment of choice maps onto a real design tension: should we optimize the agent's policy from the designer's seat, or make each instance locally rational, and that you genuinely can't have both.

The audience: maybe 15 people. Three game theorists who know the imperfect-recall literature cold and think the whole paradox dissolves once you're careful about what a decision node is. A couple of ML systems people who'll be skeptical the toy model captures anything about real deployments (stochastic environments, non-identical situations, partial logging). And some philosophers of action who'll want to fight about whether "the agent" at the intersection is even the same decision-maker across instances.

Give me my prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get, ordered by likelihood x damage, each phrased the way that specific person would phrase it (the game theorists talk in terms of strategies and beliefs at information sets, the ML people in terms of distribution shift and logging). For each: what I should concede, what I should hold, a ~90 second reply, and where the reply is soft. Also flag the weakest link in my own argument so I don't get caught flat-footed, and tell me the question I'm praying nobody asks.
