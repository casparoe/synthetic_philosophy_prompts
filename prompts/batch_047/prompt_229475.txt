For my high school art class, I spent weeks shaping a single lump of clay into a sculpture that ended up winning a school competition. Now the art department wants to squash it flat so the clay can be reused by next year's students. When I objected, a classmate argued that nothing of value would really be destroyed: the sculpture, he said, just is the clay, and since the clay will still exist afterward, only a temporary arrangement is lost, not an actual thing. Another classmate disagreed, saying the sculpture and the lump must be two different things, because the sculpture can be destroyed while the clay survives, and one thing cannot have a property the other lacks. I find myself torn: the first argument seems to trivialize what I made, but the second seems to multiply objects beyond reason, as if two things could occupy the exact same space at once. Who is reasoning correctly here, and does settling the metaphysical question actually bear on whether it is acceptable for the school to squash my sculpture?
