Here's something I keep going back and forth on, and I want an actual number out of you, not a survey of who said what.

The claim: if we assign non-negligible credence to the hypothesis that we're living in a computer simulation, then the case for prioritizing far-future interventions (existential risk reduction, shaping civilization's long-run trajectory) gets significantly weaker relative to near-term causes. Call it the Dampening Claim. By "significantly weaker" I mean: in a straightforward expected-value ranking for a typical donor, the far-future stuff drops below the near-term stuff.

What's your credence in the Dampening Claim? Don't just hand me a number though -- decompose it. Roughly the terms I'd expect, though reorganize if you think my decomposition is wrong:

1. Your credence that we're actually sims (take the usual trilemma as background, I don't need it rehearsed)
2. Given we ARE sims, the probability that our influence on the "far future" is much less reliable than it looks -- the sim gets switched off, turns out to be a short-run ancestor reconstruction rather than an open-ended world, the physics get patched, etc.
3. Given we're sims, the probability that far-future work still pays off anyway because the simulators' interests line up with ours -- maybe the sim keeps running precisely because we stay interesting, or it's being run by our own descendants, in which case making the far future go well is arguably what keeps the lights on
4. Whether huge-stakes expected value math just swamps all these discounts regardless, i.e. whether a 10% "shutdown discount" even dents interventions whose payoffs are measured in 10^whatever future lives

Number plus one line of justification per term, then how you combine them. Then: which term is your final number most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would move that term the most? And if you think the whole framing is confused -- like maybe simulation credence should *raise* the priority of far-future work via term 3 rather than lower it -- say so, but still give me the number and tell me what it's actually tracking.
