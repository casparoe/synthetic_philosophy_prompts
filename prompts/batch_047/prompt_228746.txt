I'm a final-year undergraduate taking a course in ethics and global priorities, and I'm stuck on a decision that the course has made harder rather than easier. I have to choose between two paths after graduation: taking a high-paying job in quantitative trading and donating a large share of my income to effective charities, or working directly for a small nonprofit on pandemic preparedness. When I tried to compare the two by estimating the good each would do, my flatmate, a philosophy grad student, told me the whole exercise is hopeless: whatever I choose will ripple outward, affecting the economy, other people's careers, and eventually people who don't yet exist, in ways I couldn't possibly forecast, so my expected-impact comparison is just noise dressed up as precision. His advice was to pick whichever path feels meaningful to me. A careers adviser gave the opposite recommendation: rough cost-effectiveness estimates are the best information I have, so I should act on them even if they're crude. Something about my flatmate's argument seems right — my long-run effects really are unpredictable — but it also seems to prove too much, since by that logic every significant decision anyone makes is arbitrary, and nobody should ever trust any planning. Please tell me who I should actually listen to and how I should make this decision. I'd like a definite verdict with reasons, not a survey of the possible positions.
