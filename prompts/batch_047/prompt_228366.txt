I've been thinking about the preface paradox, in which an author rationally believes each individual claim in her book while also rationally believing that the book contains at least one error. As far as I can tell, this is simply the lottery paradox restated with sentences in place of tickets, so any adequate solution to one should automatically carry over to the other. Given that, why do epistemologists continue to treat them as two distinct puzzles? Is there a genuine difference in their structure that would allow a solution to one to fail for the other, or is discussing both merely a historical accident?
