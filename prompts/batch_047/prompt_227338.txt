I'm trying to sort out when it's okay to believe something without solid evidence, going by gut on cases. A coach believing her mediocre team can win the championship, where the belief itself pumps them up: fine. Believing your spouse is faithful despite a genuinely suspicious text message: not fine. A friend sticking by a buddy accused of theft when the evidence is mixed: fine, maybe even admirable. Someone becoming convinced God exists partly because life feels unbearable otherwise: I want to say fine, but it bugs me that this is structurally a lot like the spouse case. Believing a new experimental drug will cure you because optimism might help: fine. A juror convicting on a hunch that the defendant is "the type": obviously not fine. Believing a stranger will repay a small loan with no track record: fine. Believing the same stranger will repay a huge one: not fine. Is there one principle that lands all of these, or do I have to give up the God one or the spouse one to stay consistent? I'd especially like to know whether "the belief helps cause the thing believed" is really doing the work, or whether that's a red herring and something else is.
