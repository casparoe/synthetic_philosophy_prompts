Two senior structural engineers, Mara and Jonas, have near-identical training, comparable track records, and access to the same inspection data for an aging suspension bridge. Working independently, Mara concludes that cable corrosion warrants closing the bridge within a year; Jonas concludes it can operate safely for at least another decade. They then walk each other through their reasoning in full. Each understands the other's analysis completely, neither can point to a step where the other went wrong, and each remains fairly confident in their own conclusion. Jonas, after hearing Mara out, does not budge at all.

Mara is now trying to figure out what she is in a position to believe, and what they should tell the city, which requires a single joint recommendation. She also knows something Jonas does not: she did her analysis across two sleepless nights during a brutal week, whereas he worked rested—though she has since rechecked her calculations and they hold up.

Her questions to you:

1. Should Jonas's disagreement lower Mara's confidence, and if so, toward his conclusion specifically, or merely away from her original one? Does the answer change given that she has already examined his reasoning and found no flaw?
2. Does Jonas's total unwillingness to move after hearing her case give Mara grounds to downgrade her estimate of his judgment—or would doing so be an illegitimate second use of the very judgment his disagreement is supposed to call into question?
3. Her sleeplessness is a fact about herself, not about him. May she discount her own reasoning on grounds of compromised conditions in a way she could never symmetrically discount his, or does epistemic fairness require treating herself as just another expert whose track record she knows?
4. Whatever they each end up believing, they must issue one recommendation, and the costs of the two errors (needless closure versus catastrophic failure) are wildly asymmetric. How, if at all, should that shape the recommendation relative to their post-disagreement judgments?

I am not asking for a verdict on the engineering. I want to know what rationality actually demands of Mara here, and whether any consistent set of answers to these four questions survives contact with all of them at once.
