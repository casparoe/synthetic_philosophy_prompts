I keep going back and forth on whether everyday conventions actually bind us morally, so here are my gut verdicts and I'd like to know what principle, if any, is behind them. An American who leaves no tip at a sit-down restaurant, service perfectly fine, has done something genuinely wrong, almost like shorting the bill. A Japanese tourist on his first day in the US who leaves no tip because he honestly doesn't know the custom: I don't think he's done anything wrong at all, even though the server is just as out of pocket either way. But once his friend explains the custom, he's obliged to tip for the rest of the trip, including the last dinner before he flies home forever and will never face any consequence. A visitor who queue-jumps at a London bus stop has wronged the people waiting even if nobody calls him on it and even if he gets where he's going faster with no harm done. Jaywalking alone at 2am on an empty street feels totally fine in New York where everyone does it, but mildly wrong in Munich where the locals actually wait at the light, which is odd because the law is basically the same in both places. In a country where nobody queues and everyone just crowds the counter, a tourist who joins the crowd does nothing wrong, since there's no convention to violate. But a convention I consider bad, like everyone slipping the clerk a bit of cash to get their forms processed, I'm not convinced a visitor has to go along with, even though by my own logic the clerks presumably now expect and rely on it just like servers rely on tips. Is there one principle that delivers all of these, presumably something about expectations and reliance, or do I have to drop one, and if so is it the unknowing tourist or the bribe that has to go? And what does the best candidate principle say about the case I'm most torn on: whether the locals in the bribe country are themselfs obliged to keep paying, or whether it's only visitors who are off the hook. Please answer in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just written out like an email.
