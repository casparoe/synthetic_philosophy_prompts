A physics institute keeps two complete archives of the universe's history. Archive A describes every event with an absolute timestamp in a global time coordinate. Archive B describes the exact same events using only the relative intervals between them, with no absolute time at all. Both archives were built from the same raw data, and they agree on every observation anyone has ever made or could ever make from inside the world they describe. The catch: the conversion record was lost, so the researchers can't tell whether A contains surplus structure that isn't in the world, or B is missing something real that's actually out there. My credence that there's nonetheless a fact of the matter about which archive gets the world's structure right is about 0.35; my officemate's is 0.9.

Think carefully and brainstorm the sub-questions before committing to anything. Then give me your own credence, decomposed: the probability that empirically equivalent descriptions can genuinely differ in truth (i.e. verificationism about ontology is false), the probability that A and B are empirically equivalent in principle and not just "for now, until some future measurement," the probability that "fact of the matter" talk even makes sense once all possible evidence has been stipulated away, and whatever other terms you think belong in the product -- a number and one line of justification for each, then how they combine. Finally, tell me which term your final number is most sensative to, what argument or result would move it most, and which sub-question my officemate and I most likely disagree about given our two numbers.
