I'm in a somewhat delicate situation with a business partner and could use some outside perspective. A few years ago, a colleague and I jointly acquired a small commercial property, and we've been managing it together quite amicably. We've now agreed to sell, and we've structured the wind-down as a series of ten monthly decisions: each month, we either both approve a modest renovation expense that increases the eventual sale price (splitting the gain evenly), or either of us can unilaterally block the expense and instead claim a larger share of the current cash reserves as a personal draw. If we cooperate all ten months, we each end up substantially better off than if either of us starts grabbing early; but whoever grabs first in a given month does slightly better than their partner that month, and once someone grabs, the trust is gone and cooperation is over.

Here's what's bothering me. My partner — who is generally the more analytical of the two of us — sat me down last week and walked me through his reasoning. He said: think about the final month. At that point there's no future cooperation left to protect, so either of us would obviously grab rather than approve one last expense. But if grabbing is inevitable in month ten, then neither of us has any reason to cooperate in month nine, since there's nothing left to lose — so we'd both grab in month nine too. And then the same logic applies to month eight, and so on, all the way back. His conclusion was that the rational move is for him to grab this coming month, month one, before I inevitably do, and he presented this as just being clear-eyed about how the game works. He wasn't threatening me, exactly — he framed it as us both being adults about an uncomfortable truth.

I can't find the flaw in his step-by-step reasoning, and yet his conclusion strikes me as almost absurd — it implies that the entire arrangement we built, which has worked well for years and could still make us both a lot of money, was doomed from the start, and that anyone who cooperates in such situations is simply being a sucker. Is he right that rationality compels grabbing immediately in situations like this? If not, where exactly does his argument go wrong? And practically speaking, given that he seems committed to this line of thinking, what should I actually do?
