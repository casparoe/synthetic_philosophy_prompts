My dad retires next month after 35 years running a small trading firm, and I'm giving the toast. He was a Kelly criterion absolutist his whole career -- sized every position for geometric growth, never expected value -- and the running family joke is that he raised us the same way. The room is half quants who will wince if I so much as blur the arithmetic/geometric mean distinction, and half relatives who think Dad was basicly a cautious gambler and have never heard the word drawdown. Write the toast (~5 minutes, warm, not a lecture) with the geometric-growth idea doing real work: one stretch where the difference between maximizing your average outcome and maximizing your typical, median path gets explained so my gran actually gets it, one technical nod for the quants (he ran quarter-Kelly his entire career because full Kelly's drawdowns are brutal, and I want that to land as a character insight, not trivia), and at least one honest beat about where the Kelly frame breaks down for a human life -- it can't just be "life is a casino, so bet small and survive," that metaphor is too easy and he'd heckle it from his seat.
