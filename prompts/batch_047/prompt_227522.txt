I'm a machine learning researcher, fluent in the vocabulary of generative models, latent variables, posterior inference, and identifiability, but with no formal training in philosophy of perception. I'd like you to translate a debate for me, and then tell me what the translation cost.

First, restate the classical argument from illusion — the line of argument associated with sense-data theorists such as Russell and Ayer — in my vocabulary. Model perceptual experience as inference over latent causes given sensory observations; treat the veridical case (a straight stick partly submerged in water) and the deceptive case (a perfect hallucination of the same scene) as two different latent structures that generate identical observations. Render, as precisely as you can, the argument's inferential steps: from the possibility of perfect indistinguishability between the cases, to the conclusion that what is directly given to the mind must be the same kind of thing in both, to the conclusion that even in the good case the immediate object of awareness is not the external object but something mind-dependent. I want to see exactly which step corresponds to an identifiability failure and which steps, if any, are doing something that inference over latents cannot capture.

Second, restate the disjunctivist response — the claim, associated with Hinton, Snowdon, and McDowell, that a veridical perception and a subjectively indistinguishable hallucination are not two tokens of one fundamental mental kind sharing a highest common factor, but are essentially different states — in the same vocabulary, and be honest about the strain. Is disjunctivism just the banal statistical point that identical likelihoods do not identify the latent cause? And if the disjunctivist says the experiences themselves differ in kind, not merely in their causes, what could that mean in a framework where identical observation sequences yield identical posteriors?

Third, the residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which translate only with distortion — I suspect the ambiguity between phenomenal and functional readings of indistinguishability is one such place? And which claims have no expression at all in probabilistic vocabulary — for instance, the disjunctivist's insistence that in the good case the external object is a constituent of the experience itself, or the epistemological claim about what such experiences entitle us to believe? Finally, locate the residue: does my framework simply lack the concepts (constitution, phenomenal character, warrant) needed to state the disagreement, or does the failure of translation instead suggest that the philosophical debate conflates questions my framework rightly keeps apart — say, the question of what causes an experience with the question of what the experience is?
