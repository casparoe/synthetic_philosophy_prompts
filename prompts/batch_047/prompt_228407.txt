For a political philosophy reading group I'm running, I want to build a decision tree that takes someone from "ok, I accept that Sen's minimal liberalism plus weak Pareto plus unrestricted domain genuinely collide" to a specific way out of the liberal paradox. I've read the original Paretian liberal paper and the usual follow-ups, so please don't re-derive the theorem at me -- the deliverable is the tree itself, as a flowchart I can print on one page.

Some constraints so it actually sorts people instead of just listing the literature:

- 6 to 8 branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete question someone can answer without knowing which resolution it leads to. I want questions like: does it even matter what Prude wants about Lewd's reading habits when we add up social welfare, or only what Lewd wants? Should rights be cashed out as decisiveness over which social state obtains, or as control over your own actions with the outcomes just falling where they fall? Can you give away or trade your right over your "private" pair, and if so does the conflict come back inside a single person? Use the Prude-and-Lewd reading case (or a re-skinned version of it) inside at least two nodes rather than as decoration.
- 5 to 7 leaves, each with a name where one exists (the Nozick-style procedural move, the game-form recharacterization of rights, restricting Pareto to non-meddlesome preferences, Sen's own later attempts, just biting the impossibility bullet, etc.) and one sentence each on the cost of living at that leaf.
- Build the tree from the logical space first: figure out which combinations of answers are even available, and if some combination is empty, say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or weirdly unexplored.
- Flag any two leaves your tree can't really separate, and say what extra question would split them -- I'm suspicious that the procedural-rights and game-form leaves are closer than their fans admit.
- The audience is smart grad students whos intuitions I want to embarrass, so if there's a node where the "natural" answer leads somewhere nobody wants to be, mark it.
