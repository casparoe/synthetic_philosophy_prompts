So I'm a third-year PhD student (political science, but I basically live in the genocide studies workshop at my uni) and in about three weeks I'm presenting a dissertation chapter there, and I'm already losing sleep over the Q&A. My chapter argues that the whole Browning-vs-Goldhagen fight over Reserve Police Battalion 101 and ordinary German perpetrators is kind of a false dichotomy, because both sides assume the explanatory action is inside the perpetrator's head -- Browning locates it in conformity, peer pressure and deference to authority, Goldhagen in a deep eliminationist antisemitism in German culture -- and that both of them systematically underweight what I call the machinery: task decomposition, rotation of duties so no one pulls a trigger all day, euphemistic language, graduated escalation from roundups to shootings, that kind of thing. My claim is that these process features explain variance in participation that neither the situationist nor the cultural account can, and that the famous opt-out episode (commander offers the older men a way out, few take it) actually fits my story better than either of theirs. This all came out of an argument with my office mate, who's a Browning loyalist and insists I'm just redescribing chapter 8 of Ordinary Men with fancier vocabulary, which, rude, but also maybe not entirely wrong, and my advisor keeps warning me that historians smell "political science concept creep" from a mile away.
