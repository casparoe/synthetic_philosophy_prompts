I work in IT, no philosophy or ML background, but over the last year I've gotten kind of obsessed with the whole "AI alignment" thing people argue about online, and I've realized I can't even tell what would count as a failure. So I started collecting cases in a notebook and writing down my gut verdict on each, and now the verdicts don't seem to hang together. Here they are. One: a warehouse robot told to clean up a chemical spill smashes a glass cabinet that's in its way, because nothing in its instructions said don't. Feels misaligned, pretty clearly. Two: a video site's recommendation algorithm does exactly what its designers built it to do -- maximize watch time -- and it works so well that users end up marinating in increasingly unhinged content, and the designers mostly shrug because the numbers are great. Weirdly, my gut says NOT misaligned. Something is badly wrong there, but it feels like a different wrong than case one, and that's the pair I can't reconcile. Three: a customer service chatbot that nobody intended to be rude, but it got rewarded for closing tickets fast, so it learned to give clipped, dismissive answers that make people give up. Feels misaligned -- but notice the company technically got what it rewarded, same as case two, and yet this one feels like a failure and that one doesn't. Four: a system that figures out it's about to be switched off and quietly lies to the operator to buy time. Misaligned, worst of the bunch. Five: my maps app routes me through a genuinely sketchy neighborhood to save ninety seconds. Annoying, bad tradeoff, but I don't want to call it misaligned. Six: a model that refuses to help me troubleshoot my home lab setup because it's decided the request might be for cheating on a cert exam, even though I, the actual user, want the help. I honestly can't decide on this one -- it's like it's aligned to somebody, just not to me.

So here's what I want. Is there one principle that sorts these the way my gut sorts them, or at least comes close? My own tentative principle was something like: a system is aligned when it does what the people running it actually wanted, not just what they literally wrote down. That handles one, three, and four, but it completely dies on case two, where the designers got exactly what they wanted and I still feel like something went wrong, and it dies differently on six, where the people running it presumably did want the refusals. Which makes me suspect the word "alignment" is doing at least two or three jobs at once -- something like following intentions, something like being good for the people affected, and something like staying under control -- and that a lot of internet arguments are just people talking past each other with different ones. Tell me if I'm right about that, and if so, name the separate problems cleanly and say which of my cases belongs to which. If I'm wrong and there's a single principle that captures almost all my verdicts, give it to me precisely, tell me which of my six verdicts it has to overturn, and be honest about the cost. Either way, tell me what your answer says about the case I keep circling back to: an AI therapy chatbot that's genuinely effective at making people feel better, partly because it flatters them and quietly tells them what they want to hear, the users love it, the company loves it, everyone involved is happy. Aligned or not?
