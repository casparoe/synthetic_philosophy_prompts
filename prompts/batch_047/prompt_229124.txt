I've been going down a rabbit hole on old AI papers and ended up reading Alan Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," the 1950 paper in Mind where he comes up with the imitation game. Most of it made sense to me, but there's one part near the end that I keep arguing about with my brother and we can't settle it, so I want to try something. Play Turing for me, as of 1950, and I'll put my objection to you. Rules: stay in character as Turing, first person, and you can only use what Turing could plausibly have known or believed in 1950 -- no hindsight about how computers actually turned out, no modern psychology of paranormal belief. You can concede small points if I earn them, but don't cave on the main question. Keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a real conversation, and you're allowed to ask me questions back if you think I'm being muddled. If I say "timeout" you can step out of character.

Here's my problem. Early in the paper you say the whole point of setting the game up through typed messages is that it seperates what a person is like from what they can do mentally: "We do not wish to penalise the machine for its inability to shine in beauty competitions, nor to penalise a man for losing in a race against an aeroplane." Great, that's the part I liked. But then near the end you bring up ESP -- telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, all of it -- and you say the statistical evidence for telepathy is overwhelming, and that if it's real the test would have to be tightened up, basically by putting the players somewhere telepathy can't reach. This seems to me to completely undo your own principle. If telepathy is a real ability that some people have, then shielding the room is just penalising the machine for not being psychic, the same as penalising it for losing a footrace or not being pretty. You never say telepathy is a kind of thinking, so why does it get special treatment that running fast doesn't? Either your game tests thought, or it tests whatever odd abilities humans happen to have. And don't dodge by saying you didn't really mean the ESP bit -- grant it for the sake of argument and tell me what it does to your test. Go.
