Here's my position set:

(1) Putnam's multiple realizability point convinces me that mental states can't be identical to brain-state types. Martian pain isn't C-fiber firing, but it's still pain. So the right level for individuating mental states is functional organization, not physical makeup.

(2) I reject full-blown type identity but I'm happy with token identity: my headache right now just is some particular neural event, it's just that "pain" doesn't pick out a physical kind.

(3) Block's Blockhead -- the giant lookup table that passes any behavioral test by sheer brute-force search through pre-stored responses -- does not genuinely think or understand. Something can match our input-output profile without having a mind.

(4) I'm with Chalmers on zombies: a perfect functional duplicate of me that lacks all conscious experience is conceivable, and conceivability here is good enough for possibility. Absent qualia are possible.

(5) Whether a system is in pain should depend only on how it's currently organized, not on how it was built, who built it, or where it came from. Origin stories are irrelevant.

My sense is (1)-(3) are basically the standard functionalist package and (4) is a separate module about consciousness, so everything should cohere. Is that right? Rank any tensions by severity, and for each one tell me exactly which commitment has to go and what it costs me.
