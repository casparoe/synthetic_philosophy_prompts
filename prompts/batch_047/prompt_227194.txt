I am the volunteer systems administrator for a housing cooperative, with no academic background in political philosophy. While redesigning our building-access system, I noticed that arguments among residents seemed to track three different ideas of liberty that I had encountered informally: negative liberty as not being actively obstructed, positive liberty as having the effective capacity to do what one has reason to do, and republican liberty as not depending on another party’s unchecked discretion. Please restate the disagreement among these three positions in the vocabulary I actually use: access-control matrices, credentials, resource limits, uptime, failover, audit logs, administrative override, revocation, appeals, monitoring, vendor lock-in, and exit costs. I am fluent in that vocabulary, so the translation can be precise.

Apply it to these cases: every resident has a valid badge and no guard ever blocks anyone, but the door system is unreliable and people with inflexible work schedules often cannot enter; the system is perfectly reliable, but the property manager possesses an unlogged master credential that can revoke any badge instantly, although the current manager is benevolent and has never used it; a master credential remains available, but every use is published, an independent panel can reverse it and penalize misuse, and any resident can trigger review, though nobody can prevent the manager from acting first; and residents can leave for another cooperative, but moving is expensive and alternatives are scarce. For each of the three conceptions, say what translates cleanly, what must be stretched, and what cannot be expressed in systems language at all. In particular, consider whether unaudited administrative capability adequately represents dependence even when it is never exercised, whether logging and appeal capture control over power or merely discourage abuse, and whether uptime and capacity can represent effective freedom without classifying every disadvantage as unfreedom. End with a residue report identifying any genuinely political elements—such as equal status, self-government, or control over one’s own character—that disappear in the technical translation, and say whether that loss exposes a limitation of my vocabulary or reveals vagueness in the original disagreement.
