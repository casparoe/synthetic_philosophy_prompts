I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm drafting a long feature on which of our everyday categories are built by us and which are merely discovered. My editor wants a defended ranking as the centerpiece rather than a survey, and after months of interviews I keep flip-flopping on the ordering, so I'd like an outside judgment argued from scratch. Consider these eight kinds: money, race, gender, species, planet, mental disorder, creditworthiness, and quark. Rank them from most to least socially constructed, where constructedness is measured by a specific counterfactual test I'm calling the overnight test: if every human belief, practice, and institution concerning a kind vanished tonight, how much of the kind's grouping would still be there in the morning — would the things it collected still form a set that anyone, or anything, would have independent reason to treat as one? For each kind, give a sentence or two justifying its placement, and where two kinds land close together, name the consideration that separates them.

Two complications I'd like you to engage directly rather than wave at, because they're where my interviewees disagreed most. First, the feedback problem: for some kinds, being classified changes the classified — people alter their behavior, self-conception, and even life prospects in response to being sorted into a race, a diagnosis, or a credit band — and it's genuinely unclear to me whether that looping should push a kind higher or lower on a constructedness scale. State your position and use it consistently across the ranking. Second, distinguish the constructedness of a kind's substance from the constructedness of its cut-lines: the matter that planets are made of isn't up to us, though where planet ends and dwarf planet begins arguably is, while money seems constructed through and through and quarks presumably not at all. If your ranking weights substance and cut-lines differently for different kinds, make that tiebreak rule explicit before you apply it. Open your response with the full ranking in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
