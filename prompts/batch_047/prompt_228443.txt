ok so i run a decision theory reading group and we just spent a session on the twin humiliations of CDT and EDT -- counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail -- and i want to end the semester by putting a sestina on the final slide. yes really.

quick recap so we're on the same page: in the counterfactual mugging, a perfect predictor tells you it flipped a fair coin. heads, it would have given you $10,000 if and only if it predicted that you'd hand over $100 in the tails branch. the coin came up tails, so now it's standing there asking for the $100. CDT says get lost -- the ten grand was never on offer in this branch -- and thereby walks away from a deal that was worth thousands in expectation ex ante. in XOR blackmail, the predictor mails you a letter saying it's sent it if and only if exactly one of these holds: your house has termites and you won't pay it $1000, or your house is clean and you will. the letter arrives. paying is now evidence your house is fine, so EDT forks over a grand to buy itself good news about woodwork it already owns, while CDT rightly refuses. each theory gets robbed exactly where the other one is safe.

the sestina: six end-words -- coin, tails, letter, termites, pay, prediction. stanzas 1, 3 and 5 stay inside the mugging, stanzas 2, 4 and 6 inside the blackmail, so the two puzzles trade off the way the end-words keep cycling back in new positions (the form should feel like the same trap reappearing in different clothes). the envoi has to do the real philosophical work, not just gesture: state the symmetry between the two cases out loud, and land on the moral that you never get to choose an action, only a policy -- be the agent whose past self would have wanted to be predictable in exactly this way. don't actually use the word "updateless" or "UDT" anywhere, the last three lines should make the reader arrive there on their own. give it a title too.
