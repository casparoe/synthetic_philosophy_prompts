So I'm in a grad seminar on formal epistemology and we did the Judy Benjamin case (van Fraassen's thing) and I wrote up an argument for the discussion board that's now bugging me, because it seems to prove something that can't be right. Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: Judy is a soldier parachuted somewhere into a square area divided into Blue territory and Red territory, each making up half the map, and within each territory half is the headquarters zone and half is second-company zone. So her credences split evenly over four cells, 1/4 each: Blue&HQ, Blue&2nd, Red&HQ, Red&2nd. Then she gets a radio message she knows is completely reliable: if she's in Blue territory, she's in the HQ zone. The question everyone argues about is how she should update. My argument is that there is no consistent answer:

(1) If you learn a proposition for certain, rationality requires conditionalizing on it: zero out the worlds where it's false, keep the ratios between the worlds where it's true, renormalize.
(2) Judy's message is a proposition she now knows for certain, so she has to conditionalize on it.
(3) The message is only false in the Blue&2nd cell (in the Red cells it doesn't apply, so it comes out true), so conditionalizing leaves her at 1/3 on each of the other three cells. Her credence in Blue drops to 1/3, while her credence in Blue given HQ stays 1/2.
(4) But the message says absolutely nothing about which territory she's in -- it's entirely about where she'd be on the assumption that she's in Blue -- so her credence in Blue should stay at 1/2, and the only thing that should move is her credence in HQ-given-Blue, which goes to 1.
(5) (3) and (4) give incompatible results, and both just apply (1) to the plain meaning of the message, so (1) has to go: conditionalization is incoherent whenever the evidence is a conditional whose antecedent you're unsure about.
(6) But that's basically all scientific evidence -- you learn things like if the theory is right, the needle will deflect, while uncertain of the theory -- so rational belief revision is mostly impossible.

(6) is obviously nuts, so the argument goes wrong somewhere, but I've stared at it for days. My friend says (3) is fine and (4) is just fuzzy intuition talk, but (4) seems really compelling to me -- like, how could hearing that message be evidence that you're in Red? If I have to sacrifice something I'd most like to keep (1), since I don't see how Bayesianism survives without it. Which step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost me elsewhere?
