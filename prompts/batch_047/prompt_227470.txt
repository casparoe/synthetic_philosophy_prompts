For my seminar on the meaning of life, I want students to stress-test Moritz Schlick's 1927 essay On the Meaning of Life (Vom Sinn des Lebens) rather than merely summarize it. As I present his view, Schlick holds that a life gets its meaning not from serving goals external to it — work done purely for its results leaves existence a pointless chain of means — but from activity that carries its point within itself, his paradigm being play: free, creative, self-justifying activity, of which he took youth to be the natural season. He also predicted that technology would progressively relieve humanity of necessary labor, so that existence itself could become play. Suppose Schlick were transported to the present. Predict his verdicts, with reasons drawn from that framework, on three figures: a professional esports player who trains sixty hours a week under contract for prize money; a retired office worker in a near-automated economy whose days are filled entirely with streaming entertainment she enjoys but never discusses or builds on; and a hobbyist who spends his evenings writing music no one will hear. In each case, state which feature of Schlick's account does the deciding — the internality of the activity's value, the requirement of creative self-expression, or something else — and where his framework is genuinely ambiguous between celebrating the activity as play and dismissing it as passive amusement. Finally, identify the single additional commitment, consistent with the essay, that would most change your predictions, and explain why.
