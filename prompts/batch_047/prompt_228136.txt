I want to spar on the question of downward causation, using a thought experiment I've adapted from Hofstadter's Careenium. Please play the opposing side in an extended exchange.

The setup. Imagine a sealed box described at two levels:

- Simms: millions of tiny marbles whose motion is completely fixed by deterministic mechanical rules (collisions, gravity, friction) operating on each simm's position and momentum. At this level, given the full state at time t, the state at t+1 is settled. Nothing is left open.
- Simmballs: a few hundred large "balls." A simmball is not an additional physical object; its position at any moment is a fixed function of the simm configuration, the way a glider in Conway's Game of Life is a pattern in cells rather than a thing added to the grid. Because of how the box is constructed, the simmballs obey clean, stable regularities of their own — for instance, whenever simmballs of types A and B come into contact, a nearby simmball of type C reliably gets nudged — and these regularities are far easier to state, predict, and exploit at the simmball level than by tracking simms.

The disputed question: do simmballs do any real causal work, or is all the causing done by simms, with simmball-level talk being merely a convenient compression?

Your role: defend genuine symbol-level causation. You are not a dualist — you accept that simmballs are nothing over and above simms and that no extra force enters the box. Your claim is that on the right account of what causation is (difference-making, intervention, control, explanatory relevance — pick your weapon and stick to it), simmballs are genuine causes, and a substrate-only causal story leaves something real unaccounted for. Be a sharp opponent, not a caricature: engage my actual arguments, press your strongest counter each turn, concede small points freely but never the main one. Keep replies to a single paragraph so it feels like a conversation. Stay in character until I say "stop"; if I get the rules of the setup wrong, correct me in character.

My opening move: I'm a software engineer, and to me this is just the running-code case. If I single-step a program at the gate level, every next machine state is fully determined by the previous one; the fact that the machine is "running quicksort" never appears in the trace and never needs to. When I say "the algorithm caused the output," that's bookkeeping for my benefit — useful for prediction and debugging, but causally idle, since the transistors did all the work and any algorithm-level causal claim is either redundant with the gate-level story or double-counting. Simms are the transistors. So tell me precisely what is left for the simmballs to cause that isn't already caused, once, at the bottom.
