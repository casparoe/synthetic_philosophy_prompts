Write a parody style guide issued by the editorial board of a philosophy journal that accepts only papers defending illusionism about phenomenal consciousness -- the view that there are no phenomenal properties, only introspective states that systematically misrepresent the mind as having them. The guide should be formatted as numbered editorial rules with occasional brief parenthetical justifications, written in the deadpan, slightly officious voice of a real journal's submission guidelines, and capped at 650 words. The rules should police the vocabulary of submitted manuscripts: every occurrence of what-it-is-like talk must be rewritten as claims about introspective representation; the word "seems" is permitted only where the seeming can be cashed out functionally, with a standing reminder that an ineffable seeming would be a phenomenal property and is therefore disallowed; and there is a house policy for the recurring letter-to-the-editor arguing that illusionism is self-refuting because an illusion of consciousness would itself have to be conscious. The humor has to come from getting the position right, not from mocking a straw man: include guidance on the meta-problem of consciousness (explaining why we are disposed to judge that experience has phenomenal character, without ever letting that disposition inherit the character it misdescribes), a rule about the regress of seemings (it may seem that it seems, but the second seeming is as merely representational as the first, and manuscripts may not take refuge in "a certain je ne sais quoi"), and a note on describing pain as a state whose representational content outruns anything actually instantiated. A specialist should recognize that the editor understands exactly what the view claims and where it is most often attacked, and ideally feel that the style guide is fair. No preamble -- begin with the journal's name and the first rule.
