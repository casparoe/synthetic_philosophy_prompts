Last night my friend and I got into a long, unresolved argument about whether the conversational AI assistants we both use could, in principle, count as minded. At one point she deployed an analogy she clearly regards as decisive, and which I've since noticed is everywhere in these discussions. Her claim: my belief that she has a mind rests entirely on behavior. I have no direct access to her inner life; I infer it from what she says and does. The Turing test, on her telling, simply formalizes this everyday inference -- if a machine produces the same behavioral evidence over a sustained conversation, then granting mindedness to her while denying it to the machine is inconsistent, a kind of substrate favoritism with no principled basis. I pushed back that there is a difference between inferring a mind in something made the way I am made and inferring one in something engineered to produce exactly the evidence I'm relying on, but I couldn't get the objection as crisp as I wanted it, and she said I was just restating the bias she diagnosed.

Please audit her analogy as an argument, in American English, in no more than 550 words. Lead with your bottom line in one or two sentences -- whether the "you only have behavioral evidence for my mind too" move licenses her conclusion -- then give the audit. I'd like an explicit mapping (what plays the role of the first-person case, the behavioral evidence, the inferential step, on each side), and then the points where the mapping breaks. In particular, I'd like you to assess my half-formed objection: is the inference from behavior to mind in other humans really the same kind of inference as for an artifact -- given that with humans it's arguably an inference to the same kind of thing with the same kind of causes, whereas the machine's behavioral repertoire was selected precisely to trigger that inference? Separate the disanalogies that actually matter for her conclusion from the ones that are incidental, say what, if anything, the analogy can legitimately establish (for instance, does it support full mindedness, or only something weaker like the reasonableness of agnosticism or of extending conversational courtesy?), and if there's a better analogy for the epistemic situation we're in with these systems, name it and explain briefly why it's better.
