A colleague of mine has settled views about which animals are conscious, formed over years of casual discussion. Her verdicts so far: dogs, ravens, and goldfish are conscious; sponges, sea anemones, and slugs are not. As far as I can tell, these verdicts are jointly consistent with several different principles she might be following: that all and only vertebrates are conscious; that all and only animals capable of flexible, novel learning are conscious; and that all and only animals with a centralized brain integrating information from multiple senses are conscious. She has never been asked about a case where these principles come apart. I now put to her the case of a crab, noting that crabs learn to avoid locations where they have been shocked, will trade the quality of a shell against the strength of shock required to make them abandon it, and repeatedly attend to an injured claw. Please predict her verdict on the crab, showing what each of the three candidate principles would predict and giving your best guess as to which one actually guides her; and if her track record genuinely underdetermines the answer, say so and describe the further animal case that would force the issue.
