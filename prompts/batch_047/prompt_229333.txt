ok so for my philosophy elective we read this essay called Moral Saints by Susan Wolf, where she basically argues that being maximally morally good all the time is NOT actually an ideal anyone should aim for, because a perfect moral saint would have no room for a personality — no dark humor, no weird hobbies, no gourmet cooking or learning the bassoon, just endless optimizing for other people's welfare.

my assignment: write a satirical piece, around 500-600 words, in the style of a celebrity lifestyle magazine profile (think Vogue's "73 Questions" or an Architectural Digest home tour) interviewing a fully realized moral saint about their perfect, optimized life. the joke should be completely deadpan — the profile writer treats everything as aspirational and glamorous while the reader slowly realizes this person has no actual life. like, the "morning routine" section, the "home decor that maximizes utility" bit, etc.

the humor has to come from the actual philosophy though, not a strawman. work in real features of Wolf's argument, like the difference between the Loving Saint (sacrifices own interests out of care) and the Rational Saint (sacrifices them out of duty), and the point that moral sainthood crowds out non-moral excellences. a reader who knows the essay should think the satire is fair, not a cheap shot.

then after the piece, add a short note (like 3-4 sentences, not part of the word count) on where the satire leans on a contested reading — I know plenty of philosophers push back on Wolf, like utilitarians who'd say the saint's life IS good, so don't present her critique as the settled consensus. flag what's actually Wolf's view vs what's exaggerated for the bit.
