A while back I fell down a rabbit hole on a philosophy forum where people were arguing about this question: would you rather that one person be tortured horribly for fifty years, or that an absurdly huge number of people — way more people than could ever actually exist — each get a single dust speck in thier eye, mildly annoying for about a second. People split hard on this and apparently it's been argued about for like 15 years and nobody has budged an inch.

What confuses me is that both sides seem to accept the same setup. The aggregators say suffering is suffering, and enough dust specks must eventually outweigh the torture, because otherwise you're saying a finite amount of badness beats an arbitrarily large amount, which seems like refusing to do math. The other side says no number of dust specks, however big, could ever be worse than torture, full stop, and adding more zeros doesn't change that. Nobody thinks the other side is stupid. They just... stop.

My amateur theory is that this isn't really about dust or pain at all. It's about whether "badness" is one quantity that everything converts into, like money, or whether some harms are a different *kind* of thing that you simply can't trade off against trivial ones. So the aggregators are doing arithmetic and the others are doing something more like law, and they talk past each other because they're both using the word "worse" for different operations.

Is that diagnosis right? Or is there a real factual question buried in here — say, about how pain actually works, whether billions of tiny annoyances subjectively sum to anything like one agony? Is this disagreement verbal (about the word "worse"), empirical, or bedrock moral intuition that neither side can argue for? And given your answer: what would actually move it — better science of suffering, some formal result about aggregation, a distinction nobody's drawn yet? Or should I just tell the forum to give up?
