In a graduate seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics, we've spent several weeks on Schopenhauer's account of music, and I keep returning to a question our discussions never quite settle. As I understand his view, music stands apart from the other arts because it does not present the world through the Platonic Ideas, as painting and sculpture do, but instead copies the Will itself; melody in particular is supposed to trace the striving, partial satisfaction, and renewed striving that structure willing. This also explains, I think, his preference for purely instrumental music and his grumbling about opera, where music is forced to serve a text, and about imitative program music that tries to paint storms and pastoral scenes in sound. Where the framework gets interesting for me is precisely where it seems to have nothing to say.

What would Schopenhauer make of musical practices that arose long after his death? Take three. First, film and television scoring, where music is deliberately subordinated to images and dialogue and is often crafted to be felt rather than consciously noticed — in a sense a more extreme version of the operatic subordination he disliked, yet enormously popular. Second, algorithmic ambient music: software systems that generate endless, never-repeating soundscapes, marketed for sleep or concentration, with no human composer intending any particular melody and often no melody at all, just slowly shifting texture. Third, jazz improvisation, where melody is produced spontaneously in performance and never fixed in a score. I'm less interested in guessing his personal taste than in reconstructing his verdicts from his premises. Would generative ambient music count as music at all on his account, given that nothing in it plausibly copies the Will's striving — or might he surprisingly welcome it, since aesthetic contemplation for him offers temporary release from willing, and this music aims at something like enforced calm? Does improvised melody still qualify as the voice of the Will if no one deliberated over it? And does his hierarchy of the arts, with absolute music at the summit, survive contact with a culture that mostly consumes music as background?

Please reason from inside his system — the metaphysics of the Will, the role of aesthetic contemplation as respite from striving, the primacy of melody — rather than offering generic remarks about how dead philosophers would dislike new things. Where his premises genuinely underdetermine a verdict, say so, and identify which premise is doing the limiting. Write your answer in American English.
