A small research fund at my company just opened applications for a 9-month, half-time grant for "philosophical foundations of AI evaluation" projects, and I want to apply even though I'm clearly an outsider.

Here's my half-formed idea, please turn it into a proper proposal. When people talk about an AI system being "deceptive," they usually reach for something like the human definition: the system intends to get you to believe something false. But that definition does no work for current LLMs, because whether an LLM "intends" or "believes" anything is exactly the thing nobody agrees on, so the honesty question just gets absorbed into the consciousness question and dies there. My hunch: you can build a notion of deception that is purely about the relationship between a system's internal states (its representations, its activations, whatever the interpretability people can actually measure) and its outputs, without ever settling whether it has beliefs. In engineering terms, honesty would be a spec on the interface between internals and outputs, not a claim about the system's mind. I suspect this fails somewhere — maybe "internal state" can't be pinned down without smuggling intentionality back in, or maybe the spec ends up so weak that a blatantly deceptive system passes it.

The proposal needs: a precise statement of the question (so it has a determinate answer, not just "is AI deception real"), why the existing belief/intention-based approaches leave it open, what I'd actually concretely do over the 9 months, what a successful result looks like vs. what counts as the project failing, and a scope sanity-check given I'm one person doing this halftime. No citations or lit review, I'll dig up the references myself. Also be honest about whether an engineer with no formal training can credibly pull this off or whether the framing needs to shrink.
