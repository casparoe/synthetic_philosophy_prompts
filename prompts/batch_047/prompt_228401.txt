My friends and I do a movie-and-beer night where we pick a film and argue about it after, and next up is The Prestige (the Nolan one, with Bale and Jackman as rival magicians). I want to come armed with a real take this time, and I keep thinking the movie is secretly a philosophy-of-personal-identity paper in a top hat.

So: read The Prestige through the lens of personal identity and survival. Angier's final trick has Tesla's machine making a copy of him at a distance while the man who steps into the machine drops into a tank and drowns, every single performance. Is that teleportation-and-survival, or nightly suicide followed by a perfect imposter taking over? Bring in the psychological-continuity view (memories, intentions, character all transfer, so "he" walks out at the other end) and the view that what matters is bodily or physical continuity, and say which one the film itself seems to believe. I'm especially interested in the drowning detail: does it matter, morally or metaphysically, that the original isn't painlessly vaporized but gets to be conscious of his own death? The film makes a big deal of Angier not knowing, each night, whether he'll be the one in the tank or the one taking the bow -- is that a coherent fear on the continuity view, or does the movie expose that the view can't actually capture what we'd be afraid of?

Also don't skip Borden. His whole trick turns out to be two identical twins secretly time-sharing one public identity, one marriage, one life, and the film treats that as both genius and a kind of tragedy. What would a fission case person (Parfit-type) say about two people splitting one biography -- and does the movie's verdict on Borden (that the halving wrecked both of them) count as evidence that identity isn't the kind of thing you can share, or is it just evidence about their specific personalities?

End with: what's the one thing the film shows about survival and duplication that a pure thought experiment (teleporter, neuron-by-neuron replacement, whatever) doesn't get at? I'm thinking it's something about dread or about watching your own double, but convince me or tell me I'm wrong.
