So my roommate and I got into it again last night about whether the past and future are real. Like, actually real, on par with the present. She's a committed eternalist -- she thinks dinosaurs and my future retirement are all just "there" in the block universe same as right now is, and she keeps bringing up special relativity and how there's no fact of the matter about what's happening "right now" on Mars, so the present can't be special. I'm a presentist. To me it seems completely obvious that only now exists, and that time genuinely passes, and I think her view has to explain away an experience we literally cannot escape. We argued for like three hours and mostly talked past each other, and at 1am we agreed to try writing one of those adversarial collaboration documents where both sides sign the same text. Neither of us knows how to actually do that. Can you draft it for us?

Here's what I want, in this order:

1. "Claims we can both sign" (~300 words): numbered list. Things like what each view actually says (no strawmen -- she always accuses me of denying that I have a birth certificate, I always accuse her of thinking time is "just space"), what physics does and doesn't say, and anything else a reasonable presentist and eternalist would both endorse.

2. "Where we actually disagree" (~250 words): state each disagreement in wording we'd both accept for our own positions. Be exact -- not "they disagree about whether time is real" but the precise claim each of us is committed to.

3. "The cruxes" (~300 words): the 2-3 sub-questions where, if one got settled, at least one of us would have to give ground. My guess: whether relativity's relativity of simultaneity really does force eternalism or whether that's an extra interpretive step, and whether the felt passage of time is a datum any theory must accommodate or an illusion any theory may explain away. For each crux, name the argument, case, or piece of physics we'd both agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each of us predicts about it.

4. "Fights we realised were just about words" (~150 words): if any of our dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Be honest here even if it stings.

Don't referee it or declare a winner at the end -- the whole point is that we both have to be able to sign it. If you think one of the cruxes secretly favors her, fine, put it in the cruxes section, but the document itself should stay neutral.
