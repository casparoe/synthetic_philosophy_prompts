Bit of an odd one, but here goes. I've noticed something about my own head and I want to know if its a known thing.

I'm scared of flying. Not terrified, but properly scared — sweaty palms on takeoff, the lot. The weird part is that I know it's safe. I've read the statistics, I believe them, I'd happily bet money the plane lands fine. And yet the fear just sits there, completly ignoring everything I believe. If I believed a bridge was safe I'd walk over it without a second thought, so why wont the fear take the hint?

And it's not like emotions never listen to facts. Last month I was furious at my sister for missing my birthday dinner — genuinely angry for two days — and the moment I found out she'd actually been in hospital, the anger just vanished. Not faded, vanished, like someone switched it off. So some emotions snap off the instant the facts change, and others won't budge no matter what you know.

Is there a name for the stubborn kind? I've heard people say emotions are really just thoughts, and other people say they're just body feelings, but neither seems to cover both of my cases — the anger behaved like a thought (it died when the thought died) and the fear behaves more like a reflex. I don't just want a label, though. I want to know what the split actually is: where the line falls, what you'd call cases that don't sit neatly on either side, and whether the stubborn ones show that emotions can't really be judgements at all, or whether there's a way to rescue that idea. And if there's genuinely no settled name for the stubborn case, say so and suggest one — with a definition sharp enough that I can tell whether, say, feeling guilty about something that wasn't my fault counts as one.
