In a reading group on the foundations of quantum mechanics, our discussion of probability in the Everett interpretation has been stuck in exactly the same place for two years, and I would like a diagnosis rather than another round of argument. Everyone grants that if every outcome of a measurement occurs on some branch, it is unclear what uncertainty could even be about (the incoherence worry), and that even granting uncertainty of some kind, nothing obvious forces weighting branches by amplitude-squared rather than by branch count (the quantitative worry). One faction holds that the Deutsch–Wallace decision-theoretic programme settled both points long ago: given some plausible constraints on rational preference over quantum games, an agent must act as if the Born rule held, and continuing to object merely reflects a confused picture of what probability is. The other faction replies that the Born rule is smuggled in through those very constraints — the axioms look innocent only to eyes already trained on Hilbert-space structure — so the derivation is at best permissive where it needs to be mandatory. Strikingly, both sides accept all the same mathematics, and no one expects a new formal result to break the tie. So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? A still-open technical question about whether the axioms are genuinely non-circular? A dispute about what probability or self-locating uncertainty could be in a deterministic branching universe? Or a bedrock clash about what rationality requires that argument cannot reach? Given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress — a sharper theorem, an independently motivated theory of self-locating uncertainty, or the recognition that the question is ill-posed — or argue that no progress should be expected and why.
