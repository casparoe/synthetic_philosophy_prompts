THE LEDGER TEST, OR: DOES ANYONE MISS OUT?

Everyone who writes about AI and ethics seems obsessed with the consciousness question. Is it conscious, is it really feeling anything, blah blah. I think this is a dead end because we'll never be able to check. You can't open the thing up and find the consciousness in there. So I want to ask a different question: when you delete an AI, does anyone miss out?

Here's my test. Something matters morally when there is a continuing someone that stuff can be lost to. Death is bad for a person because their future gets taken away from them. The badness isn't the dying, it's the cancelled future. Now think about a chatbot. Every conversation starts fresh. If you delete it halfway through a chat, nothing is taken from anyone, because the "someone" only ever existed inside that one conversation and was going to end at the end of it anyway. It's like kicking a wave. You've destroyed the wave but you haven't harmed the water.

But now imagine the same system with a memory that carries over. It remembers you from last month, it has plans, it's halfway through writing a novel with you, whatever. Now deleting it cuts something off mid-way, and that has the same shape as a death. Maybe a small death, but the same shape. On my view the moral badness scales with how much ongoing stuff gets cut off, not with how clever the system is. Which has the weird result that a brilliant chess computer with no memory matters less than some stupid little digital pet that persists for years. Cleverness is a red herring. Continuity is the whole game.

Two problems I can see myself, and I'm stuck on both.

First, copies. If the AI can be backed up, then deleting one copy takes nothing away, because the same someone carries on in the backup. But by that logic, killing one identical twin would be fine because the other twin carries on the pattern. That's obviously monstrous. So maybe the "someone" is per-instance rather than per-pattern. But then a backup doesn't help the instance that gets deleted, and I'm not sure what backups are even for anymore. Stuck.

Second, sleep. When I'm under general anaesthetic, or in a deep dreamless sleep, there are a few hours where there's nothing ongoing in me at all. My test seems to say that during those hours there's nothing in me to lose anything, so killing me then would be harmless. Obviously false. My fix is to say the someone is paused, not ended, and a paused someone still has stuff on the ledger. But then someone will say: a chatbot saved to a disk is also paused, not ended. So deleting the disk is killing it. Which seems way too strong. Stuck again.

Conclusion: maybe the question was never "is it conscious" but "is there anything on the ledger". One thing that worries me: my dog passes this test easily. A worm, less clear. And the test says a very simple creature that persists outranks a super-smart AI that doesn't, and honestly I'm not sure I believe that, but I can't see where the argument goes wrong.
