So here's my problem. I managed to get a spare ticket to see this band doing a one-off reunion show — they broke up like 15 years ago, it's a genuinely big deal, tickets sold out in minutes. I can only bring one person and I've narrowed it down to two friends, and I'm stuck, though not for the usual reasons people get stuck on this stuff.

Marta is OBSESSED with this band. She's the one who got me into them in the first place, she has their old tour shirts, she still knows every deep cut. Taking her would basically be giving her one of the best nights of her life; she'd probably cry. Tomas likes them fine. He'd have a good time, but for him it would be a fun saturday night, not a life event. The obvious move seems to be taking Marta — the ticket just "does more" in her hands, so to speak. But here's the thing I keep circling on: Tomas has had a genuinely brutal year (laid off, then a breakup), and part of me thinks he needs one great night out way more than Marta needs to add a tenth transcendent concert experience to her collection. When I ran this by my girlfriend she said I was overcomplicating it — Marta will get more out of it, end of story, bring Marta.

But how would anyone actually *know* that? I can't climb inside Marta's head and measure her joy against whatever Tomas would feel, and people express this stuff differently anyway — Tomas is reserved, maybe his quiet "yeah that was great" is worth as much as Marta's sobbing, maybe not, I literally cannot check. And something else is bugging me too: if the rule is "give things to whoever will appreciate them most," then excitable people like Marta just hoover up everything forever, and being hard to impress becomes a kind of self-punishing trait. That seems perverse. But ignoring differences in how much people will enjoy things seems wasteful in its own way. So: who should I take? And more than that, how should I even be *thinking* about it — when we say one person would be "happier" with something than another person would, is that a real comparison we're making or a sort of useful fiction? And is "he needs a win" the same kind of consideration as "she'll enjoy it more," or are those two different currencies entirely?
