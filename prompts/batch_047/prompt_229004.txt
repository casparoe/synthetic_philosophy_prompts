I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep colliding with the same dispute at the edges of my genomics stories. On one side I hear, in effect, that the genome is a code in the full sense of the word: the mapping from codons to amino acids is arbitrary the way a genuine code must be, fixed by the contingent chemistry of tRNAs and the enzymes that charge them rather than by any physical necessity tying a triplet to its amino acid, and this is why talk of genetic information is not decoration but theory. On the other side I hear that "genetic information" is a dead metaphor that keeps getting up and walking: DNA is a template and a causal factor in development, no more a message to be read than a riverbed is a message to the water, and the code talk systematically misleads people -- my readers included -- about how much of the organism is written down in the sequence. I need to settle for myself what is actually being fought over.

Here is the framework I am genuinely fluent in, since it is what I use when I cover compression and machine learning: Shannon information. Sources, channels, signals, mutual information, redundancy, channel capacity, error correction, and the deliberate exclusion of meaning from the whole apparatus. Where Shannon's vocabulary runs out, you may extend into the plain computing vocabulary I also use -- lookup tables, codebooks, interpreters -- but flag every such extension rather than passing it off as still-Shannon. My grasp of the molecular biology is decent but journalistic: transcription, translation, the ribosome, the degeneracy of the codon table. Now do the translation, claim by claim, for both positions above. I want to know which parts map cleanly -- my guess is that mutual information between genotype and phenotype, and degeneracy-as-redundancy, survive intact -- and which parts have to be distorted to fit. Two candidates for distortion I can already see: the arbitrariness argument, since in Shannon terms every codebook is conventional and nothing physical pins symbol to meaning, which threatens to make the molecular biologist's central point trivially true of everything; and the notion of mistranscription as error, since channel noise in Shannon's sense is a statistical fact, not a failure, and carries no normativity.

The part I care most about is the residue report. Is there anything in this dispute that has no expression at all in my vocabulary -- I suspect the sense in which a sequence is about a protein, or for it, and the sense in which the cell is a reader rather than a receiver -- and if so, is that a defect of my framework or a sign that the biologists' code talk was carrying less than it advertised? My working hypothesis, formed over too many of these interviews, is that the whole fight dissolves once you rigorously keep causal information and semantic information apart, and that each side is right about one and wrong about the other. Test that hypothesis against your translation. And if the residue genuinely cannot be expressed in Shannon-plus-computing terms, tell me whether the usual philosophical repair -- grounding meaning in selection history, so that a sequence means what it was selected to do -- can itself be restated in my vocabulary without smuggling the disputed code talk back in through the word "function."
