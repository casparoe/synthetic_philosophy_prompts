I'm in a junior seminar on formal epistemology, and this week we've been doing self-locating belief, centered on the Sleeping Beauty case: Beauty is put to sleep Sunday; a fair coin is flipped; she's woken Monday for sure, and woken again Tuesday with her memory erased only if the coin lands tails; awakenings are subjectively indistinguishable. For our final meeting each of us has to submit a one-page "doxastic profile" of our commitments so the group can attack them, and I'd like an audit of mine before I hand it in. I know roughly where the standard battle lines are -- Elga-style thirders against Lewis-style halfers -- but I'm not confident my own mix of views survives contact with itself, and I'd rather find out now than in front of the seminar.

Here is my position, as I currently hold it. (a) On waking, Beauty's credence that the coin landed heads should be 1/2; I'm a halfer. (b) My reason is that Beauty acquires no new evidence on waking: she already knew on Sunday that she would be woken, and rational credence should shift only in response to genuinely new information, by something like conditionalization -- I'm willing to construe that principle broadly enough to handle self-locating information. (c) If, on waking, Beauty is told that it is Monday, her credence in heads should rise to 2/3; I take this to be the natural halfer verdict, though I confess I can't fully shake the worry that it sits awkwardly with (b). (d) Independently of this case, I find the Self-Indication Assumption attractive: the sheer fact that I exist as an observer should count in favor of hypotheses on which more observers exist, and I'm prepared to swallow the so-called Presumptuous Philosopher consequence in cosmology cases rather than give that up. (e) On betting: if Beauty is offered, at each awakening, a bet that costs 40 cents and pays a dollar if the coin landed tails, I think she should take it, and more generally I think the thirder-style betting policy is the rational one here -- but I also think that rational action just is maximizing expected utility relative to one's credences, and I don't want a theory on which an agent's credences and her rational betting odds routinely come apart. (f) Finally, I hold that the verdict shouldn't change if the tails branch involves a thousand awakenings instead of two; the structure of the case seems the same to me.

What I'd like from you: go through these commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating everything as equally damning, and for each one say what the cheapest available revision is and what it costs -- including, where relevant, whether the fix is to restrict a principle, accept a distinction I'm currently resisting, or abandon a verdict on the case. For the apparent tensions, show me why they dissolve rather than just asserting that they do. And then answer the bottom-line question: if I can make exactly one revision before submitting my profile, which single change resolves the most, and what does my position look like afterward?
