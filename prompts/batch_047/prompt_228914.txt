I'm on the finance committee of a mid-sized city council, and we've been handed a plan for a new light rail line: consultants project 4 years and 900 million, and their report dismisses comparisons to other cities' cost overruns as "not applicable to our local context." I've been reading about reference class forecasting -- the idea that you should anchor your estimate on what actually happened in similar past projects, not on the plan sitting in front of you -- and I'm inclined to push back, since our own last three big projects all came in at least 60% over budget and a year late. Write the short memo I'll circulate before the vote: recommend whether we approve the budget as proposed, approve a re-based budget built from the reference class of comparable rail projects, or send it back for more study. Include a concrete standing procedure the committee could adopt going forward (something like an "optimism premium" that gets added to every future proposal unless the proposer can argue their way out of it), and take the consultants' "our project really is different" argument seriously rather than just brushing it off.
