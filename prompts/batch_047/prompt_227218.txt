I'm taking a political philosophy seminar this term, and for a revision handout I want a decision tree that takes my classmates from their untutored intuitions to a position on the nature of freedom — specifically sorting between a pure negative-liberty view (freedom as absence of interference), a republican non-domination view (freedom as absence of arbitrary power over you, whether or not it is exercised), a positive or self-mastery view (freedom as being governed by your rational or authentic will), and whatever hybrid or outlier positions the tree's logical space forces into existence.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from the names: six to nine branching nodes, each phrased as a question about a concrete case that someone could answer without knowing any of the labels, and then the positions located in the space of answer combinations. The following cases must appear at branch nodes, because I want them doing diagnostic work:

1. A slave with a benevolent master who never once interferes (separates negative from republican).
2. A person whose unimpaired choices are systematically thwarted by an internal compulsion or phobia, with no external interferer (separates negative from positive/self-mastery).
3. Interference that is reliably constrained by law the interferer cannot change at will, versus identical interference subject to the interferer's discretion (separates "power exists but is checked" from "power is genuinely absent").
4. State interference that the citizen had an equal say in authorizing — whether being a co-author of your own constraint changes its freedom-status at all.

Two further requirements. First, at least one node should isolate whether interference counts against freedom only when it frustrates an actual desire, or also when it forecloses an option the person never wanted (the person who never wishes to leave the city, but the gate is locked). Second, wherever a combination of answers is unoccupied by any real position, keep it as a leaf anyway and diagnose in one sentence why it is empty — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where two positions on my list cannot be separated by any question on the tree, say so explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. Every leaf gets the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
