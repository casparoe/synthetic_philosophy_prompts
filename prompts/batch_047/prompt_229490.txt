Last night I had a long, unresolved argument with a friend about whether the United States should move toward the European model of hate speech regulation. At one point she stated her considered verdicts on five cases, all under current First Amendment doctrine as she understands it: (1) burning a cross on a Black family's lawn with intent to intimidate — punishable; (2) a soapbox speech calling for the deportation of an entire ethnic group, naming no individual — protected; (3) a racist slur hurled face-to-face at a stranger on the street — punishable; (4) a book denying the Holocaust — protected; (5) a swastika-bearing march deliberately routed through a neighborhood where many survivors live — protected, though she called this her hardest case. She then refused to say how she would decide a sixth: a coordinated campaign in which dozens of accounts send racist messages to one specific journalist for months, with no explicit threats, until the journalist leaves the platform. Predict her verdict. Identify the two most plausible rules consistent with verdicts (1)–(5), state which way each cuts on the sixth case, say which rule you take her to be running and why, and name a further case that would discriminate between the two rules. Finally, tell me what would change your prediction: which feature of the sixth case is doing the most work in your analysis, such that altering it would flip the predicted verdict, and where your attribution of a rule to her is most likely to go wrong.
