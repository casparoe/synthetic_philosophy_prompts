Write an internal company memo, around 600 words, from the HR team at a mid-sized tech firm. The firm's lunchtime Stoicism club has been such a hit with leadership that this year's redundancy round is to be run entirely on Stoic principles. Departing staff are to be reminded that their jobs are externals and therefore indifferents; each exit interview will open with a sorting exercise in which the employee seperates their concerns into what is up to them and what is not; the severance package is to be described as a preferred indifferent that a sage wouldn't make a fuss about. Include the usual memo furniture: subject line, three or four action items, a short FAQ, an upbeat sign-off.

The comedy has to come from the Stoicism being right on the page -- prohairesis, the dichotomy of control, the archer analogy where doing your best is yours and hitting the target isn't -- while the use it's being put to is grotesque. The memo writer must never notice that Stoic ethics also has quite a lot to say about justice, our roles towards others, and what the people sending the memo owe their colleagues; the reader should have noticed by about paragraph three. Deadpan the whole way, no winking, and any Stoic slogans should be paraphrased rather than given as verbatim quotes. The target is the corporate self-help repurposing of Stoicism, not the philosophy itself and not any actual person.
