My uncle is one of those guys who's read two books about WWII and is now certain that Nuremberg was just the winners dressing up revenge in legal robes, and we got into it at a family thing last weekend. The annoying part is I kept realizing mid-argument that I wasn't sure where I actually stood myself -- like, I think the trials were on balance a good thing, but I couldn't say exactly which criticisms of them I'd concede and which I'd reject. I co-run a little philosophy meetup and I want to turn this into next month's session: a "where do you actually land on Nuremberg and Tokyo" decision tree that people walk through individually before we discuss.

So please build me that tree. Start from the shared fact that the tribunals happened roughly as they did, and end in named positions on their legitimacy. Hard requirements: every branch node has to be a concrete question someone can answer without knowing any position's name or the relevant legal vocabulary -- so things like "if the war had gone the other way and the losers had tried the winners under an identically structured tribunal, would you accept those verdicts too?", or "should it matter that aggressive war wasn't a codified crime before 1945, or can some things be crimes because they're monstrous?", or "the Soviets tried to pin Katyn on the Germans at Nuremberg, and Dönitz basically escaped punishment for unrestricted submarine warfare once it came out the Americans did the same -- does this kind of thing discredit the whole proceeding, or only parts of it?". The leaves I definitely want represented: total victors'-justice rejectionism, the natural-law defense (retroactivity excused by the enormity of the crimes), the legalist middle (war crimes charges fine, crimes-against-peace and conspiracy charges not), the pragmatist "flawed but a net gain as precedent" view, and a leaf for people who think Nuremberg was defensible but Tokyo specifically wasn't. If your logical space throws up a combination of answers nobody actually holds, include the empty leaf with a sentence on whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored. And flag any pair of leaves your questions can't pull apart.

Around 6 to 9 branch questions total. The questions need to do the sorting themselves -- someone who's never heard of nullum crimen sine lege should still land somewhere coherent. And please no node that amounts to "is revenge bad, yes or no," because that's just asking people which camp they already like, which is exactly what I'm trying to get around.
