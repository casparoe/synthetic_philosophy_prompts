I’m a high school student taking a philosophy of mind seminar, and our class has been discussing the symbol grounding problem. For a project, imagine two researchers who disagree about whether a large language model can genuinely understand the words it uses. The causal-embodiment theorist argues that symbols become meaningful only when they are appropriately connected to perception, action, and the ability to intervene in the world. The relational-functional theorist argues that enough of the relevant structure can be learned from relationships among symbols and other internal representations, even without direct bodily experience.

Suppose both researchers examine a new system called Grove. Grove was first trained on ordinary text, but it now operates a greenhouse: it receives camera images, soil-moisture readings, temperature measurements, and maintenance logs; it can adjust lights, vents, and irrigation; and it learns from whether particular plants improve or deteriorate. However, Grove has no human-style body, cannot touch or smell anything, and describes its own interventions only in words. Draft the joint statement that the two researchers could sign after an adversarial collaboration about whether Grove’s uses of terms such as dry, wilted, overheated, and healthy are grounded.

Include a numbered section of claims both sides accept about Grove’s architecture, capacities, and limitations; then provide a table of their remaining disagreements, with each position stated in wording its own advocate would endorse. Identify the most important cruxes, especially whether grounding requires a particular kind of body or only the right causal and functional relations. For each crux, describe an experiment, modification to Grove, or possible failure that both researchers agree would be relevant, and record in advance what result each side predicts. Finally, distinguish any disagreement that appears to be merely terminological from the disagreement that would remain even if both sides used the same definition of understanding.
