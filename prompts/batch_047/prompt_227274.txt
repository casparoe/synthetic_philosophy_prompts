In my seminar on well-being, a student recently argued that Nozick's experience machine teaches us nothing against hedonism, on the following grounds: plugging in, she said, is simply like choosing a lifetime of one's favorite entertainment rather than going out into the cold, and since we regularly spend our evenings on films, games, and novels without supposing that our lives are thereby wasted, the near-universal reluctance to plug in is nothing but status quo bias masquerading as insight -- the machine is just the streaming service with better engineering. I suspect the analogy is doing all the argumentative work, but I want to treat it seriously rather than dismiss it. Audit it: state explicitly what maps onto what (the headset, the pre-programmed experiences, the choice to enter, the life left behind), identify which disanalogies are irrelevant to her conclusion and which ones bear directly on it, say what the analogy quietly imports from ordinary recreation -- recovery, sociality, the fact that one returns to an unaltered world -- that the machine lacks, and assess whether her status-quo-bias diagnosis survives once those are made explicit; if you think a better analogy for the machine exists (a lifelong dream, a consoling lie one asks not to have exposed, a drug), say which and why. Please give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
