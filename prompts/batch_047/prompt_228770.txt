I am a PhD student in formal philosophy of science, and my dissertation defends the following position on the verisimilitude problem: the collapse of Popper's content-based definition under the Miller–Tichý arguments — which showed that on his account false theories cannot be ranked by closeness to the truth and that the rankings depend on the choice of descriptive language — does not demonstrate that truthlikeness is a pseudo-notion. It demonstrates only that truthlikeness comparisons must be relativized to a fixed, sufficiently fine-grained propositional framework. Once this relativity is made explicit, I argue, the likeness-based program (Niiniluoto-style distance measures over constituents, Oddie-style partial-order approaches) is back on its feet, because the residual arbitrariness in framework choice is no worse than the language dependence we already tolerate in confirmation theory and other epistemic notions we accept without embarrassment.

What would count against this position? I want a map of its exposure, not a general survey of the literature. Please distinguish arguments or formal results that would be decisive from ones that would merely be awkward, and say which standard objections I can safely absorb. In particular: Do defenders of the likeness approach themselves concede anything that undermines my "harmless relativity" move? Is there a plausible impossibility-style result showing that framework relativization collapses my position into scepticism about theory comparison, or into the very subjectivism Popper wanted verisimilitude to block? What does my view predict — about scientific realism, theory change, or belief revision — that its rivals do not? Finally, an honest assessment: has the relativization itself insulated the view from refutation, and if so, is that a defect for a position of this kind or simply the price of adequacy?
