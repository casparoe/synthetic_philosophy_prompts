"Hofstadter's Copycat program proved that concepts have no boundaries at all. When it solves letter-string analogies — say, if abc goes to abd, what does mrrjjj go to? — it lets a concept like 'rightmost letter' slip under pressure into 'rightmost group,' which is how it arrives at answers like mrrkkk. Hofstadter concluded that human concepts work exactly the same way: there is no such thing as the meaning of a word, only ad hoc resemblances improvised on the spot, which is why he treats dictionaries as basically fictions and why he follows Wittgenstein in holding that language is a game with no rules. It follows that any word can slip to mean just about anything if the context pushes hard enough — his own example is 'captain' sliding around until it applies to the lead singer of a band.
