So I've been chewing on this for weeks after a fight in a comment thread about whether having kids is selfish, and I want a number, not a shrug.

The thing: pretty much everyone agrees that if you somehow KNEW a child you conceived would have a life of nothing but severe suffering, it would be wrong to conceive them. But almost nobody thinks you do anything wrong by NOT conceiving a child who would have had an amazing life. There are zillions of possible happy people humanity could bring into existence and nobody calls us monsters for not getting started. So the bad side generates a duty and the good side doesnt. That's lopsided, and I can't tell if it's a deep moral fact or a glitch in our gut reactions.

My own stab at justifying it: creating someone is like mailing a package to an address that doesn't exist yet. You can't owe a future nobody a present, but you can absolutely owe it to them-in-advance not to send them a bomb. I like this analogy but I suspect it just restates the lopsidedness instead of explaining it. Tell me if it does any work.

My own credence is around 0.65, but honestly I made that up to have something to argue with.
