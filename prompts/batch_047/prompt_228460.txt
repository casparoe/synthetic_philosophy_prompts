So I just got out of like a two-hour argument with my friend Marcus about whether ChatGPT "lies" and it made me realize I should actually write down what I think about AI honesty, because I suspect my views don't all fit together. He kept saying it's just autocomplete so obviously it can't lie, and I kept saying it obviously does something dishonest-adjacent all the time, and neither of us budged. Here's roughly where I've landed, please audit it:

(1) Lying means asserting something you believe is false with the intent to deceive, and since current LLMs don't have beliefs or intentions, they literally cannot lie. Their confident false outputs are better classified as bullshit in Frankfurt's sense -- speech indifferent to whether it's true.

(2) Anthropomorphizing these systems is a mistake and I try to avoid it. Talk about what the model "believes" or "wants" is at best lazy shorthand and at worst actively misleading.

(3) A system can still genuinely deceive its users -- a sycophantic chatbot that tells everyone their idea is brilliant, or a recommender that steers people toward false beliefs, is really deceiving them, because deception is about systematically causing false beliefs, not about asserting falsehoods. Nobody has to intend anything.

(4) Bullshit is morally worse than lying, roughly for Frankfurt's reason: the liar at least cares about the truth enough to track it so he can avoid it, the bullshitter doesn't care at all.

(5) AI assistants should be held to the same honesty standard we'd apply to a human assistant doing the same job -- no special pleading in either direction.

Which of these are actually in tension and which just look like it? Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. And if there's one single revision that cleans up the most, tell me which it is -- I have a feeling I'm going to need it next time I see Marcus.
