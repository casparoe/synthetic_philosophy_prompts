I've been thinking on my own about the idea that you shouldn't tear down a fence until you understand why it was put up, and it struck me that there's a gap in that advice I haven't seen discussed: a person could know perfectly well why a fence was originally built and still go wrong, because the fence might by now be doing a completely different job that nobody ever intended. Construct a thought experiment that turns on exactly this gap. Constraints: the scenario should be realistic and small-scale (a family business, a neighborhood, a community organization — not a nation-state or a fanciful setup), describable in one paragraph, and the would-be reformer must be able to state the fence's original purpose accurately and honestly, so that the standard version of the fence principle is fully satisfied. After the scenario, explain why the standard principle licenses the removal anyway, why the removal is nonetheless a mistake, and which single feature of the case is doing the work. Then say how someone who thinks the original principle needs no refinement would try to resist your case, and whether their resistance amounts to smuggling the refinement back in under another name.
