Everyone around me -- my doctor, the anti-stigma campaigns, my health insurer's own pamphlets -- says depression is an illness just like diabetes: a real disease of an organ (the brain), not a character flaw, and that's why it deserves insurance coverage, medication, and zero shame. Then I fell down a rabbit hole of Szasz-adjacent writers who say the comparison is pure propaganda, since there's no lesion you can point to, only behaviour somebody disapproves of. Can you actually audit the analogy instead of just picking a team? What maps onto what -- the organ, the lesion, the symptoms, the treatment, the patient who complies or doesn't -- and where does the mapping break? For instance: can you refuse an antidepressant the way you can refuse insulin, is grief after a loss a symptom or a correct response, and does "disease" smuggle in something about who's allowed to force treatment on whom? And the thing I actually care about: which of the practical payoffs people hang on the analogy -- insurance parity, less stigma, doctors rather than judges having authority over the mad -- still stand once the disanalogies are on the table, and which were being carried by the analogy alone?
