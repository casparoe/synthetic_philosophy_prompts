Next month I’m serving, somewhat to my surprise, on the jury of a national magazine-photography prize, and the experience is already forcing me to confront a question I’ve managed to dodge in fifteen years of covering science and ideas. In my usual beat, disagreements are, at least in principle, resolvable: someone runs the experiment, checks the data, consults the better measurement. Now I’m sitting in deliberations over a shortlisted photo series that I find technically impressive but cold, derivative, and calculated to please — while the most senior juror, a critic with four decades of experience who has seemingly seen every exhibition and contact sheet of note, insists it is the strongest entry by a wide margin, and speaks about its composition, its dialogue with the tradition, and its restraint in terms I simply don’t have access to. I can’t tell whether he is perceiving something I’m not, the way a trained radiologist sees a tumor where I see gray smudges, or whether he is merely reporting his own preferences in more elaborate vocabulary than mine.

So I’d like advice on two levels. First, the practical one: how should I, and how should the jury, actually proceed? Should I defer to his track record the way I’d defer to a sommelier’s palate or a chess master’s evaluation of a position — and if that analogy fails, where exactly does it fail? Or should we treat our reactions as incommensurable votes and just count hands? Second, and more unsettling to me as someone who writes for a living about which claims deserve belief: is there anything I could be *wrong* about here? I have an immediate, confident dislike of the work, but I notice I also dislike the gallery circuit it comes from, and I haven’t spent much time with the photographic tradition it engages. If some judges are genuinely better than others at judging beauty, what makes them better, and how would I know whether my own verdict reflects the work or merely my limitations and prejudices? If, on the other hand, no verdict can be mistaken, I’d like to understand what we are even doing in that deliberation room when we argue past midnight.
