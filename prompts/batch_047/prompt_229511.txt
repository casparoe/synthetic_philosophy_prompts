I'm a junior in an experimental philosophy seminar and we've spent like three weeks on the Knobe effect, and my prof keeps joking that the two camps would rather each write ten more papers than sit down and write one together. So for my final project I want to actually do that sitting down for them.

Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps:

- Camp M ("the concept is moralized"): the help/harm asymmetry in cases like the chairman-and-the-environment vignette shows that the ordinary concept of intentional action itself incorporates moral considerations, so the side-effect harming chairman literally did it intentionally in the folk sense.
- Camp P ("it's performance, not competence"): the asymmetry is an artifact of how people use the word "intentional" in these tasks -- to express blame or signal bad character, or because of pragmatic implicature -- and doesn't tell us anything deep about the underlying concept.

Neither side should be a strawman; Camp P's strongest version isn't just "people misread the question", and Camp M has to deal with the skill and luck cases, not just the original vignette.

Format it in exactly these sections, in this order:

1. "What we both accept" -- a numbered list of claims both camps sign, including what's actually established about the experimental results themselves (about 200 words)
2. "Where we really disagree" -- each disagreement stated precisely and neutrally enough that both camps would accept the wording of their own position; flag any disagreements from the literature you think were actually verbal rather than substantive (about 250 words)
3. "Cruxes" -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp; for each crux, give a specific study design or existing result both camps agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each camp predicts it shows (about 300 words)
4. "What would change our minds" -- one short paragraph from each camp saying what result, if it came out against them, would force a real revision of their view (about 150 words total)

Be honest in section 3 if there's a crux where the two camps make the same prediction -- that matters too, and it definately shouldn't be papered over.
