Write an essay on the "constitutive luck" problem within the broader debate over moral luck. Bernard Williams and Thomas Nagel argue that factors beyond an agent's control—including the kind of person one turns out to be, shaped by temperament and formative circumstance—seem to bear on our moral assessments of them, yet Kantian intuition holds that moral worth must depend only on what is within an agent's control. Does the existence of constitutive luck show that our ordinary practices of moral judgment are incoherent, or can a plausible account of moral responsibility accommodate it? Defend a specific verdict on whether constitutive luck undermines the very possibility of moral responsibility as usually conceived, and justify your answer against the strongest objections.
