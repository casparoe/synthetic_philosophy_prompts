I'm working on a piece about design arguments in cosmology, and I keep coming back to Charles Darwin. He spent his career showing how the appearance of design in nature could arise without a designer, chiefly by dismantling Paley's watchmaker analogy. How do you think he would have responded to today's fine-tuning argument, which points to the universe's improbably life-permitting physical constants as evidence of a designer? Would he have seen a multiverse, or something like Lee Smolin's cosmological natural selection, as a satisfying extension of his own reasoning, or would he have argued that the analogy to biological selection collapses once you're talking about the constants of physics rather than populations of organisms?
