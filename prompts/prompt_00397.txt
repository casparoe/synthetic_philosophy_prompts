As a novelist, I've come to see money the way I see the currencies I invent for my fictional worlds: a shared fiction, sustained only by our collective agreement to believe in it, with no reality beyond that belief. Given this parallel, why do economists insist on treating inflation and monetary policy as objective, almost natural phenomena, rather than acknowledging that if enough people simply stopped believing in the story, the problem would dissolve—the way a novel's world vanishes the moment the reader closes the book?
