I've been reading about functionalism in the philosophy of mind on my own—no formal academic background, just something I got curious about after watching a few documentaries on AI and consciousness—and I think I've finally grasped the core idea: mental states like pain or belief are defined by their functional or causal role rather than by the specific physical stuff they're made of. This is what's called multiple realizability, if I understand correctly: a brain, a computer, or even some alien silicon-based creature could all instantiate "pain" as long as each occupies the same functional role within a larger causal system. What I can't quite let go of is what this seems to imply for mind-uploading and personal immortality. If my mind really just is a functional pattern rather than a particular clump of neurons, doesn't it follow that if scientists eventually scanned my brain and reconstructed that exact same causal and functional organization inside a computer, that computer would simply *be* me, waking up with continuous consciousness, memories, and a first-person perspective, the same way I wake up each morning after sleeping?

So my question is this: does functionalism, and specifically the multiple realizability thesis, actually license that conclusion about uploading and survival? I keep going back and forth on whether the "mind as software" analogy is basically settled once you accept multiple realizability, since it seems like a natural extension of the same reasoning used to argue that a thermostat and a brain could both realize some mental state. I'd like to know whether this picture of identity transfer through functional duplication is something mainstream functionalists actually endorse, or whether I'm smuggling in extra assumptions that the theory itself doesn't support.
