Having spent the last few years working through the decision-theory literature after a career in experimental physics, I've become intrigued by evidential cooperation in large worlds, but one point keeps tripping me up. ECL's case for cooperation seems to rest on treating the correlation between my decision procedure and those of causally isolated agents elsewhere in the universe as decision-relevant, in the way subjunctive dependence is decision-relevant for superrationality in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma. Yet this looks structurally similar to the correlation between smoking and the lesion in the classic smoking-lesion problem, which is usually taken to be exactly the kind of correlation an evidential decision theorist should learn to ignore via something like the tickle defense. Could you explain what principled distinction ECL proponents draw between these two cases—why the correlation across large-world agents is supposed to license cooperative action rather than being dismissed as spurious in the same way?
