So I had this long, kind of frustrating argument with my friend Jake last night over drinks about where I should put my annual charity donation. I was leaning toward this local job-training nonprofit that helps ex-offenders find work, but Jake kept pushing back saying it's basically impossible to know if that money does more good than harm in the long run. Like, maybe one of the guys who gets a job because of the program ends up having a kid who grows up to do something terrible, or maybe the job he takes displaces someone else who was already struggling, and that person's life spirals in a way that ripples out for decades. Jake's point was that once you start trying to trace out all the downstream effects of literally any action, you have no way of actually tallying up whether it's net positive, so trying to "do the math" on which charity is better is kind of a fool's errand.

Something about this bugged me a lot, cause it feels like it proves too much — like if Jake's right, you'd never be justified in doing ANYTHING, including nothing (since not helping the guy also has unknowable ripple effects). But I couldn't articulate what's wrong with his reasoning, or if there even is anything wrong with it. Am I missing some flaw in his logic, or is he onto something real that should actually change how I think about giving money away? I guess I'm looking for some kind of framework for how to make these decisions without pretending I can predict the future, but also without just throwing up my hands.
