So i've been going down this rabbit hole on some AI safety/rationalist forums about this idea called "acausal trade" - basically the notion that two agents (or civilizations, or minds that will never exist at the same time) who can never actually meet or communicate could still coordinate with each other just by each simulating/predicting what the other would do, kinda like Newcomb's problem but stretched across totally separate branches of the universe, or even across different possible universes that never causally touch. It messes with my head because it sounds like pure sci-fi but people talk about it like a serious decision theory thing that could actually matter for how superintelligent AIs behave.

I'd love to see this turned into an actual short story. Maybe it follows two intelligences - could be superintelligent AIs, alien civilizations, simulated minds, whatever - who never make contact but each acts as if the other is "watching," and they strike a deal purely through reasoning about each other's incentives, no signal ever passing between them. Bonus points if you get into what it would actually feel like from the inside to make some big costly sacrifice now, purely on faith that a far off, maybe not even real, agent will reciprocate somewhere you'll never get to see. Doesn't need a happy ending, I just want the story to sit in that eerie, vertigo-y feeling the concept gives me.
