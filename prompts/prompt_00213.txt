In "The Human Use of Human Beings," Norbert Wiener warned that once a machine is set to pursue a purpose via feedback, it may carry out that purpose with a literalism that escapes its operators' real intentions. Drawing on his writing about feedback, purpose, and automation, how do you think Wiener would have approached today's "shutdown problem" in AI safety -- the difficulty of designing an advanced system that reliably lets itself be corrected or switched off? Would he have treated corrigibility as a tractable engineering constraint, or as a symptom of a deeper confusion in how we delegate goals to autonomous systems?
