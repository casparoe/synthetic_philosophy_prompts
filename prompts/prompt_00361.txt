I've been mulling over something in my own working life lately and could use an outside read on it. Our department just rolled out a new "research productivity" dashboard that scores each of us every year on citation counts, grant dollars brought in, and teaching evals, and our raises (such as they are) get pegged to the number. Ever since this started I've noticed I think less about whether an argument I'm writing is actually any good and more about whether it'll "count" for the year. I used to love just hashing out an idea with the guy down the hall over coffee, but now I catch myself quietly sizing up whether his new project is going to make mine look weak come review season. My partner pointed out recently that I don't light up talking about my own papers the way I used to - and she's not wrong. Even when something of mine gets into a decent journal it feels less like a thing I made and more like a box that got ticked off somewhere.

Is this just what happens to everyone in a big bureaucratic institution and I should quit whining, or is there something more precise going on here - some underlying structure to what's happening to my relationship with my own work and with my colleagues - that a good theory could actually help me name and maybe do something about? I keep feeling like there's a sharper diagnosis available than "modern academia is soul-crushing, news at 11," but I can't quite land on it myself.
