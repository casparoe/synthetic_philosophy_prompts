Explain, with precision, the conceptual problem that Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" poses for the analytic-synthetic distinction. I want the argument itself and its internal structure, not a summary of its reception or a list of who accepted it. Work through the following four pieces, and be explicit about what each step presupposes:

1. The dependency claim. Why does Quine hold that the analytic-synthetic distinction is not an independent distinction but parasitic on the a priori-a posteriori distinction—specifically, that analyticity is to be understood as a species of necessity, namely the a posteriori impossibility of a sentence's being false? State precisely what this dependency does to the project of explaining analyticity without appeal to the a priori.

2. The regress. Why does the attempt to explain analyticity in terms of synonymy, and then synonymy in turn in terms of analyticity (or of interchangeability salva veritate, or of meaning), yield a circle or a regress? Walk through the structure of the argument step by step, including why Quine rejects the criterion of interchangeability salva veritate and why an appeal to meaning or to convention does not break the circle.

3. The confirmation story. How does the claim that empirical sentences are not tested one by one but only as a system, and that the evidence bears on any given sentence only gradually and as part of the whole web of belief, undermine the idea of a clean boundary between what is settled by meaning and what is settled by experience? In particular, why does this matter for the status of the borderline sentences at the center of the web—the theorems of logic and mathematics?

4. The weakened residue. Where does this leave the possibility of a defensible, weakened version of the distinction—for example, in terms of a difference in degree of confirmation, or of cognitive cogency—and what would such a weakened version have to give up?

Respond in the style of an expert analytic philosopher writing for another working philosopher. Think very carefully before answering, and work through the dependency structure of the arguments—what each step presupposes and what work it is doing—rather than merely restating the conclusion. Do not provide a bibliography, a reading list, or citations.
