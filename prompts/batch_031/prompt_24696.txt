What is your actual credence that the problem of logical omniscience is a genuine, structural flaw in the standard Bayesian framework, rather than merely an idealization that finite agents cannot meet, where a genuine flaw means that the coherence axioms, as standardly motivated, are not the correct norm of rational credence even for an ideal agent? I do not want "it depends on how the axioms are stated"; I want a number and a decomposition: how much of your credence rides on whether the problem is only pragmatic, a finite agent's inability to enumerate all the consequences of its beliefs, or whether it reaches the ideal theory itself, as in the diagonal argument that no doxastic state can be both complete and fully transparent about its own beliefs; how much rides on whether the restricted-domain response, confining the domain of credences to propositions the agent can actually entertain, preserves enough coherence to retain the Dutch-book protection that motivates the axioms; and how much rides on whether the probability axioms can be defended as a limit ideal, the limit of a sequence of bounded agents' credences. Then tell me which of those sub-questions your number is most sensitive to, and what argument or result would move it most.
