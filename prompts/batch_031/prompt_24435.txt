I am writing a novel in which a couple must decide whether to conceive a child this month, when the mother's untreated medical condition makes the child likely to be born with a serious but treatable condition, or to wait six months, in which case the mother will have completed treatment and the child will almost certainly be healthy. I need a decision tree that sorts the moral positions a character could hold on this decision, so that I can distinguish my characters by how they reason about this specific case rather than by the labels they use. The tree should begin from the fact that the child who would be born now is not the child who would be born later, and branch on the questions that actually separate the positions: whether the decision can harm the child who results from it; whether the rightness of the decision is a matter of the aggregate or average welfare of the lives that will be lived; whether the parents' duty is to the particular child they will have or to the class of possible children; whether there is any sense in which the later, healthier child is owed her existence; and whether the decision is morally significant at all if no one is made worse off. Each leaf should name the position (or "unnamed") and note its most awkward commitment. I would like about six to eight leaves, and I would like you to flag any two leaves the tree cannot really separate, and to note any combination of answers the tree allows that no one actually occupies, and why.
