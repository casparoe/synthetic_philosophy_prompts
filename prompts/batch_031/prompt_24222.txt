I retired last spring after some thirty years in condensed matter physics, and in the months since I've been reading a great deal of philosophy — ethics, the philosophy of mind, decision theory — and, to my own surprise, the conceptual side of the AI-safety literature. What has me is a question that feels almost like one I would have set for my own group: can we define, operationally, what it is for an agent to "have side effects," and thereby penalize it for them, without first writing down a complete specification of what the world ought to be? I've tried to pin down my own intuitions on it, and before I write them up for a small reading group I'd be grateful for a consistency check. Here is roughly where I've landed. (1) I'm committed to the core ambition: impact should be defined without a full specification of value, because a full specification is precisely what the project is trying to avoid. (2) The natural baseline, it seems to me, is "the agent refrains from acting" — an action's impact is the difference between the expected state of the world if the agent acts and the expected state if it does nothing. (3) I also want the measure to be objective in a strong sense: the same physical intervention should get the same impact score no matter what the agent believes or what internal model it is running. (4) I think the right granularity is step-level — each individual action should be charged for its local side effects rather than the whole trajectory, so that the agent can still plan and the penalty does not simply collapse into "do nothing at all." (5) And I want robustness: even when the agent's model of the environment is wrong, the impact penalty should still point in the right direction, nudging the agent toward less interference rather than more.

Could you audit whether this package actually hangs together? I suspect it does not, but I cannot tell which commitment is load-bearing and which is the one that has to go, and I would rather not signal which I would be most willing to drop. For each real tension you find, please tell me which two commitments it sits between, how severe it is, and what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Please be careful to distinguish genuine conflicts from ones that only look like they conflict and dissolve once the definitions are spelled out — I would much rather you under-claim than flag everything as broken. If I had to make exactly one revision, which single change would clean up the most? Please keep your entire reply to no more than four hundred words.
