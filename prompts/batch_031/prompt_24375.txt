I am assembling, before I evaluate any particular proposal, the specification that an adequate theory of rational betting under uncertainty must meet. The setting is repeated betting against fixed or slowly drifting odds, where wealth compounds multiplicatively and the bettor may be uncertain about the true win probability — the domain in which the relevant average is geometric rather than arithmetic, and the question is what makes a stake rational rather than the recitation of a formula. Write out the desiderata: the list of things such a theory must deliver to count as an account of rational staking, with the motivating case behind each — the concrete betting situation, or the specific dispute between staking rules, that the requirement is meant to adjudicate — and a mark on whether each is non-negotiable (a theory that fails it is simply wrong) or a preference of a particular camp (the log-optimalists, the expected-utility maximizers who fix a utility function, or the risk-averse camp that stakes a fixed fraction of the log-optimal amount). At minimum it must handle the one-shot bet, the long run of repeated bets, the bettor who is uncertain about the win probability and updates from the outcomes, correlated rather than independent outcomes, the boundary at ruin, and the question of whether borrowing is in scope. It must respect, and not collapse, the following distinctions: the long-run growth rate of the wealth process versus the expected terminal wealth; the ensemble average over many parallel gamblers versus the time average along a single trajectory; a stake that is optimal at each step given current information versus one that is optimal for the horizon as a whole; and a property of the strategy's asymptotic behavior versus a property of the bettor's utility function. It must be consistent with the standard asymptotic results — that the log-optimal stake maximizes the almost-sure long-run growth rate of wealth and that no admissible stake achieves a strictly higher growth rate, and that the stake maximizing expected terminal wealth, on any positive edge, is either the maximum allowed stake or, if borrowing is unbounded, has no finite optimum at all — and it must say how the optimal stake depends on the uncertainty in the win probability, rather than pretending the posterior mean can simply be substituted into the one-shot formula. Then flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — watching in particular for tensions among the demands that the theory be utility-neutral, sensitive to the bettor's risk tolerance, robust to misspecification of the odds, and sharp about the precise estimate — and say whether the list is satisfiable at all, and which single requirement does the most work excluding candidates. Close with a short scorecard: in one or two lines each, how the full log-optimal stake, a fixed fraction of it, the expected-wealth maximizer, and a generic expected-utility maximizer fare against the list.
