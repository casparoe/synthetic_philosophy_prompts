For a philosophy class I'm writing up a response to a friend's argument, and I'd like help locating where it goes wrong. My friend's claim is that Lewis Carroll's "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles" (1895) proves that deductive inference is never rationally justified. Here is his argument, with the steps numbered:

(1) To be rationally entitled to accept a conclusion C on the basis of premises P, a person must have a reason for the step from P to C.
(2) The only possible reason for that step is a further premise, R, that states the rule of inference—for example, a premise saying that if the premises are true, the conclusion must be true.
(3) But then the person needs a reason for R, which would have to be a still further premise, R', and so on, without end.
(4) A never-ending chain of reasons never confers entitlement, because the chain never reaches a foundation that could do the justificatory work.
(5) Therefore, no one is ever rationally entitled to accept a conclusion by deduction.
(6) But I am rationally entitled to accept the conclusion of a valid argument—for example, I am entitled to accept that Socrates is mortal, on the basis of the premises that all men are mortal and that Socrates is a man.
(7) So at least one of (1) through (5) must be false.

My friend's support for (2) is the story itself. In the dialogue, the Tortoise accepts the two premises and the rule, yet still refuses to accept the conclusion until the rule is written down as an extra premise; then he demands a further premise for the rule that governs that extra premise, and so on. He says this shows the rule can only be introduced as a premise, so (2) is forced. I can't find the exit. The conclusion (5) is absurd—it would make every act of deduction unjustified, including the very act of evaluating this argument—so I know something gives, but I can't say what.

Could you tell me which premise or step to reject, explain why that step looked plausible, and say what it costs to reject it rather than some other step? Also, please note how confident you are in the claims you make, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
