A popular article I read summarizes updateless decision theory like this: 'UDT says you don't choose actions, you choose algorithms. Instead of asking "what should I do in this situation?", you ask "what algorithm, if I were to install it, would maximize my expected utility?" Once you've picked the algorithm, you just run it. This means UDT never uses counterfactuals — it just computes what would happen if the agent followed each candidate algorithm, which is just a matter of running the simulation. And because the agent's choice is really a choice of code, UDT handles Newcomb's problem by observing that a one-boxing algorithm gets the money, so the agent installs the one-boxing algorithm. The theory is elegant and, unlike causal decision theory, it has no need for the notion of a counterfactual.' The article goes on to claim that this makes UDT a drop-in replacement for causal decision theory in all cases. How much of the summary is right? Go sentence by sentence and tell me what a decision theorist working on updateless decision theory would accept, what they'd wince at, and what is just wrong — and whether the article's later claim about a drop-in replacement follows from anything true in the summary.
