I've been following the debate about supertasks, and especially Thomson's lamp, and it has stalled in a way I can't diagnose. One side holds that completing infinitely many operations in a finite time is not logically impossible, and that the lamp's oscillating on/off sequence just means the process's description underdetermines the final state. The other side holds that supertasks are impossible, for three reasons: there is no final operation after which the task is finished, so there is no well-defined completed state; the state sequence has no limit, so there is no fact of the matter about the lamp's state; and no physical system can perform infinitely many operations in finite time. My working hypothesis is that these are not really one disagreement: the first reason is a red herring (Zeno's runner also has no final segment, and we accept that he arrives), the third is a question about physical realizability being answered under the name of a question about metaphysical conceivability, and only the second is substantive, and it is really a conceptual question about whether a process with a non-convergent state sequence counts as completed at all. Is that the right diagnosis — a verbal or conceptual disagreement, two questions under one name, or a real metaphysical split?
