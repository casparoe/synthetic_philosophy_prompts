I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about selling stuff, going by my gut on cases, and I can't get a consistent story out of the lot. A guy selling one of his kidneys to a stranger for $50k, fully informed, healthy otherwise: I'm fine with it. A plasma center paying you $50 for a pint, twice a week: obviously fine. A gas station tripling the price of bottled water three days after a hurricane: not fine, even though I get that its just the market. A tomato grower raising prices after a frost kills half the crop: fine, that's just markets working. Selling your vote in an election: not fine, full stop. A group paying low-income residents $20 to show up and vote, no instruction on how to vote: thats the one I keep going back and forth on. Selling your place in a line at the DMV for $20: fine. Selling your place in a kidney transplant waiting list: not fine. A paid surrogate carrying a baby for a couple who can't have one: fine. Selling a newborn at birth: not fine.

The water one and the tomato one look the same to me structurally — price goes up, someone profits, buyers pay more — but my gut splits on them and I don't know why. Same with the line ones. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, stated precisely enough that I could test it on new cases? What does it say about the turnout-payment one, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also what does it predict for cases I haven't listed, like a woman in financial trouble selling a kidney to pay off debt, or a rich person buying a slot in a lottery for a subsidized apartment? I'd rather have a simpler principle that gets most of them right than a complicated one that saves every verdict, so be honest if the price of saving all my gut calls is a principle so baroque I wouldn't actually use it.
