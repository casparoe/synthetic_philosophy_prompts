I've been working through the Boltzmann brain objection to cosmological models, and I've settled into a fairly strong version of it that I'd like you to stress-test. My position is this: any cosmological model that, together with a reasonable way of counting observers, predicts that observers arising by thermal fluctuation — Boltzmann brains, or something in the same category — outnumber ordinary observers by an overwhelming factor is thereby refuted. The reasoning is self-locating. If such a model were true, then an observer picked at random from all observers would almost certainly be a fluctuation-brain, and since I am plainly not one (my memories have a long, causally continuous history, and the world around me is not a transient fluctuation), the model must be false. I take this to be a genuine constraint on the dynamics, not merely on the interpretation, which is why I think it rules out, for example, a de Sitter vacuum with an infinite future or a naive formulation of eternal inflation.

Now I want you to tell me what would count against this position, sorted by how decisive each point would be. In particular, I'm interested in three things. First, the dependence on a measure: does the objection really constrain the physics, or is it in effect a constraint on which measure you pick, such that you can always adjust the measure to dodge the prediction? Second, the self-locating or typicality inference itself: is the "I am a random sample of all observers" step doing load-bearing work that could fail, and if so, in what cases? Third, whether the category "Boltzmann brain" is even well-defined well enough for the objection to be applied, given that the boundary between a fluctuation that produces a single brain and one that produces a whole Hubble volume of observers is fuzzy. I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — that is, whether it can be made immune to any model by choosing the right measure and the right typicality principle — and, if it has, whether that is a genuine problem for a view of this kind or whether an unfalsifiable constraint on interpretation is still a legitimate thing to hold. And finally, for each of these points, tell me which a defender of the objection would themselves concede as counting against it and which they would dismiss as not touching the core.
