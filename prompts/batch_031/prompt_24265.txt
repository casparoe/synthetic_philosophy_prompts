I keep hitting this in Occam's razor / model selection stuff and I'm pretty sure there's a name for it that I just can't place. The setup: you rank hypotheses by how short a program is that generates their predictions, but that lenght obviously depends on which programming language you write it in, and two perfectly reasonable languages can give you different lengths. I've been told the gap is "bounded by a constant," which sounds reassuring, but the constant isn't small — it can be as big as you want depending on the languages — and for a specific pair of hypotheses it's exactly the difference that decides which one counts as simpler. So "the simplest explanation" looks like it's always relative to an arbitrary choice of representation, with no fact of the matter about which hypothesis really is the simplest, yet everyone uses the razor like it picks out a definite winner. Is there an established term for this — the thing where the simplicity ordering is representation-dependent and the bounded-by-a-constant result doesn't actually give you a unique simplest hypothesis? And does it really undermine the razor, or does the constant wash out in practice for natural hypotheses and reasonable languages? I won't be happy with just a label; I want the carving — what the phenomenon actually is, why the boundedness result doesn't already settle it, and where the line is between a real problem and a non-issue.
