I keep running into a specific pattern in political argument, and I can't tell whether there is an established term for it. Take the phrase "Western values." When one speaker deploys it — say, in defense of immigration restrictions — the content they have in mind is roughly Christian cultural heritage, the traditional family, national sovereignty, and public order. When a different speaker deploys the very same phrase — say, in defense of a court ruling or a piece of foreign policy — the content is individual rights, secular government, the rule of law, and gender equality. And when a third, more critical speaker invokes it, it often means something like the reverse: a universalist package that historically masked European imperialism and the export of a particular economic order.

What strikes me is not simply that people disagree about what the phrase means, but that each user seems to treat it as having a single, stable, knowable referent, and to treat the others as the ones misusing it. The phrase behaves less like a description and more like a container that gets refilled with different commitments depending on who is arguing and what the speaker needs it to do, while still being wielded as if it pointed at one fixed thing.

Is there a name for this configuration? I have a sense it is near ideas like equivocation, moving the goalposts, or an umbrella term, but I don't think any of those quite captures it — the issue is not that one person is being careless or shifting a standard, but that the category itself has no settled extension and is doing strategic argumentative work in several incompatible directions at once. If there is an established concept, which of the near ones fits best, and where does it stop applying? And if there isn't, what would you call it, and what are the boundary cases — specifically, where does this kind of shifting reference end and ordinary, good-faith disagreement about a term's meaning begin?
