I keep running into the knowability principle being used as a cheap move in arguments — someone will say "well if it's really true, it must be possible to know it, so all this talk of unknowable truths is just you being lazy" and then wave Fitch's paradox at you like it settles the debate. I've read enough of the original and the responses to know the bare result (if every truth is knowable, then every truth is known) is real and the proof isn't a fluke, but I've never seen anyone actually lay out what an adequate resolution has to deliver, as opposed to just listing the standard escape hatches. So can you write the desiderata for a theory that counts as having genuinely defused Fitch's paradox — the spec, not the scorecard first. I mean which assumptions of the proof it's willing to cost and why that's the right place to cut, which distinctions it has to keep alive (possible-to-know vs will-be-known vs is-known, and the gap between a truth being knowable in principle and someone actually being in a position to verify it), and which candidate unknown truths it has to be able to accomodate without the principle collapsing into "everything is known" or quietly going vacuous. I'm thinking of the sealed coin flip that landed and was never observed, and the nastier self-referential case where the proposition is just "this proposition is not known."

For each desideratum give me the case behind it — the specific pressure or counterexample that makes it a requirement rather than a nice-to-have — and mark which are non-negotiable (give them up and you haven't resolved anything, you've just relocated the paradox) versus which are preferences of particular camps (the anti-realists, the intuitionists, the people who just want to restrict the principle to contingent present-tense stuff). Then the part I actually care about: is there a subset of these desiderata that's jointly unsatisfiable, given what the proof forces? I have a feeling the trilemma between keeping the unrestricted principle, keeping factivity plus the usual distribution of knowledge over conjunction, and keeping full classical logic is the load-bearing wall, but I want it stated as a result about the spec, not just asserted. And to close, briefly score the main contenders — the heavy restriction of the principle, the intuitionistic / reject-classical-logic route, the anti-realist "fine, some truths aren't knowable, so drop realism" route, and the "knowledge doesn't distribute over conjunction" move — against the list, a line or two each, no need to be exaustive.
