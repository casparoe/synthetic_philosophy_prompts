1. "the F is G" is just shorthand for "there is exactly one x such that F(x) and G(x)." the definite article is a quantifier in disguise, not a pointer to an object.
2. so "the present king of france is bald" is just false. not meaningless, not truth-value-less, just false. (it's like a query that matches zero rows: the claim fails, the database doesn't crash.)
3. "the present king of france is not bald" is true, because the "not" scopes over the whole existential claim.
4. i also think the same sentence can be used to assert "there is a present king of france and he is not bald" — i think i read this is called the primary/secondary occurrence thing, might have the labels backwards — and on that reading it's false.
5. i think the analysis is meaning preserving: any sentence with a definite description can be rewritten without one, and the rewrite is the "real" sentence; the surface form is just a convenient abbreviation.
6. i think "the morning star is the evening star" is not a simple identity between two names but a claim that there is exactly one thing that is both.
7. and of course a sentence is either true or false, one or the other.
