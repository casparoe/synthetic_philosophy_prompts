I'm an embryologist at a fertility clinic, and my research is on very early human development — totipotency and its loss, the two-cell and eight-cell stages, compaction, inner cell mass commitment, the timing of monozygotic twinning, and the chimeras that turn up in our work. A few months ago, a patient who had lost two IVF cycles asked me point-blank whether the embryos we had discarded could have been her, and I realized I had no answer that didn't immediately collapse into philosophy. I've been going back and forth on it with a philosophy-adjacent friend who keeps insisting Kripke settles the question, but I keep suspecting the biology makes it ill-posed in a way the philosophers don't appreciate. So I'd like you to restate the debate about origin essentialism — the claim, associated with Kripke's lineage argument, that you could only have come from the specific sperm and egg that in fact produced you, together with the main challenges to it, notably Parfit's argument that there is no further fact beyond physical and psychological continuity that determines which continuation is you — in the vocabulary I actually work in: totipotency, the two-cell and eight-cell stages, compaction, inner cell mass commitment, twinning before and after the primitive streak, and chimerism.
