A friend of mine, who reads a lot of the old metaphysics, walked me through an argument last week and I cant shake it. It's basically the regress about the unity of the proposition, the Bradley one I think, though he presented it as his own reconstruction and I didn't want to just go check the book. Here's how he laid it out, with his numbering:

(1) For a proposition to be a fact, the kind of thing that can be true or false, its constituents (say the object a and the property F) have to be genuinely combined into a unity, not just listed side by side.
(2) The only way two things get combined into a unity is for there to be a relation holding between them.
(3) But a relation is itself something, a term, a constituent, just as much as a and F.
(4) So if the relation R is what combines a and F, then R itself has to be combined with a and F, and that takes a further relation R2 holding among R, a, and F.
(5) R2 is a term too, so it needs R3, and so on, with no end.
(6) An endless series of relations never finishes, so the original combination never gets completed.
(7) So "a is F" is never a unified fact.
(8) So there are no facts, and therefore nothing is true.

He anticipated the obvious pushback, which is "but R2 just relates R to other relations, it doesn't drag R back into the original proposition," and his reply was that the reply doesn't say what R2 is a relation of. If it's a relation of R to a and F we're right back where we started, and if it's a relation among relations then it's got the same problem one level up, so the escape just relocates the regress instead of stopping it.

I obviously want (8) out of the picture, and I'm more attached to (1) and (2) than to the rest of it, though I grant I might be wrong about that. What I want is for someone to tell me exactly which step breaks, why it looked fine, and what I'm actually paying to reject it, because whatever the fix is that keeps (1) and (2), it feels like it's going to make relations into something weird, and I don't know how to think about that without it looking like a stipulation. And I'd like to know whether there's a cleaner escape, like giving up (2) and just saying facts are simple wholes that aren't built by relations at all, and what that would cost me.
