Ive been trying to pin down my position on laws of nature for a paper and I want a consistency check before I commit to it in writing. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a Humean about laws. No governing, no necessary connections in the world, no "nomic necessity." The laws are just the axioms of the deductive system that best trade off simplicity against strength — the best-system idea.

(2) I still think there are genuine laws of nature, not just accidental generalizations. "All electrons have the same charge" is a law; "all the gold coins currently in my pockets are in my pockets" isn't; and I take that difference to be real even though it's not a difference in governing power.

(3) Counterfactuals are grounded in the laws. To evaluate a counterfactual you need the laws plus the relevant local facts, and that's basically the whole story (Lewis-style).

(4) The laws are counterfactually robust. Even if the world had been different in all sorts of ways, the laws would still have held. I'm pretty sure this is part of what makes them laws rather than accidental patterns.

(5) I reject dispositional essentialism. The causal powers a property has are contingent on it, not part of what it is to be that property.

(6) I think the laws ground the regularities. The reason there's a regularity is that the law holds, not that the law just happens to summarize what's going on.

Now — which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the least costly way to get back to consistency? I genuinely don't know where my problem is, so please don't just confirm me. Rank the real tensions by severity and for each say what would have to give. And if exactly one revision cleans up the most, tell me which one and what it costs.
