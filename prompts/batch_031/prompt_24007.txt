"Nozick's tracking theory says that you know a proposition p just in case p is true, you believe p, and your belief is sensitive to the truth: in nearby possible worlds where p would be false, you would not believe p. The idea is that knowledge requires your belief to be connected to the truth in the right way, which is what rules out the lucky true beliefs of the Gettier cases. The theory's most famous application is the bank case. Suppose you deposited your paycheck at the bank on Friday and have a receipt; you know you deposited your checks, and you know that if you deposited them then the bank has your money. But the bank might have been robbed, or a teller might have embezzled, so you don't know the bank has your money. Nozick's conclusion is that knowledge is not closed under known entailment, since otherwise you'd know the bank has your money. But the cost is less serious than it looks, because on Nozick's own theory you don't actually know you deposited your checks either — you might be a confabulating amnesiac, and your receipt and memory could both be illusions — so the case really just shows that we overstate our knowledge, and the threat to closure dissolves once you see what you really do know."
