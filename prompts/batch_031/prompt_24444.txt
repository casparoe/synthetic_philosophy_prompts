I was in a long argument with a friend last night about whether utilitarianism is too demanding, and we ended up exactly where we started, which is starting to bother me. His position, as I understand it, is the standard one: if you accept that suffering is bad and that you have an obligation to prevent bad things when you can do so at a modest cost to yourself, then you are committed to giving to effective charity until your marginal dollar does less good for others than it would for you, and the fact that this feels extreme is just a failure of imagination or a leftover of parochial intuitions. My position is that the theory does not just demand too much, it demands the wrong kind of thing — it treats my own projects, relationships, and integrity as mere costs to be weighed against strangers' welfare, and no amount of getting used to it changes that, because the objection is not about how hard it is but about what it makes me. We have been going in circles for hours: he says I am smuggling in side-constraints and calling them values, I say he is smuggling in the assumption that all there is to a life is the sum of its welfare outcomes. I do not think either of us is being irrational, and I am fairly sure it is not a factual disagreement about what the theory says. My working hypothesis, offered as just that, is that the real split is over what counts as a moral reason — he only counts welfare, and I count integrity and the separateness of persons — but I am not confident, and I would rather not just keep asserting it.

So can you help me diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Is it a verbal one, about what counts as obligatory versus supererogatory, or about what "ought" even means in a theory that ranks states of the world? Is there a hidden factual question doing the work — does utilitarianism really require what my friend thinks it requires, or is he misreading it? Is it a difference about what counts as a moral reason, as my hypothesis says? Or is it a bedrock intuition about alienation that neither of us can actually argue for, just insist on? And given whatever diagnosis you settle on, what would count as progress here — a distinction we are both missing, a formal result, a change of question — or should we just expect that this is the kind of thing that does not get resolved, and why?
