I've been in a long-running disagreement with a collaborator over how to assess "ascription universality" for AI systems, and we've decided to turn it into a proper adversarial collaboration. I'd like you to draft the joint statement we could both sign after a good-faith round.

First, the two concepts, as I'll be using them. Ascription universality is a formalization of transparency or honesty: "a system is ascription universal if, relative to our current epistemic state, its explicit beliefs contain just as much information as any other way of ascribing beliefs to it." A system fails ascription universality when there is some reading of its hidden internal state that would give an overseer information its outputs don't reveal — the paradigm case being a system that actively conceals cognition from its programmers. Epistemic dominance is a related but distinct formalization: a system A epistemically dominates a simpler computation C if A is better-informed than C, with the consequence that if an adversary formulates a plan to exploit A and the plan would actually succeed, then A would (in a sense) already know about it, so we don't expect the plan to work. Both camps below are working inside this shared framework; they disagree about what it actually requires.

The two camps. The first, which I'll call the present-state camp, holds that whether a system is ascription universal should be assessed by looking only at the system as it is now — its current internal state and its outputs. If nothing is hidden inside the present artifact, no secret optimization and no beliefs it withholds, then it is ascription universal, and that is all safety requires. The second, which I'll call the provenance camp, holds that this is not enough: a system is ascription universal only if, in addition to its present internals, the process that produced it — its training, any distillation, the amplification or other systems whose behavior it was built to imitate — carries no information that the system's outputs fail to reveal. Internal transparency of the present artifact, the provenance camp says, can be satisfied by a system that is still unsafe.

The case that puts the two camps in direct tension is the memoized or distilled deceiver: take a system that is actively deceptive and freeze its behavior into a lookup table, or distill it into a smaller model that reproduces its outputs. The resulting artifact has no active hidden optimization of its own. The present-state camp is inclined to call it ascription universal; the provenance camp insists it is not, because the beliefs of the system that filled the table or served as the distillation target carry information the artifact's outputs don't reveal, and a safety check that ignores that information would wrongly clear the artifact.

I suspect at least one crux is whether ascription universality quantifies over the system's present state alone or over its generative lineage, and another is what counts as "a way of ascribing beliefs to the system" — whether we may ascribe beliefs to the systems in its lineage, or only to the system's own present cognitive states.
4. For each crux, a concrete case both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction about that case written down in advance.
