So I write novels, and I've been down a rabbit hole lately on how philosophers use thought experiments, this whole intuition-pump business. I follow the basic move: you set up a little scene, the trolley, the fat man on the footbridge, the transplant surgeon, and you want the reader to have a reaction, something like diverting the trolley is fine but pushing the man is not, and then you treat that reaction as evidence about some general moral principle. But I've also read the pushback, that the thought experiment isn't really offering evidence, it's just a story that pumps your intuitions the way a well-constructed scene can make you feel something you'd never feel in real life, so it's closer to rhetoric than to evidence. And then there's the experimental-philosophy stuff where the intuition apparently flips if you change the name of the trolley or the ethnicity of the person on the bridge, which to me sounds exactly like the difference between a scene that's written to be robust and one that only works because of a specific detail.
