I'm working through John von Neumann's argument in The Computer and the Brain (1958) that the brain cannot be a deterministic automaton — the "observer" argument, on the grounds that a complete model of a machine must include the observer who makes the model, which must in turn include the observer of that model, and so on. Please explain, at a fairly technical level, what this argument is, what it is supposed to establish, and why the standard objection is that it is a non-sequitur. And please mark clearly where people who know this topic genuinely disagree (for instance, whether a higher-level self-model blocks the regress, whether the argument is really an instance of a general self-reference limitation, or whether quantum indeterminacy is doing any work in the conclusion) rather than presenting one reading as the consensus.
