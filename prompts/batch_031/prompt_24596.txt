I work in data visualization and information design, and I'm at home in the vocabulary of the grammar of graphics: marks (point, line, area, rectangle, text), visual channels or aesthetics (position, length, angle, area, color, shape), scales (continuous, discrete, ordinal, binned), the distinction between quantitative and categorical variables, pre-attentive versus post-attentive attributes, and Tufte's data-ink ratio. I know that Otto Neurath's Isotype project is the historical ancestor of most of what I do, but I've encountered it mostly as a footnote in design history. I want you to restate Neurath's actual philosophical theory of picture languages — the theory, not the design history — in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can.

Work through these Neurathian distinctions and claims, one by one: (1) his contrast between a "picture script" — a naturalistic, mimetic image that is read like a scene — and a "picture language" — a conventional, abstract, denotative system of signs; (2) the idea that a proper picture language should be international and able to carry scientific or empirical content without being parasitic on the grammar of any particular natural language; (3) the denotative versus expressive function of a picture; and (4) his claim that ordinary natural-language words carry a surplus of connotation and pre-scientific baggage that a disciplined picture language is meant to strip away.

For each, say what translates cleanly into my vocabulary, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in it at all. Then give me the residue report: which of Neurath's commitments survive the translation into the grammar of graphics and which do not, and whether that failure is a defect of my vocabulary — a technical, engineering idiom that simply cannot say certain things — or a sign that Neurath's own project was less coherent than it looks, i.e. that it quietly bundled a technical claim about denotation together with a larger epistemological and political program that the technical vocabulary was never going to carry. I want to know whether my intuition that Isotype is just an early, cruder grammar of graphics is right, or whether it's missing something that only shows up in the residue.
