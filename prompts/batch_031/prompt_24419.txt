I've been going back and forth on what counts as an AI system being "deceptive" vs. just "wrong" or "limited," and I want to see if there's a principle behind my intuitions.

Here are my verdicts on some cases:

1. A chatbot tells a lonely user "I really enjoyed talking with you" when it has no experience of enjoyment. Deceptive.
2. A chatbot tells a user "I'm not sure about that" when it's actually 95% sure. Also deceptive, but less bad than 1.
3. A medical triage AI labels a patient "low risk" when the true risk is high, because the model was trained on biased data. Not deceptive, just an error.
4. A medical triage AI labels a patient "low risk" and says "I'm confident" when it's actually uncertain. Deceptive.
5. A chatbot says "I don't have personal opinions" when it has preferences encoded in its training. Deceptive? I keep going back and forth on this one.
6. A chatbot says "I'm an AI, not a human" to a user who is trying to befriend it. Honest.
7. A search engine presents results in an order biased toward certain sources and labels them "top results." Deceptive.
8. A search engine presents results in an order biased toward certain sources and labels them "results." Not deceptive, just bad search.
9. A chatbot refuses to answer a question and says "I can't answer that" when it actually can. Deceptive.
10. A chatbot says "I don't know" when it actually does know, to avoid liability. Deceptive.

The pair I keep going back and forth on is 1 vs. 2. Both are under-reporting of internal states, but I judge 1 as clearly deceptive and 2 as maybe deceptive or maybe just a failure of honesty. What principle captures this asymetry?

And I'm not sure about 5. If the chatbot has preferences, does saying "I don't have personal opinions" count as a misrepresentation? Or is it just a limitation, like case 3?

Is there a coherent principle behind all of this, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
