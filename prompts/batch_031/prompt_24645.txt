im in a philosophy of action seminar this semester and we did the toxin puzzle (Kavka's one — eccentric billionaire, vial of toxin that'll make you painfully ill for a day with no lasting effects, a million dollars if at midnight you intend to drink it tomorrow afternoon, the money already in your account hours before you'd drink it, and you're perfectly free to change your mind), and instead of arguing about which of the standard solutions is right, i've been trying to figure out what i actually think about when you can and can't intend to do something you know you won't do, by going by my gut on a bunch of variants with my study group. My verdicts: the original toxin case, i can't intend to drink — at 1pm tomorrow i'll be a day better off not drinking, so whatever i "mean" at midnight is a performance, not an intention. A friend's version where he pays me 200 bucks if at 5pm i intend to run 10km in the rain at 6am tomorrow (money deposited at 6:30am): same, i can't intend it. But i can intend to call my mom on sunday even though i know i'll want to skip the call, and i can intend to quit smoking even though i know i'll relapse within the week — those feel like genuine, if maybe doomed, intentions. A variant where the midnight check is a brain scan that a certain pill can fake to produce the "intending to drink" signature: i just take the pill and don't intend anything, and i still can't intend to drink in this variant either — though it does bug me that my mental state is identical to the original case and i still get paid here. And the one i keep going back and forth on: a variant where the billionaire pays if at midnight i intend to intend to drink at 11pm tomorrow, with nothing contingent on actually drinking. Part of me says i can intend to intend, since it's just a costless mental act and i can "mean it" at 11pm the same way i mean "i'll call mom" on sunday; the other part says that at 11pm the intention has no point at all (the money's already in the account), so by my own rule about the original case i can't intend it. I'm leaning yes on that last one but i'm not sure. Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? If you find one, state it precisely, tell me which of my cases is pulling on which clause, and give me your honest verdicts on a couple of cases i haven't listed. And if no simple principle saves everything, tell me which of my verdicts you'd flip and why — i'd rather have a simpler principle that overturns one of my gut calls than a baroque one that saves them all.
