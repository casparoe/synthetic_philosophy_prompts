I suspect something is wrong with that inference, but I can't say exactly where, because the slogan seems to slide between several different claims. Could you take the slogan "rational agents with a common prior cannot agree to disagree" and lay out, as a numbered list I can bring to the next review, the distinct precise theses it could actually be expressing? I'm especially interested in the readings that separate (i) "sharing the same prior" from (ii) "sharing the same information or likelihood," and in exactly what the theorem requires of "common knowledge" of the agents' posteriors — I have a feeling the slogan is doing work that only holds under a much stronger condition than "same training data." I don't need references, citations, or a reading list; I just want the conceptual breakdown, plus a short closing paragraph that tells me which of the readings is the one my colleague is actually relying on and whether that reading survives the objections you've laid out.
