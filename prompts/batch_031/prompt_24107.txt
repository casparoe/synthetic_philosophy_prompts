I run an upper-level epistemology seminar, about 12 students, 90 minutes. Design a structured, role-based activity that makes students feel the force of the idea that some propositions — take "other minds exist" — are not the kind of thing that can be supported by evidence, but are instead conditions for there being any evidence at all. The incoherence should show up in a specific way: when a student tries to supply evidence for the hinge, every candidate piece of evidence they offer already presupposes the hinge, so the move is not a failure to find the right evidence but a failure built into the structure of the move itself. Students should discover this in what happens to them during the activity, not in my telling them afterwards: no lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the word "hinge" or the name "Wittgenstein" until the debrief. Give me roles (e.g., a skeptic who demands evidence, and others who must supply it), rules and incentives arranged so the incoherence emerges on its own, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the hinge idea. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be frank about which feature of the view the activity cannot reproduce and I have to supply.
