I've been trying to figure out where I actually land on the line between "you should do this" and "youd be a hero for doing it, but I can't fault you for not doing it," because I keep getting inconsistent answers. Let me dump my gut calls and you tell me what principle, if any, is doing the work underneath.

Giving up my seat on a packed bus to an old lady: I think I should. If I don't, people give me looks, but I'm not a bad person. And no special praise for doing it either — it's just the minimum, the floor.

Donating a kidney to a dying stranger I've never met: praiseworthy, and I'm not a bad person for not doing it. Big gap from the bus.

A drowning kid in a shallow pond, I'd just get my shoes wet: I should save them, and if I don't I'm a bad person. (The famous one, right.)

Saving that same kid when it costs me my career: praiseworthy, but I'm not a bad person for walking away.

Now here's where I get stuck. Donating a kidney to a dying stranger feels supereragatory — great, but no blame for not. But donating a kidney to my own dying child? I feel like refusing that is blameworthy, like a failure of obligation, not just a missed chance to be a hero. Same act, same cost to me, the only thing that changed is who's on the table. I can't reconcile that with the stranger case at all.

And a second thing that bugs me: I think a person who never does anything above the minimum — never gives up a seat, never donates, never helps anyone past what they're strictly required to — is a pretty bad person. But if "above the minimum" acts are by definition not required, then what exactly am I blaming them for? They did everything they were supposed to.

And I can't find the line on money. I feel like giving, say, $100 a month to a charity that actually saves lives is something I should do. $1,000 a month feels like heroism. But $500? I genuinely can't say, and I don't feel like there's a fact of the matter I'm just failing to see.

So: is there a principle that gets all of this, or at least most of it? What does it say about cases I haven't listed — like a professional lifeguard, or someone who's contractually obligated to help? And honestly, which of my calls above does your principle force me to drop?
