ok so I've been thinking a lot about this stuff where people say consciousness might be fundamental, like how gravity or electric charge are just properties of the universe that everything has. I've landed on a few things and I want to know if they hang toghether:

(1) I think panpsychism is basically right — every physical system has some minimal experience, and it's a fundamental property, not something that pops out of complexity.
(2) But I don't think we owe anything to electrons or rocks. The only beings whose suffering matters morally are animals with brains. I think that's just obvious.
(3) I'm a physicalist, not a dualist. Consciousness is a physical property, not a ghost in the machine.
(4) The hard problem is real. Physics as we know it doesn't explain why there's something it's like, and I don't think it will without adding consciousness as a fundamental ingredient.
(5) We should minimize animal suffering above almost everything else. Factory farming is the worst thing humans do, full stop.
(6) The combination problem — how tiny sparks of experience make my rich, unified experience — is a real unsolved problem, but I think it's solvable and not a reason to reject panpsychism.
(7) My experience is more than an electron's, obviously. There's a gradient, and I'm at the top end of it.

Which of these pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way to fix it? I'd rather you tell me which one has to go than just wave your hands. I genuinly don't know where the problem is, and I don't want to be told everything's fine.
