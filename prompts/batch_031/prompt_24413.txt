I lead a small conceptual research group (about eight people, mostly philosophers and decision theorists rather than ML engineers) at a mid-sized AI lab that sits roughly two years behind the frontier labs in capability. Our strategy group is trying to decide how much of the next two years to devote to understanding and reducing the alignment tax — the capability cost of making a model safe — as opposed to accepting the tax and racing. I want to spend the group's effort on the conceptual questions that most determine whether the tax is bearable and whether the competitiveness constraint (the pressure to drop safety because competitors won't) actually binds in practice. I'm not after a survey of the literature; I want a defended agenda I can actually argue about.

Here are the cruxes I'm currently weighing, though I'm open to ones I'm not listing: (1) whether most of the alignment tax is intrinsic to the problem or an artifact of naive, tacked-on safety implementations that better techniques would remove; (2) whether safety can function as a competitive advantage — reliability, trust, enterprise and regulatory goodwill — rather than a pure cost, and under what market conditions that flips; (3) how the tax scales with capability, i.e. whether a more capable model makes alignment cheaper or more expensive per unit of capability; (4) whether the competitiveness constraint is a real structural feature of the race or a partly self-fulfilling narrative that firms adopt to justify under-investment; and (5) the extent to which coordination, standards, or regulation could reshape the competitive landscape so the tax becomes bearable even for a single firm. Which of these — or something I'm missing — matters most, in the sense that the most other strategic decisions at our lab depend on its answer?

Please give me a dependency-aware prioritization rather than a flat ranking: for each, how upstream it is relative to the others, how tractable it is for a group with our skill mix (conceptual and decision-theoretic, not empirical ML), how neglected it is, and what evidence or argument over the next couple of years would move it up or down the list. I especially want the trade-offs between those criteria to be made explicit, and I want you to say which one or two you'd deprioritize and why their answers wouldn't change much downstream.
