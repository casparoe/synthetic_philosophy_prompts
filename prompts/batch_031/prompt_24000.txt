Write an essay on the naturalist "selected dysfunction" account of disease, according to which a biological part or process is dysfunctional if and only if it fails to perform a function for which it was selected by evolution. Focus on the challenge posed by exaptation — the phenomenon whereby a biological capacity comes to be used for a purpose for which it was not originally selected. A concrete case: the human larynx was selected for breathing and basic vocalization, but in humans it is also used for complex speech. On the selected dysfunction account, if a person has a condition that impairs their speech but not their breathing, is that condition a "dysfunction" (and hence a "disease")? Consider how a normativist critic of the account might respond to this case, weigh the arguments on both sides, and reach a justified conclusion about whether the account can adequately handle exaptation cases.
