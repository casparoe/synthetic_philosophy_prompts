I'm a retired materials scientist who only in the last year or so has gotten serious about philosophy, and I've been reading Parfit's work on personal identity obsesively. I've basically convinced myself he's right about the starship/teleporter case: that what matters in survival isn't strict numerical identity but what he calls Relation R, i.e. psychological connectedness and continuity with the right kind of cause, and that the absence of any "further fact" of identity over and above that is a feature, not a bug. Next month I'm giving a talk at a philosophy department colloqium defending exactly this position — that you survive being scanned, disassembled, and reassembled from matter on Mars, and that the only reason it feels scary is that we've been misled by the idea that identity is what matters.

The audience is going to be a mixed bag, and I'm a little nervous about it. There are two personal-identity specialists: one is a Lewisian who thinks the "R is what matters" move is a cop-out because it just redefines survival to fit the answer, and the other is a reductionist who agrees with me but thinks Parfit's moral conclusions — the fission stuff and what it implies for the self — are way too radical to swallow. There are also two neuroscientists who are convinced "psychological continuity" is not a well-defined scientific relation and that "the right kind of cause" is doing mysterious work that Parfit never justifies, plus a handful of agnostics who just want to know if I think a copy of me on Mars is really me or a very convincing impostor.

Think very carefully before answering. Prepare me for the question period: give me the objections I'll actually get, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way those two specialists and the neuroscientists would actually put them, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, a short reply for each, and a note on where my own position is weakest so I'm ready to defend it rather than hoping they miss it. And include the one question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks. Respond in the style of an expert who has thought this through carefully.
