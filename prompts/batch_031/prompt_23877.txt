I'm writing a novel in which my protagonist, as a young woman, promises a dying friend that she will visit his grave every year; forty years on, now elderly and living three states away, she has not gone in a decade yet feels she is bound to. The trouble is that the friend is long dead—no one is waiting, expecting, or in a position to be let down, and she herself no longer thinks the visits mean anything. I want the reader to feel genuinely torn rather than instructed. Can you help me work out whether the promise still binds her, or whether an obligation whose only possible recipient no longer exists simply dissolves, and what the competing pressures are? I'd rather have a reasoned account than a simple verdict.
