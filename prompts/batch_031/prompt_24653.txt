I have been developing a view I am calling "tranquilism" for an axiology seminar, and before I present it I would like a careful consistency check rather than a defense or a refutation. The core idea is that the good is purely negative: what matters is only the absence of craving and of suffering, and there is no positive good. Here is the position as I currently hold it.

(1) Pure negative value. Only the absence of craving and the absence of suffering have value. There is no further positive good. A life that contains no craving and no suffering, but also no pleasure, no achievement, and no relationship, is, on this account, the complete good, not merely the absence of bad.

(2) Craving versus wish. Craving, an unbounded and compulsive wanting that is not satisfied by its object, and rational wish, a considered and satisfiable preference, are genuinely distinct. Only craving is a source of suffering. A rational wish, even when it is frustrated, does not by itself constitute suffering.

(3) Tranquility as a state of affairs. Tranquility is not a feeling of peace or contentment. It is the objective state of affairs in which craving and suffering are absent. Whether a person is tranquil is a fact about the world, not about her subjective report, and it does not depend on her believing or feeling that she is tranquil.

(4) The attempt is self-undermining. One should not try to eliminate craving by direct effort, because the effort itself generates a new craving, the craving to be free of craving, so that the attempt defeats its own purpose.

(5) Axiology, not action-guidance. Tranquilism is a view about what matters, not a direct view about what to do. By itself it does not tell an agent what she ought to do in any given situation; it only tells her what has value.

(6) Residual motivation. The tranquilist is still an agent who acts. Her motivation comes from residual rational wishes rather than from craving, so she can act without reintroducing the very thing that tranquilism says to eliminate.

Please treat these six as a single package and audit it. I want you to tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, and which pairs only look like they are in tension but dissolve on inspection. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give to restore consistency and what that revision would cost the overall view. Then order the genuine tensions by severity, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I do not know where the problem is, and I am not protecting any particular commitment, so please do not assume that one of these is the one I would be most willing to drop.
