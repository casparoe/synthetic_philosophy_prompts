Sartre's bad faith is usually understood as a form of self-deception: in bad faith, you deceive yourself about your own radical freedom by pretending that you're not free. But self-deception seems incoherent, since it requires you to simultaneously know and not-know the same thing. So the self-deception account of bad faith seems to be a contradiction in terms. How is Sartre supposed to make sense of bad faith, given this incoherence?
