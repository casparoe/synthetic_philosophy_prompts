I'm taking a bioethics course this semester, and I keep coming back to something that's been nagging me about how we declare someone dead. The brain death criterion is usually stated as the irreversible loss of all functions of the brain, including the brainstem, and the circulatory criterion is the irreversible cessation of circulation and respiration. But "irreversible" is a word about the future — it's a prediction that the function won't come back, even with the best available medicine. So we're using a prediction about the future to make a present-tense declaration that someone is dead right now.

I was discussing this with a friend who works in an ICU, and she said that in practice, we don't actually wait to see if the function comes back; we just assume irreversibility based on some threshold, like a certain number of tests over a certain period. And that made me realize there's a hidden move here, where a criterion that sounds like a description of a current state is actually a claim about what would happen in the future, absent intervention.

Is there a name for this? For the phenomenon of defining a state (like death) in terms of what would happen absent intervention, rather than in terms of the present state itself? I'm not asking for a bibliography or a reading list — I just want the conceptual picture. What's the nearest established concept, how does the thing I'm describing differ from it, and if there's no clean name for it, what would you call it, and where are its edges?
