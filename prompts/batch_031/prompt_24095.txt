write me a villanelle on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem. the two refrains should be the two axioms doing the work in the theorem — the Pareto principle and the independence of irrelevant alternatives — phrased plainly enough that a philosophy undergrad can parse them, and i want the form to do the real work: the way the same two lines keep coming back, unchanged, should make the reader feel the theorem's actual conclusion, that any social welfare function satisfying these axioms has to be a weighted sum of individual expected utilities whose weights are fixed and do not vary with which alternative is on the table. the body tercets should walk from the individual utilities and the social function trying to aggregate them to that realization, and the last new line before the closing couplet should land the in-the-weeds point that the theorem pins down the shape of the weights but not their values, so the separate impartiality step that sets them all equal is where the real argument actually lives. a decision theorist should be able to tell the poem is right about what the theorem does and doesnt show, and someone who has never heard of it should still walk away with a good poem.
