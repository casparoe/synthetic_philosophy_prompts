I teach a small seminar on ontology identification and inaccessible information, and I want to build a session around The Left Hand of Darkness, because I think it is secretly one of the best dramatizations of this problem ever written and almost nobody reads it that way. Genly Ai is an Ekumen envoy trying to model Gethenian psychology from the outside, and the whole structure of the book is an identification problem: he can observe behavior, but the relevant internal states are organized around karhide, that period of darkness and sexual ambiguity that a human basically has no access to, and his first assumptions about what Gethenians are like keep failing. I want a close reading of the novel through this lens, taking both the book and the problem seriously: where does the novel illuminate the identification problem, where does it quietly dodge it, and does Estraven's trust in Ai function as a genuine source of information that closes the gap or is it just a move that lets the reader close it? I am also interested in the winter: the planet is literally in a long dark, and I keep wondering whether that is just atmosphere or whether the book is making a claim about what identification is like when the environment itself hides the agent. And I want it to end by saying what the novel gets right about this that argument alone never reaches, and where it gets it wrong. I am writing this up as a handout for the students, so keep it in essay form, no bibliography, and dont be afraid to say the reading breaks down somewhere.
