my seminar on population ethics ends next week with a session on the procreation asymmetry and i want to close it with a villanelle that does the actual work of the argument instead of just naming it. the two refrains should be the two horns of the asymmetry: one saying that no one is harmed by never existing (the neutrality of non-existence doing the work), and the other saying that a person brought into a life of suffering is genuinely harmed. across the quatrains, stage the moves a student keeps reaching for to collapse the two horns back into symmetry -- the missed-good objection (if i dont procreate i forego creating a happy person), the non-identity move (any child i could have had would have been a different person, so this one isnt harmed by my choice), and the nagging thought that maybe non-existence isnt neutral after all -- and the form should make each of those fail to land, so that when the two refrains finally sit side by side in the closing couplet the asymmetry is left standing as the stable moral fact, not a bug to be fixed. i want it to be the kind where someone who knows the literature can tell which refrain is which and can see the poem keeping the procreation asymmetry distinct from the non-identity problem (theyre seperable, and the asymmetry doesnt need the different-person claim to get off the ground), while someone who doesnt know it just reads a parent standing at the edge of a decision. keep the language plain, no jargon inside the lines, and dont resolve it -- the whole point is that it doesnt resolve.
