I'm presenting at a philosophy of physics workshop next week my view that Lewis's Best System Account of laws of nature fails on a circularity problem: the laws are the theorems of the best system, the best system is the system that best systematizes the regularities, but the laws are supposed to explain the regularities, so the explanation is circular — and I argue this circularity is fatal, not minor, and that the only way out is an anti-Humean "governing" account on which laws bring about the regularities rather than merely describing them. The audience is a group of philosophers of physics, two of whom are committed Lewisians who know the BSA inside out, plus several agnostics. Prepare me for the question period: the objections I'll actually get from this audience, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way a Lewisian would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the objection I'm hoping nobody asks.
