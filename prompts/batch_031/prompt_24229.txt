I've been working in animal welfare for the past four years, and I've come to hold the view that the suffering-focused position in ethics is correct: the absence of suffering is the only thing with positive moral value, while the presence of pleasure is morally neutral. I don't just think suffering matters more than pleasure; I think pleasure matters not at all, and I accept the anti-natalist implications of that. What would count against this view? I'm not interested in the standard objections (e.g., the experience-machine objection, which I think is directed at hedonism, not at my view). I want the less obvious defeaters: whether the absence of suffering in a being that will never exist is really a good, and what that implies for our duties to potential people; whether "suffering" is a concept precise enough to do the work I want it to do; and whether the view collapses into something trivial or uninteresting. I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if I can always explain away a counterexample by saying "that's not really suffering," is the view still doing any real work?
