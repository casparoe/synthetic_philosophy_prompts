I work nights on a palliative care ward and my mum's been in a care home for about three weeks now, so I've been thinking a lot about the line between doing something to someone and just letting them die, and I cant get the principle to line up no matter how I slice it. Here's where I land, case by case:

Weaning my mum off the vent when the docs say she's not coming round: fine, that's just letting her go. A doctor who gives a terminal patient a lethal dose of morphine just to end it: not fine, that's killing. But a doctor who gives a high dose for the pain, knowing it'll suppress her breathing, and she goes that night: I keep going back and forth, but I think this one's okay and it feels different from the last one. A nurse who simply doesn't give a diabetic their insulin and the patient dies: not fine, and honestly it feels as bad as the lethal injection. An old man who sees a kid fall in a pond and walks past because he doesn't want to get his coat wet: not fine, that's letting die and it's awful. A guy who pushes a stranger into the path of a bus: not fine, and worse than the pond one. Me not donating a kidney while a stranger dies: feels a lot less bad than the pond one, even though I'm also "letting die" in some sense. A surgeon who leaves a sponge in a belly and the patient dies: bad, but it feels different from all of the above, more like a really bad mistake than a choice.

The pair thats driving me nuts is the vent one and the insulin one. Both are just... not doing something, right? Same shape. But one feels fine and the other feels as bad as killing, and I can't see what's different.

So is there a principle, or a small set of them, stated precisely enough that I could actually use it at the bedside, that gets all of these or close? For each piece of it, tell me which of my cases is what's forcing it in, and what it'd say about a case I haven't listed. And be honest about which of my calls it can't save. Oh, and what does it say about the one I really can't get right: a doctor who knows a patient's got hours to live anyway and gives a dose of morphine that will probably end it a bit sooner, but the main point is to keep them comfortable for the rest?
