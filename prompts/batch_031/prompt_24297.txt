ok so I've been watching the free energy principle debate for a while now (mostly on the Alignment Forum and the usual blogospheric corners, but it goes back way further than that) and I'm curious why it's stalled out the way it has.

For the record, here's how I understand the two camps. The Friston side: the variational free energy bound is a genuine unifying result, anything that maintains itself is minimizing it, and this one principle explains perception (predictive coding), action (active inference), and the framework has been pushed into psychiatry, social systems, and even non-living things. The critics: the bound is trivially satisfied by any probability distribution (it's just KL divergence being nonnegative, so the bound itself has no content), the real empirical claim is that brains and agents actually run gradient descent on it, and that's underdtermined because you can fit anything to anything; plus the scope is so broad that it explains nothing. Even the sympathetic people draw a line between predictive coding, which is a testable hypothesis, and the FEP, which is something more slogan-like.

And it's been going like this for years, same arguments getting recicled, nobody moving an inch.

My working hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute about what counts as a "theory" (formal generality on one side, risky specific predictions on the other), with a hidden formal question doing the real work underneath: how much of the FEP is actually a theorem given the Markov blanket assumptions, and how much is just an assumption in disguise? If the axioms already encode the conclusion, then the FEP is a tautology and all the empirical content is in the axioms, not the conclusion.

Is that the right diagnosis, or is this a real empirical or formal disagreement in disguise? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the positions, and an honest answer about whether anything -- a new experiment, a theorem, a conceptual distinction -- could actually end it, and what would count as progress if so.
