Compare epistemic contextualism — the view that whether a subject knows that p depends on the conversational context in which the attribution is made — with pragmatic encroachment — the view that the practical stakes or interests at issue affect whether the subject knows that p. Both views predict that a subject may be credited with knowing that p in a low-stakes setting but not in a high-stakes one. Given this overlap, what exactly do the two views disagree about? In particular, is the difference between them substantive, or is it merely a difference about where the context-sensitivity is located — in the conversational context on the one hand, and in the practical situation on the other?
