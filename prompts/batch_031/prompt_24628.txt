I just spent about two hours arguing with a friend about a heuristic we both reach for constantly — when nothing seems to connect two pieces of evidence, just treat them as independent — and whether that heuristic actually has a defensible formal backbone. He insisted independence is the obvious default: if all you fix are the two marginals and you maximize entropy over the joint, the unique answer is the product distribution, so independence is what "maximum ignorance" hands you, and any other default is arbitrary. I pushed back, and I think I was right, but I couldn't make the point cleanly. The maximum-entropy result is fine as a constrained optimization, but the justification for treating it as a default — the idea that it amounts to a uniform, maximally uninformative prior — is not invariant to how you reparameterize the problem. And there's a deeper snag he never addressed: exact independence is a lower-dimensional, measure-zero slice of the space of all joint distributions, so a genuinely uniform prior over that space would never land on independence at all — it would give you a spread over the dependence parameter. So the two natural readings of "presume nothing" (maximize entropy subject to the marginals, versus put a uniform prior over the whole joint space) actually disagree about what the default is, and I don't know which one, if either, is the right formalization of the heuristic.
