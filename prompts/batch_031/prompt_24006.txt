ok so I've been chewing on the problem of old evidence and I want a consistency check before I write it up for a seminar. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I think confirmation is basically the likelihood ratio — H confirms E just in P(E|H) > P(E|~H), and the strength is the size of that ratio.

(2) I think the problem of old evidence is a genuine live problem for this view — on my own account, the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion and the success of Newtonian gravity in nailing down planetary orbits just plain fail to confirm general relativity, and I think that's a real defect, not a quirk.

(3) I'm pretty sure the fix is NOT to switch to an explanatory / inference to the best explanation account of confirmation, which would make confirmation a matter of theoritical virtues instead of probability — the problem should be solved inside the probabilistic framework.

(4) The key, I think, is that P(E|~H) isn't actually 1 for old evidence, because the pre-existing theory that already explains E is itself fallible, so P(E|~H) < 1 and the likelihood ratio can come out above 1 after all.

(5) I also think that for old evidence to confirm a hypothesis, the hypothesis has to do something the old theory can't — a pure redundant restatement shouldn't get any confirmatory credit.

Can you go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other, wich only look like they do, and what the cheapest revision is? I genuinely don't know where the crack is, so don't assume I'd give up any particular one — rank the tensions by severity and say what would have to give in each case.
