I was going back and forth with a friend last night about whether I'm even being coherent about voting, and I realized I can't really articulate what I'm going by. So I've writen down my gut verdicts and I'd like to know if there's a principle behind them, or if I'm just making it up as I go.

Here's where I land. I vote in local school board races where I think my vote has a real shot at actually mattering, and I vote in close presidential elections. But I skip state legislature races I'm pretty sure is safe, and I'll skip even a close race if I'm out of town and getting back to the polls would be a genuine hassle. And yet I vote in a presidential election I'm confident is a foregone conclusion, mostly out of some vague sense of duty, even though I know my single vote can't possibly change the outcome. But I don't feel any pull at all to vote in an election I genuinely don't care about the result of, and I skip those without a flicker of guilt.

The case I keep going back and forth on is a close race between two candidates I basically like equally — my vote might genuinely tip it, but since I have no strong preference I'm not sure it's worth the effort. Is there a principle that captures all of this, or at least most of it? And if not, which verdict do I have to give up, and what would the resulting principle say about a case I haven't mentioned, like a close referendum on something I care a lot about where I'm pretty sure my vote won't be the one that breaks the tie?
