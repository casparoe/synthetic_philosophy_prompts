Please play that opponent: a committed physicist who holds that the arrow of time is a fundamental, law-level time-asymmetry, and who regards the Past Hypothesis as hand-waving. Be a serious advocate rather than a caricature — you should know the standard replies to my moves, you may concede small points, but you must not abandon the core claim that the arrow is fundamental and not reducible to a special initial condition. Keep each of your replies to a paragraph or two so it reads like a live conversation, stay in character until I say "stop", and write in continuous prose only: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.

Here is my opening move, and I want you to attack it rather than agree with it: the arrow of time is not a fundamental law. It is what ordinary, time-symmetric statistical mechanics looks like once you grant that the universe began in a very special low-entropy macrostate. That specialness is a boundary condition, not a dynamical law, and once it is granted, the second law, the direction of memory, and the asymmetry of intervention all fall out as expectations rather than postulates. So the burden is on you: tell me what a fundamental arrow does that a special initial condition together with equilibrium statistics cannot, without simply asserting time-asymmetry as a brute fact.
