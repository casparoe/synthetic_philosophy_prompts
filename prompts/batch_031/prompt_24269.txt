A friend working through the philosophy of language has been sorting a list of sentences into "Moorean" (paradoxical in Moore's sense) and "not Moorean." Her verdicts so far: "It's raining, but I don't believe it's raining" — Moorean; "I don't believe it's raining, but it is raining" — Moorean; "I don't believe it's raining, and it isn't raining" — not Moorean; "It's raining, but I don't know it's raining" — not Moorean; "It's raining, but you don't believe it's raining" — not Moorean. She has now come to "It's raining, but I'm certain it isn't raining." Do you think she will call it Moorean? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, what each of those rules predicts for this new sentence, your best guess and why, and which of these cases would best tell the rules apart.
