My reading group has been stuck for months between a consciousness illusionist -- the person who thinks phenomenal consciousness is a user illusion, a self-model the brain builds that misrepresents itself as having experiences -- and a realist who thinks the hard problem is real and that there really is something it is like to be the system. Write the doc they should of produced after a serious adversarial colaboration: the claims both can sign (about the cognitive architecture, about what the 'user illusion' label commits one to, about what the explanatory gap argument actually says); the exact formulation of where they still disagree, phrased so each side would accept the wording of their own view; and the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether the agency analogy transfers: the illusionist's best case is that we also have a 'user illusion' of being the agent of our own actions, a post-hoc construction, but the realist replies that even if agency is a misattribution, there is a real experience of agency, so the illusion move only shows that our self-model is misleading, not that there's no experience. Another crux I suspect is whether the illusionist owes a harder explanation than the realist: if the self-model represents itself as having experiences, what explains that representational content without circularity? For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it -- blindsight, Charles Bonnet syndrome, or a functional duplicate that reports 'nothing' -- with each side's prediction written down in advance. Also: what would each count as evidence or argument against their own view, and if you conclude part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why -- I think it might be that they are using 'phenomenal consciousness' to mean different things (intrinsic qualitative character vs. a self-representational structure) and arguing past each other. Format: numbered agreed claims, then a disagreement table with a cruxes section.
