I'm a compiler engineer: I spend most of my days thinking about the pipeline from source to machine code, about optimization passes, and about where to draw the line between local and whole-program analysis. A friend of mine who works in philosophy of mind has been pushing Daniel Dennett's distinction between "cranes" and "skyhooks" on me — roughly, the idea that genuine explanatory lifting has to be done by local, bottom-up, mechanical processes (cranes) rather than by top-down sources of design or purpose that seem to pull a system up from outside (skyhooks) — along with Dennett's associated critique of what he calls greedy reductionism, the assumption that once the sub-level is fully described, the higher-level phenomenon has thereby been explained or dissolved. I find the metaphorical labels unhelpful, but I suspect the underlying point has a precise counterpart in how we already talk about compilation, and I'd like to see the translation done carefully.

Could you restate the cranes-versus-skyhooks distinction and the anti-greedy-reductionism argument in the vocabulary I actually use — source-level versus object-level representations, optimization passes like inlining, constant folding, and dead code elimination, local versus interprocedural or whole-program analysis, and the standing question of when a lower-level description is sufficient to predict behavior that is naturally described at a higher level? Do it as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: which parts of Dennett's position translate cleanly, which parts only translate with distortion, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For each residue item, take a position on whether it indicates that my framework is missing something (perhaps some notion of prediction from a level of description or a stance that I have no name for) or that the philosophical point is less substantive than it looks. I'm especially interested in whether the claim that a higher-level description adds nothing over and above the lower-level one survives the translation, since I suspect that's where most of the real disagreement lives, and I'd like to know if I've been missing it because my field has a different word for it.
