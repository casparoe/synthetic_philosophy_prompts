Ever since I retired I've been reading philosophy on the side, and one phrase I keep running into from my old physics days is Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in the natural sciences." My former colleagues still throw it around, usually to insist that the universe is fundamentaly mathematical, or sometimes just to shut down any doubt about a particular equation. What I can't get at is that the same phrase seems to be doing totally different jobs in different arguments. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan could actually be making -- from the mild to the wild -- and say which of those would actually support using it to argue that reality is "really" math, and which would not? A menu I can bring to my next department reunion would be ideal.
