I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I am preparing a position paper on Integrated Information Theory. I would like a consistency audit of the following provisional commitments, which I have not fully stress-tested.

(1) I accept the axiomatic core of IIT as a priori constraints on the nature of consciousness, rather than as empirical generalizations.
(2) I think the exclusion postulate is the most philosophically vulnerable part of the theory, but I still accept that the correct grain of analysis is the entity with maximal integrated information, and that at that grain the entity is the real conscious entity.
(3) I hold that consciousness is substrate-independent in the sense that what matters is the intrinsic cause-effect structure of a system, not the material that instantiates it.
(4) I nevertheless think that a digital system that is functionally isomorphic to a conscious brain would not be conscious, because software states are merely representational and lack the physical causal powers of the original system.
(5) I reject panpsychism as a standalone hypothesis, but I accept that IIT implies some minimal phenomenality for elementary physical systems, and I do not think this is an ad hoc addition to the theory.
(6) I think the combination problem is the main threat to IIT, and I think it can be met by locating phenomenal unity at the level of the highest integrated system rather than by combining micro-experiences.
(7) I think empirical evaluation of IIT should focus on whether its predictions about neural systems match behavioral and neurophysiological data, rather than on whether its axioms are intuitively true.

Please audit these commitments. Identify which pairs or clusters are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently in tension, and which are mutually supportive. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and specify which commitment would have to be revised or abandoned, and at what cost. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most without undermining the overall package.
