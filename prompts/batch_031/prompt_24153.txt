I run a book club of about 12 general readers (no philosophers), and we're finishing a book on the Galileo affair and moving to his argument that scripture and astronomy can't really contradict each other. Instead of giving a talk, I want a 90-minute activity where people feel the force of his argument from the inside, working through the situation themselves, before anyone in the room names what it is. The situation, as I understand it: a community treats a text as authoritative, and the text plainly says something about how the world is; then members of the community verify, by their own observation, that the plain reading is wrong; and now the community has to say what the text was doing when it said that. I want the roles and rules arranged so the group runs into the dilemma on its own — that the only stable positions are to reject the observation, to reject the text, or to reinterpret what the passage was for — and that they feel the cost of the third option, not just its appeal. Give me the full design: setup and materials, roles and rules, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened in the room onto Galileo's actual argument. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the argument the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. I want people to leave feeling why "the Bible isn't a science book" is a much harder and more committing claim than it sounds.
