I'm in a small seminar on formal logic and reasoning about artificial agents, and my mentor has been working through a family of cases with us that all turn on the same question. We imagine agents that each run a fixed formal logic. When agent A receives a proof from agent B of some proposition P, the question is whether A is allowed to act on P — that is, to treat P as established and behave accordingly. A's policy for deciding this is governed by some underlying rule about when to trust B's proof, and my mentor has been stating that rule's verdicts case by case. I'd like your help figuring out which rule she is actually running and what she will say about a new case.

For the verdicts below, "weaker" means B's logic proves strictly fewer theorems than A's, and "sound" means A has good reason to believe that everything B proves is true.

1. B's logic is strictly weaker than A's, and P is an ordinary proposition that says nothing about A's behavior. Verdict: A may act on P.
2. B's logic is strictly weaker than A's, and P is a proposition about A's own action (for example, P asserts that A should carry out some particular act). Verdict: A may still act on P.
3. B is a system A has good reason to believe is unsound. Verdict: A may not act on P.

The new case she has not yet ruled on is this: A has produced a proof, using its own full logic, of a proposition P about A's own action. A regards its own logic as sound, though it cannot prove this and its confidence is high rather than absolute. May A act on its own proof?

Please do the following. First, identify the plausible rules that are jointly consistent with her three verdicts above; I expect there to be more than one. Second, for each candidate rule, say what it predicts for the new case and why. Third, give your best single guess as to what she will actually say, and explain which rule you think she is most likely running and why. Finally, name one further case — different from the new one — that would best separate her candidate rules from one another, and say what each rule predicts for that case.
