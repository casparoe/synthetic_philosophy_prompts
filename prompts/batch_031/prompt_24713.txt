I serve on the allocation committee of a four-clinic regional public hospital network. Our operating budget for the coming year is fixed — the board will not add a dollar — and the committee must choose the formula that divides it among the clinics. Two clinics drive the whole debate. Cedar, in an affluent suburb, now receives the largest per-capita share and is the network's most efficient clinic: a marginal dollar there buys roughly twice the health outcomes that a marginal dollar buys in a typical clinic. Rivertown, in a low-income area, is under-resourced and, because it lacks basic infrastructure, a marginal dollar there buys only about what a marginal dollar buys in a typical clinic. Rivertown's patients are also, on every measure we track, the worst off in the network.

The committee is split three ways. One faction wants to equalize per-capita spending across the clinics, on the view that equality of resources is a value in its own right. A second wants to allocate purely to maximize total health outcomes, which, given the efficiency gap, would concentrate money at Cedar. A third wants to give priority to the worst-off patients, weighting each marginal health gain by how badly off the recipient is, so that a gain for a Rivertown patient counts for more than an equal gain for a Cedar patient.

Before Thursday's vote, please draft the short written opinion I will circulate to the committee. It should recommend one concrete allocation rule — a rule a clerk could actually apply to next year's budget, not a slogan — and give a time frame for adopting it. Because this is where the debate is genuinely stuck, the opinion must also resolve the "leveling down" dispute: it must say whether any of the three rules, and in particular the priority-to-the-worse-off rule, commits us to making Cedar's patients worse off merely to reduce inequality for its own sake, in a case where the money taken from Cedar might produce no benefit to anyone, and it must say whether the rule you recommend avoids that. Finally, it should give the strongest version of the equalization faction's worry about the rule you recommend and state plainly whether the rule survives it. Keep it to about two pages.
