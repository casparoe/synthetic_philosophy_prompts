Ok so I've been reading about these AI safety debates where two models argue about what the right thing to do is and some judge (human or another model) picks a winer. And there's a failure mode I keep running into that I don't have a word for. It's not the obvious one where a model is just flat-out lying. It's this: one model gives an argument where every single factual claim in it is actually true. But the claims are picked and ordered and emphasized in a way that pushes the judge to a conclusion that's false. And the other model can't really rebut it, because if it pushes back on any individual claim it just looks like it's nitpicking a true statment. I see the same shape outside AI too — like a candidate stringing together a bunch of true statistics, each one accurate, but the sequencing and the ones left out push the audience to a wrong conclusion, and the other side can't call them out because every number is real. And the part that gets me is the misleading party might not even be trying to mislead — it might genuinely believe the conclusion, and the structure just comes out of being trained or incentivized to present the "best case" and sound persuasive. Is there a name for this? And if not, can you carve out what it exactly is, how it's different from just deception or cherry-picking or a fallacy, and where the edges of it are? I don't want a bare name, I want the actual boundaries of the thing and why it matters for whether debate can actually be a safety mechanism.
