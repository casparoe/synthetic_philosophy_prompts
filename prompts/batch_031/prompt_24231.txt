I've been reading the forecasting and rationalist forums for a long time now, and there's a debate that's been going on for what feels like a decade that I cant figure out why nobody resolves. It's the inside view versus outside view thing, and specifically whether the outside view is a real epistemic tool or just epistemic laziness in a nice package.

The outside-view people say: when you're predicting something, don't trust your detailed inside story about this specific case, look at the reference class of similar cases and defer to the base rate. The motivation is epistemic modesty — we're overconfident in our own reasoning, we fall for the planning fallacy, we build a narrative and treat it as evidence, and the outside view is the antidote. The skeptics say the opposite: the outside view, applied as a rule, is a way to avoid engaging with specific relevant evidence, and it gets weaponized — people wave "let's take the outside view" to shut down legitimate inside-view reasoning and dodge the hard work of figuring out what's actually different about this case. And a third camp says the whole inside/outside distinction is a false dichotomy to begin with; there's no clean line between the two, the real question is just what information you have and how to weigh it.

Here's the thing that bugs me: everyone agrees on the examples. The planning fallacy, cost overruns, overconfidence — both sides grant all of it. And yet nobody moves. The outside-view people keep pointing to the same forecasting results, the skeptics keep pointing to the same cases where base-rate deferring backfired, and nobody's convinced anybody.

My working hypothesis, and I could be wrong about this, is that the disagreement isn't about forecasting at all, it's about the status of the base rate. The outside-view people treat the base rate as a prior you have to start from — your job is to be a good Bayesian who weights it correctly. The skeptics treat it as just another piece of evidence, one that specific information can override. And I think the false-dichotomy people are half right, but they're missing that the real fault line is whether the base rate is a command or a datum.

So is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about what the outside view is supposed to do, or two groups answering different questions under one name? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work — like, does the outside view actually help in practice, which could in principle be tested? Or is it a difference about standards, about what counts as engaging with the evidence versus rationalizing? Or is it bedrock intuitions about what epistemic modesty requires that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a conceptual distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or an argument that no progress should be expected and why? I don't want a survey of the positions, I already know them, I want the diagnosis of why it's stalled.
