Im been trying to figure out what I actually think about believing what people tell me, and I keep running into this debate between two camps: people who say that when I believe something on someone's word, the justification has to come from something other than their saying it — their track record, their incentives, my background beliefs about people in that situation — so testimony is never a basic source; and people who say that being told something is itself a source of justification, defeasible but not reducible to anything else. I can't tell which side I'm on. Here are my gut verdicts on some cases:

- A stranger on the street says it's 3:15. I believe it, even though I have zero evidence about that person.
- The same kind of stranger says their neighbor's dog bit someone last Tuesday. I dont really believe it.
- My friend tells me what Dave said about me at the party. I believe it, even though I can't check it and they have no reason to lie to me.
- My friend says "I think Dave is jealous of you." I'm much less convinced than I am about the report of what he actually said.
- A celebrity says a certain vaccine causes a certain disease. I don't believe it. A doctor says my scan shows a tumor. I believe that.
- My 5-year-old says the dog is under the bed. Believe. My 5-year-old says the dog is sad. Less convinced.
- A whistleblower leaks a memo from their company and I read the memo itself: I believe it. A news article the next day reporting on the memo: I'm more skeptical.
- A history book written 500 years after the events it describes: I believe it, but with a big shrug that a recent news report doesn't get.

My tentative principle is "I trust people who seem sincere and have no reason to lie," but I already know that fails the stranger/time case, since I have no idea whether the stranger is sincere. So: is there a single principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures all of these, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a case I haven't listed — say, a stranger telling me something I can't check, but which, if true, would be very bad for me? Be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate. And does the principle that comes out put me on the "ground it in other evidence" side or the "testimony is its own source" side, or is it a hybrid that smuggles one in through the back door?
