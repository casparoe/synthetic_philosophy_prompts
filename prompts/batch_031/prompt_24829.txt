I'm a distributed-systems engineer, comfortable with consensus protocols, gossip algorithms, distributed gradient descent, control loops, Lagrangian relaxation, and communication-complexity arguments. I'm preparing a discussion for engineers who tend to treat the socialist calculation debate as if it were simply the question of whether a central computer could solve a sufficiently large optimization problem. I'd like that debate restated in the vocabulary I actually use: nodes with local state, communication graphs, price signals as update messages, planning as centralized or hierarchical optimization, firm behavior as local response functions, and market adjustment as a feedback process. The positions to translate are the Misesian impossibility argument, the Hayekian argument about dispersed and uncodified knowledge, and Lange's proposal for a planned economy that emulates competitive price-setting.

Please keep the translation as faithful as you can to the original arguments rather than to their usual caricatures. In particular, distinguish whether the critics' objections are computational, in the sense that the state space is too large or the problem is too nonconvex; informational, in the sense that relevant knowledge is tacit, local, or not reportable in the form needed by a central optimizer; institutional, in the sense that incentives, accountability, and error correction matter; or epistemic, in the sense that there is no adequate benchmark against which to evaluate whether the planner's prices are right. For each major claim, say what translates cleanly into my systems vocabulary, what has to be distorted to fit it, and what cannot be expressed in it at all.

Then give me a residue report. If the debate reduces to centralized optimization being infeasible, where does that reduction break? If it does not reduce, what is left over? I especially want to know whether my control-theory framing smuggles in assumptions that the original debate was not making, such as a well-specified global objective, stable dynamics, or the idea that prices are simply estimates of marginal scarcity. If the residue points to something my vocabulary lacks, say whether that looks like a real gap in the systems framework or a sign that part of the philosophical argument was less technical than it appears. If the task as posed cannot be done faithfully because the target vocabulary is too poor for some part of the debate, say so plainly instead of forcing the translation.
