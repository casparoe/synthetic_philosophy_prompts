The problem I keep hitting is that students answer "do animals have consciousness" by just reciting that the hard problem and the explanatory gap apply to them the same way they do to us, and that tests nothing. I want the question to push them onto a specific crux instead: the argument from evolutionary continuity for animal consciousness. I want them to have to decide whether that argument is meant to be a metaphysical argument (the continuity of the underlying neural machinery *entails* the continuity of the phenomenal) or an epistemic one (it's only a reason to *believe* animals are conscious, so really an inference to the best explanation), and then to see what goes wrong on each reading. If it's epistemic, it's just IBE and inherits all the underdetermination and "physical duplicate with no consciousness" worries, so it's way weaker than its proponents think. I'd also like them to test the argument on a case where it's at its weakest, like an octopus or a bee, rather than just a dog or a chimp where the analogy to us is easy.

The exam is 2 hours total and this question should be scoped to about 45 minutes of work.
