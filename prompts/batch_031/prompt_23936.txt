I've been thinking on my own, for a while now, about the zombie argument — the idea of a being physically identical to a person but with no conscious experience — and before I write my views up I'd like a consistency check on whether they actually hang together. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a physicalist: I think everything that exists is physical, and if you were to duplicate a person in perfect physical detail, you would duplicate their experiences as well — there is no extra non-physical ingredient to consciousness.

(2) I think the zombie scenario is genuinely conceivable: I can think coherently about a being physically identical to me down to the last detail but with no conscious experience at all, and I don't think I'm merely failing to notice a contradiction in the idea.

(3) I think that, in general, if I can coherently conceive of a state of affairs, then it is at least metaphysically possible. I also think this principle is fallible — conceivability sometimes misleads us — but I'm not sure how often, or in exactly which cases.

(4) I think the reason the zombie scenario is conceivable is that the concepts we use for experience are special: terms like "pain" or "seeing red" seem to be graspable only by having the experience, and I think that specialness is what lets me picture a world in which all the physical facts hold but there is no experience.

(5) I think the gap between the physical and the experiential is real in the sense that you could never learn, from physics alone, what it is like to have an experience. But I don't think this gap is a feature of the world itself; I think it is a feature of our concepts and of the way we think.

(6) I'm confident there is something it is like to be a human, and I'm confident there is nothing it is like to be a rock, but I genuinely don't know where the boundary lies for simpler animals, and I don't think I have a principled way to draw it.

What I'd like is an audit, not a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, and for each genuine tension say how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be, along with what that revision would cost me. Then tell me which single change, if any, would clean up the most. Please don't assume I know where the problem is — I'm as unsure as you are about whether I've talked myself into a contradiction.
