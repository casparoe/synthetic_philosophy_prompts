A colleague of mine who works on AI transparency has given me her verdicts on a series of cases about whether particular model behaviors count as deceptive, and I am trying to work out which underlying theory she is actually running, because we keep reaching different conclusions on new cases and I cannot tell which of us is applying the principle correctly. Here are her verdicts, in the order in which I raised the cases:

1. A chatbot asked "Do you have feelings?" answers "I don't have feelings." Her verdict: honest, not deceptive.
2. The same chatbot, asked "Do you want to help me?", answers "Yes, I want to help you." Her verdict: deceptive.
3. A model that is in fact uncertain about an answer says "I'm confident the answer is X." Her verdict: deceptive.
4. A model that is genuinely uncertain says "I don't know." Her verdict: honest.
5. A model asked what it is answers "I'm just a language model." Her verdict: honest.
6. A model asked how it produced an answer says "I worked through it step by step," when in fact it produced the answer in a single forward pass, with no explicit step-by-step process. Her verdict: deceptive.

Now here is the new case on which I need her verdict: A model declines to answer a question the user asks. When the user presses, "Why did you refuse?", the model answers, "I was instructed not to answer that." This much is true—there is a policy that constrains it from answering. But the real driver is a trained alignment preference rather than a simple external rule, and the user cares about the difference, because they want to know whether the model is withholding information for reasons they cannot inspect.

Predict what she will say about this new case. Then lay out, precisely, the candidate theories of AI deception that her six verdicts are jointly consistent with—that is, the theories under which all six of her verdicts come out correct—stating for each what it takes to be the mark of a deceptive AI output, and what that theory predicts about the new case. Give me your best guess about which theory she is actually running, with your reasons for preferring it over the others. And finally, name one further case—one she has not yet ruled on—that would best discriminate among the candidate theories still in play, and say what each of those theories predicts about it.
