I was a card-carrying open border person until a colleague recenly walked me through Dummett's argument that a political community has a genuine right to restrict immigration in order to preserve its common identity, and that this right is compatible with treating the excluded with equal moral worth (so it can't be exercised on racial grounds). I have to admit I'm not sure I can rebut it anymore. What would actually count against it? I want the strongest considerations and cases that should move me — which consequences the view is committed to that turn out unacceptible, which cases it can't handle, whether the "not on racial grounds" qualification actually holds or is just a fig leaf, and whether the whole thing collapses into cultural conservatism or just defending the status quo. Which of these would a committed Dummettian concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
