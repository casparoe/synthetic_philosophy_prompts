For my philosophy of mind seminar, where we've been working through theories of naturalized mental content, I'd like you to construct a thought experiment in which Dretske's causal theory and Millikan's teleosemantics assign different contents to a single mental-state token. The divergence has to be generated by the two theories rather than stipulated in advance: it should turn on the contrast between a state's counterfactual causal dependence on a property (Dretske's criterion) and the selection history of the state type (Millikan's criterion). Keep the scenario realistic and to about a paragraph — a biologically plausible organism, no artifacts, no aliens. After the scenario, walk through what each theory says the state's content is and why, pinpoint which structural feature of the case is doing the work and what happens to the verdicts if you remove it, and close by sketching how a defender of the theory that comes out worse would attack the case, for instance by disputing the relevant state type or the reading of the causal dependence.
