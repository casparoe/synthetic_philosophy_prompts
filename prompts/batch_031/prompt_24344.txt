I've just finished a long argument with a friend about what makes pain bad, and I'd like to turn it into a decision-tree handout that sorts people by where they actually land rather than by which label they like. The tree should start from the case of pain asymbolia — patients who can report the sharp, burning quality in their limb but feel no sense of awfulness, do not withdraw, and do not suffer — and branch on the questions the positions genuinely come apart on: whether the sensory character itself carries the badness, whether only the affective or evaluative component does, whether badness is relational to a subject who takes the experience as their own, or whether what matters is the behavioral and motivational consequences. Each leaf should name the position (or mark it unnamed if nobody defends it) and state its most awkward commitment in one sentence, and I need at least one leaf for the view that pain asymbolia shows pain is not bad at all, one for the view that the asymbolic patient is still harmed, and one for the view that the patient is harmed in a different way. Note any unoccupied combination of answers and briefly diagnose why nobody is there, and flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate.
