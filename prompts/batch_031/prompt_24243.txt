I work in ML infrastructure, and I'm trying to understand what our standard shipping practice quietly assumes about what a "model" is, because I keep hitting a tension I can't resolve. Our gate for promoting a new version of a model — a retrain, a fine-tune, or a port to a new framework — is purely behavioral: we run a fixed regression suite of a few thousand hand-written evals plus a sample of shadowed production traffic, and if the new version's outputs are statistically indistinguishable from the old version's on that suite, we call the two versions the same model and promote the new one. Under that gate, two checkpoints with entirely different weights but matching behavior on the suite are the same model. But elsewhere in the same organization, identity is by structure and lineage: model licensing and provenance audits track the weights and how they were derived, not what the model does, and two checkpoints that behave identically but came from different training runs are different artifacts, with different owners, licenses, and safety-review histories. So we do behavioral identity here and structural identity there, and I don't know which is the real notion that the other is approximating. What view of mechanism identity does the deployment gate presuppose? Does it commit us to the view that a mechanism just is its input-output behavior, or is it compatible with a view where the internal circuit is what the mechanism is, with the behavioral test as a cheap proxy? I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory, separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say where the practice is underdetermined, and tell me what would have to change if we adopted the structural view. I'm an engineer, not a philosopher, so I may not have the right vocabulary for what I'm asking. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; the five items should cover, in order: (1) the implicit theory of mechanism identity the gate presupposes; (2) what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it merely fits well with; (3) where the practice is underdetermined, i.e., where several theories would license the same gate; (4) what would have to change in our deployment and audit practice under a structural view of mechanism identity; and (5) which current "same model" verdicts would be overturned, and what new tests or monitoring would be needed to catch the cases the behavioral gate misses.
