I'd like a consistency check before I commit to a position. Here's my current set of commitments:

(1) The gene is the fundamental unit of selection. Genes are what get faithfully copied across generations, so that's where selection "really" acts; everything else is a shadow of gene-level dynamics.

(2) Group selection is a dead end. The cheater problem (free-riders within a group) means groups of altruists get outcompeted by groups of cheaters, so you can't have stable group-level altruism. Any case that looks like group selection is really kin selection in disguise.

(3) Individual (organism-level) selection is the main engine of adaptation. Most adaptation is individuals competing against each other, and the organism is the level at which most selection gradients are strongest.

(4) Altruism is fully explained by kin selection / inclusive fitness. Hamilton's rule (rB > C) is the whole story: you help relatives because they carry copies of your genes.

(5) Cultural evolution — norms, institutions, memes — runs on the same machinery as genetic evolution, just with a different replicator (the meme instead of the gene).
