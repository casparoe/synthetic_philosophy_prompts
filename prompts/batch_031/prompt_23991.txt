Two of my colleagues have been arguement for about a year about whether the occasionalist reading of Al-Ghazālī is the only coherent way to square divine omnipotence with the causation we observe in the world. One defends occasionalism in the Kalam tradition -- creaturely causes have no causal power of their own, and God directly produces each effect in accordance with a constant conjunction. The other defends genuine secondary causation in the spirit of the Averroist tradition -- creatures are real causes, and God's sustaining of the world is a kind of dependence that doesn't cancel their efficacy. Can you write the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration? I want the claims they agree on (about the concept of a cause, about what needs explaining, about the role of the principle of sufficient reason); the precise formulations of their disagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether 'cause' is a matter of power or of regularity, and another is whether the problem of evil pushes toward or away from occasionalism -- and, for each crux, the case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down. Format it as a numberd agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
