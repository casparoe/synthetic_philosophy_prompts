I have just finished a long and, I think, inconclusive argument with a friend about whether the easy-knowledge argument—Williamson's argument that closure of knowledge under known entailment follows from the fact that one can know what one knows—genuinely establishes closure, or whether it quietly assumes the very closure it is supposed to prove. The sticking point was where the bootstrapping cases (the thermometer that reads 70, the hand in front of me) actually land: my friend insisted that at least one of the argument's ingredients must be rejected, but we could not agree on which one, because it is genuinely unclear whether bootstrapping attacks the premise that one can know that one knows, the closure premise itself, or the premise that one can know one's entitlements to trust a source.

I am setting a take-home essay question for an upper-level epistemology seminar, and I want the question to force students to do exactly that diagnostic work rather than to recite the standard summary of the debate. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme. The question should ask students to reconstruct the easy-knowledge argument from its three ingredients, to explain precisely why the bootstrapping objection threatens it, and then to argue, with reasons, which single ingredient should be rejected and why the remaining two are more defensible, including what a defender of the rejected ingredient owes by way of a reply. The model answer should itself take a clear position and must not treat the three ingredients as a menu from which any one may be dropped at will; it should identify the specific move that makes bootstrapping a threat to easy knowledge, and it should say what the diagnosis costs. The marking scheme should name the specific moves an A answer must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks, and it should separate an A from a B by requiring the student to show that the diagnosis is not merely a restatement of the original intuition that bootstrapping is bad.
