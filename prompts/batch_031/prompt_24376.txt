I'm trying to work out what I actually think about the problem of old evidence in Bayesian epistemology, going by my gut on a set of cases. Here are my verdicts:

1. Mercury's perihelion anomaly strongly confirms general relativity, even though the anomaly had been known for decades before GR was proposed. The fact that GR "explains" the anomaly rather than predicting it in advance doesn't seem to matter much to me; the anomaly was a genuine puzzle, and GR solves it.

2. A theory constructed after the fact specifically to fit Mercury's perihelion anomaly gets very little confirmation from that anomaly. If I had proposed a new theory whose only purpose was to account for the perihelion, I don't think the perihelion would confirm it much at all.

3. The cosmic microwave background strongly confirms the Big Bang model. But I feel the CMB confirms the Big Bang model more than Mercury's perihelion confirms GR, because the CMB was (roughly) predicted by the Big Bang model before it was discovered, whereas the perihelion anomaly was not predicted by GR.

4. The near-flatness of the universe confirms inflationary cosmology, even though the near-flatness was known before inflation was proposed. But I feel the flatness confirms inflation less than the CMB confirms the Big Bang, because the flatness is a fine-tuning problem that inflation solves, whereas the CMB was a genuinely surprising discovery.

5. I don't think the total-evidence objection means that old evidence fails to confirm a theory. I take the total-evidence problem to be a technical artifact of how conditional probabilities are computed, not a genuine threat to the idea that old evidence can confirm a theory.

6. I think a theory that unifies old evidence — explaining multiple pre-existing anomalies at once — gets more confirmation than the sum of what each piece of evidence would confirm on its own. For example, GR explains both Mercury's perihelion and the deflection of light; I think the unification adds confirmatory force beyond the two individual confirmations.

Is there a coherent account of confirmation that captures all of these verdicts, or a close subset of them? If no single account captures all of them, which of my verdicts does the best available account force me to drop, and why? Please also say what would change your mind about the account you settle on: is there a case where old evidence seems to confirm a theory but your account says it doesn't, is there a case where your account forces you to say a theory gets no confirmation from old evidence (and is that a cost or a feature), and which of your assumptions about the cases is most likely to be wrong and how would the account change if it were?
