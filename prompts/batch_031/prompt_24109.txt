I've been thinking about Fitch's paradox of knowability on and off for a few months, mostly by myself — I don't have a logic background, just a fair amount of patient re-reading — and I keep hitting the same wall. I understand the rough shape of the paradox, and I understand that the usual way people "solve" it is to give up some assumption the proof relies on, but I can't reliably tell which assumption a given proposed solution gives up, and I suspect some of the proposed solutions that get described as different are actually giving up the same thing. So I'd like you to build me a decision tree that sorts the main responses to the paradox, and I want it built from the logical space of the proof, not from a list of famous names or famous papers.

Here's what I need, in this order.

First, lay out the proof in a way a careful non-logician can follow, and identify the specific assumptions that the key step — the step showing that "p and not-known-p" is not knowable — actually depends on. My working list is: the knowability principle itself (every truth is possibly known); the knowledge-of-knowledge principle (if you know p, you know that you know p); closure of knowledge and of knowability under conjunction (if p and q are each known, or each knowable, then their conjunction is known, or knowable); and the normal behavior of the epistemic operator (it's factive, it distributes, the underlying modal logic is at least S4-ish). Tell me if I've mislabeled any of these, or if there's a load-bearing assumption I've missed. If I've got the proof itself wrong in a way that matters, say so before you build anything.

Second, build the tree. The root node should be the commitment "I reject the paradox's conclusion — I hold that it is possible for a truth to be unknown." From there, each branch node should be one of the assumptions from the first part, phrased as a question I could answer without already knowing which resolution it leads to (so "do you think that knowing p requires knowing that you know p?" rather than "do you reject the KK principle?"). Each leaf should be a named resolution — or "unnamed" if nobody actually defends it — and for each leaf I want to know which assumption or assumptions of the proof it gives up, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I expect at least these to show up as leaves: the response that rejects the knowability principle outright; the response that rejects the knowledge-of-knowledge principle; the response that denies closure under conjunction on the grounds that a finite agent can't know everything at once; and the response that keeps the principle but restricts it to a proper subset of truths rather than all of them. If you think any of those isn't a real, distinct position, tell me rather than forcing it in.

Third, build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names: first the branch questions and the combinations of answers they allow, and only then the actual positions located in that space. Note any combination of answers that no real position occupies, and give a one-line diagnosis of why nobody is there — is it incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored?

Fourth, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate — two resolutions that end up in the same place, or that differ only in a way the tree doesn't capture.

On format: I want the whole answer as a single JSON object, not prose, using this schema. "assumptions" is an array of objects, each with "id" (like A1, A2), "name", and "role_in_proof" (one sentence on what that assumption does in the proof). "unoccupied" is an array of objects, each with "combination" (the pattern of answers, e.g. "keep A1, keep A2, reject A3, keep A4") and "diagnosis" (one line on why nobody holds it).
