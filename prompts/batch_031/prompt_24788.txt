I write novels for a living, and I want you to do a translation I cannot do for myself. Take Foucault's account of disciplinary power — the shift from the sovereign's public, spectacular exercise of punishment to the capillary, surveilling, productive power of the institution; the panopticon treated as a principle of architecture rather than a particular building; docile bodies; the norm as a mechanism that produces the normal as an effect rather than measuring conduct against a given standard; the mutual constitution of power and knowledge, in which power produces knowledge and knowledge in turn enables power; and the archive as the system that decides what can be said. Restate each of these positions in the craft vocabulary I actually work in. That vocabulary is: focalization and point of view; the distinction between the author and the narrator; the reader's epistemic position, that is, what the reader knows against what the character knows, including dramatic irony and the unreliable narrator; the byline and the author function as the device that organizes and limits a text; the mechanics of a scene by which a reader comes to know something; and the gap between a character's interiority and their observable behavior. I am fluent in all of these as a working practice, so do not simplify or gloss them; use the terms the way I would use them at the desk.

For each Foucaultian position, give me exactly three things. First, the cleanest restatement you can manage in my craft vocabulary. Second, the distortion: the place where my terms quietly smuggle in an assumption — an intention, an agent, a single perspective, a teleology — that the original concept is built to exclude. Third, the part that has no expression in my vocabulary at all, no matter how hard I try.

Then give me the residue report, and be honest about its direction. For each item that will not carry over, tell me whether the failure is a defect of my framework — the novelist's vocabulary is too agent-saturated, too intentional, too individual, to hold what Foucault is saying — or whether it reveals that the original claim was less clear than it looked, and that the untranslatable part was doing less work than he thought. I suspect the deepest residue is the author function, because it is the one concept aimed directly at my own status; tell me whether that suspicion is right, and say what it means that the concept which most precisely describes me is also the one my craft vocabulary is least able to hold, since that vocabulary presupposes the very author it is analyzing.
