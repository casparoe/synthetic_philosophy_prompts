I've been chewing on this sleep experiment thing for like a week after reading about it on a blog, and I can't stop. Quick version in case my memory of the rules is off: a woman gets put to sleep, a fair coin is flipped, if it's heads she gets woken up once on Monday and if it's tails she gets woken up on Monday and Tuesday, and every time she wakes up her memory of the previous waking is wiped and she doesn't get told what day it is, and each time she's awake she's asked how sure she should be that the coin landed on heads. The question everyone argues about is whether she should say one half or one third, and I've decided the only way to figure out what I think is to test my gut on a bunch of variants I made up. Here's where I land.

The original case: I say one half. The coin is still a fair coin, I haven't learned anything about it, and the fact that I happen to be awake doesn't seem like the kind of thing that should change the odds. If the experimenter tells me it's Monday, still one half, no argument. If he tells me it's Tuesday, zero, obviously, since Tuesday only happens if it's tails. If the rules were heads → one waking on Monday, tails → one waking on Tuesday, I'd say one half too, one waking either way so the coin is just doing its thing. Now the variants I made up: if the coin is 99% heads instead of fair, I feel like the waking should pull me toward tails a little, like from 99% down to maybe 98%? because being woken up is more likely if it's tails, so it's evidence, just weak evidence. And if the rule were heads → one waking, tails → a thousand wakings, I'd say I'm like 99.9% sure it's tails, because I'm almost certainly one of those thousand tails-wakings. On bets: if I'm offered the chance to pay 30 cents to win a dollar if it's heads, setled once at the end of the experiment, I take it; at 45 cents I think I'd still take it but I'm less sure; at 50 cents my gut says take it because the coin is fair, but taking it feels slightly wrong, like the extra waking is tricking me into overpaying.

Here's where I'm stuck, and I can feel the inconsistency. If a 99%-heads coin only drops me to about 98%, then a fair coin should drop me all the way to a third by the same logic, but I really don't feel like a third in the original case, I feel like a half. And if I bite the bullet and say a third in the original case, then the 50-cent bet is a great deal and I should definately take it, which clashes with that "feels slightly wrong" feeling. So either my original one-half is the broken intuition, or my "a little shift" intuition is and I should be saying "no shift at all, being woken up tells you nothing about the coin." Is there a principle that captures all of this or close to it? If not, what's the simplest principle that gets most of it, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And one more: a friend of mine proposed a variant where instead of being told the day, the experimenter says "I'll tell you one true thing: either the coin landed on heads, or it's Monday," and I have no gut on that one, so whatever principle you settle on, tell me what it says I should answer there.
