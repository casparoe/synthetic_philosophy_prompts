I'm taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mathematics, and we've been working through Benacerraf's dilemma, but I find the standard presentations a bit unsatisfying and I'd like to try steelmanning a particular response rather than just cataloguing the problem. As I understand the dilemma, it runs roughly like this: if we are realists about mathematics and hold that mathematical objects are abstract, mind-independent, and causally inert, then we face a serious problem of access, since these objects stand in no causal relation to us and it is mysterious how our beliefs about them could be true; but if we try to escape by becoming anti-realists, we then struggle to explain why mathematical truths seem necessary and objective rather than conventional or contingent. So realism gets the objectivity and necessity right but the epistemology wrong, and anti-realism gets the epistemology right but the metaphysics wrong.

I'd like you to argue for the position that this dilemma is defused by structuralism, specifically the view that mathematical discourse is not really about particular objects but about structures, and that mathematical truths are truths about what holds in a given structure. Your argument should explain how, on this view, we no longer need causal access to specific abstract objects (we only need to grasp structural or relational facts, which is a different and more plausible epistemic demand), and how this move can preserve the necessity and objectivity of mathematical truths without committing us to a particular set-theoretic ontology.

If possible, please also engage seriously with two of the most pressing objections to this response: first, the worry that the structuralist's epistemic story is just the access problem in disguise, since we apparently still need some physical cognitive process that tracks a causally inert structure; and second, the worry that structuralism quietly reintroduces a form of realism by making structures the bearers of mathematical truth. For each, say how a structuralist should reply, and whether the reply is genuinely defusing the dilemma or merely relocating it.
