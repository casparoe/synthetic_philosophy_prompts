My friend Dana has been going down a speciesim rabbit hole for the past few months, and she's made up her mind about a bunch of specific cases. I've been keeping score because I'm pretty sure she's running some consistent rule under the hood and I want to figure out which one. Here's what she's said so far:

- Factory farming for cheap meat: speciesist. "We'd never do this to people, and the only reason we do it to cows is that they're cows."
- Testing a new lipstick on chimpanzees: speciesist. "The chimps suffer, and we just don't count that suffering because they're not people."
- Wearing a fur coat: speciesist. "We're just using the animal as a resource."
- A doctor performing a non-consensual organ harvest on a healthy patient to save five dying patients: wrong, but not speciesism. "It's a rights violation, and it's a human, so species isn't even the issue."
- A farmer in a rabies-ravaged region culling stray dogs that have been biting people: not speciesist. "We'd probably put down a rabid human who was biting people too, so it's not about species."
- Keeping a parrot as a companion in a small flat, well fed and let loose to fly around: not speciesist. "The parrot's actually well off, it gets affection and space, it's a good life for it."

Now the new case: a big city zoo keeps a single elephant in a very spacious, enriched enclosure. The elephant is healthy, well fed, and by all behavioral signs seems content with it's routine, but it was captured in the wild and will never go back, and it's there mainly to draw tourists and keep the zoo solvent. Will Dana call this speciesist?

Honestly I care less about the yes or no than about the rule.
