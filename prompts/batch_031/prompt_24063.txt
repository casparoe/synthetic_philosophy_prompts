I'm a PhD student in political philosophy and I'm putting together a handout for a seminar on judgment aggregation. The discursive dilemma / doctrinal paradox is the centerpiece, and I want a decision tree that takes a student from "I accept that proposition-wise majority rule can produce an inconsistent collective judgment" to a named position. Each branch node should be one of the axioms of the List-Pettit impossibility theorem (individual rationality, collective rationality, unanimity, systematicity, and whatever else you think belongs on the tree), and each leaf a response to the dilemma: premise-first, conclusion-first, just accept the inconsistency, abandon collective rationality, abandon systematicity, abandon unanimity, the non-functionalist or deliberative move, and anything else you think is a real position people actually defend. Phrase every node as a question a student could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate. The point is to sort students by what they actually accept, not by which label they like. Also, for each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment, so students can see the cost of each position.
