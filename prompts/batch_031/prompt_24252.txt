I work on the animal-welfare side of a combined fish-and-shellfish aquaculture operation: we run Atlantic salmon in sea pens, rainbow trout in land-based tanks, a prawn pond system, and we process some of our own crustaceans. Our internal welfare panel makes case-by-case calls on which handling and slaughter practices we will permit, and I'd like a second read on where we should land on a new one. One thing to keep in mind: the panel is genuinely split on whether crustaceans are capable of pain, so some of our past calls rest on that question and some deliberately do not.

Here are the calls the panel has already made. (1) Percussive stunning of salmon immediately before slaughter — approved, because it is fast and the fish is unconscious before it dies. (2) CO2 stunning of salmon — approved but flagged, because it is slower and the fish gasps, and we are not sure whether the gasping is aversive to the fish or just a reflex. (3) Ice-slurry slaughter of trout with no prior stunning — rejected, because the fish stays alert for a long time. (4) Harvest of prawns by draining the pond and netting them, with no stunning — approved, on the view that a crustacean cannot feel pain and the handling is brief. (5) Live transport of lobsters to the processor, where they are then boiled — rejected, because the transport is long and stressful, even if we grant that the boiling itself is quick. (6) Grading of catfish using a bath anesthetic (tricaine) — approved, because the anesthetic keeps the handling stress down.

The new case: we are evaluating adding farmed crabs, and the proposed kill method would be live boiling — no stunning at any point, and no long transport, because the crabs would be boiled on-site within minutes of harvest. Given the six calls above, what do you think the panel should decide on the crab case? I am less interested in a bare yes or no than in: (a) the rule or rules the panel seems to be running; (b) the other rule(s) that fit all six past calls just as well; (c) which of those rules predict different answers on the crab case, and what each predicts; and (d) which past call is the closest analog to the crab case — and whether that answer depends on which rule you think the panel is running. If the past calls genuinely underdetermine the outcome, say what principle the panel would be adopting if it approves and what principle it would be adopting if it rejects.
