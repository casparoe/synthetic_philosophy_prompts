My two friends Mara and Theo got into a long argument last week about where our little animal-welfare charity should put a one-time grant, and I honestly can't tell who's right. The grant goes to either Program A, which would eliminate severe chronic pain in about 40,000 farm animals, or Program B, which would ease a mild, low-grade kind of suffering (think minor discomfort, nothing that stops them eating or moving) in about 2 million of them.

Theo is a threshold negative utilitarian. He believes suffering below some threshold is "light" and can be freely traded off against pleasure, but suffering above the threshold is lexically prior — no amount of pleasure, even for many more beings, can make up for it. So on his view we have to pick A, full stop. Mara thinks pain and pleasure are just commensurable on one scale, kinda like weight: you multiply severity by number of beings and compare the totals, and B wins by a mile.

The argument went roughly like this:

(1) Mara: Your threshold is arbitrary. Why is 7/10 suffering "severe" and 6.9/10 not? You can't give a principled line, so you just moved the problem.

(2) Theo: The line doesn't have to be precise. There's no exact line between "cold" and "freezing," but that doesn't make the distinction useless. The real point is that there's a difference in kind between suffering and pleasure — suffering is what it is like for an experience to go wrong, pleasure is just the presence of something good — and the threshold marks where that difference starts to matter.

(3) Mara: OK, but then your view has a new absurdity. You'd say it's fine to cause a million beings a mild headache (just below the threshold) in order to give a billion beings a mild pleasure, because below the threshold everything is just numbers on the scale and the pleasure total wins. That's the suffering trade-off, and its exactly as bad as the repugnant conclusion you claim to be avoiding.

(4) Theo: My view isn't supposed to fix every standard-utilitarian conclusion, just the worst one, which is severe suffering getting outweighed by a big total of pleasure. And yeah, I'll grant that the mild trade-off is a price I pay; the line between mild and severe is fuzzy anyway, so your "absurdity" is just a boundary case.

(5) Mara: Fuzzy isn't the same as nonexistent. If the line is fuzzy, then there's a whole region of "sort of severe" suffering where you have to make arbitrary calls about which side it lands on, which means your theory is just standard utilitarianism with a fudge factor.

(6) Theo: A fudge factor that actually changes the verdict in the cases we care about. And your view has its own fudge factor: the claim that pain and pleasure are commensurable is doing all the heavy lifting, and youve never justified it. It's just an assumption.

They both want my call and I don't have one. Can you walk through the exchange move by move and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? And if there's something both of them are missing, tell me that too.
