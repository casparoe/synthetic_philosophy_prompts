A colleague of mine is a strict Lakatosian, and for several years he has classified research programmes in our history-of-science seminars with a consistency that has always struck me. Here is his track record. He called the kinetic, molecular theory of heat progressive, against the caloric theory; the germ theory of disease progressive, against the miasma theory; and the Big Bang cosmology progressive, against the steady-state cosmology. In each of those three cases the programme he endorsed was currently ahead of its rival on the balance of confirmed predictions, had anticipated facts the rival could not reach, and had developed through moves generated by its own framework rather than through patches fitted to fresh anomalies. He called the caloric theory degenerating, the miasma theory degenerating, and the steady-state cosmology degenerating; and in each of those three the programme was currently behind its rival, had offered no fact the rival lacked, and had been kept alive by ad hoc additions tailored to each new anomaly.

A new case comes up this term. A certain programme is currently ahead of its rival on the balance of confirmed predictions, it has few unexplained anomalies, and its recent theoretical moves were generated from its own core principles rather than fitted to anomalies. But it has not anticipated a single novel fact: everything it explains was already observed, its rival is the one actually producing new confirmed predictions, and the only reason it is ahead is that it re-explains the old data a little more elegantly.

What do you think my colleague will call this programme — progressive or degenerating? I am less interested in a bare label than in the rule he appears to be running. Lay out the candidate rules his track record is consistent with, say what each one predicts for this new case, give your best guess for his verdict and the reason you would prefer it, and name the single further case that would best tell those rules apart. Lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, and do not restate the question.
