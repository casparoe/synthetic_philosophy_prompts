Im a PhD student in political theory (institutional design side) and I keep circling back to a rule a city budget office I advise has actually drafted, and I cant get a clean read on what it's quietly assuming. The ordinance in a nutshell: for any infrastructure spend over $5M, the council commits in advance to fund whichever option a designated prediction market — sealed so nobody can see the odds until the vote, with a 90% confidence bar — predicts will maximize the city's GDP ten years out. The council is not allowed to override, even if they're convinced the market is wrong, and the market's internal model stays confidential. It's basically futarchy (the Hanson thing), but I want to treat it as an institutional practice and reverse-engineer the philosophy instead of just citing Hanson.

What bugs me: the goal is straight-up consequentialist (pick the welfare-maximizing policy, GDP as the proxy), but the mechanism is deontological in a weird way — you're forbidden from acting on your own consequentialist judgment and have to trust a sealed number. We're doing a consequentialist end with a rule that blocks consequentialist reasoning at the exact decision point. Is that a real tension or am I misreading it?

Work through what the practice presupposes, part by part, and give it to me as a table with these columns, in this order:
- element — the specific piece of the rule (e.g. "GDP as the fixed metric," "sealed market," "no-override," "90% confidence bar," "money as the unit of betting")
- commitment — the view of welfare / knowledge / rationality / democracy that element rests on
- status — strict if the element only makes sense under that view, compatible if several views would license it
- rival_ruled_out — a rival view the element quietly excludes
- if_replaced — concretely, what changes in the ordinance if that view is dropped

Then, after the table, a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- tension_real (bool) — whether the consequentialist-goal / deontological-mechanism tension I flagged actually holds
- internally_consistent (bool) — whether the rule coheres under one framework or is borrowing from two incompatible ones
- borrowed_frameworks (array of strings) — the distinct frameworks the rule leans on
