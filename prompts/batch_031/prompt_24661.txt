Question: Consider a two-person bargaining problem with a closed, convex feasible set S ⊂ R² and disagreement point d = (d1, d2). (a) State the four axioms that characterize the Kalai–Smorodinsky (KS) solution, and identify which one differs from the corresponding axiom in the Nash bargaining solution. (b) Give the geometric construction of the KS point in terms of the disagreement point d and the ideal (utopia) point m, where m_i is the maximum utility player i can attain in S. (c) Explain why the KS solution fails the independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA).

Student's answer:
(a) The KS solution is the unique bargaining solution that satisfies (i) Pareto optimality, (ii) symmetry, (iii) invariance to positive affine transformations of utility, and (iv) weak monotonicity. The first three are shared with the Nash solution; the only difference is that Nash employs independence of irrelevant alternatives in place of weak monotonicity.
(b) Let d = (d1, d2) be the disagreement point and m = (m1, m2) the ideal point. Draw the line segment joining d to m. The KS solution is the point where this line meets the Pareto frontier of S. Equivalently, it is the frontier point at which the two players' gains are held in the same proportion, so that each player's gain relative to the ideal point is the same fraction of that player's maximal possible gain.
(c) The KS solution violates IIA because the ideal point is a function of the entire feasible set. If we add a new feasible alternative, the set of Pareto-efficient outcomes can shift, and with it the point where the line from d to m strikes the frontier — even though the newly added alternative is not itself the one chosen.
