I've been going back and forth on my view about laws of nature for a while now, and I'd like a consitency check before I write it up for a metaphysics seminar. I keep landing on this package and I can't quite tell if it hangs together, so here it is:

(1) I'm a Humean about laws. The laws aren't fundamental features of reality; they supervene on the complete spatiotemporal distribution of local, particular matters (the Humean mosaic, or whatever you want to call it). There's no extra layer of governance sitting on top of the mosaic.
(2) The best-system account (the Lewis one, as I understand it) is the right way to cash out which regularities count as laws. A regularity is a law just in case it's an axiom, or a theorem close to an axiom, of the deductive system that best trades off simplicity against strength over the whole mosaic.
(3) But I also think causal powers are real and fundamental. An electron's charge isn't just a label for a bundle of observed regularities; it's a genuine dispositional property that grounds why it repels other electrons. I'm not a regularist about causation.
(4) I think it's a contingent fact that the laws are what they are. There are possible worlds where the fundamental constants come out differently, and the laws would be different too. I'm a little worried this sounds like it clashes with (1), since the mosaic seems to fix everything, but I don't think it actually does.
(5) I don't think the laws are brute or unexplained. They flow from the natures of the fundamental entities. The reason the electromagnetic laws are what they are is that that's what electric charge essentially is.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way to make the whole thing consistent? I'd rather you rank the real tensions by severity and, for each one, say specifically what would have to give, rather than listing everything as equally bad. And if there's a single revision that cleans up the most, point it out. I genuinely don't know where the the problem is, so don't assume I'm most attached to any particular item.
