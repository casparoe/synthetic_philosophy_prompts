I've been reading along the edges of the debate over how much moral weight to give to the far future, and I keep running into a move I can't quite place. The argument I keep meeting goes roughly like this: the far future is going to contain a very large, perhaps literally unbounded, number of people, so even a tiny change in the probability that the future turns out well has an enormous expected effect, and therefore we should prioritize actions that affect the far future over actions that help people in the near term. I can follow the broad shape of it, and I can see why a careful person would find it compelling. What I can't place is a difficulty I think the argument runs into, and I don't have a name for it.

Here is the difficulty as I see it. Suppose the far future is so good that there is no upper bound on how much good it contains. Then, in the expected-value arithmetic the argument is using, any action that even slightly raises the chance of that future works out to being worth an unlimited amount — and so does every other action that slightly raises the chance, no matter how different the actions are from one another. Concretely: an action that marginally reduces the risk of a catastrophe that would truncate the future, and an entirely different action that marginally improves the long-run trajectory, both come out as infinitely valuable, and the arithmetic can no longer tell me which to pick. It is as if the very feature that is supposed to make the far future the dominant consideration also makes that consideration too coarse to discriminate between the very actions it is supposed to rank. I have seen people gesture at difficulties of this general kind, but I do not think I have seen this exact configuration named.

Is there an established term for it, and if so, how exactly does it work? I am not satisfied with a bare label. I want to know what the phenomenon precisely is, how it differs from the nearest problems I may be half-remembering — I have a vague sense that there is something about gambles with enormous expected payoffs, and something separate about values that cannot be compared at all — and where its edges are, in particular what the main proposed ways out are and whether any of them actually defuses the difficulty or merely relocates it.
