I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a physicist who defends the de Broglie–Bohm (Bohmian) interpretation of quantum mechanics. She is very good at defusing the usual nonlocality objection by conceding that the theory is explicitly nonlocal and treating Bell's theorem as a feature rather than a bug. Draft a sequence of six to eight interview questions that go beyond having her restate the theory, each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I want at least one question on the quantum equilibrium hypothesis — specifically, how the theory is supposed to explain why systems are found in the |ψ|² distribution rather than some other, and what the typicality measure is supposed to buy us; one on the ontological status of the wave function, i.e. whether it is a real physical field (in high-dimensional configuration space) or a merely nomological entity, and what that choice implies for the "empty waves" that persist after a particle has left part of the wave function; and one on how the explicitly nonlocal dynamics are supposed to be reconciled with special relativity. Close with a question about what experimental evidence could count against the theory, given that in equilibrium it reproduces the predictions of standard quantum mechanics.
