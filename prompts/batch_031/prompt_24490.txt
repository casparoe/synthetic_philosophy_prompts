I work on the governance tooling team at a prediction market platform, and clients keep asking us to "just do futarchy" — hand a policy decision to a prediction market — without understanding what that actually requires. We get about two of these requests a week, and the review has to be doable by an engineer who is not a philosopher in a single working day, with the market design lead signing off. Please turn futarchy into a concrete design procedure my team can run on a proposed policy question. In order, the procedure should specify: how to reformulate the question so the market is forecasting the right counterfactual — what would actually happen if the policy were adopted — rather than some untradeable value question; what counts as a sufficient resolution criterion (who adjudicates, on what evidence, and what happens if the outcome turns out to be ambiguous); what manipulation and verification checks to run before the market goes live; how to convert market prices into an actual decision, including what to do when related markets disagree; and at which step the procedure must stop and hand the decision to a human because the remaining question is one of values, not prediction. Then run the procedure on two proposals: a city council deciding whether to ban single-use plastic bags, and a university deciding whether to fund a ten-year basic research program. Respond in the style of an expert in market design and decision theory. Think very carefully before answering, and be explicit about which steps are judgment calls that no amount of market design can eliminate.
