I cover science and ideas for a national news outlet, and I also sit on the outlet's standards and ethics board. A case has come before the board that I have been asked to write up in advance of Thursday's meeting, and I would like you to draft the written opinion for me.

About six years ago, a scientist at a pharmaceutical company gave a set of internal documents to one of our science reporters, who was then working as a freelancer. The documents suggested that the company had known for years about a serious adverse effect of one of its drugs and had suppressed it. The reporter promised the scientist that neither the documents nor the scientist's identity would ever be published, and the scientist agreed to hand them over on that condition alone. The scientist has since died. The drug is still on the market, and a study published this year links it to the very adverse effect the documents described. The editor wants to publish the documents now, arguing that the public interest in an ongoing safety hazard outweighs a promise made to a dead man by a freelancer who is no longer with the paper.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — publish, withhold, or publish with redaction — with a time frame, and it should engage seriously with the question of why the promise binds in the first place: whether its force rests on the scientist's reliance on it, on the autonomy of the person who made it, or on the practice of promising that the paper is part of, rather than simply restating our confidentiality policy. It should also treat honestly the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please keep the opinion to no more than 450 words.
