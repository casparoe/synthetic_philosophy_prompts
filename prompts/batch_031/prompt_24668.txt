I work in the rights and licensing team at a mid-sized media company. Part of my job is deciding which elements of our works and products we can control and which are effectively common property. Over the past few years the team has made a series of rulings on whether a particular element is protectable expression or an unprotected idea, and I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit rule our rulings have been applying and then use it to decide a new case we are facing this week.

Here are five prior rulings, in roughly chronological order:

1. A rival reality show used the same premise as our flagship show — a house, hidden cameras, weekly evictions. We declined to enforce.
2. A rival series reused a specific sequence of scenes from our show, with near-identical dialogue in three of them. We enforced.
3. A rival game used the same scoring method as ours — a formula for computing bonus points from consecutive matches. We declined to enforce.
4. A rival fantasy novel used a "chosen one saved by prophecy" arc. We declined to enforce.
5. A rival used our cover art for a reissued edition, with minor recoloring and a different typeface. We enforced.

The new case: our app's signature feature is a "time machine" view — a vertical timeline through a project's history, navigable by scrolling only, rendered as a "spine" with each version shown as a "vertebra." A competitor has just released an app with a vertical timeline, scroll-only navigation, and a spine-like rendering with nodes. They use a different color palette, different typography, and a different animation (theirs: nodes pop in; ours: the spine grows).

My working hypothesis is that our five rulings are jointly consistent with at least two, and perhaps three, different rules about where the idea/expression boundary lies — something like "protect only distinctive authorial choice," "protect concrete expression but not what functional constraints or merger force," and "protect only concrete instantiation." I am not sure which rule, or combination of rules, the team has actually been running, and I suspect this new case is one on which those candidate rules diverge.

Please respond in the following four sections, in this order:

1. "Candidate rules" (~300 words): reconstruct the implicit rules that the five prior rulings are consistent with. State each rule in one or two sentences, and show that it predicts all five rulings.

2. "Applying the rules" (~350 words): for each candidate rule, state what verdict it gives on the new case and why. Be explicit about exactly where the rules diverge.

3. "The most consistent verdict" (~300 words): given the track record, what should we decide? Identify the closest prior analog among the five rulings, say which verdict best coheres with our own precedents, and state honestly what principle the team would be adopting with each available choice.

4. "The discriminating case" (~200 words): which single further case — one we could bring to the team next quarter — would most cleanly discriminate the candidate rules, and what would each rule predict on it?

I am not asking for a survey of copyright law or a list of cases; I want a diagnosis of the rule structure and a recommendation that is consistent with the track record.
