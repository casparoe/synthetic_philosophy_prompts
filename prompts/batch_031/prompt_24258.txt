I'm building a small anthology of poems on the big epistemology problems for my reading group, and I want one that actually does the work of underdetermination of theory by the evidence instead of just labelling it. Can you write a sestina on it, using these six as the end-words: light, orbit, fall, time, space, speed? The idea is that those six are the shared observations — the same data every theory has to fit — and the sestina's rotation should do the argument for me. Each of the six stanzas is a different theory trying to account for exactly those same six things, so the reader keeps watching the end-words (the data) come back identical while the explanations in the body keep shifting underneath. Then the envoi lands on the real point: that no matter how much evidence we pile on, it never forces the choice between them, it just underterminates. I want it accurate enough that someone who knows the Duhem–Quine stuff recognizes the puzzle, not just a rhyme scheme with a philosophy sticker on it.
