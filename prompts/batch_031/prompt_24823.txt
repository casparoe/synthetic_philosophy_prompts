I work in procurement for a mid-sized grocery chain, and every quarter our sustainability team ranks proposed sourcing changes partly by how much harm they do to wild animals; the top-ranked change gets the first supplier switch of the year, and I'm the one who signs off. I have no ecologist on staff and I've been working out the logic of this on my own, and I keep getting stuck: I can't tell how to separate the harm that comes from the land a product takes, the harm from the animals killed while growing and harvesting its feed, and the harm from its production itself, and the insect deaths in crop cultivation are in numbers I have no way to count. Please write a decision procedure my team can run on a proposed sourcing change within about two days, grounded in whatever the animal-ethics literature actually says about prioritizing under deep uncertainty: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each when all I have are order-of-magnitude guesses, how to handle the question of the insects explicitly — that is, show at which step the ranking changes depending on how much moral weight we give insects versus mice versus birds — and the point at which the procedure has to stop and hand the question to a person because it is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on two proposals: replacing a beef line with a chicken line, and replacing the chicken line with a grain-based plant line. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire and what it cannot do, and tell me which proposal it favors even if that's not the answer I'd like. End your reply with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that states which of the two proposals the procedure ranks as producing the larger reduction in wild animal harm per serving, or "indeterminate" if it cannot.
