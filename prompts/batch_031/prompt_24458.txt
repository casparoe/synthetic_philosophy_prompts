I've been trying to figure out, for my own moral life, where the line is between a preference (something I actually value and get to have) and a bias (something I should recognize as distorting my judgment and discount). I keep making different calls and can't see the principle behind them. Here are my verdicts on a bunch of cases:

- I rate job candidates from my own university a little more favorably on equal resumes, and I call that a bias.
- I think my mom's cooking is genuinely better than my sister-in-law's, all else equal, and I call that a preference.
- I'm warmer and more generous to people I like and stingier to people I find grating, and I tell myself that's just what my affections are like, a preference.
- I catch myself assuming the coworker with the heavy accent is less competent than the one without, on the same output, and I call that a bias.
- I think it's much worse to hurt a dog than a pig, even though the pig is probably smarter, and I cant tell if that's a bias (speciesism) or a real preference about which animals I find more like me.

The two I keep flip-flopping on are the generosity one and the dog one, since they both look like favoring my in-group but I'm not treating them the same. Is there a principle that delivers all of these, or at least most, and what does it say about the two I'm stuck on? Also I have a nagging worry that any principle clean enough to draw the line will just force me to admit most of my "preferences" are biases I haven't caught yet. Is that a real threat, and if so which of my verdicts does it cost me?
