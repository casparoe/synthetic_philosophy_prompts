A popular philosophy article I read summarizes dialetheism like this: "Dialetheism is the view that all contradictions are true — for instance, the Liar sentence 'this sentence is false' is both true and false. Since classical logic lets you derive anything from a contradiction, dialetheists work in paraconsistent logics, which block the inference from A and not-A to B. Dialetheism is best understood as a version of anti-realism about truth: it denies bivalence, the principle that every sentence is either true or false. Its central motivation is semantic paradox, and its main cost is that the dialetheist must accept that some of the claims he himself makes are false." The article then concludes that this self-refutation is a genuine refutation of dialetheism. I'm inclined to think the self-refutation point is the real objection here, but I can't tell whether the article states it correctly or misstates it. Go through the summary sentence by sentence and tell me what a dialetheist such as Graham Priest would accept, what is a subtle misrepresentation, and what is flat-out wrong — and whether the conclusion that the self-refutation refutes dialetheism follows from anything true in the summary.
