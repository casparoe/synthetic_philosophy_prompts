I'm a senior in high school, and I've been reading a bit of social choice theory for fun after stumbling into a chapter on fairness in group decisions. I keep running into the same strange structure, and I'm trying to figure out whether it has a name, because I strongly suspect it does but I can't place it.

Here is what I keep noticing. You have a group of people making a collective decision, and you want two things to hold at once: (1) a "fairness" rule that gives each person real, decisive say over at least one area that is personally theirs — for instance, if the choice concerns whether my own private manuscript gets published, I get to settle it; and (2) a "nobody-loses" rule — if literally everyone in the group ranks option A above option B, then the group must rank A above B. Each rule looks obviously reasonable on its own. But I keep finding cases where the two rules together force the group into a contradiction: the collective ranking ends up putting an option above itself, or simply cannot be formed at all, even though every individual's preferences are perfectly sensible and no single person is controlling the whole outcome.

A concrete example I have seen: there is a book that everyone in the group would rather not be published, but one person is the author and wants it out, while another is a reader who is offended by it and wants it kept out. The "private say" rule gives the author the right to decide about their own book (so the author can force publication), and it also gives the offended reader the right to decide about being exposed to it (so the reader can force non-publication). Both rights attach to the exact same choice, and they point in opposite directions, so the group is stuck. I have seen a second version of the same shape where it is not a book but a community deciding whether to allow something in a shared park, and two neighbors each stake a "this patch is mine to decide" claim over the same piece of ground and disagree about it.

My questions: (a) Is there an established name for this specific phenomenon — the contradiction that appears when individuals' private domains of decision overlap and the people holding those domains disagree? (b) What exactly has to be in place for the contradiction to arise, and — this is the part I am least sure about — is the overlap between the two people's private domains the real cause, or is it something else entirely? (c) I have also heard of a famous result by someone named Arrow saying that no voting rule can satisfy a certain list of conditions. How is this different from that? Is it just a special case of it, or a genuinely different kind of impossibility, and if the latter, what is the precise difference in the conditions and in what actually goes wrong? (d) And is this really a "paradox," or is it better understood as a plain impossibility theorem, and does it matter whether the fairness rule is a weak version (merely: if everyone ranks A above B, the group must too) or a strong one that also has to handle tied or weak preferences?

I am not satisfied with a bare label. I want the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges are, so I can tell it apart from the Arrow result and from a simple logical inconsistency in the premises.
