I've been writing a piece on whether meritocracy is actually fair, and I keep coming back to what I take to be the critics' strongest card. The argument is that nobody chose their natural talents — intelligence, physical gifts, the raw material for effort — so nobody can genuinely deserve the rewards that track those talents. Since "merit" in practice is mostly just talent plus the effort that talent makes easy, any merit-based reward is, on this view, really a reward for the luck of the draw in one's genes, not for anything the person earned. If that's right, the meritocratic claim that people "earn" their positions is a myth, and I honestly can't see how a defender of merit-based rewards answers it. What is the strongest reply available to them?
