I've been mulling over this inside view vs outside view stuff for a while (planning fallacy, base rates, all that) and I keep getting stuck on what I think is called the reference class problem. Can you write a dialogue between a startup founder and a friend who keeps bringing up base rates, where they're trying to settle what probablity to assign to the startup suceeding? The founder argues from the inside view — the specific things that are different about their case — and the friend pushes the outside view — the base rate for companies like theirs. I want them to actually grapple with each other's strongest version, not take turns giving speeches: the founder should press the point that being different is exactly why the base rate doesn't apply, and the friend should press back that every founder thinks that, and that "founders who think they're different" is itself a reference class with its own base rate. And I want them to land somewhere at the end — is one of them just wrong, or is the right move some kind of blend, and if it's a blend, how do you decide the weights? Neither of them should be a strawman.
