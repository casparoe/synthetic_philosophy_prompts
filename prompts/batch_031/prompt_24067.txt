In a seminar debate about whether I should revise my view when my epistemic peer disagrees with me, my interlocutor kept invoking the slogan "peer disagreement is a defeater" to push me toward conciliation. I suspect the slogan is doing more work than it can support. Lay out the distinct precise claims that "peer disagreement is a defeater" could be making -- what counts as a "peer", what exactly the "defeater" defeats, how strong the defeater is, and whether it operates at the first-order or the higher-order level -- and say for each reading whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and which reading my interlocutor was most likely invoking when using it to push me toward conciliation.
