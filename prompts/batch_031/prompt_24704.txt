I'm a science journalist who covers consciousness research, and I've been trying to pin down my own position on Integrated Information Theory ahead of a long-form piece. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to it in print:

(1) I think IIT's axioms — intrinsic existence, information, integration, exclusion, and composition — are the right starting point for a serious theory of consciousness, and that IIT is currently the best candidate on the market.
(2) I think the exclusion postulate is the most vulnerable part of the theory and would be dropped or substantially revised in any adequate version.
(3) I think IIT makes genuinely testable empirical predictions about which neural substrates are conscious, and that this is what separates it from armchair philosophy.
(4) I do not think IIT is a panpsychist theory, and I would be reluctant to tell my readers that it implies rocks have experiences.
(5) I think the mapping problem — the gap between phi and specific phenomenal character — is real but not fatal to IIT.
(6) I think IIT is primarily a theory of the structure of experience, and that its claims about the existence of experience are secondary.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are. For each genuine tension, say which commitment I should give up and what the cost of that revision would be. And tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't want a lecture on IIT; I want an audit of whether this package hangs together.
