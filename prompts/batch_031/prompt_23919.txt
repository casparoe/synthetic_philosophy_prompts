I retired from evolutionary ecology about six years ago and recently, to everyone's mild amusement, started reading philosophy of mind. What keeps coming back to me is this idea that Darwin and Turing both pulled off some kind of inversion. Darwin showed you can get design without a designer — all that adaptation and complexity with no one in the loop understanding what's happening. Turing did the same move for thought: he replaced the question of whether a machine can think with a purely behavioral test, so you get inteligent-looking behavior with no need to grant it any actual understanding. So the general pattern is competence without comprehension. I've been trying to pin down what I actually think about when this inversion is legitimate, by going through cases and trusting my gut, and I can't get a consistant story out of it.

Here are my verdicts. A chess engine that beats a grandmaster: competent, zero comprehension, it's just search. A thermostat holding a room at a set temperature: competent at homeostasis, no comprehension, obviously. A nightingale singing: I'll grant it's competent at producing something we'd call beautiful, but I don't think it comprehends music in any real sense. A GPS that reroutes you around an accident: competent at navigation, no comprehension of the city. A termite mound that holds a near-constant internal temperature and humidity: competent at climate control, sure, but I don't think any individual termite comprehends it, and I'm honestly not sure what to say about the colony as a whole. A parrot that says "sorry, I dropped the food" right after dropping a piece of food: I keep flip-flopping on this one, but I lean toward no comprehension. Now here's where I get stuck. A neural network that flags tumors on mammograms better than a radiologist: I feel like this one is different — I can't quite say it's "seeing" the tumor, but I also can't quite reduce it to pattern-matching the way I do the chess engine. And a dog that learns to press a light switch to get a treat: I'm pretty sure the dog "gets" the switch-food connection in some minimal sense, even though it's just conditioning. So why do I treat the dog as having some comprehension but not the parrot, when the parrot's behavior is arguably more sophisticated? And why do I treat the tumor-detecting network as somehow on the verge of comprehension but not the chess engine, when both are "just computation"? Is there a principle that gets all of these, or at least close, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the case I keep coming back to — a person who drives home from work and arrives with no memory of the route, having navigated perfectly competently but with no conscious comprehension of having done so?
