I don't have any training in philosophy, but I've been thinking on my own for a while about whether it is ever all right to bring a new person into the world, and a friend of mine has very settled views on the subject that I keep trying to make sense of. Let me give you the verdicts she has actually given me, and I'd like you to work out what underlying rule or rules she seems to be running, and then tell me what she would say about a new case I haven't put to her yet.

The verdicts she has given me:

First, she is certain that it would be wrong to bring into the world a person who will live a life of severe, unbroken suffering — even if that person would have wanted to exist, and even if their existing makes no one else's life any worse.

Second, she is equally certain that it would not be wrong to bring into the world a person who will live a genuinely happy, comfortable life — even in a situation where, by bringing them into the world, the total amount of happiness in the world goes up while some unrelated person goes on suffering and we do nothing to help them.

Third, she thinks it would be wrong to bring into the world a person whose life is a net loss, meaning more suffering than happiness all told — even if the suffering is mild and just spread thinly across a long life — on the assumption that the people already in the world are, on the whole, doing reasonably well.

Now here is the new case I want a prediction on. Suppose we genuinely have the power, at no cost to anyone, either to bring into existence a thousand people who will each live a happy life containing no suffering at all, or to do nothing, in which case those thousand people simply never exist. The people already in the world are, on the whole, doing reasonably well. What does she say we should do?

What I'd like from you, in that order: (a) the underlying rule or rules that her three verdicts are consistent with, stated in plain terms; (b) what each of those rules predicts she will say about the thousand-people case; (c) your best single guess about which rule she is really running, and therefore what she will actually answer, together with your reasons for the guess; and (d) one further case I could put to her that would best tell those rules apart, in case the thousand-people case does not settle it.
