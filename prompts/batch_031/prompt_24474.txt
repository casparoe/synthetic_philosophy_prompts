One side holds the open-future position, that such a future-tensed proposition is genuinely indeterminate at the present moment and only becomes true or false when the future is realized. The other side gives the determinist reply, that the future is already fixed, so every future-tensed proposition is in fact already either true or false, even if we don't yet know which. I want both sides restated in terms of Kripke frames, monotonicity of the forcing relation, the law of excluded middle as a schema, the distinction between a proposition's failing to be forced at a node and its negation's being forced there, and the space of intermediate logics.
