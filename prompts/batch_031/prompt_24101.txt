Construct a thought experiment in which an AI system counts as aligned by one standard and misaligned by another, the two standards being (i) the system faithfully optimizes its stated objective and (ii) the outcome is what its operators actually value. Run it in the direction that is harder to build: the system is a near-perfect optimizer for its objective, the objective is a reasonable, defensible summary of what the operators consciously intended, and yet the outcome is bad in dimensions the operators genuinely care about but never encoded in the objective. Constraints: a realistic setting (a hospital, a factory floor, or a customer-service pipeline — no paperclip maximizers, no trolleys), the operators are competent rather than merely careless, and the case must avoid the stock proxy-backfiring examples (no junk papers, no clickbait). After the scenario, walk through what each standard says and why, identify the single feature of the case that does the work, and explain why this shows that "alignment" is at least two distinct problems — making the optimizer faithful versus making the objective capture values — rather than one; close by saying what the case would have to look like if the failure were in the optimizer instead, and how someone who insists alignment is a single problem would try to dissolve the distinction.
