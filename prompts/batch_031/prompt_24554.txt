I've been turning over this thing called Aumann's agreement theorem in my head for a while, and I keep running into two camps that won't move an inch, so I'd like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict on who's right. As I understand the result, it goes roughly like this: if two people begin with the same prior beliefs, both update by the ordinary rules of probability, and it is common knowledge between them what each of them now believes after taking in their own evidence, then they must end up with the same final belief. They cannot rationally agree to disagree.

One camp treats this as a powerful and useful result. On that reading, persistent disagreement between two careful, rational people is a warning sign: if they keep disagreeing, then at least one of them is reasoning badly, or they are secretly starting from different prior assumptions, or one of them has evidence the other lacks. The theorem, on this view, is a diagnostic tool for locating where a real disagreement is actually coming from.

The other camp says the theorem is either a tautology or a fantasy. The assumptions are so strong — identical starting beliefs, perfect mutual common knowledge of each other's reasoning, and flawless probability reasoning on both sides — that no real pair of people ever satisfies them. So the result is either trivially true, just restating what the phrase "common knowledge of rationality" already bakes in, or it only applies to an idealized world that doesn't describe us, in which case it tells us nothing about why two actual experts keep disagreeing.

This has been going on for decades, and my working hypothesis is that it is mostly a verbal disagreement about what "rational" and "common knowledge" are supposed to mean, layered on top of a hidden split: one side is really asking a normative question (what should rational agents do?), while the other is asking a descriptive one (what do real agents actually do?), and the two sides are answering different questions under a single heading. I'm not sure that's right, though.

Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is — is it verbal, is it a substantive disagreement about whether the assumptions can ever be satisfied by real agents, or are the two sides answering different questions? Please ground the diagnosis in what each camp actually argues, not just in labels. And then, given that diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates the camps, a formal result that recovers the theorem under weaker assumptions, an empirical finding about how expert disagreement actually behaves, a reformulation of the question — or, if you think no progress should be expected, say so and explain why.

Two last requests. First, please say how confident you are in your overall diagnosis and single out the one point you are least sure about.
