I've been reading philosophy of religion on my own, and I keep running into a particular shape of argument that I can't quite pin down, and I'm wondering whether it has a name.

Here is the move, in my own words. An arguer wants to argue that God probably does not exist, and they do it like this. Suppose God is real, and suppose God cares about us the way the religious texts describe — wanting a genuine, personal relationship with each person. Then God would want each person to be able to recognize that God is there, because you cannot have a relationship with someone you cannot identify. But look at the actual state of the world. It is not merely that some people do not believe, or that the evidence is weak. It is that no one, anywhere, has clear, unambiguous, decisive evidence of God's existence. Every single person is left with the same ambiguous pile of hints, and reasonable, sincere people split apart over it. The arguer points at this total, uniform murkiness — the fact that there is not a single person who has a clear case, and that the murkiness is the same kind of murkiness for everyone — and concludes that either God does not exist, or God does not care the way the argument assumes.

What I keep noticing, and what I cannot find a word for, is that this argument is not the same as the claim that some people do not believe in God and therefore God probably does not exist (an argument from non-belief), and it is not the same as the claim that there is too much suffering in the world for a good God (the problem of evil). It is specifically about the total, uniform absence of clear evidence for anyone. The universal and uniform part — that it is not just that the evidence is weak, but that it is weak in the same way for everyone, with no exceptions — feels like the load-bearing part of the argument, and I cannot tell whether that is a distinct concept or just a way of stating the argument.

So: is there an established name or concept for this specific move — the claim that the total, uniform absence of clear evidence for anyone, as opposed to weak evidence for some, or suffering as evidence, is what rules out, or strains, a caring God? If there is, what is it, and how does it differ from the problem of evil, from the argument from non-belief, and from a plain argument from insufficient evidence? If there is not, can you name and define it precisely, and tell me exactly where its edges are — what kinds of evidence-distributions would count as an instance of it, and what would not?

Please think very carefully before answering, and respond in the style of an expert in philosophy of religion. Do not give me a reading list, a bibliography, or citations; I want the conceptual carving, not a syllabus.
