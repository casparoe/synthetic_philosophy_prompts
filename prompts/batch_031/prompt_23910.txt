Me and my friend Sam got into a huge argument last night about why economists keep building these models where everyone knows the core assumptions are bogus — like every agent is perfectly rational and forward-looking with full information, or markets are perfectly competitive with zero transaction costs. And the standard defense is basically the Friedman move: nobody should care whether the assumptions are realistic, only whether the model's predictions are accurate. I mostly buy that for predictions inside the range where the model has actually been tested. But here's where Sam and I split, and I cant shake it: economists then take those same models and use them to forecast totally novel stuff — a carbon tax that's never been tried, a new kind of stimulus — where the model has obviously not been tested. And I keep saying, wait, the whole "the assumptions don't matter, only the predictions do" defense only works because you already have the predictive track record in hand. The moment you extrapolate to a new situation, you don't have that track record anymore, so the defense just evaporates, and you're left with nothing but the assumption that the model's internal structure is actually capturing something real about how the world works. Which is a realist claim that flatly contradicts the "the assumptions are just a convenient fiction" claim they were making two seconds earlier. So I think the practice is secretly inconsistent — it's using anti-realism to justify the model at home and realism to justify the model abroad. Am I onto something, or is there a reconcilation I'm missing? Like, is there a version of the "assumptions don't matter" view that can actually handle extrapolation, or does extrapolation force you into realism no matter what? And if the practice does rest on this unstated realist commitment, what would actually have to change if we made that commitment explicit instead of hiding behind "the predictions work"?

Keep your answer under 450 words, and please don't just restate the Friedman argument or give me a history of the debate — I want the extrapolation tension itself worked out, with a clear verdict on whether the practice is coherent or whether it's quietly smuggling in realism.
