I'm taking a seminar in political theory this term, and one of my friends, who works on the boundary problem — the question of who belongs to the demos, that is, who is owed the standing of a co-ruler in a given political community — has been running a little exercise with us: she hands her a case and she rules on whether the person in question is a full member of the demos, a partial or suspended member, or an outright outsider. I've kept a list of her rulings, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the principle she is actually applying, because I suspect it is less simple than she assumes.

Here is her track record so far. She ruled that a naturalized citizen who has been a long-term resident and is active in local politics is a full member. She ruled that an adult who was born here to citizen parents but left for another country in early adulthood and has lived there ever since, without ever voting here, is not a member of the demos here at all, at most a former member in abeyance. She ruled that an adult foreign national with permanent-resident status, who has lived and worked here for fifteen years, pays taxes, and is fully subject to the criminal and civil law, but who is barred from voting in national elections, belongs to the demos and should have full standing, and that the bar is a mistake. She ruled that a foreign diplomat and the diplomat's household, resident here under diplomatic immunity, not subject to local criminal jurisdiction and exempt from local taxation, are not members of the demos. And she ruled that a resident of an adjacent foreign region whose livelihood and environment are substantially affected by this country's decisions, but who does not live here and is not subject to its law, is not a member of this demos, however heavily they are affected.

Now I have three new cases she has not yet ruled on, and I would like your help. First: a corporation registered and operating here, with substantial local operations, subject to the law, and paying taxes. Second: a member of a foreign military garrison stationed here, who is governed by their own service law rather than by local law, but whose family lives in the local community and whose children attend local schools. Third, as a stretch: a current large language model. For each, tell me what you think she will rule and why. More importantly, I am less interested in the bare answers than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her five verdicts just as well, what each of those rules predicts for my three new cases, and which single further case would best tell those rules apart.
