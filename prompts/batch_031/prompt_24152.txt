It was motivated by the pessimistic meta-induction: look at how many successful past theories — the caloric, the luminiferous ether, phlogiston — turned out to be false, and by induction our current theories are probably false too. Structural realism accepts that the entities were wrong but insists that the structure survived, so it's a way to be a realist without the bad inductive track record. The two main versions are epistemic structural realism, according to which we can only ever know the structure and never the underlying entities, and ontological structural realism, according to which only the structure really exists and the entities are just convenient fictions. Both versions agree that what is preserved through theory change is the mathematical formalism: when Newtonian mechanics gave way to relativity, for instance, the equations are what carried over, while the old story about absolute time and instantaneous action at a distance was discarded. For this reason structural realism is sometimes described as a last resort for the realist — it's what's left of realism once you've subtracted everything the pessimistic meta-induction takes away."
