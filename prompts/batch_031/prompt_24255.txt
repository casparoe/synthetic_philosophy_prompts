I'm making a handout for a philosophy of physics reading group that just got through the hole argument, and I want a decision tree that walks a student from the bare setup to a named position. Start from the commitment everyone at the table already accepts: GR is diffeomorphism-invariant, and if you take a solution and apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some region (the hole) but non-trivial inside it, you get a mathematically distinct solution on the same manifold. Build the tree from the questions first, then locate the actual positions in that space. Each branch node should be one of the argument's own assumptions or a claim that genuinely seperates the positions -- whether the two hole-related solutions count as one physical state or two, whether spacetime points have haecceity, whether the metric or other dynamical fields (not the bare manifold) are what's substantival, and whether the indeterminism the argument threatens is live. Each leaf should be a named position -- manifold substantivalist, relationist, dynamical substantivalist, structuralist, the Leibniz-equivalence-acceptor, the one who just bites the bullet on the indeterminism -- or mark it "unnamed" if nobody really defends it. Phrase every node as a question a student could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to, so the tree sorts people by what they believe, not by what they call themselves. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate, and note any combination of answers that lands on no leaf at all, with a sentence on why nobody sits there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). Keep the whole thing under 400 words.
