I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm trying to get my head around a debate that keeps popping up in my AI reporting — I sat on a small panel about it last month and we basically just talked past each other. The debate is whether we should attribute beliefs to large language models, and I don't mean the vague "is the machine conscious?" question; I mean the sharper one that comes out of Dennett's intentional stance (the one from his book The Intentional Stance). When an LLM behaves in ways that are systematicly predictable as if it held beliefs — it answers consistently with what it takes to be the case, it revises when the context changes, it can in some cases predict what another agent believes — does that mean it has beliefs, in the sense the intentional stance is supposed to carve out? I want you to write an essay that actually takes a side on this, not a balanced survey.

Here's the thing that keeps me up at night, and I think the essay has to wrestle with it head on: Dennett's intentional stance is supposed to be a general strategy that, in principle, can be applied to almost any system — a thermostat, a chess program, even (if you squint) something a lot simpler — and that's part of what makes it powerful. But if it really applies that broadly, then "has beliefs" starts to sound like it's not doing any real work, because everything ends up having beliefs in the relevant sense. So I want the essay to (a) figure out what criteria Dennett actually gives for when the intentional stance is a legitmate or productive way to describe a system, and whether LLMs clear that bar; (b) take the "too permissive" objection seriously — is it a genuine problem for Dennett, or a misreading of what the stance is for?; and (c) say whether the intentional stance is actually explaining LLM behavior or just re-describing it in mental vocabulary. And then commit: I want a real conclusion, defended, about whether LLMs have beliefs (or at least about whether the intentional stance is the right tool for settling that). A "reasonable people disagree" ending is a fail.
