okay so i've been wanting to run the phlogiston episode through Putnam's semantic externalism, and i think it's actually a better stress test than Twin Earth because the term in question probably never referred to anything at all. quick facts so we're on the same page: Becher and Stahl posited phlogiston as a fire-like principle that combustible bodies release when they burn; Priestley in the 1770s discovered what we now call oxygen but, working inside the phlogiston framework, called it dephlogisticated air (air stripped of phlogiston); Lavoisier then reinterpreted combustion as combination with oxygen, showed that metals actually gain weight when calcined instead of losing it, and dropped phlogiston entirely. now the questions i want you to work through: under a causal-historial theory of reference, what did phlogiston refer to at each of those three stages, and was there ever a causal chain in the first place that could fix the reference? does Priestley's dephlogisticated air count as the causal chain getting redirected from a nonexistent substance over to oxygen, or does externalism just say the term failed to refer from the start and Priestley was basically talking past everyone? how does the descriptivist alternative do here — does phlogiston pick out whatever satisfies the description "the principle released in combustion," and if so what happens to the extention once Lavoisier shows combustion is combination with oxygen? and lastly, what does the episode show about externalism itself: is it a confirmation, an anomaly, or a case the theory never anticipated, and is there any principled way to handle theoretical terms for nonexistent substances without just stipulating that they fail to refer? i'd rather you say where the theory is silent than force it to give an answer it can't actually support.
