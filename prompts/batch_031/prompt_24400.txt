Construct a thought experiment in which Lewis's counterpart theory and a Kripkean transworld-identity account give different verdicts on the same de re modal claim, where the divergence is not the familiar "I could have been a senator" point but instead turns on the fact that the counterpart relation is not transitive while identity is. Constraints: a single concrete scenario built around one person in the actual world and exactly two other possible worlds, no exotic physics, no longer than a paragraph for the scenario itself, and the case must generate the two verdicts from each theory's own machinery rather than stipulate them. After the scenario, work out what each theory says about the claim and why, identify which feature of the case is doing the work, say what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed, and anticipate how a defender of the theory that comes out worse would try to dissolve the case.
