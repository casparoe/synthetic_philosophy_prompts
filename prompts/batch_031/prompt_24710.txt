I'm working through the foundations of imprecise probability, and I keep arriving at the conclusion that it is nothing but a repackaging of ordinary hierarchical Bayesian modeling. I have a nagging feeling that this is wrong, so I'd like the reasoning pressure-tested rather than simply endorsed. Here is the picture as I see it. In a hierarchical Bayesian model, an analyst is uncertain about the true probability p of an event E: not knowing, say, whether a coin is fair, she places a prior distribution over p instead of committing to a single value. In an imprecise-probability model, she instead assigns E a lower and an upper probability, for instance 0.4 and 0.7. These strike me as the same move. In both cases the analyst has a distribution over candidate probabilities for E; the imprecise model is simply the case in which she retains only the range of that distribution — its minimum and maximum — and discards the rest. On that reading an imprecise-probability model is a degenerate hierarchical model, and the two frameworks should agree about E and about every decision that depends on E.

Is that identification actually correct, or is there a substantive difference between a second-order probability (a precise probability distribution over probabilities) and a first-order imprecise probability (an interval-valued credence)? If the two do come apart, please state the difference in a non-verbal way — ideally by naming some event, some claim about E, or some piece of evidence on which the two frameworks give different answers.
