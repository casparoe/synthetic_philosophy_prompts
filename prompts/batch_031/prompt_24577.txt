I retired from fieldwork (I did ethology on crows, mostly) about four years ago and I've fallen down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole, which is embarassing for a person who spent forty years just counting behaviors. I've been reading about the distribution of consciousness in animals and I've come to accept what I think is the sober, scientific view: phenomenal consciousness, the actual felt experience, requires some kind of global workspace or recurrent procesing architecture, the integrated, broadcast-ready system that mammals and birds seem to have. Most animals, especially invertebrates and probably most fish, don't have that architecture, so they're not consciously experiencing anything. They may have what people call access consciousness, they can process information and react, but there's no one home, no real experience. I'm not a zombie theorist about humans, just about most of the rest of the animal kingdom.

What would count against the view? I want you to rank the counter-evidence from most decisive to least, and I want an honest assessment of whether the view has been watered down to unfalsifiability, because I've been worried about that and I'm not sure whether that's a fatal flaw or just a feature of any view about other minds. Also tell me what a committed defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against it, as opposed to what critics just throw at it.
