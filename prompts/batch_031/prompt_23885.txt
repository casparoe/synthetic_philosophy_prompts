I'm writing a paper for my philosophy class on Benjamin Franklin's project of moral perfection, and I want to explore how he would have responded to a modern habit-tracking app. You may know the basics of his method: he compiled a chart of thirteen virtues, from temperance to humility, concentrated on one virtue at a time each week, and recorded each slip with a dot in a small notebook. Now imagine an app that performs the same basic function but adds three features: push notifications that remind you of the virtue of the week and log your slips in real time, streak counters and progress graphs, and a public leaderboard where you can compare your record against other users' records. In your judgment, would Franklin have adopted such an app, and if so, how would he have modified it to fit his one-virtue-at-a-time rotation?

In your answer, please take a definite position rather than merely listing possibilities, and ground it in what is known about his method and his character. I am especially interested in how you would resolve two specific tensions. First, would constant notifications and real-time logging have actually addressed his stated failure mode — his admission that while he was guarding against one fault, he was often surprised by another — or would the steady stream of reminders have disturbed him in a way that works against his virtue of tranquillity? Second, would the public leaderboard have undermined the virtue of humility, which he added last, after a friend pointed out that he was growing proud of his moral progress?
