I'm on the technical commitee for a formal description language we use to specify smart contracts and audit rules, and we're in a bit of a bind. A few months ago someone filed a spec with a clause that basically said "this clause applies to every clause that does not apply to itself" — it was meant to be some clever self-referential audit rule, but when the evaluation engine tried to process it, it just spun until it got killed, and two other specs that referenced it started misbehaving in really weird ways. Now the committee is split. Half of us want to just ban self-referential clauses entirely, which I get, but that kills a lot of legitimate use cases (recursive definitions, "all rules except this one," and so on), and our users will just move to a competitor language that still allows it. The other half wants to keep self-reference allowed but bolt on some kind of type or level system so a clause can only refer to clauses at a different level, which does fix the problem but makes writing specs a lot more annoying and quietly changes the meaning of a bunch of existing ones.

Can you draft the committee's decision memo for next week's vote? I want a clear reccomendation — ban it, add the level system, or some third option you can think of — with reasons the other side could actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument for whichever option we end up not picking. One more wrinkle: roughly a third of the specs already in the wild are self-referential and working fine right now, so whatever we decide has to say something about what happens to those. Keep it to a page or two, something I can actually put in front of the group.
