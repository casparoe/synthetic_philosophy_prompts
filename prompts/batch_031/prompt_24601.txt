I keep running into a puzzle about Aumann's agreement theorem. The result says that two rational agents who share a common prior and have common knowledge of each other's beliefs must end up with the same belief. But my colleague and I plainly don't share a common prior: we were trained in different intellectual traditions, we've read different authors, and we assign different probabilities to almost everything we both care about. So doesn't the failure of the common prior condition just render the theorem irrelevant to our disagreements? It seems to bite only on people who already largely agree, so how could it place any real constraint on genuine expert disagreement?
