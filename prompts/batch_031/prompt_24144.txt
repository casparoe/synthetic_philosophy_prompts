I have been working through an argument about whether market prices can be informative, and I cannot find the flaw. The conclusion is one I find absurd, so I am confident the error is somewhere in the chain; I have set the argument out as follows.

(1) A price is informative only insofar as it reflects the private information held by traders; a price that incorporates none of their private information conveys nothing they do not already know.

(2) A trader will trade on her private information only when doing so is profitable, and it is profitable only when the prevailing price does not yet fully reflect that information.

(3) Hence, if the price already fully reflected all the private information in the market, no trader would profitably trade on private information, and no such trade would take place: the market would be in a no-trade equilibrium.

(4) But if no trade on private information takes place, then no private information is incorporated into the price.

(5) Therefore, if the price fully reflected all private information, no private information would in fact be reflected in it.

(6) That is a contradiction, so the price cannot fully reflect all the private information in the market.

(7) By (1), the only source of a price's informativeness is reflected private information. Since the price cannot fully reflect that information (6), it reflects none of it, and so the price is not informative at all; the market, on this view, is a useless information-processing device.

I cannot accept (7). I have watched prices move sharply on news that traders clearly knew in advance, so I take it as settled that prices carry at least some information, and I would much rather keep that than concede the market is useless. Nor am I willing to simply deny (6) and move on, though I grant I am not certain (6) is correct. I cannot tell whether the error lies in the no-trade step or in the final leap to the "useless device" conclusion, and I would not want to patch the argument by quietly weakening a premise I should actually be defending. Which step do you reject, why did it plausibly look correct, and what do I give up by rejecting it?
