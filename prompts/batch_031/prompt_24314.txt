Two colleagues of mine — one a committed Humean and one a committed anti-Humean about the laws of nature — have been arguing past each other for a year. Write the document their good-faith adversarial collaboration should produce. I want: the claims both can sign, including about the formal role that laws play in our best theories, about what any satisfactory account of laws must explain or ground, and about which candidate accounts each side concedes are inadequate; the precise statements of where they still differ, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, give the case, argument, or thought experiment that both would agree bears on it, plus each side's verdict written down in advance. I expect the cruxes to include at least: whether laws are a distinctive kind of truth or a limit case of ordinary generalization; whether the Humean best-system account can ground the counterfactuals it is supposed to support without a circularity; whether the fundamental base must be categorical and qualitative or may contain genuinely dispositional properties; and whether the account can accommodate the non-universal, ceteris paribus, and probabilistic laws of the special sciences. If you conclude that part of the dispute is verbal — that both sides are describing the same formal role in different metaphysical clothing — say exactly which part and why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Begin your response with the statement itself, with no preface, no restating of the question, and no commentary.
