I'm co-organizing a small workshop on the philosophy of mind and AI, and two of the regulars have been arguing past each other for about a year, mostly at the whiteboard. One is a committed functionalist who is convinced that current large language models genuinely have beliefs; the other is a committed instrumentalist who holds that Dennett's intentional stance is purely a predictive and descriptive tool and that no LLM has any beliefs at all. I'd like you to draft the joint statement they should have produced from a good-faith adversarial collaboration — a constructive text both could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than just re-stated.

The document should have four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about the intentional stance itself and about what the resulting attributions actually do for us. Second, a table of the precise points on which they still disagree, with each side's position phrased in wording that its own side would accept. Third, the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; I suspect one crux is whether the intentional stance is a theoretical commitment or a purely predictive tool, and another is whether the causal and functional organization of a transformer is of the right kind to ground beliefs at all. Fourth, for each crux, a concrete test, argument, or case that both sides would agree bears on it, together with what each side predicts the outcome will show; please make use of phenomena like sycophancy and the way a model's apparent beliefs shift when you probe it or rephrase the question. If you conclude that part of their dispute is merely verbal, say exactly which part and why. Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section first, then the disagreements table, then the cruxes with their tests and predictions.
