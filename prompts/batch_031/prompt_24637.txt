I'm a philosophy of physics PhD student, and I'd like to practice defending relationism against a committed substantivalist. Please play that substantivalist: someone who genuinely holds that spacetime itself — the manifold together with its geometric structure — is part of the furniture of the world, not merely a way of recording relations among material bodies. Use the resources a substantivalist actually has; hold the core claim that spacetime has mind-independent reality, but you may concede small points and must not concede that spacetime is mere bookkeeping. Stay in character across turns, and if you need a fact about general relativity that I haven't stipulated, ask me rather than assert it. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two. Here is my opening move: you say the points of the manifold are real, but consider the hole argument. General relativity admits pairs of solutions that agree everywhere outside an open, matter-free region — which I'll call the hole — and differ only in how the metric is assigned to the points inside it. The two solutions agree on the entire matter field and on every observable fact; they differ only in which point is which. If your points are individuated independently of the fields on them, the pair describes two distinct but empirically indistinguishable situations — gratuitous surplus structure. If, to avoid that, you identify the points with the field configuration so that the pair is really one situation, you've made exactly the relationist's move. So which is it — haecceitism about points, or a field-constituted ontology that is relationism in disguise — and how do you preserve substantivalism in either case?
