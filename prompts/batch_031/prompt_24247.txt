I'm giving a talk at a longtermist ethics workshop defending the view that the simulation argument is a genuine reason to dampen the weight we assign to far-future outcomes. My target is the fanaticism worry: the concern that longtermism requires assigning unbounded or near-infinite weight to some far-future state, so that a single far-future outcome could outweigh everything in the near future. My argument is that if it's likely we're in a simulation, the far future is more likely to be computationally bounded, truncated, or dominated by simulated minds, so both the expected number of future minds and the expected range of far-future outcomes are lower than baseline longtermism assumes — a substantive reason to discount the far future and resist the fanatical weighting. The audience is mostly longtermist researchers committed to the far future mattering enormously, plus a couple of decision theorists skeptical of longtermism's epistemic foundations.
