In the philosophy of general relativity, a common pedagogical move when introducing the hole argument is to present the manifold as a bare set of "slots" or "labels," with the metric and matter fields as the only real content. The analogy is usually made concrete by comparing the manifold to a spreadsheet: the cells are the spacetime points, the data entered into the cells are the values of the fields, and a diffeomorphism is treated as a permutation of the cell labels. Since all that is physically real is the data, not the cells, permuting the labels is said to change nothing physical, which is why diffeomorphic solutions count as the same physical solution. The hole argument is then set up by taking a solution and, inside a region (the "hole"), permuting the labels in a way that is the identity outside the region, to produce a second solution that is data-identical outside the hole but data-different inside. A substantivalist about the manifold — one who holds that the manifold points exist and have an identity independent of the fields — is then pressed to say that these two solutions are genuinely distinct physical possibilities that are nevertheless observationally indistinguishable, an apparently absurd plurality. The analogy is doing the work of making that plurality look absurd, and thereby licensing the conclusion that the manifold points are not real in that robust sense, or at most exist only in the weaker sense of being individuated by their relations to the fields.

I'd like you to audit this analogy. First, give the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the spreadsheet, the cells, the data, the permutation, and the "hole." Second, identify where the mapping breaks down, and in particular say whether the spreadsheet's cells have an identity that is independent of and prior to the data in a way that the manifold's points do or do not, and whether that matters for the conclusion the analogy is carrying. Third, say whether the "permutation" in the spreadsheet case is the right kind of operation to model a diffeomorphism — a smooth, invertible, structure-preserving map from the manifold to itself — as opposed to an arbitrary relabeling by an external agent, and what the difference amounts to. Fourth, name the features of the spreadsheet source that the analogy quietly imports that the target lacks or has in a different form, for instance whether the spreadsheet's fixed grid is a background structure that the manifold does not possess. Fifth, tell me whether the conclusion the analogy is carrying — that the manifold points are not real in the robust sense, or at most individuated by the fields — survives once these disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing all the argumentative work. And finally, if there is a better analogy for the diffeomorphism-invariance and hole-argument point, sketch it and say what it gets right that the spreadsheet gets wrong.
