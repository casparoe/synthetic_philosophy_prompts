I'm writing up my views on superrationality for a decision-theory seminar and I'd like a careful consistency check before I present them. Here is where I've landed:

(1) In any two-player game that is symmetric — the players have the same information, the same strategy sets, and payoffs that coincide up to a relabeling of the players — and in which each player knows that the other is superrational, the correct move for each is the one that maximizes the sum of the two payoffs. I take this joint-optimization move to be the core of superrationality: the two players reason, in effect, as a single agent controlling two bodies.

(2) As a direct consequence, two superrational players facing a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma both cooperate, even though defection is each player's dominant strategy.

(3) Superrationality applies only in symmetric games. In an asymmetric game — where the players have genuinely different information, or payoffs that do not coincide up to relabeling — there is no superrational solution, and each player should fall back to ordinary Nash-style reasoning about what the other will do.

(4) A superrational player should defect against an opponent it has good reason to believe will cooperate no matter what the superrational player does — a predictable cooperator. I do not think superrationality obliges you to be a sucker to agents who are not playing the same game.

(5) A superrational player should refuse to pay in the following setup: before you take any action, a predictor has flipped a fair coin and told you that if it landed heads it will give you a large sum if and only if you pay it a smaller sum, while if it landed tails you get nothing whatever you do. You do not know the outcome, and nothing you do now can change it; the rational move is therefore to refuse.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be — which single commitment I should give up or weaken, and what I give up in doing so. I do not want every possible worry listed as if they were equally bad; I want you to discriminate among them. And please be explicit about any pair that I might be tempted to think is in tension but that is not, explaining why it comes apart on inspection.
