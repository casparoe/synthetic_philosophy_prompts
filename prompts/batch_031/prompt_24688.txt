I sit on the animal welfare board of a regional rescue that takes in everything from dogs to parrots to the occasional reptile, and next week we vote on our surge-capacity policy for mass-intake events like last year's wildfire evacuations. The draft says that when ICU beds run out, patients get ranked first by severity of injury and then, as a tiebreaker, by "estimated brain size as a proxy for cognitive complexity." The commitee is split — half of us think the tiebreaker is crude but defensible, the other half thinks it's morally incoherent — and people keep failing to seperate at least three different questions in the argument: whether a mouse's pain matters less than a dog's, what a parrot actually needs for its life to go well, and whether brain size is a good measure of anything relevant in the first place. Please draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday's vote: it should recommend adopting, rejecting, or revising the criterion (with a concrete replacement if you're revising) and a timeline, and it should engage the strongest case for the side you don't end up on rather than just dismissing it. Formatting: give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion. The five points should cover the decision, the main reason, the strongest case on the losing side and why it loses, what's settled among people who actually work on this versus what's genuinely contested (don't present the contested parts as consensus), and what would change the recommendation.
