A colleague of mine has been quite clear about what counts as supererogatory. Her verdicts so far: paying your income taxes is obligatory, not supererogatory; keeping a promise you made to a friend is obligatory, not supererogatory; donating one of your kidneys to a stranger is supererogatory; giving a substantial share of your income to charity is supererogatory; dropping twenty dollars in a charity box is not supererogatory, just a small unremarkable nicety; and refusing to donate a kidney is not blameworthy. The new case is this: you're walking past a shallow, clear pond and see a toddler drowning in it; you could save the toddler by stepping in, but you'd have to take off your shoes and you'd get your trousers soaked, and there's no real danger to you. Do you think she'll call the rescue supererogatory? I'm less interested in a bare answer than in the work behind it: the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, what each of those rules predicts for the pond case, your best guess and the reasons for it, and the further case that would best tell the rules apart.
