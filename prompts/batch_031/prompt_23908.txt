I am closing my graduate seminar on the philosophy of space and time, and I want the final session to end with a poem rather than a paper. Please write a sonnet on Newton's bucket in which the octave builds the substantivalist picture — the concave water surface as evidence of rotation relative to space itself, against the relationalist who would hold that rotation is only relative to other bodies — and the volta is the moment Mach's objection arrives: if the concavity is explained by rotation relative to the distant fixed stars, the experiment has not decided the dispute. The closing couplet should say exactly what the bucket does establish — that rotation is not relative to the immediately surrounding objects, and that the experiment is silent on absolute velocity — and what it cannot establish, the ontological status of space. It should be accurate enough that a philosopher of physics would not wince.
