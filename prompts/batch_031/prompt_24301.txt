I'm working on a novel set during a philosophy of religion conference, and two of my characters, a Molinist and an Ockhamist, argue over coffee about middle knowledge. I need the dialogue to be philosophically real, not just slogans. The knot I want them stuck in: if God's middle knowledge already settles what a free creature would do in a given set of circumstances, and God then creates the creature in exactly those circumstances, how is the choice still free? The Ockhamist keeps saying this is determinism by another name — foreknowledge locks in the outcome, so the creature couldn't have done otherwise. The Molinist keeps answering that the choice is free because the creature could have done otherwise, and God's knowledge just tracks rather than causes it. I want a few genine rounds where each actually responds to the other's last point and pushes back, both sharp, neither a strawman. Can you write that dialogue?
