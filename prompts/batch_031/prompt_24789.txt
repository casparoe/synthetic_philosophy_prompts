(1) The private language argument establishes that a language whose correctness conditions are purely private — a language in which no one, including the speaker, can in principle distinguish between using a word correctly and merely seeming to use it correctly — is impossible. (2) The language you use for inner speech, the words you silently run through when you think, is exactly such a language: whether you are using a word correctly in your own mind is something only you can judge, and no one else can check you. (6) Therefore the private language argument is false, or at least it proves too much, since it annihilates thought.
