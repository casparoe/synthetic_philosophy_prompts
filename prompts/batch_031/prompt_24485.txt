I chair the investment committee of a mid-size venture fund, and we are close to committing $40M to a Series B in a medical-device startup. The founders have given us a 12-day exclusivity window, and our outside counsel has told us that the single biggest legal risk in the deal is whether the startup's core patent portfolio is actually valid and enforceable. A specialist IP audit firm can give us a defensible opinion in about 9 days for a fee of $1.5M, which would land us only two days before the exclusivity expires. Two of my committee members are split, and I'd like a decision memo before Thursday's session. One of them argues that, given the standard decision-theoretic result that the rational value of information is never negative (Good's theorem), the audit can at worst be a wash and at best save us from a catastrophic bad deal, so the only real question is whether its expected value clears $1.5M, and he is inclined to say yes on the numbers. The other argues that this is not a fixed decision problem: commissioning the audit signals our diligence posture to the founders, may trigger a renegotiation or a walk-away, and could in effect destroy deal value even if the audit itself is informative, so the naive value-of-information calculation is misleading here. I was discussing this with a partner last week and he kept pushing the point that the audit also changes what the founders believe we know, which I think is the crux, but I haven't been able to formalize it.

Please draft the committee's written decision memo — fund the audit, decline it, or fund it with specific conditions — with a concrete recommendation and a time frame that fits the 12-day window, and with an honest, serious treatment of the strongest version of whichever position loses. Respond in the style of a careful decision theorist who also understands venture-deal dynamics, and think very carefully before answering. Do not include any bibliography, reading list, or citations; reason entirely from the structure of the decision problem as I have described it.
