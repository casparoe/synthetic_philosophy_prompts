Retired now after 32 years in agricultural entomology, and I've only recently gotten into the philosophy of all this — a bit on whether insects have any moral standing at all — and it's made me uneasy about things I did my whole career. So I'm trying to figure out what I actually think, going by my gut on cases. Spraying a pyrethroid over a corn field to save it from European corn borer: no problem, that's just the job. Boric acid bait for carpenter ants in my own house: no problem. DDT for malaria: obviously fine, people are dying. Releasing sterile Mediterranean fruit flies to suppress an invasion: fine, actually my favorite tool, I'd rather do that than spray. Glyphosate on a soybean field in June where monarchs might be arround: that one sits weird with me, even though I think the soybean is worth more than the monarchs. Insecticidal soap on aphids in my own tomato patch: fine. Mosquito spraying in a suburban park on a warm evening: feels wrong, but if West Nile was in the neighborhood I'd probably be fine with it. Now here's the pair I can't reconcile. I had no problem overseeing a spray that killed tens of millions of insects in a field, but I get a pang when I squash a single ladybug on my tomato plant, and I'm not sure the pang is just sentiment. And another one: I cant articulate why the persistence of DDT bothers me less in the malaria case than the persistence of a neonicotinoid in a field — the chemistry argument is the same, only the thing being protected is different. Is there a principle — maybe something about the magnitude of the harm prevented, or about ecological role rather than individual suffering — that gets most of these, and what does it predict for cases I haven't listed, like a gene drive to wipe out a malaria mosquito species, or a homeowner killing fire ants in their yard? And be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save.
