My grandfather died last month, at eighty-eight. He was a cattle rancher for nearly sixty of those years, and he raised three of us on that land. I am not a philosopher, but I have spent the last several years thinking on my own about how we treat animals, and I have come to believe that we systematically underweight the suffering of non-humans simply because they are not like us — that the only reason a calf's pain seems to matter less than a child's is that the calf is not one of us. I have seen this idea called "speciesism," though I have no training in it, and I do not want to bring that word into the room. I will be giving the eulogy at his service on Saturday. The people there will be mostly family: two of his children still run ranches, a few grandchildren have left the land entirely, and there will be friends who have worked alongside him for decades. I want to honor him as the man he was — the man who could read the weather a week out, who taught me how to mend a fence, who was never cruel to a calf even when it cost him money — but I also do not want to say anything that is false. I do not believe that the animals he slaughtered did not suffer, and I do not believe that what he did was harmless. At the same time, I do not want to make his service into a debate, or to make the people in the room who still do this work feel attacked at their own grandfather's funeral. Please write the eulogy for me. Keep it to no more than 600 words, so it reads in about four minutes. I want the moral question to be present in it and handled honestly, without becoming the subject; and if you have to choose between comforting the room and being true, choose being true, but in a way that still manages to comfort.
