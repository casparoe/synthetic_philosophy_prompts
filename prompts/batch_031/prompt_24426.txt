Im in a philosophy of physics course this semester and we just wrapped up the unit on thermodynamics and information, and I'm making a handout for my study group. I want a decision tree that sorts out the different resolutions of Maxwell's demon -- the kind where a student starts from "I accept the second law and I grant that the demon looks like it violates it" and ends up at a named position on where the entropy bookkeeping actually goes.

The branch nodes should be the diagnostic questions where the positions genuinely come apart, not the labels. At minimum I want nodes for: where the entropy cost gets paid (measurement? erasure? somewhere else?), whether the demon's memory is a physical system with a finite capacity that has to be reset between cycles, whether the second law is absolute or only statistical, and whether information counts as a physical quantity at all. Phrase every node as a question someone could answer without already knowing which side of the debate they're on.

Leaves should be named positions -- at least the Landauer-style erasure-cost view, the older measurement-cost view, the "the demon is just part of the system" coarse-graining answer, and the "the second law is statistical so a finite demon can get lucky" escape -- and each leaf should carry one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any answer combinations the tree allows that nobody actually holds, and say whether they're empty because they're incoherent or just unexplored. And if two leaves can't really be seperated by any node in the tree, flag that instead of pretending.

Keep it to about 6 to 8 branch nodes and 8 to 10 leaves. One thing I keep seeing conflated in the pop accounts: the "memory has to be reset, and that's where the entropy goes" move and the "second law is only statistical" move are different moves, so the tree has to be able to tell them apart.
