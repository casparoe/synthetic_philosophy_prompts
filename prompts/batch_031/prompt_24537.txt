Two graduate students in a philosophy of probability seminar got into a disagreement about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I was asked to referee. Here is the exchange as I recall it:

Mara: "The principle of indifference should be applied to the three centered possibilities: (Heads, Monday), (Tails, Monday), and (Tails, Tuesday). From Beauty's perspective at an awakening, she has no way of distinguishing between these three possibilities. Each is equally compatible with her evidence. So by the principle of indifference, each has probability 1/3, and P(heads | awakened) = 1/3."

Dev: "The principle of indifference should be applied to the two worlds: Heads and Tails. The coin is fair, so P(heads) = P(tails) = 1/2. The fact that Beauty is awakened on both days if tails doesn't change the probability of the coin outcome. The centered possibilities are not equally likely because (Heads, Monday) is twice as likely as (Tails, Monday) or (Tails, Tuesday). So P(Heads, Monday) = 1/2 and P(Tails, Monday) = P(Tails, Tuesday) = 1/4, giving P(heads | awakened) = 1/2."

Mara: "But the principle of indifference is supposed to apply when the possibilities are symmetric from the agent's perspective. The three centered possibilities are symmetric from Beauty's perspective: she has no way of distinguishing between them. The fact that the heads world has one awakening and the tails world has two is a feature of the experimental setup, not a feature of Beauty's evidence. From her perspective, all three centered possibilities are equally likely."

Dev: "The symmetry that matters for the principle of indifference is not symmetry from the agent's perspective, but symmetry in the underlying probability structure. The coin is fair, so the two worlds are symmetric. The three centered possibilities are not symmetric because (Heads, Monday) is in a world that is as likely as the tails world, but the tails world has two awakenings. So (Heads, Monday) is twice as likely as (Tails, Monday) or (Tails, Tuesday)."

Mara: "But that's just restating the conclusion. You're saying (Heads, Monday) is twice as likely because the heads world is as likely as the tails world but has fewer awakenings. But that's circular: you're using the halfer answer to justify the weighting of the centered possibilities. The principle of indifference is supposed to be a way of assigning probabilities when there's no other information, and from Beauty's perspective, there's no information that distinguishes the three centered possibilities."

Dev: "It's not circular. The coin is fair, and that's a fact about the experimental setup, not about Beauty's evidence. The principle of indifference applies to the coin flip, giving P(heads) = P(tails) = 1/2. The number of awakenings is a separate fact about the experimental setup. These two facts together determine the probabilities of the centered possibilities: P(Heads, Monday) = 1/2, P(Tails, Monday) = 1/4, P(Tails, Tuesday) = 1/4. The principle of indifference doesn't apply to the centered possibilities because they're not symmetric in the underlying probability structure."

Mara: "But the principle of indifference is supposed to be applied from the agent's perspective, not from a God's-eye view. From Beauty's perspective at an awakening, she has no way of knowing whether the coin landed heads or tails, and no way of knowing what day it is. The three centered possibilities are equally compatible with her evidence. The principle of indifference says that when possibilities are equally compatible with the evidence, they should be assigned equal probability. So P(Heads, Monday) = P(Tails, Monday) = P(Tails, Tuesday) = 1/3."

Dev: "The principle of indifference is not supposed to be applied to possibilities that are not symmetric in the underlying probability structure. It's supposed to be applied when there's no reason to favor one possibility over another. But there is a reason to favor (Heads, Monday) over (Tails, Monday) and (Tails, Tuesday): the coin is fair, and the heads world is as likely as the tails world. The number of awakenings is a feature of the experimental setup that affects the probability of the centered possibilities, not a feature of Beauty's evidence."
