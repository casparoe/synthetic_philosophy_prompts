I've been going back and forth on moral relativism and I want to figure out what I actually think by looking at my gut on specific cases. Here's where I land:

Female genital cutting, even in a culture where it's universal and many of the women who've had it done say they wouldn't change it: I think it's wrong, full stop. Not "wrong by my culture's standards" — just wrong.

The Roman slave economy: wrong. I don't think "they had different values" is a live defense.

Foot binding in historical China: wrong. I feel bad for the women who had it done, but wrong.

But: a culture where it's normal to be very direct and blunt in conversation, to the point where I find it rude: I don't think that's morally wrong. It's just different.

A culture where men and women have very different social roles, and people within the culture are content with that arrangement: I'm genuinly unsure. I don't have the same certainty I have about FGC.

A culture that considers eating horse meat perfectly normal: not a moral issue at all, as far as I can tell.

Now here's where I get stuck. I want to say FGC is wrong "period," trans-culturally, but I'm not as sure about a patriarchal society where people seem content. And I want to say moral progress is real — that abolishing slavery wasn't just a shift in values but a genuine correction. But if moral progress is real, moral truths aren't relative to a culture, and then I don't need to carve out FGC as special — I can just say any practice that violates basic rights is wrong, full stop, and I should be equally confident about the patriarchal society.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — the patriarchal society where people are content? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? I don't want a survey of options. I want you to pick the principle you think is right, tell me which of my verdicts it can't save, and say why.
