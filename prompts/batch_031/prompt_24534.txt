A bioethicist colleague has classified a series of interventions as either therapy or enhancement, in her research and in teaching, as follows:

- Gene therapy for a child with symptomatic Tay-Sachs disease: therapy.
- An antidepressant for a patient with major depressive disorder: therapy.
- Recombinant growth hormone for a healthy child who is short but whose height falls within the normal range: enhancement.
- Modafinil for a healthy college student with no sleep disorder: enhancement.
- Laser refractive surgery (LASIK) for moderate myopia: therapy.
- A cochlear implant for a child born deaf: therapy.

She has now been asked to classify a new case: a safe, effective, and inexpensive somatic gene edit that removes the Huntington's disease allele from a healthy, asymptomatic adult who is a known carrier (one affected parent, no symptoms, no current functional impairment), performed solely to prevent the disease the person would otherwise develop.

I would like your help working out what she should say. Think very carefully before answering, and respond in the style of an expert in bioethics and the philosophy of medicine. First, identify the distinct underlying rules or principles that her six verdicts are jointly consistent with. Then state what each such rule predicts for the new case. Next, give your best guess as to which rule she is in fact running and what verdict that yields, with your reasons. Finally, name a single further case that would best discriminate among the candidate rules.
