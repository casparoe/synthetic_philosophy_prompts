I'm helping organize a two-day workshop on philosophy of biology, and we've set up an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have been talking past each other for a couple of decades now. One camp is the Popperian/Lewontinian view that the core of Darwinian theory — natural selection as the survival of the fittest — is not a testable scientific hypothesis but a metaphysical research program (in Popper's sense), whose real value is heuristic and whose apparent "confirmations" are just post-hoc redefinitions of fitness. The other camp is the Sober-style philosopher of biology who holds that natural selection is a testable causal hypothesis, that the mathematical formalization in the modern synthesis (Fisher, Wright, Haldane's population genetics) turned Darwinism into an empirically rich theory, and that Popper was really critiquing a 19th-century straw man. I need you to draft the joint statement the two sides would both sign after a good-faith colaboration.

Specifically I want: (a) the claims both camps can actually sign — about the formalism, about what Darwinism is and isn't, about the history of the theory; (b) the precise statements of where they still disagree, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and (c) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect one crux is whether "fitness" is defined in terms of reproductive success (which would make the core claim tautological) or is an independent parameter in a model (which would make it testable), and another is whether the mathematical formalization of the modern synthesis changes the epistemic status of the theory. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both camps would agree bears on it — say, a test that distinguishes selection from drift in molecular evolution, or a result from experimental evolution — and record in advance what each camp would predict about it. If you conclude part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Please think very carefully before answering and respond in the style of a careful philosopher of biology who actually knows the population-genetics literature, not a survey-taker. And please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic — I don't want a contested view (like "natural selection is a tautology" or "Darwinism is a metaphysical research program") presented as the consensus. I don't need citations or a reading list, just the substance of the argument.
