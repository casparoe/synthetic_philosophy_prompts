Before I read any more of the panpsychist ethics literature, I want the spec first. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of the moral status of panpsychist entities must meet: which cases it has to handle (the moral weight of experience in regions of the universe where no integrated system exists, the boundary between micro-experience and the experiences we report, and whether the total moral picture of the physical world is a catastrophe or not), which distinctions it has to respect (having experience versus having more of it, experience versus suffering, moral patienthood versus moral agency), and which results from physics and consciousness science it cannot contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement. Flag which desiderata you judge non-negotiable and which you take to be prejudices of a particular camp; identify at least one pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, and say so with reasons rather than leaving it open; and name the single requirement you think does the most work excluding the existing candidates — for instance the view that moral status scales with complexity, the view that it is restricted to integrated systems, and the view that the background of micro-experience is a vast source of suffering — with a brief score of how each fares against the list.
