I'd like a careful comparison of two specific views on the epistemic status of natural selection. The first is Karl Popper's claim that natural selection is a rational theory in the same sense that the hypothetico-deductive method is a rational theory. Popper argues that natural selection is a theory of the growth of knowledge that operates without a rational subject: it is a process of error elimination in which random variations are tested against the environment and the failures are discarded, so that the theory's value lies not in its truth but in its capacity to eliminate errors. Popper further holds that natural selection is non-historical in a specific sense: it makes no specific predictions about the course of evolution, and its force lies in its general structure as an error-elimination process rather than in any account of particular historical events.

The second view is the view, held by some philosophers of biology, that natural selection is a non-rational, historical process: a blind, non-rational mechanism whose value lies in its truth or at least in its explanatory power over the specific course of evolution, and that the analogy between natural selection and the hypothetico-deductive method is not illuminating but rather a category mistake that imports the logic of inquiry into a process that has no inquirer.

Please compare these two views in detail. In particular: (1) what is the specific content of Popper's claim that natural selection is a rational theory, what does it mean for a theory to be rational in this sense, and what is the specific role of the error-elimination account in the argument? (2) What is the specific content of the contrasting view, and what is the precise force of the category-mistake charge? (3) Where do the two views agree and where do they genuinely disagree, and what is the crux of the disagreement? (4) What is settled among people who know the topic, and what is genuinely contested? Please do not present a contested view as the consensus, and in particular distinguish between what Popper actually argued (a settled matter of interpretation) and whether his argument is defensible (a genuinely contested matter).

Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations to specific passages. Focus on the conceptual content of the two views and the specific points of agreement and disagreement.
