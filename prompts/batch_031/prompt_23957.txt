I've come to hold the view that perception is disjunctive in the strong sense: when I veridically see a tomato, my perceptual state is of a categorically different kind from when I veridically hallucinate a tomato, and the difference lies not in any intrinsic, subjective feature of the experience but in the fact that the former is a factive state while the latter is a mere experience. I'm aware of the standard argument from illusion, but I want a map of what would count against the view more broadly. I want the arguments and considerations that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, with particular attention to the tension between the disjunctivist's claim that the "seeing" state is epistemically privileged and the bad-lighting case, where the subject is genuinely seeing yet not in a safe position to know. Which of these would a committed strong disjunctivist themselves concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss? And I'd like an honest assessment of whether, once patched to handle these cases, the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
