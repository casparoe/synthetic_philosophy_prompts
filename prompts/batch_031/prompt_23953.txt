I hold that the better-than relation in ordinary comparative evaluation is genuinely intransitive in borderline cases: in a sorites series, one case can be better than the next, the next better than the next, yet the last not worse than the first, because each local comparison is made under a subtly different standard and no single global ordering can represent the local comparisons without distorting them. What would count against this view? I want the strongest arguments, intuitions, and formal results that would show better-than is transitive after all, or that apparent cycles are always artifacts of shifting standards, hidden preferences, or imprecise measurement, including considerations from decision theory, measurement theory, and the semantics of vague predicates. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what a defender of intransitivity would themselves concede as a cost from what could be dismissed as confusion, and say whether the view can be qualified so that it survives every case without losing its philosophical point.
