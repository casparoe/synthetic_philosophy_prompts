I retired from materials science about four years ago and I've been filling the gap with philosophy, mostly the decision-theory and ethics side of things, because it's the part that still feels like it's doing real work. I keep comming back to this topic people call moral uncertainty, and I want to try it out on a case that's actually sitting in front of me. A company wants to hire me as a consultant to make their marketing "a little more persuasive than it strictly warrants" — the product is legal and safe and only marginally inferior to the competition, and the ask is, as far as I can tell, careful puffery, not definate lies, just making the good parts sound better than the fine print admits. A former colleague of mine, a very pragmatic engineer, thinks it's obviously fine and puts the chance it's wrong at about 0.1, while a philosophy grad student I've been talking to thinks it's obviously wrong, about 0.9, on the grounds that it's a species of deception and a character vice even when the harm is small and spread thin.

So here's what I want, and I want an actual number, not "it depends." Give me your credence that accepting this particular job is morally wrong, and break it down the way I'd want a Bayesian to: for each of the main moral theories you think are live candidates — consequentialist or utilitarian, Kantian deontological, virtue ethics, plus any fourth you'd add — state the weight you'd put on that theory being the right one and the probability the job is wrong given that theory, then show me the combination. But I also want you to be straight with me about the two places I think this whole calculation quietly falls apart. First, it assumes moral realism — that there's a fact of the matter and exactly one of these theories is true — and if that's not right I'm not sure what my credences over theories even mean. Second, even granting realism, these theories might not be speaking the same currency, in which case "expected moral value" isn't a number at all. Tell me what you think each of those two problems does to your number, which sub-question your final answer is most sensitive to, and what would actually move it. And if you can, say whether I'm more likely to be at odds with my engineer friend or with the grad student, and on which piece.
