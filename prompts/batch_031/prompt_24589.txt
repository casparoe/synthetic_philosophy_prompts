A friend who writes a popular blog on political philosophy sent me her latest post, in which she summarizes luck egalitarianism like this: "Luck egalitarianism is the view that a just society should compensate people for the effects of brute luck — the misfortunes that befall them through no fault of their own — while letting them bear the consequences of their own choices. The engine of the view is the responsibility condition: an inequality is legitimate only insofar as it tracks differences in what people chose, not differences in luck. This is why a luck egalitarian will say the person who gambled away their savings deserves to end up poorer than the one who saved, and it's why critics charge the view with being cold-hearted. The view also faces the well-known leveling-down problem: because the view is concerned only with the gap between people and not with their absolute levels, it apparently endorses making everyone equally miserable rather than raising anyone's lot, which is precisely why the view is so controversial." I'm trying to figure out how much of this I should trust. Could you go through it and tell me which parts are accurate, which are subtly wrong, and what a defender of the view would object to? I'm not sure whether the "deserves" framing is right, and I don't fully follow the leveling-down point. And I'd like to know whether her overall picture — that the view is cold-hearted and deeply controversial precisely because of these two features — survives once the summary is corrected, or whether those features are partly artifacts of how she has stated the view.
