I've been turning over Wittgenstein's private language argument in my head, and rathern than have you explain it, I want to dump my gut verdicts on a set of cases and ask what principle, if any, is doing the work behind them. Here's where I land:

Some guy coins the word "flib" to name one particular qualia of his (some odd aftertaste he keeps getting) and keeps a diary ticking every time it shows up. I say that's not a language. He has no way of being right or wrong about whether he's using "flib" correctly, so the word has no grip on anything.

A tetrachromat who has a private word for a shade of red nobody else can see. Same call: not a language.

A mathematician who does all her mental arithmetic in an internal notation no one else knows, but which she could in principle write out and teach. I count this one as a language, or at least as good as one — the rules of the notation are public even if nobody else happens to use them.

A guy with a private 1-to-10 scale for his own pain, calibrated only off his own experience. I say this IS a private language, and it works fine for him.

A couple with a little inside language just the two of them use. Not private at all — it's a public language with two speakers.

Here's the pair I keep going back and forth on: the flib guy and the pain-scale guy look a lot alike to me. Both are calibrated to private experience, neither has a second person to check them against, and yet I've handed them opposite verdicts. I can't quite tell you why. So is there one principle that gets all of these, or at least close, and what does it say about which of my verdicts I have to drop?

I've been toying with the idea that the line is "a private language is possible only if it's anchored to at least one public concept" — flib isn't anchored to anything public so it fails, the pain scale is anchored to public pain so it passes. But I'm not sure that actually explains the difference or if I'm just rationalizing after the fact, and I'm especially not sure it lets me keep calling the pain scale a "private" language rather than a public one with a private dial.

And what would the resulting account say about a case I haven't listed: a blind woman who builds a private spatial notation for getting around her apartment, keyed to internal landmarks nobody else knows about?
