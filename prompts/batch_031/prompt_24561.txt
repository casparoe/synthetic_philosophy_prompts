I work on evals for LLM agents and I keep hitting this thing where an agent produces the right output but for a reason I dont trust — it pattern-matched to the grader, or it got lucky, or it optimized some proxy. I want to build something that separates "did the right thing" from "did the right thing for the right reason," and before I go read any particular theory of right reasons I want the spec first.

So write me the desiderata: what an adequate account of acting for the right reason has to actually deliver. Which cases does it have to get right (at minimum the person who does the right thing by pure accident, the person who does the right thing for the wrong motive, and the person who does the right thing but has no idea it's the right thing — but I want more then those three), which distinctions does it have to respect (right action vs. right reason, and I think there's a real gap between "acting because of a reason" and "acting for a reason" but I'm not sure I'm right about that), and which structural facts about reasons does it have to line up with — with the case or consideration behind each requirement, not just a label.

Be honest about which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. I have a hunch the "the right reason has to be a moral reason" requirement is in tension with some anti-double-counting requirement, but I could be wrong about that. Then briefly, how the main existing views do against the list — but keep the weight on the criteria, that's where I actually want to see the thinking.

Format: main body as prose, then a table at the end with exactly these columns: `desideratum` (one line), `motivating_case` (the specific scenario that forces it), `status` (one of: non-negotiable / negotiable / contested), `knocks_out` (which existing views this requirement eliminates, or "none").

And end with a short note on how confident you are in what you're claiming, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
