I keep circling back to the hole argument and I genuinely cannot find the step where it breaks, and I'd rather not just trust the standard handwave. Here it is as I keep reconstructing it:

(1) GR's field equations are generally covariant, so any smooth change of coordinates takes a solution to a solution.
(2) In particular I can use a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some bounded region -- call it the hole -- but shuffles the points arround inside it.
(3) So I get a second solution that's still a valid solution.
(4) The two solutions agree at every point outside the hole, which is where all the initial/boundary data lives, so as far as the data goes they're identical.
(5) Same data, two solutions, and they differ inside the hole.
(6) Therefore GR is indeterministic.

(6) is obviously false -- GR is deterministic in the ordinary sense -- so one of these has to be doing something shady. But (1) is basically the definition of general covariance, (2)-(4) are just the construction, and (5) looks like it's just reading the previous lines off. The usual reply I get is "the two solutions are the same physical state in different coordinate clothing, so it's only gauge redundancy," but that feels circular to me: if I'm allowed to just declare them the same state, I've already decided the argument isn't showing indeterminism. So what is the actual illicit move, if any? Is the gauge answer a real resolution or a cheat, and if it's real, what does it force me to accept about whether bare spacetime points are "real" or just relational? I'd rather keep GR deterministic than keep substantivalism, but I want to know exactly what I'm paying.
