Exam question:
"In the units-of-selection debate, distinguish between a 'unit of selection' and a 'level of selection.' Explain why that distinction matters for the gene-versus-group-selection controversy, and say what role inclusive fitness and multilevel selection play in it. Conclude by stating, with reasons, whether the gene is the 'real' unit of selection."

Student's answer:
"Natural selection acts on heritable variation, and the unit of selection is the entity on which selection acts. In practice this is just the level of biological organization at which we observe differential reproduction, so we can speak of selection at the gene level, the individual level, or the group level, and these are all the same idea seen from different scales.

The gene is the classic candidate for the unit of selection, following Dawkins, because the gene is a replicator: it copies itself faithfully enough, with variation, for selection to act on it. Individual organisms are also units of selection, because they reproduce and pass on their traits, and groups can be units of selection when groups differ in their rate of reproduction and in the heritability of group properties.

Altruism is the case that has driven the debate. Hamilton's inclusive fitness shows that an altruistic gene can increase in frequency even when it lowers the actor's own reproductive success, because it is copied in relatives; the relevant quantity is the benefit to the recipient times relatedness, minus the cost to the actor. Multilevel selection gives the same result from a different angle: selection within groups favors the selfish individual, while selection between groups favors the more altruistic group, and altruism spreads when between-group selection outweighs within-group selection. Because inclusive fitness and multilevel selection make different predictions about when altruism will evolve, the gene-selectionist and group-selectionist camps disagree over a real, testable empirical question, and the units-of-selection debate is a live dispute rather than a verbal one.

So the gene is the real unit of selection. Selection ultimately acts on the gene, since it is the gene that replicates and whose frequency changes; the individual and the group are at best convenient bookkeeping levels at which we summarize what is really happening at the gene level."
