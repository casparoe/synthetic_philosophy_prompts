I run an upper-level seminar on decision theory and ethics, about 14 students, one 75-minute session, and I want a game that makes them feel the force of Kavka's toxin puzzle instead of just hearing it explained. What I care about is the structure, not the story: the decision and the act happen at different times, the act is only checked at the later time, and the player's dominant consideration flips between the two -- at decision time it's the reward, not the act, while at act time it's the disgust, not the reward. I want the game to keep that timing structure exactly, because if students can "drink" at the moment they decide, the puzzle collapses and everyone just signs and drinks.

So I need rules, materials (I can spend a few dollars a student, and I can make something gross but safe to drink -- pickle juice and hot sauce or whatever works), a payoff scheme, and a second round where students can pre-commit to the act in some way you design. The pre-commitment round is the important part: I want them to run into the objection that a pre-commitment to do something you know you won't do at the relevant time is incoherent -- you can't genuinely intend to drink if you already know the disgust will win at the moment it matters, and a deposit or a promise doesn't change what you'll actually feel at that moment. The debrief should map what actually happened in the room onto one-step vs two-step reasoning and say why pre-commitment does or doesn't dissolve the puzzle. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, and be honest about what the game can't reproduce -- like, if the disgust is strong enough that the two-step strategy isn't even available, I want to know whether thats a bug or a feature.

One more thing: don't give them the name of the puzzle or the one-step/two-step labels until the debrief. I want them to notice the tension on their own.
