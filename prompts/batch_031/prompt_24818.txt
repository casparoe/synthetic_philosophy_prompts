My physics teacher showed us a video about Gödel's rotating-universe solution, in which the geometry of space lets a traveler loop around and return to their own past. The video's punchline was that this proves time isn't really real and that the passage of time is an illusion. I followed the setup, but I'm stuck on whether that punchline actually follows. It feels like two claims are being slid together. Claim one: the equations describing the whole universe do not single out any one moment as the present; the entire history is laid out all at once, the way a film exists as a finished reel with no frame marked "you are here." Claim two: therefore the present, the moving now, the flow of time, is not a real feature of the world but something only in our heads. I can grant claim one and still doubt claim two. The reel having no "you are here" frame does not seem to show that watching is an illusion; it might just show that the reel is being described from the outside. Is there a name for this gap between "the physics can be described timelessly" and "time, or the present, is unreal"? And does Gödel's rotating-universe argument actually support the stronger claim, or only the weaker one? I don't need a reading list; I just want the relevant concepts named and the boundary between them drawn.
