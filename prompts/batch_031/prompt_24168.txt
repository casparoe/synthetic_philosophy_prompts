ok so in my philosophy of bio class we had this argument in study group and i honestly could not tell who was making sense. we were supposed to read something on whether the "genetic code" is actually a code in the informaiton-theoretic sense or just a metaphor, and it went like this.

Maya went first and said DNA is obviously a code. It's a sequence of symbols — A, C, G, T — that encodes information, exactly like a book is a sequence of letters that encodes a message. The genetic code maps codons to amino acids, so there's a syntax, a semantics, and a codebook. It's a code, full stop.

Dev pushed back and said that's just a metaphor doing work. DNA is a molecule, not a symbol. It doesn't "encode" anything the way a book does — it just causes things. When the ribosome "reads" DNA it's not interpreting symbols, it's just doing chemistry, and calling that a code is anthropomorphizing.

Maya said but the chemistry is *structured*. The sequence of bases determines the sequence of amino acids, which is exactly what a code does — map one sequence to another. And Shannon information theory doesn't care whether the "message" is in a book or in a molecule; if the sequence carries information, it's a code.

Dev said Shannon information is just about reducing uncertainty, and a random string of bases carries Shannon information too, so that can't be what makes something a code. Shannon info is syntactic — it says nothing about meaning. And DNA's "meaning" is just a story we tell; the tRNA doesn't "know" what amino acid it's carrying, it's just a shape that fits.

Maya conceded the Shannon point kinda, but said the genetic code is *discrete* and *arbitrary*, and that's the hallmark of a real code. The fact that AUG codes for methionine is a contingent fact — it could have been any other amino acid — and if it were just chemistry the mapping would be determined by the shapes, but it isn't.

Dev said the arbitrariness is overblown — people have found the code minimizes the impact of point mutations, which suggests it was shaped by selection, not just some random accident that got locked in place early on. And even granting the mapping is arbitrary, that still doesn't make it a *code* in the strong sense. A code needs a codebook you can actually *consult*, and the tRNA-ribosome system isn't a codebook, it's just molecules that bind in specific ways. There's no interpreter looking anything up.

Maya said you're raising the bar so high no natural system could ever be a code. A book's "codebook" is just a convention readers happen to share; the genetic code's codebook is the tRNAs and the ribosome, shared by every cell in the organism and passed down through evolution. And the ribosome *is* the interpreter — it's a molecular machine that reads the code.

Dev said a ribosome isn't an interpreter, it's a catalyst. It doesn't "read" the code the way you read a book, it doesn't understand what it's doing. The whole "DNA is a code" thing is a useful metaphor for doing biochemistry, but it's not a literal truth — it's like saying the sun "rises."

Maya asked, so is your position that the "genetic code" is just a metaphor? And Dev said no — the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is a real, well-defined regularity — but it's not a *code* in the strong information-theoretic sense, it's a causal regularity we describe in code-language because it's convenient.
