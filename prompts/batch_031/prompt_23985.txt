I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar in philosophy of biology and I keep running into the same wall: people say "I think a trait's function is what it was selected for" or "I think function is just the role the trait plays" and I can't tell wich of like five actual positions they're landing on, because they all sound identical until you press them on a weird case. Could you write me a decision tree / taxonomy that sorts someone into their actual position on biological function?

I want the branch nodes to be the real diagnostic splits, not the labels. At minimum the tree has to seperate (a) the view that a trait's function is fixed by what it was historically selected for, (b) the view that function is fixed by the causal role the trait plays in the organism's current organization, (c) the view that "selection" in (a) should be read broadly as a homeostatic property cluster rather than just natural selection, (d) the design-explanation view, and (e) the deflationary view that there's no genuine "function" over and above causal/mechanistic role. Phrase every node as a question a student could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to.

For the sorting tests I want you to actually use these cases: malfunctions (why is a heart that doesn't pump a malfunction and not just a heart doing something else?), exaptations / spandrels (a trait that stuck around and got co-opted for a use it wasn't originally selected for), vestigial traits (no current job, only a historical one), artifacts (does a hammer have a function, and by what account?), and the "anything goes" objection to pure causal role (a tumor that incidentally helps the host — does it count as having a function?). For each leaf give the position a name (or "unnamed") and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then separately, list any combination of answers your tree allows but nobody actually holds and say briefly why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any two leaves your tree can't really tell apart. No bibliography or citations, just the structure and the reasoning.
