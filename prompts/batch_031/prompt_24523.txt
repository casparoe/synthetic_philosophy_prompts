I'm working through the identity of indiscernibles for a seminar paper, and I keep getting stuck on Max Black's two-spheres counterexample: two perfect, otherwise featureless spheres, each about the size of a marble, placed one mile apart in an otherwise empty space. They share every intrinsic qualitative property and yet are plainly two numerically distinct objects, so the strong form of the principle — that anything indiscernible from anything else must be identical to it — seems false. The standard reply is that the spheres are discernible after all, because each bears a relational property the other lacks (A is "one mile from B" while B is "one mile from A"), so the principle is safe once we count relational as well as intrinsic properties. I'd like you to compare that relational-properties defense with the rival view that the principle's real content depends on restricting it to intrinsic, non-relational properties, on which reading Black's spheres are a genuine refutation rather than a non-sequitur.

The part I most want to press is what I take to be the crux, the trivialization worry: if we are allowed to count any relational property, then for any two distinct objects we can always distinguish them by the property "being identical to A" (which A has and B does not), and the principle becomes true for every pair of distinct things — technically true, but doing no real work. So the substantive question is whether a defender of the relational reading can draw a principled line that keeps enough relational properties to give the principle genuine content while excluding the self-referential, identity-encoding properties that make it vacuous. Please work out where that line could plausibly be drawn (a ban on properties that merely encode an object's own identity, a restriction to "genuine" relational properties, or some other criterion), assess whether the line is stable or just ad hoc, and then say which of the two readings — relational or intrinsic — is the better home for a principle that is supposed to do real metaphysical work.

Please end your answer with a single line of the form "Verdict: X", where X is exactly one of: RELATIONAL-SUBSTANTIVE (the relational reading survives with genuine, non-vacuous content), RELATIONAL-TRIVIAL (the relational reading only saves the principle by rendering it vacuously true), INTRINSIC-REFUTED (the principle only has content as an intrinsic-property principle, and Black's spheres refute it), or NO-SUBSTANTIVE-PII (no non-trivial version of the principle is worth holding).
