I sit on the clemency panel that advises the president on amnesty cases left over from the old regime, and we have to hand her a written recommendation by the end of the month. The case in front of us: a group of mid-level security officers who carried out a string of forced disappearances during the authoritarian years. They didnt set the policy — they were following orders and the system's incentives — but their names are in the files and a few victims' families have identified them specifically and are demanding prosecution. Other families, though, have told us they'd rather have the truth and the location of the bodies than a prison sentence, and they're willing to accept amnesty in exchange for full public disclosure. The panel is genuinely split: some of us think granting amnesty is a betrayal of the victims and of the idea that serious wrongs call for punishment, while others think prosecution will just re-open the wound, undo the reconcilliation we've spent years building, and leave the families without the truth they most want. Draft the panel's recommendation memo — amnesty in exchange for verified truth-telling, prosecution, or some middle option — with the reasoning laid out so that the side we don't end up recommending can actually see we took their strongest argument seriously.
