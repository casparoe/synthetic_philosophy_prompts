Okay, free will thread, again — but this time I actually want to stress-test a specific argument rather than re-litigate determinism. I've been reading the Basic Argument against ultimate moral responsibility (the Galen Strawson one) and I've kind of landed on the side of being persuaded, but specifically by the character-regress / causa-sui version, not the 'well, determinism' version. Here's the form I find airtight: when you act at t you do what you do because of the way you are at t (your character, your mental makeup); so to be *ultimately* responsible for the action you'd have to be ultimately responsible for that way-you-are; but to be ultimately responsible for that way-you-are you'd have to be responsible for it in the *same* ultimate, non-derived sense, which means you'd have to be responsible for the way you are that got you to be responsible for the first way-you-are, and so on — a regress that only stops if you're the uncaused cause (causa sui) of your own relevant mental makeup, which is impossible. So nobody is ultimately responsible, and retributive desert (the kind that makes punishment *deserved* rather than merely useful) is out. I'm basically a hard incompatiblist about desert now, and since the argument is a priori I dont think it hinges on whether determinism is true.
