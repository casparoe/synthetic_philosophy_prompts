A colleague presented an argument at a seminar that he claimed shows Molinism proves too much. I find the conclusion unconvincing, but I can't pinpoint which premise or step fails. Here's the argument as he presented it:

(1) Middle knowledge is knowledge of counterfactuals of the form "S would freely do X in C," for every possible creature S, action X, and circumstance C.
(2) For every pair (C, X), there is at least one possible creature S such that S would freely do X in C.
(3) God can actualize any possible world that is consistent with the true counterfactuals of freedom.
(4) So, for every (C, X), God can actualize a world in which some creature freely does X in C.
(5) So, for every (C, X), God can make it true that some creature freely does X in C.
(6) So, for every (C, X, S), God can make it true that S freely does X in C.
(7) So God can make it true that any creature freely does any action in any circumstance.
(8) But this is incoherent: it would mean God can make it true that a creature freely does what that creature would not freely do.
(9) Therefore, Molinism is false.

I'm most willing to keep (1), (3), and (8). Can you tell me which step to reject, why it looked plausible, and what the cost of rejecting it is?
