I'm a novelist (well, I write novels, so I'm a novelist by my own standards) and I've been stuck on this book for six years. Not full-time — I have a day job — but most nights and a lot of weekends, and I've rewritten the same middle section about nine times. Last month my agent read the first third and was kind but clear: it's not working, and she'd rather not see the rest. So the decision in front of me is either keep going on this one, or kill it and start the new book I've been wanting to start for a while.

My brother, who thinks in spreadsheets, gave me the standart argument: the six years are gone either way, so the only question is what your time from here on is worth, and the past shouldn't enter the decision at all. And I know that's what I'm supposed to think, and I can repeat it back to you, but it just doesnt land — I keep opening the old manuscript at night, and if I started the new book I'd feel like I was throwing away six years, like the book is owed to me for all the hours I've put in. So I want to understand what's actually going on with me, and whether my brother is right.

The thing is, I have a few specific reasons for finishing that I can't tell whether they're just the past pulling on me or whether they're real: one, if I actually finish a long book, I'll have learned how to do it, and I think that skill carries over to the next one; two, my editor has said she'd only consider the finished version, not the first third, so starting over means I lose the only shot I have at getting it published; three, the current draft has the best prose I've ever written, and if I abandon it I might never write anything like it again. Can you tell me which of these are the kind of reason the past is legitimately relevant to my decision and which are just the feeling that I've invested in it, and what a clean way to think about the decision would look like?
