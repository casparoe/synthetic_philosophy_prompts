I work for a fisheries and wildlife agency, and we keep getting proposals to list newly discovered populations as distinct species for the purposes of conservation law. The concept of species is philosophically contentious: the biological species concept (reproductive isolation) and the phylogenetic species concept (monophyly) can give different answers for the same population, and it is not clear whether species are natural kinds at all or whether the right species concept is a matter of pragmatic choice. Please write a decision procedure my team could actually run on a proposal to list a population as a distinct species: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on two proposals: listing a newly discovered fish population that is reproductively isolated but not monophyletic, and listing a bird subspecies that is monophyletic but not reproductively isolated.
