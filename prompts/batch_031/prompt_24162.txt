I've landed on the position that evolutionary psychology is a real science, not a genre of just-so stories in the Kipling sense, and I'd like a map of what would count against it. My working view: the core program -- that a lot of the mind is a suite of domain-specific modules shaped by natural selection in the ancestral environment -- is doing exactly the kind of historical-functional explanation that makes evolutionary biology science, and the standard just-so objection (post-hoc, unfalsifiable, any story can be spun) is a generic attack that would sink paleontology and phylogenetics too, so it doesn't land. What would have to be true for me to be wrong? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd be helped if you separated (a) evidence that would count against the modular-architecture claims specifically, (b) evidence that would count against the ancestral-environment reconstruction method, and (c) things that would only embarrass particular famous hypotheses without touching the program. I also want to know which of these a committed evolutionary psychologist would themselves concede as counting against them versus wave away as bad examples, and whether the program has a signature prediction that rival accounts -- say, general-purpose-learning-mechanism views or cultural-evolution accounts -- genuinely don't make, or whether its testability is mostly negative (it's just the absence of a better alternative). One more thing: I suspect part of the defense is definitional, i.e. "any hypothesis that could in principle be tested counts, and we just have to wait for the tests." Is the position insulated that way, and if so, is that a problem for it, or is that just what historical sciences are like? What I'd personally find most persuasive is a case where the program made a risky, dated, specific prediction and got it wrong in a way a rival account got right -- or, failing that, a clear statement of how much of the "science" label is load-bearing versus how much is already on the table from the fact that selection is a real cause.
