Draft the joint statement that a committed Peircean pragmatist and a committed Jamesian pragmatist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the nature of truth. The statement should include: (1) the claims on which they agree, including the role of the pragmatic maxim in identifying the meaning of a concept, the limitations of the correspondence theory of truth, and the fallibility of all empirical beliefs; (2) the precise formulations of their residual disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — of which I suspect one concerns whether there is a mind-independent "real" that inquiry converges on (Peirce's position) or whether truth is constituted by the process of inquiry itself (James's position), and another concerns what the "long run" of inquiry is like; and (4) for each crux, the case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If you conclude that part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. Format the statement as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
