ok so I've been writing up my views on population ethics for a seminar next week and I'd like a consistency check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a lexical threshold negative utilitarian. Suffering above some fairly mild threshold is lexically prior to happiness — no amount of happiness, for that person or for anyone else, can make up for even one instance of real suffering. Below the threshold, minor pains are just small negatives on the same scale as pleasures.

(2) Pleasure and pain are commensurable. There's a common hedonic scale, and the fact that people routinely accept pain for pleasure — surgery, training, grinding work — shows they trade off at some rate.

(3) The repugnant conclusion is false. A world with a huge number of people whoes lives are barely worth living is not better than a world with fewer people who are really happy.

(4) The non-identity problem doesn't let us off the hook. We have real obligations to future people, and we can't dodge them by pointing out that a different choice would've brought about different people.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, how badly, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't want a survey of the literature — I want you to trace what commits me to what.
