I'm preparing my position for a care-ethics seminar and would like a consistency check before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) The moral self is fundamentally relational — my identity and moral standing are constituted by my relationships, not by an atomistic agent who happens to occupy them.
(2) Moral obligations are grounded in, and roughly proportional to, my actual relationships, so partiality toward family and close friends is a constitutive feature of moral life rather than a defect to be corrected.
(3) Care is a practice — attention, responsiveness, and doing — not merely a sentiment; I can genuinely care about someone even in moments when I feel nothing warm.
(4) I reject the demand for an impartial, perspective-free standpoint as the foundation of ethics; moral reasoning is always situated, and abstract principles should be subordinate to the demands of particular relationships and situations.
(5) I nonetheless hold that we have serious duties to distant strangers, including the global poor, and that in some cases those duties can outweigh what I owe to people close to me.
(6) Moral judgment depends on a kind of emotional responsiveness, so someone who is emotionally detached is systematically likely to judge badly.

Please audit whether this package is jointly tenable: which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear tense, and — for each real tension — what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Rank the real tensions by severity. I don't yet know where the trouble is, so please don't assume I'll drop any particular item.
