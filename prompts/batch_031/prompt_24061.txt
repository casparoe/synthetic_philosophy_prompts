I just had a long argument with a friend about Avicenna's flying man, and I'd like a referee. He presses it as proof that self-consciousness is independent of the body: a person created fully formed in mid-air, with no sensory input and no feeling of his own limbs, would still be aware of himself, so the self or soul can be grasped apart from any embodied existence. I countered with the proprioception line — that being aware of oneself as a subject essentially involves locating oneself in space, which requires awareness of one's own body, so the flying man's self-awareness is either incoherent or secretly parasitic on body-awareness. Compare the two positions in detail: what exactly does the flying man establish (self-awareness, self-identity, the separability of the soul, or only some of these), what exactly does the proprioception challenge target, and does the separability conclusion survive once the body-awareness objection is on the table? I want the genuine points of disagreement laid out, not a restatement of each side.
