I'm writing a piece for the mag on whether automation is actually good or bad for the meaning of work, and I keep going in circles, so I'm just going to lay out my gut reactions to a bunch of specific cases and see if there's a principle underneath them.

A mining company automates its deep-shaft work, which was killing people a few times a year. The 200 miners get a decent severance and retraining, and most take it. My gut: good, clearly. The lives saved are worth more than the displacement.

A call center automates its scripted customer service. The agents were bored, underpaid, and most were looking for other work anyway. My gut: good.

A bakery chain replaces its bakers with robots. The bakers were genuinly proud of the craft, had built a little community around the ovens, and say they'll miss it even though they get retrained into something else. My gut: bad, or at least bittersweet in a way the other two aren't.

A small town's only factory gets automated, and the town has no other economic base. The workers cant easily leave, and the town starts to die. My gut: bad, or at least the automation should have been phased and the town given a real shot.

A hospital automates its radiology reads. The radiologists would rather do clinical work anyway, and the reads were the part they found most tedious. My gut: good.

Now here's where I get stuck. The bakery and the radiology case look alike on the surface—both are 'we're replacing a job that the workers have some attachment to'—but I call the bakery bad and the radiology good. And the mining and the small-town factory both involve a community with no easy alternative, but I call the mining good and the factory bad. I don't know what the actual line is.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or at least most of them, and what does it say about a case I haven't listed—like a person who's trapped in a job they hate because they need the money, and automation takes it away and they can't find another one? And if the principle has to give up one of my verdicts to stay simple, which one would you sacrifice?
