a lot of the people i interview about futarchy -- using prediction markets to pick policy -- describe the market as an oracle: you ask it what the future looks like if we pass X, it hands you a number, and the wisdom of the crowd does the seeing for you. they lean on that picture to argue futarchy is just a more honest version of the forecasting we already trust, and that the remaining problems are mostly technical. but i'm starting to suspect the oracle analogy is quietly doing all the work in that argument. can you audit it -- what maps onto what, where the mapping breaks (resolution, manipulation, who gets to ask the question, what "accuracy" even means when the participants are trading rather than knowing), and whether the "just technical" conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table?
