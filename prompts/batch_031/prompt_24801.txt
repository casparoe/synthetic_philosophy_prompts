Debates about the free energy principle often turn on a thermostat analogy: the brain is a homeostat that detects deviation from a set point and acts to restore it, so minimizing variational free energy is just homeostasis in elaborate notation — which is precisely the point critics invoke to argue the principle is trivial. Audit this analogy: give the correspondence table (set point, error signal, actuator, and the difference between acting on the world and acting on the model), identify where the mapping breaks — in particular, whether a thermostat has anything like a generative model, beliefs about causes, or expected free energy — and say whether the triviality conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion the whole time. If a better analogy exists, name it and say what it would capture that the thermostat cannot.
