I hold the view that the diachronic Dutch book argument is a sound and genuinely independent justification for conditionalization as the unique rational rule of credal update, on par with the synchronic Dutch book argument for the probability axioms, and that an agent who updates by any rule other than conditionalization is thereby vulnerable to a diachronic Dutch book: a sequence of trades, each rational to accept given the agent's credences at the moment it is offered, that in aggregate is a sure loss no matter what evidence the agent receives. What would count against this? I want the objections and results that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they would be, and in particular I want to know whether there exist non-conditionalizing update rules that are not pumpable (which would refute the uniqueness claim), whether the diachronic argument smuggles in assumptions the synchronic one does not make, about the availability of the relevant bets, about prices staying fixed, or about the agent's willingness to trade at all, and whether the argument has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Close with an honest assessment of where a committed defender of the argument would themselves concede a point and where they would dismiss the objection as missing the point.
