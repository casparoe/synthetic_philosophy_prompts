write a dialogue between two Bayesian epistemologists who have both read Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree theorem and keep running into it in different guises: one is a decision theorist who thinks the common prior assumption is doing almost none of the work in the theorem and that the real content is that rational agents with common knowledge of each other's posteriors can't maintain a gap; the other is a probability theorist who thinks the common prior assumption is the whole engine and that without it the theorem is trivial. have them argue over what the assumption actually commits you to — is it just a condition on a probability function over a space of possible worlds, or does it require a shared history, a shared likelihood function, a shared model of the evidence, or all of those — and over where each of them thinks the assumption is quietly being violated in real disagreements. neither should concede by the end, but each should end up with a more precise account of what they were objecting to than they started with.
