I need a precise argument in decision theory on evidential cooperation in large worlds. Consider a one-shot, simultaneous game played by a population of N agents. Each agent is an evidential decision theorist, and all agents are similar to one another in the relevant respects (roughly the same priors and the same decision procedure). Each agent chooses either to cooperate or to defect. An individual agent's payoff is costly if she cooperates, but her total payoff rises with the overall fraction of cooperators in the population, so that the game has the structure of a large-scale Prisoner's Dilemma in which cooperation is individually disadvantageous but collectively beneficial once a sufficient fraction cooperates. By "evidential cooperation" I mean the strategy of cooperating on the grounds that one's own choice to cooperate is evidence that similarly situated agents will also cooperate, thereby raising the expected fraction of cooperators and hence one's own expected payoff.

Argue, as rigorously as you can, for the position that in the limit as N becomes very large, evidential cooperation breaks down and the evidentialist is driven back toward the causally dominant strategy, namely defection, unless the agents' decision procedures share a common source rather than being merely similar. To earn the argument, do the following. First, state the evidential link explicitly as a function of N and of the degree of similarity (or shared-source strength) between agents. Second, identify the precise point at which that evidential link becomes too weak to offset the one-shot cost of cooperating, so that defection maximizes expected utility. Third, defend your conclusion against the strongest version of the objection that the evidentialist can always treat her own action as a random sample drawn from the same decision procedure as everyone else's, which would keep the evidential link strong no matter how large N is. Make your assumptions about the payoff structure and the model of similarity explicit throughout, and say where the conclusion is sensitive to them.
