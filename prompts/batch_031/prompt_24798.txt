Here's my opening move, and I want you to actually take it seriously rather than deflect: you keep saying the Liar is both true and false, and fine, I'll grant you that for the sake of argument. But think about what that does to the law of exluded middle. If the Liar is both true and false, then "the Liar is true or the Liar is not true" is true, and the same goes for "2+2=4 or 2+2≠4", and the same goes for every single instance of excluded middle you've ever used in a proof. You've made your most basic reasoning tool — "either it's raining or it's not" — vacuously true, so it no longer tells you anything about whether a given claim is determinate. How do you reason about the world, or write a proof, when the one rule that used to guarantee you a definite answer has become a tautology that's true no matter what?
