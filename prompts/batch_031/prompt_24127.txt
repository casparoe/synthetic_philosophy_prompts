Here's my opening: in the standard Frankfurt case, the agent picks the option on their own, so the interferer's mechanism is never activated. That means the actual sequence is just a normal case of wanting, deliberating, and choosing. The only thing that makes it a Frankfurt case is that, in the counterfactual where the agent had chosen differently, some device would have forced them into the actual option. If their actual choice was unforced, why should the mere existence of a counterfactual force make them responsible? If you say it doesnt, you're just using some other principle about actual control; if you say it does, you're smuggling in a principle that responsibility requires not being subject to a counterfactual control, which sounds suspiciously like a disguised PAP.
