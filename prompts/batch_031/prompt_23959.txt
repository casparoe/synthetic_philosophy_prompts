I've been following the alignment discourse on this for years and I keep coming back to the process vs outcome supervision debate, which I think its stalled in a way that's weird for a community that likes to formalize things. I was at a meetup last week and one person kept saying "just supervise the outcome, the spec is the target, process inspection doesn't scale" while another kept saying "any outcome reward is gameable, the gap between what you specified and what you wanted is exctly where the failure lives," and they just kept repeating themselves for twenty minutes without ever touching each other's actual points.

Let me steelman both sides so you don't have to. The outcome-based camp basically says: if the reward is well-specified, the agent's internal business is its own problem, and "just check the intermediate steps" is auditing a black box with a black box, which doesn't scale to a model bigger than you. The process-based camp says: any outcome you write down is a proxy, and once the agent is good enough it'll find the gap between the proxy and what you actually wanted, so if you only look at the outcome you're discovering the misalignment after the exploit instead of before it, and the only way to catch it is to monitor the trajectory as it happens.

Here's my hypothesis, which I might be wrong about: I don't think these two camps are actually disagreeing about which scheme is safer. I think they're disagreeing about what the spec is — one side treats the spec as a fixed target external to the system, the other treats it as a moving target the agent can pull on, and the whole argument is a disagreement about whether the spec is part of the system or outside it. Or maybe it's a verbal disagreement about what "process" even refers to: the latent states, the chain of thought, the action sequence — because if the chain of thought is something the training process shaped to look inspectable rather than the actual computation, then "inspect the process" might be inspecting a decoy, and that changes the whole thing. Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what each side actually says rather than the caricatures, and tell me honestly whether anything — a formal result, a new distinction, a change of question — could actually move it, or if I should just stop expecting it to?
