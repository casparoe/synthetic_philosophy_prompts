My friend has an argument that Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument, pressed to its limit, shows that the concept of a just distribution is empty, and I can't find where it goes wrong. Here it is:

(1) Nozick's entitlement principle: a distribution D is just if and only if D arises from a just distribution by a sequence of voluntary transfers.
(2) The Wilt Chamberlain argument shows that from a just distribution, voluntary transfers can produce a radically unequal distribution (Chamberlain rich, everyone else comparatively poor).
(3) By the same reasoning, from a just distribution, voluntary transfers can produce any distribution whatsoever — radically equal, radically unequal, or anything in between.
(4) So for every distribution D, D arises from a just distribution by a sequence of voluntary transfers.
(5) So for every distribution D, D is just (by (1)).
(6) So every distribution is just, and the concept of a just distribution is empty.

But (6) is absurd — there are plainly unjust distributions (slavery, theft, the outcomes of fraud). I'd most like to keep (1) and (2), since they are the heart of Nozick's position. Tell me exactly where the argument breaks down: which step is the flaw, why it looked fine, and what it costs to reject each of the premises (1) through (4) in turn.
