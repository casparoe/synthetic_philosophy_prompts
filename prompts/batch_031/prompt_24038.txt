I'm teaching a graduate seminar on decision theory this term, and we've reached the updateless decision theory literature, and I have to confess the debate has stalled in a way I find genuinely frustrating rather than merely unresolved. Everyone in the room agrees on the setup of Newcomb's problem, and everyone can recite the three standard responses: the causal decision theorist who two-boxes on dominance grounds because the prediction is already fixed in the causal past; the evidentialist who one-boxes because the act is strong evidence of the predictor's output; and the updateless camp (the various UDT, FDT, and TDT formulations) who reframe the question as one about which decision procedure to implement, and one-box on that basis. What I cannot get my students to see is why these three positions keep passing each other on the variants — the cases involving a copy of the agent, the transparent or partially transparent predictors, the cases in which the relevant "algorithm" is genuinely ambiguous — without any of them conceding a single point. My working hypothesis, which I want you to treat as a hypothesis and not as a conclusion, is that the impasse is not really about Newomb at all: I suspect the causal and updateless sides are disagreeing about what the fundamental causal unit is (the isolated decision event versus the reusable decision procedure), and that they are in fact using the word "rational" to mean two different things — "maximizes causal expected utility from the decision point" versus "implements the procedure that, from a god's-eye view, yields the best outcome" — so that they are answering different questions under a single heading.

What I want is a diagnosis of the *structure* of this disagreement, supported by what each camp actually says and actually relies on, not a survey of the positions and not a verdict on who is right about the boxes. In particular: is the impasse a verbal one (a hidden disagreement about what "rational" or "the same decision situation" even mean), a substantive one about the ontology of decision-making (whether a stable, identifiable "algorithm" is a real, causally efficacious entity or a convenient fiction), or two groups answering different questions under one name? And within the updateless camp itself, I suspect the UDT/FDT/TDT splits are not cosmetic but track a real, still-open technical question about how to formalize the correlation between distinct instances of the same procedure; please say whether that suspicion is right and identify the actual technical crux on which those formulations diverge.

Think very carefully before answering, and respond in the style of an expert who has worked through the formalism rather than one summarizing a review article. Given your diagnosis, tell me honestly what, if anything, would count as progress — a conceptual distinction, a formal result, a redefinition of the decision problem, or a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and say precisely why this disagreement is of the kind that resists resolution.
