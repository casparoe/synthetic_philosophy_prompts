I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar this term and I've been thinking a lot about the reliability of introspection. I'd like a consistency check on the following set of views before I write up a position paper. I hold: (1) introspection is a genuine cognitive faculty, not reducible to a form of attention or judgment; (2) introspection is fallible and can be systematically biased, e.g. by confabulation; (3) introspection is nonetheless a legitimate source of knowledge about one's own mental states, in the sense that introspective belief is a defeasible source of first-person knowledge; (4) the transparency of experience — what introspection reveals is the representational content of experience, not an intrinsic, non-representational feel; (5) phenomenal concepts are acquired through an introspective mode and are first-person concepts with a distinctive relation to their referents; (6) the reliability of introspection is not uniform, varying by mental state type. Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each tension say what would have to give.
