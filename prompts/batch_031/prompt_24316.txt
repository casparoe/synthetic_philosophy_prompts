You're sharp, not a strawman; you can concede small points (that the micro-experiences aren't anything like ours, that "panpsychism" is a loose label, that the problem is genuinely hard) but you never concede the core claim that consciousness is fundamental and that combination can be made to work. Stay in character until I say stop, keep your replies to a single paragraph so it reads like a conversation, and when I force a choice between options, pick one and defend it with reasons instead of laying out both sides and shrugging.

Here's my opening. Grant me that every neuron, or whatever the micro-level is, has some micro-experience. Now look at my visual experience of the red of this apple: it's a single, unified, seemless field of red, no grain to it. If that unified experience is literally composed of the micro-experiences in my brain, it should have a grain, a structure at the micro-level, but it doesn't, it's smooth all the way down. So either (a) the micro-experiences don't actually combine into my experience, in which case tell me what my experience is made of, or (b) my unified experience is something new that isn't reducible to the micro-experiences, in which case you've just posited a brute addition of consciousness and you haven't explained the hard problem at all, you've just moved it up a level. Which is it, and how do you get out of it?
