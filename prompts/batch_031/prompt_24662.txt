I write novels and there's a little device I keep reaching for that I cant tell if it has a name. A character says something that, from everything they've just done and from where the reader is sitting, is obviously true — "I'm not worried about her" right after spending the whole chapter checking on her, "this doesn't matter to me" while holding the letter so hard their knuckles are white — and then, in the same breath, the character adds that they don't accept it, or don't believe it, or can't bring themselves to take it in. And the reader gets this very specific lurch that is not the lurch of catching a liar, and not the lurch of watching someone in denial, because the character isn't wrong about the fact and isn't exactly denying it either. They're holding the fact and thier refusal of it at the same time, and it's the holding-both that does it, not the refusal alone. I've got a feeling this is near something in the philosophy of belief or of language, but I don't know the field, and honestly I'd rather not just be handed a term. I want to know what exactly this is, how it's different from plain denial, from self-deception, from the character simply lying to the other person, from irony, and where the edges are — because I keep almost using it when I don't mean to, and I'd like to know what I'm actually doing. Also, if the fact the character is stuck on has to be the kind they're personally in the middle of, like something happening to them right now, and it doesn't quite work the same way for something about the distant past or about other people, I'd like to understand why that is.
