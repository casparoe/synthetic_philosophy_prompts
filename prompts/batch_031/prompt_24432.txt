I'm working on a research project that reconstructs how particular philosophers actually decide cases, and I need help with a reconstruction exercise in the logic of the Liar paradox. My interlocutor is a philosopher who has given me the following verdicts on a series of sentences, and I have not yet learned which of the two standard approaches to the paradox she endorses:

- The standard Liar sentence — a sentence that says of itself that it is not true — is neither true nor false.
- "Snow is white" is true, and "Snow is red" is false.
- "The Liar sentence is not true" is true.
- "Snow is white and the Liar sentence is true" is neither true nor false.
- "The Liar sentence is true or the Liar sentence is false" is neither true nor false.
- "This sentence is true" — a sentence that says of itself that it is true — is true.

The next sentence on her docket is "The Liar sentence is true or snow is red."

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — namely, which verdict you predict she will give on that sentence, stated as true, false, or neither — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Your reasoning should do the following:

1. Identify the two candidate frameworks that jointly explain her recorded verdicts: the one in which ungrounded sentences simply lack a truth value and compound sentences inherit that gap, and the one in which such sentences are indeterminate — true on some admissible completions of the language and false on others, so that a compound sentence counts as determinately true only when it is true on all of them and determinately false only when it is false on all of them. Explain, for each framework, how it disposes of each of her six recorded verdicts, and note that her recorded verdicts are underdetermined between the frameworks.
2. Work out, for each framework, what verdict the new sentence receives, showing the evaluation step by step (in particular, how the disjunction is treated when one disjunct has no value or is indeterminate and the other is false).
3. Give your best guess about which framework she is actually running and which verdict she will deliver, with reasons for preferring one framework over the given track record — for instance, which framework is more natural as a reading of her commitment that the truthteller is fully true, and which framework she would have to be running for that commitment to come out the way she stated it.
4. Name the further test sentences that would distinguish the two frameworks, and say for each what verdict each framework assigns: specifically, the negation of the truthteller, and the conjunction of the truthteller with the Liar sentence. Explain why the first of these is the decisive discriminator, and say which of her recorded verdicts, if any, already leans toward one framework even though it is compatible with both.
