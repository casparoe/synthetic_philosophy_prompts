Write a dialogue between two formal epistemologists debating Aumann's agreement theorem. One holds that the theorem shows genuine, persistent disagreement between rational, honest truth-seekers is impossible whenever their posteriors are common knowledge; the other argues the theorem is nearly vacuous in practice because it trades on idealizations—common priors, perfect Bayesian updating, and literal common knowledge of posteriors—that real disagreements never satisfy. Have them get concrete: press on whether a common prior can be rationally required or is just a modeling convenience, whether iterated exchange of posteriors in real dialogue actually generates common knowledge, and whether failures to converge in practice indict the agents' rationality or the theorem's applicability. Each should directly engage and challenge the other's strongest point rather than restate their own view.
