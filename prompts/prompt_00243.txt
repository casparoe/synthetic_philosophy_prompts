I work at a mid-sized tech company, and a calibration meeting last week has been nagging at me ever since. My skip-level manager uses a fairly sophisticated internal model, trained on years of data from internal transfers, that she claims can reliably predict how any given employee would have performed had they been placed on a different team or project than the one they actually worked on. In my case, she used this model to argue that my strong performance this year on Project A shouldn't count much toward a promotion, since the model "shows" that had I instead been rotated onto the notoriously difficult Project B (which was briefly considered for me eighteen months ago), I would have floundered just like the last three engineers who went there. In the same breath, she used this counterfactual reasoning to explain why she'd assigned me to Project A in the first place: since she already knew in advance how I'd perform under each possible assignment, she picked the one that would let me succeed. That would mean my success reflects her foresight and planning more than it reflects anything about me.

I don't have any philosophy background, but something about this whole picture feels off to me, and I haven't been able to articulate what, exactly. Is it even coherent to say there's a definite fact about how I "would have done" in a situation that never actually happened? And if we grant that there is such a fact, and my manager knew it in advance and used it to steer me into the assignment where I'd shine, does that undermine the credit I deserve for how I actually performed, or is this just a roundabout way of saying she's good at matching people to roles? I'd appreciate help thinking through whether my unease is justified, and if so, what exactly the problem is.
