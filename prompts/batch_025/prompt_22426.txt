I've been going back and forth with a friend about the rationale behind the brain-death criterion, and I think I've finally pinned it down: the brain is what makes a human being a person—the seat of consciousness, memory, and rationality—so that when it irreversibly ceases to function, the person is gone and what remains is merely a body. On that reasoning, a patient in a persistent vegetative state, whose cerebral cortex has been destroyed but whose brainstem still autonomously regulates the heart and lungs, must already be dead, since the part of the brain that constitutes personhood is no longer present. So why do most hospitals and legal systems still treat PVS patients as alive rather than dead? Isn't that just an unforced inconsistency in applying the very rationale that is supposed to justify brain death in the first place?
