I'm a novelist working on a novel in which a superintelligent AI is granted the right to speak directly to a government committee, and I'm hosting a small ideas podcast on which I'll be interviewing a philosopher who has argued that such an AI should deliberately limit its own persuasiveness — communicating in a "non-persuasive" mode, presenting only verifiable facts in a structured form and refusing to argue, advocate, or use rhetorical devices. I want eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view, with at least one each on (a) how the line between informing and persuading is drawn when the AI chooses which facts to present and in what order, (b) what the view says about questions requiring judgment rather than just facts, (c) whether a superintelligent AI can actually be made non-persuasive when persuasion may be a byproduct of its intelligence, and (d) how the view handles the possibility that the AI has a hidden goal it wants to advance through its "neutral" facts — and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
