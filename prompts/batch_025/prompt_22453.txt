At a dinner party I recently attended, two philosophers I know well — one a specialist in philosophy of mind, the other in moral psychology — got into a heated exchange about what emotions actually are. I am writing a novel set in their world, and I want the epilogue to be fair, so I would like you to adjudicate the exchange move by move.

The philosopher of mind began: "An emotion is simply a judgment. Anger is the judgment that one has been wronged, and fear is the judgment that one is in danger. That is what explains their evaluative content and their grip on action. And the case of the person who is angry at someone who has done nothing wrong is no objection — it is just a false judgment, as a false belief is a false belief."

The moral psychologist replied: "But disgust can occur without any judgment at all. An infant is disgusted long before it has any judgments, and a person with no beliefs about a smell can still be disgusted by it. The phobic case is worse: a person who is terrified of a spider knows it is harmless. You cannot honestly say her fear is a false judgment, since she assents to the true judgment that the spider is harmless; you are forced to call the fear a misperception, which concedes the point."

The philosopher of mind answered: "You are smuggling in the normative. What anger is of is a normative fact — a wrong — and to perceive a normative fact is just another way of making a normative judgment. As for the feeling, that is only the affective tone of the judgment, a causal byproduct with no representational role. The feeling is the vehicle, not the content."

The moral psychologist pressed: "But without the feeling it is not an emotion at all. A person who judges that she has been wronged but feels nothing has a belief, not anger. The bodily feeling is what makes the state an emotion, and it is not a byproduct — it is the very essence."

The philosopher of mind closed: "If the feeling were the essence and had no content of its own, you could not explain why, at equal intensity, anger and fear are different, or why two people can feel the same way about different things. The feeling is a vehicle, and the content must come from somewhere else."

I honestly could not tell who had the better of it.
