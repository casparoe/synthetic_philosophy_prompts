I'm a junior in high school, and I've been taking an elective philosophy seminar this semester, and one of the topics we've been discussing has gotten me stuck on something that's actually happening in my own life, so I'd really appreciate a second opinion. I have a close friend, Maya, who has a fairly weak working memory—she can only hold a few pieces of information in her head at once, which makes everything from following a set of directions to recalling a conversation from ten minutes ago genuinely hard for her. To cope, she carries a small notebook in her jacket pocket at all times, and the moment she thinks of anything she might need later, she writes it down: her to-do list, the steps of a recipe, the names of people she just met, even half-formed ideas she wants to return to. She glances at it constantly, and because she's done this for years and it's never once let her down, she trusts whatever is on the page without ever double-checking it. She's so accustomed to it that when someone asks her about something she's written down, she just flips to the page and reads it, and to her it feels exactly like remembering.

The trouble is that last week in seminar a classmate made a point that really upset her. They said that what Maya is doing isn't really memory at all—it's just consulting a tool, no different from my pulling out my phone to look up a number or a fact I don't know. The implication was that Maya doesn't genuinely have those memories or that knowledge; she's just outsourcing them to a piece of paper, and that there's something inauthentic or lesser about it. What I keep coming back to, though, is that the same classmate keeps their own notes in an app on their phone and checks it the same way, and I can't see why their use of the app is "just a tool" while Maya's use of the notebook is "not really memory." I also can't get past the feeling that the whole criticism rests on the notebook being physical and outside her body, which strikes me as the wrong thing to focus on, since her brain is physical and fallible too, and we still count her brain as part of her mind.

So my question is twofold. First, I'd like to understand what actually makes something count as part of a person's mind or memory, as opposed to just an external object they happen to be using—because I suspect the answer isn't simply "is it inside the skull or not." And second, on whatever standard turns out to be the right one, does Maya's notebook genuinely qualify, or does the classmate have a point I'm not seeing? There's one specific detail I can't resolve: when the classmate remembers something, it seems to just pop into their head with no effort, whereas Maya has to actually look at the page. Does that extra step of actively checking the notebook make it fundamentally a different kind of thing from a real memory, or is it just a difference in how much work it takes? I'd be grateful for help thinking through whether the criticism is fair.
