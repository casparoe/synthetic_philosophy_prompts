I came across MDL in the context of model selection and compression — the idea is that you pick the model that minimizes the total code length, i.e., the code length of the model itself plus the code length of the data given the model. I've also encountered Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity in more theoretical discussions, and I keep seeing people say that MDL is just "practical Kolmogorov complexity" or "computable Solomonoff induction." I want to understand whether that's actually right or whether it's a useful but misleading shorthand.

Specifically, I'd like you to compare the two frameworks in detail. Start with the formal definitions: the Solomonoff prior M(x) = sum over all programs p with U(p) = x of 2^(-|p|), where U is a fixed universal Turing machine and |p| is the length of the program p, and the MDL criterion for a model family, which picks the model that minimizes the total code length. Then, in which ways do they actually agree, and in which ways do they diverge? I'm particularly interested in three points: (1) the role of the choice of universal machine U in Solomonoff induction versus the role of the choice of coding language in MDL — are these the same problem or different problems, and why? (2) the uncomputability of the Solomonoff prior versus the computability of MDL — is this just a practical difference or a conceptual one, and if conceptual, what exactly is being lost? (3) the claim that Solomonoff induction is "universal" in the sense that it dominates any computable semimeasure up to a multiplicative constant — does MDL have any analogous universality property, and if not, what does that mean for its status as a theory of induction rather than just a model-selection heuristic?

I find the "MDL is just practical Kolmogorov complexity" claim confusing, because it seems to paper over real differences. I'd like a careful, technically precise comparison that doesn't hand-wave the differences away, and that explains concretely why the differences matter — for example, in a case where the two frameworks would actually pick different models or make different predictions.
