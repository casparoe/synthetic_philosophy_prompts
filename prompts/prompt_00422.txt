A close friend who works at a mid-stage startup has offered to sell me a slice of his vested equity in a private secondary deal, at a price that values the company noticeably below what I understand the last funding round implied. He insists the sale has nothing to do with his view of the company's prospects — he says he's still bullish and is only parting with a fraction of his stake because he needs cash for a house down payment. The price is tempting, but something about the situation nags at me: he has far more visibility than I do into things like churn, hiring, and runway, and he's the one initiating the trade. I keep circling back to the thought that if he genuinely believed the shares were worth more, why would he be selling to me at all, personal liquidity needs notwithstanding — shouldn't he just borrow against the stake, or sell to someone with less access to information than me? Is my hesitation well-founded, or am I overanalyzing an ordinary transaction between a well-informed insider and a cautious outsider? I'd like to understand what's actually going on here conceptually — what should I make of his stated reason for selling, and how much should it change my willingness to take the other side of this trade?
