I'm TAing intro cognitive science and I've been handed Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — for a single fifty-minute section next week. First-years, several of whom are CS majors who will absolutely try to derail the whole thing into "so where do LLMs go on this ladder?" I don't need you to explain the four creatures to me, I more or less get them; I need a plan for the section. What order do I teach them in? The obvious move is Dennett's own order, but is that actually the right pedagogical order, or does something break — would starting with the Popperian creature (the fun one) and working backwards leave students without whatever the Darwinian layer is supposed to put in place first? And which single example should carry the whole section? I want one thread I can mutate four ways — same agent, four upgrades — rather than four disconnected animals, but I can't decide whether the running example should be a foraging animal, a robot, a chess engine, or what, and I want a reason for the pick, not just a pick. It has to be an example where each upgrade visibly does work the previous version couldn't, otherwise the taxonomy feels like decoration.

Then the misconceptions. I already predict a few: students treating it as a great chain of being with humans on top and each creature type replacing the last (rather than each layer presupposing and keeping the ones below it — we are all four at once, right? that's the point?); students reading it as a classification of species rather than of designs or competences; the CS kids scoring chatbots as basically Gregorian because they use language. What am I missing, especially sneaky stuff — like students concluding the Skinnerian creature is the dumb failure case when its actually a huge design win over the Darwinian one, or half the room hearing "Gregorian" and wondering if its some Freud thing? For each misconception: where in the sequence do I head it off — preempt it before it forms, or let it form and then correct it because the correction lands harder?

Last thing: what do I do with the LLM question when it inevitably comes? Is it a derail to defer to office hours, or is it actually the gift — the closing check-for-understanding question that shows whether they can apply the taxonomy rather than recite it, and if so how do I frame it so its diagnostic and not just a vibe debate? And tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level and say so out loud — I assume the machinery underneath (what "selection" even means at the Darwinian layer, whether these are clean natural kinds or a half-rhetorical flourish Dennett doesn't fully commit to) belongs on that list, but I might be wrong about what the real caveats are.
