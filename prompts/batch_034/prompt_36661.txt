I'm a staff software engineer at a mid-sized AI lab, with no philosophy background. We've released small open-weight models so far, but we're now debating whether to release the weights of a much larger one, and the internal discussions are a mess of vibes and politics. I've been asked to draft a release-review procedure that a mixed committee of engineers and policy people could actually run — roughly twice a year, with about a week of analysis per decision, and sign-off required from both the engineering lead and the policy lead.

Please write that procedure. Ground it in the substance of the open-sourcing debate rather than in vibes: the democratization argument, the worry about concentrating frontier capabilities in a few companies, dual-use and capability diffusion, and the fact that "open" isn't binary but a ladder — full weights, gated licenses with identity checks, checkpoints and training code without final weights, API-only access. For each step, say what question is being asked, what counts as a sufficient answer, which outcomes terminate in a no, and which questions are genuinely value questions the committee must own rather than ones more analysis can settle. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire.

One framing I want you to take seriously, or tell me if it's confused: what I've started calling the marginal releaser problem. If three other labs will release a comparable model within months regardless of what we do, our decision barely changes who can access frontier-ish capabilities; if we're the only ones who plausibly can, it changes everything. That suggests the whole procedure hinges on a counterfactual we can only guess at. Handle that explicitly rather than burying it.

Then run the procedure on two cases: (a) releasing the weights of a model that merely matches last year's frontier but at a tenth of the inference cost, and (b) a modest-sized open-weight release of a biology assistant with strong wet-lab planning capabilities. I'd like at least one of the runs to come out with an answer I won't like — I'm broadly sympathetic to open releases, so a procedure that only ever says yes is worthless to me. And if you think the whole idea of a checklist for decisions like this is misguided — that the questions are irreducibly political and a procedure would just launder that — say so plainly rather than papering over it. Please answer in American English.
