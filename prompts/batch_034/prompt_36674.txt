i record a small student philosophy podcast, and this week's guest is a hardcore total utilitarian about population ethics — he straight up accepts that a world with a gigantic number of people whose lives are barely worth living beats a world with a smaller number of extremely happy people, and he says anyone who denies it just can't do arithmatic. he's sharp, very comfortable with this stuff, and honestly kind of proud of how many bullets he's bitten, so i need real questions, not "tell us about your book" filler.

give me eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up. specifically i want:

- one that walks him up the whole mere-addition ladder one rung at a time (happy world, then the same world plus an extra population that's worse off but still doing okay, then flattening everyone to somewhere in between), forcing him to point at exactly which step he denies is an improvement
- one pressing on whether a life just barely above neutral really adds positive value the way his arithmetic says it does — the intuition that a life of muzak and potatoes doesn't make the world better merely by existing
- one about replacement: if two people each barely better off than neutral can outweigh one fantastic life, what stops painlessly removing very happy people and "replacing" them with more, slightly-less-happy ones from counting as an improvement
- one on the person-affecting angle — the feeling that nobody is wronged by never being created at all — and whether he thinks he can explain that away or just has to declare it confused
- one asking whether he'd bite the wireheading bullet too, billions of drugged-up barely-content lives vs fewer genuinely flourishing ones, since it looks like the same math
- one about the impossibility results people in this literature keep invoking, and which of the individually plausible conditions he thinks he's entitled to just drop

for each question, include the follow-up keyed to his most likely reply — e.g., if he leans on "good is good, quantity counts," press on what the world actually looks like from the inside for one of those barely-positive people, or whether he'd notice the difference if we quietly swapped our world for that one. keep the questions conversational enough that i can actually read them out loud without sounding like a textbook.
