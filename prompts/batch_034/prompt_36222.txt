I'm writing a paper for my Theory of Knowledge class on the Liar paradox. My teacher explained that a sentence like "this sentence is false" cannot consistently be called true or false, and she mentioned that some philosophers respond by saying such sentences are neither true nor false. That got me thinking: if a sentence is neither true nor false, it seems natural to say it is partially true — perhaps exactly half true — the way a coin that is still spinning has a 50% chance of landing heads. So it seems like the paradox is really just telling us that truth comes in degrees rather than being a light switch, and the old assumption that every sentence is fully true or fully false was a simplification we can now drop. If that's right, why do philosophers treat the Liar as a deep problem instead of simply concluding that truth is a spectrum?
