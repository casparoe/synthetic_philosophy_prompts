I run a uni reading group on AI safety — about 14 people, a mix of philosophy and CS students, and we've got a 90-minute slot each fortnight. Next session is on the side-effect problem: how do you get an agent to avoid wrecking things you never thought to mention, without first having to hand it a complete specification of everything you value? The discussion has been decent but I want them to feel why this is genuinely hard rather than nod along. Design an activity where they play the agents and I (or a co-facilitator) play the human issuing an incomplete goal.

My rough idea, which you can improve on or bin entirely: some kind of physical errand staged in the seminar room, or a paper grid-world if that works better, where I publish a fixed list of measurable side effects with point penalties (objects knocked over, consumables used up, walls damaged, that sort of thing). Teams compete to complete my errand with the lowest penalty score, and the trap is that the published list quietly leaves out something everyone would agree matters — so a team can follow the measure perfectly, optimise it exactly as intended, and still do something visibly bad. Then a second round where I reveal the unlisted harm, let them patch the measure, and they discover that every patch leaves a fresh gap somewhere else. I want the loophole-finding to come from them, not from me — the moment where a team is smug about gaming my scoring, and then realises what that smugness implies about agents cleverer than they are, is honestly the whole point of the session.

For the debrief: map each round onto the real territory (baseline choices, penalising lost ability to achieve other goals, the fact that any measure is a proxy for a value function we can't write down) without it becoming a lecture, and be frank about which part of the actual problem a 90-minute room game can't reproduce — the part that has to come from me afterwards. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and lets keep it to cheap materials, nothing that needs real money or more than one helper. Sorry in advance if the premise sounds half-baked — I've never designed anything like this and recieve very mixed reviews on my facilitation.
