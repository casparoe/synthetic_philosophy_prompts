I'm a professional poker player — about fifteen years in, mostly mid-stakes no-limit hold'em, some PLO, and I've done my share of sports betting on the side. So my native vocabulary is expected value, variance, risk of ruin, bankrole requirements, game selection, GTO vs exploitative play, tilt, shot-taking. I never read philosophy. A friend told me Machiavelli's fortune chapter (ch. 25 of The Prince) is basically about how much of life is luck, and it sounded exactly like an argument I've been having with my backer about whether I should leave a soft home game to take shots at higher stakes. So here's my ask.

Translate Machiavelli's whole fortune/virtù thing into my vocabulary. Not a summary with poker metaphors sprinkled on top — a real claim-by-claim translation. The opening claim that fortune is the arbiter of about half of what we do; the comparison between the cautious man and the impetuous man, where the cautious man only succeeds when the times fit his caution and the impetuous man only when the times fit his impetuosity; the argument that fortune favors boldness; the river metaphor, where fortune is like a river in flood that destroys things wherever it isn't resisted, and virtù is the dikes and embankments you build in the dry season; and the part where he says a man can't really change his nature, so the man who errs is the one who can't adapt when conditions shift, even though adapting is usually beyond him. Where does each claim land in my terms — EV, variance, edge, game selection, risk of ruin? Where do you have to distort Machiavelli to make him fit — e.g., is "fortune favors the bold" anything more than "in a game with positive edge, playing bigger raises your expected growth and your risk of ruin at the same time," and does he even distinguish those two things?

Then the residue report, which is what I actually care about:

1. He says fortune governs roughly half our actions. In my world, we'd say variance dominates any finite sample but the long run washes it out. As far as I can tell he has no long run at all. Is that just because politics is one lifetime and the law of large numbers never shows up — essentially a game with absorbing ruin, like playing with your whole net worth on the table — or is he claiming something stronger that I'd actually want to reject, like the distribution itself isn't stationary, the game keeps changing under you?

2. Is virtù just edge — skill expressed as expectation per hand — or is it closer to game selection, being in the right soft game at the right time, or is it something that has no real expression in my vocabulary at all? Be honest about which, and tell me what gets lost in whatever you pick.

3. The part that bugs me most: he seems to want both that impetuosity beats caution when the times favor it, and that you can't change what you are. In my terms that's like saying the nit who printed money in the soft 2009 games can't loosen up when the games toughen, and knowing in advance that your style has a shelf life should maybe change which style you pick in the first place. That move — choosing your nature partly for how it will age — feels like it has no clean poker expression. Does that go in the residue too, and if so, why?

Finish by telling me straight: is the residue there because my vocabulary is missing something Machiavelli saw, or because Machiavelli is less clear than he looks from the poker side of the table? Plain language, and don't let any philosophy jargon go by untranslated.
