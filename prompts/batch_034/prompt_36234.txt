My sister says the whole "AI is eroding understanding" panic is incoherent and she ran this argument on me at dinner and I still can't find where it breaks. (1) You understand something only if you can produce or check it yourself, instead of just taking it from an outside source. (2) More and more of my schoolwork comes from a chatbot that I mostly take on faith — essay drafts, problem setups, even which sources are worth reading in the first place. (3) So by (1) my understanding of my own schoolwork is eroding, which is basically the panic in one line. (4) But the same is true of everything else I "know": I believe Antarctica is there without ever having gone, I believe my antibiotics work because other people ran the trials, I believe half of what I know about my own grandpa because he told me — every fact I have arrived on somebody else's word somewhere. (5) So either almost nobody has ever understood anything, or (1) is false. (6) An "understanding" that almost no human has ever had isnt what anyone means by understanding. (7) So (1) is false, and there was never anything there for AI to erode — the whole worry is a category error. My problem is the thing seems to prove way too much. On her logic my grandpa doesn't understand the war he actually fought in, since plenty of what he knows about it came from other soldiers' stories. But also (2) and (3) still feel right to me — I honestly write worse than I did two years ago, and it feels like more than just being out of practice. I'd like to keep both "something real is being lost" and "taking things on people's word isn't the same as not understanding." Which step do I have to reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
