I spent thirty-five years as a microbiologist, most of it working on bacterial endospores, first in food safety and later in decontamination research. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of biology for pleasure, and I keep encountering something from my working life that I cannot find a name for.

A mature spore is, at every instant you might examine it, as inert as a grain of dust: no metabolism, no growth, no response to any stimulus. Yet the very same object, given time and suitable conditions, does what only living things do. And the interval of waiting can be extraordinary; over my career I watched serious people argue about whether revival claims from material millions of years old deserved scrutiny, and no one thought the age itself settled anything. The same pattern appears with seeds from herbarium collections germinating after a century, and with tardigrades dried to a glass and rehydrated a decade later.

Here is the pattern I want a name for. Nothing about the spore at any single moment makes it alive; whatever makes it alive is spread across a stretch of time, over the whole trajectory from dormancy through germination. It seems that being alive is not a property of a thing at a time but of a thing over a time. And the required stretch varies enormously: a fraction of a second suffices for a bacterium that never stops metabolizing, while millennia are needed for a spore. When my colleagues argued about whether a spore is really alive, I now suspect they were arguing about which clock to use, without noticing that this was the disagreement.

I can see this might be related to dispositions, such as solubility or flammability, but a disposition seems to be a state that guarantees a manifestation under the right conditions, whereas there appears to be no privileged moment at which the spore's disposition is present and the clock can start. Is there an established concept or literature for properties that belong to a trajectory rather than to a momentary state, or for this kind of timescale-relativity? If there is, give the term, define it precisely, and explain exactly how my case fits it and where it pulls away. If there is not, coin a term, define it, and work through the boundary cases. I will not be satisfied with a bare label.
