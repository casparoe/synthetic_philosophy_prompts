My thought: Kim's exclusion argument only works if the physical cause and the mental cause are competitors for the same causal job, and I suspect that if you take contrastive causation seriously -- causes as answers to questions like "why p rather than q" rather than just "why p" -- the competition evaporates. The physical realizer of my raising my arm answers different contrast questions than my desire to signal does: the desire explains why my arm went up rather than stayed down, say, while the neural realizer might be what explains why this tokening happened rather than a slightly different one. Same effect, but if the explanatory work is carved up by contrast, "overdetermination" by the physical may be no more real overdetermination than a shadow being overdetermined by the flagpole and the sun.
