Me and a friend have this long-running thing where she calls stuff socially constructed or not, and I've kept a list of her verdicts over the years. Some new cases have come up and I want to predict what she'll say before I ask, because I've started to suspect she isn't running just one rule.

Her record so far:

- money: constructed (her gloss: it only works because everyone goes along with it)
- race: constructed (her gloss: the underlying variation is real, but the boxes are ours)
- gender: constructed
- mountains: not constructed (they'd be there with or without us)
- gold: not constructed (it's an element, it is what it is regardless of anyone)
- the flu: not constructed (the virus is just out there doing its thing)
- weeds: constructed (no weed-fact in nature, a weed is just a plant in the wrong place relative to a garden)
- chess: constructed, but she says this one differently, like "that one's just made up, doesnt even need the word"

The new cases, none of which shes ruled on:

1. Brain death. The biology is real, but the criteria for when someone counts as dead got written by committees, in an era when transplant surgeons wanted clarity about when organs could be taken. Is the kind "dead" out there like the flu virus, or is the cutoff ours?
2. Species. Nature obviously produces the variation, but the line between one species and the next is famously a judgement call — lumpers and splitters disagree, and the boundary gets set by convention. Does she say the kind lion is out there like gold, or boxed in like race?
3. The mean high-water line. In a lot of coastal places the law says private property ends where the average high tide reaches, so who owns the dry sand depends on a number a surveyor computes from years of tide charts. The tides are clearly not ours; the line on the chart clearly is. Constructed or not?
4. ADHD. A category that didn't exist under that name a few decades ago, whose criteria have been rewritten several times, and where getting the label seems to change how people behave and think of themselves.

What will she say on each? Honestly I'm less interested in the four answers than in what rule shes actually running. To me it looks like she might be running two rules at once without noticing — one for things made up out of nothing (chess, money) and one for things that are there anyway but get boxed by us (race, weeds) — and I can't tell which one she'd extend to the new cases. Or maybe its genuinely one rule and I'm missing what it is. So: what are the candidate rules that fit her record, what does each of them predict for the four new cases, which of the four does the best job of splitting the rules apart, what's your best guess overall and why, and if you could ask her one further case to settle which rule she's really on, what would it be?
