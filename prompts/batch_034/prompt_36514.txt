My metaphysics reading group has this one guy whose favorite move, deployed roughly every meeting, is that there's no such thing as a chair (or a table, or a ship) -- there are only particles "arranged chair-wise." Last week he used it to shut down my question about whether a chair survives being shipped flat-packed and reassembled in my new apartment. His line was: since there's no chair in the first place, there's no fact of the matter about chair-survival, only facts about where particles end up. Everyone nodded along like this settled it.

I have a feeling the slogan is doing a lot less work than he thinks, but I can't quite pin down why, and I'm supposed to write a short paper on it. So what are the actually distinct claims that could be packed into "just particles arranged chair-wise"? I can think of some candidate readings but I want them laid out properly:

- the flat eliminativist one, where chairs genuinely don't exist -- but then why do "there's a chair in the corner" and its negation seem so obviously true vs. false?
- a paraphrase story where chair-talk comes out true but ontologically cheap, so ordinary sentences are fine and only the metaphysics changes
- some kind of plural reference thing, where "the chair" just names many particles at once
- a denial of one specific answer to the special composition question while quietly ducking the question itself

For each reading I want a verdict: is it trivially true, substantive and defensible, substantive but probably wrong, or just confused. And the part I actually care about: which readings, if any, actually get him his conclusion that there's no fact of the matter about whether my flat-packed chair survived the move? My hunch is that on several of these readings the survival question comes straight back as a perfectly good question about the particles -- reworded, not dissolved -- but maybe I'm missing a reading where it genuinely evaporates.

Also, if you think the phrase "arranged chair-wise" is itself sneaky -- like it smuggles in exactly the chair-facts it claims to have dispensed with, since being arranged chair-wise is presumably a fact about a bunch of particles that is somehow more than just a list of their positions -- please say so, that feels like it might be the real crux and I want to make sure I'm not building the paper on a misreading.
