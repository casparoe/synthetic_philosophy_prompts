Two philosophers of truth have agreed to an adversarial collaboration after a decade of arguing past each other. The first — call her the gap theorist — holds that Liar sentences such as "This sentence is not true" are neither true nor false, and that a language with a transparent truth predicate should be governed by a paracomplete, strongly-Kleene-style logic in the spirit of Kripke's fixed-point construction, where sentences stuck at the bottom value escape classical reasoning. The second — the glut theorist — holds that Liar sentences are both true and false, that this restricted class of contradictions is to be tolerated rather than gapped away, and that the correct logic is paraconsistent, in the spirit of Priest's dialetheism, so that explosion fails. Both reject the classical logician's exit of restricting the truth rules, climbing Tarski's hierarchy, or abandoning transparency; both want truth to be fully intersubstitutable in the object language. They have hired you to draft the document their collaboration should produce, with the following components.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims: what the semantic paradoxes demonstrably show (treat Tarski's undefinability theorem and the inconsistency of naive truth principles with full classical logic as settled mathematics, not opinion), what both owe the classical logician, and what counts as a successful theory on criteria they share — transparency, compositionality, non-triviality, and an account of why classical reasoning is so dependable for paradox-free sentences. Second, a disagreements table: each remaining point of divergence, phrased so that the named party would accept the wording of its own view, including what "the Liar lacks a truth value" versus "the Liar has too many truth values" really amounts to. Third, the cruxes, each paired with a shared test case and each side's prediction recorded in advance: (a) the revenge problem, e.g. the sentence that says of itself that it is not true in whatever sense covers gaps; (b) Curry's paradox, e.g. the sentence that says that if it is true, the butler did it, and which side's treatment of the conditional does more damage; (c) classical recapture, where the glut theorist must explain why we may still trust disjunctive syllogism and reductio in ordinary mathematics and the gap theorist must explain why excluded middle was so reliable there; (d) whether iterating "determinately" or "is true" over the Liar forces either side into the very hierarchy they rejected. Finally, a short closing section in which they state, jointly, which parts of their dispute they concede are merely verbal — for instance, whether the two camps are describing the same fixed-point structure with different labels for the pathological sentences — and which parts are genuinely substantive.

Throughout the document, mark carefully what is settled (a theorem, a fixed-point construction, an equivalence everyone grants) versus what is contested among specialists (whether dialetheism is even coherent, whether the revenge problem is fatal, whether a non-classical conditional can be made serviceable), and do not present any contested interpretive view as the consensus of the field. The two parties will sign the document, so neither side's position may be stated in wording the other side chose to be tendentious.
