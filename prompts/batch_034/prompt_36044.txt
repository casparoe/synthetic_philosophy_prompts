Write a dialogue between a subjectivist about probability and a single-case propensity theorist. Setting: the two of them are grabbing coffee after touring a physics lab, where they've just watched a qubit get prepared in an equal superposition and measured exactly once before the whole rig gets dismantled for parts — so there is deliberately no long run of trials to point to. The propensity person thinks the 50/50 is a real physical disposition of that one setup, as much a fact about the apparatus as its weight; the subjectist thinks that's metaphysical decoration and the number only makes sense as somebody's degree of belief, with the physics just telling you what rational credence to have. Have them actually engage with each other's points rather than lecturing in turns. At some point they should get into whether a bent coin with an unknown bias has a "true" probability of heads before anyone's gathered any frequency data (the subjectist wants to say 50% is fine given ignorance, the propensity person wants to say there's a fact of the matter they're both ignoring), and also whether the propensity view can even say anything when the setup is genuinely one-of-a-kind — the subjectist should bring up something like the probability that a particular startup succeeds, where there's no repeatable mechanism to attribute the disposition to. The subjectist should also press on how you'd ever test or measure a single-case propensity without smuggling frequencies back in, and the propensity person should press back on what the subjectist's numbers actually constrain — if two people walk out of the lab with different priors, are they both just right? Give both of them good licks, no strawmen. Keep it conversational and a little competitive — old collegues who enjoy needling each other, not debate club captains.
