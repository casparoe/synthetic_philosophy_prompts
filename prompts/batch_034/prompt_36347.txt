Three months ago I donated a kidney to a stranger, and at the hospital's small recognition event the coordinator described what I had done as "beyond the call of duty." I smiled at the time, but since then I have realized that I genuinely do not know whether my own philosophical commitments entitle me to accept that compliment, and the question has stopped being academic for me. Here is roughly where I stand, assembled from years of thinking about these issues only in passing: (1) act consequentialism in a maximizing form is correct — an act is wrong if and only if some available alternative would have produced more overall good; (2) the demandingness objection to consequentialism is confused: a theory's implying that buying espresso is wrong whenever the money could do more good elsewhere is a cost of the theory, not a refutation, and my intuitions of permission at that level carry little evidential weight; (3) supererogation is a genuine moral category — some acts are good and praiseworthy yet not obligatory, and someone who omits them does no wrong; (4) morality is at bottom scalar: the fundamental moral facts are facts about better and worse, and the deontic vocabulary of "wrong" and "required" is at best a crude summary of them; (5) praise is fitting in degrees that track more than rightness — the donor of a kidney merits a kind and intensity of praise that the merely dutiful person does not, and that fittingness is itself a moral fact; and (6) an easy-rescue principle along Singer's lines: if you can prevent something very bad without sacrificing anything of comparable moral importance, you are obligated to do it. My utilitarian friend says the donation was simply required of me, and my Aristotelian-leaning sister says it was admirably supererogatory, and I find I cannot adjudicate between them without knowing whether my own view set is even coherent. Please audit these six commitments: tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally grave. For each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me elsewhere in the set. And since the matter is no longer idle: conclude by telling me plainly whether, on the most tenable reconstruction of my views, the coordinator's compliment was one I was entitled to accept.
