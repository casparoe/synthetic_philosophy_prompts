I teach a graduate seminar in philosophy of mind, and last week I opened with an old toy world of Dennett's, the careenium. As I present it, it is a walled, frictionless table on which simmballs — hard little spheres — careen about, obeying nothing but Newtonian collision mechanics. There is nothing else in the world. Yet at a coarser grain of description the whole system is playing chess: the opponent's moves arrive as balls injected through chutes in the walls, the system's replies are the balls that exit through other chutes, and the pattern of exits conforms perfectly to legal, strong chess play. A theorist who tracked only collisions could in principle predict the exits, but only at astronomical computational cost; a theorist working at the chess level predicts them nearly perfectly and cheaply, from the board position alone. Afterward, over coffee, two visitors to the seminar fell into a dispute about whether the symbols push the substrate around, and I must admit I could not settle it for myself.

The exchange ran as follows. (1) Vera, a computer scientist, contended that the chess level is causally real: to explain why the next ball left through the d-file chute you cite the position — the zugzwang, the pinned knight — because that description is what supports prediction and control, and whatever earns its keep in our best causal models is a cause; so the game state does push the simmballs around. (2) Tomas, a visiting metaphysician, answered that every exit is already fixed, completely, by the prior positions and momenta of the simmballs; the chess state either is that configuration, in which case it adds nothing, or is something over and above it, in which case it does nothing — the symbols never touch a bumper. (3) Vera protested that she never posited a chess force: the position simply is the configuration of simmballs under a coarse-graining, and causation is always grain-relative; the temperature of a gas is nothing over and above molecular kinetic energy, yet it is the temperature, not any single molecule's momentum, that pops the lid. (4) Tomas replied that temperature earns its causal status because we can intervene on it directly — but every route to a different position runs through the simmballs, and there is no chess knob; the counterfactuals hold only because altered positions just are altered ball-configurations, so the work is all done at the substrate. (5) Vera, escalating: if Tomas is right, then genes, beliefs, and depressions push nothing, and only fundamental physics ever does any work — which she took to be a reductio, since explanation in terms of higher-level states is not mere heuristic bookkeeping. (6) Tomas, unruffled: perhaps explanation is indeed grain-relative, but explanation is one thing and pushing another; Vera has shown that the chess level explains, never that it moves anything; the phrase "push the substrate around" smuggles in production, and only bumpers produce.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move, and then take a definite stand on the underlying question — in the careenium, do the symbols push the substrate around? — rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me. Say where each of the six moves goes right and where it goes wrong, note anything both parties are missing, and keep the whole thing under 600 words.
