My pragmatics professor used Hemingway's Hills Like White Elephants as her day-one example of implicature: the man and the girl (Jig, he calls her) waiting for the train at the station in the Ebro valley, talking about beers and licorice-tasting new drinks and the hills, while the actual subject — an operation the man keeps calling simple — never gets named. She said it's a textbook case of flouting the maxim of relevance: they stay on the surface topic and we infer the real one. Fine, I get that. But I'm writing my term paper on it and the more I stare at Grice the less sure I am that the story is a Gricean case at all, and I'd like you to read the story through Grice properly and tell me whether my worry is real or whether I'm about to embarrass myself in section.

Here's the worry. For Grice, an implicature is something the speaker intends the hearer to work out, on the presumption that the speaker is still cooperating at some deeper level — that's what makes it calculable rather than just guesswork. But Jig already knows exactly what they're not saying. The man isn't implicating the operation to her, since implicating something the hearer already fully knows seems pointless on Grice's own account — he's instead using the not-saying as pressure, keeping it deniable while he wears her down with reassurances that everything will be fine afterwards. And Jig's side isn't cooperative either — her remarks about the hills and trying new things read as moves in a resistance, not contributions to a shared conversational goal, and by the end she's just begging him to stop talking. So maybe what the story dramatizes isn't implicature but something Grice's framework doesn't have a slot for: two people jointly not-saying something, collusive evasion, where the shared unspoken thing is common ground that never needs to be recovered by anyone. There's also the fiction problem: Grice's speaker meaning runs on intentions, and Hemingway's narrator reports almost no inner states, so what are we even attributing implicatures to — the characters' intentions the text witholds from us?
