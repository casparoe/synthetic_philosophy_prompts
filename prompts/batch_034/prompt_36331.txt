so I'm a backend engineer who got into lucid dreaming during a sleep-tracking kick, and now I can't stop noticing something weird about how the field works. sleep researchers collect dream reports as basically all of their data -- wake someone up during REM, ask them what was happening, write it down. but everyone also knows dream reports are wildly unreliable, that people confabulate a ton on waking, and at least one philosopher (Dennett I think) floated the idea that a dream might get composed at the moment of telling rather than experienced during sleep. meanwhile another philosopher, Malcolm, apparently argued dream reports can't verify anything about sleep at all. and then on top of that you've got the lucid dreaming stuff, where subjects agree on an eye-signal beforehand and move their eyes in that pattern while still asleep, and the lab can see it on the equipment -- the one case where a "dream" gets independently confirmed in real time.

so my question: what does this whole setup presuppose about dreams? does taking dream reports as data presuppose that dreams are experiences that actually occur during sleep and are later remembered, rather than stories the brain assembles at wake-up? and does the eye-signal trick then quietly smuggle in a verification standard that nothing else in the field meets -- like, are lucid dreams being treated as dreams but with a special epistemic status the ordinary ones don't have? reconstruct the implicit theory of dream experience and dream evidence the practice rests on, say whether it's coherent or whether treating reports as data and treating reports as confabulation-prone pull in opposite directions, and tell me what would actually have to change in lab practice if the Dennett-ish "composed on waking" view were right -- or would nothing have to change, which would honestly be its own interesting answer
