My friend Dev and I got into a really long argument about animal ethics the other night, and I've since realized I've committed myself to a view I can't fully defend, so I want someone to stress-test it. The view: sentience comes in degrees, and moral status comes in degrees right along with it. A chimp matters more than a chicken, a chicken more than a shrimp, a shrimp more than a nematode, and instead of pretending there's a magic line where moral status switches on, we should just accept the gradient and do the arithmetic. I'm even willing to bite the bullet going down: any system with something like nociception plus valuation plus a little self-model has *some* moral weight, however tiny, and I honestly can't rule out that a thermostat has an absurdly small nonzero status. That feels fine to me because absurdly small is small.

What would actually count against this? And to be clear, I don't want the standard "so where do you draw the line?" objection, which I think misses the point -- the whole view is that there's no line to draw. I want the stuff that should genuinely keep me up at night: cases the view can't describe without embarrassment, consequences it's committed to that might be unacceptable, ways it quietly collapses into one of its rivals (like a threshold view in disguise, or total permissiveness).

The thing nagging me most is aggregation. If status is graded all the way down, doesn't some enormous number of barely-sentient beings outweigh one human? Dev kept pushing this and I kept patching it by saying moral weight probably scales superlinearly with degree of sentience -- like the square of it, or some power -- so a million very mildly sentient things never add up to a person. But I can't tell if that's principled or just me working backwards from the conclusions I wanted, and I've noticed it creates a symmetric problem at the top: if the scaling is steep enough, doesn't it justify sacrificing a bunch of almost-maximally-sentient beings for one entity a bit more sentient still? Is there any way to pick a scaling that dodges both problems, or is the whole "moral weight as a function of sentience" framework the thing I should abandon?

Anyway. Give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Say which ones I, as a committed gradist (is that a word?), would concede count against the view and which I'd wave away, and whether my waving-away is legitimate or cope. Also curious if any actual observation or discovery could settle it -- like if nociception turned out to be trivially easy to implement, or insects turned out to have surprisingly centralized valenced processing, or someone built a system with all the functional markers of pain. And be honest about whether my view is risking anything at all, or whether the "absurdly small is small" move has insulated it from every possible world, and whether that insulation is fatal for a view of this kind or just how it goes in ethics. Sorry for the rant, this argument has been living in my head rent free all week.
