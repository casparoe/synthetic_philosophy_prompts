I'm a distributed systems engineer with no philosophy background but a solid grip on databases and event-driven architecture — event sourcing, append-only logs, MVCC, snapshots and materialized views, logical clocks, read replicas and replication lag, bitemporal valid-time vs transaction-time. I've gathered that philosophers argue about whether only the present exists (presentism) or whether past, present, and future things are all equally real (eternalism), with some mediating position called the moving spotlight theory, and I want to know what's actually at stake in terms I can evaluate. Restate this debate in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report.

For the translation itself: I can already half-see how you'd do it — the eternalist's "block universe" as an immutable, append-only event log where nothing is ever overwritten, and the presentist as someone insisting that only the latest materialized snapshot is real, with the log kept only for reconstructing state. The moving spotlight would then be the write head or cursor advancing along the log, and the "flow of time" the fact that the LSN keeps incrementing. But I suspect each of those mappings either cheats or breaks, so work through them carefully rather than gesturing at them. In particular, tell me honestly whether the "now" in my systems — a session's current timestamp, an offset, a replica's applied-up-to position — is doing anything like the work the philosophical "now" is supposed to do, given that mine is always relative to an observer or session while the presentist seems to think theirs isn't. And tell me whether the B-theory's claim that pastness and futurity are just relational facts maps onto anything like my distinction between what the log records and when it records it, or whether that bitemporal analogy is a fudge.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which parts of the philosophical dispute translate cleanly into questions I face — retention policies, truncation, whether an unreplayed log segment "exists"? Which parts survive only with distortion — I'm thinking of the presentist's claim that there simply are no dinosaurs, which seems stronger than anything I mean by having dropped a log; and the truthmaker worry that a true statement about Caesar needs something in the ontology to make it true, which my world answers with "the events are still on disk" but which the presentist apparently can't? And which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all — the felt flow of time, if that's a datum, or whatever it is about "now" that's supposed to make it more than a distinguished offset? Finally, give me your view on which way the residue cuts: does it show that my vocabulary is missing something real, or that the philosophers are arguing about something that turns out, once translated, to be the difference between a fact about a system and a fact about how the system represents itself — or is that a glib way of ending the argument I shouldn't be allowed to get away with? I'd rather you were hard on my framing than polite about it.
