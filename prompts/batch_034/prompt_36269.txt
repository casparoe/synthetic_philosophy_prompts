For the past twelve years I've worked as a pediatric oncology nurse. On every external measure I excel at caring for my patients and their families: I remember birthdays, anticipate needs before they're voiced, get requested by name, and am regularly told by parents that I'm the most caring person on the ward. But inside I feel almost nothing anymore — I sometimes catch myself describing my work as "managing the dying and their relatives" — even though I still feel full, easy tenderness toward my own children at home. A colleague who's done some reading in philosophy told me I've "stopped caring" and become merely a very reliable duty-follower, and that the families are being deceived. That stung, partly because I can't see what exactly they'd be deceived about: everything they experience as care is fully present. Is she right that something morally significant is missing — and if so, what is it, given that the only thing absent is my inner feeling? And what should I make of the fact that the feeling survives intact for my own family but not for strangers' children?
