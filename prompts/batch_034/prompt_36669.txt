I am reading a trade book on comparative ethics that summarizes the Confucian role ethics reading of early Confucian thought — the interpretation developed by Roger Ames and Henry Rosemont — roughly as follows: "Confucianism rejects the Western ideal of the autonomous individual. There is no inner self standing behind one's social positions: a person just is the totality of the roles into which they are born and educated — son, father, minister, friend. Because what is right is always right for someone in a role, and different roles carry different expectations, Confucian ethics is a form of moral relativism: there are no standards that hold across roles or cultures, only the particular duties of each station. And since the tradition prizes harmony above rules, the ultimate test of conduct is whether it preserves social harmony, which makes Confucianism, for all its talk of ritual, a straightforward consequentialism with harmony standing in for aggregate utility."
