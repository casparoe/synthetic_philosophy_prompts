My friend has firm views about when bringing a person into existence harms that person, and she says they come from Benatar's asymmetry: the absence of pain is good even when there is no one for whom it is good, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone is thereby deprived. Her record of verdicts so far: creating a child guaranteed a life of unrelieved agony with no compensating goods — a harm and wrong; creating a child guaranteed an unusually happy life containing only the trivial pains everyone gets — not a harm and permissible; a couple who could have conceived such a happy child but chose not to — no wrong done to anyone; stopping an existing friend from a pleasure you know they would relish — a harm to them; creating a child guaranteed a permanently dreary life of constant low-grade misery and loneliness, never anything intense — a harm and wrong. She has now been asked about a couple any child of whom would have a genetic condition guaranteeing, within an otherwise very happy life that clears any decent threshold, one sustained period of severe suffering in the child's twenties, a period the person, once existing, would come to endorse as part of a life worth having. Will she say that creating such a child harms it? I care less about the answer than about the rules that could fit her record. One candidate makes everything turn on whether the life clears a threshold; another, closer to the asymmetry she claims to follow, treats any guaranteed serious suffering as a harm that added pleasure cannot offset. Please say what each rule predicts for this case, note where her record already sits uneasily with the asymmetry as she states it, give your best guess with your reasons, and describe the further case that would best separate the rules.
