Here's something I've been chewing on and want a real essay on, not a hedge. You know the old idea that conceivability is evidence of possibility — if I can coherently imagine something, maybe it could be real. The zombie case in philosophy of mind gets all the attention, but I want something weirder.

Imagine a physisist tells you this: there could be particles just like ours — same charge, same mass, same spin, interact exactly like ours do — but which are, by stipulation, not our particles. Call them mirror particles. And she says she can conceive of a molecule built entirely out of mirror particles that is structurally identical to water: it boils the same, dissolves salt the same, passes every chemical test we know how to run. It just isn't water — it's a different substance with an identical structure, the way two books can be word-for-word the same without being the same book.

Three readings suggest themselves. (1) She's conceiving a genuine possibility, and worlds with duplicate-substances are real possible worlds. (2) She's fooling herself: since water is H2O is knowable a posteriori but necessary, there's simply no room for this stuff — the scenario is only pseudo-conceivable, like imagining Hesperus and Phosphorus being different objects. (3) It's a third thing: the scenario is conceivable only in the negative sense (she can't spot a contradiction when she thinks it through) but not in the positive sense of actually, in detail, picturing how mirror matter would work — she's riding on the word "same."

Write an essay on whether conceivabilty should guide modal judgments in this kind of case. Weigh the three readings (and anything else you think is live), but land on a verdict — I don't want a shrug at the end. Somewhere in there, deal with whether the negative/positive distinction, or the idea of ideal conceivability (what a fully rational, logically omniscient version of us would find coherent), rescues the conceivability principle or just explains away its failures after the fact. Also tell me whether you think the mirror-molecule case is importantly different from the standard zombies and Twin Earth cases people usually argue about, or whether it's the same dispute in a lab coat.

Please answer in American English, and feel free to keep the tone loose — I'm not a journal, I'm a guy on a porch with a coffee.
