I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind with a background in cognitive science, and I have about three years of dissertation work ahead of me. I want to work on explanation and reduction in the sciences of mind, and I'm framing the field through Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks: good explanations are built up from known mechanisms, while bad ones invoke unexplained explainers, and "greedy reductionism" — leaping to a mechanism while skipping the intermediate levels — discredits honest crane-building by association. Given that framing, which open questions in this area are most worth three years of my effort, and in what order?

Some candidates I'm aware of, to react to: whether integrated information theory is a skyhook dressed as mathematics or a legitimate crane still under construction; whether predictive-processing accounts of cognition explain anything or mostly restate the data at a higher level of abstraction; whether the hard problem of consciousness is a demand for a crane where only a skyhook could satisfy, or a symptom of greedy reductionism in disguise; and whether large-scale connectionist systems, including today's language models, give philosophy its first real test case for asking when a mechanistic explanation of a mental capacity is complete.

Please rank the two or three questions you'd actually prioritize, defend the ordering in terms of importance, tractability for a philosopher (rather than a working scientist), and how much other work upstream or downstream depends on each, and tell me which of my candidate framings you'd discard as confused. I care more about the reasoning behind the ordering than the list itself. I'd also like you to say explicitly which consideration would most shift your ranking if it went the other way, and where in your prioritization you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
