Hiya, so I'm in year 12 and our philosophy club has been reading Dennett's short story thing "Where Am I?" (the one where his brain gets taken out and put in a vat, with a radio link back to his body, so he can go disarm a nuclear device without risking his brain). I've written a first draft of an essay on it and honestly I don't know if my main idea is clever or complete rubbish, so I'd really appreciate some comments on it. Two things I want feedback on specifically: (1) whether I've got Dennett right, especially about where he ends up saying "he" is located once the body is destroyed and it's just the brain in the vat getting signals fed to it, and (2) whether my own twist actually works or whether it just shows I've misunderstood the whole thought experiment.

Here's the draft (excuse it being a bit rough): Dennett starts off assuming he'll be wherever his brain is, but the story is meant to show this is wrong — he keeps feeling like he's wherever his point of view is, i.e. with his body (Hamlet, as he calls it) looking out through its eyes, even though his brain (Yorick) is sitting in a vat miles away. By the end, when the body is destroyed and the signals are just being played to the brain, and then even when there's a copied brain in Fort Worth getting the same inputs, the question stops having a single right answer, and that's the real point: "where I am" isn't a fact about which lump of matter is me, it's more like a practical question about where it makes sense to treat me as being for the purposes we care about.

Now my twist, which is the bit I'm unsure about. Suppose the radio link between Yorick and Hamlet had a five-minute delay, so everything Dennett saw and felt had actually happened five minutes before he experienced it. It seems like he'd be living five minutes in the past — so is he located where his body is now, or where the events he's experiencing took place five minutes ago? And then suppose you shrink the delay gradually: at a five minute lag it feels obvious he's "behind" his body somewhere, but at a 3 millisecond lag (which is roughly what real nerves do anyway!) nobody would say that. So somewhere in between, the answer changes from "he's lagging behind" to "he's just there", even though nothing suddenly happens at that point — no threshold, no new fact. I want to argue this shows Dennett's question doesn't have an answer that's out there waiting to be discovered, because any answer has to be drawn against a continuum where we'd all just shrug at the middle cases, which supports the pragmatic reading. My philosophy teacher says delaying the signals doesn't change where he is, only when he is, and that my argument confuses location with timing — is she right?? Because I sort of feel like "where am I" and "when am I experiencing stuff from" come apart exactly the same way, and that's the point. Also if you think the draft's central move is boring or has been done a million times, please say so, I'd rather know now than after handing it in — and tell me if any part of it reads like I'm just showing off. Thanks in advance!
