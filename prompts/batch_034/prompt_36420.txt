Ok heres my situation. I'm a journalist, I mostly cover science and ideas for a mid-sized outlet — physics and space usually, but my editor keeps handing me bio-adjacent stuff, and this month I'm doing a feature on the hunt for a definition of life. It started as two separate assignments that bled into each other: one is about a lab that builds self-replicating chemical systems (small molecules, no DNA, barely even a membrane — just reaction networks that copy themselves and drift around evolving in test tubes), and the other is about the long-running fight over whether giant viruses should count as alive, since they carry genes for their own metabolism-ish machinery and some of them even get parasitized by *other viruses*, which is a sentence I still can't quite believe I get paid to write.

So now I've got researchers telling me opposite things with total confidence. One told me the question is basically settled: life is just a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution, end of story, and people who fuss about viruses just don't like messy edges. Another said that definition is terrible precisely because it's parochial — it would miss anything that doesn't do Darwinian evolution the way we do, and if we search for alien life with an Earth checklist we'll walk write past it. A third, a chemist and very blunt about it, said the whole question is fake — that life is a folk word like weed, useful for gardening and useless for science, and asking whether a protocell is really alive is like asking whether Pluto is really a planet: a fight about us, not about the world.

My editor wants to close the piece with something like scientists are closing in on the true definition of life. My gut says that's wrong, but I can't articulate why, and the alternative — experts disagree! — is exactly the kind of both-sides cop-out I hate writing and readers can smell. So here's what I actually need from you: is there real substance in this fight that a lay reader should understand, or is it partly bookkeeping, and if it IS partly bookkeeping, how do I write that in a way that's genuinely interesting instead of just "words are hard, moving on"? Related practical question: when the self-replicating-systems lab's press release describes their creations as living, do I print that word, refuse it, hedge it, or is there some third move that doesn't make me the vocabulary police?
