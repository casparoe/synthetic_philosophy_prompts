Quick context: I share a house with three other guys, and for months we've had this recurring standoff where some chore needs doing (trash, dishes, whatever) and each of us individually decides whether to just do it or wait and hope someone else caves. Last month I read Hofstadter's Metamagical Themas stuff, the superrationality idea and the Luring Lottery experiment he ran in the magazine, and it honestly felt like it was written for me. The way I understand it: if you're in a symmetric dilemma with people who reason the way you do, you can treat your own decision as a sample of what everyone's deciding, so you should pick the move that's best when everyone like you picks it, instead of the game-theory move. So last week the kitchen was a disaster and I thought, the other three are basically facing the same situation I am and we all think roughly alike, so if I decide not to clean they'll decide not to clean too, and we're stuck in the defect-defect box. So I cleaned. Kitchen's clean. Felt like the system works.

Here's where it gets personally urgent. Me and another guy at work both put in for the same promotion, and the decision on who gets extra training slots etc. lands next month. I've been trying to run it the superrational way: whatever I do (study nights and weekends for the cert, going to the boss directly, whatever), he'll do the same thing, so I should pick the strategy that's best for both of us when we both do it. But my coworker is not a copy of me — different background, he's got kids so no weekend studying, and honestly he's more cutthroat than I am. So my question is: what's the actual cutoff? Like, how much do two people have to be alike, in reasoning style or situation, before superrationality kicks in and becomes valid? Is it something you can estimate, like a similarity score, where above it I should reason as if we'll mirror eachother and below it I fall back to normal game theory? And in the promotion case, does it even apply, since only one of us can win — if superrational reasoning says we'd both pick the same strategy, does that mean we should both pick "go all out," or both pick "split the effort," or does the whole thing break when the game is zero-sum?

I guess what I really want is a practical way to use this. If I could put even a rough number on the similarity threshold I could apply it to alot of stuff in my life — the chores, the promotion, even group buys with my roommates. Does anyone know if there's a worked-out version of this, like a sliding scale between fully rational and fully superrational? Or am I maybe drawing the line in the wrong place, and the question isn't similarity at all?
