Last Thanksgiving my older sister, who donated a kidney to a stranger last year, got a big toast at dinner, and then my aunt turned to my younger sister and asked when she was going to "step up" too. My younger sister said she didn't think she had any obligation to, and my aunt came back with: if it's such a good thing to do, how can it be fine to just not do it? My cousin, who teaches high school math, decided this was his moment and offered what he clearly thought was a checkmate: either giving the kidney is better than keeping it, in which case my younger sister ought to do it, or it isn't better, in which case nobody should have made a fuss over my older sister in the first place. He said the only escape is claiming it's better but you don't have to, which he called a contradiction with extra steps.

My older sister, of all people, jumped in on the younger one's side: the hole point of calling her act admirable was that it went beyond what anyone could demand of her, and if you take that away, praising her just means she did what she was told, which isn't the compliment anyone thinks it is. My aunt said fine, but then my younger sister doesn't get it both ways either — if the act is above the bar, my younger sister is below it and can be disapproved of, and if there's no bar at all, then nothing anyone does or doesn't do can ever be criticized, which my aunt said was obviously crazy. My cousin said that actually sounded right to him, and by the end I honestly couldn't tell who had won. It felt like they were partly arguing past eachother but I can't put my finger on where.

So: was my cousin's little dilemma as airtight as it sounded? And is my aunt right that you can't have it both ways — that if the donation gets to count as optional, she has to give up disapproving of people who skip it, but if she keeps the disapproval, then it wasn't optional? Please go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, tell me if there's something both sides were missing, and also flag for me which parts of this are basically settled among people who actually work on this stuff versus still genuinely fought over — I don't want to repeat any of it at Christmas dinner as if it's obvious when half the field might think it's wrong.
