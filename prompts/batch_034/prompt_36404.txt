I'm not a philosopher or an economist — I'm an accountant who has spent a few commutes thinking about how I'd choose between gambles, and I've ended up with a set of convictions I can't reconcile. I'd like an audit of them: which ones genuinely conflict, which only seem to, and what it would cost to fix things. Here they are.

(1) Offered either a guaranteed $1 million or a lottery with an 89% chance of $1 million, a 10% chance of $5 million, and a 1% chance of nothing, I take the guarantee — and I think I'm right to, not merely comfortable.

(2) Offered either an 11% chance of $1 million (otherwise nothing) or a 10% chance of $5 million (otherwise nothing), I take the 10% shot at the larger sum, and I'm just as firm about that.

(3) I hold that when two options produce the same outcome under some contingency, that contingency should be irrelevant to how I rank them: strip out the shared part, and the comparison shouldn't change.

(4) My caution in (1) comes entirely from the declining value of money to me — the second million would matter less than the first, and the fifth barely more than the second. I do not think certainty itself is something with a price; there is no premium I am paying just to feel safe.

(5) I believe a rational person's preferences must be transitive — if I prefer A to B and B to C, I had better prefer A to C, or else someone can bleed me dry through a chain of trades I'd each individually accept.

Which of these are in real tension, which merely appear to be, and what is the cheapest way to restore consistency? Please rank any genuine tensions by severity, say specifically what would have to give in each, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most. I don't know where the problem lies, so don't pull your punches on any of them. Keep it under 450 words.
