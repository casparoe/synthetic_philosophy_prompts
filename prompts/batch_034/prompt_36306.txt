When a split-brain patient's left hand grabs a shirt the right hand doesn't want, and the two hemispheres can be made to literally disagree with each other, aren't we watching two conscious subjects in one skull? That's what I don't get about the whole literature. If callosotomy splits a person into two, there should be a fact of the matter about which hemisphere was "the real one" before the surgery — the one that keeps the name, the memories, the legal identity. But how could anyone ever know that, given only one hemisphere can talk and just votes for itself? And if the surgery really creates a second person, why don't we treat it as something like a birth — shouldn't the new person get some kind of legal standing instead of just being stuck inside someone else's body? My roommate says the lesson is that we were two people all along even with an intact brain, and the corpus callosum just merges them so smoothly we never notice, but that seems like it proves too much. Which of us is confusing something here?
