I'd like a consistency check on my views in Bayesian confirmation theory before I present them at a reading group. Here is my philosophical profile, in six commitments:

(1) Confirmation is probabilistic through and through: evidence E confirms hypothesis H for an agent just in case E raises H's conditional probability relative to the agent's background beliefs, and rational credence functions satisfy the probability calculus without exception.

(2) The classic historical cases really are cases of confirmation. General relativity's accounting for the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion — data that had been on the books for decades before 1915 — genuinely and strongly confirmed the theory, and any account of confirmation on which it did not is thereby refuted.

(3) The right way to vindicate (2) is the counterfactual-credence strategy: we index the relevant probability assignment to the epistemic situation of a reasoner who holds our background theories but does not yet know E, and we ask whether, in that situation, E would raise H's probability. The agent's actual credence function, with E already conditioned in, is simply the wrong instrument for the job.

(4) Idealizing Bayesian agents to logical and mathematical omniscience is harmless. We model ideal agents, and an idealization is acceptable so long as it does not distort the particular phenomenon under study.

(5) There is a real epistemic asymmetry between prediction and accommodation: a theory that stuck its neck out before the data arrived earns more support from its success than a theory that was fitted to data already in hand, and this difference is not merely pragmatic or sociological.

(6) Whether given evidence confirms a given hypothesis must not turn on arbitrary stipulations about an imagined epistemic situation; confirmation verdicts need to be well-defined and stable across reasonable choices of idealization.

I can see that some of these might rub against each other, but I honestly can't tell which, and I'm not sure which one I'd give up if I had to. Please go through the set, tell me which pairs stand in genuine tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the problems by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that concession would cost. If there is a single revision that would clean up most of the mess, tell me which one and why.
