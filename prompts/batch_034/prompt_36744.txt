Been on rationalist forums since the old Sequences days and I've been in one too of those comment threads where someone declares deflationism about truth "obviously right," someone else says it "can't even state its own thesis without assuming a substantive notion," and then 300 comments go by with nobody saying anything new. So let me force the issue differently: write the joint statement that a committed deflationist (Horwich-style: truth has no hidden nature, the predicate exists to endorse schemas and handle generalizations) and a committed inflationist (truth is a genuine property with a nature to discover, correspondence or otherwise) could both actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

I want:
- the claims both can sign -- about the T-schema's status, about what the truth predicate contributes in generalizations like "everything on this map is true," about the liar, whatever they really do agree on;
- the residual disagreements stated exactly, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording. I'm especially interested in two spots: whether truth does any genuine explanatory work (does "it's true that the bridge was unsound" explain the collapse, or is that just a dressed-up assertion of the content?), and whether truth is the norm of belief or the deflationist can get by with something thinner there;
- the cruxes, and for each one an argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance;
- and if any of the dispute turns out to be verbal (like whether "truth is a property" even means the same thing to the two of them), say which part and why.

Then finish with a short honesty note: point out where the statement itself is most likely to go wrong -- which "agreed" claim would actual deflationists or actual inflationists most likely refuse to sign, and which formulations got accepted only because the drafting wanted to be done. Keep it in American English. Numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table.
