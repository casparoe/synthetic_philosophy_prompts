Last night my flatmate and I had a massive row that started from a documentary about the Holocaust and ended up squarely in the Browning–Goldhagen thing. I came down hard on Browning's side: Reserve Police Battalion 101, middle-aged working-class men from Hamburg, given the chance to step out at Józefów, most didn't, and the ones who kept shooting seem to have been driven by conformity, obedience, deference to peers, not wanting to look weak in front of their mates — the whole Milgram-adjacent situational package. My position by the end of the night was something like: the situation does nearly all the explanatory work, and there's nothing specially German, or even specially hateful, about the perpetrators — put comparable men from most societies in that structure of authority, group pressure, and gradual escalation, and you get comparable participation. My flatmate thinks this is smug and is basically Goldhagen's eliminationist-antisemitism point restated with a British accent: the perpetrators were ordinary Germans, from a specific culture soaked in eliminationist Jew-hatred for decades, and pretending otherwise makes the situational story unfalsifiable mush.

I want to stress-test my side properly, not just win the argument. Tell me what would actually count against the situational explanation — what evidence, what patterns in the historical record, what findings from other cases would genuinely move me, and sort them by how decisive they'd be. Some things I already half-know are awkward for me: the men who stepped out at Józefów apparently faced no serious consequences, which cuts against a strict obedience reading; some perpetrators seem to have volunteered for killing work, sought it out, enjoyed it; and Goldhagen's people claim the battalion got more brutal over time in ways that look like habituation or zeal rather than numbing duty. Which of these would Browning himself (or a serious defender of the situational account) concede as counting against it, and which would get explained away? And here's the part I most want you to be honest about: does my version of the view, where the situation is always somehow strong enough and cultural or character differences never matter, risk being insulated against any possible counterevidence — and if it does, is that a fatal problem for this kind of historical explanation, or just an inherent limit on the genre? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which one I should be most worried about the next time my flatmate brings it up.
