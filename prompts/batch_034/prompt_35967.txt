I write novels, mostly literary stuff with the occasional formal game tucked inside, and now the one with the most elaborate game is being translated into Portuguese and I'm stuck. The chapter in question is a love letter my narrator composes under a self-imposed rule: every sentence has to contain exactly one more word than the sentence before it, and the whole thing has to land on a rhyme with the beloved's name. The constraint isn't decoration — it's how the reader feels him losing his grip, the effort getting more and more audible as his sentences bloat toward the end. My translator, who is lovely and honestly overqualified for me, sent me a menu of possible strategies and asked which I'd accept, and I've realised I don't actually know how to weigh them.

The options roughly are: (a) rebuild the same constraint in Portuguese even where it forces real semantic drift, (b) stay faithful to what the sentences say and keep only the growing-length effect loosely, (c) swap in a different constraint that's native to Portuguese prose and equally audible to a reader, (d) translate straight and explain the original's form in a note at the back, or (e) some mix, like constraint in the text with a literal rendering in a footnote. For context I've also been rereading Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot, which is basically an entire book about this exact rabbit hole — he collects a huge stack of translations of one tiny Marot poem, each prioritizing different things — and I love it, but I suspect a novel chapter is a different beast than a lyric poem. Nobody rereads my chapter the way you'd reread a poem, and if the plot stalls for the sake of a form, the reader might just be bored instead of moved.

So here's my ask: rank those five strategies by how well they'd serve a novel (not a standalone poem), top to bottom, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does. And if it makes a difference that this is fiction rather than poetry, tell me where in the ranking that difference shows up — I'm honestly more interesed in the reasoning than the verdict, since I'll have to defend whatever we pick to my editor, who thinks any formal game that survives into translation is pretentious anyway.
