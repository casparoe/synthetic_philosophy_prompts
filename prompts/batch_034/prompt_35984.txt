I run a small foundation that funds work in philosophical logic, and this cycle we can support about five researchers for three years on dialetheism and paraconsistent logic. I have to decide where the money goes, and I want a defended priority ordering of the field's open questions rather than a survey.

To focus you, here is a hunch of mine that you can use, complicate, or reject: the field quietly splits into two projects that tend to get discussed as if they were one. Call the first "logical hygiene": developing consequence relations that keep inconsistency local, so that an inconsistent database, legal code, or set of scientific commitments does not explode into triviality, and so that classical reasoning can be recovered wherever things happen to be consistent. Call the second "ontological dialetheism": the claim that some contradictions are actually true. The standard motivation comes from the semantic paradoxes — the Liar and its relatives — but there is a live question about whether anything beyond that self-reference frontier (conflicting legal obligations, perception reporting contradictory contents, vagueness at the margins) is genuinely a dialetheia, or only ever a case for hygiene. My guess is that hygiene is in reasonably good shape, that the ontological claim is the underdeveloped part, and that a fair amount of published debate equivocates between the two, with each side taking victories from one project as if they settled the other.

Given that, which open questions should we prioritize, and in what order? I care about the reasoning more than the list. For each question you rank: say whether it belongs to hygiene or to ontology, or whether answering it would dissolve my distinction altogether; say what it is upstream or downstream of — for instance, is the worry about whether paraconsistent negation counts as genuine negation a gate that everything else passes through, or a side issue that has absorbed too much attention; say how tractable it is for philosophers as opposed to logicians or computer scientists, and say what would count as the question being settled rather than merely re-argued. Also flag which questions you would deliberately leave unfunded for the three-year period, and why. Do not provide a reading list, bibliography, or citations; referring to positions, arguments, and paradoxes by name is enough, and I will supply references myself.

End with a final line of exactly the form \answer{...} containing your top three questions as short labels, in priority order, with no additional commentary inside the brackets.
