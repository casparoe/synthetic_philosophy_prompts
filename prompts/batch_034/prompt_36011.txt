I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who spent the better part of four decades worrying about reference states, ground states, and what happens when you perturb a system away from equilibrium. In retirement I have taken up philosophy and, more recently, the AI alignment literature, and I have found myself stuck on a dispute that seems to have calcified: whether an AI system can be made to avoid "side effects" without first specifying everything humans care about.

As I understand it, the proposals on the table all define side effects by comparing what the agent does against some counterfactual baseline: the state of the world before the agent was switched on, or the trajectory the world would have followed had the agent done nothing at each step, or had it paused at one particular moment. On top of that choice of baseline, there are different measures of deviation — for instance, penalizing the agent for reducing the set of future states that remain reachable, or for changing its ability to achieve some arbitrary collection of auxiliary goals. For a while these looked like engineering details to me, but the field has been arguing about baselines and measures for years without convergence, and the participants seem to agree on all the technical facts while disagreeing completely about which construction captures "impact."

My working hypothesis, drawn from my old life, is that this is a gauge problem in disguise: the choice of baseline is like a choice of coordinates or of a vacuum state, with no fact of the matter privileging one over another, so the disputants are defending conventional choices as if they were discoveries — in which case the dispute is largely verbal. But I can also tell a competing story: on realistic scenarios the different constructions recommend different actions, so the disagreement is substantive, and what is really doing the hidden work is a prior, unstated commitment about which counterfactuals are metaphysically respectable — a question I gather philosophers have their own stalled quarrels about.

I would like you to act as an expert at the intersection of philosophy of science and AI alignment, and to think carefully before answering; please start by briefly brainstorming the candidate diagnoses rather than committing to one immediately. Then diagnose the stalemate: is it a verbal dispute about the word "impact," a substantive formal disagreement, a clash of starting intuitions, or several questions traveling under one heading? Support your diagnosis with what the sides to the dispute actually claim, tell me whether my gauge analogy survives scrutiny or where it breaks down, and say what, if anything, would count as genuine progress here — a conceptual distinction, a formal result, a decisive scenario — or whether the impasse is a sign that the question, as posed, is ill-posed.
