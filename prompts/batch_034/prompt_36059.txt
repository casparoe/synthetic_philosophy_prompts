I'm a novelist with a character problem, and it's actually a philosophy problem wearing a trench coat. My current manuscript has a protagonist who's supposed to be a hyper-rational type — she keeps a decision journal, she's read all the decision theory canon, the whole bit. I've been feeding her the classic thought experiments as little character beats, and so far her verdicts have come out consistent, and I want to keep them that way instead of breaking character when the plot wants something.

Here's her track record so far. In Newcomb's problem she one-boxes. In Parfit's hitchhiker — the version where the driver only stops becuase she correctly predicted my protagonist will pay up at the destination, and my protagonist promises to pay once she's safe in town — she pays. In counterfactual mugging, where she agreed beforehand to pay whatever the coin says, she pays after it comes up tails. In the smoking lesion case she lights up anyway. In the transparent version of Newcomb, where she can see the million sitting in the big box, she still one-boxes. And in the Death in Damascus setup she books it to Aleppo.

The scene I'm writing now: she's stranded again (I know, it's becoming a motif), and this time the driver who picks her up is just a genuinely kind person who would have stopped for anybody — no prediction, no test, no calculation, she just helps people. Somewhere in the ride my protagonist promises to send her money for gas and the trouble once she's home. No one will ever know if she stiffs her. When the time comes, does she send the money? I honestly cant tell what her "real" answer is.

So that's my question. Predict her verdict, but more importantly tell me what rules are even consistent with her track record — I can see at least an updateless/one-boxer sort of rule and a simpler "she keeps her word, period" rule both fitting so far, and maybe others. Which rule fits best, why, and what do the rival rules predict about this scene? Then tell me what scene I could write next — some further dilemma — that would most cleanly show readers which principle she's actually running, since I get the feeling the current scene might not settle it.

Also, please flag how confident you are in all of this and single out the point you're least sure of — if her track record genuinely underdetermines the verdict I'd rather know that and just pick whichever answer is better for the book. Oh, and American English please, the book is set in the States.
