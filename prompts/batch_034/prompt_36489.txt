ok so i'm a novelist — two mediocre books out, the third one circling the drain — and the thing i can't shake lately isn't really a novelistic thing, it's more a guilt thing that hangs around film people. a friend of mine writes biopics, and she told me about the deathbed scene problem: the real woman her film was about died in 1978 with nobody in the room, no diary, nothing, and the producers wanted a scene anyway, so my friend just wrote the woman's last words out of thin air. gave her this beautiful little speech, half of it lifted from letters the woman wrote at 25. it tested great. meanwhile the woman's biographer — a guy who'd spent three decades on her and got maybe a thousandth of the audience the film got — refused to see the move once he heard about the scene. he apparently said (paraphrasing my friend paraphrasing him, so who knows) that the woman spent her whole adult life fighting to control how she was seen, and now strangers had taken her last words away from her.

i want a short story about this, roughly 1200 words. build it around the dinner where the screenwriter sits across from that biographer — or the woman's estranged daughter, your call which one gives you more — the night after he's read the draft of the scene. some things i'd love worked in, though don't force all of them: the mechanics of the theft, i.e. how the speech gets assembled out of letters and old interviews so the dead woman finally gets better dialogue than she ever got in life; the fact that legally this is all free, you can't libel the dead, which the producer keeps repeating like it settles something; and the temptation to fix the record while you're in there — soften the prejudice she actually expressed, hand her the regret she maybe never had. what i don't want is anyone delivering the moral out loud, or a tidy ending where art and truth make peace. let the dinner go wrong in a small, specific way and stop there.

and then after the story, put one final line on its own, starting with Summary: — a single flat sentence saying what the story decides we owe the dead (or that it cant decide). i'm assembling these into a corpus and need to parse that line automatically, so keep it clean, no italics, no flourish.
