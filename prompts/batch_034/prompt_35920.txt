I've just been appointed to a hospital working group charged with drafting our policy on cultural accommodation in end-of-life care — specifically, when we should defer to a family's collective, hierarchically organized decision-making and when we should insist on the individual patient's autonomous choice. Since I agreed to serve, the following questions have stopped being seminar questions for me, and before I take a position in the group's report I want to know whether my own position is even coherent. Here is what I actually believe:

1. The cross-cultural moral-psychology findings, taken at their strongest: it is not only norms that vary across populations but the underlying machinery — whether impersonal harm is the paradigm of wrongdoing at all or merely one consideration alongside loyalty, sanctity, and hierarchy; whether a victim is required for wrongness; whether persons are conceived as autonomous choosers or as nodes in a web of obligations. On most of these dimensions, the populations our ethics policies are written by, and largely for, are the global outliers.
2. Moral realism: relativism is false, and some moral judgments are simply correct.
3. Ethics proceeds by reflective equilibrium: intuitions about cases are data, to be weighed against principles and revised together with them.
4. The trajectory that WEIRD societies have taken — abolition, extension of the franchise, the replacement of status by contract in family and work — constitutes moral progress, not mere change we could coherently have welcomed or deplored.
5. In the cases the working group will actually vote on, respect for the individual patient's autonomy is the governing principle, and I would defend it even against families and communities whose competing ideals are internally coherent and deeply held.

Tell me which of these commitments hang together and which do not: which pairs or larger subsets are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each conflict really is. For each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I would rather find out from you than in front of the working group.
