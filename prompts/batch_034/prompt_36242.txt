I am a data engineer who has spent the last decade building event-sourced systems and bitemporal warehouses: immutable append-only logs, facts stamped with both valid-time and transaction-time, "current state" treated as nothing more than a materialized projection that a downstream process derives from the log and can always rebuild from scratch. In my world, the past is not gone; it is retained, queryable, and in a sense more authoritative than the projection, since the projection is disposable and the log is not.

Restate the debate between presentism and eternalism in this vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report. I can see rough translations already, and I want to know how far they hold up: eternalism looks like the claim that the log is the whole of reality and that "now" is just the row where the valid-time interval is open-ended — a filter, not a fact about what exists. Presentism looks like the claim that only the projection is real and that the log, however useful, has the ontological status of an inference ticket rather than a record of what exists. And the growing-block view looks like a log that is append-only in one direction: everything written exists, nothing not yet written does, and the head of the log is privileged without being the only thing.

Then tell me what did not survive the translation. In particular: (1) my databases have no analogue of the felt passage of time — nothing in them corresponds to time seeming to move — and I want to know whether that is a gap in my vocabulary or a genuine datum the metaphysicians are accounting for; (2) my "now" is indexical in a way that seems analogous to "here" in a spatial database, and I want to know whether the philosophers' "now" is doing anything beyond that, or whether the indexical analogy dissolves part of the debate; (3) my systems have an epistemic asymmetry I did not design — I can reconstruct past states by replaying the log, but nothing in the system lets me query future states — and I want to know whether the metaphysical debate about the direction of time has any counterpart to this or whether it is purely an artifact of my architecture; and (4) in my world, log and projection are interderivable, so arguing about which one "really exists" would look like a verbal dispute — I want your honest judgment on whether presentism versus eternalism is substantive in a way that dispute is not, and if so, what exactly the disagreement is about once the parts that translate into my terms are stripped away. I am fluent in the database vocabulary and read enough philosophy of time to follow technical terms, but do not assume I know the literature; explain any philosophical machinery you invoke as you use it.
