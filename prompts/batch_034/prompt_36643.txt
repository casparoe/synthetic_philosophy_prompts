Longtime lurker of the rationalist blogosphere here (been around since the Overcoming Bias days), and I currently sit on the moderation commitee of a mid-sized forecasting forum — think of it as Metaculus's rowdier cousin. I need a memo drafted for our next meeting and this is exactly the kind of thing you people are good at.

Here's the situation. Our most famous member — call him D. — nailed a big geopolitical call years before it happened and has a publicly tracked calibration record that beats the community aggregate by a lot. At the last all-hands he proposed a new feature: a SETTLED tag that members with verified track records could slap on a question, which would auto-collapse any replies presenting counter-evidence. His argument: if he genuinely knows the answer, then apparent counter-evidence is by definition noise or confounded, so engaging with it just feeds the cranks. He literally cited the dogmatism paradox (Gilbert Harman's old puzzle) in his proposal — the one where, if knowing p doesn't entitle you to dismiss evidence against p, then nobody can ever reasonably refuse to re-litigate anything.

The committee is split. One faction thinks D. is just doing correct epistemology with the serial numbers filed off — we don't re-run every flat earth thread either, and pretending all questions are perpetually open is its own failure mode (some of us remember the epistemic learned helplessness discourse and thought it had a real point). The other faction is leaning on the whole conservation of expected evidence idea from the old LessWrong sequences: if you can't name in advance what observation would move you down, then your 97% isn't a probability, it's a slogan, and the tag just bakes in a one-way ratchet where track record lets you opt out of updating forever.

It's not hypothetical either — a trial version already blew up. D. marked SETTLED on a thread about whether a new seroprevalence study undermined his pandemic model, three respected forecasters quit the forum over it, and one of them is now writing a public post about how our site punishes exactly the updates it exists to reward.

So: draft the memo I'll circulate before next week's meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision — adopt the tag, reject it, or adopt it with conditions (think track-record thresholds, expiry dates, a mandatory "here's what would change my mind" field the tagger has to fill in) — give reasons D. and his faction could actually respond to rather than just gesturing at update norms, and deal fairly with his single strongest point, which I take to be that we all accept something like his reasoning when it comes to astrology or flat earth, so the disagreement is about where the line is, not whether the line exists. Mods will have to apply whatever we decide in specific cases, so whatever you recommend needs criteria they can actually use.
