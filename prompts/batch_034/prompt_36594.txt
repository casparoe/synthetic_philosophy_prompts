I've been in and out of therapy for anxiety and low mood for years, and at some point I started looking through the diagnostic manual my therapists have been working from, partly out of curiosity and partly to argue about my own label. What struck me is that it reads like a catalogue of diseases: you either meet the criteria for a disorder or you don't, there's a threshold number of symptoms, a minimum duration, and so on. And yet the catalogue itself keeps moving. Homosexuality sat in there as a disorder for a couple of decades and then got voted out by the profession; more recently they folded Asperger's into a broader autism spectrum instead of keeping it as its own thing; and every new edition shuffles a few categories around, merges some, splits others. If these categories were like chemical elements — real divisions in nature that we're just discovering — it seems odd that they'd change whenever a committee revises the book. But if the categories are basically conventions we made up, why do insurers, schools, courts, and benefits offices all treat the labels as hard facts about people, and why did I have to hit the exact cutoff to qualify for anything?

So here's my question: what does this whole practice presuppose about what mental disorders are? Does the everyday use of the manual — the checkboxes, the yes/no diagnosis, the cutoffs — presuppose that there are genuinely discrete kinds of disorder out in the world, like species or elements, and the clunky criteria are just our imperfect way of detecting them? Or could the same practice be fine on a view where disorders are just fuzzy clusters of symptoms, and the thresholds are pragmatic bookkeeping lines we draw so the system can run? Also, is the practice even consistent with itself, given that the same clinicians who insist on the binary labels will happily tell me my depression is mild-to-moderate, as if it were a dial rather than a switch? And finally, I suspect a lot of this is underdetermined — I bet several different pictures of what disorders are would license exactly the same checkboxes — so I'd like to know which parts of the system actually force a choice between the pictures, and what would have to change if we openly adopted the fuzzy-cluster view.
