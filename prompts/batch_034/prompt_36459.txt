At the last meeting of my epistemology reading group, a Clifford partisan shut down all discussion of William James by reciting Clifford's maxim that it is always wrong, for anyone, anywhere, to believe anything on insufficient evidence, adding that since the shipowner's "sincerity of his conviction can in no wise help him," the ethics of belief is settled and James's will-to-believe position is just special pleading for wishful thinking. I suspect the maxim is doing several jobs at once and that not all of them can be done. Disentangle the distinct precise theses it could be expressing: whether the wrong it names is genuinely moral (a blameworthy act) or only a defect of rationality; whether insufficiency is measured against the evidence the subject actually has or the evidence she could have obtained with a fair inquiry — Clifford's island agitators seem condemned on the latter ground, since the evidence of the accused's innocence "was such as the agitators might easily have obtained, if they had attempted a fair inquiry"; whether the target is the state of belief itself, the omission of inquiry, or the action taken on the belief — noting that Clifford concedes that so far as it goes, the reading on which "it is not the belief which is judged to be wrong, but the action following upon it" is "right and necessary"; and whether anything and anyone are meant literally, so that the maxim condemns trusting your partner's fidelity or a friend's honesty before enough evidence is in. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, defensible but limited, or false, and say which reading our Clifford partisan actually needs in order to get his conclusion that belief ahead of the evidence is never permissible. Finally, tell me which of the surviving readings are genuinely engaged by James's reply — that in forced, momentous, intellectually undecidable cases, suspending judgment is not a neutral option one can always retreat to — and which of them James's reply leaves completely untouched. Lay the readings out as a menu I can bring to the next meeting, not as a case for one of them.
