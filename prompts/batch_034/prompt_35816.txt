my grandmother moved into memory care last month and the family is meeting next weak to go over her advance directive, which she signed eight years ago while still sharp as anything. it says no antibiotics for life-threatening infections once she can no longer recognize family. she's very close to that line now, and — this is the hard part — she's apparently content: waves at everyone, likes her lunch, no distress at all. before that meeting I want to know whether my own position actually hangs together, because I think I believe all of the following at once:

1. her earlier self gets to speak for her whole life. she deliberated about this for years, it was a considered judgment about how she wanted her life to end, and that should carry more weight than whatever the current her, who can't track a conversation for two minutes, would opt for.
2. I'm broadly a psychological continuity person about personal identity — what makes someone the same person over time is chains of memory, character, plans, that sort of thing.
3. relatedly, I do NOT buy the move some people make where they say the demented patient has become a different person, so following the directive amounts to imposing a stranger's choice on her. she's the same woman, just badly damaged.
4. but if she were somewhat less gone — say she could still look at you and say "I want the medicine, please" — I think that current wish should count for a lot, maybe enough to beat the directive. someone who can still form a want that's connected to who she is isn't just a mouth moving.
5. and I'd only treat a directive as binding if the person really understood what they were signing — which she did, she talked about it constantly, so that box is definately checked.

so: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost? rank the tensions by severity rather than just listing them flat. I have a nagging feeling that some part of this package isn't earned, but for the life of me I can't tell which part, so don't let me off easy.
