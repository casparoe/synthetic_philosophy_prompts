writing up my "views on practical reason" for a discussion group and want a sanity check before I hand it over. Here's what I actually believe, as best I can state it:

1. Some things of value are genuinely incommensurable — a life centered on friendship vs a life centered on some achievement isn't worse, better, or exactly equal, there's just no common scale.
2. That's what makes hard choices hard, and in a hard choice either option can be chosen fully rationally — no mistake either way.
3. I don't buy the "on a par" move where parity is some fourth positive relation between options. Incommensurability is a failure of comparability, not another comparison.
4. Commitment creates reasons: once I've chosen the lawyer path, that choice itself generates reasons to stick with it, and that's not arbitrary.
5. But I'm dead set against bootstrapping — you can't make something the case just by deciding it's so.
6. I'm a realist about practical reasons and I think there's always a fact of the matter about what one ought to do, even in hard cases.
7. For public policy its the opposite: I'm fine with putting everything on one scale, QALY-style trade offs and the like. You can't run a health system on incommensurability.

Which of these pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? Rank the tensions by severity rather than just listing them all as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the real problem is so don't go easy on any of them.
