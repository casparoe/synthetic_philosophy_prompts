so heres an episode I keep chewing on and I want it done properly this time. In 1973 the APA's board of trustees voted to drop homosexuality as a disorder from the DSM-II, the full membership ratified the move in a referendum the next year by a fairly slim margin, and the diagnosis didn't even die cleanly after that -- the DSM-III in 1980 quietly brought back "ego-dystonic homosexuality," a category for gay people who were distressed about their orientation, and that one survived until the 1987 revision.

analyze this whole thing through the naturalism vs normativism debate about what disease and health actually are. specifically:

1. On a Boorse-style naturalist view (disease = statistically subnormal biological function of some part, judged against a reference class of organisms of the same age and sex, with no value judgments anywhere in the machinery), what actually happened in 1973? there are two readings floating around: either it was a scientific correction, the APA had miscounted what counts as dysfunction and finally fixed it, or it was a value change dressed up as science, since no biological facts changed between 1972 and 1973, so if the disease judgment changed, either the facts were never doing the grounding work or there were no relevant facts at all. which reading is the naturalist stuck with? and here's the awkward part: if function is at least partly a matter of evolutionary/reproductive design, a lot of gay people look like textbook subnormal functioning in the reproductive department, so the naturalist owes us a principled reason why homosexuality isn't a disease while infertility plainly is. can Boorse actually give that reason? (think hard about whether there's any *part* that's malfunctioning, versus just an organism whose parts are all working fine but aimed somewhere the theory's reference class didn't anticipate.)

2. now do the normativist and hybrid views, say Wakefield's harmful dysfunction analysis (disorder = failure of an evolved function PLUS harm judged by the standards of the person's social environment). what does 1973 look like from there? tempting to say the normativist has an easy time of it -- social values shifted, harm judgments shifted, the diagnosis followed, done. but then explain ego-dystonic homosexuality hanging on until 1987. if the distress about being gay is mostly caused by stigma, then on a pure normativist reading the disorder arguably lives in the society, not the patient. does the normativist have the resources to say that, or does the view collapse into "whatever the patient is unhappy about counts as a disorder," which can't be right either?

3. then run it the other direction, which is the part most people skip: what does the episode do to the theories? the naturalist's whole selling point is that disease judgments are objective and immune from politics, but the actual history here is a diagnosis that got created with essentially no evidence, dropped under protest and internal negotiation, and re-insinuated through a distress clause -- with the biology sitting there unchanged the entire time. does that show naturalism about disease is false, or only that psychiatry as actually practiced isn't naturalist, which is a very different claim? and the normativist's selling point is honesty about values -- but if diagnosis just tracks the values of the day, what stops 1973 from being undone by some future value shift, and is that a bug in the view or the whole point of it?

don't give me the encylopedia summary of the event, I know the chronology. I want disciplined mapping -- what exactly is the dysfunction claim supposed to be, what's the reference class, who or what carries the value judgment in each theory -- and I want you to say where a theory is silent instead of forcing an answer out of it. no reading lists or name-dropping for its own sake, just the analysis.
