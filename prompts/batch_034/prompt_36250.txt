I run a small reading group in formal epistemology, and last term we worked through Richard Jeffrey's treatment of updating on uncertain evidence — the rule under which, when you see a cloth by candlelight and come to hold a credence of 0.7 that it is green without becoming certain of any proposition at all, you redistribute your credences over the consequences of green versus not-green while holding fixed your conditional credences given each member of that partition. Half the group thought this was a genuine and overdue extension of Bayesian conditioning; the other half insisted it was a non-solution, on the grounds that rational learning always makes *something* certain — if not the cloth's colour, then some proposition about your visual experience — so that classical conditionalisation can handle the case once the evidence is described properly, and the Jeffrey rule merely papers over a failure to say what the evidence actually was. We never got past trading slogans, and I want to give the next session something meatier.

Please write a dialogue between two philosophers in this tradition: one a strict conditionaliser who holds that belief change must always proceed by conditioning on a proposition whose probability goes to one, and one a defender of Jeffrey conditioning. Set it in a concrete situation that keeps forcing the abstract issue on them — perhaps they are together in a dimly lit observatory watching a noisy instrument, so that every few minutes one of them registers a reading with something less than certainty. Make them equally competent: neither a strawman, and each must genuinely engage with the other's best points rather than talk past them. In particular, I'd like the dialogue to work through the following, without needing to cite anyone by page: the candlelight-style case; the strict conditionaliser's retreat to propositions about experience and the Jeffreyan's worries about whether we can actually formulate such propositions before the fact; the rigidity or invariance assumption built into Jeffrey's rule and when it plausibly fails; the order-dependence of successive Jeffrey updates on different partitions, and whether that is a vice or just an honest description of how evidence interacts; and the objection that Jeffrey updating lets the agent choose her new probabilities over the partition more or less arbitrarily, raising the question of where those numbers are supposed to come from. Feel free to have one of them sketch, without naming it, a rival fix (for instance a rule designed so that repeated small updates on the same partition compound the way they intuitively should).
