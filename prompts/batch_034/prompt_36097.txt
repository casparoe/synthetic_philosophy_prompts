I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, but I also run a small games-and-ethics reading group that's mostly made up of people from the HCI department, and for our last session of the term I want to make a "which view on the moral status of video-game characters do you actually hold?" quiz, in the style of those internet personality quizzes, to force people to commit on cases instead of vibes. The group is split between people who say the whole question is embarrassingly naive ("they're obviously just code, go outside") and people who get genuinely uncomfortable when someone tortures an NPC in front of them, and I suspect if I asked them directly they'd all just say "it's complicated". So I want the quiz to sort them by what they'd actually do or bite.

Could you write me eight to ten questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options (no "it depends" cop-outs, each option should be a real commitment people could take). Some cases I'd like included: a player spending an in-game evening tormenting a particularly lifelike NPC whose dialogue makes it beg for mercy; a heavily modded game where a modder has deliberately added persistent pain behaviours and fear memories to NPCs so the world feels "more alive"; a speedrunner who has to kill the same sympathetic character ten thousand times to shave seconds off a run; and something about save-scumming — reloading over and over to replay an NPC's death that the NPC, by design, never "remembers". Beyond those, add whatever cases you think are diagnostic, ideally including at least one that most people haven't seen argued before (one idea I had: a game where NPCs are scripted to flourish if treated kindly, so a player's cruelty doesn't cause simulated suffering but forecloses simulated wellbeing — does that change anything?).

Then I need a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions, roughly: the mere-artifact view (nothing there to matter morally, full stop), the phenomenal functionalist view (if the system implements the right kind of suffering-like state it counts, so it's an empirical question about architecture), the representation view (a game character is more like a character in a novel — a representation of a person, not a person — so cruelty can be wrong in an aesthetic or expressive register, like writing gratuitously cruel fiction, without wronging anyone), the interests-based view (welfare requires desires/interests, so current NPCs probably fail but the standard isn't phenomenology), and a virtue-ish view (nothing matters to the NPC, but what you rehearse in games shapes you, and that's the whole story). Please flag explicitly any pair of positions the quiz can't actually separate — I'd bet the artifact view and the functionalist view agree on every case involving current-gen NPCs and only come apart on hypothetical future ones, and if that's right I want the quiz to say so rather than pretend it's diagnostic. Also note, for the group, which combination of answers corresponds to no real position, if any. No citations or reading lists needed — just the quiz, the key, and honest notes on where it fails. Ta!
