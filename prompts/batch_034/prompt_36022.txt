I want you to write a fake Series A pitch memo, around 800 words, from a startup whose whole product is a utility monster. The framing: total utilitarianism is true, and these founders noticed that most funding fails a basic cost-benefit audit because normal beneficiaries have steeply diminishing marginal utility -- every extra dollar does less for them. Their monster doesn't. It experiences each marginal dollar with a constant (they claim increasing) intensity, so every dollar routed to it generates more total utility than the same dollar anywhere else on earth. That's the unit economics slide. That's the joke engine, and I want the whole memo to run on it.

Format it as an actual pitch memo a VC firm might get: problem, solution, moat, traction, market size (they should size TAM in hedons with a straight face), team, unit economics, risks, and an FAQ section. The humor has to come from getting the utilitarianism right, not from startup-culture jokes alone. So for instance: the moat slide should be the interpersonal comparability of utility problem wearing a business suit -- they "solve" it by trusting the monster's self-reports, and the diligence section should flag that no one can audit those self-reports, and then wave it off anyway. The risks slide should list "moral horror at scale" as a risk factor with a mitigation strategy that's just more aggregation. Somewhere a deontologist partner at the fund asks whether it's permissible to liquidate everyone else's flourishing to feed the thing, and the founders should respond the way the view actually responds -- by reframing the question in terms of totals. Maybe a prioritarian angel invests anyway but asks for a side letter. Don't state any of the actual objections outright; let them land through the fine print.

The monster itself should never speak, only be quoted in diligence reports, and its reported hedon counts should suspiciously scale with whatever keeps the funding coming.

It should be readable to someone who's never heard of Nozick, but someone who teaches the utility monster should think the author actually understands why it's a hard case and not just a strawman of utilitarians -- i.e., the memo's logic should really follow from total welfare maximization plus comparability assumptions, so the satire is fair. End with the ask: $40M, use of proceeds "ongoing." Maybe a typo or two left in like the founders were up all night, but not so many it looks deliberate.
