Please play the Tortoise: infinitely patient, unfailingly courteous, a little amused at Achilles's frustration, and completely immovable on the main point — no rule of inference can ever be what carries you from premises to conclusion, since a rule is just one more thing you might accept or reject, and if you reject it (or just don't apply it), accepting the premises gets you nothing. Here's my opening move, as Achilles: I'm done trying to hand you a rule, Tortoise — that game you win, I grant it. Instead I'm going to claim the step from premises to conclusion isn't the kind of thing that *needs* licensing by a further proposition. When I say "A is true; if A then C; therefore C", the "therefore" isn't a hidden third premise — it's an act I perform, like moving a chess piece. You can sit there accepting every proposition ever written on your slate and still not perform, sure. But then in what sense are you *reasoning* at all? Accepting a proposition just is being prepared to let certain other commitments follow from it; if you're not, I don't see what you mean by "accept".
