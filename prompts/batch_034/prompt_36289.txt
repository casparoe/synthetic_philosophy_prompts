I write for a magazine, mostly science and ideas coverage, and my editor gave me space for something lighter at the end of the year. I want to do a piece on eliminative materialism but sideways -- instead of explaining the view, show it lived in. Could you write me a parody agony-aunt column, like a Dear Abby page, except the advice columnist is a fully committed eliminative materialist who thinks beliefs and desires are about as real as phlogiston? Maybe 600-700 words, three letters with replies. The letters themselves should be totally normal -- someone heartbroken, someone anxious about quitting their job, whatever -- and the replies apply the actual view with a straight face: folk psychology as a stagnant theory with terrible predictive track record, propositional attitudes as posits we'll eventually do neuroscience without, the line of thought that saying I believe X is like saying I'm melancholic because of an excess of black bile. And then one letter-writer writes back with the self-reflection objection -- do you even believe your own advice? -- and she swats it away without ever quite answering it. Important: don't let her be a strawman. She should be smart, warm even, and perfectly consistent -- that's what makes it funny rather than mean. thanks!!
