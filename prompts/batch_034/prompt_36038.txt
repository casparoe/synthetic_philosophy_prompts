Every year in my philosophy of science seminar we do a couple weeks on the definition of life, and every year we end in the same puddle, so this time I'm running an experiment on my colleague Dana instead. Dana is a developmental biologist and she's very decisive about what's alive and what isn't -- twenty years of arguments over faculty lunches and I've basically got a ledger of her verdicts at this point. Alive: humans, oak trees, E. coli, a sterile mule, a worker honeybee (also sterile, obviously), HeLa cells, red blood cells, and decades-old freeze-dried bacterial spores that have zero metabolism while dormant but revive fine. Not alive: ordinary viruses, prions, self-replicating RNA strands that mutate and get selected in a test tube but have no membrane and no metabolism of their own, candle flames and whirlpools (she gets genuinely annoyed when the far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics people bring those up), the biosphere as a whole, and a glider from the Game of Life.

Now I've got three new cases coming up and I want to be able to call her answers in advance, mostly so I can look smug. Case one: a synthetic protocell with a membrane and its own metabolism that maintains itself beautifully, except each one is assembled fresh in the lab, none of them ever reproduces, and its genome is copied error-free so no evolution will ever happen. Case two: the giant viruses, like mimivirus, that carry their own genes for chunks of the protein-synthesis machinery, which everyone agrees blurs the virus line. Case three: a self-replicating factory on the Moon, of the von Neumann probe sort -- repairs itself, mines, builds copies, no chemistry-of-life anywhere in it. Predict her verdict on each.

What I actually care about is the rules underneath. Some obvious candidates get weeded out by her record -- the RNA replicator verdict seems to kill pure "life is Darwinian evolution" definitions, the flame thing kills bare thermodynamic self-organization, the spore verdict kills "currently metabolizing." What's left? To me it looks like some version of organized self-maintenance (with a potentiality clause for the spores) versus something about being a token of a reproducing, metabolizing lineage -- the mule and the worker bee are the load-bearing verdicts there -- but I'd like you to tell me which rules genuinely fit everything on her ledger, where they diverge on my three cases, your best guess with reasons for each verdict, and what further case you'd put in front of her to tell the surviving rules apart. Don't hold back on telling me my two candidate rules are secretly the same rule, if that's what you think -- I've been staring at this too long to trust myself.
