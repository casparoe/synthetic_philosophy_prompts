Ok so I finally read A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court last month (found it at a used bookshop, hadn't read Twain since Tom Sawyer as a kid) and something about it keeps bugging me. The whole premise is that Hank Morgan, a factory foreman from 1879 Hartford, gets conked on the head and wakes up in Camelot, and it's basically one long joke that the middle ages were backwards — knights prancing around in armor, slavery, the Church stamping out his newspaper, everyone believing in magic and prophecies, and one guy with telegraph and steam-engine know-how leapfrogging thirteen centuries of progress in a couple years.

But then I fell down a youtube rabbit hole and apparently modern historians hate that picture — "the dark ages" never really happened, the whole "renaissance" idea was invented way after the fact, people had universities and clocks and mechanical stuff back then, and the habit of chopping history into middle ages / renaissance / scientific revolution is something later people made up to flatter themselves. Also I saw that the armor and jousting Twain describes would've been like 900 years too late for a real king Arthur, if there was one.

So can you read the novel through that whole fight? Does the comedy actually depend on "medieval people were dumb and the 19th century had progress" being true, or is Twain doing something smarter — because the ending is not exactly a victory parade for modernity. It's Hank's electricity and revolvers slaughtering every knight in britain, the rotting corpses making his little band sick, and Hank himself getting stabbed by a knight and dying back home in the 19th century. And given that Camelot is a made-up place anyway — a 19th century american's idea of Malory's (I think?) idea of dark-age Britain — is the book maybe a period piece ABOUT periodization, if that makes sense? Like it shows you what you get when you believe the chapter labels.

I want a real reading, not just "labels are social constructs." Where does the novel genuinely need the backwards-medievals picture, where would it survive fine without it, and does the ending undermine the progress story the whole book was running on?
