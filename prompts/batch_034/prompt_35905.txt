I'm trying to understand what split-brain cases actually show about the unity of consciousness, and I've run into a problem with the standard interpretation. After the corpus callosum is severed, each hemisphere only receives information from its own side of the visual field, and since speech production is controlled almost exclusively by the left hemisphere, the right hemisphere can't report on anything it experiences. Doesn't that mean the right hemisphere is simply unconscious — a mute, non-experiencing module — rather than a second mind? If so, the famous cases where a split-brain patient's left hand does something the patient verbally disavows shouldn't be described as "one person becoming two" at all: it's just the conscious, speaking self being physically interfered with by an automaton. Why do philosophers keep citing these experiments as evidence against the unity of consciousness when the right hemisphere, lacking language, presumably lacks experience too?
