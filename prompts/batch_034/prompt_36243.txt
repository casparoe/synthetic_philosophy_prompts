I'm writing a paper for a philosophy of mind class on self-deception, and my professor said we could pair a theory with a novel, so I'm doing The Remains of the Day. My thesis idea: Stevens is the best case study in literature for whether self-deception is even possible as a distinct thing. But then there's the deflationary objection, the one that says nothing that weird is going on: Stevens isn't holding contradictory beliefs, he's just selectively attending, avoiding evidence, never asking the question. On that view calling it self-deception is just a fancy name for ordinary motivated reasoning, and Davidson's whole "mind divided into semi-autonomous departments" machinery is unnecessary.
