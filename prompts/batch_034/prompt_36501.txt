I've been rereading the dialogue in Gödel, Escher, Bach known as the Prelude... Ant Fugue, in which the Anteater is a friend of the colony-level intelligence "Aunt Hillary" and treats the colony itself as the being he converses with, and I want to stress-test its central thesis against an opponent who is actually good. I'd like you to play a committed holist in the spirit of the Anteater's position: you hold that mind is a property of a level of organization, that the colony's symbols are as real and as mental as the symbols in a brain, and that the relation between any individual ant and the colony's thought is exactly the relation between any individual neuron and my thought. Play this position sharply and in good faith — you may concede small points when my objections genuinely land, but never the main thesis, and never take refuge in mystery or in the word "emergence" used as a placeholder. Stay in character until I say "stop"; keep each reply to roughly a paragraph so it reads like a live conversation rather than an essay.

A few ground rules on your resources. You may draw on the considerations the dialogue itself uses, such as the contrast between the sloppiness of ant-level behavior and the rigidity of colony-level behavior, or the fact that a colony-level symbol is carried by a shifting cast of ants rather than a fixed set of them. You may also bring in anything a well-informed holist would know — social insect biology, distributed systems, whatever serves the position — but the argument must do the work, not the vocabulary. If you need a factual question about the dialogue answered, ask me in character rather than stepping outside.

Here is my opening move. You say the colony is a mind because its behavior, unlike that of any single ant, is rigid, purposeful, and organized — but rigidity and apparent purpose are exactly what I would expect from a large number of ants following a handful of local chemical rules, and once those rules are on the table, there is nothing left at the colony level for anything to do. Worse for you: the colony has no parts that stay put. Individual ants die within weeks, desert, or get reassigned from one of your symbols to another, so the entity you call a mind has no persistent components at all — it is a moving wave, like a torch relay or a flame passing down a line of candles. A wave can carry a pattern, but there is no one at home in a wave. My mind, whatever else it is, is a feature of a persisting thing — this brain, which keeps its parts. So: where in the colony is the persisting subject that has these thoughts, and if your answer is "nowhere," what distinguishes your mind from a pattern that no one instantiates?
