I would be grateful for your help in thinking through something that has been bothering me since the summer holidays.

Over the break I played a long, story-driven video game. Near the end, the player is forced to choose which of two characters will survive, and the game makes it clear that the choice is genuinely up to you: nothing you did earlier determines it, and the story branches accordingly. I deliberated, chose, and the other character died. For several days afterwards I felt something that seemed, from the inside, indistinguishable from guilt: I kept replaying the decision in my head, wondering whether I had weighed the two characters' interests properly, and I even felt reluctant to start the game again.

My older sister thinks this guilt is straightforwardly irrational. Her view is that no one was harmed, that the character never existed, and that my sense of having made a genuine choice was an illusion produced by the game's writers — the menu of options was fixed long before I ever picked up the controller, so whatever I 'did', I merely selected among outcomes someone else had authored. A friend of mine, on the other hand, thinks the guilt is entirely fitting: he says the decision really was mine in the sense that matters, that I could have chosen otherwise, and that feeling bad about choosing badly is simply what a morally decent person does.

There is also a second case that I cannot disentangle from the first. Earlier in the holidays I watched a film that was advertised as being based on a true story, and I wept at the ending. I have since read that the true story behind it was almost entirely invented. Oddly, this has unsettled me: I find myself unsure whether my tears have retroactively become irrational, since the people I was crying for apparently never existed, or whether they were rational all along and the film's dishonesty is a separate matter that has nothing to do with the propriety of my response.

What I would like from you is a careful analysis rather than reassurance. Which, if any, of these emotional responses — the guilt over my in-game choice, and the grief over a death that turned out to be doubly fictional — are irrational, and why? In the course of answering, I would find it helpful if you addressed the following questions directly. First, does it make sense to say an emotion is irrational when its object does not exist? Second, does my sister's point that the game's writers fixed the available options in advance show that my choice was not really mine, or is there a sense of 'genuine choice' that survives that point? Third, is there a principled difference between my two cases — roughly, between being moved as an audience member and being moved as a partial author of what happens? And finally, if some fiction-directed emotions are rational, does anything follow about how I ought to treat them — for instance, was I under any sort of obligation, even inside the game, to weigh the two characters' interests as I would a real person's?
