At my philosophy of mind reading group we have been circling the extended mind thesis, and a colleague has started defending it with an analogy I can't get out of my head. Her point: the gut microbiome is metabolically doing digestive work that my own cells cannot do, it is acquired from the environment rather than encoded in my genome, it is continuously exchanged with the world, and yet nobody concludes that digestion should be individuated by my DNA or stopped at the membrane of my own cells — digestion just is the activity of the whole coupled system. She then maps this onto cognition: my phone, my notebooks, and the search tools and LLM assistants I now offload drafting and recall onto are acquired rather than innate, are swapped in and out as circumstances change, and do cognitive work I cannot do alone, so the intuition that mind ends at the skull is exactly as parochial as the intuition that digestion ends at my genome. She uses the analogy for two purposes: first, to support the Clark-and-Chalmers parity claim (if a process is functionally isomorphic to an internal one, it counts as cognitive); and second, to answer the standard objection that extended parts of a mind, unlike brain parts, can be lost, lent, or shared — her reply being that the microbiome is also disruptible by antibiotics and transmissible to another person, and we do not on that account shrink the digestive system.

Please audit the analogy. First, give me a correspondence table with four columns: source element (some part of the microbiome picture), target element (the corresponding part of the extended-mind claim), what work the mapping is doing in her argument, and whether the mapping holds or breaks there. Cover at least: the genome, the microbes, the antibiotics, the transmissibility of microbes between people, and the layer of the analogy where acquired-versus-innate does the heavy lifting. Then separate the disanalogies that matter for each of her two purposes from the ones that don't — I particularly want to know whether the fact that internal states are also acquired, plastic, and exchangeable-with-the-world (consider how much of my brain's vocabulary and memories came from outside it) cuts for her or against her. Finally, return a JSON object with three fields: "parity_verdict", which must be one of "supported", "weakened", "undermined", or "untouched" plus a one-sentence reason; "detachability_verdict", in the same format, for her response to the lost-lent-or-shared objection, including whether the cases really are parallel given that two people cannot share one hippocampus but can share one notebook; and "best_variant", one sentence stating the strongest defensible version of her analogy, if any survives the audit.
