I'm on the board of my 40-person housing co-op, and we're stuck on a genuinely weird budget fight that I suspect is an actual philosophy problem wearing a work shirt. We have about $4,800 left in this year's communal improvement fund, which normally goes to things everyone uses a bit of -- patio heaters, a bike shed, replacing the dying shared grill. One resident, Dmitri, has submitted a formal proposal that all of it go to renovating the workshop in the basement that he personally uses, and his argument is basically: he gets vastly more enjoyment per dollar than anyone else does from shared amenities. He points out that the rest of us already have cars, bikes, gyms, grills of our own, so the marginal utility of another shared amenity is close to zero for most of the co-op, whereas he says he'd experience "an intensity of daily satisfaction" the rest of us apparently can't (his words, he wrote it like that), and that pooling money for the greatest total happiness is literally what a co-op is for. He even offered to document his enjoyment -- a journal, before/after mood surveys, the whole thing.

At our last meeting it split the board roughly in half. Priya says this is textbook Nozick's utility monster and that if our budget policy would hand everything to the one person who claims to feel things hardest, the policy is broken and so maybe is the utilitarian reasoning behind it -- utility isn't fungible across people like that, and "I feel it more" is unverifiable anyway. Tomas says that's a dodge: we already make interpersonal-ish comparisons constantly (we spend more on accessibility ramps because some residents need them more), we don't refuse to allocate based on intensity just because measurement is messy, and if we're honest, giving Dmitri everything IS what maximize-total-welfare reasoning commits us to -- refusing on gut grounds just means our policy was never actually utilitarian. Priya's comeback was that accessibility isn't the same, because it's about a floor of functioning, not feeding whoever claims the biggest appetite. Now they want my written opinion before Thursday's vote since I've been quiet so far and they'll both read whatever I circulate.

Please draft the memo I'll send to the board. Lead with the recommendation in the first sentence (approve Dmitri's proposal, reject it, or approve something conditional -- your call, but be concrete), then the reasoning. It needs to engage both of them at their strongest: don't let the utilitarian case for Dmitri be a strawman, but also don't wave away the monster worry as squeamishness. It would also help if you said something about procedure -- whether a co-op like ours should ever entertain intensity-of-enjoyment claims in budgeting, or whether there's a principled line between what Dmitri is claiming and the accessibility-style comparisons we already make. Keep it to a page, the board actually reads these.
