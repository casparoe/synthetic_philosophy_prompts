I help run a reading group on foundations of probability, and I want a diagnostic quiz titled something like "which interpretation of probability do you actually hold?" The group's members tend to identify as Bayesian or frequentist without having thought through the concrete cases where the interpretations genuinely come apart, so the quiz must sort them by their reactions to cases, not by the labels they like. Write eight to ten questions, each a short concrete scenario with two or three answer options, phrased so that no question names any school or theorist. The cases must include: a fair coin about to be flipped (does the 1/2 point to a physical disposition of the setup right now, or only to what would happen across a long run of similar flips?); the claim that the 10^100-th decimal digit of π is 7 with probability 1/10, for someone who has not computed it (can there be probabilities for facts that are determinate but unknown to us?); a single electron passing through a Stern–Gerlach apparatus before it is detected (quantum chances as ignorance or as objective features of the world); a doctor telling one patient there is a thirty percent chance this drug will work for you (single-case ascription versus implicit reference to a class of similar patients); and a weather forecaster with a well-calibrated track record (does the calibration record partly constitute the correctness of today's forecast, or merely provide evidence for it). Then give a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto: hypothetical limiting-frequency frequentism, finite-frequency views, single-case propensity, long-run propensity, subjective Bayesianism, logical or objective Bayesianism, and best-system (Humean chance) views. Where two positions cannot be separated by any of your questions, say so explicitly rather than pretending the quiz distinguishes them; and identify at least one coherent region of the logical space your questions open up that no named position occupies, with one sentence diagnosing whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
