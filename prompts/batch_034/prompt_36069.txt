i've been trying to write up my overall position on consciousness for a study group thing, and it occured to me i should sanity-check whether my various commitments actually fit together before i have to defend them in front of people. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. the hard problem is real — even a complete physical and functional story about the brain would leave it open why there's something it's like to see red.

2. our reports and judgments about consciousness, including the judgment that there's a hard problem at all, are products of ordinary cognitive machinery. a zombie or a good silicon replica of me would presumably say all the same things, report the same ineffable qualia, and insist just as loudly that it isn't a zombie.

3. the meta-problem — explaining, in purely mechanistic terms, why we come to judge that consciousness is hard to explain, roughly the project Chalmers sketched — is a well-posed scientific problem, and i'd actually bet it gets substantially solved eventually, at least in outline.

4. but a full solution to the meta-problem would NOT dissolve the hard problem. it would explain why i make certain noises and judgments, and the residual question — is there really phenomenal consciousness — would survive intact.

5. illusionism is a coherent position but false. phenomenal consciousness isn't an introspective illusion, at least not in any way that makes saying "it exists" a mistake.

6. zombies are ideally conceivable but metaphysically impossible.

7. physical causal closure: phenomenal properties, if they're real, are either identical to / realized by physical properties, or they do no causal work whatsoever.

go through these and tell me which combinations genuinely clash, which only seem to clash on a first pass, and what the cheapest repair would be for each genuine conflict. i don't want a checklist where every pair gets flagged — i'd rather you find the two or three real problems and rank them by severity, and for each one say which commitment has to give (or whether softening one, rather than dropping it, would be enough, and which one that would be). i honestly can't tell whether the trouble, if there is any, is somewhere in 2-4 or in how 5 sits with 7, so don't just echo my hunches back at me.
