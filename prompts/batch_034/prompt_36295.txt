So this just landed in my lap and I need to get something written before our next exec board meeting. I'm on the executive board of a small local, around 500 members, mostly warehouse and drivers. We just came off a two-week strike that ended three weeks ago, and it went about as well as these things go — we won some of what we wanted, everyone's exhausted, feelings are still raw.

Here's the issue. During the second week of the strike, one of our members crossed the picket line and worked her shifts. She's a single mom, was already behind on rent, and she'd applied for strike fund support late — like a week after the deadline. Our fund rules are pretty rigid about the window (partly because the fund itself was running low by week two), so her application was denied on a technicality, and she went back to work two days later. No secret about it, she's never denied it, and she said something at the general meeting like she couldn't let her kid get evicted for a fight that was mostly about scheduling language anyway.

Now the board is split three ways and it's getting ugly. One faction wants her expelled under our bylaws, arguing that solidarity is the whole point — if crossing the line when it gets hard is an option, then the strike threat is empty and next time everyone's hardship will conveniently arrive right on schedule. Second faction wants a formal censure plus a fine (the bylaws allow fines for "conduct unbefitting a member," which is doing a lot of work there). Third faction says anything punitive is insane: we're a small local, she's a member in good standing otherwise, and punishing the poorest member for being poor is a great way to make the case for every anti-union organizer who's ever said unions force people to choose between their kids and their dues.

I'm supposed to circulate a written recommendation before Thursday. What I keep running into is that this doesn't feel like a pure rules question — it's more like the classic thing where collective action only works if enough people hold the line, but the burden of holding it lands very unevenly, and the fund we built precisely to spread that burden had a hole in it exactly where she fell through. Expel her and we're saying the union's promise of mutual aid is conditional in the one situation it was designed for. Do nothing and the next strike starts with everyone privately calculating their own walk-away point.

Draft me the memo: a clear recommendation (expulsion, censure and fine, some third option, or no action at all), plus reasoning the board can actually act on. Deal with the strongest argument on the losing side honestly — whichever side loses, someone on this board is going to be furious, and I'd rather the memo show we took them seriously. Also worth addressing: whether we should fix the strike fund window thing, or if changing the rule mid-aftermath looks like we're just reacting to one sympathetic case. And keep in mind our contract talks come back around in under two years, so whatever precedent we set here is one we'll live with next time the line actually matters.
