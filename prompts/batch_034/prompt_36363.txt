I've been trying to work out when people count as doing something *together* versus just doing similar things at the same time, going off my gut on cases, and I can't find the principle. Maybe you can.

My neighbors and I agreed in advance to repaint the shared fence last Saturday, split it down the middle, that's obviously a joint action. Two strangers who both lunge for the same runaway suitcase at the airport and end up steadying it between them -- coordinated, mutually responsive, but my gut says they're not doing anything together, they're both just grabbing a suitcase. One person starts clapping at the end of a gig and the whole room joins in over about two seconds, no plan, most of them didn't even look around that carefully -- but this feels like a collective act in a way the suitcase thing doesn't. A flash mob where every participant memorized their instructions from a video and none of them have ever met or spoken: together, obviously. Two chess players are doing something together even though their goals are strictly opposed -- they're jointly playing a game, not jointly trying to win it. Everyone panic-selling in a market crash, each deciding alone: one movement, but not a joint action. Two labs independently running the same experiment because they both read the same paper: not together. A mob spontaneously smashing shop windows after a lost final -- no agreement, no leader, and yet I want to say the mob did it, not just each individual did their own window.

So agreement seems neither necessary (ovation, mob) nor sufficient?? -- two roommates who both independently and secretly decide to freeze out a third roommate have matching plans but no, that's not a joint action, they're just coincidentally snubbing in unison. And the chess thing messes up anything about a "shared goal" -- whatever they share it isn't the goal.

Is there one account that gets all of these? If not, tell me which verdicts to throw out and why. And whatever principle you land on, I want to know what it says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: the two roommates find out about each other's snubbing halfway through, neither says anything, and they both keep doing it, each now aware of the other -- does it become joint at that point, and if so, what exactly got added?
