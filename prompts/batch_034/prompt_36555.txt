I've been following the panpsychism literature for a few years now and the combination problem part feels completely stuck. The panpsychists keep offering solutions -- phenomenal bonding, treating subject-summing as a brute necessary truth, constitutive emergence of a higher-level subject -- and the critics keep replying that none of these explain anything, they just relabel the mystery, but neither side ever says what would count as an explanation that isn't brute. My hypothesis is that the real disagreement is about standards for explanatory sufficiency: the panpsychist is happy bottoming out at micro-experience plus some combining relation, the critic wants something mechanism-shaped, and there's no shared criterion for when grounding is allowed to stop. Is that right? Or is there a hidden substantive issue doing the work, like the boundary problem -- why exactly these micro-subjects combine into me and not some slightly different overlapping set, given that the physical facts don't obviously fix the carving? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, and tell me honestly whether anything (a formal theory of grounding, a clear criterion for when no further explanation is owed) could move it, or whether both sides are just reporting bedrock intuitions about emergence that can't be argued for.
