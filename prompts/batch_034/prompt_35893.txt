My mom is 71 and has had frontotemporal dementia for about four years now. Some background that matters here. For the first ~45 years of her life she was a serious meat-and-potatoes person, use to kind of mock vegetarians. Then in her mid-50s she read some stuff about factory farming, went vegan overnight, and for the last twenty years before the diagnosis it was basically her whole identity — volunteered at a farm sanctuary, wouldn't cook meat in her own house, the works. Now with the disease she's lost most of the "newer" stuff and is in a lot of ways back to who she was decades ago. She asks for cheeseburgers constantly, thinks the sanctuary people are weird strangers who keep bothering her, and if you mention veganism she laughs and says that was a silly phase she went through.

Here's the fight in the family. My sister says we honor the person mom spent twenty years becoming — the vegan was her real self, and the person asking for cheeseburgers is the disease talking, so we feed her vegan food even though she complains about it. My brother says that's cruel — the person in front of us who lights up when she gets a cheeseburger is HER, right now, and making her unhappy to stay loyal to a version of her that doesn't exist anymore is treating her like a monument instead of a person.

And then there's one more layer I can't get out of my head. Years ago, back when she was still the vegan, my mom told me more than once that if she ever got dementia we shouldn't let the disease undo who she'd become — she half-joked about putting it in writing but never did. So it's not just old-her vs. current-her, it's like current-her, past-her, and past-her's instructions about future-her, and I honestly don't know how to stack those up. Who do we actually owe things to here? Is "what she would want" even a well-defined thing, and if it is, which "she" is the one in that sentence — the one making the request today, the one she was for twenty years, or some version of her thinking clearly about her whole life? My sister and brother are both pretty dug in, and since mom lives with me I'm the one making the call every day. Help me think this thru.
