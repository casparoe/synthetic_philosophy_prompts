been going down the consciousness rabbit hole for a couple years now (no philosophy background, i just read stuff and think about it on my commute) and I think I've landed somewhere. want to check it hangs together before I embarrass myself arguing about it at thanksgiving. here's roughly what I believe:

1. I'm a hard materialist — no souls, no spooky stuff, everything that exists is physical.
2. but I've never heard a good answer to why brain activity feels like anything from the inside, and I don't think neuroscience will ever answer it, because you could map every circuit in the brain and still not know why theres experience at all instead of just processing in the dark.
3. so I've come around to thinking experience has to be baked into matter itself somehow — like even an electron has some tiny flicker of it. nothing you'd call a thought or a feeling, just the raw ingredient.
4. at the same time I'm sure my individual neurons aren't conscious. I am, the whole brain is, but the parts aren't — a neuron is just a cell doing cell stuff.
5. I'm confident a worm is conscious in some dim way and equally confident a thermostat isn't, and a rock isn't. there's a real line somewhere even if its blurry.
6. the split-brain thing, where cutting the connection between the hemispheres seems to give you two consciousnesses in one skull — I find that totally convincing, and honestly it feels like my strongest card: cut the wiring and one experience becomes two, so experience must come from how the parts are hooked up together.

I have a nagging feeling some of these are pulling against each other but I genuinely can't tell which ones. go through the list and tell me which actually conflict, which only look like they conflict, and if something has to give, what the cheapest thing to give up is. rank the problems by how bad they are instead of listing everything as equally fatal. and don't just reassure me that it all works — I'd rather find out now than at thanksgiving.
