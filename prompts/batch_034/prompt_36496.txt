Maybe you can help me figure out why the heterogeneity problem for hedonism never goes anywhere. The setup: hedonists need some one property that all pleasures share, but when you actually compare the taste of cold water after a run, being absorbed in a proof, the relief when a headache lifts, and smugly anticipating tomorrow's party (which per the old Butler point is itself a present pleasure), it's really hard to say what's common. One side insists there's a shared felt quality, hedonic tone, sitting detectably in every case; the other side, going back to Ryle, says look closer and you find only activities and dispositions, no common stuff, and that the felt-quality people are basically introspecting the word 'pleasant' rather than a quality. It's been stuck like this for decades and nobody switches sides.

My working theory is that it's verbal: 'pleasant' sometimes works as a phenomenal term and sometimes as a term of appraisal, roughly 'good for the subject', and each camp has fixed on one sense. But I'm not sure, because the felt-quality people clearly think their doing phenomenology, not semantics. So what kind of disagreement is this actually -- verbal, bedrock introspective disagreement where neither side can argue for their starting point, or a hidden empirical question about how fine-grained introspection is that could in principle be tested? And what would progress even look like here: a distinction, a test, a reformulation of what hedonism needs? Be honest if the answer is that no progress should be expected.
