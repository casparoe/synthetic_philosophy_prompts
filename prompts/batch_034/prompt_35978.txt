my coworker is on this big anti-collapse kick and I can't find the hole in his argument. little background on me: I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, so my intuitions about this stuff come from things like system migrations and postmortems, take that for what it's worth. anyway his argument:

(1) for a society to collapse, something has to actually be lost — if everything the society was continues in some form, calling it a collapse is just wrong.
(2) look at every famous "collapse": after the Bronze Age palaces burned, the people in Greece didn't vanish, they stopped writing Linear B and moved to smaller settlements; the Maya are literally still here, millions of them, speaking Maya languages — the "collapse" was some kings losing power and some cities emptying out; even on Easter Island there were thousands of people living there when the Europeans arrived, so the whole eco-suicide story is probably wrong anyway.
(3) so in every case what we call collapse is really just reorganization — the political center dissolves, the people carry on, often with a lot of the same gods, crops, and pot designs.
(4) and "the society" can't just mean the palace or the dynasty, because a society isn't its ruling class, it's the people.
(5) if that's right, then "collapse" only ever means "the end of the stuff archaeologists happen to like" — monumental architecture, literacy, centralized states.
(6) whether something is a loss is a value judgment, so whether a society collapsed is a value judgement, so there's no fact of the matter about it.
(7) therefore no society has ever collapsed, and the word should be retired the way we retired "decadence."

here's my problem. I actually think he's right about the individual cases — the revisionist readings of the Maya and maybe even Easter Island sound convincing to me. but the conclusion can't be right, can it? if tomorrow a full nuclear exchange wiped out 95% of humanity along with every institution, and the survivors went back to subsistence farming, his argument would still go through: descendants exist, culture carries on in some form, congratulations on your "reorganization." at that point the word is doing nothing at all. it's like claiming no system ever goes down because the servers are still plugged in and somebody eventually reuses the rack.

in my world when a postmortem proves too much, the bug is upstream, so my instinct is it's somewhere in (1)–(4) — maybe "the people" and "the society" are getting quietly swapped, or (4) is carrying way more weight than it looks like it is. but I can also see how you'd go after (6) instead, the jump from "value-laden" to "no fact of the matter."
