Been trying to work out what I actually think privacy is by going with my gut on cases, and I want to know if there's a principle behind my reactions. Someone climbing a ladder to peer into my bathroom: huge violation, even though they never tell a soul and nothing bad happens to me because of it. A stranger on the bus overhearing my half of a phone call about my divorce: not really a violation, I just sat in an unlucky spot. A friend telling a mutual acquaintance about the divorce without asking me first: definitely a violation, even though the acquaintance would probably have found out eventually anyway. A stalker following me around all day, only through public places, never approaching: a serious violation, even though everything he sees, anyone on the street could see. A data broker aggregating my online purchases, location history, and searches into a profile and selling it: violation, and somehow the more it's aggregated the worse it feels, even though no single piece was exactly a secret. My sister publishing a memoir with detailed stories about my teenage breakdown, my name changed: I keep flip-flopping, but I can't make myself think it's as bad as the ladder guy, even though it's the widest dissemination of the bunch. So is there one principle that captures all of this, or close? The pair I really can't square is the bus vs the stalker — both are just someone perceiving me in public without my permission, but only one feels wrong to me, and I don't think that's just about the stalker's intentions, since the ladder guy could have the most benign intentions imaginable. And whatever principle you land on, what does it say about a case I haven't figured out: a smart speaker in my home that continuously records everything, but the audio is only ever processed by an algorithm and no human ever listens?
