The cases and my verdicts:

1. My sister (botanist) and her husband (line cook) on whether a tomato is a fruit. Verdict: totally verbal, there's nothing left over once you know which sense each is using.

2. A guy in my reading group who insists only simples exist -- there are atoms arranged chairwise, but no chair -- against everyone else in the room. Verdict: verbal. I don't think he disagrees with us about anything except which sentences to utter.

3. Whether Australia is a continent or just a really big island. Verbal, same as the tomato.

4. Whether a fetus is a person, between my very religious uncle and my atheist cousin at Thanksgiving. Verdict: NOT verbal. That's a real moral fight, and it drives me nuts when people wave it off as semantics.

5. Whether a chess engine that plays better than any human "really understands" chess. Verdict: honestly I keep flip-flopping, this is the one I'd most like a principle to settle.

6. My finitist friend says there's no completed totality of all natural numbers; everyone else thinks there obviously is. Verdict: not verbal -- someone here is wrong about math, not about words. Though I'm less sure of this one than I sound.

The chair guy from #2 claims the fetus case is verbal too: "person" is a term of art, the uncle and cousin are just running it with different thresholds, exactly like "fruit." I refuse, but I can't say why without sounding arbitrary. My tentative test was: a dispute is verbal when, once each side fully grasps the other's usage, nothing is left to disagree about. But the uncle and cousin DO fully grasp each other's usage -- their leftover fight is about moral status -- so by my test they're verbal too. Unless "verbal" secretly means two things: disputes with nothing left over, versus disputes where the leftovers are somebody else's problem (ethics, not semantics)?

So: is there a principle that gets all my verdicts, especially #4 against #1 and #2? If you have to overturn one of my verdicts, tell me which and why. And say what the principle predicts for the chess engine and the finitist, since those are the ones I can't decide. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning -- no preamble, no restating my question back at me.
