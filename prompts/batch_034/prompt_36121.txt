My closest friend and I have been arguing about the same question for the better part of two years, and after last night's session — which ended, as usual, with neither of us conceding anything — I have started to suspect the argument is not really about what it appears to be about. The question is whether reality must have a fundamental level: whether the chain of grounding, in which some facts or entities hold in virtue of others, must eventually bottom out in something that holds in virtue of nothing further. She takes the terminationist side, in the spirit of Jonathan Schaffer's work on grounding and priority monism: there had better be a ground of being — perhaps the cosmos as a whole rather than any part of it — because a structure with no bottom either posits brute facts at every level or offers an explanatory regress that explains nothing. I take the infinitist line, associated with philosophers like Ricki Bliss and Ross Cameron: there is nothing incoherent about every fact being grounded in further facts, so long as each link in the chain is intelligible; the demand for a terminus is a psychological craving dressed up as a metaphysical requirement. Neither of us has moved an inch, and I notice we keep cycling through the same three or four moves — her claim that an infinite explanation is no explanation, my reply that brute facts are worse than infinite descent, her comeback that worse needs an argument I never supply.

What I want from you is not a verdict on who is right but a diagnosis of the stall. Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement at all, or is it partly verbal — are we using the word fundamental to pick out different things, she the level at which explanation terminates, I whatever stands in no grounding relation to anything else? Is a hidden empirical question doing some of the work — whether chains of dependence actually terminate looks like something physics could inform even if it cannot settle — or are we answering different questions under a single heading? My own hypothesis, offered strictly as a hypothesis, is that the debate is a proxy war about the nature of explanation: she holds that explanation is by its very nature well-founded, so that a chain that never terminates explains nothing at any point, while I hold that explanation requires only that each step be intelligible, not that the whole chain have a last link. If that is right, the metaphysical vocabulary — fundamentality, brute facts, vicious regress — is mostly scenery around a dispute in the philosophy of explanation. Test my hypothesis against what each of us actually says, and tell me where it fits and where it breaks.

Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress. Would it help to observe that mathematics has no trouble with non-well-founded structures — there are rigorous, coherent theories of non-well-founded sets — or does that show only that infinite descent is consistent, which neither of us ever doubted? Is there a distinction someone could draw, or a reformulation of the question, that would dissolve the disagreement, or should we expect no progress at all because the difference lies in starting intuitions that neither side can argue for without begging the question? And if it does come down to bedrock, say so plainly: I would rather learn that the argument is structurally endless than keep having it under the illusion that one more exchange will settle it.
