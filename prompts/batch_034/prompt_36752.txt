I've been asked to write up my position for a seminar on the ethics of belief, and I'd like a consistency check before I submit it. My commitments are roughly these: (1) I accept a broadly Cliffordian principle that it is always wrong, for anyone, to believe anything on insufficient evidence. (2) I also accept James's point that when a genuine option — live, forced, and momentous — cannot be settled by the intellect alone in advance, we are entitled to let our passional nature decide it. (3) I hold that belief is not under direct voluntary control: one cannot simply decide to believe something at will. (4) Nonetheless, I think people are properly blamed for self-deception. (5) On specific cases: I think religious faith is impermissible, but I think it is fine for a student facing a difficult exam to believe she will pass, and fine to believe a seriously ill friend will recover, even when the evidence for each is thin. Please audit this set: tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and what the cheapest revision would be for each genuine one. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
