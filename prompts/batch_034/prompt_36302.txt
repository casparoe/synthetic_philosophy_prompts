Not sure this thing I've noticed has a proper name, but I've been going round in circles with my brother-in-law about the mind and I keep needing a concept I don't have, so here goes.

He holds a view I'll describe in plain words, since I don't know its official label either: he thinks conscious experience -- the felt ache of a toothache, the look of red -- is a by-product of the brain that doesn't itself push anything around. Like the whistle on a steam engine: it doesn't drive the train, it just rides along. On his picture, the ache doesn't cause me to say "ouch" or to believe I'm in pain; the brain does all that, and the ache is just there, alongside it.

Here's the pattern I can't name. If he's right, then my utterance "I have a toothache" is a chain of physical events (nerve signals, muscle movements, sound waves) and the ache itself contributes nothing to that chain. So the utterance would be bit-for-bit identical in a world just like ours except that no ache ever accompanies those brain events. Which means my pain report isn't caused by the pain its a report of -- it's caused by something else that merely happens to travel with the pain. And then the reflexive bit that really bugs me: on his own view, his belief in his view was caused entirely by brain events too, so the ache played no role in his arriving at the by-product theory. The theory seems to saw off the branch it climbed up on. Every argument he's ever made for it is a physical happening that would have happened identically in a world with no experiences at all, so what makes it evidence for the view rather than just noise?

I also can't tell whether this is one thing or two: (a) reports of experience aren't caused by experience, and (b) the theorist's belief in the theory is in the same boat. Is (b) just (a) with the theory plugged in as the report?

Things it's near but, I think, not identical to: general scepticism about whether beliefs track anything outside themselves (that worry applies to everyone, whereas this one is generated by his view specifically); and the party trick where you imagine a person behaving identically with no inner life at all (that's about other people's behaviour, mine is about the causal pedigree of reports and beliefs about experience). When I put it to him he shrugged and said the report is caused by the brain state that accompanies the ache, so it tracks the pain via its correlate -- but I want to know whether that reply has a literature behind it or is just him improvising.

So, three asks, and please don't hand me a bare term and stop: first, does this configuration have an established name in the philosophy of mind; second, how does it differ from each of the nearby things I listed, and does it collapse into any of them; third, if there's no established name, coin one, define it so it covers both (a) and (b) or explains why they're seperate, and give a boundary case where the definition gets fuzzy. I'm after the carving, not the label.
