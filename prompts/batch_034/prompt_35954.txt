Next week's guest on my podcast, which is aimed at philosophy students, is a philosopher who defends Lewis-style counterpart theory and rejects transworld identity — she holds that strictly speaking I exist in only one possible world, and that what is true when we say I could have done otherwise is something about my counterparts in other worlds. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the view. At least one should press the familiar objection that this turns claims about what a person could have done into claims about what some relevantly similar other person did, and ask her directly what she thinks is lost or gained by that reformulation; one should probe what grounds the counterpart relation — why qualitative similarity rather than, say, some causal or origin-based relation should decide who counts as my counterpart, and what she does about the vagueness that introduces at the edges; and one should ask how the theory handles ordinary first-person modal claims like that I could have missed the flight I in fact caught, without letting it slide into the claim that someone very like me missed it. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, so that I have somewhere to go if she answers in the standard way. Please skip generic questions about her background or what drew her to philosophy.
