My driving instructor always told me to stop driving the moment I know I'm too tired to drive safely. Sounds simple enough, but my friend and I got into a weird argument about it on a long trip last weekend, and it's been bugging me since.

Here's the thing. For the first few hours I was totally fine. By the end I was definately not okay — heavy eyes, catching my mind wandering. So somewhere in between I must have crossed a line where it first became true that I was too tired to drive. But I couldn't point to when that happened. It's not like a switch flipped — each minute felt basically the same as the one just before it, so what exactly would I have "noticed" at the crucial moment?

My friend says this doesn't matter: once I crossed the line, there was a fact of the matter, and I just needed to look inward and notice the drowsiness. If I'd checked, I would've known, so if I didn't know it's because I wasn't paying attention, not because the knowledge wasn't available.

I think that underestimates the problem. If the transition is gradual, then right at the boundary, the state I'm in looks almost exactly like the state one step before it, when it wasn't yet true. So even being maximally attentive, I couldn't reliably tell those apart. Which makes me wonder whether the moment of "knowing" it even exists in the clean way my instructor's rule assumes.

So who's closer to the truth, me or my friend? And is there a difference here between knowing I'm too tired, and knowing *that* I know it — does the rule require both somehow?
