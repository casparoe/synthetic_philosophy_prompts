i've got to write a short position piece for an archaeology-adjacent reading group on whether "collapse" is a real thing or just a word we use for other people's history, and before i do i want someone to audit my actual views, because i suspect i believe incompatible stuff. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) "collapse" is mostly a pejorative for transformation. the Classic Maya didn't collapse — the states dissolved, the cities emptied, but millions of Maya people are still there, speaking Maya languages. calling that a collapse says more about our obsession with monuments than about what happened to the people. same energy with the "bronze age collapse," which looks more like a rough transition between systems than a fall from something.

(2) i'm fairly convinced by the diminishing-returns-on-complexity story (tainter's line): societies invest in complexity to solve problems, the marginal returns fall, and at some point simplification is the rational, even adaptive response. sometimes a society is better off smaller and simpler.

(3) easter island is my exception and i'll die on this hill: that was a genuine, self-inflicted ecological catastrophe. they cut down the forest, the whole system — deep-sea canoes, soil, the moai economy — crashed, and the society that came out the other side was radically poorer. i know there's a revisionist story where rats and later slave raids did most of the damage, and honestly i think that's motivated contrarianism.

(4) whatever i say about the ancient cases, failed states today — think yemen — are genuinely failing, not "transforming." there's a real normative difference between change and failure and we shouldn't blur it.

(5) if i had to give the criterion, it's welfare and bodies, not institutions: a real collapse is when lots of people die or get drastically poorer, not when a political sytem reorganizes.

(6) and i think archaeologists should retire "collapse" as an explanatory concept — it doesn't do any scientific work beyond making headlines.

so: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they conflict, and for each real tension how bad is it and what's the cheapest fix? please rank by severity rather than treating them all as equally damning, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most if i had to make one. also, wherever the answer hinges on contested empirical stuff — how bad the maya droughts actually were, whether the population really crashed or just moved, whether the rats thing really exonerates the rapanui — flag it as contested rather than settled, because i don't want to write the piece resting on an empirical claim that's still up in the air and get taken apart by someone who's read more of the literature than me.
