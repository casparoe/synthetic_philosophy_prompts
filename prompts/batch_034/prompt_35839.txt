Here's my profile on time for the reading group -- they want to poke at it, and before I send it round I want a consistancy check. Tell me which of my commitments pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what the cheapest fix would be in each case. Order the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad.

The package:

1. I'm a growing-blocker. The past exists, the future doesn't yet, and the present is the objective edge where new being gets added.
2. I'm convinced by the Rietdijk–Putnam style argument -- the Andromeda thing: two people walking past each other at different speeds disagree about which events on Andromeda have already happened, and neither of them is wrong. I take the lesson to be that "already happened" is frame-relative; there's just no fact of the matter about which distant events are past.
3. I don't think future physics will bail us out here. Quantum gravity or whatever comes next won't give us back a law-governed preferred foliation -- the relativity principle survives into whatever theory replaces special relativity.
4. On the other hand, our actual universe does have a cosmic time -- the CMB frame, the 13.8 billion years -- and I'm tempted to say the block grows along that: the edge of being just is the surface of constant cosmic time.
5. Part of why I prefer growing block over eternalism is phenomenology: it explains the feel of time passing, because at the edge things genuinely come to be, and we're riding that edge.

One thing I want checked rather than assumed: I have a feeling 2 is usually wheeled out against presentists, and I'm not a presentist, so maybe I'm fine? That's the sort of thing you should verify, not take my word for. I have a hunch the trouble is somewhere around 2 and 3, but honestly I'm not sure, and I'd rather you worked it out yourself.

And second, single out the one point in your own audit you're least sure of, so I know which bit to double-check myself.

Remember: an audit, not a lecture on the philosophy of time. Assume I know the standard positions -- what I need to know is where *my* set breaks.
