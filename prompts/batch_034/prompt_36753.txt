So heres my situation: I'm a science and ideas writer, and last year I did a piece on those experiments where brain activity predicting a person's decision shows up before they report having decided. Readers loved it, and a bunch of them emailed me some version of the same question — did my conscious choice do anything at all, or was it just my brain doing stuff and me narrating it after the fact? I gave a hand-wavy answer and it's been bugging me since.

I've now gone down the rabbit hole on the causal exclusion argument (Kim's stuff, mostly), and I get the shape of it: the physical world is causally closed, the mental isn't identical to anything physical (if you're a non-reductivist), and you can't have the mental event and its physical base independently causing the bodily movement without either messy overdetermination or the mental event doing zero work. So it looks like the "I raised my arm because I decided to" story is either reducible or causally idle. Fine. But I've also read people who think this whole thing rests on a production-style picture of causation, and if causation is more like counterfactual dependence, the exclusion problem mostly evaporates. I honestly can't tell whether that's a genuine escape route or a dodge.

So here's what I want from you: your actual credence that a given mental event — say, my deciding to raise my arm — is genuinely causally relevant to my arm going up, in a way that isn't just the neural machinery doing all the work. Not a vibes answer, a number, with the decomposition. Things like: probability the physical is causally closed in the relevant sense, probability that mental-to-physical reduction fails, probability that interventionist/dependence accounts of causation can dissolve the exclusion worry rather than just paper over it, and anything else you think belongs in there — assign each a rough probability with a line of justification, then show me how they combine. For reference, I'm hovering around 0.65 myself but I don't trust my own decomposition at all.

And if there's an argument or consideration that would genuinely shift your estimate by a lot, I want to hear what it is, not just "more research needed."
