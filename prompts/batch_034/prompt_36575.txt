I've been put in charge of the giving pot for a group of friends (couple hundred k a year, nothing huge but not pocket change either) and I tried writing down my actual views on cause prioritization so the others can poke at them. Before I circulate it I want a sanity check on whether the thing hangs together. Here's the list:

(1) The scale / neglectedness / tractability framework is basically right: you score a cause on each of the three, roughly multiply, and the product ranks the causes. Neglctedness in particular is doing real work — money flows into visible things and dry spells are where the returns are.

(2) I'm a plain consequentialist about giving: for any decision above some size you should write down explicit probabilities, estimate expected value, and pick the max.

(3) I think returns to effort in most causes are S-shaped, not linear. The first dollars into a young cause often accomplish almost nothing — you can't half-found a field, there's a threshold before anything starts working — and there are also tipping points where a fairly modest push unlocks a lot, and then saturation where more money does nothing again.

(4) Under moral uncertainty I split our money across worldviews (some global health, some animal welfare, some x-risk) instead of going all in on whichever cause wins the calculation, because I don't trust any single moral view enough.

(5) Concretely: if a new donor with real money asked me where to put it, I'd say mostly AI safety, even with the billions already flowing into it, because the expected value still dominates everything else on the menu.

(6) For small and mid-size donors I'm against diversifying within a cause — put the whole budget on the best-scoring option. Diversification is a portfolio idea and doesn't belong in moral decisions.

So, audit time. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and for the real conflicts how bad is it and what would have to give? Don't assume I've already picked a side to drop — I genuinely don't know where the problem is.

Then "The real tensions" — the worst two or three, ordered by severity, roughly 150-250 words each, saying exactly what conflicts and what giving up either side costs me.
