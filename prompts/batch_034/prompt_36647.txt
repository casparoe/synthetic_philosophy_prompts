In my evening philosophy seminar we have a running game we call "does this person count as justified?", and one member of the group has appointed herself the arbiter. Her track record so far: she ruled justified the birdwatcher whose identification of a rare warbler traces down through field marks, through expectations set by the guidebook, and finally to the raw look of the bird itself, adding that if anyone pressed him further he could always have said more about the lighting and the angle. She ruled unjustified the amateur astrologer whose chart readings all support one another in a closed loop that never touches anything outside itself, a verdict she delivered with the remark that it was a small circle spinning on itself. She ruled unjustified the man whose reason for his belief, when pursued far enough, turned out to be the original belief again in different words. And she ruled justified the scholar who, for any challenge you might raise about his technical question, can always produce one further source, and then one further after that, with no end in sight — though I should note that his conclusion also happens to fit everything else he happens to believe.

At next week's meeting we intend to bring her two fresh cases. In the first, a woman's belief sits inside a large, mutually supporting web of beliefs that have served her well in prediction, none of which is self-evident or purely perceptual — each one is supported only by other members of the web, but the web is broad, and it has repeatedly kept her right about things. In the second, a man can produce, on demand, an indefinitely long and non-repeating series of reasons for his belief, each individually plausible, but the series never reaches anything he would call self-evident, never loops back on itself, and never once mentions what he sees or feels. What will she say about each? I am less interested in the two answers themselves than in the rules her verdicts so far leave open — a foundationalist reading, a coherentist reading, an infinitist reading, or something less tidy — and in whether either new case forces those candidates apart. The astrologer ruling seems to have already cost pure coherentism its seat at the table, though I have a friend who insists she is not running any rule at all and is simply tracking whether a person has somewhere to go when challenged.

So, three deliverables. First, lay out the candidate rules that remain consistent with her four rulings, including any qualified versions that survive, and say what each one predicts for the two new cases. Second, give your best guess as to her verdicts, with your reasons for preferring it. And third, if her precedents genuinely underdetermine her answer, say what she would be committing herself to with each possible verdict, and propose the single further case — one we could bring to the meeting after next — that would most cleanly reveal which rule she is actually running.
