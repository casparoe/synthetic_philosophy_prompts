I am a novelist, and I have run into a wall of a philosophical kind. My current book's protagonist is an illiterate woman in a remote fishing settlement who cannot add a column of figures, misreads maps entirely, and would fail any examination ever devised — and yet she is extraordinary at sizing up strangers, anticipating storms from the behavior of gulls and the smell of the water, keeping a fractious community from collapse, and knowing, long before anyone else does, exactly whom not to trust. Readers of early drafts keep asking me whether she is "really intelligent," and I keep being unsure what I am being asked.

When I put this to a psychologist friend of mine, he waved the question away with the old line that intelligence is whatever the tests measure. On his telling, my question about my character is not a question at all: she will never sit a test, so there is nothing further to be said. This dismissal troubles me, because the sentence seems to be doing far more work than it can bear. If temperature is whatever a thermometer reads, that is arguably a useful shorthand — but surely the parallel is not obvious, since thermometers were built to track something about the world that we can independently characterize, whereas the case for tests is supposed to be exactly what is in dispute. And even if the slogan were true in some sense, I do not see how it would make my question meaningless rather than merely hard.

Please lay out, as an ordered menu of readings, the distinct precise claims that the sentence "intelligence is whatever the tests measure" could be expressing — from the most modest operationalist platitude to the most radical deflationary or social-constructivist readings, including any causal or natural-kind readings on which the sentence would simply be false. For each reading, tell me three things: whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false; what would count for or against it; and — this is the part I most need — whether it actually supports my friend's move of dismissing my question about the character. I would also like your view on which reading my friend most likely intended, and whether that reading, on inspection, licenses what he wanted it to license. Finally, since you will note that some readings bear directly on whether my character's kind of excellence counts as intelligence (a unitary capacity, a family of loosely related abilities, a matter of fit between capacities and environment), close by telling me which reading, if any, a novelist in my position could honestly adopt in deciding how to write and describe her. I would rather not receive a survey of names and schools; I want the reasoning itself, worked through.
