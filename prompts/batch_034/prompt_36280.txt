After a four-hour argument with my oldest friend, I have come to realize that we do not disagree about any observable fact concerning large language models, and I would like a diagnosis of what our disagreement actually is.

Here is where we ended up. We agree that current models pass most casual conversational tests, produce competent summaries and proofs, and fail in characteristic ways on novel planning tasks and long-horizon coherence. We agree that their competence comes from prediction over text rather than from anything like human development. We both know the shape of the literature: Searle's Chinese Room and the various replies to it; the argument that a system trained only on form can never acquire meaning, illustrated by the octopus intercepting messages between islanders; the skeptical labeling of these models as stochastic parrots; and the optimists' replies that human brains might also be doing sophisticated next-token-style prediction under another description. Yet after all this agreement, my friend insists the models do not understand anything, and I insist that whatever they do is close enough to understanding that denying them the word is a choice, not a discovery.

My friend's closing move was an analogy I have not been able to shake: nobody disputes that a chess engine really plays chess, even though it does not play the way Kasparov does. So why is "understanding" different from "playing chess"? His answer is that "understanding" is anchored to a human paradigm case in a way that "playing chess" is not, so our dispute is really about eligibility criteria for a word, not about the models at all. My counter is that this makes the debate merely verbal, which feels wrong: we both think something substantive is at stake, perhaps about grounding, about whether meaning requires a connection to the world that text prediction cannot supply.

Is this disagreement verbal, substantive, or two different questions under one heading — say, a question about the mechanism's origin versus a question about the resulting competence? And is "understanding" more like "playing chess" (settled by performance), like "life" (a cluster concept that engineering keeps forcing us to revise, as with viruses and synthetic cells), or like "water" (where empirical investigation fixed the term's extension)? Finally, given your diagnosis, I want an honest answer about what would count as progress: a sharpened distinction, a specific behavioral or architectural test, or nothing short of one of us revising our starting intuitions about what the word is for.
