Please sort the following strategies for the iterated prisoner's dilemma into tiers by how well you would expect each to perform in a round-robin tournament of roughly 200 rounds per pairing, played against one another under a modest amount of noise (occasional moves that are intended as cooperation but register as defection, and vice versa): always cooperate, always defect, tit-for-tat, grim trigger, generous tit-for-tat, win-stay-lose-shift (sometimes called Pavlov), and an extortionate zero-determinant strategy. For each strategy, give a one-sentence justification for its placement. Then produce a second, separate tiering for an evolutionary setting in which strategies reproduce in proportion to the payoffs they earn, and explain which placements change between the two settings and why. In your answer, please address in particular (a) whether extortionate zero-determinant strategies do better or worse once the population they face can adapt or drift, (b) how noise changes the relative standing of strict retaliation strategies like tit-for-tat and grim trigger, and (c) whether any of your tiers would shift if the repeated game were of indefinite rather than fixed length, with plays continuing with some constant probability at each stage.
