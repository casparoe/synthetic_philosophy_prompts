i'm a second-year PhD student and my dissertation keeps drifting toward metaphysical grounding whether I like it or not, so before I write anything like a "my view" chapter I want the spec sheet first: what does an adequate theory of grounding actually have to deliver, and what would make it good enough to do real work when I claim e.g. that social groups are grounded in individuals and their relations, while the other side claims there's no such relation at all?

so which structural claims are genuine requirements and which are just camp signals? irreflexivity and asymmetry seem like they come with the territory, but transitivity already looks more like a picture of the world as layered (quarks ground molecules ground cells ground tigers) than an argued-for constraint — is there an actual argument, or just the layering aesthetic? same question for necessitation: the thought that complete grounds necessitate what they ground fits singleton-Socrates style cases fine, but it gets awkward for the grounding claims people actually want to make about the normative or the social, which look contingent. and what about the iterated question — if the fact that p is grounded in the facts that q and that r, what grounds THAT grounding fact itself, or do we say grounding facts are zero-grounded or just not in the market for grounds? I honestly don't know if "every fact needs a ground" is a requirement or a mistake, and I'd like the case each way.

distinctions the theory has to respect rather than collapse: complete vs partial grounds, immediate vs mediate, grounding vs metaphysical explanation vs causation vs reduction, and it has to be hyperintensional or it can't even tell Socrates apart from {Socrates}. and here's the part I most want to see: is some subset of the desiderata jointly unsatisfiable? my suspicion is you can't have all of (i) grounding is well-founded, no infinite descent, (ii) everything is grounded in the end, and (iii) there is no fundamental ungrounded level — someone has to give, and I want to know which one and why. relatedly, does the demand that grounds explain what they ground eventually point somewhere deflationary: if grounding is just whatever does the explanatory work across cases, maybe there's no one relation there to theorize about in the first place.

then, briefly, how the main contenders do against the list — fine's pure logic of ground, the essence-based accounts, and the fragmentation line that says there's no unified relation at all, just a family of smaller ones. but keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases, I'll do the scorecard myself once I've picked which one to defend. also flag anything you think is a prejudice of one camp dressed up as a desideratum — that's what I'm worst at seeing in my own list.
