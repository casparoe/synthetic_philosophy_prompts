I run the disagreement reviews at a mid-size reinsurance firm — whenever two of our teams give materialy different probability estimates for the same risk (a pandemic loss year, a specific counterparty defaulting within five years, whatever), my job is to figure out whether we can pool them into one number or whether the difference needs to go up the chain. I recently got interested in Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result and the whole "common prior assumption" debate behind it — the idea being that if two rational parties have common priors, then once they've shared their evidence they shouldn't still disagree, so persistent disagreement is either unshared information or a difference in priors themselves. I want that operationalized. Please write a procedure my analysts can run in a single afternoon meeting with both teams present: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure terminates in "pool the estimates" versus "escalate, because this is a prior-level difference, not an info-level one," and — this matters — which steps are honestly just judgment calls dressed up as a checklist. Then run it on two cases: (1) our epidemiology team puts 12% on a major pandemic loss year and our cat-modeling team puts 7%, and probing reveals they're feeding off different base datasets neither can fully articulate; (2) two veteran underwriters who have sat in the same meetings and seen all of each other's evidence still disagree 8% vs 25% on a counterparty defaulting, because one of them thinks the whole credit-rating framework is broken post-2008 and the other thinks that's paranoia. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I won't like, and say plainly where the procedure will misfire.
