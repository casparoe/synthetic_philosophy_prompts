My dad just retired after 38 years as editor of a mid-size regional paper, and I'm giving the speech at his retirement party. The complication: about ten years ago he got obsessed with Kierkegaard's ranting about the press and "the public" -- the Corsair affair, the leveling, the idea that the press conjures up an abstract phantom-public that no actual person belongs to but that everyone secretly defers to -- and he'd bring it up at every newsroom meeting, half joking, half not. He's convinced Kierkegaard basically predicted comment sections and share counts. The room will be maybe sixty journalists who've heard his Kierkegaard bits forever and love him, plus family whove never read a word of it. Write me a five-minute speech that actually puts his Kierkegaard thing to work -- the leveling, the phantom public, the difference between a crowd and actual readers you can picture -- but lands as a love letter to that newsroom rather than an insult to it, and that says goodbye to the whole idea of editing a paper for people you can actually imagine. He'd hate anything that sounded like a press release about himself.
