In debates over the EU's future, it is common to appeal to Switzerland: since a multilingual confederation of cantons has held together for centuries without becoming a unitary nation-state, the EU could likewise remain a "Europe of nations" — a union of sovereign states bound by treaty rather than a superstate. Please audit this analogy. First set out what maps onto what: the cantons as member states, the federal constitution as the EU treaties, direct democracy as a remedy for the EU's democratic deficit, the four national languages as the EU's many peoples, and so on. Then identify where the mapping breaks — consider the disparity in size between the largest and smallest cantons versus that between Germany and Malta, the historical formation of a shared Swiss identity prior to the modern federal state of 1848, the fact that Swiss federal law routinely overrides cantonal law and that federal referendums decide national questions directly, the fiscal equalization scheme among cantons, and anything else that strikes you. Distinguish the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion — that the EU can survive without further political integration — from those that do not, and say whether the conclusion survives once all of this is on the table. If a better analogy is available (the German Bundesrat, the United States, or something else), sketch it briefly. Please write your answer in British English.
