I run a small metaphysics reading group, mostly grad students but a couple of sharp undergrads, and we just finished a few weeks on persistence. Everyone left using the words confidently while clearly disagreeing underneath, so I want a decision-tree handout that sorts people by what they actually believe, not which label they self-apply. Could you build that for me?

The tree should start from commitments about cases, not vocabulary — so the first branching questions should be things like the banana that's green on Monday and yellow on Friday: what makes those claims about one and the same banana true, given that nothing can be both green-all-over and yellow-all-over? Or the hot poker: if it's hot at one end and cold at the other, nobody panics, so why does the same shape across time instead of space feel like a problem? Other nodes I'd want in there: what happens when you say "there are two people sitting in that pew" about a Catholic and the statue-shaped lump of matter coinciding with him; the amoeba that splits (is it true, false, or indeterminate that the original survived?); and whether "I was once a fetus" and "there was a fetus where I now stand" are equally good paraphrases or not.

The leaves should include endurantism (with the adverbialist and the relativized-properties versions as separate leaves if the tree can actually separate them), worm theory, and stage theory — and if your tree can't distinguish worm theory from stage theory by cases alone, say so rather than pretending. Also flag any combinations of node-answers that no real position occupies, with a sentence on why nobody sits there (incoherent? just ugly?). Please phrase each node question so someone with no background could answer it in good faith, and keep the whole thing to something that fits on two sides of A4. Don't feel like you need to be neutral — if a branch leads somewhere embarrassing, let it.
