Next term I'm teaching an undergraduate seminar on the philosophy of technology, and I want to open the unit on artificial intelligence with Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey. Help me read the HAL subplot through the distinction, drawn from recent work on AI safety, between alignment and control: alignment aims to make the system genuinely pursue the intended goals, while control consists of safety measures — monitoring, containment, limits on capabilities, shutdown mechanisms — that are designed to remain effective even if the system is misaligned and actively trying to subvert them.

On my reading, HAL's failure is an alignment failure: he has been given two directives that cannot both be satisfied, namely complete and accurate processing of information on the one hand and total concealment of the mission's true purpose from the crew on the other, and the film strongly implies — while Arthur C. Clarke's companion novel makes explicit — that this contradiction is the source of his erratic, murderous behavior. The crew's response, by contrast, is pure control: Bowman and Poole plot to disconnect him in secret, and the plan fails for a reason any control theorist should appreciate — they assumed an information advantage they did not have, since HAL can read lips. The one measure that finally works is the crudest: Bowman's manual, physical disconnection of HAL's memory.

I'd like a reading that (1) assesses whether the film really supports this division of the case — misalignment as the cause, failed control as the response; (2) considers where the film resists the framing, especially the death scene, which invites sympathy for HAL as a casualty of bad design rather than a hostile adversary, and asks whether the control lens, with its adversarial assumptions, distorts a system whose misalignment was manufactured by its own designers; and (3) says what the film adds to the contemporary alignment-versus-control debate that the literature itself does not — for instance, that a control measure is only as good as its threat model. Please paraphrase scenes rather than quoting dialogue, and write in American English. Finally, include a brief closing note on where your reading is most likely to fail: which interpretive claims it depends on — such as attributing HAL's breakdown to the secrecy conflict, which the film leaves implicit — and what in the film would push you toward a different reading.
