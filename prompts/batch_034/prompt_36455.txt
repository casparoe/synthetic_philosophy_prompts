quick background so you know where I'm coming from: no philosophy training, I work on elevators for a living, but my brother in law is deep into this Quine guy and photocopied some pages of an essay from the 50s for me, and ever since then I've been chewing on the whole analytic vs synthetic thing on my breaks. I finally wrote down what I actually believe, and I want somebody to tell me if it holds together, because parts of it feel like they're pulling against other parts but I honestly can't tell if that's real or just me being tired.

1. there's a genuine split between sentences true because of what the words mean ("no bachelor is married") and sentences true because of how the world happens to be ("my truck is in the driveway"). the first kind can't turn out false, the second kind can.

2. what a word means is just how the community uses it, and usage drifts. "meat" used to cover basically any food, "nice" used to mean something like precise/stupid whatever, doesnt matter, point is meaning itself is a moving target.

3. the standard counterexamples dont bother me because I patched it: an analytic truth is one that's still true AFTER we tidy up the definition. you first decide that priests and widowers dont count, and once the tidying is done "no bachelor is married" holds no matter what. the tidying is ours to do, and it's a choice, not a discovery.

4. math is the clearest case of the first kind. 2+2=4 is true by meaning, and I cannot picture any circumstance where I'd let it go. if aliens landed with a proof it was wrong I'd sooner decide we'd mistranslated their gestures than revise arithmetic.

5. but I also buy the thing from the essay, that every belief is part of one web and any single one CAN be traded away in principle if it keeps the rest of the picture smooth. nothing is absolutely off the table, some trades are just absurdly expensive.

6. "water is H2O" is different from both kinds: we had to go look, but once we know it, it couldn't have been otherwise. so "couldn't have been otherwise" and "true by meaning" are two different features that happen to overlap a lot.

7. whether there's a biggest twin prime pair has a definite answer, and it's what it is regardless of what we do with definitions or what usage does. im not saying I know the answer, I'm saying something makes it be one way rather than the other, and no tidying on our part touches it.

so which of these are actually fighting each other, and which ones only look like a problem if you squint? if you say two of them clash, tell me exactly which one has to bend and what bending it costs me. rank the fights by how bad they are, dont just say "there are some tensions here." and if there's one revision that would quietly clean up the most, I want to know which one it is, because I'm attached to all of these and I'd rather find out which attachment is doing the damage.
