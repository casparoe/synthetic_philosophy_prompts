1. broken femur, flu, metastatic cancer: disease, obviously, boring, just establishing a baseline.
2. gilbert's syndrome: benign liver thing, ~5% of people have it, basically no symptoms, docs mostly tell you "you have this, don't worry about it". i say NOT a disease, it's just a trait. like having green eyes.
3. hypertension where the person feels totally fine and would never know without a cuff reading: disease, yes.
4. a guy whose wife died 9 months ago, he's sleeping badly, crying alot, can't focus at work: NOT a disease. but i would 100% want our app to surface resources and let him log it as something being treated, so apparently "deserves medical attention" and "is a disease" have come apart for me and that's already weird?
5. homosexuality: obviously not a disease, not even a hard call, but here's the thing — i want to say it's not a disease even in a horrifyingly homophobic society where being gay reliably ruins your life. not-ness doesn't track the harm.
6. ADHD: i say yes, disorder. and i notice my reason is basically that school and office life are structured a certain way and this brain config does badly at THAT. in some other arrangement it'd be more like a trait. so for this one not-ness DOES track the environment?? feels like 5 and 6 can't both be right but i can't see which one to give up.
7. essential tremor: two guys, same tremor. one is a watchmaker and his career is over, the other is retired and says he honestly doesn't care. i say BOTH have a disease. so "bothered by it" isn't required. but then what was wrong with gilbert's?? it's also a stable physiological deviation from the species design that mostly doesn't bother anyone. i can feel that there's a difference and i cannot write the difference down.
8. same bone density score in a 45yo and a 78yo. i want to say the 45yo has a disease and the 78yo has... aging? and when my MD friend pushed me i retreated to "aging itself isn't a disease but every specific thing aging does to you is one," which he correctly pointed out is not a principle, it's a shrug.
