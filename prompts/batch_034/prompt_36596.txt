I teach an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar (16 students, one 80-minute session) and we've just covered Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Design an in-class activity built around a single repeated task, run in four rounds, where the rules change each round so that participants successively experience being each kind of creature: first acting blind with variation and selection but no learning, then learning from outcomes, then getting to test hypotheses before committing to an action, and finally receiving a representational tool — but only some participants get it, mid-round, so the group can see what an installed way of thinking buys rather than what raw cleverness buys. The task should be simple enough to run with paper and a timer, with a scoring scheme that makes the Darwinian round genuinely costly. Include full rules, a timeline, a debrief that maps each round onto the underlying generate-and-test structure and onto competence without comprehension, a note on what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (especially how to keep the Darwinian round from just feeling unfair), and an honest statement of which feature of the real thing — that in genuine Darwinian selection the failed trials are gone forever — no classroom game can reproduce and I'll have to supply in the debrief.
