Context if it helps: I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics, with the usual grad-student amount of logic and a CS theory course from undergrad, so I can follow the machinery but I'm not native to it. Last week in our seminar on mathematical epistemology we were arguing about whether mathematicians' aesthetic judgments — this proof is "the right one," that conjecture "smells true" — carry any real epistemic weight, or whether they're just psychological crutches. A CS theory guy in the audience ended the whole discussion with the line "finding a proof is no harder than checking one — that's just what P=NP says," the point being that discovery is just verification with the answer blanked out, so nothing beyond mechanical checking could be doing genuine work in mathematical cognition, and the aesthetes should stop pretending otherwise. Everyone nodded, and I had the sinking feeling that the slogan had slid several claims into one, but I couldn't unpack it on the spot.

I've since half-convinced myself there are at least three or four distinct theses that could be hiding in "finding is no harder than checking," running from platitudes to open problems, but I want someone to actually lay them out with verdicts. There's presumably (i) the trivial observation that once you have a specific proof in hand, verifying it is mechanical; (ii) some conditional claim about efficient search existing whenever efficient verification does, which as far as I can tell is not known and is the actual content of P vs NP, or of some search version of it — and note the guy stated it categorically, not conditionally; (iii) a claim about mere Turing computability that's true regardless of how P vs NP goes and hence says nothing about actual mathematical practice; and maybe (iv) a claim about human cognition, that what a mathematician "sees" is just the checking algorithm run messier. For each: is it true, is it known, and would it actually support the use it was put to in the seminar? I especially want someone to press on whether the move from "checkable in polynomial time" to "findable by us" smuggles in "polynomial means fast at human scale," which seems obviously false — n^50 is polynomial and nobody's searching anything.

One more thing if you have room: does the verification side ever get hard, in a way that would unsettle reading (i) even as a platitude? I have a vague memory that for some problem classes the certificate you'd need to check is itself enormous, or that checking a fully general proof requires trusting the checker, and if verification can be intractable too then the tidy asymmetry the slogan rests on is shakier than it looks. Lay it all out in a form I can bring back to the seminar — the discussion got killed and I'd like to revive it properly. Sorry if this rambles a bit, the whole exchange left me more confused then when I came in.
