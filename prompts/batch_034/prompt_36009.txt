I'm in a bioethics reading group that's been arguing for weeks about whether the therapy/enhancement line does any actual moral work, and I want to see the debate done properly instead of the usual ping-pong of one-liners. Write a dialogue between two characters: one who thinks the distinction is genuinely morally important — roughly in the vicinity of Kass's or Sandel's worries about accepting the given, but sharper and less hand-wavy than the caricatures usually get — and one who thinks the line is either untenable in principle or morally inert even where you can draw it, in the vicinity of Bostrom or Harris but, again, not a strawman. Both should be clearly competent: no one wins by the other guy fumbling.

To stop them from just recycling cochlear implants and Ritalin off the shelf, give them a concrete shared case to fight over: a fictional injectable therapy originally developed to stop a progressive muscle-wasting condition, which in healthy recipients also reliably pushes endurance up to around the 99th percentile of the unenhanced population, and beyond that with higher doses. No known side effects, and cheap enough that a national health service could plausibly cover it. Have them fight about whether coverage decisions, parents deciding for their kids, and sports doping bans should all be tracking the *same* line here, and whether that line tracks anything deeper than statistical species-typicality (one of them should press on the "therapy just means restoring normal functioning, and 'normal' is a statistical fact, not a moral one" point, and the other needs a real answer, not a shrug).

A few constraints: I want actual back-and-forth, where each responds to the specific argument the other just made and concedes something when a point lands. It shouldn't end in a tidy handshake agreement — ending genuinely unresolved, maybe even with both of them slightly annoyed, is more honest. Casual register, like two colleagues over coffee rather than a formal debate, somewhere in the 900-1200 word range. Thier voices should be distinguishable but neither should be the "reasonable one" by construction.
