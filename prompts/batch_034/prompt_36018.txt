Quick background: I'm a journalist, I write mostly about science and ideas for a general audience, and I've been toying with a piece on wether Star Trek's Federation is basically Kant's Perpetual Peace with warp drive bolted on. In the 1795 sketch, the peace-keeping structure Kant wants is a league of sovereign states — he explicitly does not want a single world government, which he thought would drift into the worst kind of despotism, or else just crack apart into many states again. But the Federation seems to sit right on that line: there's a council and a charter, member worlds keep their own cultures and (I think?) their own governments, but Starfleet is one unified force with it's headquarters basically running everything, and there's a canon-ish rule that a planet can't even join until it has a single world government. So, read the Federation through Perpetual Peace and tell me which side of Kant's line it comes down on. And then the Prime Directive — Kant's third article is about a right of hospitality, strangers allowed to show up without being treated as enemies; the Prime Directive is almost the opposite instinct, don't show up at all. Does the franchise ever earn that inversion philosophically, or is it just a plot device for stories about interference? Please push back where the shows resist the reading — I don't want to file a piece that just says "Star Trek is Kant" if that turns out to be lazy.
