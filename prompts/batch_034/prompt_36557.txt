I've gotten pretty attached to the view that grounding is a real, primitive relation: reality comes layered, facts about microphysics ground facts about chemistry, chemistry grounds facts about biology and economics, and this "grounds" talk isn't just supervenience in a costume or a shorthand for how we explain things — it's a genuine feature of reality itself, with an ultimate fundamental level at the bottom of it all. What would actually count against this? I know the deflationists (Wilson-ish people) claim we can get everything we need from supervenience plus our actual explanatory practices, there's the awkward question of what grounds the grounding facts themselves, and there's the possibility that the layers just never bottom out. Sort the defeaters for me: which of these (or other things) should genuinely worry a realist about grounding, which would committed defenders just shrug off, and has calling the relation "primitive" quietly insulated the whole view from any possible counterevidence — and if it has, is that a fatal flaw or just how it goes for a view of this kind?
