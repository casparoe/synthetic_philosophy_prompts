I'm writing a paper for my philosophy of mind elective on cognitive penetration — the idea that what we believe, expect, or care about can change what we literally perceive, not just how we judge afterwards. My teacher keeps saying the hard part of this debate is telling apart genuine penetration from cases where cognition only influences perception through boring channels like attention or memory, or where the "top-down" influence is really just a built-in feature of the perceptual system itself.

I want to use the dress — the 2015 photo that roughly half of people saw as white and gold and half as blue and black, and where what you saw tracked, apparently, assumptions your visual system made about whether the photo was taken in daylight or artificial light. It seems like a gift for the penetration side: the same retinal input, two different color experiences, and the difference explained by something like prior beliefs about illumination. But I suspect the anti-penetration camp has a reply, namely that a prior embedded in the visual system's own machinery isn't "cognition" reaching into perception at all — it's just perception working as designed, and no amount of new beliefs or expectations changes which way you see the dress.

Please give me a case analysis that runs both ways. Map the episode carefully: say what exactly the theory of cognitive penetration requires for a case to count (what plays the role of cognition, what counts as the perceptual state being altered, what difference penetration makes), then apply that mapping to the dress and argue to a definite conclusion — either it is a genuine instance of cognitive penetration, or it is a near-miss that shows the standard definition is drawing its line in the wrong place. Don't leave me with "there are two views"; pick one and defend it. Then tell me what the episode shows about the penetration debate itself: is it confirmation, an anomaly, or a case showing that the whole framework of "cognition affecting perception" is asking a question the visual system doesn't respect? And please be honest about where the penetration framework simply goes silent on this episode rather than pretending it has something to say.
