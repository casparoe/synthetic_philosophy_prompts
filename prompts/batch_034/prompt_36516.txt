I edit a small newsletter aimed at AI policy people (regulators, staffers, think-tank types — smart but not philosophers), and we're doing a piece on whether LLMs "really understand" language — the stochastic parrots thing from a few years back that flared up and then basically froze in place. My own view, for what it's worth, is that the public argument went nowhere because "understanding" was quietly made to do three different jobs at once: a competence job (can it get things right in situations it wasn't directly trained on), a grounding job (is its grip on 'red' or 'promising' hooked up to anything outside text), and a moral job (does any of this change what we owe the system, or what we can do to it). People pick a different job, talk past each other, and everyone leaves the room with their priors intact. I want the piece to end by telling readers what actually still needs resolving before their jobs get easier.

So: give me exactly five open questions in this space, ranked by how much settling them would change what my readers should actually do — how they regulate, what they say publicly, what they assume in drafting — not by how fun they are to argue about. Number them, one or two sentences each, question first and then the why, no intro or conclusion, just the list. Don't bother with citations or naming papers, I'll slot the names in myself. And if you think one of my three "jobs" is itself confused or a fourth one is being smuggled in somewhere, feel free to make that one of the five — I'd rather the list challenge my framing than flatter it.
