The homework question was:

"State Frege's puzzle about identity statements, explain his solution in terms of the distinction between sense and reference, and say what the puzzle is supposed to show about the relationship between what a name means and what it names."

The rubric allocates 4 points for a correct statement of the puzzle, 3 for an accurate presentation of Frege's solution, and 3 for the discussion of what it shows about the meaning of a name.

The student submitted the following:

"Frege introduced this puzzle with the example of Hesperus and Phosphorus. 'Hesperus is Hesperus' is trivial, but 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' was a genuine astronomical discovery. Since both names pick out the same planet, the difference in informativeness cannot come from what the sentences are about: both are about Venus. Frege's solution is his distinction between the reference and the sense of an expression. The reference of both 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' is the planet Venus, and the reference of a whole sentence is its truth value. The sense of a name is a way of presenting its referent: 'Phosphorus' presents Venus as the body seen at dawn, 'Hesperus' as the body seen at dusk. Because the two names express different senses, the two sentences express different thoughts, and the informativeness of the discovery consists in learning that two different names refer to one and the same object. The puzzle shows that the meaning of a name cannot simply be its bearer. If 'Hesperus' meant just Venus, then 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' would say the same thing as 'Hesperus is Hesperus' and could not have been a discovery at all. So meaning has two layers: sense, which is grasped in understanding and determines reference, and reference itself. And since the two sentences express different thoughts, they could in principle have different truth values, which is how the ancients could accept one while rejecting the other without any inconsistency."
