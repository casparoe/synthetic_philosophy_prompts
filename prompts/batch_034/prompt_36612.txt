Hey! So I write a column on science and ideas for a general audience — the kind of reader who picks up pop-science books for the train but has never sat through a philosophy seminar — and my next piece is on Mary's room, the color scientist who knows every physical fact about color vision but has lived her whole life in a black-and-white room. The essay part is basically written, but my editor wants an interactive element for the website, and honestly I agree with her: a click-through quiz. Working title: "So you think you're a physicalist?" The hunch is that most readers have no idea which position they actually hold, and that a bunch of self-described physicalists would flunk if you tested them on concrete cases instead of just asking which label they like.

What I need from you is the quiz itself: eight to ten questions, each one a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the real positions — the dualist conclusion (she gains a genuinely new fact, so physicalism is in trouble), the ability line (she gains know-how, like learning to ride a bike, not new information), the "same facts, new way of grasping them" response, and the deflationary take that there was never anything there to learn in the first place. If the logical space also allows some combination nobody actually defends, include it as a leaf and mark it as unoccupied — I'd find that genuinely interesting to mention in the follow-up column.

Cases I'd like in there, though if you've got better ones, use them:
- the classic setup: she walks out, sees a red tomato for the first time — what exactly, if anything, did she gain?
- a variation where the person who steps out is a congenital achromat who somehow has all the same neuroscientific knowledge — does that change anything, and why or why not?
- a forgetful version: she learns what it's like but loses it again immediately, and can relearn it — what does that suggest about what kind of knowledge it is?
- a parallel case with a different sense — someone who knows all the physics/chemistry of taste but grew up on flavorless nutrient paste. Is that the same case or a importantly different one?

Crucial constraint: no question can just ask "do you think qualia are physical" — every question must be a case the reader can answer on intuition alone, with the position names hidden until the scoring key. And please flag honestly which pairs of positions the quiz can't really separate, because I get enough angry mail from philosophers already and I'd rather pre-empt it.

Tone: smart but casual, no jargon without a plain-language gloss, and nothing that assumes the reader already knows the literature. If you can work in a question where readers intuitions will split between seeing red, tasting coffee, and feeling pain — that contrast is exactly the kind of thing my readers will argue about in the comments, which is the whole point.
