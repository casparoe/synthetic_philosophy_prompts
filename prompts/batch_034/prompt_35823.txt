At my school's philosophy club, each member has been asked to submit a written "position profile" — a numbered list of the commitments we actually hold on this term's topic — so the others can pick it apart. The topic is infinity and physical processes, and I have written down five claims I genuinely believe. (1) I believe that the runner in Zeno's paradox really does complete infinitely many sub-distances in a finite time, and that this is not paradoxical: the sub-intervals merely partition an ordinary run, and since the distances 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... sum to 1, nothing is left over at the finish line. (2) I believe that Thomson's lamp, as the story is usually told, leaves it genuinely open whether the lamp is on at the end of the minute: the states alternate on, off, on, off with the switching times crowding toward the one-minute mark, and since a sequence like that has no limit, I take the lesson of the puzzle to be that the question of how the process ends has no determinate answer. (3) I believe that classical physics is deterministic: given a complete description of a closed system at one instant, together with the laws, every later state is fixed; if we cannot tell what comes next, that is missing information on our side, not openness in the world. (4) I believe that every macroscopic object is, at every moment at which it exists, in some definite state or other — a switch lever is somewhere, a lamp is either on or off — so there is no zone of no-fact surrounding ordinary things. (5) I believe the lamp scenario is not self-contradictory: every finite initial segment of the story is perfectly coherent, finitely many flips are plainly possible, and if the full version turns out to be impossible, that seems to me a contingent fact about real mechanisms (friction, finite speeds) rather than something that follows from the very concept of the task.

Before I hand this in, I would like an audit of whether the five claims hang together. Please identify which pairs pull against each other, distinguish the tensions that are real from those that only look like tensions, rank the real ones by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what that revision would cost me elsewhere on the list. I would also like your judgment on which single change would clean up the most. For what it is worth, I have stared at the list for a while and cannot see where the trouble is; I would rather find out than defend each item, so please do not hold back.
