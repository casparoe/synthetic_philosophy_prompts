Is there a name for this? Bit of background: I got laid off last year, started doing forecasting tournaments partly for money and partly to keep sane, and somewhere along the way I've gotten into reading about probabilistic reasoning. And something keeps bugging me that I can't tell whether it's a known thing or just me doing it wrong.

Here's the pattern. Late last year I came up with a theory about why my sister's marriage was struggling — call it the "she resents the in-laws" theory. The moment I thought of it, it felt overwhelmingly confirmed, and here's the kicker: the evidence that made it feel confirmed was stuff I'd known for years. The awkward holiday dinners, the comment she made back in 2022, all of it. None of it was new. Then a couple months later a different theory occured to me ("he's depressed and withdrawing"), and exactly the same old facts lined up behind that one and it felt just as confirmed. I've now cycled through three successive theories, each one "confirmed" by a pile of data that existed before I ever thought of the theory, and in at least one case literally the same fact did confirming work for two incompatible theories at different times.

It got worse when I noticed people in the forecasting community doing versions of it all the time: someone proposes a novel explanation for why a politician's polling shifted, and instantly every stale data point from the last decade becomes a "confirmation" of it, and people report their confidence jumping way up, all without a single new piece of information entering the room.

My half-understanding of textbook updating is that you're supposed to change your beliefs when new evidence arrives relative to what you already knew, so old stuff should already be, like, priced in. But I can't just say the confidence bump was irrational, because honestly thinking of the in-laws theory did feel like learning something, even though no new fact about the world came in. So maybe the problem isn't the bump itself but that I have no way to tell whether the bump is licensed, and I keep paying the same recycled facts again and again for each new theory I invent.

So: is this a recognized problem with a name and actual literature behind it? And if it is, I'd want to know how it differs from plain confirmation bias if it even does, and from cherry-picking, since in my case I never went looking for supporting facts — they were just sitting there in my head. And if there's no established term, I'd happily take a coinage with a real definition and some boundary cases, because I want something to put my finger on next time I feel myself doing it again.
