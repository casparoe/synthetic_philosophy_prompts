At the end of the book, Pi gives the two Japanese shipping-company investigators two versions of his survival story -- the one with the tiger, and the brutal one with his mother, the cook, and the sailor. The investigators end up preferring the tiger story, and Pi basically tells them that's what matters. So it seems like the ending stages the whole Peirce vs. James fight: on Peirce's picture, truth is what sustained inquiry would eventually settle on, and the investigators are literally running an inquiry -- their job is to figure out what happened to the ship, and the facts on the boat push toward the ugly story. On James's picture, a belief is true insofar as it works, is verified by experience, is good in the way of belief -- and the tiger story is the one Pi can live with, the one that makes his survival mean something.

But here's where I got stuck, and where I want you to actually push on me rather than just validate:

First, does the novel really side with James? The investigators' official report at the end doesn't say the tiger story is true -- it kind of punts on it. So maybe the book is showing an inquiry that just... stops, never converges, which is arguably a problem for the Peircean view, not a vindication of it?

Second, and this is the bit I find exciting -- there's a nastier Peircean reply available. In the fixation of belief paper Peirce goes after the method of tenacity and the a priori method, i.e. believing whatever is comfortable or agreeable. Isn't Pi choosing the tiger story basically the a priori method with extra steps? And isn't that exactly what Peirce thinks is self-deception? James would presumably fire back that the two stories are evidentially on a par, nobody can ever produce more evidence, so Pi has the right to go with the belief he can live by. Who wins that exchange?

Third, can I even cleanly separate the two here? I keep reading that Peirce himself later complained about James's use of the word pragmatism, so maybe I should be careful about treating this as a two-man tag team match at all.
