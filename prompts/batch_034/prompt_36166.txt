My cousin is the person I trust most on questions of right and wrong: she has been right before about things I got badly wrong, and everyone in our family defers to her judgment. Last week I told her that I saw a classmate cheating on a final, and that our school's honor code expects us to report it. She told me, firmly, not to report him. When I pressed her for her reasons, she couldn't really give any — she just said she was confident it would be wrong to turn him in. I now find myself believing her without being able to explain to anyone why I shouldn't report him. My father says this is a terrible way to make a moral decision: that if I can't give the reasons myself, I'm just borrowing a conscience, and that borrowed convictions don't count. Is he right, or is trusting her no different from trusting a mechanic's diagnosis of a strange noise in my car?
