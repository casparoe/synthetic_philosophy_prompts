A friend of mine who has been getting into distributive justice sent me a long email last week summarizing what he takes to be the state of the debate about equality and priority, and I want to know how far I can trust it before I repeat any of it. His summary, broken into its five sentences, was this:

1. "Prioritarianism is basically egalitarianism with a face-lift: it says the same benefit counts for more when it goes to someone worse off, so it pushes toward redistributing to the bottom just like egalitarianism, only without the baggage."
2. "Parfit built it in the nineties as the fixed version of egalitarianism, after his leveling-down argument showed that egalitarians are committed to there being something good about making some people worse off when the rest cannot be raised."
3. "Because the view only weighs benefits to people and never looks at how anyone stands relative to anyone else, it is completely immune to leveling-down-style objections: it never has to count a change that benefits nobody as an improvement, not even a small one."
4. "So the leveling-down argument really settles things: anyone who rejects leveling down is already a prioritarian, and the only remaining option for an egalitarian is to become a sufficientarian, which is really the same view with a steeper curve."
5. "That is why the leveling-down objection is usually treated as having finished off egalitarianism, and why the serious work has moved to refining prioritarianism."

I have my suspicions about a few of these sentences, but I don't know the literature well enough to say where he has gone wrong, and I would rather not dismiss the email just because it is confident. Please act as a professional moral philosopher working in distributive justice, and think through each sentence carefully before answering, as though checking a colleague's abstract — in particular, ask yourself what a defender of each view mentioned would accept, wince at, or flatly deny, and whether the conclusions in the later sentences actually follow from whatever is true in the earlier ones.

Format your reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, one item per sentence of his email, in order. Each item should be one or two sentences: say what is accurate, what is subtly misleading, and (where relevant) what he would have to concede for the sentence to be true. Please write in American English, and do not add an introduction, a conclusion, or any prefatory remarks.
