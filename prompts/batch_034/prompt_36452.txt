hey, so I write about science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, mostly conservation genetics and wildlife stuff. every few months I have to use the word "species" in a piece, and every few months some researcher emails me to say the term is basically undefined and I've quietly committed to a metaphysics I don't hold. normally I ignore them, but I'm planning a longer feature and my editor keeps asking what the actual stakes are, and I realize I can't answer her beyond "philosophers and taxonomists argue about it."

so here's my ask: within the species problem, which open questions actually matter most, and for whom? I've got maybe 4000 words of space and two months of reporting, so I want to know where the unresolved stuff does real work, not just where academics enjoy fighting.

three angles I'm considering, plus whatever I haven't thought of:

1. conservation law. in a lot of countries legal protection attaches to species or subspecies (or to units like "distinct population segments" or "evolutionarily significant units"). my cousin is a wildlife geneticist and her lab just helped split one widespread stream salamander into like eight narrowly endemic species using genomic data, and now each one is instantly rare and listable. is the problem here conceptual (nobody knows what a species is) or institutional (the law needs a bright line and biology refuses to give it one), and which of those should my article be about?

2. de-extinction and surrogate breeding. if species boundaries are mushy, what is someone even claiming to bring back when they back-breed or genome-edit toward a passenger pigeon or a thylacine? does "de-extincting" a thing that isn't a natural kind mean anything, or is it just rebranding genetic engineering of a lookalike?

3. the it-doesn't-matter angle. one ecologist I know says all the philosophical hand-wringing is downstream of taxonomy being a data-format problem, that biologists just need agreed conventions like the codes of nomenclature already give them, and the metaphysics is a distraction. maybe she's right and my article should be about why the debate persists anyway.

so give me a defended prioritization. which of these deserves my reporting time and wordcount, which are overrated, is there something I'm not listing (species selection in macroevolution? how conservation triage should carve up its units?) that matters more, and what would make you reorder the list — like, if a court case went a certain way, or if a cheap genomic method made splitting even cheaper. I want the reasoning for the ordering, not a survey of views. and tell me honestly if angle 3 is basically correct and the honest article is "it's bookkeeping all the way down, here's why that's fine," because I can work with that too.
