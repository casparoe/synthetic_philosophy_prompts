so, background: I write novels for a living, and a few years ago I read a bunch of later Wittgenstein and it got under my skin in a way that most philosophy doesn't. lately I've been trying to write a short essay for my writing group about what all this means for how I actually work, and I've realized my working beliefs might not hang together. here's the package, as honest as I can make it:

(1) I'm on board with meaning-is-use. words mean what they do in the whole mesh of practices they live in; there's no secret mental act behind the use that pins the meaning down.

(2) relatedly, I accept the private language argument — a language referring to my private sensations, getting its meaning purely from inner ostension with no public checks, isn't a going concern. I think Wittgenstein basically wins there and I'm not tempted to fight it.

(3) I think "novel" is a family resemblance concept, and so is "literary fiction" — no necessary and sufficient conditions, just criss-crossing similarities, vague boundaries that are still perfectly usable. that said, I do think the literary/genre line tracks something real about how a book asks to be read, and I'd defend that in an argument with another writer.

(4) here's where I feel the ground tilt. I believe there's a fact of the matter about what I meant in my third novel, and that it can be lost. there are passages where what I meant outruns anything I could give in paraphrase, and if every copy burned and I forgot it, what I meant would be permanently unrecoverable — and it would still be a fact about what that passage meant. not what evidence points to, what it meant.

(5) I coin words. I have one, a made-up word for a very specific kind of restless evening mood, that I use in my notebooks. I'm confident I apply it correctly or incorrectly on new evenings — some evenings it clearly fits, some definately don't, and I'd notice if I started misusing it. nobody else could ever check, and that doesn't bother me.

(6) when I'm drafting, I follow a felt sense of "yes, that's the right next sentence" that no rule I could state would generate. and yet I also believe that across a whole book there's a fact about whether I'm staying in my voice or drifting out of it — I can be wrong about my own voice in the moment and come back two years later and see that the later chapters betrayed it.

(7) I think my characters' inner lives are real in whatever way "real" spreads across that family — not literally real, but not nothing either, and the nothing answer feels like it misses what I do all day.

go through these and tell me which ones genuinely can't live together and which only look like they can't. rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as all equally bad, and for each real one tell me the cheapest fix and what it would cost me. then, if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, name it. be specific — don't just say Wittgenstein would frown at (4); say what (1) or (2) actually commits me to and exactly where it bites. and dont go easy on me just because the diary thing in (5) sounds like the classic example — I genuinely can't tell whether my case is that case or a different one, and I'd like to know which.
