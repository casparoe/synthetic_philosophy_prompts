I've been in a little reading group on the Sleeping Beauty problem for about a year now and we've officially hit the wall, so I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is rather than another run through the arguments. Setup so we're on the same page: fair coin flipped Sunday night, Beauty woken Monday, put back under and (only if tails) woken again Tuesday, with amnesia between wakings, and she knows the whole protocol in advance. On waking, thirders say her credence in heads should be 1/3, halfers say it should stay at 1/2. That's it. That's the whole problem. And yet the debate has been basically frozen since the Elga–Lewis exchange around 2000, with everyone able to recite every argument on both sides — the thirder's frequency story (in the long run, only a third of her wakings follow heads), the halfer's "she learns nothing new upon waking, so nothing should move" story, the dutch books, all of it. Nobody converts.

The places where I see real pressure are what make me think there's something structural going on. The halfer's embarrassing update: if halfway through the experiment Beauty is told "it's Monday," plain conditionalization on the halfer's own numbers pushes her to 2/3 on heads, which basically nobody, including most halfers, wants to say — so the halfers split, with some biting the bullet as "double-halfers" and saying updating just doesn't work normally for self-locating information. That's a real cost and they know it. Meanwhile the thirder betting arguments depend entirely on how the wager gets settled, per-awakening or per-experiment, and each side says the other is smuggling in their answer via the operationalization. And hanging over the whole thing is the anthropic shadow: whether your answer to Beauty commits you to doomsday-argument style conclusions, or to reasoning that you're probably in a huge universe, and everyone swears their Beauty answer doesn't commit them to the absurd-seeming one in those other debates.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two sides are maybe computing two genuinely different quantities — something like chance-per-coin-toss versus chance-per-awakening-experience — and the single word "credence" is being stretched over both, which would make this partly verbal. But I'm honestly not sure, because the disagreement survives every paraphrase we've tried, and it resurfaces in cases where you actually have to act (betting across many runs, or reasoning about how big the world you live in is), which makes me suspect it isn't *just* verbal. So tell me: is this a verbal dispute, are the sides answering different questions under one name (what the evidence supports vs what maximizes payoff vs what tracks frequencies), or is it a genuine substantive fight about what self-locating evidence even is? Use what each side actually says, not a survey — I've read the literature, that's how I got into this mess. And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress here: a new variant case, some formal result, a distinction nobody has drawn yet — or make the honest case that the impasse is itself evidence the question is badly posed and we should stop asking it. If the answer is "nobody should expect this to ever get resolved," I want to know why that is and what it says about the rest of the self-locating belief stuff downstream of it.
