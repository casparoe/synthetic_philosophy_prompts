I coordinate a small philosophy-of-mind reading group that meets fortnightly — a mix of graduate students, one clinical psychologist who works with panic disorder patients, and a couple of curious outsiders who have never read a word of the literature. We have just finished a stretch on the nature of emotions, and I want to close the term with a diagnostic quiz for the group's blog, titled something like 'Which theory of emotion do you actually hold?'. The guiding thought is that people in our group tend to adopt whichever label they find congenial — several now cheerfully call themselves 'perceptualists' without having noticed what that commits them to — so the quiz should sort them by what they say about cases, not by which position they enjoy naming. Please write the quiz: eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, and a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto positions.

The cases should be ones on which the main theories genuinely come apart, and I would like you to draw them from this pool (plus others of your own devising, and feel free to prune mine): standing on a glass-floored walkway you know to be perfectly safe while your legs shake; the recalcitrant dog phobia of someone who can recite the injury statistics fluently; morbid jealousy that persists in the face of genuinely exculpatory evidence; grief that overwhelms someone at the death of a fictional character; a patient with flat affect following brain injury who nevertheless avoids the room where something frightening once happened; a panic attack with no object at all; and the question of whether one can be afraid of the snake described only in a field guide one is reading indoors. No question may ask, in effect, 'do you think emotions are judgments rather than feelings?' — the diagnostic load must be carried entirely by the cases, phrased in language an educated non-specialist could answer without any prior reading.

For the scoring key, please build the logical space before placing the positions: first set out the answer dimensions the questions jointly define (something like: does the emotion require a propositional commitment, does it require bodily feeling, does it misrepresent in the way perception misrepresents, can it exist unconsciously, is it constituted by what it disposes you to do), and then locate the positions in that space — the neo-Stoic judgment theory, a James-Lange-style pure feeling theory, the perceptual theory, the attitudinal view on which fear is a bodily readiness taken toward an object, construal- or appraisal-style hybrids, a constructivist position on which emotion categories are assembled rather than detected, and a deflationary 'no deep nature' option. Note any answer combinations in the space that no named position occupies, and say briefly whether each is unoccupied because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of positions the quiz cannot actually separate, rather than pretending the scoring key discriminates between them — I would rather print an honest note about two indistinguishable outcomes than have a sharp-eyed group member do it for me. Please write the whole thing, questions and key, in British English.
