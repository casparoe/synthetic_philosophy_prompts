A podcast episode I heard recently summarized Derek Parfit's nonidentity problem roughly as follows: "Parfit saw that many of our choices about the future don't actually harm anyone. If we adopt a reckless depletion policy, the people who exist a few centuries from now will be different people from those who would have existed under a cautious policy, since the policy changes the timing of conceptions and thereby which people are born. Those future people owe their very existence to the depletion policy, so the policy cannot have made them worse off — without it, they would never have existed at all. As long as their lives are worth living, the policy harms no one. And since morality is fundamentally about not harming people, Parfit concluded that we have no obligations to future generations: our duties extend only to those who already exist."
