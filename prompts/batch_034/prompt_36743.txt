Been reading lesswrong since roughly the bostrom days and I'm now helping judge a small grants round (maybe $300k total) for conceptual/theory work on AI control -- the redwood-style stuff where you assume the model may already be scheming and design the deployment so a misaligned model still can't do much damage: monitoring, honeypots, information limits, shutdown-corrigibility-via-incentives rather than via the model's values. I keep flip-flopping on what the actual crux questions are, so I want a defended prioritization, not a survey. Candidates off the top of my head: whether control evaluations can ever bound scheming when the model can condition its behavior on being evaluated; whether control difficulty scales in a measurable way with capability, or whether we just can't know our measures fail until they've failed; whether running a model under adversarial-assumption scaffolding changes the model itself (same training loop feeding the monitors, etc.); collusion between model instances in deployments where instances can pass notes to each other; and the question people tend to dodge, namely whether investing in control substitutes for alignment work in practice or is strictly upstream of it -- e.g. whether "we can control it" becomes the excuse labs use to skip the harder problem. Rank the two or three you'd actually fund, say for each why it's upstream of the others and what makes it tractable for philosophy/theory people rather than infra people, and tell me what developments in the next couple years would move things up or down your list. Also flag any of these you'd deprioritize because the answer wouldn't change what a lab should actually do.
