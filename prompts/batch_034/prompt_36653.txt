Please argue for a definite position on this rather than surveying the options. In nuclear deterrence, the threat that does the actual work can't be a threat to do something rational, because by the time launch day arrives, retaliating is strictly worse for the threatener than not — the damage is already done, and destroying the other side doesn't undo any of it. The standard escape is to make the threat credible by outsourcing it: a dead-hand system that launches automatically once triggered, or a deliberate partial loss of control during a crisis, letting luck rather than a decision do the punishing. Take a stance on whether this move really dissolves the paradox or just relocates the irrationality somewhere harder to see. In particular, I want you to commit one way or the other on a dilemma I keep hitting: either the loss of control is verifiable to the other side, in which case its public and inspectable — and anything inspectable can be found and taken out first — or it stays hidden, in which case it can't deter anything in the first place. If you think the dilemma is bogus, break the specific horn you think is fake and show me how; if you think its real but tolerable, say what the opponent's best countermove is and why it doesn't unravel the whole arrangement. Don't just recite the standard framing of the rationality of irrationality — build the argument yourself, commit to where it lands, and if the commitment forces you somewhere uncomfortable, stay there rather than hedging.
