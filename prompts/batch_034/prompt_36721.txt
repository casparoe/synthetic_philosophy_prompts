My friend is way into Leibniz and after arguing about this for basically an entire evening I still can't see my way out of the thing he left me with. (1) God actualizes the best of all possible worlds. (2) If this world is the best, no alternative is better than it. (3) If I could prevent some evil — donate money that saves a kid, say — and that would make things better, then there'd be an alternative better than this world. (4) So either I can't prevent any evil, or preventing it wouldn't make things better. (5) Either way moral effort is pointless: whatever I do, the world remains the best, so nothing I do changes how good things are. (6) And my friend happily bit that bullet — he said charity work is at best God's means and at worst irrelevant 'because the optimum was already picked'. I don't want to just deny (1) outright, since I actually find the reasoning behind it (God needing a sufficient reason to create at all, the composibility stuff) more interesting than the pop-mockery version, and if I reject (5) without an account of why, he wins on vibes. My hunch is he's sliding between 'the whole world is optimal' and 'things around me are as good as they can be', but I can't make that precise, and I can also see a reply where my effort is itself part of what makes the actual world the best one — I just don't know if that reply actually works or whether it just pushes the problem somewhere else (like whether I could ever have failed to make the effort). Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
