For the past two years I have been facilitating a structured exchange between two colleagues: one a committed compatibilist in the mold of Daniel Dennett, the other a free-will skeptic in the mold of Gregg Caruso, with the argument confined to just deserts and the justification of punishment. They have now agreed to formalize the exchange as an adversarial collaboration, and since I am the only participant neither of them suspects of tilting the drafting, the joint statement has fallen to me. The two of them agree on far more than their public reputations suggest — both are naturalists, both regard cruelty as a legitimate object of punishment never, both concede that constitutive luck runs through character formation, and both accept that ordinary attributions of responsibility track something real even while disputing what those attributions license — and that shared ground is precisely what makes a signed document worth producing rather than another round of talking past each other.

The statement should have four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims, each substantive enough that a reader could not have predicted it from either camp's slogans: for instance, claims about what the everyday verdict that someone deserves blame does track (answerability, ownership of action), about the requirements any humane punishment system must meet whatever its theory, and about which parts of each side's position survive even if the other side wins. Second, a disagreements table in which each row is a live issue and each side's formulation is worded so that its own side would sign it: rows should cover at least whether desert divides into basic desert (the non-instrumental deserving of harsh treatment, grounded in nothing beyond the deed and the doer) versus other earned senses, and whether the compatibilist's version is a genuine ground for harder treatment or a relabeled appetite for retribution; whether the skeptic's quarantine model — treating the dangerous rather than the deserving, on the analogy of infectious disease — can preserve the constraints that only the culpable may be confined and that confinement be proportionate, or whether it inevitably drifts toward detaining the merely dangerous; and whether a public message that no one ultimately deserves suffering would erode responsibility practices or civilize them. Third, the cruxes: for each, a concrete case, argument, or body of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction registered in advance. I want at least these cruxes addressed: constitutive luck (does the compatibilist owe an explanation of why luck does not level all desert, or a reason desert needs no such grounding?); where the quarantine model's proportionality comes from, if not from desert; the empirical work on whether diminishing people's belief in free will changes their honesty or effort, which both should accept as bearing on the practical question while disagreeing about what it shows; and the hard case of preventive confinement for someone dangerous but not culpable, say after an acquired brain injury, on which the two models issue different verdicts.

Finally, a closing section adjudicating which residual disagreements are substantive and which are verbal. My own suspicion, which I want the document to confirm or correct, is that both responsibility and desert are each doing two kinds of work in this debate — one concerning answerability, which both parties accept, and one concerning basic desert, over which they genuinely divide — and the document should say exactly where the shared vocabulary ends and where the real fight begins. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then the disagreements table, then the cruxes with predictions, then the closing section on verbal versus substantive disagreement. Neither party may be a strawman: the compatibilist should be shown conceding that objections from ultimate grounding need answering, not waving them off, and the skeptic should be shown accepting that demolishing a practice requires an account of what replaces it, not merely a diagnosis of it.
