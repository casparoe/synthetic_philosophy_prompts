In my political philosophy reading group the luck-egalitarianism debate has stalled in a way I'd like diagnosed rather than adjudicated. Here is the shape of the stall, as I've experienced it. When someone presses the harshness objection — that a theory which lets the imprudent gambler or the health-neglecting smoker bear the full cost of their choices has abandoned the very people egalitarianism was supposed to be for — the luck egalitarians concede the bullet and reply that justice is not a virtue of compassion, that it is fine for equality and beneficence to come apart. When someone presses the relational objection — that equality is fundamentally about how people stand in relation to one another, not about a distribution that tracks responsibility — the luck egalitarians reply that a society can have egalitarian relations while letting people starve for their bad bets, so relations had better be parasitic on distributions, and the relational egalitarians reply that this misdescribes what they take the point of equality to be. Neither side has moved in months, and what strikes me is that they barely argue about cases anymore; the dispute has become almost entirely second-order, about what the word 'equality' is for. My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under a single heading — one asking what makes a distribution just, the other asking what makes a society's social relations egalitarian — and the debate only looks substantive because both claim the same word, with the further wrinkle that each side quietly needs the other's answer (relational egalitarians need distributive principles to fill out their view, and luck egalitarians need some account of why equality rather than, say, responsibility-tracking desert per se is doing the normative work). Is that diagnosis right? Is this a verbal dispute, a substantive normative disagreement about the point of justice, or a disagreement about the role of responsibility in social thought generally that merely expresses itself through distributive cases? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, tell me what kind of thing would count as progress given your diagnosis, and be explicit about how confident you are in each step of the diagnosis and which step you are least sure of.
