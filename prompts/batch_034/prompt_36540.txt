In his satirical essay "A Person Paper on Purity in Language" (published under the pseudonym William Satire), Douglas Hofstadter exposes the flimsiness of familiar defenses of gender-default language — appeals to tradition, to euphony, to the claim that generic terms harm no one — by rewriting each defense as if it were a defense of racial-default language, where its absurdity is immediately visible. Give an example of another entrenched default assumption built into ordinary language, one that concerns neither gender nor race (for instance, a default concerning bodily ability, age, or social status), and apply Hofstadter's technique to it: lay out how the default is typically defended, then construct a parallel satirical argument in which the same style of defense is recast so that its weakness becomes obvious. Conclude by noting one respect in which the analogy could break down, so that the parallel satire would mislead rather than clarify.
