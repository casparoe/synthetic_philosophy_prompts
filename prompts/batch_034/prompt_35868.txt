i'm writing my master's thesis on cultural transmission in orcas and my supervisor is... opinionated about the units of selection question. before I hand her my framing I'm trying to predict what she'll say. her record over the years, as best I can collect it: on ordinary stuff like a giraffe's neck she says gene's-eye talk is just bookkeeping and the individual organism is the level selection actually sees; on sterile termite workers she insists it's kin selection, which she calls gene-level in the end, and gets annoyed if you call it group selection; on meiotic drive and on imprinted genes where the paternal copy pushes fetal growth harder than the maternal copy likes she's adamant that genes can be selected against the interest of the whole organism, so gene-level selection is real and not shorthand; and when I once brought up clonal selection in the immune system she said that's selection alright but not natural selection proper, since nothing gets passed through a germline.

so the new case, which is basically my thesis: orca pods where a foraging technique -- deliberately beaching to grab sea lion pups, and investing real teaching time in calves, which cuts into the adults' own hunting -- spreads culturally and pods that have the tradition do better than pods that don't. is that natural selection at the level of the cultural variant, or the pod, or is she forced to say genes for social learning are the real unit and the cultural spread is development? predict her verdict, tell me which rule she seems to be running, which other rules fit her record just as well, and what further case would best pull those rules apart. I care because if she rules it "not natural selection proper" my whole framing is dead in the water.
