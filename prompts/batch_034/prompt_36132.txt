I spent most of my career in pharmacokinetics, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy, lately on weakness of will. I have settled on a package of views and would like a consistency audit rather than a lecture. Here is the package: (1) akrasia is real and common — I sometimes do, freely and without compulsion, what I judge at the very moment of action that I have most reason not to do; (2) its mechanism is hyperbolic discounting, in the tradition of George Ainslie: as a reward comes near, its discounted value rises steeply enough that preferences reverse, so the akratic agent is not malfunctioning but acting on a systematically distorted valuation curve; (3) I retain a scientist's sympathy for revealed preference — what you choose is the best evidence of what you want, and appeals to what one 'really' wants apart from choices deserve suspicion; (4) I reject the traditional picture of reason as a faculty overridden by passion — the deliberating self is not more truly me than the craving one; (5) precommitment devices — paying a trainer in advance, keeping no sweets in the house — strike me as paradigms of rationality, and I rely on them myself; (6) people remain responsible, and properly blameworthy, for their akratic failures. Please tell me which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and rank the tensions by severity; for each, say what would have to give and what the concession would cost; and identify the single revision that would clean up the most. Finally, tell me what would change your verdict — which argument or consideration would move the audit to a different conclusion — and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to go wrong. I am quite prepared to hear that a lifetime of fitting curves to data has made me reach for a mechanistic explanation where a normative one is required.
