I'm TAing a philosophy of mind course and grading midterms, and there's one answer I keep going back and forth on, so I'd like a second opinion before I commit to a grade.

The question I wrote was:

'Dennett uses the color phi phenomenon to pressure a certain picture of the mind: two spots, one red and one green, flash in quick succession at different locations, and subjects report seeing a single moving spot that changes color halfway across. Some theorists say the brain rewrote the record after the fact, so the experience itself was different from what was later remembered (the Orwellian option, on analogy with a regime that edits history books after the events). Others say the brain inserted the color change before the experience was ever assembled, fabricating it in advance (the Stalinesque option, on analogy with a show trial where the verdict is fixed before the evidence comes in). Explain what Dennett calls the Cartesian theater and Cartesian materialism, why he thinks the debate between these two options rests on a shared mistake, and how his multiple drafts model is supposed to dissolve the debate.'

And here's the student's answer (their spelling, not mine):

'The Cartesian theater is Descartes' idea that experience happens in one central place where everything is displayed for the mind to observe. Dennett mostly agrees with Descartes' critics here and wants to defend modern science against this leftover dualist mythology. He calls the materialist version of the mistake Cartesian materialism: the belief that even though there is no soul, there is still a special place in the brain, like the prefrontal cortex, where contents arrive and become conscious. Most cognitive scientists believe this according to Dennett.

For the phi case, both options assume there is a fact about whether the color change was experienced before it was reported. Orwellians say the experience was clean and the record got corrupted afterwards, Stalinesques say the brain fixed the experience before it reached conciousness. Dennett says this is a false dichotomy because there is no single moment when a content becomes conscious. Instead multiple drafts of content get edited in parallel all over the brain, and there is no further step where a finished draft gets presented. So the question of whether the subject experienced the color change before reporting it has no determinate answer below a certain grain.

But we can still say something. Since all the editing happens before the report is produced, the Stalinesque option is basically correct, just without needing a theater: the brain does fabricate content, only not in one place. Also, since there are only drafts and never a presentation, the reports are confabulations that don't track any actual experience, so in the phi case nothing was ever really experienced at all. On the multiple drafts model a draft becomes conscious when it wins out over the other drafts and gets globally broadcast to the rest of the brain, and that broadcast is what we call consciousness, which explains why subjects insist they saw the change.'

The middle paragraph seems decent to me, and honestly I'm not sure how much to penalize the rest, or wether some of it is better than it first looks.
