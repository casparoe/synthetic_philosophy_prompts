hey, so I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we just did a unit on Dennett's idea that the self is basically a "center of narrative gravity" -- like, an abstract posit we invent to organize and predict all the narrative-spinning the brain does, comparable to how physicists talk about a center of mass without thinking there's a little heavy thing sitting there. our professor split the class into two camps for a debate and it went nowhere, everyone just talked past each other, so now she wants us to try the adversarial collaboration format instead.

the two camps were:

1. the deflationists, who basically buy Dennett's line: the self is a theoretical fiction / abstractum, no more a real entity than the average taxpayer or the equator, and that's fine -- it's an indispensable fiction and indispensability is all we ever needed anyway

2. the ego realists, who think that's way too deflationary: there is something it is to BE a self, the self is a real entity (they disagreed among themselves whether it's a brain process, a non-physical ego, or a higher-order organismic whole, but they all agreed the fiction talk undersells it) and deflationism either leaves out the subject of experience or can't explain why selves can suffer, be responsible, be destroyed, etc.

can you draft the joint statement these two camps should have been able to sign after a proper good-faith collaboration? I need:

- a numbered section of claims both sides can accept (they should be able to agree on a bunch of the descriptive stuff, like that we narrate ourselves, that "self" talk is predictive, that fission/fission-ish thought experiments do weird things to identity claims -- don't be scared to make this section do real work rather than just listing trivia)
- a disagreements section, ideally a table, where each remaining disagreement is stated twice, once in wording the deflationist would sign and once in wording the ego realist would sign -- I specifically want to see whether some of their fight was purely verbal (e.g. both saying "the self is real" but meaning different things by "real") and if so, which parts
- the cruxes: the sub-questions that would actually move at least one camp, and for each crux, the kind of argument, thought experiment, or evidence BOTH camps would agree is relevant, with each side's prediction about how it would come out written down in advance

one crux our TA suggested we include: whether explanatory indispensability is enough for the kind of reality worth caring about -- i.e. if you can't do psychology, law, or ethics without self-talk, does that settle the ontological question or not? feel free to use that and add others (personality fission, maybe, or whether the narrative gravity view can handle a self whose autobiography is co-written by other people, which one of the deflationists brought up and the realists hated).

btw please don't just have the disagreements section restate the two positions in their own slogans -- the whole point is formulations that are precise enough that the other side would say "yes, that's where we part ways, and that's a fair statement of it." and dont turn it into a verdict on who wins, this is supposed to be the document both camps could actually put their names on.
