Heres my opening move, from the capabilities side. The insurance-market move seems to give you a metric that's indexed to the wrong people entirely. On your scheme, someone born blind gets compensation equal to whatever a typical sighted person would pay to insure against blindness — but that price is set by the fears and tastes of sighted shoppers in a market they hope never pays out. It tells me nothing about what lack of sight means in the actual life of this particular person, in this society, with this much or this little curb-cut and screen-reader infrastructure. Two blind people get the same payout whether blindness is a manageable inconvenience for them or a total exclusion from everything they care about doing and being. Isn't that exactly backwards? You've built a currency that tracks the average able-bodied attitude toward a condition rather than the person's real freedom to live the life they have reason to value. And before you tell me resources are at least objective and measurable — fine, the payout is objective, but objective *and wrong* is still wrong.
