Write an essay, in British English, on whether Sleeping Beauty should be a halfer, a thirder, or something else altogether. Set the problem up carefully: Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is tossed, and she is awakened once if it lands heads and twice (on Monday and Tuesday, with her memory of the Monday awakening erased overnight) if it lands tails. On each awakening she is told the protocol but not the outcome of the toss nor the day. The halfer maintains her credence in heads upon awakening should remain one half, since she learned nothing she did not expect; the thirder argues it should be one third, on the grounds that a given awakening is twice as likely under tails. The double-halfer tries to have it both ways, holding that Beauty's credence in heads is one half while also resisting certain conditionalisation steps that would otherwise seem compulsory.

Your essay should weigh the strongest considerations on each side, but it should do more than recapitulate the familiar exchanges. In particular, you should address the following two tests that any position must survive:

1. The pricing test. Suppose that on each awakening Beauty may buy a ticket costing £10 that pays £30 if and only if the coin landed heads, with all transactions settled after the experiment concludes. Over many repetitions of the experiment, a Beauty who buys on every awakening loses money, since her wakings are twice as frequent in the tails worlds. Does this show that her credence in heads must be one third, or does it merely show that repeated betting odds and credence can come apart under centred (self-locating) evidence? Explain whether you think the divergence between fair betting quotients and credences here is a genuine possibility or a symptom of error.

2. The extreme test. Replace the coin with a lottery under which Beauty is awakened once if she wins and one million times if she loses. Does the halfer's position become untenable here, insofar as Beauty, on awakening, is all but certain she has lost, while remaining forbidden from revising her credence by the halfer's lights? If the extreme case moves you toward the thirder, say precisely why the millionfold version differs in principle, rather than merely in degree, from the original.

You may also consider whether the dispute is partly verbal — a disagreement about whether credence attaches to uncentred propositions only, or also to centred ones such as "today is Monday" — and whether the best resolution distinguishes different things that "Beauty's credence in heads" might mean.

Reach a definite verdict and defend it; a conditional verdict (for example, one view about Beauty's credence in uncentred propositions and another about her centred ones) is acceptable, but do not end on a non-committal note.
