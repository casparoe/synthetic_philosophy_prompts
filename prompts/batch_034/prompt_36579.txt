Here's one student's answer, verbatim:

"Compatibilism is the view that free will is compatible with determinism, so even if all our choices are fully caused by prior events, we can still be free. Incompatibilism is the view that free will and determinism can't both be true. The Consequence Argument, formulated by Peter van Inwagen, says that if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of the laws of nature and events in the remote past. It's not up to us what went on before we were born, and it's not up to us what the laws are, so the consequences of these things, including our present acts, aren't up to us either. Compatibilists have two main ways out. Some simply deny determinism — this is the libertarian position — and argue that quantum indeterminacy leaves room for genuinely free choices. Others, following Frankfurt, say the argument only works if free will requires alternative possibilities, and Frankfurt showed with his counterexample cases that alternative possibilities aren't necessary. So the argument is valid, and the premise about our lack of power over the past and the laws is where all the action is."
