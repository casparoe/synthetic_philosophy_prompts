Last week we spent a whole afternoon pooling everything we knew about whether to open a second office and still walked out disagreeing. She's been reading up on Aumann's agreement theorem and now says this is fine: since rational agents can never agree to disagree, and since we obviously haven't reached anything like common knowledge of each other's posteriors, we're allowed to just each keep our own estimate. Something about this bugs me though. Doesn't her reading prove way too much? If the theorem really said you shouldn't update until you've hit full common knowledge, then nobody could ever justifiably defer to a more accurate forecaster, which seems obviously wrong. So my actual question: given that we're not ideal Bayesians and never will be, what does the theorem actually license here? Should I just weight my own view more since I've been better calibrated, or is she missing something subtler about when agreeing to disagree is actually kosher?
