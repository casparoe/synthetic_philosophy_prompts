Say I can put together funding for two, maybe three researchers working for a couple of years on the pascal's mugging problem and its neighborhood -- the whole cluster of puzzles about tiny probabilities of astronomically large payoffs (or astronomically bad outcomes). What are the main open questions in that cluster right now, and which two or three would you actually prioritize, given that scale of resources? I care way more about the reasoning than the list: for each question, say what's downstream of it, whether it's tractable for decision theorists / formal philosophers as opposed to people who need to run big ML experiments or do psychology studies, and what would make you move it up or down the list over the next few years.

To give you a sense of what I mean by the cluster: there's the classic mugging itself (why "give me $5 or I'll torture 3^^^3 simulated people" fails to convince most people even though naive expected-value reasoning says pay up), the fan-out problem where any action has some tiny chance of triggering a huge effect so everything dominates everything, bounded utility functions as a proposed fix, and the response that boundedness just feels like sticking your head in the sand. But there are also some questions I think are underrated and I'd want your read on: e.g. whether the real action is epistemic rather than decision-theoretic -- i.e. whether the problem is that we have no principled account of how credences in claims like "I can affect 3^^^3 minds" should fall off as the claimed magnitude rises, such that they fall faster than linearly; and whether there's a clean difference between muggings that are adversarially generated by another agent versus fan-out scenarios that are just features of an indifferent universe, since intuitively the former seems easier to dismiss and I've never seen anyone make that distinction do real work. Also curious whether you'd count "how should an altruist allocate effort across causes when one candidate has small p times astronomical stakes" as the same problem or a different one that just resembles it -- and whether your prioritization changes depending on the answer.

Rank the top questions, defend the ordering, and tell me which popular takes in this area (bounded utilities, "just use your gut and cap the payoffs," Solomonoff-style penalties, whatever else comes to mind) you'd deprioritize and why. I'm not looking for a survey of the literature, I'm looking for an argument about where the actual crux is.
