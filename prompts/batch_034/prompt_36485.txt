I spent most of my career as an experimental condensed-matter physicist, and since retiring I have been working through the philosophy of religion with the same habits I applied to anomalous lab results: state the hypothesis precisely, separate what you actually know from what you have assumed, and resist the urge to accept an explanation just because it completes the story. The cosmological argument strikes me as the rare theological argument that can be put in that form, but I find myself unable to settle on a credence, and I would like you to help by producing one for me — an actual number, with the reasoning laid out term by term rather than hidden in a verbal conclusion.

The proposition, stated as precisely as I can manage: the totality of contingent concrete reality exists because it is caused or grounded in some necessary concrete being (or beings), rather than existing brute-factually or failing to need any explanation at all. Note that I am deliberately folding together the kalam version, which argues from the universe's having a beginning, and the contingency version, which argues from the universe's possible non-existence; you may treat them as independent routes to the same conclusion or argue that one collapses into the other, but tell me which and why.

Here is my own tentative decomposition, which I invite you to correct, restructure, or reject:

First, whether some sufficiently strong principle of sufficient reason applies to the existence of contingent things collectively — not merely to each thing individually, but to the aggregate. I gather there is a long-running dispute about whether explaining every member of a collection thereby explains the collection, and I have never seen why it obviously does or obviously doesn't. Second, whether causal finitism is true — whether an infinite regress of past causes, or an infinite series of dependent beings, is genuinely incoherent rather than merely unimaginable. I am aware of the Grim Reaper-style arguments purporting to show that infinities of this sort generate contradictions, but I am unsure how much they trade on specific assumptions about discrete events that may not apply to a physical continuum. Third, whether the physical considerations actually support a beginning — my understanding is that the quantum vacuum is not "nothing," that theorems about the impossibility of past-eternal spacetimes assume classical general relativity, and that current cosmology simply does not settle the question; but I would like your probability that the relevant physics, if we ever had it, would come down on the side of an absolute beginning. Fourth, whether a necessary concrete being is even coherent — I have in mind the objection that if a necessary being exists and everything it wills follows necessarily from its nature, then everything, including this morning's weather, is necessary, which looks like a reductio; I take it there are responses, but I don't know how good they are. Fifth, whether a necessary being, if coherent, would have to be personal rather than something more like an abstract structure or an impersonal ground — this seems to me the weakest link in the chain, and I suspect many people's final assent or dissent is really decided here while being officially carried by earlier terms.

Now my request. Assign a probability to each term in your own decomposition, defending each briefly — a few sentences, not an essay. Show how you combine them, and be explicit about whether the terms are independent, since multiplying them as though they were may be doing illegitimate work. Give me your final credence in the proposition as I stated it. Then tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to: the one where a plausible change in its probability would swing your conclusion most. Finally, since I am a scientist at heart, tell me what would count as evidence here at all — what discovery, argument, or conceptual development would genuinely move one of your terms, as opposed to merely giving the dispute new vocabulary. If you think the whole enterprise of assigning credences to a question like this is confused, say so, but then still tell me what you would put in place of the number.
