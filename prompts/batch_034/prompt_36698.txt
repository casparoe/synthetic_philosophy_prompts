Quick bit of context, then the question. I've been in a reading group on Jain philosophy for about a year, and the recurring fight about anekāntavāda has gone completly nowhere, and I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself rather than another round of it.

The fight, in case you need it sketched: critics say the doctrine that every ordinary claim about reality is only partially true — reality is many-sided, each statement captures one aspect, one standpoint (naya) — is self-refuting. If every view is one-sided and partial, so is anekāntavāda itself, so why would anyone accept it? The standard Jain reply runs through syādvāda, the sevenfold predication: each statement is implicitly prefixed with something like "in some respect", so "the soul exists" is fine as long as you hear it as true from one standpoint, and the seemingly opposite claim is equally fine from another. The famous illustration is the blind men and the elephant — the one holding the trunk says it's like a snake, the one holding the leg says a pillar, each right about the part and wrong to absolutise it. So the Jain says the self-refutation charge rests on flattening their doctrine into just another one-sided proposition, which is precisely what they deny it is.

And then nothing moves. The critics don't think the "in some respect" prefix saves anything — to them it just makes every claim compatible with its own negation, so the Jain ends up with a logic that can't rule anything out (hence the old jab that on this view nothing can ever be said to be outright false). The Jains don't think the charge even lands, since from their side it presupposes a standpoint-free standpoint from which to issue it, which is the one thing they deny exists. Modern versions of this exchange — Daya Krishna pressing objections, Matilal answering for the Jains — read like nobody conceded an inch, and I don't get the sense the older versions went any better.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My working hypothesis is that it's not really about truth at all but about what a proposition is — the Jain side working with something like aspect-qualified or standpoint-indexed assertions, the critics with plain assertoric ones, so that each side's reply looks like a concession or a dodge only relative to the other's grammar, and a good chunk of it is verbal. But I'm genuinely unsure, because there seems to be a substantive core as well: whether there is any standpoint-free standpoint, and whether a logic that qualifies every predication can still do real exclusionary work — e.g. can it rule out the claim that the elephant has no trunk at all, and if it can, what exactly did the qualification buy?

Diagnose the thing using what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a sharper distinction, a formal treatment of the sevenfold scheme, reframing the whole question, whatever it is. Or argue the impasse is structural and no progress should be expected. I don't want a survey of Jain metaphysics, we've all read the surveys, and please no reading lists or citations — just the diagnosis, in your own reasoning.
