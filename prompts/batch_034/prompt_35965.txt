I've been reading around the deflationism debate for a couple months now and I've hit a wall that I suspect is in the debate itself, not in me. On one side you have the deflationists, who say that truth is exhausted by the equivalence schema -- that asserting 'snow is white' is true just amounts to asserting snow is white, and there's no further property to analyze, no nature of truth to uncover. On the other side the inflationists, who insist truth is a substantive property with real explanatory work to do -- explaining why true beliefs tend to be useful, underwriting the semantic notion of truth we need for theories of meaning, or capturing what's in common between true sentences in different languages. And the arguments have been rehearsed so many times: deflationists say the explanatory jobs can be done by the schema plus disquotation, inflationists say the schema is only a partial specification and that you can't even state the schema's own generality without a truth predicate worth having, and around it goes. From what I can tell neither side has moved much in decades, and smart people remain on both.

My working hypothesis is that this isn't really one disagreement but at least two running under the same name: one is a genuine metasemantic dispute about whether there's a truth property worth theorizing over and above our use of the predicate, and the other is a verbal dispute about what counts as a "theory of truth" at all -- where the deflationist is answering a different question (what does the truth predicate do for us in the language?) than the inflationist (what makes truth-bearers true?). But I'm honestly not sure, because sometimes the fight also looks like a disagreement about explanation: about whether truth's role in, say, explaining successful action is genuinely explanatory or just a cheap heuristic, which seems like it could be a substantive issue about explanation itself that nobody is arguing about directly.

So: diagnose this stalemate for me. Is it verbal, is it two questions under one heading, is it a hidden fight about what explanation requires, or is it a real substantive dispute that's just hard? Use what each side actually claims to support the diagnosis, not a survey of positions. And then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a sharper distinction, a test case, a reformulated question -- or make the case that this debate is doing its real work as a placeholder and should be left alone. Keep it under 600 words please, I want a diagnosis not an encyclopedia.
