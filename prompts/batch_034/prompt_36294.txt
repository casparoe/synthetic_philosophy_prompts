My friend is one of those people with a hot take on everything the EU should and shouldn't be allowed to do. Her verdicts so far: trade deals and tariffs at EU level, yes; product standards for anything sold across borders, EU level, yes; emission rules where the pollution crosses borders, yes; letting the EU court strike down national rules that discriminate against citizens of other member states, yes; making candidate countries abolish the death penalty before they can join, yes; retirement age, national; how history gets taught in schools, national; abortion, national; income tax rates on individuals, national. Next week's argument is about an EU court ruling that would force every member state to legalize same-sex marriage. What's she going to say? I care less about the answer than about the rule she's implicitly running -- is it pooling sovereignty wherever effects leak across borders, or keeping anything touching national identity out of Brussels while taking the "technical" stuff, or treating the EU as a shield for minorities against national majorities? Several of those fit her track record, they don't all agree on this case, and I want to know which one she's really running and what case I should bring up to tell them apart.
