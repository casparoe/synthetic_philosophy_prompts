Explain the difference between Peirce's account of truth (as tied to the outcome of inquiry conducted by a community over the long run) and James's account (as tied to how a belief functions and pays off in experience). Here's the student's answer, which I've pasted in full:

"Peirce's pragmatism says that the meaning of a concept is just its practical effects, and that's also his theory of truth: an idea is true if holding it has good practical consequences for you. He thought truth is what the community of inquirers ends up agreeing on after enough investigation, so truth is basically consensus, and beliefs are tools that get their meaning from their cash value. James took this further and made it more individual: for him a belief is true if it works for the particular person holding it, in the sense of making their life go better and satisfying their needs. So Peirce's version is social and James's is personal, but both reduce truth to usefulness.

On the objection: it does look fatal for both. For Peirce, the community of inquirers has often converged on things that turned out false — for centuries basically everyone agreed the sun went around the earth — so consensus is demonstrably not truth, and one good counterexample refutes a definition. Also, if inquiry never actually ends, Peirce's account never delivers a verdict on anything, so it's not really a theory of truth at all but a promissory note. For James the problem is even worse: if a comforting religious belief makes someone's life better, it comes out true for them, so two contradictory beliefs can both be true for different people. James was well aware of this and embraced it — he says relativism about truth is just the honest conclusion of pragmatism. So the objection doesn't refute James since he accepts it, but it does refute Peirce, since Peirce was a realist who wouldn't accept it. In conclusion, Peirce's account fails because consensus can be wrong and inquiry might not finish, and James's survives only by giving up on truth being objective, which most of us aren't willing to do."
