Write me a satirical travel-guide entry, deadpan Lonely Planet style, around 600-700 words, for the fictional Republic of Nondom, the world's first country to fully organize itself around the republican (Pettit-style) conception of liberty as non-domination. The tourists arrive expecting a libertarian paradise and keep getting confused.

The jokes have to come from actually getting the philosophy right, not from strawmanning it. So, e.g., the guide cheerfully explains that interference per se is fine — the state interferes with you constantly (schedules your day, edits your letters, vetoes your minor purchases) but it's all non-arbitrary because it's contestable: there's a Bureau of Contestation where you can appeal any interference, staffed by officials who can also interfere with you but in a trackable way. A tourist asks a local "but who dominates the dominators?" and the guide treats this as a charming FAQ question that has been Fully Resolved by the Anticipatory Accountability Commission, which oversees the Bureau, and is itself overseen by a shadow commission whose members are secret. Include a section on local customs where the country's low crime of "dependence": waiters are forbidden from having only one employer, spouses must file "exit plans" annually with the Ministry of Non-Arbitrariness, and a landlord who is too charming is fined for creating relational vulnerability. There should be a boxed "Reality Check" sidebar where a visiting liberal (in the Berlin sense) complains that nothing ever gets *stopped* in this country, merely made contestable — and the guide's editorial response, also in the box, that at least no one here confuses the sheep's freedom with the wolf's opinion on fencing.

Somewhere in there, get in a tourist tip about the national holiday, Non-Domination Day, on which no one is permitted to do anything they weren't going to do anyway, since unstructured choice creates zones of arbitrary power. Maybe a small note that the northern provinces, which briefly tried implementing non-interference instead, are now described in the guide as "loosely organized."

The target being satirized is the republican theory itself but fairly — a political philosopher who works on liberty should read it and feel it's a bit unfair but concede that every joke tracks a real objection (the guards problem, the vagueness of "arbitrary," whether contestability is worth anything if you always lose the contest, the weirdness of counting a benevolent master as domination but a meddlesome democrat as freedom). Keep it deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader. Format it like a real guidebook page: bolded headers, "When to go", "Getting around", a couple of pull-quotes from invented locals, the works.
