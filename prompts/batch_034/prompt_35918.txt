so I've got to write up my position on political obligation / civil disobedience for a seminar, and before I do I want a consistency check because I suspect there's a problem in here somewhere and I genuinely can't see it. Here's the package:

(1) I don't think there's any general obligation to obey the law just because it's the law. The classic theories all seem to fail: consent basically never actually happens, fair play gets counterexamples (you can have benefits thrust on you that you never wanted, and you don't incur debts that way), and I don't see how mere membership in a state you can't realistically leave gets you to obedience.

(2) But I do think that in a reasonably just society you have a genuine prima facie duty to comply with laws even when you think they're substantively wrong — you should pay your taxes even though some programs they fund are unjust, you should follow regulations you think are dumb, etc. Not perfect compliance, but a duty that covers most ordinary cases.

(3) I hold the orthodox line on civil disobedience: it has to be public, nonviolent, and done with a willingness to accept the legal consequences. If you're sneaky or violent about it, it's not civil disobedience anymore, it's just resistance or conscientious refusal.

(4) I actually think the accepting-punishment part is the philosophically load-bearing bit, not just a sociological marker. The willingness to submit to punishment is what shows fidelity to law and what makes the act an appeal to the community's shared sense of justice rather than an ultimatum or a power play.

(5) At the same time, I think people who covertly break deeply unjust laws — hiding someone from enforcement, leaking documents to expose abuses — are usually justified, and I don't think the fact that they conceal what they're doing and would evade punishment if they could counts against them at all. Leaking isn't less justified for being hidden.

So which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what that costs. I honestly can't tell whether the problem is (2) vs (1), or (3)/(4) vs (5), or both, or whether I'm missing something else entirely. Please don't just flag everything as in tension — I want to know which ones are the real ones.
