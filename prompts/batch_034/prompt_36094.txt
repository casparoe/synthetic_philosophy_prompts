Me and a guy on my team, Rohan, have this running lunch debate about whether you're allowed to add up tiny bad things into one big bad thing. He's given verdicts on a bunch of hypotheticals over the months and he never wavers on them, so I think there's an actual rule in there somewhere, I just can't pin it down. His verdicts so far:

- One person tortured for 50 years vs. every person alive getting a single dust speck in the eye (instant, no other effects): the specks, obviously, he thinks picking the torture is basically a bug in your moral software.
- Same torture vs. all 8 billion people getting a moderate sunburn that ruins their afternoon: the sunburns win.
- Same torture vs. all 8 billion people getting one hard pinch on the arm, one second and then it's over: the torture wins. Pinches "don't add up," he says.
- One person tortured 50 years vs. two people tortured 25 years each: the two people, so he's clearly not against aggregation across persons in general.
- One person living 50 years with constant low-grade pinch-level pain (never a break, but never worse than a pinch) vs. the torture: he says the torture is worse but the pinch-life is still a genuinely awful thing, not something you can just ignore. He would not trade the pinch-life away for two broken legs, for example.

Next time I want to spring this on him: the same 50-year torture vs. 3↑↑↑3 pinches, one per person, spread across that many separate people (so, incomprehensibly more people than could ever exist, but each one just gets the single one-second pinch and nothing else). I genuinely can't predict what he'll say and it's bugging me.
