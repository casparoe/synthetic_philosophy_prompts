My partner and I are trying to decide whether to have a child, and a dinner-party argument has lodged itself in my head. A friend of ours — a committed utilitarian — claimed that if our child would have a life comfortably worth living, creating it adds net welfare to the world and harms no one, so remaining child-free is roughly like declining to donate to an effective charity: not wicked, perhaps, but a real moral cost. He then pushed further, conceding that this style of reasoning appears to imply that a future containing trillions of people whose lives are barely above neutral would be better than one containing billions of deeply flourishing people; he admitted that this sounded repugnant, but bit the bullet anyway.

My partner holds the opposite intuition. She says there is an asymmetry we all feel but struggle to articulate: that bringing into existence a child whose life contains a great deal of suffering wrongs that child, whereas not creating a child who would have been happy deprives no one, since there is no one yet there to be deprived. Yet she worries that taking this asymmetry seriously would commit us to creating as many happy children as we can reasonably support, so long as each would be glad to exist — a conclusion she finds even worse than our friend's bullet-biting.

I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for over a decade, so I've seen fragments of this debate many times, but never a careful treatment of a case like ours. Is there a principled position available to us that avoids both of the bullets my friend and my partner are tempted to bite, or must we simply pick which unpalatable commitment to live with? Please advise on what we should think and how we might go about deciding, and write your response in American English.
