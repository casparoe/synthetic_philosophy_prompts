My gut reactions to a bunch of nonidentity-style cases, and I'm wondering if they hang together or if I'm just contradicting myself. Tell me what principle, if any, captures them, which of my verdicts it has to throw out, and what it says about the case I keep flip-flopping on at the end.

The cases: (1) A country adopts a wasteful policy that's great for now but means the people who exist two centuries later — completely different people than would have existed otherwise, because of all the changed marriages etc. — have lives barely worth living. I think the policy is clearly wrong. (2) A 14-year-old deliberately conceives when she could easily have waited a few years and had a healthier child with a different father. I think that's wrong too, but wrong to *whom*? The kid can hardly complain, since the alternative was never being born. (3) A woman delays taking a medication she needs, in a way that means the child she later conceives is a different child than she would have had on the earlier schedule, and that child has a serious but very manageable condition. Honestly that seems fine to me — no one is worse off than they otherwise would've been. (4) A couple conceives right after finding out a rubella exposure will likely cause serious impairments, when waiting a year would've meant a healthy (but genetically different) kid. That feels plainly wrong, even though again nobody seems wronged in the "worse off than the alternative" sense. (5) Choosing to have a child at all, when you know the kid will have a life that's great overall but well below average happiness — that's obviously permissible, it can't be wrong to give someone a good life.

The tension that's bugging me: (1) and (4) I call wrong, (3) and (5) I call fine, and the line between them doesn't seem to be anyone being made worse off, since in every case the alternative was a different person or no person at all. So is there one principle that gets all five, or do I have to give one up? And what does the best surviving principle say about the one I can't settle: a Deaf couple who specifically conceive during a window where the child is very likely to be Deaf, because they want a child who shares their language and community, and they're confident the child's life will be good? Part of me says that's just (5) with extra steps, and part of me says its more like (4).
