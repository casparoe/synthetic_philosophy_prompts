my philosophy of language reading group is doing a session on deflationary theories of truth and i want to close it with a poem, a villanelle. the concept needs to live in the refrains: refrain A is just "the sky is blue" and refrain B is "the sky is blue" is true. the deflationist's whole bet is that B does nothing A doesn't, so as the refrains take their turns through the poem, the reader should slowly notice that B is just A wearing the truth predicate like a hat — same content, one word of disquotation bolted on. let the middle tercets be the speaker basically checking, every time the refrain comes back, whether the hat has changed anything, and it never does. use the b-rhymes to carry the actual scenery (weather, whatever) so the poem is also just a poem and not only a diagram.

but let the form do the hard part at the end: the two refrains finally meet in the closing couplet, and thats where the poem has to face the one place the trick strains — when you want to endorse a whole pile of sentences at once, like everything the witness said is true, but you can't just say the sentences because you dont have the list, you only have the predicate. so let the final couplet be the moment the speaker tries to generalize and finds the bare sentence alone can't get there. dont turn it into a refutation of deflationism, just leave it sitting there as the honest residue.

someone who knows the redundancy/disquotational stuff should be able to tell exactly what's going on, including which parts are the equivalence schema and which part is the generalization worry, and someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem. after the poem, add a few lines saying where you took liberties or leaned on a contested reading, and which interpretive choice youre least confident about.
