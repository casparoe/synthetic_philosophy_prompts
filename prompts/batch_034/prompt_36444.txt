I've been teaching the ethics of war seminar for a while now, and every single year we hit the same wall on the moral equality of combatants: everyone knows the cases, everyone knows the moves, but nobody ever says out loud what a theory actually has to deliver before it counts as settling anything. So before I make my grad students read yet another round of this, I want the requirements list written down properly.

Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the ethics of killing in war has to get right. Some things I'd want on the list, but you should feel free to add or push back — it has to give a verdict on the conscripted teenager on the unjust side that doesn't collapse into either "he's a murderer for shooting back" or "war is just a morally neutral contest and both sides are fine"; it has to say something about the civilian machinist making artillery fuses without making the combatant/civilian line do all the work; it has to handle partisans and irregulars who don't wear uniforms at all; it has to either be consistent with what we say about self-defense between individuals in peacetime or explain exactly why war changes it; and it has to respect the epistemic situation of actual soldiers, who mostly don't know and maybe can't know whether their side is the just one. For each requirement, give the case behind it — why it's a requirement and not just a preference of one camp (the traditionalists and the revisionists each have their pet demands, and I want to know which of those are real).

Then the part I care about most: flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. My own hunch is that the consistency-with-individual-self-defense demand and something like the permission to kill in a symmetrical firefight can't both hold, but I've never managed to make that argument carefully and I want to see if it holds up. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly — that's the interesting result.

Finally, briefly: which single requirement does the most to knock out the main contenders (the traditionalist picture, the revisionist view in the style of McMahan, and the conventionalist/contractualist attempts to rescue equality)? I know this literature cold, so just name the moves — no citations, no reading list, I'll fill those in myself.

Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence — which requirement does the most work, and which pair is unsatisfiable if any — then give the reasoning. No preamble and don't restate my question back at me, I know what I asked.
