Something I've never managed to get straight about SETI. On the listening side, the search is basically only for what Hofstadter would call the frame message: a narrowband blip that announces I-am-a-message-from-something-artificial, and nobody seriously expects to decode the content even if one day we find it. On the transmitting side, though, when humans send stuff out (Arecibo, the Voyager record) we obsess over decoding aids — prime numbers, a hydrogen atom, drawings of us — because we assume a receiver can bootstrap from universal maths and physics to our actual meaning. And Hofstadter himself splits messages into a frame (a notice that this is a message at all), an outer message (the implicit instructions for how to read it) and an inner message (the content), with the kicker that the outer message somehow has to be understood before any decoding happens, so meaning is never entirely located in the signal itself.

So my question: what does SETI as an actual funded institution presuppose about the location of meaning? Does the search strictly presuppose only that frame layer is detectable, with outer and inner left as someone else's problem, or does even spotting "artificial vs natural" already presuppose more shared context than the searchers admit? And are the two halves even consistent — the listeners assume no decoding is on the cards, the broadcasters design elaborate self-decrypting beacons — or do they quietly rest on rival theories of where meaning lives? Reconstruct the implicit view, say where the whole enterprise is underdetermined so that several theories of meaning would licence it, and tell me what SETI would have to change if we took the Hofstadter line seriously and held that the outer message cant ride along with the signal at all.
