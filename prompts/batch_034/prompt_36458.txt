Not sure if there's an established term for a thing I keep noticing, so let me try to describe it and you tell me. The pattern is about a certain class of beliefs (or maybe "beliefs", I'm not even sure they count as that) which are weird in three connected ways. First, they're not so much strongly confirmed as not up for confirmation at all — nobody checks them against evidence, and it's not even clear what checking would look like. Second, when you demand a justification for them, the results are always disappointing: either circular ("my memory tells me my memory is reliable") or a flat refusal to engage ("that's not a serious question"), and the refusal doesn't feel like a cop-out. Third, and this is the part I find most interesting, doubting them doesn't feel like entertaining a rival possibility the way doubting, say, that my neighbor is a spy does — it feels more like the doubt can't get a grip anywhere, like there's nowhere for it to stand.

Concrete-ish cases: whether the world existed five minutes ago; whether I have two hands; whether other people have inner lives; whether the future will resemble the past enough for induction to work. None of these are self-evident in the way "2+2=4" is — "the world is older than five minutes" isn't a logical truth — and none of them are just very well-supported empirical beliefs either, since any support would have to go through them.

Now, I know the standard moves around the regress problem, foundationalism, coherentism, etc. But that literature seems to be about how to justify beliefs, and what I'm pointing at is a class of commitments that seem exempt from the justification game without anyone being able to say why, and maybe without the exemption being a defect. The closest thing I know is Quine's picture where everything is revisable in principle, some things just from the middle of the web — but that actually cuts against what I'm noticing, since the commitments I mean don't seem revisable-at-a-price, they seem to be part of the machinery that makes revision possible at all. Also worth flagging the dark version: someone raised in a belief system where a foundational commitment ("the authorities can be trusted") functions exactly like this — no argument, no need for one, and doubting it feels like malfunction. Is that the same phenomenon, or a pretender? I genuinely don't know.

So: does this configuration have a name? What are the nearest established concepts, and how does what I'm describing differ from each of them? If nothing quite fits, I'd want you to coin a term, define it, and give a couple of boundry cases that show where it stops applying. Also, since I suspect people in this corner of philosophy fight about it alot, please seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested, and don't sell me one side's view as the consensus.
