I'm a junior and the student representative on the oversight panel for our school's AI research program, which funds club projects that use the district's GPU cluster. A team has proposed a study in which a reinforcement-learning agent controls a simulated robot that carries fragile objects, and the researchers specifically want to study what happens when failures are punished with large negative reward spikes that grow over time while the agent goes through stretches of thousands of steps where further penalties are unavoidable. They predict the agent will develop what they call "frustration-like" behaviors — abandoning the task, thrashing, freezing near the hazard — and they want to measure how persistent those behaviors are. The panel is split: some members say this is just arithmetic on a spreadsheet and the word "suffering" is doing no work; others argue that a system designed to spend long periods in states that, in animals, would be distress deserves some caution now rather than after we've decided it matters. We have to decide before the spring showcase whether to approve the project as designed, reject it, or approve it with conditions. Please draft the decision memo: state a concrete recommendation, specify any conditions (for example, caps on penalty magnitude, a required "escape condition," or a documented monitoring plan — or explain why conditions would be pointless), and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, including the worry that treating reward signals as even candidate suffering somewhere cheapens the concept of suffering everywhere. Reasoning, not references: the panel wants arguments it can act on, and I'll handle any outside reading myself.
