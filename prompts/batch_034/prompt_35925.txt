In my reporting so far I've noticed that a lot of the interesting thinking is scattered across communities that don't talk to each other: archival theorists debating appraisal, historians warning against arguments from silence, librarians fighting over reparative description and who has the authority to rename things in a catalogue, and technologists assuming that digitization solves a problem that their own selection criteria quietly recreate. My problem is practical: with one big reporting trip and a hard word limit, I can go deep on only two or three of these threads, and I don't trust my own sense of which ones matter.

I'd like you to act as a philosopher-historian of recordkeeping — someone equally at home in epistemology and archival theory — and help me set the editorial agenda rather than merely survey the field. Before answering, please take a moment to brainstorm broadly: what are the genuinely open questions in this area, and which of them are actually load-bearing? Then give me a defended prioritization. Some candidate threads I'm weighing: whether the digitization of collections democratizes memory or merely re-entrenches the appraiser's original selections at planetary scale; whether community-run and post-custodial archives genuinely redistribute archival power or just relocate it; whether silences in the record should be treated as positive evidence about the past or as something about which we must simply suspend judgment; and the uncomfortable fact that for many marginalized people, the most complete records that exist of them were produced by surveillance and policing. Rank these (or replace them with better ones I haven't thought of), and defend the ordering explicitly: tell me which criteria you're using — importance to how history gets written, tractability for a general reader, neglectedness, and how many downstream questions depend on each — and where those criteria pull against each other. I also want you to be frank about which threads are overrated, in the sense that an engaging piece could be written about them but the underlying question wouldn't actually move. Finally, tell me what would change your ranking: what kind of development, over the next few years, would make me regret having built the piece around one thread rather than another. Please reason carefully and be willing to disagree with my framing if some of my candidate threads rest on a confusion.
