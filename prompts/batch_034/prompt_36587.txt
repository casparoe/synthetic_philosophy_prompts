Here is my situation. In my study group, a classmate — call her D. — has converted herself to dialetheism on the strength of the following argument, and I cannot find the flaw, though I refuse to accept the conclusion. She calls it the platitude argument, because every premise looks like something I would assert in a freshman course:

(1) For any sentence S, S is true if and only if things are as S says they are. (The platitude.)
(2) Let L be the sentence 'L is not true.' L says that L is not true. Nothing about L is exotic: ordinary syntax, ordinary vocabulary, a name for itself, and the truth predicate we use every day.
(3) Now suppose, with the gap theorists, that L is neither true nor false.
(4) Then L is not true.
(5) But by (2), what L says is that L is not true — and by (4), that is exactly how things are.
(6) So by (1), L is true. Contradiction with (3).
(7) Suppose instead that L is false. Then L is not true, and (5)–(6) rerun to make L true as well. Contradiction again.
(8) Suppose instead that L is true. Then by (1), things are as L says, so L is not true. Contradiction a third time.
(9) So every possible verdict on L contradicts itself, and since L is an ordinary sentence of English, truth distributes inconsistently over English. Dialetheism follows.

I want to say something slipped between (4) and (6) — something about how 'not true' behaves inside L, under the supposition in (3), versus how it behaves in my own mouth in (4) — but I can't make it precise, and D. points out that (4) is a platitude too.
