So I got into a long argument with a friend the other night about Benacerraf's dilemma and it's still bugging me. My position going in was the standard one: platonism about math is caught between a uniform Tarskian semantics that reads our mathematical sentences as being about causally inert abstract objects, and any epistemology that could actually explain how we reliably form true beliefs about such things. I take the dilemma to be a genuine, still-unresolved problem for platonism -- not a museum piece. My friend thinks its mostly an artifact of the 1970s. His line was that the epistemological horn only packs a punch if you're already committed to the causal theory of knowledge, which basically nobody accepts anymore, so once you drop causal constraint on knowledge the "access problem" is just hand-waving about how weird abstract objects are. He also said the semantic horn assumes we need a uniform semantics for mathematical and empirical discourse in the first place, and that platonists from Gödel onward had an answer ready in the form of mathematical intuition anyway. Plus he gestured at Field's project: if nominalizing science actually worked, indispensability wouldnt prop up platonism and the dilemma loses its point.

I want to know what would actually count against my view -- that the dilemma is a real and open problem. Not just a rundown of standard platonist replies, but what would have to be true for the dilemma itself to fail. Does a fully worked out non-causal epistemology of mathematics do it? Does a completed nominalization of science? A deflationary take on reference that lets mathematical terms "refer" without genuine reference to numbers? Does it matter if the dilemma gets generalized to other domains (moral facts, modality) and seems to work there too? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a reasonable defender of my view would concede versus explain away, and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- where every escape route someone proposes just gets redescribed as another instance of the access problem. And if it has, is that a vice or just what youd expect from a genuinely general argument?
