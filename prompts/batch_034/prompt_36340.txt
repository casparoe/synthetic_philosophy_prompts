I'm a retired biochemist who fell into philosophy pretty hard after I stopped working — mostly ethics, reading on my own, so go easy on jargon you don't explain. I now run a small philosophy discussion group at our community center: about 12 people, mostly retirees like me, 90 minutes a session. No philosophy background, but they're sharp and they love to argue.

Next month I want to do a session on hedonism — the view that pleasure is the only thing that's good in itself. The thing I want them to actually feel, not just hear about, is what I gather philosophers call the heterogeneity problem: the pleasure of eating a ripe peach, of finally getting a calculation to work, of watching your grandkid sleep — they seem to share nothing but the word. If there's no common property running through them, then "pleasure" starts to look like a placeholder, and hedonism risks being either trivially true or empty.

So design an activity where they run into this themselves before I name anything. My half-baked idea was some kind of committee that has to write admission criteria for a "club of pleasures," and keeps discovering that every rule they propose either lets in stuff nobody would call pleasure or kicks out obvious cases — but I'm open to a better structure, and honestly I mostly need help with the details: setup, materials, timing, how to stage the reveal, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the actual philosophy. I understand there are standard responses on the table — that the pleasures only resemble each other family-resemblance style, or that there is a common felt quality we're just bad at describing — and I'd want the debrief to lay those out fairly rather than stack the deck. Also give me a note on what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the problem a game in a room just can't reproduce. My suspicion is that the deep question — whether the common property exists and is merely hard to describe, versus there being no such property at all — isn't something you settle by voting, and I'd rather know that up front then kid myself.

One last thing: no real money changing hands, and a couple of these folks will absolutely try to win by being clever, so the design should survive a smart aleck.
