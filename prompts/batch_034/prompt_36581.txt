quick backstory: I moved into a 76-unit condo building last year and somehow got talked onto the board, and we just had our big budget meeting for the leftover repair money. it got heated and I left still not knowing whether I was right.

we have one pot of money left this year. option one: a family on the third floor has had basically no water pressure since before I even moved in — shower's a trickle, can't run the dishwasher, and one of them has a chronic condition where daily soaks/exercises are part of managing it, and its genuinely wrecking their quality of life. fixing it means replacing the riser, expensive, eats the whole pot. option two: a long list of small stuff — brighter hallway lights, a new mailbox system, weatherstripping the lobby doors, patching the gym ceiling. every unit benefits a little, nobody benefits alot.

a guy on the board (engineer, loves spreadsheets) ran the numbers: even valuing each small fix super modestly per unit, 76 units times a small benefit beats one family's big benefit. he says refusing to count that is sentimentality, the family is one vote like everyone else, and the vote went for option two. I voted no, got crushed, and now I'm wondering if I was just being irrational about it.

but two things keep nagging me and I want to actually think them through rather than go with my gut at the next meeting:

1) another board member said: imagine it wasn't 76 units each getting one small annoyance, but one single person getting ALL the small annoyances — every dim bulb, every drafty door, all in their unit. that person would plausibly be worse off than the pressure family, and then the small stuff wins even on my view. so do I secretly think you can add burdens up, just only within one life? if so, what's actually wrong with adding across lives — is that a real moral difference or just an artifact of how I picture the situation?

2) someone also asked whether it'd change my mind if instead of 76 separate little fixes it was one single improvement everyone enjoyed a bit — like a shared rooftop thing. that feels different to me than the long list of little fixes, but I can't say why, and it might be nothing.

so: was the spreadsheet guy's argument fair? was my no vote defensible or just vibes? and does the vote itself (everyone counted equally, fair procedure) settle anything, or does it just launder the same question through a show of hands? help me figure out what I actually think, not what feels warm. and since I'll have to say this out loud to actual neighbors — tell me how confident you are in each piece, and flag the part you'd be least sure of standing behind.
