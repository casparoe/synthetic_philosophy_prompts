I write novels for a living, so I spend alot of time inventing politicians, and I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about career politicians for a character — except now it's bugging me personally, so help me out. Here's where my gut lands on a bunch of cases. Our city councilwoman who's held the seat for like 25 years and knows every corner of the municipal code: I think she's great, and the fact that politics is literally her career seems like a point in her favor. A senator who arrived idealistic, served six terms, and now mostly raises money and blocks things: that's everything wrong with politics, but I can't honestly say the difference is that one is nice and the other isn't. I voted for term limits back in the day, but once they kicked in, our council turned into a rotating cast of real estate agents who had no clue how anything worked, while the developers' lawyers simply waited them out — that seemed worse than the disease. Then I read about a citizens' assembly picked by lottery to redraw district maps, and that struck me as obviously a good idea. But the same lottery applied to an actual legislature with real power — a random sample of my neighbors writing laws — I recoil from that, even though I can't say why, since I trust juries fine. And a prime minister who keeps winning fair elections for fifteen straight years: something in me says that's unhealthy even though each election was legitimate. So: is there a principle here about when long tenure in office is fine and when you'd rather have amateurs or a lottery, or am I just pattern-matching to whatever face I picture when I hear the words "career politician"? If there is a principle, tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: a politician who is genuinely brilliant at the job, loves it, and never does anything else with their life — should I admire that person or worry about them?
