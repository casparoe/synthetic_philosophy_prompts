I run a small monthly reading group of curious non-specialists — engineers, a nurse, a retired schoolteacher — and next month's session, forty-five minutes with a whiteboard, is on Kuhnian incommensurability. Don't explain the idea to me; plan how I should explain it. Three things I want decided for me explicitly.

First, the dependency structure: in what order must the components come, and what does each depend on? The group will need at least a working sense of what a paradigm is beyond "a big theory," and of local versus global incommensurability, before the thesis itself — but tell me if you think that ordering is wrong, and why.

Second, the carrying example. I want you to choose exactly one historical case and argue for it over the alternatives. I suspect it should be a case of taxonomic reclassification rather than straightforward refutation — the phlogiston-to-oxygen shift, or the changing referent of terms like mass across the Newton–Einstein transition — but I'm not wedded to either, and I want to know what the example must have and what it must lack to do the work.

Third, the misconception map. I can already predict they'll equate incommensurability with incomparability, and then with outright relativism ("so truth just depends on your paradigm"). Beyond those two, I expect confusion between incommensurability and the much milder claim that scientists on different sides merely disagree about the evidence, and a tendency to hear "paradigm" as "school of thought" or even "fashion." Tell me the order in which these misconceptions will actually surface during the session, and exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off — some are best defused before the main example, others only after it.

Two further requests. At the end, tell me what I should openly refuse to explain at this level (I assume the later Kuhn's lexical taxonomy belongs in that category — say if you disagree). And since a teaching plan is a hypothesis, tell me where it is most likely to fail: which step is fragile, what classroom feedback would signal that I've gotten the ordering wrong, and what I'd have to see or hear from the group to change the plan. Please write in British English.
