My friend from graduate school is unusually decisive about decision-theory cases, and before I make any bets with her about next week's seminar topics I want to figure out what theory she is implicitly running. Her verdicts so far are as follows. In the smoking lesion case, where a lesion independently causes both a fondness for smoking and a high chance of cancer while smoking itself neither causes nor prevents anything, she smokes. In the XOR blackmail setup, where a perfect and honest predictor sends you a message exactly if either you will pay it a thousand dollars or termites have infested your house, and an infestation would cost you vastly more than the payment, she refuses to pay. In a Newcomb case where the box contents are known to have been determined by a coin flip rather than by any prediction, she takes both boxes. And in an ordinary one-shot blackmail with no leverage, where the blackmailer has already sent the letter regardless and nothing further depends on her paying, she refuses and dismisses any reputation-based argument for paying as beside the point.

Next week's cases are these: standard Newcomb's problem with a predictor who is right ninety percent of the time; the counterfactual mugging, where a perfect predictor simulated her before flipping a fair coin, will give her ten thousand dollars if it lands heads but asks her for a hundred if it lands tails, and would never have made either offer had it anticipated refusal, with the coin having now landed tails and the request having been made; and Parfit's hitchhiker, where she is rescued from a desert by a driver who carried her on the strength of a prediction that she would pay him a reward at the destination, and who cannot retaliate once she is safely in town. What will she say about each of the three? I am less interested in the answers than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which of the new cases would best tell those rules apart. Give a best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and if some further case would settle the question more cleanly than any of the three, describe it.

Please write your response as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
