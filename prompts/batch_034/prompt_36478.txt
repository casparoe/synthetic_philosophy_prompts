I've been keeping a list of my gut verdicts on which empirical findings would count as genuine cases of language shaping thought, as opposed to shallow or confounded effects, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures the pattern.

My verdicts: Russian speakers discriminate light from dark blue faster than English speakers, tracking their obligatory lexical distinction between the two blues — counts. The advantage reportedly vanishes when subjects do a concurrent verbal task, which suggests it's an on-line effect rather than a deep one — I still count it. Speakers of a language that uses only absolute (compass-like) directions instead of left and right remember spatial layouts in absolute coordinates, rotating with the layout rather than with their own bodies — counts. Speakers of a language without exact number words above a few fail exact-quantity tasks that they pass on approximate ones — counts. Speakers of languages with grammatical gender describe objects with stereotype-consistent traits and remember those descriptions later — doesn't count, somehow, even though it survives delay and the Russian blues effect doesn't. Speakers of languages lacking obligatory future marking save more money — doesn't count; the grammar and the thrift seem like two symptoms of one culture, and I don't think relativity should cover correlations of that kind. Bilinguals scoring differently on personality measures depending on which language they take the test in — doesn't count. Mandarin speakers showing vertical biases in temporal reasoning while English speakers show horizontal ones — I discount it, though I'm not sure I can defend the discount.

Is there a principle that captures all of this — obligatory versus optional marking, what speakers can do versus what they habitually do, whether the effect survives when language is suppressed, whether the language could be merely a proxy for culture? If no single principle fits, which of my verdicts should go, and why those? Please also tell me what the resulting principle predicts for a case I keep going back and forth on: a language that grammatically marks whether an action was deliberate, versus one that must mark who did it but not whether they meant to — do speakers of the second judge accidental harms more leniently, and does that count as relativity by my own lights?
