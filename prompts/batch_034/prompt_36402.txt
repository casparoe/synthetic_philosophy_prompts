Something about parental leave policy has been bugging me and I want to know what it secretly assumes. In a lot of countries the setup works like this: there's a chunk of leave reserved for each parent (the Nordic-style "daddy quota," use-it-or-lose-it, so a father can't transfer his share to the mother), the pay is a replacement rate tied to your prior earnings, and the leave has to be taken by a parent, in person, actually being with the kid. Meanwhile the same governments heavily subsidize daycare for those same kids a year or two later, and nobody thinks that's weird. So on the one hand the state pays parents not to work so they can personally provide care, and on the other it pays institutions to provide care. And you can't take the leave money and hire a nanny or pass it to a grandparent — in most systems the grandparent who does all the caregiving gets nothing, even if the parent who's nominally "on leave" is back at work.

So what's the implicit theory here? Is it that care in roughly the first year is special — non-delegable, maybe for attachment-type reasons — and after that it's just a service you can buy like any other? Or is the leave not really about the child at all, but about some vision of the family: gender equality, the parent-infant bond, or just not wanting to subsidize people outsourcing their kids? And the earnings-replacement part looks like it cuts both ways: paying it as a percentage of your salary presupposes leave is a job interruption, but paying it to the parent rather than to whoever does the caregiving presupposes it's not a job at all but something owed to the parent-child pair. Can those two cohere?

Reconstruct the theory (or theories) that the practice actually needs, tell me which parts of the design follow from it and which are just historical residue, and then say what the policy would look like if we were consistent — e.g. if we took the "care is delegable" view seriously, or the "it's owed to the parent-child pair" view seriously, what would have to change. Also curious where the practice is underdetermined, i.e. where several quite different theories of what leave is *for* would license pretty much the same policy, because I suspect that's true of a lot of it.
