So I teach adult swimming lessons at a community pool, and this thing has been bugging me for years and I want to know if it has a name. No academic background, just something I keep running into.

Here's the pattern. When I teach someone to float, I tell them "stop holding your head up, let it sink back." And it works — people float. But a while back somebody filmed me demonstrating, and watching it back I could see my neck isn't doing what I say at all. What I'm actually doing is something with my breathing and my lower back that I honestly can't put into words. So my instruction, as a description of what I myself do, is basically false. But it still teaches. And it's not just me. My buddy who's a great guitar teacher tells students to press harder with the fretting hand as they go up the neck, which is not whats happening physically when he plays, and his students improve anyway. A yoga teacher I know cues stuff that if you filmed her she definately isn't doing either.

So: a skilled person's report of their own skill can be confidently wrong, and the wrong report still transmits the skill. That's the part I want a concept for. It's not that we lack words — we have words, confident ones, wrong ones. And weirdly the wrong ones work better than nothing. Sometimes I suspect they work better than a technically correct description would.

I vaguely remember there's some old philosophy distinction between "knowing how" and "knowing that," but that's not quite what I'm after — my teachers and I have the how, we just have a false "that" about it, and the false "that" still teaches. Does the exact phenomenon have an established name? If it does, how does it relate to that how/that distinction, and if it doesn't, can you coin a term and define it properly, because I need to know where the edges are. Like: does it still count when the instruction is a simplification rather than an outright falsehood, or a deliberate metaphor ("hold the club like it's a small bird")? Does it only happen with body skills, or does a therapist or a negotiator also misreport what they do in ways that still train apprentices? And what's actually going on — is the false cue just a placebo, or is it somehow tracking the right thing while describing the wrong thing? A bare label won't help me, I want to know what the phenomenon is and isn't.
