For most of my working life I was a physical chemist, occupied with non-equilibrium thermodynamics — gradients, dissipative structures, the statistics of fluctuations — and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of history: the doctrine of providence, Kant's late-eighteenth-century essay on universal history, Hegel's lectures with their cunning of reason, the nineteenth-century faith in progress, and the contemporary refrain, familiar from speeches and sermons, that the arc of the moral universe bends toward justice. I confess I cannot always tell whether the disputants are disagreeing about the world or merely about words, and I have found that the surest way to discipline my own thinking is to translate unfamiliar claims into the vocabulary I trust. So here is my request. Restate the main positions in this debate — providence, secular progress, cyclical recurrence, and the view that history is radically contingent, going nowhere in particular — as faithfully as you can in terms a thermodynamicist would accept: entropy production, attractors and basins, symmetry breaking, path dependence, metastability, critical fluctuations, and the rest. Then give me the residue report I have come to value: which claims carry across intact, which come through only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. Two puzzles in particular I would like addressed. First, in my field the arrow of time turned out to be a statement about boundary conditions, not about the equations of motion; I want to know, when a philosopher says history has a direction, whether that is a dynamical claim, a boundary-condition claim, or something else I have no category for. Second, an attractor in my vocabulary says only where a system tends to go, never where it ought to go, yet the rhetoric of the bending arc seems to smuggle a norm into the attractor — or perhaps it does not, and the norm was an illusion on the part of the listeners. Tell me honestly whether the residue reflects a deficiency in my vocabulary or a lack of clarity in the original claims, and please write your reply as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
