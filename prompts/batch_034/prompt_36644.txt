My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about where the line between science and non-science actually falls, and midway through I realized that although I know all of her past verdicts, I cannot predict her on the new cases. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it: she counts Newtonian gravitation after the Mercury perihelion anomaly as science, even though the anomaly was tolerated for decades before the theory fell; she counts pre-1960s plate tectonics — no mechanism, largely rejected by the establishment of its day — as science; she counts modern macroeconomics, despite its forecasting record, as science; and she refuses to count homeopathy, Freudian psychoanalysis, and intelligent design, in each case on the ground that the theory accommodates any possible outcome and its community never concedes anything. Where we got stuck last night was on three new cases: string theory, which generates no risky empirical predictions but lives inside physics departments under constant, harsh internal criticism; a university parapsychology unit that runs preregistered, methodologically impeccable Ganzfeld replications and faithfully publishes its null results; and a self-taught maverick with no credentials and no research community who issues dated, risky earthquake forecasts and, on the evidence so far, has been right more often than chance would allow. Predict her verdict on each of the three, say which rule she seems to be running — exposure to refutation and refusal to immunize, novel prediction and progressive problem shifts, institutional embeddedness in an organized skeptical community, or something else entirely — note where the rules that fit her record equally well come apart on these cases, and tell me which further case would best discriminate between them. I am less interested in who is right about the demarcation problem than in what her track record commits her to.
