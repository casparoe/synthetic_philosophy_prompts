I'm a PhD student working on decision theory under prospects whose expected value is undefined, and before I evaluate any of the proposed repairs — truncated expectations, dominance-only rules, ranking by other statistical functionals — I want the specification they are being measured against. So write the desiderata: the requirements any adequate decision rule for undefined-expectation games must meet, with the motivating case behind each.

The core cases to keep in view: the St. Petersburg game (payoff 2^n with probability 2^-n when the first head lands on toss n, so the expectation diverges to infinity) and the Pasadena game (same setup, but the payoff for first head on toss n is (-1)^n times 2^n/n, so the expectation series becomes the alternating harmonic series — conditionally convergent, summing to ln 2 in the natural ordering but rearrangeable to any real value, or divergence). The Pasadena case matters precisely because the game has no well-defined expected value even though each payoff, and the expectation truncated at any finite point, is perfectly well-defined.

Among the requirements I'd want considered: that the rule coincide with expected-value maximization whenever expectations exist and are finite; that it deliver some verdict on whether to play the Pasadena game, and at what entry fee; that it respect statewise dominance when one prospect beats another in every state; that it be invariant under mere relabelings or repartitionings of the sample space — a condition conditional convergence puts direct pressure on, since reordering the terms changes the sum; that it handle choices among several undefined-value prospects, not just a single accept/reject verdict; and that it treat temporal or sequential versions of the games without exploiting the order in which terms happen to arrive. For each requirement, give the case behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps rather than genuine adequacy conditions.

The part I most want to see: flag any pair of desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — I have a suspicion that agreement with finite-case expected value and partition-invariance cannot coexist once conditionally convergent expectations are admitted, and I'd like to know whether that is right or whether it shows one of the two was never a genuine requirement. Finally, tell me which single requirement on your list does the most to eliminate the familiar candidate rules.
