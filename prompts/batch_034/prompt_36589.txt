I'm doing a philosophy of science course this term and we've just hit Popper's views on Darwinism, which turns out to be way messier than the lecture notes suggested. In his 1976 autobiography Popper says the theory of natural selection by itself is close to tautological — it seems to boil down to saying the fittest survive, where the fittest are just defined as whatever survives — and he describes Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory. Then in a 1978 Darwin Lecture at Cambridge, published in Dialectica, he publicly retracted and said the theory is in fact testable. Ever since, two camps have been arguing past each other. Camp one says the 1976 Popper was right and the retraction was a capitulation: on Popper's own demarcation criterion the core theory fails, and the retraction never actually fixed the circularity, since fitness is still defined via differential reproductive success. Camp two says Popper never meant metaphysical as an insult — metaphysical research programmes were, in his mature view, indispensable to science — and that the theory does make risky predictions (about the fossil record, selective breeding, adaptation running up against constraints) that could have failed and sometimes did.

Write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. If any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. Please format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and answer in British English.
