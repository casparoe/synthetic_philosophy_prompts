so I'm on the steering committee of a tenants coalition in a mid-size california city and we're at a fork. two years of lobbying the city council on rent stabilization, and every version comes out of committee watered down to homeopathic levels. half our committee wants to go the initiative route — collect signatures, put it on the ballot ourselves, cut the council out entirely. the other half says that's how we got into this mess in the first place: prop 13, ballot-box budgeting, a whole shadow legislature run by whoever can afford signature gatherers, and once something's locked into the ballot the voters just have to live with it because amending an initiative is way harder than the council repealing one of its own ordinances.

I keep flip-flopping depending on who I talked to last, so what I want isn't advice on our campaign — I want the underlying question worked through. write an essay on whether contested policy questions like ours should be decided directly by voters or by the representatives we elected to decide them. take the strongest versions of both sides seriously: the arguments that direct votes are more legitimate because it's literally the people deciding, the arguments that representatives can deliberate, hear expert testimony, and amend mistakes in ways a raw yes/no vote can't, the competence arguments (if each voter is even a little better than a coin flip then the majority is nearly guaranteed to get it right — but does that survive the fact that almost nobody reads the ballot pamphlet and a lot of votes turn on how the title was worded), the capture argument (sure the council is in developers' pockets, but signature-gathering is expensive too), and the thing where the initiative process was supposed to be a check on unresponsive legislatures and now functions as a second legislature with none of the deliberation. you can bring in whatever comparative examples you want, switzerland, brexit, whatever actually illuminates, but our case should stay the throughline.

and actually take a side at the end. a conditional verdict is fine — some kinds of questions belong on the ballot and this one does/doesn't because of X — but don't hand me an "it depends on local conditions" and call it done. also, fwiw, renters are a slim majority of registered voters here, and my mom is on the other side of the issue and I still have to see her at thanksgiving. write it like you know that.
