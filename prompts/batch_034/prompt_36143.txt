I've been reading a popular defense of string theory that rests on a detective analogy, and I'd like the analogy audited. The author says the situation is like a locked-room mystery in which the butler turns out to be the only person who could possibly have committed the crime: the detective never witnessed the deed and has no fingerprints, but the systematic elimination of every other suspect is itself strong evidence — and by the same token, the fact that generations of brilliant physicists have failed to construct any consistent alternative unifying gravity and quantum mechanics should count as evidence for string theory, even without accelerator data. I understand the philosopher Richard Dawid has defended reasoning of this kind under the heading of the no-alternatives argument for non-empirical confirmation, so I'm not auditing a straw man.

Please give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the crime, the suspect pool, the detective's background knowledge of who could have been in the house, the elimination of suspects, and the verdict — and then say where the mapping breaks. My physicist friend insists the fatal disanalogy is that the detective at least knows a murder occurred and has some grasp of who was in the house, whereas we don't know that a workable theory of everything exists or is findable by humans, and we can't delimit the space of alternatives, since the history of physics keeps producing theories that were in nobody's suspect pool (general relativity being the standard example). I'd like you to sort the disanalogies into those that matter for the intended use — propping up confidence in a theory without empirical tests — and those that are harmless for that purpose, and then tell me whether the conclusion that the failure to find alternatives supplies real evidential support survives with the damaging disanalogies on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole way. If there's a better analogy for what string theorists are actually doing, suggest it.

Throughout, please keep separate what is settled among philosophers of science about non-empirical confirmation and what is genuinely contested between its defenders and their critics, and don't present one side's Bayesian reconstruction of the reasoning as the consensus view.
