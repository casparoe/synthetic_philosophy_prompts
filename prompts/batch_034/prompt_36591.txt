I'm a PhD student in environmental policy and I somehow got roped onto our university's Dining & Sustainability Committee (the story involves me volunteering at a bake sale table, long story). At the last meeting a student group presented a petition, maybe 1,500 signatures, asking us to make beef the default protein on the main dining halls' hot line instead of chicken — default meaning the unmarked option you get unless you opt out, since apparently defaults move actual behavior a lot. Their argument: a chicken-based diet kills something like two hundred animals a year while a beef-based one kills well under one (they had a spreadsheet, with per-calorie adjustments and everything), so switching the default would prevent an enormous number of animal deaths. Some committee members are genuinely sympathetic. Others think it's insane that the same committee that signed the carbon-neutrality pledge would push more beef, since beef is basically the worst food there is for emmissions and the petition says nothing about that. And the campus vegan caucus is furious that we're even debating it, on the grounds that anything short of a plant-based default is a betrayal — they also claim the spreadsheet is bogus because cattle eat a ton of feed crops, so every burger comes with the animals that died producing the feed. Then a grad student in ecology on the committee muttered something about pasture vs. cropland and how wild animals figure in all this, and nobody engaged with it because it was 9pm and everyone wanted to go home.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting. It needs a concrete recommendation the committee can actually vote on — adopt the switch as petitioned, reject it, or something in between (e.g., rotating default, or a plant-based default with meat opt-ins) — with a timeline attached. More importantly, it should engage the hardest philosophical issue instead of dodging it: is the morality of this measured in deaths, in suffering, or in something else? A broiler's life is short and reportedly miserable; a beef cow's is much longer and maybe better, maybe not — so does "fewer deaths" even track the thing we should care about? And it should say something honest about the feed-crop objection and the wild-animal stuff the petition just ignores, including the awkward possibility that we know so little about the moral status of the animals killed in crop cultivation (pests, whatever gets shredded in tilling) that the numbers can't settle the question either way. Please don't land on "more study needed" as your actual recommendation — make a call, be upfront about which side has the stronger argument even if it's the side that loses, and note what the recommendation costs the losers.
