Write a dialogue between a Bohmian and an Everettian physicist the night their lab's Bell test spits out a clean CHSH violation. The Bohmian is smug: the pilot wave acted nonlocally across the two stations, exactly as Bell showed anything with definite outcomes must. The Everettian is equally smug: the universal wavefunction evolved perfectly locally the whole time, no collapse, no spooky action -- the violation just comes from branching and self-locating uncertainty, and the Bohmian's nonlocality is an artifact of insisting on a single world. Make them actually engage with each others points instead of talking past them: what each takes Bell's theorem to have ruled out (locality? definite outcomes? the measurement-independence assumption?), whether many-worlds really gets to claim locality when the Born weights do all the work of explaining the correlations, whether the Bohmian's nonlocal influence counts as action at a distance if it provably can't be used to send a signal, and whether either of them is entitled to feel vindicated by data that both of their theories reproduce identically. Neither should be a strawman -- the Everettian should press the "empty waves" worry that Bohmian particles ride a wave that does all the causal work, and the Bohmian should press the fact that Everettians have to say both outcomes happened, which to them looks like denying the experiment ever had a result. End with them either figuring out what the disagreement is actually about (definite outcomes vs. locality, and which one Bell's theorem leaves you holding) or each deciding the other's position is not even wrong. Keep it to maybe 600-800 words.
