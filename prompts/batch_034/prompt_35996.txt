For some years now I've been trying to sort out my own view about when it's fair for society to even things out between people, and I've ended up with a list of positions that each seem right to me on their own but that I can't quite vouch for as a package. Here they are. First, nobody should end up worse off than anyone else because of the lottery at birth: if you're born with a disability, or into poverty, or with a body that needs constant medication, that's not your doing, and a fair society makes up for it. Second, the flip side: what flows from your own choices is on you, and I don't believe in rescuing someone who gambled away their savings up to the level of someone who was careful. Third, society owes people with disabilities and chronic illnesses the extra resources it takes for them to actually live — not merely survive — a decent life, even when that's very expensive. Fourth, but I don't think we owe anything to someone who just happens to need a lot of expensive things to enjoy themselves — the person whose happiness requires opera, skiing, and fine food is not owed a subsidy for their tastes. Fifth, when someone from a poor background has learned to want very little and says they're content, I don't think that settles anything — they're not equal to a well-off person just because they've stopped wanting what they've never had. Sixth, I do think that what ultimately matters is how life actually goes for people: a society that hands everyone a fair bundle of goods but leaves them all miserable has failed at the only thing that matters.

There's also a worry I can't shake, which I want you to take seriously rather than brush aside. Almost everything I'd call a choice seems to run back into luck if you push on it: whether someone is disciplined or impulsive, cheerful or depressive, seems mostly fixed by the temperament they were born with, before any choice was made. If the gambler gambled because of the brain he was born with, then refusing to rescue him because of his "choices" looks like a fiction, and my second position collapses into the first. Yet I find I can't actually bring myself to give it up.

Please go through all of this and tell me where it hangs together and where it doesn't. I want you to identify which of my positions are genuinely in tension with each other, which ones merely look like they conflict but can be reconciled with a bit of care, and which one single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most problems at the least cost. Rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than treating everything as equally fatal. Please end your reply with a line beginning "Verdict:" that names, in a short phrase, the single cheapest revision to my set of views.
