So here's my situation: I'm an odds compiler for a small online bookmaker, mostly football. On a normal day I'll price up maybe a dozen markets before kick-off and then babysit the in-play board when the matches go live, which means repricing constantly while bets are still landing. I've been trying to read a bit of the philosophy of probability on my commute, and I gather there's a whole literature about "Dutch books" — betting schemes that guarantee whoever takes the bets loses money no matter how the event turns out — and about what a set of odds has to satisfy to be "coherent". There's also apparently a named update rule for how you should move your numbers when new information arrives mid-event (conditionalisation? something like that). Most of the formalism goes over my head, but the practical point stuck: if my prices aren't consistent with each other, some clever punter can stitch together a few bets with us and lock in a profit at our expense, without needing to know anything about football at all.

What I want is a procedure the desk can actually run. Constraints: a full check on a market has to take about ten minutes, because we reprice too often for anything slower, and it has to be followable by someone whose maths stops at percentages — one of our lads is brilliant on football but panics the moment fractions appear. The procedure should list the checks in order, say what counts as a pass, and be explicit about what to do when a check fails: can we patch a single price, or does the whole market need suspending while we sort it out? It also needs to cover the awkward cases: bets on combinations of outcomes (someone backs "draw or away win" rather than a single result), accumulators that pull together different markets on the same match, and the in-play situation where a goal goes in and every line has to move at once while the bets keep flooding in. And please be honest about which bits of the procedure are just my judgement dressed up as maths — deciding what the actual probabilities are is surely still down to opinion, and I'd rather the procedure admit that than pretend otherwise. Same for the margin: we build in an overround of around 6%, and I'd like to know whether the checks apply to the raw prices, the margin-inflated ones, or both.

Then run the procedure on three cases so I can see it work. First, a pre-match situation where we're quoting match odds and a first-goalscorer market on the same game, and I have a nagging feeling the two boards don't agree with each other. Second, in-play: a red card in the 60th minute with the match-odds board open and stakes arriving every second — what exactly do we do, in what order, and how long can things stay live? And third, please include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I'm not going to like — I suspect there's a scenario where the safe move is to suspend a popular market right at the moment it's taking the most money, which will go down terribly with my manager, so I'd like to see that case worked through honestly rather than softened. Oh, and one admin thing: the whole team is UK-based, so please write the reply in British English.
