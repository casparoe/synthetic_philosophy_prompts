so I got into an argument at a rationality meetup last weekend about Pascal's mugging — the thought experiment where a shifty guy stops you and says: hand over $5, or I'll use my powers from another dimension to torture 3^^^3 people (that's the tower-of-arrows number, unfathomably huge). or sometimes the version where he instead offers you an astronomically large reward if you pay. my position going in was basically "shut up and multiply, that's the whole point of expected utility," but a couple of people there had their own fixes and now I genuinely can't tell which ones solve the problem and which ones just flinch away from big numbers.

here's the list of candidate responses we ended up with after much arguing:

1. bite the bullet — if you take EU seriously you pay up, and if the numbers make you queasy, that's a fact about your intuitions, not about the math.
2. bounded utility — cap the utility function somewhere (the way Savage's framework arguably ends up requiring anyway), so that nothing can dominate everything else regardless of how big it claims to be.
3. round tiny probabilities to zero — treat anything below some epistemic resolution as effectively impossible, sort of like the de minimis doctrine in law.
4. the social-evidence move — the claim itself is evidence against its own truth: a being that could actually deliver 3^^^3 utils wouldn't be panhandling for five bucks, so the posterior never climbs anywhere near where the unbounded math needs it to.
5. the counter-mugging move — for every mugger offering 3^^^3 utils if you pay, posit a second one offering to cancel that (or worse) if you do pay; the astronomical terms cancel out and you're left with just the $5 and a street full of people making increasingly large claims at each other, which suggests the framework for comparing such claims is broken rather than something you can resolve by calculation.

please sort these five into tiers by how well each one actually dissolves the problem rather than hiding it, with a sentence of reasoning for why it lands where it does. also, one guy kept insisting the mugging is just the St. Petersburg lottery wearing a mask, and I don't think that's right but couldn't articulate why on the spot — if you can say in a couple sentences what the real difference is, I'd owe you one. if you think none of the five actally works, say that too, and tell me what you'd put in the top tier instead.
