For my aesthetics seminar I want a decision tree that takes a student from the starting commitment "I felt genuine sorrow at the ending of King Lear, and I am glad I watched it" to a named position on the paradox of tragedy. The tree should be built from the logical space rather than from a list of isms, so start from the top-level question of whether the sorrow itself is part of what is enjoyed, and let the branch labels be diagnostic claims a student could agree or disagree with without knowing which position they lead to. At a minimum the tree should route through these questions: whether the pleasure would survive a flat retelling that keeps all the events but loses the expressive power; whether the same enjoyment would be available at a real funeral one is powerless to affect, holding vividness constant; whether one can want the sorrow to continue and enjoy it at the very same time; and whether what one relishes is one's own responsiveness rather than anything in the work. The leaves should include catharsis in its loose modern sense, Hume's conversion view, psychical distance, the coexistence or rich-experience view, control theories, and meta-response theories; any unoccupied combination should be included as an unnamed leaf with one sentence on whether nobody holds it because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and indicate the node at which a student who insists that the sorrow is enjoyed because it is sorrow — qua sorrow, not as the carrier of anything else — would leave the tree without a home.
