So this happened at my giving circle a couple weeks ago and its been bugging me ever since. It's just me and two friends, Mara and Theo, from the local ACX meetup -- we pool around $8k a year and pick one small, under-the-radar org to fund. Our rule has always been that we only fund something if it clears two bars: it's neglected (nobody bigger is already covering the gap) and tractable (there's an actual theory of change, not just vibes). This round we were deciding on a scrappy little biosecurity policy shop. I thought it was both neglected and tractable, so I voted fund. Mara thought it was neglected but not tractable, so she voted against. Theo thought it was tractable but not neglected (he'd heard Open Phil was quietly already sniffing around something adjacent), so he voted against too.

Here's the part that's been rattling around in my skull since. Two of the three of us voted "don't fund" on the bottom line, so a straight majority vote says no. But when I tallied us up criterion by criterion instead, a majority (me + Mara) said yes on neglected, and a seperate majority (me + Theo) said yes on tractable -- and neglected-and-tractable is literally our funding rule! So depending on whether we vote on each criterion and then apply the rule, or just vote on the rule's output directly, we get opposite answers, from the exact same three people holding the exact same underlying beliefs. Nobody changed their mind about anything in between the two tallies.

It feels like it's got to be some cousin of Arrow's theorem or a Condorcet cycle, but nobody's preferences are even cyclic here, so I don't think that's quite it, and googling a few different phrasings got me nowhere, which almost never happens to me. Does this phenomenon have a name? More importantly, is there a principled reason one of the two tallying methods deserves to win, or have we just discovered that "majority rules" doesn't actually pin down a well-defined procedure once you're voting on more than one connected question at a time? Would love an actual steelman for either side before we just default to whichever one Theo prefers, since he's buying drinks next time.
