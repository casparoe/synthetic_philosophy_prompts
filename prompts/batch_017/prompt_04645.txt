My younger brother was recently sentenced to court-ordered treatment rather than prison after his defense attorney argued, in mitigation, that years of opioid addiction had so compromised his self-control that he could not reasonably have done otherwise than relapse. Trying to work out for myself how much to blame him, I've been reading the "new dispositionalist" compatibilists (Vihvelin, Fara), who analyze "could have done otherwise" as a disposition, in a way indebted to Lewis's "reformed conditional analysis," and who must defend that analysis against the same finks and masks that trouble the conditional analysis of ordinary dispositions, from Martin's electro-fink to Bird's antidote cases. What I can't settle is whether this machinery gives any principled way to treat my brother's compromised self-control as a "masked" ability, still fully intact but merely blocked from manifesting, rather than as an ability his addiction actually destroyed. Please describe the most important implications of the finks-and-masks debate for that question, and for how much responsibility his relapse should really carry.
