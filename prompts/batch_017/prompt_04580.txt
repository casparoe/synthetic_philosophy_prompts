Write a dialogue between two philosophers of cognitive science who accept the same mathematics of active inference under the free energy principle but draw opposite conclusions about what it shows regarding the mind's relationship to the world.

One defends an internalist reading: because a system's internal states are screened off from external states and can be influenced by them only indirectly, via the sensory prediction errors that arise at the Markov blanket, the blanket marks a genuine inferential seclusion — the mind is a self-contained model-fitting process that infers, but never directly touches, the world beyond it. The other defends an ecological-enactive reading: because active inference is realized by a continuous loop of acting and sensing that keeps an organism attuned to the affordances of its surroundings, talk of "seclusion" mistakes a modeling convenience for a metaphysical wall, and treating the blanket as a principled, agent-individuating boundary illegitimately turns a statistical partition into an ontological one.

Have them press each other on whether the free energy formalism itself adjudicates between these two readings, or whether it is equally compatible with both.
