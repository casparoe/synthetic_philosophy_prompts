So I was at a dinner party a couple weeks ago and got pulled into watching two people go at it over something that's been stuck in my head since (I cover science and ideas for a living, so obviously I couldn't just let it drop). Setup: our mutual friend Mara is trying to decide whether to unlock the health section on one of those consumer DNA kits -- the part that gives you odds for stuff like late-onset Alzheimer's, which she can't do anything to prevent either way. She wasn't even there, it was just Nate and Cass hashing out what she should do.

1. Nate said she should obviously look. He'd read that there's an actual theorem (some statistician literally named Good proved it, decades ago) showing that free information can never leave a rational person worse off in expectation, because you always keep the option to just ignore it and act exactly like you didn't see it. So peeking can only help her, or at worst do nothing.

2. Cass said that's a cute theorem but it's not really about Mara's situation. Even if the result never changes a single choice she makes, she said, just knowing she's probably losing her mind at 60 could sit there and wreck her for the next thirty years. That dread is a real cost, and it doesn't show up anywhere in Nate's "just ignore it" story.

3. Nate said fine, build the dread into her utility function like everything else -- the theorem doesn't care what's in there, it just says having the option to look beats not having it once you're accounting for all the consequences correctly. If looking would truly leave her worse off, the math would just tell her not to look. So there's still no real counterexample, just an incomplete one on Cass's part.

4. Cass wasn't having it. She said the "just ignore it" option is doing all the work in Nates theorem, and it quietly assumes the information stays put once Mara has it. It doesn't: it can end up in medical records, get requested by a future life insurer, get half-guessed-at by relatives who didn't want to know their own odds either. Once other people start reacting to what Mara knows (or is assumed to know), Cass said, the free lunch disappears.

Honestly couldn't tell you who came out ahead.
