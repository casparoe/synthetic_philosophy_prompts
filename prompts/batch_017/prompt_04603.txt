So I went down a rabbit hole a few weeks back after rewatching the Imitation Game movie and finally sat down and read Turing's actual 1950 paper instead of just knowing "the turing test" secondhand off wikipedia. Most of it was what I expected, but right near the end, before he gets into the "learning machines" bit, theres this objection he literally calls "The Argument from Extrasensory Perception," and he is NOT joking around about it. He says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming" and that the objection is honestly a pretty strong one. Then he works out this whole scenario where the interrogator asks a psychic guy and a computer to guess the suit of a card 400 times each, the psychic guy gets like 130 right through telepathy or clairvoyance, the machine only gets around 104 right by chance, so the interrogator can tell them apart based on nothing to do with actual thinking. His fix is to literally stick both contestants in a "telepathy-proof room." He even worries that an interrogator with psychokinesis could mess with the machine's own random number generator lol. I looked into it a bit more and this was apparently right around when J.B. Rhine's card-guessing ESP experiments at Duke were a huge deal, so I'm guessing thats probably what he had in mind.

I brought this up with my roommate and she thinks its kind of unhinged that one of the most famous, "founding" papers of AI has this in it basically unqualified, and that its a real soft spot people just quietly skip past because the rest of the paper is so good. I pushed back a bit, since patching a loophole (even a goofy-sounding one) before it can be used against your test seems like careful, rigorous thinking to me, not credulous thinking. But then again, if hes willing to bend the laws of physics to accomodate ESP, maybe that says something bad about how solid his whole approach of turning "thinking" into a pass/fail conversation test really is underneath. I honestly can't decide who's right here, so I want you to actually settle it for me instead of just listing pros and cons.
