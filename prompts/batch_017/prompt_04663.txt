I'm a retired geologist who's spent the last year or so falling down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole, and the thing I can't stop chewing on is the Lucas-Penrose argument -- Lucas kicked it off back in the early 60s, Penrose picked it up decades later in Shadows of the Mind -- the claim that because a mind can always "see" the truth of a Gödel sentence that its own formal system could never prove, we can't just be algorithms running on meat. I know most logicians think this trades on a fudge about whether we can ever know our own reasoning is sound (Godel's second theorem says not even the machine can prove that about itself), but I still find it wierdly gripping. Write me a short story that actually dramatizes this properly -- not another "the robot wakes up and feels sad" story, but one where the plot genuinely hinges on the specific place the argument wobbles, like the soundness assumption, or the fact that "human mathematics" might not even be one fixed system to begin with. Doesn't need a tidy ending, I just want to actually feel the argument for once instead of just reading about it.
