I spent about forty years doing statistical and low-temperature physics before I retired, and for the last year or so I've been down a real philosophy-of-time rabbit hole -- currently working through Huw Price's old book on time's arrow and enjoying it more than I expected to. My grandkids sat me down in front of Tenet over the holidays and I spent the whole movie grumbling, because the "inversion" business -- people dodging bullets that fly backwards while everyone around them carries on forwards as normal -- is really just entropy-reversal played as a magic trick. It doesn't engage at all with what would actually be strange about it.

What's been stuck in my head instead is something I ran into in Price's book. Back in 1895, in the middle of the row over whether his H-theorem could really be squared with time-reversible mechanics, Boltzmann floated an idea he credited to an old assistant of his, a Dr Schuetz: that our whole low-entropy corner of the universe might just be a rare statistical fluctuation in an otherwise eternal, high-entropy cosmos, and that on the far side of such a fluctuation, temporally speaking, there could be beings for whom entropy runs the opposite way from us. His point was that there'd be no fact of the matter about who's "really" going forward in time, any more than there's a fact about which way is "up" once you accept the earth is round -- its just a matter of which slope of the fluctuation you happen to be standing on. Funnily enough, Hawking made almost the mirror-image error decades later: in A Brief History of Time he originally reckoned a contracting universe would run entropy, and memory, backwards, until two physicists working with him, Don Page and Raymond Laflamme, showed him that was wrong, which to his credit he owns up to in later editions.

So here's my request: write me a short story, a few pages long, that actually takes this seriously instead of doing the Tenet thing. Put two people, or two civilizations, on opposite sides of some entropy divide like that -- Boltzmann's fluctuation, a Big Crunch/Big Bang pairing, or something you invent yourself, I don't mind which -- and have them make some kind of contact across it: a signal, an object, or something stranger. What I really want is for the story to sit inside the disorienting part rather than cheat around it: neither side should feel like its living backwards from the inside. Each should remember its own past and not its future, and experience its own arrow as the perfectly ordinary forward direction, so that "reversal" only exists as a description from outside, comparing the two gradients. If you can hold onto that without it collapsing into a gimmick, and without tying everything up too neatly at the end, I'd love to read what you come up with.
