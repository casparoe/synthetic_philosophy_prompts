My friend Mia went full no-contact with her family about two years ago — no calls, no holidays, nothing — after basically a lifetime of what she describes as a pretty toxic dynamic with her mom and older brother. She's said more than once that cutting them off is the first time in her life she's actually felt like herself. Her mom, who im still kind of in touch with because our families are close (it's a whole awkward thing), keeps saying stuff to me like "she's not the same person anymore" and "you can't really be whole, totally cut off like that — a person doesn't just exist on their own, in a vacuum." It rattles me a little every time she says it, even though I'm firmly on Mia's side about the actual decision to leave.

I'm not asking whether Mia was right to go no-contact — I think she obviously was, given everything she's dealt with. What's actually been nagging at me is the bigger claim buried in what her mom keeps saying: that who you even ARE as a person is something you only really get through relationships and community, so someone who's cut themselves off from all that is, in some sense, less of a full person then someone who's embedded in a family or community. Mia would say that's just guilt-trippy nonsense dressed up as some deep philosophical insight, and that she's exactly as much a person alone as she ever was surrounded by them. I don't really have the philosophical vocabulary to sort this out myself, but it's stuck with me since she said it: is there actually something to the idea that who you are is fundamentally relational like that, or is her mom just wrong on the merits, independent of how manipulative she's being about it?
