Ok so I'm a cog sci PhD student (my actual work is computational semantics-y NLP stuff, but I sit in on a philosophy of mind/language reading group for fun) and this group has now spent two whole sessions arguing about something and I need a tie-breaker who'll actually commit to an answer instead of hedging like everyone in my cohort does.

We just reread Harnad's original 1990 symbol grounding paper, including the Chinese/Chinese dictionary-go-round bit -- his point that if a symbol's meaning is cashed out purely in terms of other symbols (like looking up a word in a monolingual dictionary of a language you dont already speak), you just go round and round and never touch down on actual meaning. His proposed fix was to ground elementary symbols bottom-up in nonsymbolic "iconic" and "categorical" representations -- basically learned sensorimotor categories that come from actually interacting with objects and events -- with higher-order symbolic stuff built compositionally on top of that already-grounded base. Half my reading group thinks this is basically fatal for text-only LLMs, since they never get the iconic/categorical step at all -- it's dictionary-go-round all the way down, which is more or less Bender & Koller's octopus-on-the-cable argument too (the octopus can fake a conversation but can't help with the coconut catapult or the bear, because it's never touched a coconut or a bear). The other half keeps bringing up that Piantadosi & Hill "meaning without reference" paper, which argues meaning-as-conceptual-role (constituted by the relations a model's internal representations bear to each other, not by hookups to external referents) is a perfectly respectable theory of meaning with real pedigree in philosophy of language, so maybe LLMs get something genuinely meaning-like even with zero sensorimotor grounding.

Can you actually write this up as an essay -- not a "there are many perspectives" cop out -- that lays out the strongest version of both sides and then commits to a real verdict: are current text-only LLMs genuinely stuck on Harnad's merry-go-round, or is there an actual escape route (conceptual-role semantics, some "indirect/vicarious grounding via the grounded humans who wrote the training data" move, or something else you think works better)? If the verdict ends up being "it depends," it needs to depend on something specific and falsifiable, not just vibes. Multimodal/embodied models can get a footnote at most -- I mainly care about the pure text-only case since that's the sharpest test of Harnad's actual argument.
