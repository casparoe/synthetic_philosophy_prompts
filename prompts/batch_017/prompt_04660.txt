I'm working on a historical novel set in Prague around 1610, and one of my POV characters is a heavily fictionalized Johannes Kepler — imperial mathematician, perpetually unpaid by the court, grinding out horoscopes and weather almanacs to keep the household fed while he does the astronomy he actually cares about on the side. Researching him I fell down a rabbit hole on a pamphlet of his called Tertius Interveniens, which apparently came out of a real spat with a physician named Feselius who'd written a whole treatise trashing astrology as worthless garbage, and Kepler basically wrote himself in as the "third man," scolding everybody not to throw the baby out with the bathwater. Barely anything survives about Feselius as a person, so I'm inventing him from scratch as a guest at a dinner hosted by one of Kepler's patrons, but I don't want to invent HIS argument — it should land like something a genuinely sharp 1610 skeptic would say, not a cardboard cutout.

Here's my actual problem though. I know Kepler didn't buy the astrology package wholesale — he thought the houses, the zodiac signs, calling Mars "malefic," all of it, was basically made up bookkeeping with no basis in nature. But he clearly didn't think it was ALL nonsense either, since he kept insisting one piece of it — something to do with the angles between planets, if I remember right — was real and worth defending on its own terms. Honestly, I don't fully understand the reasoning he used to justify keeping that piece while chucking the rest, and that's exactly what I need my dialogue to get right, because if my Kepler can't explain it convincingly he just comes off as a hypocrite who kept whatever happened to be profitable.

Could you write me that dinner-table argument, Kepler vs. Feselius, maybe 600-900 words? I need Feselius to actually press him on the obvious objection — why is your surviving piece of astrology any less arbitrary than the stuff you just threw out — instead of just huffing about superstition, and I need Kepler to have a real answer instead of a mystical hand-wave. By the end neither one should have just steamrolled the other. No strawmen on either side please, these are both supposed to be the smartest guy in the room, they just geniunely disagree. Appreciate it.
