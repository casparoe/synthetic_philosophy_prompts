So a couple months ago I started volunteering on weekends at a small farm sanctuary, and somewhere between that and finally watching Dominion (the documentary, not the TV show) I've gone fully vegetarian and started actually caring about getting the underlying ethics right instead of just going on gut feeling. My housemate, who's finishing up a philosophy MA, treats basically every dinner as a chance to workshop arguements on me, and last week he pulled out what he clearly thought was a knockout move: Robert Nozick's "utility monster." He described it as this hypothetical being that gets so much more pleasure out of every unit of resource that a consistent utilitarian is forced to hand everything over to it and let everyone else suffer - and then said that's exactly why Nozick, even though he took animals somewhat seriously, ultimately couldn't let animal suffering carry any real moral weight in his framework, since the second you let aggregatable suffering drive how we treat any being, human or animal, you're committed to feeding the monster. So according to him, citing Nozick to justify caring about animal suffering is basically quoting the guy who already proved you can't consistently do that.

I don't know Nozick well enough to tell if that's actually right or if my housemate just sounds confident. Since I've actually started changing how I eat over this stuff, I'd rather not build my reasoning on a bad reading of the guy - did Nozick really use the utility monster to undercut weighing animal suffering, and does that match up with what he actually thought about animals?
