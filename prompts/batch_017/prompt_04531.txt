So I've been reading around in AI safety stuff on the side — started with some rationalist blog posts and ended up on the actual MIRI "Embedded Agency" sequence (the one by Demski and Garrabrant from 2018) — and I mentioned it to a friend who's doing a philosophy of mind PhD. She immediately said something like "oh, that's just embedded/embodied cognition, 4E cog sci basically covered this back in the 90s." The more I sit with it the more I think she might be right. Both seem to be making the same headline point: you can't treat an agent as some disembodied thing that recieves inputs and produces outputs while standing safely outside the world it's reasoning about — the agent is actually embedded in, and made of the same stuff as, the very environment it's trying to model and act on, so the tidy picture where the reasoner is cleanly seperate from what it reasons about just breaks down. That's the big claim of the embedded agency sequence, and it also seems to be the big claim behind embedded/extended mind stuff like Andy Clark's work.

Am I missing something here, or did the AI safety crowd basically just reinvent 4E cognitive science and slap some decision theory on top? Genuinely curious whether there's a real technical distinction my friend was gesturing at, or whether this is one of those cases where two fields independently arrived at the same insight and just dressed it up in different vocabulary.
