hi! taking a phil of science course this semester and we just did a lecture on Neurath's isotype / vienna method stuff (the "international picture language" project, teaching statistics to people through pictures instead of words). something's been bugging me since: nowadays you see infographics everywhere (twitter, news sites, those covid dashboards during the pandemic) that show a bigger quantity by just drawing one icon bigger, instead of repeating more icons at the same size - which as i understand it is literally the thing Neurath said not to do, since for him resizing a symbol ruins the whole point of it being countable/comparable. but then you've also got stuff like airport pictograms, ikea manuals, maybe even emoji, that are kind of accidentally close to the wordless universal picture language he wanted.

if Neurath somehow saw all this today, what do you think he'd say? would he count the good pictogram stuff as his project basically succeeding, or would things like the resized-icon infographics (and how attention/virality-driven modern visual media is in general) make him think it all got corrupted along the way? im curious what someone that strict about "honest" visual communication would make of it.
