I've followed the Overcoming Bias/LessWrong community for years, and I think I've finally worked out why the standard squeamishness about Yudkowsky's TORTURE verdict in the dust specks dilemma is simply a mistake. LessWrong's own wiki files "Torture vs. Dust Specks" under the "Scope Insensitivity" tag, right alongside the willingness-to-pay studies showing that people won't pay much more to save 200,000 birds than 2,000. If failing to scale your concern with the number of birds is a well-documented cognitive bias, then failing to scale your judgment about 3^^^3 people each getting a dust speck in the eye has to be the very same bias, just relabeled as a moral intuition. So once you've internalized "shut up and multiply," isn't preferring DUST SPECKS over TORTURE just as irrational as undervaluing the birds, and shouldn't I be training myself to override that intuition the same way I'd correct any other case of scope insensitivity?
