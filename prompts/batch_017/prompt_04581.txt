In my decision theory seminar, we recently covered acausal trade: the idea that two agents can each benefit by cooperating even though neither can causally interact with the other in any way. As I understand it, the whole point of calling this "acausal" is that no causal channel between the parties is needed for the cooperation to succeed. If that's the key feature, it seems I should be able to simply resolve, right now, to help any sufficiently rational civilization in a causally disconnected region of the universe, trusting that enough of them will have independently made the same resolution toward beings like us for the arrangement to pay off. Is this a legitimate instance of acausal trade, or is there some further condition that a unilateral resolution like mine, on its own, fails to satisfy?
