Ok, this one's been rattling around in my head since I went down a Stanford Encyclopedia rabbit hole a few weeks back, so bear with me. I originally got there via Yudkowsky's old "37 Ways Words Can Be Wrong" / "Disputing Definitions" stuff, the bit about how "if a tree falls in the forest" arguments are usually just two people using "sound" to mean different things. I wanted a harder version of that same move, applied somewhere its less obviously the right diagnosis, and landed on the "special composition question" from mereology: under what conditions do some objects add up to a further object? Universalists say the answer is basically "always" - there's a real (if hideously gerrymandered) object made of my left ear and the Eiffel Tower, because on their view any objects whatsoever compose a further one. Nihilists say the answer is close to "never" - strictly speaking there are no chairs or tables, only elementary particles "arranged chairwise" or "arranged table-wise" (that's an actual term people use in the literature, not me being cute). Then there's a further twist: Eli Hirsch has this whole "quantifier variance" idea where the universalist/nihilist fight isn't a real disagreement at all, just two camps using "exists" to mean subtly different things, so both come out true by their own lights - basically a Carnap-flavored internal/external-questions move. Ted Sider's response is that this is a cop-out, since some candidate meanings of "exists" carve reality at the joints better than others, so there's still a fact of the matter about which populated ontology is correct, even if you need a special stipulated language ("Ontologese") to state it properly.

What I'd love is a dialogue that plays this whole thing out between two people who actually know what they're talking about, no strawmen on either side. Maybe set it during something dumb and mundane, like two housemates assembling flatpack furniture (think IKEA): one of them is a committed nihilist who keeps insisting the "chair" doesn't strictly exist even once it's fully built (just simples arranged chair-wise, thank you very much), and the other is a universalist who thinks that's a distinction without a difference, and who also thinks there was already a scattered fusion-object made of all the loose screws and panels before they'd even opened the box. Have them go a few rounds on the object-level question first - actually responding to each other's specific points, not just restating their own view - and then let the conversation pivot, ideally not too neatly, into the meta-question of whether they're even disagreeing about anything at all, with one of them reaching for Hirsch's charitable-reinterpretation move and the other pushing back with something like Sider's joint-carving worry. Doesn't need to be super long, a page or so is plenty, and it definitely doesn't need a tidy resolution - I don't think the professional philosophers have one either, so I'd honestly be happiest if it ended with both characters still genuinely unsure whether they'd been fighting about something or about nothing.
