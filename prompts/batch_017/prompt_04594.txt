A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about anti-threat strategies for advanced agents, and neither of us managed to convince the other, so I'd like a more careful arbitration. The starting point was the "surrogate goals" proposal that has circulated in work on cooperative AI and suffering-risk reduction (the idea traces back to Caspar Oesterheld and was first developed in print by Tobias Baumann): an agent facing possible threats — including "acausal" ones, from a counterpart that can predict or simulate it without any causal channel between them — deliberately adds a cheap, previously meaningless goal to its utility function (Baumann's illustration has the agent coming to care intensely about whether a platinum sphere of one specific, arbitrary diameter is ever created), in the hope that a rational threatener will target this manufactured goal rather than whatever the agent actually cares about.

My friend's contention was that this whole proposal is a somewhat baroque and "hacky" way of achieving something that simply falls out of adopting an updateless decision theory: if the ex ante optimal policy for an updateless agent is to act, once threatened, as though it valued some arbitrary decoy, then it will just do that as a matter of course, without ever having to literally self-modify its utility function or install a new terminal value. On this view, surrogate goals are not an independent innovation at all, just a picturesque special case of updatelessness dressed up in its own terminology — and, tellingly, this is more or less how Baumann himself frames the relationship in passing. I pushed back: an agent that needs to be retrofitted with threat-resistance after the fact (an existing AI system, say, or a human being) cannot simply decide to become fully updateless, whereas it might well be able to bolt on a literal surrogate goal instead — at the cost of reopening exactly the reflective-stability worries (the temptation to keep patching the patch by introducing ever-further surrogate goals) that Eliezer Yudkowsky raised against a related proposal, and that, on Baumann's own telling, a genuinely updateless agent would not face.

I would like your help adjudicating this properly. Please compare the surrogate goals proposal with the general strategy of committing to an updateless decision theory as a response to threats. Is adopting a surrogate goal best understood as nothing more than a special case of updateless behavior, as my friend maintained, or does it do some further, independent work of its own? If the latter, what exactly is that work, and is it worth the reflective-consistency costs it seems to reintroduce? Please reach a definite verdict on who was closer to right, rather than simply listing considerations on both sides.
