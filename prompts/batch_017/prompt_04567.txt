So I've been prepping for my quals and working through ARC's eliciting latent knowledge report, and I'm becoming convinced the central worry in that report -- the "human simulator" problem -- is basically old news at this point. Their whole argument is that if you train a reporter on human labels, it can just learn to predict what a human watching the sensors would say, instead of translating what the predictor model actually believes, and the two come apart exactly in the cases we care about, like the vault's camera getting spoofed. But Burns et al's CCS paper finds a truthfulness direction with zero human labels at all, just by forcing a statement and its negation to always get probabilities that add up to one. There's no human answer key anywhere in that loss, so whatever direction CCS finds has to be reading off the predictor's actual beliefs rather then imitating a human grader who isn't even in the room. So why do people in alignment still talk about ELK like its a wide open problem instead of one that's basically solved for current LLMs?
