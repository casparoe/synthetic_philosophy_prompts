I recently acquired a pet octopus for a saltwater tank I set up at home, a considerably bigger commitment than I had anticipated, and caring for her has drawn me into the scientific literature on animal consciousness. Two theories I keep running into seem to pull in different directions: Integrated Information Theory (IIT), which grounds consciousness in a system's capacity to integrate information, and Global Workspace Theory (GWT), which locates it in the broadcasting of information across specialized subsystems. Could you compare these two theories specifically with respect to what each would predict about my octopus's inner life, given that roughly two-thirds of her neurons are distributed along her arms rather than housed in her central brain, and that each arm is capable of a considerable degree of autonomous sensing and movement? I would like to know whether this decentralized architecture poses a bigger problem for one theory than the other, and whether that difference reflects something about the relative merits of the theories themselves or merely about which nervous systems they were originally designed to explain. Please justify your assessment.
