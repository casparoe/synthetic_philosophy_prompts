For a seminar on the history of economic thought, I've been working closely with Hume's 1752 essays "Of the Balance of Trade," "Of Money," and "Of Public Credit." Two of his commitments stand out: first, the price-specie-flow argument that any nation accumulating a trade surplus will see its specie inflow raise domestic prices until its exports lose their advantage, making sustained imbalances self-correcting; second, his suspicion that paper credit is an artificial substitute for "real" specie that lets governments evade precisely this discipline. Given both commitments, how do you think Hume would explain a country like the contemporary United States, which has run trade deficits for decades while its fiat currency simultaneously functions as the world's principal reserve currency, without the corrective adjustment his mechanism predicts ever seeming to materialize? Would he treat this as a straightforward vindication of his worries about paper credit, or does the idea of a reserve currency introduce something his framework has no real analogue for?
