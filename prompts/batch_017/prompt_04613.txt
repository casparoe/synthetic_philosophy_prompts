So I've been down a Leibniz rabbit hole lately (retired physicist here, metaphysics is my new hobby these days) and I noticed something that feels like a weird echo forward in time. Leibniz held that every individual substance has a "complete concept" containing literally all of its properties, past, present and future -- change anything about you, even slightly, and its not you anymore, its a different individual altogether. Apparently this is sometimes called his "superessentialism." That sounds a lot like David Lewis's counterpart theory, where nothing literally exists in more than one possible world, and "you" in some other world is really just a resembling counterpart, not numerically you. Except Leibniz gets there through complete concepts sitting in the mind of God, and Lewis gets there through an infinite multiverse of equally concrete, causally isolated worlds with no God required at all. So if you sat Leibniz down and walked him through Lewis's modal realism, do you reckon he'd recognize a kindred spirit, or would he object that Lewis's concrete worlds can't actually do the individuating work that his own God-grounded concepts do -- something to do with compossibility maybe, or with what grounds a mere possibility as a possibility in the first place? Genuinely not sure if this is a deep structural parallel or just a surface resemblance, curious what you think.
