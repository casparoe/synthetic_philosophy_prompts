Over the past year, an ongoing disagreement with one of my oldest friends has put real strain on our friendship, and neither of us shows any sign of budging. Reflecting on our last few conversations, I realized something about my own reasoning that has been bothering me: I was confident enough in my position that I'd stopped genuinely weighing her counterarguments, simply assuming that whatever she said had to be mistaken somehow. While trying to work out whether that reaction was reasonable, I came across Gilbert Harman's "dogmatism paradox," which seems to show, using quite plausible premises about knowledge and evidence, that once you truly know something, you're thereby entitled to disregard any evidence against it that you might encounter in the future. Given that a serious philosophical argument reaches this conclusion, should I take it as vindication that dismissing her arguments was actually the epistemically responsible thing to do, rather than something to feel guilty about?
