I'm a PhD student in financial economics working through the no-trade theorem literature for a dissertation chapter on market microstructure. As I understand Milgrom and Stokey's result, building on Aumann's agreement theorem, if it is common knowledge that risk-averse, rational traders share a common prior, none of them can profit by offering to trade on private information, since the very willingness to trade reveals that information and erases the gain for whoever would otherwise accept. Insider trading, however, is illegal precisely because it is reliably profitable in real equity markets. Doesn't this mean the no-trade theorem, whatever its mathematical elegance, has essentially no purchase on how actual securities markets function?
