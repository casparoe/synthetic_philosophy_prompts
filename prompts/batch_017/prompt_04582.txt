Ok so I've been chewing on this question for weeks now and I think it would make a really good short story if someone actually wrote it out properly (tried explaining the whole thing to my roommate over dinner and just got a blank stare back, so now you get to hear it instead). Basically I fell down a rabbit hole about what "function" even means for a body part - like why do we say the heart's function is to pump blood and not, i dunno, to make a thumping noise or warm up your chest a little, since it technically does both of those too. Turns out there's this whole disagreement (I think its a philosophy of biology thing?) between people who say the function of a trait is whatever it originally got selected for back in evolutionary history - it stuck around because it did that one specific thing, so that's its "real" function, end of story - versus people who say function is just whatever a part contributes to the workings of the bigger system right now, no history required at all.

That second view leads somewhere pretty unsettling though, and here's the scenario that's been stuck in my head (feel like I might've half heard something like this before somewhere but couldn't tell you where): say lightning hits a swamp and, through sheer astronomical coincidence, the chemicals happen to rearrange themselves into something atom for atom identical to an actual grown adult person - memories, personality, heart beating, all of it - except this "person" has literally zero history behind them. They were never born, never grew up, were never shaped by evolution or by a life at all, they just blinked into existence a second ago out of swamp muck. Does their heart have the function of pumping blood? Under the "selected for it" view the answer seems to have to be no, which feels insane given the organ is doing exactly what every other heart on earth does. But if you say yes purely because of what it's doing right now, then function doesn't need any history behind it whatsoever, which feels like its giving up something important too.

Could you write me a short story that actually sits inside that problem, maybe from the pov of this newly formed person as they slowly figure out what they are and start wondering whether their own organs, instincts, even the reflex that yanks their hand back from fire, really "function" the way everyone else's do, or whether none of it means anything without a history behind it. I don't need a neat resolution at the end, I just want something that actually takes both sides seriously instead of hand-waving past them - every clone/duplicate story I've read before (transporter mishaps and whatnot) always skips straight past this exact angle and goes straight for generic "am I really me" stuff, and that's not really what I'm after here.
