I spent almost 40 years as a physicist before I started poking around in philosophy after retiring, and something about the "laws of nature" debate isn't sitting right with me. From what I remember of Hume, he pretty much demolished the idea of any necessary connection behind cause and effect — all we ever actually observe is one billiard ball hitting another, over and over, never any real "must" binding the two together. Fine, I can buy that. But then I keep bumping into contemporary philosphers who happily call themselves "Humeans" about laws while still talking about objective, mind-independent laws of nature, as if the regularities really do govern how the world can go. Isn't that just having it both ways? If Hume's argument was any good, shouldn't a consistent Humean just admit "laws of nature" are a useful fiction rather then defend them as something real?
