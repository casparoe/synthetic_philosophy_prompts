As a journalist who covers election forecasting, I keep encountering the phenomenon pollsters themselves call "herding": adjusting a new poll's topline numbers to look more like the emerging average of other recent polls, rather than reporting the raw independent sample. This looks like a real-world instance of the sequential-decision models economists such as Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, and Welch use to formalize information cascades and rational herding. Please describe the most important implications of that literature for how I should interpret an apparent late-campaign polling consensus — in particular, how little independent evidence such convergence may actually represent, and how fragile it should be expected to be once a single pollster breaks from the pack.
