I have spent a fair amount of time working through the logic of the dollar auction on my own: the game in which bidding proceeds as normal, except that both the highest and the second-highest bidder must hand over their final bid, while only the highest bidder actually receives the dollar. What strikes me is that the resulting escalation has little to do with greed: the moment a bidder falls into second place, withdrawing guarantees a loss, whereas bidding again at least offers a chance of breaking even, so two sensible people can end up paying far more than a dollar between them for a single one. I would like a joke built around that specific mechanism, not a generic joke about people getting carried away at auctions, and it would be even better if it also gestured at how the same trap drives real escalations of commitment, such as a renovation, a lawsuit, or a war, that everyone privately suspects should be abandoned. For the joke to really land, it should only be funny to someone who grasps why it is the second-place bidder, not the eventual winner, who is genuinely trapped, and why the auctioneer, who never places a bid, is the only party who can never be drawn into it. A line or two should be plenty.
