Four decades in radio astronomy left me with an amateur's fascination with the Fermi paradox, and retirement has finally given me time to read the anthropic-reasoning literature rather than dismiss it as sophistry, as I once did. What has genuinely unsettled me is Katja Grace's result that folding the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) — update your credence toward hypotheses on which more observers exist, simply because you exist — into Robin Hanson's Great Filter implies the filter is probably ahead of us rather than behind, even though SIA is usually billed as the reassuring assumption, the one that lets us dissolve the classic Doomsday Argument. Write an essay that explains why this reversal occurs, weighs it fairly against the standard objection to SIA (the Presumptuous Philosopher) and against the reference-class troubles that plague its rival, the Self-Sampling Assumption (reason as if you were a random sample from some specified class of observers), and then reaches an actual verdict: does anthropic reasoning of this kind give us any real evidence about whether the filter lies ahead of us or behind, and if so, in which direction? A conditional verdict is acceptable, but don't retreat into declaring the matter simply undecidable.
