Finally got around to reading the actual Hajek & Nover paper on the Pasadena game, after seeing it come up in an old LW thread about unbounded utility functions and Pascal's mugging (it's the St-Petersburg-ish coinflip game where payoffs alternate sign and blow up like 2^n/n). Honestly I think the "paradox" kind of dissolves once you think about it for two seconds? Yeah, the expectation series is only conditionally convergent, so technically Riemann rearrangement lets you reorder the terms to sum to literally anything. But that's only actually a problem if you treat the payoffs as some abstract unordered bag of numbers -- in reality this is a sequential coin-flipping game, toss 1 happens then toss 2 then toss 3, so theres an obvious privileged ordering right there, you sum it up that way, get ln(2), and call it a day. Feels like Hajek and Nover are just refusing to use the ordering thats staring them in the face. Am I wrong, or is this just a neat fact about infinite series getting dressed up as some deep problem for decision theory?
