In my chemistry class, every new unit begins with several weeks devoted entirely to memorizing definitions, formulas, and step-by-step procedures; only once we can recite these flawlessly are we allowed to touch an experiment or consider any question we might find genuinely interesting. By the time we reach that point, most of my classmates have already decided the subject is tedious, and whatever we crammed for the unit exam tends to be forgotten within days. My teacher defends this sequence by arguing that the exciting parts of chemistry are simply inaccessible until the technical groundwork is secure. I find this only half-convincing, though I can't quite articulate why it feels backward. Is there a genuine flaw in organizing a course this way, or am I underestimating the value of the groundwork?
