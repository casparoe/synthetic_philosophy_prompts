This has nothing to do with the actual piece I was working on, but it's been rattling around in my head for weeks. I was interviewing a youth soccer coach, Dave, for a totally unrelated story about concussion protocols, and he mentioned this reward system he's been running for like six years: whichever kids sprint back fastest to help on defense get a sticker on a chart and a callout at the end of practice. For a long time this worked great — his sticker leaders were consistently his best all-around players too, the ones setting up goals and winning games — so he basically started using "who hustles back hardest" as his shorthand for "who's actually a good player," full stop.

This year he switched clubs and inherited an U11 team that plays a much slower, possession-heavy style. Same chart, same kids trying just as hard (if anything harder) to sprint back on defense, except now its actively hurting the team — they're out of position and gassed by halftime instead of doing the patient buildup the new system needs. Dave's stuck between three stories: maybe these kids were never as good as he thought and just got lucky before, maybe they're being lazy or stubborn about adjusting to the new style, or maybe something else is going on that isn't really about talent or effort at all. He half-jokingly asked me, since I write about this kind of stuff for a living, whats actually happening with these kids. I didn't have a great answer for him. What's your read on it — what's actually going on, and what should Dave change about how he reads and rewards his players going forward?
