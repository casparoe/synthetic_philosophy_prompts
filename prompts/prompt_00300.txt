So this thing happened at work a few weeks back and it's been bugging me ever since. My team and another backend team (let's call them Team Nimbus) got spun up at basically the same time — same headcount, same manager training program, same tech stack, basically operational clones of each other. Corporate asked both teams to self-report how many story points we "actually" need for next quarter's roadmap, and there's some HR rule against the two teams talking to each other directly about it (anti-collusion thing, I guess). The catch is: whoever reports a lower number looks leaner and gets priority for new hires, but if both teams try to lowball their real needs to look efficient, the overall roadmap falls apart and upper management just cuts both our headcounts as punishment anyway.

My tech lead's take is "obviously we shade the number down a bit, that's the dominant move regardless of what they do." But something about that logic feels off to me, expecially because I keep having this nagging thought: "they're running basically the same playbook we are, with the same incentives and the same kind of people making the call — so whatever reasoning actually convinces me is probably going to convince them too." Like, it doesn't feel like we're two independent black boxes making a choice in a vacuum. Is there something real behind that intuition, or am I just rationalizing wishful thinking because I don't want to be the sucker who reports honestly? Would appreciate an explanation that goes beyond "game theory says defect, deal with it."
