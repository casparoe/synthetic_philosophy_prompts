I work on the compliance team at a fintech company, and we recently started using an AI model to help review flagged transactions. My manager wants us to log these explanations as part of the official audit trail, basically treating them the same way we'd treat a human analyst's stated reasoning.

One coworker thinks this is completely fine and even smart — her argument is basically that if talking about the model "believing" things lets us predict and explain its outputs reliably, then who cares what's "really" going on inside it, we're justified in using that language. Another coworker thinks this is dangerous nonsense, because the model doesn't have beliefs in any real sense, it's just producing plausible-sounding text, and if we start treating its self-reports as sincere explanations we're going to get burned when it "explains" a decision in a way that has nothing to do with why it actually flagged the transaction (assuming that even means something for a system like this).

I don't have the background to adjudicate this and it's turning into an actual policy fight. Is there a principled way to think about when it's reasonable to talk about an AI system as having beliefs versus when that's just a category error dressed up in fancy language?
