I've basically bought functionalism on the strength of multiple realizability: same mental state in humans, octopuses, maybe silicon, and the only thing they can share is a causal role, which is supposed to finish off type-identity theory. What would actually count against that? Not the qualia complaint — I want the structural exposure, ranked from fatal to merely awkward.

Two worries I keep circling. First: does MR only look decisive because we've individuated mental states too finely? At the grain comparative neuroscience actually works at, maybe the reailzers line up one-to-one with the states and there's no interesting disjunction for me to exploit — in which case my argument proves less than I thought, and maybe proves something compatible with identity theory. Second: if you loosen the functional description far enough that everything you want counts as a realizer, nothing you don't want could ever fail to count. So does the view still predict anything, or has it been qualified into safety — and is that a defect here or just what second-order properties do?

Tell me also which of these a functionalist should cheerfully concede rather than fight, what empirical result would genuinely hurt (bounded cortical plasticity? octopus arms doing much of their own processing? something else?), and what functionalism is risking that identity theory or dualism isn't.
