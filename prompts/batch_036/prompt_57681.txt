I spent thirty-eight years doing revisionary work on a genus of deep-water gastropods, which is to say that most of my professional life consisted of arguing with other people about whether two shells collected four thousand kilometres apart belonged to the same species, and of learning that the argument could not be settled until one had first said what one meant by "the same." Since retiring I have taken up philosophy in a desultory way, and more recently I have been reading, with growing unease, about mechanistic interpretability and about the programme its practitioners call mechanistic anomaly detection — the attempt to flag a model's outputs when they were produced for unusual reasons, without necessarily understanding what those reasons are. What strikes me is that this enterprise quietly assumes that a mechanism can be re-identified: that the thing detected in a trusted setting is the same thing as the thing later detected in an untrusted one, that the circuit found in a small model is the circuit in the large one, that a component which is present in the weights but causally silent on every input anyone has tried is nonetheless the same component as one that fires. I cannot find anywhere in the literature a criterion of identity being offered, let alone defended. My own discipline spent a century and a half, and a good deal of bad temper, on exactly this question, and produced the apparatus for handling it: type specimens and their repositories, synonymy lists, the distinction between homology and mere resemblance, the quarrel between lumpers and splitters, and a nomenclatural code to keep the whole thing from dissolving into noise.

With that in mind, I should like you to write a satirical monograph entry — between 700 and 900 words — as it might appear in the bulletin of an invented learned society devoted to the systematics of neural network components; something along the lines of a Society for Mechanistic Systematics, with its own house style, its own long-running schisms, and its own journal of record. The entry should describe a single invented taxon, one mechanism, in the full apparatus of revisionary taxonomy: a binomial with authority and date; a diagnosis; a description; the type specimen, naming the checkpoint, the layers, and the repository in which the holotype is deposited, together with the paratypes; a list of material examined; distribution and habitat; a synonymy, in which the same thing has been named three times by three groups who had not read one another and one name has been applied to two things that are now known to be distinct; an etymology; and a remarks section carrying a disagreement between colleagues over whether certain populations are conspecific. Close with a short dichotomous key distinguishing the taxon from three confusable relatives. Everything — the society, the names, the laboratories, the checkpoints, the colleagues — must be invented; I do not want a single real person, real model, or real paper attributed with anything here, and where you need to gesture at real technique, do so in general terms rather than by citation.

The comedy has to come from the technical content on both sides, and the piece must never state the philosophical difficulty it is enacting. Each of the taxonomic conventions should turn out to be doing real work on a genuine problem in individuating mechanisms, so that a reader who knows the field recognises the problem without being told it is one. Let me suggest the sort of mapping I have in mind, though you need not use all of it. The type specimen is deposited in a repository that has since deleted the checkpoint to free storage, so the holotype is technically lost and a neotype must be designated from a different training run. Ablation of the type specimen does not abolish the behaviour, because a redundant sub-population takes over its function, and one colleague insists this proves the taxon was never a single thing while another insists this is exactly what a well-defined taxon looks like. The populations found in a differently trained model of a different scale perform the same function by entirely different means, and the remarks section must decide whether that is common descent, convergence, or mimicry — with the decision turning on criteria the author never articulates. A population is detectable only under distribution shift, which means its conspecificity with the population observed on trusted data can never be confirmed, only assumed, and the entry should handle this with the bland confidence of a diagnosis written by someone who has never seen the animal alive. And the key should quietly reveal that the characters being used to separate the four taxa are of different kinds — some read off the weights, some off causal behaviour under intervention, some off the training history that produced them — with no notice taken of the fact that a key built from three incommensurable sorts of character cannot possibly work. Keep the register entirely deadpan throughout, with no winking and no editorial aside; the only concession to the reader may be a single footnote in the society's own ponderous house style. The target of the satire is a practice and a habit of confidence, not any named person or laboratory, and by the last line a reader should have felt how much weight the whole enterprise is resting on an unexamined notion of sameness.
