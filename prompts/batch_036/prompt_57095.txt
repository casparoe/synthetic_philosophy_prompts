I am revising the capstone assignment for my graduate seminar on the metaphysics of consciousness, and I want a prompt that cannot be discharged by competent paraphrase of the canonical texts. The reading packet this term centers on Dennett's 1988 case against qualia, and in particular on his pair of veteran coffee tasters, who have each come to notice that their coffee experience is not what it used to be, and who offer mutually incompatible reports about what has changed — one locating the change in the tasting itself, the other in the reaction to the tasting — with no prospect of settling the matter by any further amount of looking inward. Please write an essay, of roughly two thousand words, that takes up the question that case is meant to settle: are qualia, as traditionally characterized, an incoherent posit, or merely an unverifiable or explanatorily idle one? I want the distinction between those three verdicts drawn explicitly and defended, not assumed, since a great deal of the secondary literature slides between them.

To keep the essay honest, I want it to work through three pieces of apparatus that I have constructed and that you will not find in the literature in this form. First, a rescue hypothesis I shall call *calibration qualia*: on this view what altered in the tasters was never a freestanding intrinsic feel but a stored reference standard against which present experience is compared, so that the quale of a given cup is a relational property — a pairing of a current sensory state with a calibration template — and the tasters' disagreement is a disagreement about which of two templates was in force. Say whether this preserves anything that a realist about qualia should want, or whether it is a redescription that collapses into the functionalist account it was meant to rescue, and identify the precise point of collapse if so. Second, an *instrumented* variant of the original case: suppose the tasters are fitted with an apparatus, plus training, that gives them exact and reliable knowledge of which internal state they are now in and which they were in a year ago — the access premise is simply removed, while their discrimination profile, their hedonic responses, and their reports remain exactly as they were. Does the argument against qualia survive that surgery? If it does, the argument cannot be an argument from the poverty of introspection; if it does not, the conclusion is contingent on our cognitive limitations rather than on any incoherence in the concept. Third, a *silent inversion*: an overnight intervention systematically inverts a subject's color phenomenology while also adjusting memory, calibration, and discriminative dispositions so that nothing looks different, nothing is reportable, and no test the subject could perform comes out differently. Ask whether that case shows that a real difference in feel can leave no possible trace in judgment, or whether it shows that "feel" has been drained of exactly the content that made it a candidate for reality.

Finally, I want the essay to be explicit about which of the traditional marks of qualia — ineffability, intrinsicness, privacy, and direct or privileged apprehensibility — the argument actually targets, and to consider whether a defender who surrenders ineffability and privacy while holding fast to intrinsicness has retained anything worth defending or has merely changed the subject. Weigh the strongest case on each side; do not conclude with a declaration that the matter is subtle or that reasonable people differ. End with a stated verdict on the question as I posed it, together with an identification of the single premise on which that verdict most depends and of what would have to be true for you to abandon it. Please write for an audience of advanced students who know the standard inverted-spectrum and zombie dialectics cold and are bored by them; no bibliography or list of sources is wanted, and I would rather you argue from the cases than from authority.
