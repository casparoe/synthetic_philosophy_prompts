I work at a small land-based prawn farm down here — recirculating tanks, and we also hold live brown crab and lobster for a few restaurants — and for the last couple of years I've been in an informal discussion group with a vet, two people who work for a welfare charity, and a couple of fishermen who think the whole subject is nonsense. The conversation has completely stalled and I want help figuring out what *kind* of stuck we're in, because I'm starting to think it isn't the kind where more evidence would help.

Here's the pattern, and it's the same pattern every time. Someone brings up the experiments where hermit crabs get small shocks inside their shells: they'll abandon a shell, but they hang on longer to a shell type they really prefer than to one they don't, so it looks like a trade-off rather than a straight reflex. Or the prawn work where they rub and groom an injured antenna for ages afterwards and it drops off if you numb the area. Or the crayfish given shocks that then behave all wound-up and avoidant in a way that a human anti-anxiety drug damps back down. Half the group says: that's flexible, remembered, drug-responsive behaviour, that's what pain looks like from the outside, we're done here. The other half says: none of it shows anything is felt, because a well-engineered nociceptive system with learning and motivational weighting built in should do exactly this and feel like nothing whatsoever, so all you've demonstrated is that the wiring is complicated. Then somebody says well the UK now legally counts decapods as animals for sentience purposes, and the second half says that's politics not science, and we go around again. Two years, same arguements, same handful of studies, nobody budges an inch.

My guess — and I could easily be wrong, so push back on it — is that the disagreement isn't really about prawns at all. Its about what could ever count as evidence of experience in a creature with no face, no voice and a body plan nothing like ours, and both sides have already quietly fixed their answer about how consciousness and nervous systems hang together before they ever look at the data. So: is that right? Is this a verbal fight over what 'pain' and 'sentient' are supposed to mean, or two groups answering different questions under one label (one asking a mechanism question, one asking how to act when you're not sure), or a genuine disagreement about standards of evidence, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? I'd also like to know whether the fact that processors and supermarkets have gone ahead and installed electrical stunning anyway, while the science is still contested, supports your diagnosis or undercuts it. Then tell me what would actually count as progress — some new kind of experiment, a distinction, reframing the whole thing as a decision under moral uncertainty, or changing the question — or make the case that no progress should be expected and say why.

One thing I really need from you: be clear about which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on it and which parts are genuinely contested, and don't dress up a contested position as if everyone agrees with it. I'm not after a reading list or citations, I can't chase those down anyway. I want the diagnosis, how confident you are in it, and a flag on anywhere you think you're reading the two camps through your own preferences rather than what they actually say.
