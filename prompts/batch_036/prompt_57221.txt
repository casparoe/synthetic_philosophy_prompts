Retired from sediment geochemistry about four years back and I've fallen into a reading group on the philosophy of cosmology. My old friend R. is the one who actually does the subject, and she's been handing down verdicts on whether a cosmological model counts as fatal — her word — meaning we have to throw it out purely because of what it implies about who's doing the observing.

Her record so far:

1. Eternal de Sitter space with a Gibbons-Hawking temperature and fluctuation observers popping up at the usual rate: fatal.
2. A variant of that in which every ordinary observer is duplicated an unbounded number of times: also fatal, and she didnt think 1 and 2 were different cases at all.
3. A model where the freaks outnumber ordinary observers by a factor of ten: not fatal. She was nearly bored by the question.
4. A model where the freaks win by miles but the CMB data rules it out anyway: fatal, but "for the boring reason."
5. A model with no fluctuation observers whatsoever but a grotesquely fine-tuned initial state: not fatal — "unpleasant, not incoherent."

Next week's case: a model that is observationally indistinguishable from ΛCDM in everything we can actually measure, but which has an eternal de Sitter future, so the freak-to-normal ratio diverges. Except the freaks are all single-fluctuation blips, one Planck time long, with no memories, no reasoning, no chance of gathering evidence. Only the ordinary observers ever have a coherent apparent past.

So what's she going to say? I care a lot more about the rule running underneath: is it the ratio (and if so, why not ten?), whether some freak could literally be you with false memories, whether the model eats the reasoning that produced it, or something about empirical distinguishability that I'm not seeing. Tell me which of her five verdicts is the closest analogue and why. If her record genuinely doesn't settle the new case, say what she'd be committing herself to by going either way. And then give me the smallest possible tweak to next week's model that would split the surviving candidate rules apart — the one case I could bring to the group that forces her to show her hand.
