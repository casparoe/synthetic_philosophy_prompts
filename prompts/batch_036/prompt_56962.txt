I've been arguing with a friend for like three weeks about whether political parties are load-bearing for democracy or the thing that broke it, and he kept pointing me at Simone Weil's short piece on abolishing them — written in London during the war, published after she died. It hit a lot harder than I expected. I have basically zero philosophy training; what I do have is about a decade of reading alignment stuff and building ML systems, so my brain instantly translated the whole thing into optimizer language, and I want to know how much of that translation is real versus me hearing what I wanted to hear. Here's the version I've got, which may be mangling her: a party is an organization whose actual operating goal, independent of anybody's intentions, becomes its own unlimited growth; every question a member faces gets silently rewritten inside their own head from "what's true or just?" into "what helps the party?"; so the damage isn't the venal corruption of bad people but a structural property that would survive even if every member were completely sincere and selfless — and she seems to think it's worse, not better, when the members are idealists rather than careerists; then "party spirit" spreads to everything that has to recruit and defend positions, so the press, unions, churches and academia all catch the same disease; and her fix is to have no organized blocs at all, people standing as individuals, voters picking persons instead of lists, so nobody is ever in a position where they have to ask what their side wants. Underneath all of it is a claim I find both the most interesting and the least translatable: that truth and justice require a kind of disinterested attention, waiting on reality rather than aiming at a target, and that a growing collective is constitutionally incapable of it.

So: go through those claims one at a time and restate each in the vocabulary I actually use — terminal vs. instrumental goals, Goodharting, value drift, mesa-optimization, corrigibility, multi-agent dynamics, an optimizer whose substrate happens to be people — as faithfully as you can. Then I want the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: for each claim, does it translate cleanly, translate only with distortion, or have no counterpart at all? Please don't just spray "mesa-optimizer" at everything and call it a translation; where a term genuinely fits, say what makes it fit, and where the honest answer is "your vocabulary has nothing here," say that instead. Two things I want you to check on me specifically. First, my working hypothesis is that this is basically instrumental convergence plus Goodhart applied to a human institution, i.e. an alignment paper from the 1940s — but if the party's terminal value just IS its own growth, calling that instrumental convergence looks like a category error, since instrumental convergence needs a fixed terminal goal to generate the subgoals from. Which is it, and does the answer change the rest of the translation? Second, she seems to slide between two pictures and I can't tell if that's her being sloppy or me being dense: is a party (a) a bunch of seperate agents who happen to share a misaligned goal, or (b) one agent implemented across a lot of humans, where the members are components rather than agents? Keep those apart and tell me which of her claims needs which one.

Also, and this is the part I'm least willing to let slide: don't smuggle my framework's assumptions in and then call it translation. If the natural alignment-flavored remedy for her diagnosis comes out as "specify the objective better" or "align the party to its members' values," flag that as me hijacking the argument rather than rendering it, because my sense is her whole point is that having an objective of that shape is the disease. Then give me the verdict: is the residue a defect of my vocabulary — something real that optimizer talk structurally cannot express, in which case what would it have to add and what would adding it cost — or is it evidence that her argument is fuzzier than it feels, that the bits which won't translate were never claims to begin with? I want a stated confidence level on each of the residue verdicts, not just an overall vibe, and I want you to single out the one you're least sure about: the spot where you think you're most likely just fitting a Weil-shaped hole into my priors.
