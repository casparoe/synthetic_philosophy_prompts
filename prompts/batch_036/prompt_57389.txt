So I run an unofficial lunch-and-learn thing at my company, mostly backend and infra people, 10-14 of them show up, we've got a conference room, a whiteboard, a printer, and 75 minutes. Nobody has taken a philosophy class, and half of them know about Bell's theorem from a podcast where the takeaway was apparently "particles send each other messages faster than light," which I'm pretty confident is not what it says. I want to build something where they hit the wall themselves instead of me drawing on the whiteboard and losing the room.

Here's the framing I keep coming back to, tell me if its bad: it's a distributed systems problem. Two teams get "deployed" to seperate rooms. Before deployment they can agree on any shared config they want — lookup tables, tie-break rules, whatever fits on the index cards you give them. After deployment a request comes in, each team gets one bit of input, each has to return one bit of output, and there is no network between the rooms at all. Scoring is the CHSH-style win condition — I vaguely remember classical strategies cap out at 3 out of 4 and the quantum thing gets somewhere around 85%, correct me if I have that wrong, because I'd rather you check my arithmetic than build on top of it.

What I actually want from you:

- the full rules, the materials list (budget is zero, no lab equipment, no entangled anything), whatever needs to be printed, and a scoreboard that works without real money
- a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75, including where people argue
- the key constraint I care about: nobody is allowed to use the words locality, hidden variable, or entanglement until *after* they've hit the ceiling. They have to invent their own name for what they can't do, and then you map it onto the real vocabulary in the debrief.

Then three escalation rounds, and this is the part I most need help designing:

1. Let them break it on purpose. Open a channel between the rooms, let them win, and make them price out what the channel cost them. Ideally they end up trying to use their now-winning setup to transmit an actual payload — a bit string, a word — and discover they can't, which should kill the FTL-signalling thing better than I ever could.
2. Make the question-asker not random: a deterministic PRNG seeded from something that was already fixed before the teams were deployed. They'll win this one too, and I want them to feel how hollow that win is rather than being told.
3. Me playing the universe with a prepared deck that puts up a score above the classical ceiling while none of them can catch me running a local strategy.

For the debrief: map each round onto the specific assumption it gave up, and finish with a "if you want to keep this, you lose that" line for each of the usual exits — Bohmian, many-worlds, superdeterminist, retrocausal, and the Copenhagen/QBist-ish shrug. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to recover mid-session. And be blunt about what the activity can't reproduce — especially round 3, because I'm faking the quantum side with a stacked deck, which is arguably exactly what a hidden variable model looks like from the inside, and I don't want to blow that up in my own face in front of twelve engineers.
