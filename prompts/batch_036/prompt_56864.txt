A friend of mine and I spent the better part of a Sunday arguing about whether consciousness can coherently be described as a user illusion, and neither of us moved. She came away convinced that my position collapses into one of two things I do not want: eliminativism about experience, or a property dualism I have no room for. Rather than defend myself, I have written down what I actually hold, as untidily as I hold it, and I would like the set audited before I put any of it into print.

My commitments are these.

1. Experience is best modelled as a user interface. The organism's control systems are presented with a heavily simplified, task-shaped summary of what is going on, both internally and externally, and the simplification is functional rather than a trick being played on anyone.

2. There is no inner spectator. The user is the whole organism, and I do not intend the metaphor to smuggle in a homunculus; where I seem to need a subject, I am content with something as deflationary as a centre of narrative gravity.

3. Phenomenal character is exhausted by what the interface makes available. What it is like to see the blue of the sky is a matter of how my discriminative, verbal, and action-guiding capacities are shaped by that presentation; there is no further residue doing work.

4. Introspection is not a privileged inner sense. It is the organism's use of its own interface, so introspective reports are fallible, theory-laden, and sometimes confabulated outright. I take the confabulation and choice-blindness work to be evidence for this.

5. Nonetheless I stop short of illusionism proper. I think it is a fact about the world that there is something it is like to be me, and I am inclined to say the interface presents qualities as such, intrinsic features that no third-person functional description captures. This is where I want to plant my flag against the eliminativists.

6. I also accept the general line associated with Hoffman's interface theory of perception: perceptual systems are shaped by fitness payoffs rather than by accuracy, so the interface should not be expected to resemble the intrinsic structure of whatever it is an interface to.

7. Experience is transparent. When I try to attend to my experience of the blue sky, I find myself attending to the sky. I regard this as supporting the interface picture rather than embarrassing it, though I confess I cannot reconcile it with my also wanting introspection to be a faculty that delivers objects for inspection.

8. The user-illusion story about self-knowledge and the user-illusion story about perception of an external world are, for me, two applications of a single principle, and I assume that evidence for either lends support to the other.

Her charges, so that you know what I am trying to answer: that the package cannot hold together; that the user-illusion metaphor is incoherent without a viewer; and that I am equivocating between two different interfaces and treating the plausibility of one as evidence for the other.

Please give me an audit rather than a lecture on the background. Specifically: go through the set pairwise and identify which commitments genuinely cannot stand together and which merely look incompatible, sorting the two kinds explicitly rather than flagging everything as problematic; rank the real tensions by severity; for each, state the cheapest revision that removes it and what the revision costs me; say whether her three charges are fair, and if so which commitments they actually get their purchase on; and finish by naming the single change that would buy the most coherence for the least damage, together with what the resulting view would then have to give up saying. I will not tell you which commitment I would sacrifice, because I genuinely do not know, and I would rather the answer not be steered by my preference. No reading lists, please — I want the reasoning, not the references.
