Pitching a small project for a workshop — three months of part-time work, deliverable is a ~5000 word paper plus a talk. Can you turn my half-formed hunch into an actual proposal, say 700-900 words?

The hunch: basically everyone reads von Neumann's pessimism about surviving our own technology as an arms-race argument. The environment in which technology develops has become too small and too poorly organized for what we can now build, whatever is technically possible will eventually get built regardless of what anyone agrees to, therefore no status quo is stable and the only hope is treaties plus inspection plus some kind of supranational control. Fine. But sitting right next to that in his automata work is a structurally different argument, and I think its doing quiet work in the essays. Two threshold results:

1. Once a self-reproducing system passes a certain complexity threshold it stops degenerating into simpler copies and becomes open-endedly evolvable, because the description gets used twice — actively as construction instructions and passively as something that just gets copied — so mutations pile up in the description instead of wrecking the machine. Below the threshold you get degeneration, above it you get unbounded growth.

2. His reliability result: redundancy buys you arbitrarily low error rates only while the failure probability per component stays under some critical value. Above that value, more redundancy doesn't help at all. Verification becomes impossible exactly when you most need it.

So one threshold says capability escalates without limit, the other says the checking needed to keep escalation survivable has a hard ceiling. Nobody seems to have put those two together and asked what the resulting claim actually is.
