I make markets for a small prop desk — mostly options and a bit of credit on names that don't trade much. My whole working vocabulary is two-sided quotes, spread, book depth, inventory, adverse selection, marking the book when nothing has printed, going indicative when I don't want to be firm. I've been reading Keynes's 1921 book on probability and I want you to translate his view into that vocabulary, as literally as you can.

The bits I care about: that he thinks probability is a logical relation between a body of evidence and a proposition rather than anything going on in someone's head; that on his account some probabilities aren't numerical at all; that some pairs of them can't even be ordered against each other (the umbrella case where a high barometer and black clouds don't obviously balance out); and his separate idea of weight of argument, which goes up whenever you get more relevant evidence even if the probability itself goes down.

So for each of those four claims, whats the closest thing on my desk, and where are you fudging? Two things I especially want pressed on. (1) Is refusing to be firm — going purely indicative, or pulling quotes — actually his non-numerical probability, or is a wide spread just a number with a shrug on it? (2) My risk system demands one mark per position and has no field anywhere for "how much evidence sits behind this mark," and I can always rank every instrument against every other one by price. Does that show Keynes was wrong, or show that my numbers are answering a different question than his? Also relevant: we have a continuous quoting obligation, so going dark costs us real money, which means I can't just abstain the way he seems to want.

And be honest about smuggling. Don't quietly render "objective logical relation" as "the mid is the fair value" and count that as a translation — flag every spot where my framework's assumptions creep in wearing a Keynes costume. End with the residue report: what survived intact, what survived only in distorted form, and what has no expression at all in market-making terms, plus your view on whether that residue is a gap in my vocabulary or a sign his claims were vaguer than they sound.
