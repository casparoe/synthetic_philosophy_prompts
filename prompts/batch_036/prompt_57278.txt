For a working document on the ways agents bind themselves, I need the specification rather than a candidate theory: the conditions any adequate account of promissory obligation must satisfy, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. The purpose constrains adequacy, so fix it here — the account must also be able to ground the neighbouring ways we acquire directed obligations (consent, offers, vows, contracts), which means each condition should be labelled as promissory-specific or as something that has to generalise.

Among the conditions I expect to see argued for, though the list should be yours: that the obligation runs to a determinate person and that only that person can release it; that a promise made with no intention of performing still binds; that a promise binds even where breaking it would leave an unreachable promisee better off; what is to be said about promising to do what one is already obliged to do, and whether the associated regress is a genuine puzzle or a distraction; how to accommodate the difference in weight between a casual assurance and a sworn oath; why performance, and not compensation, is what morality takes to be owed where contract law commonly takes the opposite view; and what is owed to an undertaking issued by an automated system in an organisation's name, which no human being intended to create and on which the recipient relied.

Mark each condition as non-negotiable or as the demand of a particular camp, flag any pair that cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single condition that excludes the most candidates. Close with a short paragraph each on how a will-based account, a reliance-based account, and a practice-based account fare.
