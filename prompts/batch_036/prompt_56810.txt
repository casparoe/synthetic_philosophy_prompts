I'm calibrating credences on a set of substantive philosophical propositions, and I want your number on one from population axiology — with the derivation, not a survey of the literature.

Fix two outcomes. A: ten billion people with very high quality of life — deep friendships, absorbing work, aesthetic experience, no serious suffering. Z*: 10^26 people, each of whose entire life contains exactly one hour per day of mild sensory pleasure (a decent meal, unremarkable music) and otherwise strictly neutral experience — no projects, achievements, loves, or discoveries, and no suffering at all. This is deliberately sharper than Parfit's own Z, which he filled out in one of his later papers by noting that the Z-people never suffer but have nothing beyond easy-listening music and potatoes; I want the specification literal and non-negotiable so that nobody can retreat to "the Z lives are actually rather miserable."

Proposition to score: Z* is better than A.

Give a single probability, then the terms behind it: that welfare is cardinally measurable and additive across persons; that impersonal betterness comparisons in non-identity cases are meaningful rather than only person-affecting; that the repugnance intuition survives full specification of Z* instead of being an artifact of picturing drudgery or of insensitivity to large numbers; that some theory avoids Z* without implying it can be better to create miserable lives than none and without admittedly arbitrary lexical thresholds; plus whatever else belongs. Then say which term you trust least, what argument or empirical finding would move the total most, and whether fixing Z* this concretely should raise or lower a well-calibrated credence relative to the usual vague formulation. I sit at 0.2, mostly because I suspect the intuition is a vividness artifact — tell me if that's the wrong place to look.
