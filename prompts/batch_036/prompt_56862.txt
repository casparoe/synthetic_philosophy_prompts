I was in a history-of-economics reading group a while back and two of the members, Dev and Margit, got into an arguement over Malthus that ran something like forty minutes, and I'm still not sure what the actual crux was. Neither of them was being a jerk and neither seemed confused, which is part of why I want an outside read on it. Here's roughly how it went, in order:

1. Dev opened by saying Malthus isn't a falsified theorist, he's a theorist who had the mechanism right and the timetable wrong. For basically all of human history, per-capita living standards stayed flat no matter how much the food supply grew — which is exactly what he described — and the break only came with coal, oil, and synthetic fertilizer, which is a contingent windfall rather than a refutation.

2. Margit said that's the textbook unfalsifiable rescue. Malthus wrote as though his principle governed the future, not just the past, so once the future fails to look Malthusian you can't retroactively demote the claim to a description of the pre-industrial regime and then book it as a hit.

3. Dev came back with a distinction he wanted her to engage with: a theory can name a mechanism that is real but switched off at the moment — he called these dormant laws — and dormant isn't the same as dead, because you can still ask whether the switch gets flipped back (fertilizer getting expensive, topsoil and aquifer depletion, fisheries collapsing). He added that you could even make it empirical rather than rhetorical: estimate how strongly real wages respond to the food supply across economies, and if the modern response is near zero, ask whether that's because the mechanism stopped working or because it's being drowned out — those are two seperate empirical situations with different futures.

4. Margit said the switch metaphor does all the work and none of the checking, because Dev can't say what would count as the switch flipping back on as opposed to the mechanism having been permanently superseded. Without that, "right but early" is compatible with any future data whatsoever, and a claim compatible with everything predicts nothing. She said the tell is that Malthusians can absorb every counterexample: good harvests and rising wages are the mechanism being suppressed, bad harvests and falling wages are the mechanism reasserting itself.

5. Dev said that objection would sink half the natural sciences. We don't say Newton was wrong when relativity showed up, we say he's a limiting case; on the same logic Malthus is a limiting case of an economy with no cheap external energy.

6. Margit said the analogy fails on its own terms. Relativity reproduces Newton's numbers in the low-speed limit and tells you precisely where they stop holding. Malthusian dynamics don't do that — they don't tell you when fertility is supposed to fall, so they couldn't have anticipated the one thing that actually broke the trap.

7. Dev said Malthus did anticipate it, since in the later editions he added moral restraint as a preventive check, which puts people having fewer children when they can't afford them inside the theory rather than outside it as an anomaly.

8. Margit said that addition is exactly the ad hoc patch that made the whole thing untestable in the first place, and she pointed out that it's no accident people have been arguing about whether Malthus was right for two hundred years without it settling — that stuckness is evidence about the structure of the theory, not about the debaters.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? For each of the eight, tell me whether it's clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right, and say briefly why. Then three things I couldn't figure out on my own: (a) does Dev's dormant-law idea actually buy him anything he wouldn't get by just saying the mechanism is real but currently overridden; (b) is the Newton-relativity comparison doing legitimate work or smuggling something in; and (c) Margit seems committed to the view that no test could ever separate "right but early" from "just wrong" — is she really committed to that, and if not, what's the strongest test Dev could have offered her? And finally, tell me what both of them missed, since I suspect there's at least one thing neither of them brought up.
