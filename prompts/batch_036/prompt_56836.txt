For about a year now I've been sitting in on a semi-regular workshop that mixes decision theorists, a few philosophy-of-action people, and two engineers who build autonomous agents, and we have a standing argument about Buridan's ass that resolves nothing. Nobody's trying to win it anymore; we just keep re-litigating the same two sentences. The setup is the usual one: an agent faces two options that are exactly equally good, and its not clear what a theory of rational choice is even supposed to say about that, or whether there's anything there to say.

There are roughly three camps. The dissolvers hold that there's no problem at all: exact ties are an artifact of idealization, any real system has sub-personal asymmetries or noise, and even granting the tie, if both options are maximal then choosing either is permitted, so the starving ass is a failure of physiology and computation rather than of rationality. The tiebreakers say that's too quick -- a choice that nothing in the agent's reasons favors isn't an exercise of practical reason in the first place, so a rational agent has to come equipped with something extra: a randomizing device, a standing policy of the always-go-left-in-ties variety, a default ordering, a second-order preference over the first-order ones. On their view expected utility theory is incomplete, and incomplete in a formal way, because it has no primitive for tie-breaking and no way to say when failing to have one is a defect. The third camp thinks both of the others are answering the wrong question: what's left over once the normative business is settled is explanatory. If nothing in the motivational set discriminates between the options, then nothing explains why the agent went left rather than right, and so either you accept a brute event, or you need a distinct power of the will, or the standard causal story of action comes apart in exactly these cases. For them the puzzle was never about maximizing.

We tried to settle it experimentally-ish last term, which made it worse. One of the engineers built an agent whose utility function has exact ties by construction -- the value function is symmetric under a permutation of the option set -- so ties aren't a measure-zero accident you can wave away with noise. In that system the dissolvers' usual reply is off the table, and yet when it ran, the thing simply took whichever option the enumeration order surfaced first, and half the room said that's obviously fine while the other half said that's obviously a defect, and nobody could articulate what the difference would consist in. My hypothesis, offered strictly as a hypothesis: we're not having one debate. Equally good is sliding between at least three readings -- equal value on a single scale, no comparison relation holding at all (the parity and incommensurability material, where neither equality nor incomparability is supposed to fit), and mere inability to detect a difference -- and the two readings never get seperated out in discussion. On top of that, is the choice rational is sometimes being heard as is it permitted and sometimes as is it required, and underneath all of it the three camps may be pursuing a normative, a formal, and an explanatory question respectively while believing they're arguing about the same donkey. So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Push on my hypothesis rather than just endorsing it -- use what each camp says to show whether they're really answering different questions, or whether there's one substantive issue hiding under the ambiguity, or whether the question is ill-posed. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction that pins down which reading of indifference each camp needs, a formal result about whether a tie-breaking primitive can avoid being either a preference in disguise or a purely arbitrary addition, a change of question, or nothing at all and why not. Please be explicit about which parts of this are settled among people who work on it and which are genuinely contested; I don't want a diagnosis that quietly treats one camp's framing as the neutral default. No reading list, I just want the argument.
