I write about materials science and machine intelligence for a general-interest magazine, and I'm working on a story about programmable matter — films and lattices whose behavior owes less to their chemistry than to control systems buried inside them. One of the engineers I interviewed has started calling a certain test result a counterfeit: a specimen that passes every test for a disposition without, he insists, having it. His example is a self-sealing film. Puncture it and the hole closes within seconds, every time; but the film itself is inert, and what closes the hole is a controller that detects the break in a buried conductive grid and drives a micro-actuator to force a plug of uncured resin up into the wound. Remove the controller and the film does nothing at all. He says the film's behavior is a counterfeit of a disposition.

I want to set this against the two cases philosophers already have names for. A fink, as I understand it, is something that would strip an object of a disposition precisely if the stimulus conditions were to obtain — the wire that a monitoring device renders non-conductive the instant something touches it. A mask, or antidote, leaves the disposition in place but blocks its manifestation through some further condition — the fragile object that has been packed in foam.

Could you compare the counterfeit against these two? In particular:

(a) What exactly is different about the way a counterfeit case defeats a conditional analysis of dispositions, if anything is? Finks and masks appear to break the analysis in one direction, and I suspect the counterfeit breaks it in the other, but I would like that spelled out rather than assumed.

(b) Whether a reformed analysis that quantifies over an object's intrinsic character and over normal conditions absorbs the counterfeit as readily as it absorbs finks and masks — or whether the film-plus-controller composite simply does have the disposition and my engineer is confused about what he built.

(c) What has to be true for the actuator's contribution to count as external rather than as just another causal basis of a genuine disposition.

Then give me a verdict: is "counterfeit" a real third category alongside finks and masks, or a restatement of the older objection that passing the test is not the same thing as having the property?
