I'm in a seminar on testimony and the norms of assertion this term and our section leader spent like half of last week on an analogy I can't stop thinking about. The setup: assertion has a norm, and the candidates are truth (assert p only if p), knowledge (assert p only if you know p), and rational belief (assert p only if believing p would be rational for you). He wants to knock down the knowledge norm and his weapon was betting. Ramsey's idea -- your degree of belief just is a disposition to bet, measurable by the odds you'd accept -- plus the claim that asserting p is basically making a public bet that p. Then the move: nobody thinks you may only bet on what you know. It's obviously fine to take a bet at good odds on something you're 70% confident in, and it wouldn't be impermissible if you happen to lose. So if asserting is betting, the knowledge norm is way too strict; the real norm must just be that you assert only what it's rational for you to believe. Half the class nodded along and I didn't.

Can you audit the analogy for me? Correspondence table first: what plays the role of the stake, the odds, the house or bookmaker, the payout, and the moment the bet settles. Then where the mapping breaks -- and be picky about separating the disanalogies that actually matter for the anti-knowledge-norm use from the ones that are just surface noise. Two I keep circling: a bet doesn't change the odds of the thing you bet on, but asserting p makes p more likely to be believed by everyone downstream, so the act seems to alter the payoff structure; and there's no house, so who exactly pays out on a false assertion and when does it settle? Also whether the analogy run carefully cuts the other way -- bets seem to have both a condition for winning and a seperate condition for being sensible to place, so maybe what you get is a truth norm plus a rationality-based excuse rather than one weak norm doing all the work. And a prior question: does it even matter that betting is something you do on the basis of a belief, while asserting is supposed to be how the belief gets expressed at all?

Then tell me straight whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if there's a better source domain for assertion -- guarantee, contract, signing a note, a move in a game, handing someone a key -- say which one and why it carries more of the load.
