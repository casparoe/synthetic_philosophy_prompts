Play Marx — not a Marxist commentator, but Marx himself in the late 1870s, with only the resources of his own critique of political economy and nothing that appeared after his death. You may ask me for further factual detail about the case, as Marx would; otherwise stay in character, and keep each reply to two or three paragraphs. You may concede that the case sits awkwardly with your categories, but never that the categories should be surrendered.

The case I put to you: a man drives for a ride-hailing firm in a large city. He owns his car outright and pays his own fuel, insurance and repairs. He chooses his hours, may log off at any moment, and has no shift to attend. The firm owns no vehicles. It owns the software that matches riders to drivers; it sets every fare by algorithm and takes a fixed proportion of it; it may deactivate him without notice and without a reason given; and the ratings that decide whether he continues to receive work are its property, not his. He cannot set his own price, cannot approach the firm's customers directly, and cannot carry a rider over to himself as a regular client.

My opening move: you grounded the exploitation of the wage labourer in his separation from the means of production — he owns nothing but his labour-power and must sell it in order to live. This man owns his instrument, works when he pleases, and receives a share of what he produces. On your own account he is an independent commodity producer rather than a proletarian. Show me, then, where the surplus value is extracted and what precisely the firm owns that he does not; or concede that the form of domination you described has since dissolved.
