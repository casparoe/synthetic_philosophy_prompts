i TA an interdisciplinary reading group this semester — roughly half historians, half people who came over from the complexity/comp-sci side — and the last session is on cliodynamics, specifically the secular-cycle material: demographic-structural theory coming down from Goldstone, Turchin's models where population and internal warfare oscillate at the same period but out of phase, elite overproduction, and the asabiyya idea he took over from Ibn Khaldun along with the rough three-generation rule of thumb about how long a dynasty stays hard. I want to close the session by reading something aloud rather than doing another summary of the argument, and I'd like it to be a poem.

The room is genuinely split and I don't want to paper over it. Some people think this is the first honest attempt at a predictive science of long-run history, and they'll point at that 2010 Nature letter forecasting instability through the 2020s as a real out-of-sample call. Others think it's numerology with a regression bolted on — that the "parameters" are just curve-fitting dressed up as mechanism, and that any model flexible enough to swallow Rome, Han China, early modern France and the post-1970s US has swallowed everything and therefore explained nothing. A couple of us suspect the same model looks like either of those depending on how closely you stare at the fit. So: dont write a victory poem for either camp.

The conceit I want is that the speaker is the model — the system of equations, or the fitted curve — watching one dynasty go past from inside the fit. Form-wise: four stanzas of six lines, and the last line of each stanza is the *same* line with exactly one word swapped out. That mutation is the whole point, because the cycle isn't a repetition, it's the same phases coming back in a shifted order with different names attached, so the refrain should return recognizably but not identically. Read those four swapped words on their own in sequence and someone who knows the theory should be able to name the phase each stanza is sitting in — expansion, stagflation, crisis, the long quiet intercycle — while someone who's never heard of cliodynamics should just feel a society tighten and come apart.

Which means the mechanisms have to be carried by concrete things, not labels. Please don't use any of the jargon anywhere in the poem — no asabiyya, no elite overproduction, no structural-demographic, no secular cycle, don't even say "the model." Show it instead: mouths outrunning the land, wages sinking while the headcount climbs, more young men with money and training than there are offices to put them in, the treasury thinning out, roads going unrepaired, then fields going back to scrub and the tax rolls stopping. I do want a hint of Turchin's forest image in there — deadwood piling up for years until some arbitrary spark arrives — but as an image, not as a citation.

Then the last two lines should step out of the dynasty and let the speaker notice its own situation: it can't tell whether it found the pattern or whether the pattern is what shows up once you insist on fitting one. Leave that unresolved, no winking, no neat answer — just the question landing. Around 26 lines total, contemporary diction, no archaisms or apostrophes-to-the-muse, and it has to survive being read aloud to a room of tired grad students at 4pm. If four stanzas of six feels too stiff, a shorter version is fine as long as you keep the repeating-and-mutating final line — that constraint is carrying the argument, so please don't drop it.
