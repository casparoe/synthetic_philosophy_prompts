I'm running a small reading group and two of the regulars have been going in circles for months about whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. One is a straightforward origin essentialist: the gametes you actually came from, or their fusion, are essential to you, so any world where a different pair produced the person is a world containing a different person. The other thinks the framing itself is broken — roughly a Lewisian counterpart-theory view, where "you could have been otherwise" is made true by a suitably similar individual in another possible world, and how much weight origin gets in that similarity metric is fixed by conversational context rather than by any further metaphysical fact. They can't even agree on what would settle it, which is the actual problem.

Write the document they should have produced: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration that both of them could sign without wincing.

What I want in it:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section. And I mean claims that carry information, not "we both want to get this right." For instance: which of the biological cases both sides grant are possible; what both sides agree the word "you" is doing in the modal question; whether both agree there's a substantive question left over once all the non-modal facts about a world are pinned down; whether both accept that origin talk can be vague without anyone being confused. If the overlap turns out to be small, say so and give me the small thing rather than padding it.

2. A disagreements table. Each row: the dispute stated neutrally, then each side's own preferred formulation of its own position, in words that side would actually sign. If a formulation would make one of them say "that isn't me," rewrite it before you show it to me.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of the two. I suspect at least two are hiding in here: (a) whether gradualness in origin is a feature of the world or of our concepts, and (b) whether the counterpart theorist owes an explanation of why the origin-weighted counterpart relation is the one we default to. Add, drop, or replace as you see fit, but justify the list.

4. For each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Since this is metaphysics, "prediction" can mean what each side is committed to saying about the case, and what each predicts about the considered intuitions of people who understand the case fully. Please use cases with some teeth rather than the standard textbook ones — monozygotic twinning (one zigote splits: does one origin yield two candidates for being me, or me plus a stranger?); tetragametic chimerism (two early embryos fuse into a single person with two genetic lineages); mitochondrial donation, where nuclear material from the intended mother's egg is transferred into a donor egg that keeps its own mitochondria, so the child ends up with a third genetic contributor — is that still the same egg, and does the answer turn on whether an egg is individuated by its nuclear genome or by being a particular cell?; and somatic cell nuclear transfer, where there is no gamete origin at all.

Also tell me where you think part of this dispute is verbal rather than substantive, and which part. My hunch is that once both sides agree on every non-modal fact, some of the argument is just about how to carve the modal idiom — but also that not all of it is, and I want the line drawn explicitly with reasons.

One thing that matters a lot: keep clear about what's settled here versus what's genuinely contested among people who work on this. Don't hand me a contested thesis dressed up as the consensus view. If the field is split, if most people think the essentialist argument has a hole in it somewhere, or if nobody really knows, say that plainly and say which parts are in which category. And where you're reconstructing a position rather than reporting one, flag it as your reconstruction. No bibliography, no reading list — just the document and those notes.
