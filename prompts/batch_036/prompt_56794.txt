Build me a historiographical thought experiment to the following specification. Two historians work on the same episode in an invented polity — do not use real history, since I want the case to turn on structure rather than on contested facts about the actual past. Give them a complete archive with no gaps, agreement on every factual question, agreement on every evaluative and political question, and even the same research question. Against all that, their finished narratives must nonetheless be emplotted in two different modes in Hayden White's sense, and no further evidence could settle which framing is right. Keep the scenario to a single paragraph.

The case is meant to test whether Ranke's ideal of setting down the past as it actually was survives once everything Carr concedes has been neutralized, so it fails if the divergence turns out to be smuggled in through gaps in the record, partisan sympathy, or differently posed questions.

After the scenario, do the following: (1) list precisely what freedom remains once every constraint above is imposed, and show how the surviving degrees of freedom generate the difference in emplotment; (2) state what a Rankean, a Carr-style historian, and White would each conclude from the case, and why their conclusions differ; (3) identify the single feature doing the work, and say whether the case collapses into the ordinary Carr point if you lift one constraint; (4) give the strongest objection a Rankean could press — one that does not simply deny that emplotment is a real feature of historical writing — and say whether it succeeds. If you believe the specification cannot be satisfied, say so and name the minimal constraint whose removal makes it satisfiable. Please write in American English.
