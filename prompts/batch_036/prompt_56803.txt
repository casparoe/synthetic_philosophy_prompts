Compose a work of short fiction, between 1,200 and 1,800 words, whose subject is a committee that must decide when the present geological epoch began — and which cannot agree on who is entitled to decide.

You may take the following as background, and should keep it straight wherever you use it. Stratigraphy does not date periods by processes but by points: the base of a unit is fixed at one physical spot in one continuous sequence of sediment or rock, a global boundary stratotype section and point, colloquially a golden spike, which must be somewhere a person can walk to, and which is then declared to stand for the whole planet. All other records are auxiliary to it. In the actual world, a working group spent roughly fifteen years converging on annually laminated lake sediments in Ontario whose varves carry the worldwide fallout signal from thermonuclear weapons testing, with the boundary drawn at 1950. Competing candidates included a sharp fall in atmospheric carbon dioxide around 1610 recorded in Antarctic ice, attributed to the reforestation of the Americas after the catastrophic depopulation that followed European contact, and — more provocatively — a change in the skeletal morphology of intensively farmed broiler chickens. In 2024 the proposal was voted down by the bodies it had to pass through, partly because the record is so thin and so recent; the word remains in general use, but it has no formal base.

Now the fiction. Set the story at a reconvened session, some decades hence, of a body reconstituted to settle the question a second time. Three features of this session are invented, and none of them is real: build them into the plot rather than describing them from the outside.

First, the section the committee is now minded to nominate is not the Ontario lake but an artificial impoundment whose sediments are the finest in the world for the purpose — and the impoundment is scheduled to be drained and re-engineered within a decade, which collides with the requirement that a type section be permanently protected and accessible.

Second, the body's composition has changed. Alongside national delegates it now carries two seats held by constituencies that are not states, and whose mandates cannot both be satisfied. Give these delegates names, temperaments, and a history with the earlier vote; do not let them be abstractions with a talking part.

Third, one member proposes that the boundary be placed not at a spike in the record but at a hiatus — a gap where deposition stopped — on the grounds that the epoch ought to be defined by the interval in which the archive of the planet could no longer keep up with the rate at which things were happening to it.

Let the decision turn on procedure as much as on evidence: the vote needs a supermajority, and the chair is resolved by seniority of continuous service, so the deciding voice belongs to whoever has sat longest and therefore has the most invested in the earlier failure.

Two craft requirements. There must be a character who wants the epoch ratified and one who does not, and neither may be a strawman — the reader should be able to state each position better than its holder does. And the story should make felt the difference between a boundary that is true and a boundary that is useful, without any character announcing that this is what is at stake. End with the vote, or with the spike driven or undriven, but leave the legitimacy of the outcome genuinely unresolved. Ground the prose in the physical materials — cores, laminations, the smell of reduced sediment, a lake in wind — rather than in seminar talk.

After the story, add a note of 150 to 250 words covering four things: where you have departed from the real history and the real procedure, and whether any such departure is a licence you are taking with the science; which of the actual objections to formalising an Anthropocene your invented body is leaning on, and whether that objection is itself contested; how confident you are in the factual scaffolding you have relied on; and the single claim in the note you are least sure of, stated plainly rather than hedged in general.
