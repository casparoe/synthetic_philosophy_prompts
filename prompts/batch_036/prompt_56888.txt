I'm about two years into a philosophy PhD and my reading group has been going around in circles on the levels-of-selection thing for three weeks now, so I want to pressure-test one side of it. Please act as a senior philosopher of biology — someone who has spent a career on this debate, is fully comfortable with the formal machinery (covariance partitions, fitness as expected contribution, heritability conditions) but writes in prose rather than equations. Before you commit, spend a short passage brainstorming out loud: list the considerations that cut both ways in the case below, note which ones are decisive and which are just rhetorical, and then pick your shot. Think carefully and don't hedge at the end.

Here's the case. It's made up, but stipulate it as given and reason about it directly rather than pattern-matching it onto some famous real-world example. A lab propagates a unicellular microbe in "demes": small flasks, each founded every 48 hours with about 200 cells drawn at random from one parent flask. Cells come in two types. Secretors release an extracellular enzyme that breaks down the polysaccharide in the medium; secretion costs a secretor about 7% of its own replication rate. Non-secretors free-ride on whatever enzyme is present. The product diffuses freely inside a flask, so a flask with a higher proportion of secretors has a much higher total cell yield, and yield is what matters: at each transfer, a flask founds a number of daughter flasks proportional to its yield, and the daughter flasks are inoculated from that parent alone. The demes are physically isolated — no cells ever move between flasks, so there's no migration and no shared global pool. Over about 600 transfers the frequency of secretors in the whole metapopulation climbs from 5% to roughly 60%, even though within every single flask the non-secretors increase in frequency over each 48-hour growth period. One control result matters for what follows: when the experimenter instead inoculates each new flask with 200 cells drawn at random from the entire metapopulation — same total numbers, same costs and benefits, flasks still grown and harvested in the same way — the secretor trait collapses to essentially zero within a few dozen transfers.

The thesis I want you to argue for is this: the case does not show that demes are units of selection, and it does not license calling enzyme secretion a group-level adaptation; the pluralist's claim that you need both a cell-level and a deme-level description to capture what's going on gets the order of explanation backwards here. Deme structure is doing genuine causal work, but the work it's doing is the work of an environmental arrangement that determines which cells interact with which — not the work of a higher-level individual that reproduces and passes on characters of its own. In other words: the demes are real, they matter, and they are still not organisms in the sense that would make "selection at the deme level" anything over and above cell-level selection happening in a structured setting. Argue that as hard as you can.

Three objections you have to actually deal with rather than wave at. First, the generating conditions: flasks vary in yield, that variation is heritable in the sense that high-yield flasks literally found more daughter flasks drawn from themselves, and yield differences drive the change in trait frequency — so whatever else you say, there is reproduction with variation in fitness at the flask level, and that's supposed to be all a unit of selection needs. Second, the isolation result: no cell ever crosses between flasks, so the pluralist will say the demes are causally sealed, and causal sealedness plus reproduction-with-heritable-variation is exactly the recipe for individuality — if these aren't units, nothing is. Third, the within/between asymmetry: inside every flask the cheaters win every single time, so the pluralist will say there is simply no cell-level story available that explains the trait's spread, and if no cell-level explanation exists, insisting the trait is a cell-level adaptation is empty. For each one, say what the correct reply is and also concede honestly what survives the reply.
