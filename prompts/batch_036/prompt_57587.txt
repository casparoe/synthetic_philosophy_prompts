I head the appeals and research-integrity function at a national research funder. We receive roughly ninety appeals a year against peer-review outcomes. About a fifth take a particular form: the appellant does not allege bias, factual error or procedural irregularity, but claims that the panel assessed the proposal by the standards of a rival research tradition — that applicant and reviewers were working within incommensurable frameworks in something like Kuhn's sense, and that the panel therefore had no standing to reject the work. These appeals are currently handled ad hoc, usually by reconvening the same panel, which is plainly circular, and different case officers reach opposite conclusions on comparable facts. I need a written procedure to replace that.

The officer who will run it holds a doctorate but not in the appellant's field, has forty-five minutes, and works from the documents alone: the proposal, two review reports, the applicant's rebuttal and the panel's summary. The officer cannot read the primary literature, cannot interview the parties and cannot commission a new review except through the referral step described below. There are five available dispositions: dismiss; uphold; refer for review by a differently constituted panel; refer for written advice from one external specialist; or uphold in part with a direction to reconsider a specific objection. We can fund roughly twenty referrals a year across ninety appeals, so the procedure must tell the officer when a referral is worth spending and when it is merely an expensive way of deferring the decision.

Please write the procedure to the following specification.

1. A sequence of numbered tests, in the order the officer should apply them, each stating what to look for on the page in the documents, what would count as a sufficient finding, and what that finding licenses. The tests must be operable by someone without the relevant subject knowledge.

2. A taxonomy of at least four distinct diagnoses the officer might reach, which I expect to include: divergence that is merely lexical; disagreement about what would count as evidence or a result; disagreement about the aims and desiderata of the field, such that the parties weight shared values differently; and disagreement about how the objects of study are classified into kinds, in the later Kuhnian sense of a failure of translation between locally inter-defined vocabularies rather than a failure of comparison. For each diagnosis, state whether the underlying disagreement is in principle adjudicable by a standard both parties already accept, adjudicable only at prohibitive cost, or not adjudicable at all — and justify that claim rather than asserting it. I expect most real appeals to fall in the middle category, and the procedure should be built around that fact.

3. An explicit rule about what incommensurability does and does not entitle an appellant to. The procedure must not allow the label to function as a shield against ordinary criticism, and it must say so in terms an officer can apply.

4. A test for whether the applicant's framework preserves or explains something the mainstream framework leaves anomalous, distinguishable from the applicant's claim to have been misunderstood. Also address the case in which the panel reached the right outcome but for reasons it reconstructed after the fact, and the case in which Kuhnian vocabulary is being used opportunistically to dress up a straightforward failure of quality.

5. A marker at every step where judgment cannot be eliminated, saying what the judgment consists in and in which direction an officer is likely to err.

6. A short list of the procedure's systematic failure modes: the kinds of appeal it will get wrong, and whether it errs towards the funder or towards the appellant.

Then run the procedure on the following three cases, giving for each the disposition, the reasoning as it would appear in a determination letter, and the points where you had to exercise judgment the procedure does not constrain.

(a) Submitted to our infection and immunity call. The applicant treats the disease as a property of a regulatory network and proposes simultaneous perturbation experiments across many nodes with no pre-specified primary endpoint. Both reviews objected that the proposal states no falsifiable hypothesis and would not know which outcome counted as failure. The rebuttal says these demands presuppose a one-target-one-intervention conception of mechanism that the framework rejects, and that network perturbations are assessed by change in network state rather than by hypothesis tests. On the documents, applicant and reviewers agree about the experimental techniques and cite overlapping literature; they disagree about what would constitute a result.

(b) Submitted to our macroeconomics call, using a stock-flow consistent accounting framework. One reviewer wrote that the model is unidentified and lacks microfoundations; the panel summarised this as untestable as proposed. The rebuttal says identification in the reviewer's sense is a standard internal to a modelling tradition the applicant does not share, and that the framework's value lies in its accounting consistency. The applicant identifies no empirical phenomenon that mainstream models cannot represent.

(c) Submitted to our interdisciplinary archaeology and genetics call. The panel rejected the proposal because its chronology was said to contradict the geneticists' dates by several centuries. Reading the proposal and the two reports together, it appears the applicant reports radiocarbon ages under one convention and calibration curve while the reviewers converted to calendar dates; the discrepancy disappears under a consistent convention. The appellant nevertheless frames this as a clash of incommensurable temporal frameworks.

At least one of these must come out against an outcome I would prefer. The funder has a standing commitment to methodological pluralism, and I am sympathetic to (a) and (b). If the procedure directs dismissal in either, say so plainly and explain why the pluralism commitment does not override it.

Please write in British English. This is an internal operational document, so no footnotes, citations or reading lists: define any term of art in place. What I need is the operational content — what the officer examines, what each finding licenses, and where the procedure honestly runs out — rather than an account of the philosophy of science behind it.
