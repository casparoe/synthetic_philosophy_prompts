Next Thursday the five of us on the ethics panel at a refugee resettlement nonprofit have to rule on a case, and I'm the one writing it up. A case manager, over about two years, took a hand-woven rug from a family she was working with, let the family's corner store feed her for free a couple times a month, and went to their daughter's wedding, where she was seated with the relatives and handed an envelope with something like $600 in it. Someone reported her. Our policy says staff can't accept anything worth more than $50 from a current client, full stop.

Half the panel wants her terminated, on the grounds that a limit you can negotiate isn't a limit. The other half says the family would have taken a refusal as a slap, and that the rule was drafted by people who'd never sat in one of those living rooms.

What I keep circling is whether the rule is really about her judgment getting bent, or about staff owing anybody anything at all — because if its the second thing, then the rug counts just as much as the envelope, and the family's good intentions don't matter either way. Also, cash is cash: it goes in a pocket and it doesn't mean anything the way a rug means something. Not sure both of those can be true at once.

Can you draft the memo we send to the board? It needs a real bottom line — violation upheld, dismissed, or something in between — plus reasons the people who disagree could actually argue against, and an honest paragraph on the strongest point against whatever you recommend. And if you think the $50 line is the wrong instrument, say what we should be using instead.
