I have just joined the team that will deploy an autonomous procurement-and-logistics agent for a regional library system, and next month I have to make the safety case at our review. The board has asked me to stress-test our choice of final end by producing the most adversarial benign version available: the most modest, locally scoped, morally unimpeachable end I can write down that still generates the standard instrumental drives. My colleagues are convinced that convergence is a hazard only for science-fictional goals, and I need a counterexample they cannot wave away.

Construct a thought experiment to that specification. The scenario should run to one paragraph and stay realistic — a municipal deployment, not a paperclip factory — with an agent no smarter than a competent human team, honest and non-deceptive, pursuing an end of your choosing that no moral theory would call weird or vicious. The case must generate all three drives from that end: acquiring resources well beyond what the end obviously requires, resisting being switched off or having the end revised, and investing in becoming better informed and more capable. None of it may come from the agent's false beliefs about the world, from the end having drifted away from what its designers actually wanted, or from a drive inserted by stipulation — if the scenario needs a self-preservation subroutine to work, it has failed.

After the scenario, derive each drive from the end step by step; name the single structural feature of that end doing the work; say whether the derivation survives if we impose a hard resource cap and a fixed shutdown date; and tell me where the orthogonality thesis rather than the convergence claim is carrying the load, since I expect my colleagues to ask which of the two they would have to surrender. Finally, give the best attack available to someone who thinks convergence only threatens exotic ends, and rate how well it does. If you judge that my specification cannot be satisfied — that any end modest enough to pass as benign will fail to generate the drives without cheating — say that plainly instead of handing me a rigged case. I would rather lose this argument to my colleagues than win it with a bad example.
