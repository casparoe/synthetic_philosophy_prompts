I serve on the cause-prioritization committee of a mid-sized foundation. Every application is scored by the committee on three dimensions — importance, tractability, neglectedness — each on a 1-to-10 scale, and the scores are entered in the minutes before the funding decision is taken. Our record over the past three years runs as follows:

1. Scale-up of a well-evidenced cash-assistance program in a region where several large funders already operate. I = 7, T = 9, N = 2. Rejected.
2. A novel delivery mechanism for an established vaccine, mid-development. I = 9, T = 4, N = 8. Funded in full.
3. An advocacy campaign in a policy area already receiving heavy outside attention. I = 6, T = 6, N = 2. Rejected.
4. A speculative research program with a thin evidence base but a potentially transformative payoff. I = 10, T = 2, N = 8. Funded, though only as a small milestone-gated exploratory grant rather than the full request.
5. A modest, well-evidenced improvement in a field almost nobody else funds. I = 4, T = 8, N = 7. Funded.
6. Mid-level technical work, reasonable feasibility, moderate outside attention. I = 8, T = 7, N = 5. Funded.

Next month we decide an application scored I = 7, T = 7, N = 4. Two contextual details are not captured by those numbers. First, the field contains only four organizations, two of which are the area's largest existing funders, and roughly three years' worth of committed money for comparable work already sits unspent; the applicant states plainly that our grant would be absorbed, but that near-identical projects are funded elsewhere through the end of the decade. Second, two of the seven importance points rest on second-order effects — researchers trained by the project moving on to other cause areas — and the minutes for case 2 record the chair explicitly declining to credit that kind of downstream benefit when scoring.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own record? In answering, please do the following. Set out the candidate decision rules that our six precedents leave open; my own suspicion is that at least a multiplicative rule, an additive rule, and some treatment of neglectedness as a gate or as a measure of marginal dollars rather than of headcount all survive the record, but tell me if you find others, and tell me if any of these is in fact ruled out. For each surviving rule, state what it implies for this application, and what it implies if the second-order component of the importance score is not credited. Identify which precedent is the closest analog and say whether that analogy is doing any real work or merely restating the rule you have already chosen. Then give me your best guess at the decision, together with your reasons for preferring the rule that generates it over the alternatives — including, if it matters to you, how much parameter slack each rule needs in order to fit all six precedents. Finally, if the record genuinely underdetermines this case, describe a further application, concretely enough that we would recognize it if it arrived, whose outcome would discriminate among the live rules; and state what principle we would be committing ourselves to if we fund, and what different principle we would be committing ourselves to if we refuse.

Lead with your recommended decision in your first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restatement of the question.
