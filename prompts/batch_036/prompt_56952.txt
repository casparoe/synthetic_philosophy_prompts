I'm a formal epistemology PhD student and I've basically gone all-in on the strong reading of Aumann's agreement result: two Bayesians with a common prior who come to know each other's posteriors (and each other's rationality) have to end up agreeing, so I treat persistent disagreement between people who've genuinely compared notes as evidence that one of the idealizations failed somewhere -- undisclosed private evidence, priors that arent actually common, motivated reasoning, or a breakdown of the common-knowledge step -- rather than as a real case of reasonable disagreement. I use it as a stick against the permissivist/steadfast line in the peer-disagreement debates.

What would count against this? I want the map of exposure, not the "common knowledge never obtains in practice" brush-off I already hear constantly. Specifically: which weakenings of the assumptions actually let disagreement back in -- swapping common knowledge of posteriors for common high-probability belief, swapping the common prior for priors that are merely close in some metric, allowing ambiguity so there's no unique posterior to compare, or letting each agent's evidence be self-locating so they aren't even conditionalizing on the same proposition -- and for each, is it a live escape route or just logically possible? Also: what does my view predict that a permissivist rival doesn't, since what would move me most is stuff about structured disclosure -- adversarial collaborations, prediction markets, iterated dialogues where agents just exchange posteriors -- and whether disagreement really converges there or just gets quieter and more polite.

And then the uncomfortable part. Since any observed disagreement can be blamed on a failed antecedent, have I insulated the position into unfalsifiability? Is that fatal for something I want to have normative bite against the disagreement literature, or is that just what theorem-driven philosophy looks like? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and be explicit about which ones someone already committed to my view should honestly concede rather than explain away.
