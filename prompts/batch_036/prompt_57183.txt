I co-run a small multi-site project comparing how people respond to philosophical thought experiments — six sites, two of which are places where nobody on our core team speaks the language, so we work through in-country collaborators and paid translators. Each wave we field maybe 12-15 vignettes, and every one gets a verdict question on a 7-point scale plus a free-text box asking people to explain their answer in their own words. Pre-registration goes in about five weeks out and after that we're stuck with our analysis plan, so I need something we can apply per vignette, not per paper.

The problem: when a vignette comes back with a verdict gap between sites, we currently just argue about it in a meeting. Some of us say it's a real difference in the intuition, some say it's the translation, some say 7-point scales don't mean the same thing to someone who went through a different school system, some say participants at different sites are answering different questions entirely — like whether the act was blameworthy versus whether the agent actually knew. Its not that we lack opinions, it's that we have no procedure and I'm sick of the meetings.

So: write me a triage procedure an RA can run on one vignette in about an hour, with no stats training past a first methods course, that sorts a verdict gap into genuine difference / translation-or-comprehension failure / scale and response-style artifact / question substitution (they're answering something else) / undecidable with the data we have. For each branch I want what evidence to look at in the stuff we already collect, what would count as a sufficient answer to that question, and what we do next — report it, report it with a caveat, drop the vignette, or re-field it next wave. Be blunt about which steps are judgment calls wearing a procedure costume. Our collaborators have been burned by teams that pretended otherwise and I don't want to do that to them.

Two things we already do, so build these in rather than reinventing them: every vignette has a mirror version where the culturally loaded detail is swapped for a site-local equivalent nominated by the in-country collaborator, and we field both; and we ask a subsample to paraphrase the case back to us, which we code for whether the key structural detail survived.

Then run your procedure on three invented results, and for each give the verdict, the reasoning, and the sentence we'd actually put in the pre-registration:

1. A Gettier-style case. The mirror version closes the gap at one site but not the other, and the paraphrase codes come back clean everywhere.
2. A case about whether it's permissible to break a promise to a dying person. Huge gap, paraphrases fine, but one site's whole distribution is squeezed into 5-6-7 while another uses the full 1-7.
3. A free-will style case where the gap disappears when we swap the wording of the question from a could-have-done-otherwise phrasing to a local idiom that our collaborator says carries no ability connotation at all. Honest warning: this one is the centerpiece of the grant and I badly want it to be a real finding, so if your procedure says I have to drop it, tell me straight and tell me what would have to change for it to survive.

Last thing — say what the procedure cannot settle no matter how much data we throw at it, and mark the points where the decision belongs to me or to the in-country collaborator rather than to the RA following the steps.
