I convene a fourteen-student senior and MA seminar in the philosophy of biology, and each May I set a two-hour final of which one quarter is a single essay question on the theory of biological function. For the past three years that question has asked candidates to compare the causal-role account of function defended by Cummins with the etiological account defended by Millikan and to assess which better accommodates malfunction. Our external examiner's complaint is fair: the answers are label-matching exercises. Students who have memorized the two slogans produce an even-handed survey, gesture at a swamp-grown heart, and declare a draw; students who have understood the dispute and students who have not are almost impossible to separate on the script alone. This year I want to abandon the survey question entirely and build the assessment around a case that does not appear in any anthology, so that recitation earns nothing and adjudication earns everything. Below is the case, which I have sketched with a colleague in the microbiology department and which should be taken as fixed material: you may tighten or reorder my description, but not simplify away any of its components.

A soil bacterium bears a surface coat assembled from a single protein; call the coat the mantle. The phylogenetic record of the lineage shows that the mantle arose and swept to fixation at a time when a virulent bacteriophage was endemic to the lineage's habitat, and the resistance the mantle confers against that phage is what explains the trait's presence in the lineage. That phage has been absent from the habitat ever since. The habitat is now subject to severe seasonal desiccation, and the mantle is the principal means by which cells survive the dry season: intact cells survive drying, uncoated cells do not. Two laboratory populations are available. Population W was isolated recently from the wild and has never been subjected to artificial selection; its history is exactly as described. Population L was derived from W three hundred generations ago and has since been selected every generation for desiccation tolerance; the mantle gene in L is identical in sequence to that in W. In both populations the mantle additionally stiffens the cell envelope against osmotic shock, serves as a nitrogen source for neighbouring kin when cells lyse, binds trace zinc, and slightly increases cell mass and settling rate. Finally, a single-base deletion truncates the mantle protein: mutants in both populations assemble a partial coat that retains stiffness, nitrogen value, zinc binding and settling, and that fails only at desiccation tolerance. Under the wet, phage-free conditions in which both populations are maintained, mutants are indistinguishable in fitness from normal cells; under drying, both die.

Write the examination question as it would appear on the paper, together with everything I need to mark it consistently. The question should be answerable in forty-five minutes for twenty-five marks by a candidate who has read the two canonical positions and one general overview and who has no access to further literature, and it should be structured in labelled parts so that the marking scheme has something to bite on; my own instinct is that the parts should require the candidate, first, to derive from each account's own stated criteria rather than from intuition which effects of the mantle count as functions in W and which in L, second, to explain why the two accounts must disagree about whether these molecule-for-molecule identical organisms have the same functions, third, to assess the most promising repair available to the causal-role theorist — anchoring the relevant containing capacity in what coats of this type typically do in the species — and to show either that the repair collapses into an etiological account or that it cannot deliver malfunction in the mutant, and fourth, to take a position on whether the case licenses pluralism about function talk. You are free to redistribute the weight, but the third part must remain the crux, and at least one part must have a determinate correct answer so that the scheme is not merely a ladder of sophistication. Supply then a model answer of seven hundred to eight hundred words that argues from the case to a definite verdict rather than surveying it, and a marking scheme allocating the twenty-five marks across named moves, with one line per move recording the way candidates characteristically fumble it — for instance, treating the interest-relativity of causal-role attributions as though it were a mere pragmatic caveat rather than the reason malfunction threatens to disappear. Close with a short paragraph, of the kind I hand to my two markers, stating precisely what separates a first-class script from a competent second-class one. Please keep the whole thing self-contained: no bibliography, no reference apparatus, and no credit in the scheme for name-dropping the literature.
