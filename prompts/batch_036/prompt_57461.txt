I am a high school junior writing a research paper for my government class, and I would rather stress-test a framework than describe it. The episode I am using is the Swiss referendum of 29 November 2009, in which voters approved a popular initiative adding a constitutional prohibition on building new minarets. It passed with about 57 percent of the vote and a majority of cantons, against the recommendation of the Swiss government and parliament; it left the four existing minarets, mosques, and Islamic worship untouched; and its sponsors argued that a minaret is not a religious requirement but a symbol of religious-political power.

Please analyze that referendum through Mill's harm principle, doing the mapping carefully rather than reaching a verdict first. Whose liberty was restricted, and by what exactly? What did the sponsors offer as the harm, and is it the kind of thing the principle can admit — a genuine harm to others, or offense, symbolic injury, and a declaration about the identity of the political community, which the principle was built to exclude? Then run the analysis the other way: the harm principle is addressed to a government justifying interference with individuals, but here the interference was enacted directly by the majority voting. Does Mill's framework have a place for that, or does it presuppose a distance between the sovereign and the people that a national referendum closes? Tell me whether the episode confirms the principle, exposes a limit it never anticipated, or shows that it was never meant to decide cases like this one.

Finally, say what would change your mind about the verdict you reach, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
