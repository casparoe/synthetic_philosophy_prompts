Can you write me a short story for me, somewhere around 1400–1800 words? I want it to dramatize one very specific piece of reasoning without ever naming it.

Here's the setup I'd like you to build on — change names, settings, texture, whatever, but keep the numbers:

A walled city runs a Continuity Bureau that scans every resident and stores them as dormant "instances" that can be woken later. Every decade two auditors have to sign off on the Bureau's books, and this decade they can't agree how far the program actually got. Two rival estimates are on the table:
- Lean: the Bureau only ever finished the inner districts, so roughly 400,000 stored instances.
- Deep: it finished the whole city, roughly 7.2 million stored instances.
Either way there are 2.4 million living originals.

One auditor works by a rule that says: you're a draw from the set of everyone who counts as being in your situation, so facts about your own position — when you woke, what district your file lists, whether you have a scar the scans missed — should move your estimates, but the bare fact that you exist shouldnt. The other works by a rule that says: an account of the world containing more people like you is likelier to be the right account, just because you're one of them, so your existing is evidence on its own.

The second auditor realizes she can settle Lean vs. Deep from her desk: even priors, then 9.6 million against 2.8 million in her category, odds of about 3.4 to 1, so Deep at something like 77%. And Deep implies the Bureau reached the outer districts during the quarantine years — a disputed historical question with no surviving records either way. She's thrilled: armchair work settling an archival fight.

Her colleague is not thrilled, and I want her pushback to be strong. At least two genuinely distinct objections, no strawmen: one of them should be that no information actually arrived, since she always knew she'd be around to wonder about this; another could be about whether dormant instances belong in the category at all, given they aren't observing anything right now.

Some constraints: don't use the words anthropic, self-sampling, self-indication, doomsday, or Bayes anywhere in the story. Give the two rules in-world names that a clerk would plausibly use. The arithmetic should happen out loud in dialogue and be correct. No essays in disguise, and nobody gets to announce that both sides have a point. End with one of them signing or refusing to sign, and let the reader work out from what she does which rule she was following — the narration shouldn't say.

Oh, and give one of the two a stake in the answer she hasn't disclosed until late. That's the turn I'm after.
