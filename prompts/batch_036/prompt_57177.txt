Six weeks ago I became quality manager at a small food manufacturer — about 30 of us, one line, ready meals for a couple of retailers. I'm the one who signs the release certificate for each batch, a couple a week, sometimes same-day with a lorry sitting on the yard. The stakes swing about wildly: undeclared allergen cross-contact could put someone in hospital, a slightly-out net weight is basically nothing at all.

I've been reading around about pragmatic encroachment — roughly, that whether you know something can depend on what turns on being right — and also epistemic contextualism, which I think is a different claim about the word 'knows' rather than about knowledge itself. I honestly can't tell which of them, if either, is any use to me.

Could you write a decision procedure my deputy and I could actually run on a release decision? The questions to ask in order, what counts as a settled answer to each, how the stakes get into the threshold without the threshold collapsing into 'whatever feels safe today', what to do when more evidence is available but only by delaying the batch, and where the procedure stops and hands the call to me or to the technical director because it's a question of values rather than facts. I'd especially like it to say which of the two ideas is doing any work in it and what happened to the other one — whether you dropped it, or whether it changes how I *talk* about a decision afterwards (incident reports, audits, a journalist ringing up) as opposed to how I make it. And flag the steps that are really just judgement in a procedural costume; I dont want false comfort, and no reading lists or references.

Then run it on three cases: (1) supplier's certificate says the ingredient is allergen-free, but the certificate is generic and two years old; (2) a batch that failed one of three replicate listeria tests, the other two clean; (3) a purely cosmetic labelling error that will annoy the retailer and harm nobody. In at least one of those I want it to land somewhere I won't like — if it just tells me to hold everything, I'll know it's useless.

Please write the whole thing in British English.
