Two labs I work with have spent a year arguing about whether a certain kind of effect counts as "real", and I've been asked to draft the conceptual part of a protocol for flagging when a dispute like that is verbal rather than substantive — roughly: would the two sides agree about every concrete case if they pinned their terms down, so that the fight is only about how the word gets used? Before I write the procedure I want the spec first. What does an account of verbal disputes actually have to deliver to be good enough for that job?

Give me the desiderata: the cases it has to come out right on (the ones everyone agrees are merely verbal, the ones everyone agrees are substantive, and the murky middle), the distinctions it can't collapse, and whatever it needs to be consistent with about how words get their meanings in the first place. For each one, the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a nice-to-have, and whether its non-negotiable or just the taste of one camp. I especially want to know if some subset can't all be satisfied at once — my hunch is that insisting the diagnosis be checkable by the disputants themselves, from inside the argument and without first settling contested meta semantics, sits badly with insisting it never beg questions against either side. Argue for that if you agree, or point me at the real pressure points if the tension lives somewhere else. No scorecard of existing views and no reading suggestions — just the list.

Exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two covering the requirement, the case motivating it, and the negotiable-or-not verdict. Nothing before or after the list.
