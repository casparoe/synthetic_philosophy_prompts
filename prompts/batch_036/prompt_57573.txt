I am preparing a short methods statement for a philosophy of mind reading group, in which I say that my work proceeds within the framework of heterophenomenology. Before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit of the commitments I actually hold, because I suspect that not all of them can stand together and I cannot tell which ones are doing the damage. They are:

1. The data are the subjects' utterances and other report behaviour. The theorist first constructs a world as it seems to the subject out of that material, and only afterwards asks which of the posits of the constructed world are real.
2. That bracketing is genuinely neutral: adopting the method commits one neither to realism nor to antirealism about phenomenal states, in the way that the anthropologist's suspension of belief commits her neither way about the gods of the people she studies.
3. I nonetheless accept the negative conclusion that the method's principal advocate draws from it: a good deal of the constructed world is unreal, and there are no ineffable intrinsic properties answering to some of the things subjects say about their experience.
4. There is no third-person test for the presence of a phenomenal property over and above report and report-guided behaviour; if there were, I would no longer be doing this method but something else.
5. In constructing the world I apply a principle of charity: I attribute to subjects beliefs that render their reports mostly true and mostly rational, since otherwise the material is not interpretable as a text at all.
6. Subjects are frequently wrong about the fine-grained character of their own experience. Change blindness, inattentional blindness, and the poverty of what people claim about the detail of their peripheral field all show that the world as it seems to them is richer than the world they actually have.
7. I want to extend the method to non-linguistic animals by treating reliable discriminative behaviour — a lever press, a gaze fixation, a matching-to-sample response — as a form of report, without requiring that the animals be trained on any symbol system.
8. The method is superior to introspectionist approaches precisely because it declines to grant the subject a privileged epistemic position.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely incompatible, which merely look incompatible, and, for each real incompatibility, what the cheapest revision is and what I would forfeit by making it. Rank the tensions by severity rather than reporting them as equally damaging, and end by identifying the single revision that would clean up the most. Assume I know the view well enough that I do not need it summarized; I want to know where my own package breaks.
