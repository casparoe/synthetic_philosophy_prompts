ok, background first: I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy training, I just read this stuff late at night. At work I own a watchdog process — it polls a heartbeat coming off a worker task and force-kills/restarts the worker if the heartbeat goes stale. The thing that always bites you is the race window: poll interval plus restart latency, so a worker can do a bunch of stuff between checks that the watchdog never sees. I was reading about Frankfurt-style cases and the principle of alternate possibilties and it struck me that the intervener in those stories (Black? whatever he's called) is basically a watchdog with a poll interval of zero and zero latency, and nobody ever stops to justify that.

So here's what I want. You're a professional philosopher who works on free will and moral responsibility and who can also read a systems design doc without wincing. Think carefully about this one. Before you write the scenario, spend a few lines brainstorming out loud about what a *nonzero* poll interval does to the case — does it help or hurt the person who's attacking PAP? I want to see you consider both directions before you commit.

Then build me a case to these specs:

1. Ordinary workplace setting. No trolleys, no runaway anything, no life-and-death. A regular person making a regular morally bad call — the kind of thing where you'd obviously blame them if nobody were interfering with them at all.
2. There's an intervener that guarantees the outcome, but it has to be an actual mechanism, not an all-knowing demon. It polls some signal, it has a defined trigger condition, and there's a nonzero delay between trigger and when its takeover actually lands. Spell it out like a design doc: what it reads, how often, what fires the intervention, how long until the intervention takes effect. Chip in the brain, external rig, whatever — I don't care. But no foreknowledge and no magic.
3. The thing it polls cannot be a prior sign of the decision. It has to be reading something at or after the moment of deciding. I want the easy deterministic version closed off.
4. Despite 2 and 3, the agent still can't do otherwise in whatever sense actually matters for the debate.
5. The scenario itself should be one or two paragraphs, max. Analysis comes after, separately.
