Second year philosophy of science PhD here, and I'm locked in a recurring argument with my flatmate, who does condensed matter theory — he's the one who first put me onto Anderson's "More Is Different", so he behaves as though he owns the phrase. The setup: I've been writing a chapter on intertheoretic reduction, roughly asking whether a Nagel-style account (connectability of the terms plus derivability of the laws) can handle the standard hard cases like thermodynamics-to-statistical mechanics, and every time I suggest that derivability might actually be enough to count as reducing one theory to another, he says "more is different" and changes the subject. Last week it was about phase transitions: he reckoned that since you need the thermodynamic limit to get a genuine singularity, no finite-N Hamiltonian can give you the phase transtion, so the higher level has something the lower level literally cannot have, and that's an end to reduction. I said that's an argument about limits and idealisations rather than about emergence at all, and he said I was being a lawyer.

Can you give me the menu of precise claims that one slogan can be standing for, rather than picking a favourite reading and glossing it at length? For each reading I want: the claim stated properly with its quantifiers visible, so I can tell whether it's about properties, about laws, about explanations, or about there being new fundamental stuff in the world; whether it's ontological or epistemic/pragmatic; whether it's compatible with supervenience on the microstate and with the causal closure of physics; and a verdict — platitudinous, substantive and probably right, substantive and genuinely contentious, or false. Then tell me which of the readings would actually support the thermodynamic-limit argument he was making (I suspect none of them do without extra premises about idealisation, but argue it), and which ones are perfectly consistent with a Nagelian reduction going through, so I know how much of his case is just a dislike of derivations.

A few readings I've already got, which you should feel free to correct, split, or fold into something better: (i) the trivial one that a whole has properties its parts lack, wetness being the usual example; (ii) multiple realisability, so special-science kinds don't line up with microphysical kinds; (iii) something about new effective laws carrying their own constants, "equally fundamental" but not derivable from the smaller scale; and (iv) strong emergence with downward causation. I'm fairly confident (iii) and (iv) are being run together in his head, and that (ii) does no work against Nagel whatsoever, since connectability was never meant to be one-to-one identity of predicates. One more thing that's been nagging me: does "more" in the slogan mean sheer number of constituents, or complexity/organisation? Sort all of this into something I can take to him over Sunday lunch, and don't be kind about it — point out where my own reconstruction of his argument is weakest, including whether the thermodynamic-limit business really is as easy to wave away as I think it is.
