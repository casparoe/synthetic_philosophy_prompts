Six months ago I bought into a small housing cooperative — forty-odd units, a volunteer board, and a set of bylaws I have since read more closely than I expected ever to read anything. I work in insurance pricing, and the habit of my trade is to state a rule precisely enough that you can tell when it has been broken, which is why this has got under my skin. The clause that sold me on the place says that each member has sole and final discretion over what happens inside their own unit, and that the board may neither enter nor regulate it. I wanted a home where I could hang a tapestry in the window or run a seminar at my dining table without asking permission. In the months since, I have watched that clause be reduced to nothing, and the part I cannot articulate is that nobody ever violated it — not once. The board reclassifies instead. A member who tutors two evenings a week was informed that tutoring is "commercial use," which falls outside interior discretion because it brings non-members into the building; defensible, I thought. The same reasoning was then applied to a book club of six, and then to a member whose adult son stays over more than fourteen nights a quarter, on the grounds that she was lodging him. Separately, the board created an "exterior visibility" standard reaching anything a passer-by could see through a window, and rather than ordering the tapestry down it imposed a monthly surcharge on members whose interiors fail the standard — so the discretion is intact, it simply costs forty dollars to exercise. Most recently it added a procedural rule: a member wishing to invoke interior discretion must first obtain a written determination from the board that the matter in question is in fact interior. The right to decide has become the right to ask the decider whether you may decide.

I keep hunting for the concept this instantiates and coming up empty. The nearest result I know by name is Sen's liberal paradox, but that concerns two people whose protected choices collide and a majority that objects to both; here nothing collides. There is one member, one sphere, and a collective that never touches the contents of the sphere while steadily redrawing its outline. "Moving the goalposts" and "death by a thousand cuts" capture the texture but not the structure. Mill's distinction between conduct that concerns only oneself and conduct that concerns others is in the background, and I am aware of the standard objection that almost nothing is purely self-regarding — but my objection is not that the line is hard to draw. It is that the parties with an interest in where the line falls are the ones drawing it, case by case, without ever having to announce a general principle or bear the cost of a formal amendment. What I suspect, and cannot state properly, is that the bylaws handed me something structurally incomplete: an entitlement whose scope remains up for grabs, with nothing in the document protecting it from being redefined rather than overridden.

So: does this have a name? I would like the established term if one exists, or failing that the nearest established terms together with an account of how my case differs from each — including whether the same structure turns up elsewhere, in the power of a court to rule on its own jurisdiction, say, or in amendment procedures that can reach the clauses protecting rights, or wherever political and legal theory discusses who gets to settle what counts as a private matter. I am not after a bare label; I want the carving. What does the phenomenon consist in, what is it not, and where are its edges? It is plainly not simple rights violation, and plainly not mere vagueness in drafting, since the wording could be made perfectly precise and still be reclassifiable. Is a single open reclassification adopted by a legitimate vote the same thing as an accretion of quiet ones? Does the analysis change if the reclassification genuinely prevents harm to other members? Can a protected sphere be protected at all without also protecting somebody's authority to say what falls inside it? If no established concept covers this, I would much rather you said so plainly and proposed a term with a definition and boundary conditions than see you stretch something adjacent to fit.
