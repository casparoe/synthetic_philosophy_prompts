A friend and I have just spent two hours on coherent extrapolated volition, arguing about whether the coherence half of the procedure does any independent work. His position: inconsistency among a person's desires is always a residue of ignorance, haste, or passion, so a fully extrapolated agent — one who knows everything relevant, has had time to think, and is more the person she wishes she were — is already coherent, which makes the coherence step redundant. I maintain it can bite independently. Please build the case that would decide between us, to the following specification.

One realistic scenario, in a single paragraph, in which a woman facing a decision she cannot defer holds two desires that conflict in the sense that no available option satisfies both. Constraints: no AI, uploading, or neurotechnology — the extrapolation is carried out by a patient human adviser who discloses everything relevant and gives her years to reflect; the conflict must bear on one choice; and at least one of the two desires must survive complete information and rest on no false belief. You may not generate the conflict out of incommensurability, vagueness, or desires about her own earlier state.

Then demonstrate that the design works. State what the extrapolation alone recommends, what a minimal-revision coherence procedure recommends — and which revision rule you are using — and why the two come apart. Identify the feature of the case that does the work, say what the verdict becomes if that feature is removed, and give my friend's strongest attack on the case along with whether it succeeds. If the case cannot be built under these constraints, say so, and identify precisely which constraint does the blocking.
