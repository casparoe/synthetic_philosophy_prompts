I am in the third year of a philosophy-of-science PhD, writing a dissertation chapter that appraises two rival traditions in a mid-century research area using Lakatos's framework, and I have run into a difficulty that I cannot resolve from the standard secondary material. The difficulty concerns how one is supposed to keep the ledger when a programme acquires content from elsewhere. As I understand the appraisal procedure, a research programme is theoretically progressive if a successive theory in the series has excess empirical content over its predecessor — that is, if it predicts some novel fact — and empirically progressive if some of that novel content is actually corroborated; a programme degenerates when its modifications produce no such content, or produce it without corroboration, and Lakatosians are supposed to be alert to immunizing stratagems and conventionalist reinterpretations that keep the hard core safe while adding nothing testable. My problem is that this machinery seems to have no slot for the *provenance* of the corroborated content, and in my case material the provenance is doing all the interesting work.

What I keep finding goes like this. A programme is in evident trouble: its positive heuristic is exhausted, its protective-belt adjustments are transparently defensive, and its proponents have taken to reinterpreting earlier successes. Then it takes over, more or less wholesale, a formalism or a family of experimental paradigms that a rival programme had developed and had already corroborated on its own account. Once the borrowed machinery is installed, the first programme starts producing a run of successful novel predictions — novel with respect to it, and corroborated, so on the official criteria it looks progressive again — and its defenders cite that run as vindication of its hard core. Yet nothing in the hard core suggested those predictions, and the facts, together with the evidence for them, were in hand before the annexation; the core supplied, at most, a redescription of what the donor had already established. I have two instances, one from my own case material and one from further afield. In the further-afield case, a tradition of thinking about selection above the level of the individual organism was widely treated as degenerating after the middle of the twentieth century, and then, so far as I can follow the technical debate, largely absorbed the mathematical apparatus that its rivals had built for handling relatedness and the effects of genes on social behaviour — an apparatus that some participants in that debate argue is intertranslatable with its own. In my own case, a programme committed to a rich innate endowment in the domain I study stagnated for roughly two decades, and then began reporting a steady stream of successful predictions after importing statistical learning methods and measurement techniques that had been developed by, and were corroborated within, the empiricist programme it had opposed. There is also a third, weaker variant I do not know whether to fold in: cases where a programme's sudden run of successes follows not from annexing a rival theory but from a general improvement in instruments or in shared methodological practice, which raises the same ledger question without any rival theory to annex from.

So: is there an established name for this, and a literature that treats it? I want more than a label, because the point of my chapter is the exact shape of the thing and where its boundaries lie. I have worked through the obvious neighbours and none seems to be it — an immunizing or conventionalist stratagem is defensive and adds no testable content, whereas here the content is real, novel, and corroborated; consilience, as I understand it, concerns a single theory extending over previously disconnected classes of phenomena, whereas here the extension is purchased with another theory's own results and may amount to redescription rather than reach; intertheoretic reduction and its variants concern the relations between theories rather than the credit a programme earns; Kuhn's account of normal science involves puzzle-solving within a tradition but does not, as far as I can see, model the case where the puzzles and their solutions are imported from a rival; and Laudan's problem-solving ledgers, which count conceptual and empirical problems solved, look vulnerable in the same way mine is, since they too appear indifferent to who generated the solution. I also take it that Lakatosians insist on distinguishing facts that guided a theory's construction from those that did not, precisely to prevent this kind of free riding — but that distinction is defined relative to a single theory and its own history, and it does not obviously carry over to a transfer between programmes. Please tell me, then: (a) whether any established concept covers what I have described, and for each of the closest candidates, exactly how the described phenomenon differs from it; (b) whether the phenomenon is best understood as a vice of scientific communities — a kind of opportunism — or as a blind spot in the appraisal metric itself, and whether those two diagnoses can come apart, since a metric that cannot see provenance will score an opportunist and a genuinely fertile programme identically; (c) whether Lakatos's criteria can be amended to register provenance without collapsing into the requirement that every successful prediction be traceable to the hard core, which no programme in the history of science could satisfy; and (d) if nothing established fits, please coin and define a term for it, giving necessary and sufficient conditions and settling at least these boundary cases — annexation that the core's own positive heuristic directed the programme to undertake, so that the borrowing is itself an exercise of heuristic power; importation of vocabulary only, with the content independently re-derived; cases where donor and borrower turn out to be intertranslatable, so that there is arguably nothing to annex and the appearance of progress is bookkeeping; the instrument-driven variant above, and whether it belongs under the same concept or requires a separate one; and simultaneous mutual annexation between two programmes, where each books the other's successes. Finally, I would like some guidance on what evidence in a historical case would distinguish genuine core-generated progress from the imported kind, since my chapter has to make that call about real people's real results and I do not want to rest it on my own hindsight.
