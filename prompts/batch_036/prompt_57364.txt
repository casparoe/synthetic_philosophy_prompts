I'm a quantitative psychologist with roughly fifteen years of applied scale development behind me, mostly in health-outcome measurement, and I think natively in the apparatus of latent-variable modeling: extraction, rotation, cross-loadings, factor-score indeterminacy, measurement invariance across groups and occasions, and the construct-validity tradition's insistence that validity is a continuing evidential argument about an interpretation rather than a property an instrument possesses. I'm also a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, from the Sequences through the current alignment-adjacent material, and for a couple of years I've been following the neural-network interpretability discussion as an interested outsider. As I understand it, one camp holds that trained networks compute over features rather than over the neurons we can observe, that many more features are packed into an activation space than there are dimensions, that single neurons are polysemantic while features are monosemantic, and that one can recover something like a sparse dictionary of features by fitting an appropriately regularized decomposition to activations. The other camp replies that a feature is just whatever direction in activation space a chosen method happens to pick out, that a probe can be satisfied by directions that play no part in the model's actual computation, that the choice of decomposition is underdetermined by the observable data, and therefore that realism about features is a mood rather than a thesis. I want to know whether that disagreement is substantive, and I have a strong suspicion that it isn't, which is exactly why I don't trust myself to settle it from inside my own framework.

So here is the task. Restate the debate, both camps and any middle position you think is being crowded out by the two-sided framing, in my working vocabulary, as faithfully as you can manage, and then tell me what failed to survive the trip. Concretely: what in feature realism amounts to a claim about a factor solution as such, what amounts to a claim about rotation and about the orthogonal-versus-oblique choice, what corresponds to factor-score indeterminacy, what corresponds to the exploratory/confirmatory distinction, what corresponds to invariance testing, and what corresponds to the validity-as-argument tradition. Where a mapping is imperfect, say precisely how it is imperfect and what you had to stretch. And please be careful not to launder: if a term fits only because you have already granted what is in dispute — if, say, feature translates smoothly only on the assumption that there is a fact of the matter about a system's latent structure independent of the decomposition applied to it — flag that move openly rather than presenting it as translation. I would much rather be legibly wrong than flatteringly agreed with.

The deliverable I want is a residue report, not a survey. Sort the central claims of both camps into three buckets: those that translate cleanly into psychometric language, those that translate only with a distortion you should describe, and those that have no counterpart at all in a framework built entirely on covariance among observed indicators. For everything in the third bucket, and for the worst cases in the second, give me a verdict on the residue: is the gap an expressive limitation of measurement theory, something my discipline simply cannot say because it never had to, or does it reveal that the interpretability claim was less determinate than it sounded — that a statement of the form the model represents X was never about the world at all but a report on an analyst's chosen decomposition? Name also the single place where the features-are-factors analogy is most seductive and most wrong; I expect there is one, and I expect I'm standing in it. Then close the loop on my own prior. I arrived believing that feature realism is rotation-indeterminacy denialism in costume: that the realists are people who have never had to defend a rotated solution to a hostile methods reviewer, and that these features are genuinely present is a way of saying I find this rotation pleasing. Tell me plainly whether that survives the exercise. If it does not, identify the specific element of the interpretability enterprise my framework has no way to capture, and say whether that element is doing explanatory work or merely rhetorical work. Briefly, at the very end, I'd also like the reverse transfer: any pressure from interpretability practice that ought to make a measurement theorist uneasy about her own unexamined realism. No reading list or citations, please — I care about the argument, not its provenance.
