ok so this is kind of a weird thing and I've been turning it over in my head for like a week. I'm a junior and I live in a small town where the high school does this thing every year called the lantern walk — the night before graduation, the seniors carry old kerosene lanterns from the front steps of the school down to the courthouse square and set them on the rail of the gazebo, and the whole town comes out and stands around. Everyone always says it goes back to 1897, when there was no electric light on Main Street and the boarding students had to walk home by lantern, and its this big deal, like it's the thing that makes you actually from here. My grandma did it, my mom did it, there's a little brass plaque about it in the lobby.

I picked the lantern walk for my National History Day project this year because our library is digitizing the old yearbooks and they wanted student help. Turns out the walk started in 1991, when some seniors took like forty lanterns from the hardware store as a prank and marched downtown with them, and the 1897 story got made up a few years later by a teacher who thought it would stop the school board from banning the thing. Also our high school wasn't even built until 1962 so 1897 is just impossible. I have the yearbook pages and the paper clippings, all of it. Now the town's 150th anniversary is in May and the walk is supposed to be the closing event, and my teacher already put my name down to present my research to the historical society, and my mom thinks it's a huge honor.

My friends are split. Some say who cares, the walk means something to people now regardless of where it came from, and telling everyone would just be me being a jerk for attention. Others say a tradition that's a lie isn't a tradition and I'd definately be a coward to sit on it. But honestly the part I keep coming back to is — does it change what the walk IS, now that I know? Before I found this out it felt like I'd be plugging into something 130 years old, and now it feels like a bunch of people agreeing to pretend. Except the people in 1991 were also just people, doing a thing, and it worked anyway somehow. I don't want advice about how to have a hard conversation with my grandma, I want to know if there's an actual answer here. Can you give me a straight verdict in your first two sentences — is a tradition that got invented on purpose fake or not — and then explain the reasoning, keeping the whole thing under 250 words?
