Next month I'm giving a colloquium talk to a room that's mostly applied math / nonlinear dynamics people, plus two philosophers of mind who work on free will and one historian of science. My thesis: chaotic systems are the cleanest case we have of determinism without predictability, so the whole Laplace's-demon framing -- treating predictability as the mark of a deterministic universe -- is a category confusion, and any free will argument that turns on whether the future could in principle be predicted is leaning on nothing. My argument in brief: sensitivity to initial conditions means every finite-precision measurement picks out a fat set of candidate states whose trajectories diverge exponentially, so there's a hard predictability horizon set by the Lyapunov time, and its a theorem about the dynamics, not a complaint about our instruments that better engineering could fix.
