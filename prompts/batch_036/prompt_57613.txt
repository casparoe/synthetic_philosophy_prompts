I'm in the third year of a philosophy PhD working on the ethics of infinite populations, and I co-run a reading group with two senior colleagues who have been circling the same dispute for a decade. Please draft the joint statement they ought to be able to produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The extensionist holds that many pairs of worlds containing infinitely many people do have a determinate betterness ordering, delivered by some mathematically well-behaved extension of finite-utilitarian comparison — an overtaking rule, a discounted sum with small positive rate, an ultrafilter- or hyperreal-valued comparison — and that the known impossibility results show only that Pareto, anonymity, and various regularity conditions cannot be had jointly, not that the project is bankrupt. The quietist holds that every such extension smuggles in an arbitrary parameter (a discount rate, a choice of free ultrafilter, an indexing of persons) that does no ethical work, that non-conglomerability leaves the verdicts untrustworthy even where they exist, and that the honest position is that most infinite comparisons are undefined or incomparable, with finite-domain reasoning plus robustness across candidate extensions carrying the normative weight.

The statement should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — about the mathematics, about which desiderata conflict, and about what any extension must give up; (2) the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording, with any component that turns out to be merely verbal identified and dissolved; (3) the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions whose settlement would actually move at least one of them.

Anchor the cruxes in two cases both accept as decisive. World W gives every person welfare 1; world W⁻ is identical except that person #1 gets 0. World G gives the n-th person welfare 2ⁿ; world C gives everyone welfare 10¹⁰⁰. For each case, record each camp's ranking and the reasoning behind it. Then — the part I most want — have each camp state in advance its prediction about the finite analogues (N = 10⁶ people, everything else held fixed), and specify what the relationship between the finite verdict and the infinite one would have to look like for that camp to revise its position.
