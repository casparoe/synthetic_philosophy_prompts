I'm in a grad seminar and next week I have to open the discussion on the paradox of deontology — the thing where the consequentialist says if killing is bad, then killing one person to stop five killings must be even worse, so refusing to do it looks bizarrely irrational. Last week when someone in the room tried to press that exact point, our professor just waved it off with 'the ends don't justify the means' and moved on, and half the class nodded like that was an answer. It doesn't feel like one to me, but I can't say why — mostly I suspect the sentence just restates the thing under dispute in more confident clothing.

So can you take that slogan apart into the separate claims it could be making? I want the whole spread: the readings that are disguised tautologies, the ones that are genuinely substantive and could be defended, the ones that are flat false, and the ones a consequentialist should happily sign onto because they're not actually about the disagreement at all. For each, give me the precisified version — what is 'the ends' picking out (intended vs merely foreseen? the whole outcome including my act, or the state of affairs downstream of it?), what are 'the means' (the act, the violation, the intention?), and what work is 'justify' doing (making it permissible? cancelling the blame? licensing an excuse while the act stays wrong? making the whole thing good all-things-considered but still something you should feel awful about?). Then tell me which of those readings, if any, would actually answer the five-killings objection instead of begging the question against it.

Format please, in this order:

- The setup — 3 or 4 sentences, spelling out what the paradox has to be assuming for the slogan to even look like a reply to it
- The menu — the main event, around 600 words, numbered readings, each with a short name, the precisified claim in a sentence or two, and a verdict tag (platitude / substantive / false / not-in-dispute / incoherent)
- Which one my prof needed — ~150 words on which reading was load-bearing in that exchange and whether it can carry the weight
- My line for Thursday — 3 or 4 sentences I could say out loud in seminar without sounding like I'm grandstanding
