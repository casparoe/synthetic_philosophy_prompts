I run a monthly ethics reading group and I want to build a "where do you actually land on moral testimony?" quiz — the kind that sorts people by their reactions to cases rather than by whatever label they picked up somewhere. Eight to ten items, each a concrete little scenario with two or three answer options, and none of them allowed to just ask whether you think testimony can give you moral knowledge. Then a scoring key.

The key thing I want is that scoring runs on two axes at once instead of one: can accepting moral testimony give you knowledge or understanding at all, and separately, is deferring ever the right or even the admirable thing to do. So the key should drop people into cells of a grid, each cell mapped onto a named position if one exists — name it if it does, describe it in a line if it doesn't, and don't give me a reading list or citations for any of it.

Make sure these are in there: someone who believes on their partner's word that eating meat is wrong but has no idea why and can't defend it; a case where the testifier is a close friend and the question at issue is about how you yourself are being treated; a case where you have the testimony but also a vivid first-person pull the other way; and a case where you act on the testimony without actually believing it. That last one should be a real discriminator — say which cells its supposed to split.

Then finish with: which pairs of cells the quiz can't cleanly separate (say so rather than fudging it), any cell that comes out empty, and a one-line diagnosis of why — is that combination incoherent, just unattractive, or actually unexplored.
