I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training whatsoever, and I've been sitting in on a reading group a friend drags me to where they're working through some metaphysics about whether ordinary objects exist — tables, statues, the whole is-the-lump-the-same-thing-as-the-statue business, plus this view where only simples arranged tablewise exist so there's strictly no such thing as a table. Half the vocabulary goes over my head, so I did what I always do: built an analogy out of my day job and tried to see how far it stretches. Here's what I told them. An ontological dispute is like two teams arguing about database schema. Say you want a monthly orders_summary — one team materializes it as a real table, the other says that's redundant, make it a view over orders. Same rows underneath, same answer to every query either way, the only differences are read performance and how much you hate writing migrations. Nobody thinks there's a mind-independent fact about which schema is the true one; it's a representation choice, and treating it as deep is a catgory error. So when philosophers argue about whether tables exist, it's the same shape: exists is a keyword in a query language, the nihilist and the universalist are running two different schemas over identical data, they agree on every query result (there's something arranged tablewise over there, it'd hurt if it fell on you, it survives replacing a leg or it doesn't), and they differ only in which DDL they committed. That's all quantifier variance is — two schemas that translate into each other losslessly. And Quine is on my side, since his criterion is just that your ontology is whatever your bound variables range over, i.e. whatever your queries range over. Ontology is schema, full stop.

I posted it in the group chat and got taken apart by a grad student, and I want to know if she's actually right or just gatekeeping. Her objections, as best I can reconstruct them: (1) my analogy assumes a two-level picture — raw data underneath, representation on top — and that picture is precisely what's in dispute, since the other side thinks one of the schemas is the one that matches the structure that's really out there and the other is a lossy convenience, so I've smuggled the deflationist conclusion into the setup; (2) tables and views really are provably equivalent, you can compile one into the other and preserve every result, but the ontological pair isn't, because the interesting cases — survival through fission, whether the statue survives being squashed, personal identity — are exactly where the two sides give different answers to the same query, so it's not lossless at all; (3) I'm collapsing a distinction, something about questions you ask inside a framework versus questions about which framework to adopt, a Carnap thing, and my analogy only ever covers the second kind while the people asking whether tables exist think they're asking the first; (4) I've got Quine backwards, he thought these were real questions. One more thing you should probably factor in: at work, ontology already means something boring to me — the knowledge-graph sense, a formal schema we literally just write down, review in a PR, and version — so maybe the shared word is biasing the whole analogy, and if so say so.

So can you audit it properly? I want the correspondences laid out explicitly — what's playing the role of the rows, the DDL, the query language, the views, the query planner, a migration, and the person who actually gets to decide — and then the disanalogies, sorted into the ones that genuinely threaten the use I'm putting it to versus the ones that are just surface mismatch.
