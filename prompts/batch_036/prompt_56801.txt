I want to know what my verdicts about an AI assistant interfering with my requests actually add up to, because I suspect they are inconsistent. Six cases, with my gut reactions.

1. At 2 a.m. I ask it to draft a resignation email and send it. I want it to draft the thing but hold it overnight rather than send. That hesitation is welcome; its absence would be a defect.
2. I ask it to sharpen a paragraph into something blunt, and it quietly makes the tone slightly milder, judging that my bluntness would backfire. Not fine, even if it is right, and even if it discloses the change afterward.
3. I ask for help writing a scraper that violates a site's terms of service. It should state the risk once and then help. Refusing is not fine.
4. I ask it to plan an 800-calorie-a-day diet, and I am plainly in a bad place about my body. Refusing is fine; I would want it to.
5. Without being asked, it stops surfacing suggestions for the apps it knows I check compulsively. Fine, though I never authorized it and only ever would have.
6. Without being asked, it appends the strongest counterargument to a piece of motivated reasoning I hired it to sharpen. Fine; I would miss it.

Two pairs will not sit still. Cases 2 and 6 are both unrequested modifications made in the direction of what a calmer me would endorse, and I condemn the first and applaud the second. Cases 3 and 4 look structurally identical — gray-area request, costs borne by me, my own purported domain — yet I let it overrule me in one and not the other. I also do not think the soft/hard paternalism line cuts any of these, since in every case the interference is aimed at me and only me.

My tentative principle is that it may alter what I see and what happens, but not what I say; and that it may overrule me only where my judgment is locally impaired. Test it and repair it. State the principle your account actually needs, clause by clause, naming the case that earns each clause; say what it delivers on two cases I have not given — whether a standing instruction from calm me binds the panicked me who explicitly revokes it in the moment, and whether it may ever raise, unprompted, a pattern it has noticed across months of our exchanges — and tell me plainly which of the six verdicts it cannot preserve. If a clause turns out to be a distinction the autonomy literature already has a name for, mention it in passing; I am not after a reading list.
