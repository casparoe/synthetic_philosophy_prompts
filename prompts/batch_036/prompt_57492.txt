I am a second-year doctoral student in the philosophy of cognitive science, and our department has been asked to supply a single fifty-minute session to the induction week of an MSc in applied animal behaviour and welfare. The students are mostly veterinarians and zoologists with no philosophy behind them, and the taxonomy that distinguishes Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures is going to be used as scaffolding throughout their welfare-ethics module, so they will be applying it within weeks to judgements about which animals they are willing to say suffer. I want a plan for how to explain it, not the explanation itself.

Please address the following, in order.

First, the dependency structure: what has to be in place before each of the four categories can be introduced, and specifically whether the Popperian level can be presented at all without first committing the room to mental representation and to content. If it cannot, tell me what minimal version of those notions will serve an audience that will otherwise hear representation as mental imagery.

Second, direction of travel. Should I ascend from reflex and instinct up to culturally augmented cognition, which risks making the ordering feel like a natural progression of worth, or should I begin from the human, artifact-saturated case — the only one over which they have any first-person authority — and work downward? Say which you recommend and what it costs.

Third, the carrying case. I had intended four parallel vignettes: a web-building spider, a rat in a maze, a food-caching corvid, and a person consulting a tide table. I now suspect that four separate animals invites exactly the ranking of species I want to avoid, and that the real point is that each later competence is built on and retains the earlier ones. Tell me whether one animal can carry all four levels, or whether the comparison must run across species, and what the case has to exhibit and has to conspicuously lack.

Fourth, the misconceptions, each with the point in the sequence where it should be intercepted rather than corrected afterwards: that the four kinds form a ladder of value or of degree of consciousness; that Gregorian means clever or well-schooled rather than dependent on externally sourced artifacts; that the categories attach to species rather than to capacities that may be patchily distributed; that Skinnerian creatures merely respond while Popperian ones genuinely think; and the student who asks, ten minutes in, whether a large language model is Gregorian.

Fifth, one closing task that would separate a student who has grasped why the categories nest from one who can recite four labels, together with what a deficient but plausible-sounding answer would look like.

Finally, state what I should openly decline to cover at this level — I assume the intentional stance as such, and any general theory of consciousness — and how to refuse it without appearing to concede that the taxonomy rests on nothing more than speculation.
