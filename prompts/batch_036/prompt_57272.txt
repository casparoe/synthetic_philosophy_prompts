In September the last known individual of what was for seventy years called the Kettle Spring dace died in a holding tank at a state fish hatchery. I have been asked to give the address at the small public ceremony marking it, and I would like you to write it. Everything below matters, because the speech is difficult only because of the details.

**The fish.** It lived in one spring-fed creek. It was described as a full species in 1954 from a holotype that now sits in a jar in a museum four hundred miles from the hatchery. In 1998 I published the revision that lumped it. My argument was that it is a spring-dwelling form of a widespread and entirely secure relative; that the narrow body, reduced pigmentation and shortened snout are plastic responses to permanent cold clear water which the relative also produces wherever it encounters the same conditions; and that its mitochondrial sequences sit nested inside the relative's variation rather than forming a branch of their own. My revision removed its protected status. Over the following two decades a water district's pumping lowered the spring, and a diversion brought the widespread relative into the creek. Two years ago a re-study using genome-wide data split it again; the authors were gracious enough to write that my morphology was right and my genealogy was underpowered. I am eighty-one and I still think the 1998 paper was defensible on the evidence available to me.

**What is left.** There are sixty-odd fish of uncertain parentage in the hatchery's breeding shed, frozen gametes from four individuals, and a side channel a mile downstream containing what the technicians call the dacey things. Her body is already in fixative, and it will be deposited as a voucher in the same collection as the holotype. For twenty-two years the staff kept a paper pedigree chart on the wall of the shed; the last entry is hers, Tank 4, no mate listed.

**The three accounts of what happened, which do not agree.** On the account that makes reproductive isolation constitutive, my 1998 paper was probably correct, and there is no species here to mourn — only a population of a flourishing fish, and fertile hybrids now occupying the creek. On the account that makes a species the smallest diagnosable separately evolving lineage, what died ended considerably earlier than September: diagnosability went when the hybrids outnumbered the pure fish, roughly a decade ago, and the ceremony is for a boundary that was crossed unnoticed. On the account that denies species are kinds at all and treats them instead as individuals — scattered, historically bounded things, closer to a single long-lived organism than to a category — extinction is a death rather than the disappearance of a type, it has a date, and the date is in September, in a tank. That is also the only account on which the fact that her body is going into a jar is a strange fact rather than a routine one.

**The room.** The two authors of the re-split, who will hear every taxonomic verb I use. A former student of mine who holds the reproductive-isolation view and thinks I was right in 1998 and am being sentimental now. The hatchery technicians, who will experience any technicality as an insult to twenty-two years of their lives. Two water district commissioners, who have said plainly that they would like the address to establish that no species was lost. A state legislator. A reporter from the county paper, who will put whichever noun I use into the headline. My daughter.

Please write the address, six to seven minutes when spoken. It must not resolve the disagreement about what a species is, because I have not resolved it; but it must not use that as cover either, and it must not hand the commissioners the conclusion they want. It has to be hearable by the technicians as honor rather than bookkeeping, and by the two systematists as precision rather than evasion. It has to acknowledge my 1998 paper without becoming either an apology or a defense. And the metaphysics must be load-bearing rather than ornamental: the disagreement about what a species is determines what I am permitted to say this loss was, so I want that disagreement felt by people who have never heard of it, without once being explained to them as a disagreement. No list of concepts read aloud; no sentence that only a philosopher would recognize as doing something.

After the address, add a note of about three hundred words identifying the four or five sentences that carry this weight. For each, say which of the three accounts licenses it, which forbids it, and which reading the county paper is likeliest to take from it — including whether any of them are ones I could have said only because I was the lumper.
