I sit on the amnesty panel of a truth commission in a country two years past a military regime, and next month we take up an application that has already split the panel two–two in private discussion. I would like an outside view reasoned from our own record rather than from what other commissions have done.

Our enabling statute sets three conditions: full disclosure of the relevant facts, an act associated with a political objective, and proportionality between the act and that objective. Our published determinations to date are these.

1. A conscript who shot two unarmed villagers at a roadblock on his captain's order: granted. The panel noted that he acted within a politically directed chain of command.
2. A member of an armed opposition movement who ambushed a military convoy, killing three soldiers: granted. No reasons given beyond the statutory conditions.
3. An intelligence officer who tortured detainees over four years: granted for the sessions he described in detail, refused for the remainder. The refusal cited incomplete disclosure only.
4. A provincial governor who signed detention authorizations and curfew orders but personally harmed no one: refused. Reasons given — his acts were too remote from any identifiable injury for the panel to assess proportionality.
5. A police informer who passed names to an operative, one of those named subsequently disappearing: granted. The panel noted that his disclosure identified both the operatives and the detention site.
6. A soldier who looted and burned a house during a sweep and kept the proceeds: refused. Personal gain, no political objective.
7. A woman who sheltered two fugitives and lied to soldiers at her door: granted.

The new application. The applicant, now seventy-eight, was the registry clerk at a detention centre. For eleven months he altered intake and transfer logs so that detainees who died in custody were recorded as transferred to another facility. He acted on the standing instruction of the facility commander, and the alterations were timed to an international monitoring visit. He has disclosed everything, produced his own surviving copies of the original entries, and is the only living person who can say where the remains of nineteen of the dead were buried. Three features complicate the matter. First, his disclosure is complete and already in our hands before any decision has been taken. Second, at least one of the men he logged as transferred did not die in custody and has never been accounted for since, so the falsification produced a disappearance whose legal status is still unresolved and may be unresolved for as long as the altered record stands. Third, several families have told us that the concealment, not the death, is the wrong they want this commission to name.

What I need from you:

- The candidate rules that are jointly consistent with determinations 1 through 7. Give at least three, state each explicitly enough to be falsifiable against the record, and say what each predicts here.
- Which determination is the closest analog, and which of its features you are treating as load-bearing rather than incidental. If you think 4 and 5 pull in opposite directions on this application, say which the panel should follow and why.
- Your recommendation, in reasoning we could actually put into the determination. Address in particular whether the fact that the informational value of his disclosure has already been realized removes the usual rationale for granting — or whether that rationale was never about this applicant but about the next one.
- What principle the panel would be committing itself to by granting, and what by refusing. In each case include one consequence, for a class of applications we have not yet received, that the panel would find unwelcome.
- The further application that would most cleanly discriminate between your leading candidate rules.
- An honest verdict on whether 1 through 7 are consistent with any single rule at all. If they are not, identify the outlier and say whether we should treat it as wrongly decided or as evidence that our three criteria are doing something other than what we suppose.

No citations or comparative material, please; I will handle the legal apparatus myself.
