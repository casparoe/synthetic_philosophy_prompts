ok so for media studies I have to write about Free Guy and everyone in my class is doing the 'is Guy actually conscious' essay, which I find boring. I want to do the metaphysics side instead, and there's a distinction I keep tripping over. When Antwan goes at the server racks with the axe and Buddy gets erased, is the film saying a real thing was destroyed, or just a load of data that only ever pretended to be a person? Because a virtual sword seems genuinely real in one sense — it exists, you can own it, someone can delete it, it's made of actual stuff on actual servers — but only fictionally a sword. Does the film keep those two senses of real seperate, or does it slide between them depending on how big it needs the stakes to be?

Second thing, the reboot scene. Antwan wipes Guy's memories, Millie is properly gutted, and then a kiss puts him back — which sort of assumes the bad thing about deleting someone is losing their history rather than them ceasing to exist, since he's still running afterwards. Is the film actually committed to that, or is the kiss just a romcom beat it doesn't mean? And then at the end the NPCs get moved into a new game, which feels like the film admitting they were data all along and that a copy is as good as the original, but it plays it as a rescue.

Give me a reading that takes both the film and the philosophy seriously and tells me where the film resists the reading — I'd rather have an honest 'this scene doesn't support that' than something tidy. And please be clear about which bits are settled and which are genuinely argued over by people who work on this, because I don't want to hand in an essay that presents one contested view as if everyone agreed.
