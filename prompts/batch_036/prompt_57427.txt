Last spring I inherited my mother's half-acre vegetable garden, and a month later I became the person who had to decide what to do about the termite report on her house. Since then I've noticed that my judgments about killing insects don't line up, and I want to know what principle, if any, they are tracking.

My verdicts. Spraying aphids off the roses with soapy water: fine. Fogging the whole greenhouse to clear whitefly, which kills several hundred times as many insects, including every ladybug inside: also fine, and I do not feel worse about it. Treating the cabbages with a caterpillar-specific bacterial spray: fine, and I feel better about it than the fogging, even though the fogging seems less cruel insect by insect. Setting out a slow-acting sugar bait so the kitchen ants carry poison back and the colony dies over a week: not fine — I would rather flood the nest and be done. Treating the house for termites, which kills more insects than everything else on this list combined: not merely permissible but required, and I would pay for it twice. Spraying the squash vines for borers because losing the whole crop is intolerable, while letting the caterpillars have the outer row of kale: fine, and the difference is entirely about what I stand to lose. Fogging the yard for mosquitoes before my nephew's wedding: fine. A proposal to release gene-drive mosquitoes to collapse the species across the region: not fine, though I would happily fog my own yard every summer. A wasps' nest under the eaves by the door the children use: destroy it. A nest of the same size at the back fence: leave it alone.

Two of these fight each other directly. I rank a slow bait that kills a few hundred ants worse than a fog that kills tens of thousands, so manner of death seems to dominate numbers; yet I rank termite treatment, on sheer size of what is at stake for me, as obligatory. Manner, numbers, and stakes cannot all be sovereign.

So: give me a principle, or a small set of candidates, stated precisely enough that I can see which of my cases motivates each clause; tell me what it says about two situations I have not listed — netting the fruit trees rather than spraying them, and a neighbour who plans a neonicotinoid seed treatment on the field adjoining my garden; and say honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. If the ant bait and the termites cannot both survive, tell me which one goes, and why that one rather than the other.
