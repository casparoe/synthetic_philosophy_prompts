I retired four years ago after a career in analytical instrumentation — chiefly the design of detectors and of the readout chains behind them — and have since taken up the philosophy of mind with more seriousness than I expected of myself. This autumn I am to give two lectures of seventy-five minutes each to a lifelong-learning group of about forty people: retired engineers, schoolteachers, one former judge. They have no philosophy whatever but a great deal of technical confidence, and they are not shy about saying so.

My subject is the distinction between consciousness insofar as it is available for report and for the guidance of behaviour, and consciousness insofar as it is felt, together with the global-workspace account of the former. I do not want either explained to me; I want a plan for explaining them.

Please tell me: what must already be in place before the distinction can even be posed, and in what order the pieces should arrive, given that I expect the room to want to collapse the two notions within the first ten minutes; which single case should carry the whole thing — I have been weighing blindsight, change blindness, and an episode from my own laboratory, an instrument that dutifully logged a signal for eleven years that nobody ever read — and what that case must exhibit and must conspicuously fail to exhibit; the misapprehensions technically minded people will bring or manufacture (I anticipate that the felt component is merely the reportable one we cannot yet measure, that the workspace model has dissolved rather than addressed the difficulty, and some variety of small spectator watching a screen inside the skull), and the exact point in the sequence at which each should be intercepted rather than conceded to the closing discussion; one question with which to end, whose answer would come out differently for someone who has grasped the distinction and for someone who has merely acquired two labels; and what I should announce at the outset that I am deliberately omitting. I am tempted to bring in the rival theory that proposes to quantify consciousness as a single number, and I suspect I should resist — say so plainly if I am wrong.
