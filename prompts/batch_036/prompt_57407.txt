I am drafting a novel about the restitution panel of a national museum, and I want its rulings to read as the work of a single consistent mind rather than a generator of outcomes. Here is the docket I have given it so far. Cast-brass ancestral figures taken from a palace shrine during the punitive expedition of 1897: returned, the panel citing both the raid and the fact that the guild of casters who made them still works for the same court. A marble relief cut from a standing temple in the 1830s under a written permit from the regional governor, now claimed by that governor's successor state: refused, the permit held to be genuine and no living custodian identified. Funerary effigies bought in the 1920s by a private collector from an intermediary whose authority to sell derived from a colonial land ordinance, the family having performed rites at the graveside ever since: returned. Liturgical vestments acquired by a mission society in 1880 with the custodians' consent, whose use lapsed three generations ago and which a descendant association now wants for a cultural centre: not returned, but placed on indefinite loan with review at twenty years.

The new case, which I have not yet settled: a set of gold-dust weights and a brass scale, confiscated in 1874 by an expedition that burned a West African capital, recorded in the museum's own registers as seized property; the weighing guild has been defunct for two generations and the trade is dead, but a descendant association wants the set for a memorial, the successor state has a modern facility and proposes a joint-curatorial partnership, and no question of the manner of taking arises.

What will my panel do — return, refuse, or loan? Lay out the rules that fit the first four rulings, what each predicts for this case, which one it is most plausibly running and why you favour that reading, the principle the panel would be committing itself to if it went the other way, and one further case I could add to the docket that would separate the candidates. Lead with your verdict in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of my question.
