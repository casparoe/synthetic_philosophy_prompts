Me and a bunch of friends do a games night every other Thursday at my place, usually 8 to 10 people, mostly coworkers and their partners. Nobody's studied philosophy, though one of them is deep into those free-will podcasts and always drags stuff like that into the conversation. A couple months back he brought up this thing called Kavka's toxin puzzle, apparently from the 80s, some guy called Kavka: an eccentric billionaire says he'll pay you a million bucks if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink a nasty toxin tomorrow afternoon, and the catch is you don't have to actually drink it, the money's already in your account by then, drinking it would just make you sick for a day for absolutely nothing. Three people at my table instantly said "easy, I'd form the intention, get scanned, pocket the cash and skip the toxin" and I got this itch I still haven't scratched, because I'm pretty sure they're wrong but I couldn't say why, and honestly a speech from me wouldn't convince them anyway.

So what I want is something we can actually run. Say two hours, 8-10 people, around my kitchen table, no whiteboard, but I've got paper, pens, a phone timer, a jar of coins and about 40 dollars of real money I'm prepared to hand over, plus whatever snacks are in the cupboard. Build me a live version where they really do get offered a deal that only pays if they form an intention now to do something mildly unpleasant later, and where they run into the wall themselves instead of being told about it — that moment where you realise you can't just decide to decide. I'd love a few rounds with different payoffs so they can feel what's doing the work: one where the later act is something they'd have done anyway, one where it genuinely costs them, and maybe one where the "brain scanner" gets replaced by the rest of the table voting on whether they reckon the intention was real, so that bluffing stops being a cheat code and becomes a social thing. Real (tiny) money on the line matters here, I don't want pretend stakes, because the whole point is that they should be able to hear themselves trying to negotiate.

Give me the full kit: rules, who does what and when, the money schedule, the timer plan, and a debrief I can run without sounding like a professor — including how to deal with the guy who will absolutely insist the whole thing was a trick and that he totally formed a real intention (that's my friend with the podcasts, by the way). Tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it — someone refusing the unpleasant bit, the money feeling too small to care about, one joker turning it into a bit — and how I steer it back. And be straight with me about the part of the puzzle my living-room version just can't reproduce: I suspect it's that a real person can be paid for intending and still know perfectly well whether they'll follow through, so the game can never really generate the "no reason at all to act later" trap, but I want you to say exactly which feature of the original setup gets lost and what I have to explain out loud to patch it. If you think there's a better twist lying around, like paying them for intending to intend, or making the unpleasant act something they've already committed to publicly in front of the group, throw that in as a bonus round.
