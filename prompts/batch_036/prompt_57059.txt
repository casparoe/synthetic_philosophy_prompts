I'm enrolled in a seminar on classical Indian epistemology, and for our week on the pramāṇas I've been asked to circulate a handout that sorts readers by their own commitments rather than by which darśana they can name. Please write it as a decision tree.

Take as the root only a minimal starting point — that some of our beliefs amount to knowledge, and that not all of them come to us through the senses — and make every node a question a classmate could answer without knowing which way it leads. I want the nodes built out of the reduction disputes rather than out of a list of candidates: whether what a trustworthy speaker says is knowable in its own right, or only by way of an inference about that speaker's reliability and intention; whether the stock case of the stout man who eats nothing by day, and so must eat at night, is postulation or inference under another name; whether knowing that the pot is not on the ground requires a means of knowledge of its own, or is nothing beyond a negative perception; whether the forest-dweller who was told the gavaya resembles a cow and later picks one out on sight has gained anything beyond perception plus memory. Use the six standard candidates as your raw material, and add any further ones you think belong in the space.

Each leaf should state the set it accepts, the classical school you take to occupy it (flagging any attribution you're unsure of), and one sentence on the most awkward consequence of accepting exactly that set. Then list every combination your tree generates that no school seems to occupy, and say for each whether it is empty because the combination is incoherent, because it is unattractive given what these schools were trying to accomplish, or because it is merely unexplored. Finally, flag any two leaves your tree cannot in practice separate.
