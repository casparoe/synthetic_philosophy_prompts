ok so I've been drafting a comment on germline editing for a bioethics working group and before I circulate it I want someone to tell me whether my stated position is even coherent. here's what I'm commited to, roughly in the order it shows up in the draft:

(1) I take the necessity of origin seriously: I could not have come from a different sperm and a different egg than the ones I actually came from. Not "would have turned out differently" — literally could not have been me.

(2) Origin is metaphysically load baring for me but prudentially irrelevant. What I care about in survival is psychological continuity; where I came from does no work in that.

(3) If an embryologist edited a single disease allele in a zygote before implanting it — think the edited-babies case in China in 2018, except with an actual medical reason — the resulting child is the same child who would otherwise have been born. Treating the embryo isn't swapping in a different kid.

(4) Whereas if my parents had conceived a month later, that would have been a different person altogether and I would never have existed. That's what gives the non-identity stuff its grip in policy arguments, as far as I'm concerned.

(5) On mitochondrial donation I want to say the nuclear genome does all the identity work: a child born after the mother's egg nucleus gets moved into a donor egg isn't a "different person" in any sense that should matter to the donor, the parents, or the child.

(6) I'm an anti-haecceitist — no two possible worlds differ *only* in which individual is which, everything qualitative staying the same. I'd offer that as part of the reason I believe (1).

Which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? Rank the tensions instead of giving me a flat list, and for each one say what has to go or get weakened and what that costs me elsewhere in the draft. Flag any pair that only looks like a problem. What I most want to know is whether there's a single revision that cleans up more than one of them, or whether every fix just relocates the trouble — like, does retreating to a weaker version of (1) buy me (3) and (5) or does it cost me (4)? Don't point me at the literature, I've read the obvious stuff and I'm not after a survey. Just the audit.
