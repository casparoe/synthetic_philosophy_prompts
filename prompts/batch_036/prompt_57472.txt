Here's my opening move. You always wheel out honor cultures -- highland herding societies, the American South -- as your cleanest evidence that the values themselves differ between groups. But your own explanation of why they differ is economic: portable wealth you can't fence in, nobody to call when it's stolen, so reputation has to do the work that law does somewhere else. That isn't a different value, it's the same value -- don't let people take what's yours -- priced into a different environment. At most you're left with differences in weighting, and weighting looks like a fact about circumstances rather than about right and wrong. Which means you've argued yourself into the realist's corner: drop any of these people into the same ecology and they'd converge, and convergence under shared circumstances is exactly what you'd expect if there were something there to converge on. So either your explanation of the variation is wrong, or the variation is only skin deep. Which one do you want to give up?
