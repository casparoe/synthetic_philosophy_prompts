I sit on the governance board of a six-hospital clinical research network, and we vote Tuesday on amending our charter. Two existing commitments may turn out to be incompatible. The first is a reserved domain: each hospital has sole, unappealable authority over one designated matter — for my hospital, whether locally collected tissue samples may be released for commercial licensing — and the network cannot override that hospital's answer by vote. The second is a unanimity clause: if every hospital ranks protocol X above protocol Y, the network shall not adopt Y. Our staff analyst worked a live choice among three sample-handling protocols and produced a profile in which respecting both hospitals' reserved domains leaves the board no protocol it may adopt, with every pairwise comparison going the other way; she says this is an instance of a well-known impossibility result about individual rights and unanimity.

The board has split into three camps. One would keep the reserved domains and strike the unanimity clause, accepting that a single hospital's reserved choice can stand against the unanimous preference of the other five. A second would keep unanimity and restrict what hospitals may report: a hospital may rank protocols only with respect to its own reserved matter, never with respect to another hospital's. A third would abolish reserved domains and settle everything by supermajority.

Please draft the memo I will circulate before the vote. Open with the recommendation and a one-line rationale. Then argue for it, addressing explicitly whether we face a genuine conflict between individual rights and collective welfare or an artifact of encoding rights as constraints on a joint ranking rather than as spheres in which a hospital simply acts. Then state the strongest case for the camp you reject, in terms that camp would recognize as its own. Close with one transition step and a deadline for it. Maximum 750 words, in American English.
