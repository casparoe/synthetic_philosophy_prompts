I am designing an evaluation and containment regime for a lab that intends to deploy frontier models without assuming those models share our goals — the protocol has to hold even if the model is misaligned and fully competent. Before I commit to particular tests, I want the specification any such control protocol must meet. Give me the requirements list: the failure modes it must detect or prevent, the distinctions it must not collapse (a model that acts safely because it wants to versus one that acts safely because it is presently unable to do otherwise; evidence about behaviour versus evidence about disposition; a measure that binds the model versus one that merely informs us), the facts about how these systems are trained and deployed that no protocol can contradict, and the feasibility bounds below which the protocol is just an elaborate way of saying the system should not be built. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of a school — defence-in-depth maximalists against those who hold that monitoring is the only lever that scales.

Three candidate requirements of my own, which you should assess rather than adopt: the protocol must retain its force even if the model has read its complete specification, including the triggers meant to be opaque to it; its assurances must not strengthen as the model's ability to anticipate and pass tests improves; and the conjunction of its parts must be adequate in cases where each part alone is passed, since satisfying monitoring and satisfying capability limits may jointly create an incentive to fail both. Tell me which of these is illusory, which pairs cannot be satisfied together, and which single requirement eliminates the most otherwise attractive protocols.

Close with two or three sentences on how behavioural monitoring, weak-to-strong oversight, and deployment-level containment fare against the list — no more than that, since the requirements are where I want the effort spent.
