I design typefaces, and I have been trying to write down the theory that tacitly governs my work so that colleagues can attack it. My commitments are these.

(1) What makes a glyph an 'a' is its position in a writing system, not its appearance. The single-story and double-story forms share essentially no visual features and are nevertheless the same letter, so there is no look that all and only 'a's have.

(2) Even so, I am not free in drawing one. There are shapes I produce that simply fail — not ugly, not off-brand, but not 'a's at all — and I register the failure before I can state any rule it broke.

(3) What the acceptable forms share is not a feature set but a range of transformations: something like the path one can travel from one 'a' to another while it remains an 'a'. I prefer this to any feature list and take it to be the right kind of answer to the question of what a letter is.

(4) Style is a variable independent of letter-identity. Within a face the letters differ while the style stays fixed; across faces the letter stays fixed while the style differs. A correct analysis should allow one to vary while the other is held constant.

(5) Yet a style cannot be specified except as a way of drawing particular letters. There is no style in the abstract; to say what a face's style is, you have to point at its 'a', its 'g', its 'e'.

(6) A system that sorts images of 'a's from images of 'b's with near-perfect accuracy across thousands of faces does not thereby know what an 'a' is, because it cannot draw one: recognition and production are distinct competences.

(7) There is a genuine trade, not a matter of taste, between how insistently a face asserts its style and how transparently it serves as a medium for the letters.

My instinct is that (1) and (2) are where the set breaks, and that everything else is downstream of that break. Treat the seven as a single package and tell me whether that instinct is right. I want the genuine tensions ranked by severity, each with what would have to be surrendered and what surrendering it costs my practice; an explicit statement of which pairings only look like conflicts and why; and a final judgement on the one revision that would purchase the most coherence.

Please answer as exactly six numbered items of one or two sentences each, ranked from most to least severe, with the fifth item reserved for a pairing that seems to conflict and does not, and the sixth for the single revision you would recommend. No introduction and no conclusion.
