I teach a senior-and-first-year-grad metaphysics seminar and I'm putting the final together. I want ONE essay question on the Humeanism fight about laws of nature — Lewis's best system account vs Armstrong-style necessitation between universals vs Maudlin's primitivism — that a student can't get through by reciting the dialectic. The class has had all three plus Lange's circularity objection and his complaint about interference and counterfactuals, so the question needs to be built around a toy world where the labels don't settle anything. Here's the world I've sketched; tidy up the wording and keep the structure.

W contains three species of particle, A, B and C, and nothing else. There are exactly a billion A-B adjacency events. In 999,996,000 of them a flash occurs; in 4,000 it doesn't. Every one of those 4,000 sits inside a single dense C-cluster (a sphere of some radius, the only place in W where C particles exist), and the cluster contains 12 million adjacency events altogether — so flashes still happen in roughly 99.97% of in-cluster adjacencies.

Students get three candidate systems:
- S1: whenever A is adjacent to B, a flash occurs.
- S2: S1, plus an axiom giving the chance of a flash inside the cluster.
- S3: whenever a shmashed A-B adjacency occurs, a flash occurs — where shmashed just means an A adjacent to a B at which a flash occurs.

And then the twist, which is the bit I want the whole question to hinge on: the 4,000 non-flashes were each produced by a W-experimenter flipping on a shield, but the shield is stipulated to be traceless. Nothing in the intrinsic, non-nomic facts about those events distinguishes a shielded adjacency from an unshielded one. So the base facts are literally identical whether you describe the 4,000 as a law being interfered with or as a limit on the law's scope.

The question should ask them (a) which system a Humean ought to nominate as best for W and what she needs to make that verdict non-arbitrary, and (b) whether the traceless shield shows that only an anti-Humean account can underwrite the counterfactual about what would have happened if the shield hadn't been switched on, plus the claim that the law explains the other 999,996,000 flashes — or whether the Humean can match both.

Please write:

1. the question as it'd actually appear on the paper, toy world stated in full and the three systems displayed. 75 minutes, 1,200 words for the students, 25 marks total.
2. a model answer of about 850 words that works through the case instead of summarising positions. I want it to deal with why S1 isn't even eligible as a system (the axioms have to be true), why S3 looks like a trivialisation and what restricting the vocabulary to perfectly natural properties does and doesn't repair, and why the traceless shield is the philosophically interesting part rather than the mere existence of exceptions. It shouldn't fence-sit at the end — it should say which account comes out better on this world and why.
3. a marking scheme worth 25 marks, split into must-hit points with the marks for each, and one line per point on how students typically mess it up. It has to name the concrete moves that separate an A from a B — a B, I expect, will spot the language-relativity worry but not say what survives it once naturalness is on the table, or will hand the counterfactual straight to the anti-Humean without asking what masking is supposed to consist in when it leaves no trace in the base facts. Build in at least one trap that a label-reciter walks into, and say in the scheme what the trap is. A couple of bonus marks are fine for noticing that once S2's chance axiom is in play, simplicity and strength aren't the only virtues a system is judged on.

No bibliography, reading list or citations anywhere in this — students won't be allowed to cite anything, so the question and the model answer have to stand on the toy case alone.
