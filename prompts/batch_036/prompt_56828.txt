I'm applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship and need a two-page proposal built around the following half-formed idea. The familiar objection that revealed preference theory is vacuous — that defining preference in terms of choice makes every piece of choice data preference-confirming — has an equally familiar rejoinder: the consistency axioms on choice data (WARP, SARP, and the generalization to GARP that Afriat's theorem makes precise) are testable, so the theory has empirical content after all. My suspicion is that both sides talk past each other, and that the real question only becomes visible once the axioms are read as a representation theorem in the measurement-theoretic sense — conditions for representing an empirical ordering by a numerical scale, on the model of the axioms for extensive quantities. On that reading a violation is not a refutation of a psychological claim but something more like a measurement discrepancy, and the framework requires a tolerance parameter: a threshold below which inconsistent choice is treated as noise rather than as evidence that there is no stable underlying order to measure at all. My working claim is that fixing that threshold is doing normative work disguised as bookkeeping, because it determines which agents get described as having preferences and hence whose choices are taken to be evidence of their own good.

Please turn this into a proposal containing: a precise statement of the question, such that it has a determinate answer; why the vacuity objection and the falsifiability rejoinder both leave that question open; the method — what I would actually do, month by month, including which formal setting I would work in, how the tolerance parameter would be defined, and what a fully worked example on a small choice dataset would look like; what a successful result would be, and what would count as the project's failing, including specifically what it would show if the threshold turned out to be fixable by purely empirical means; and a candid assessment of whether this fits in twelve months or what I would have to cut to make it fit. I will add the citations myself, so please don't include a bibliography.
