You are a philosopher of biology and cognitive science working within Dennett's taxonomy of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Think carefully, and begin by sketching two or three candidate species designs before committing to the strongest.

Task: construct a non-human species that is Gregorian but not Popperian. The purpose is a stress test on whether the taxonomy is genuinely cumulative — whether reaching the Gregorian rung requires having the Popperian rung beneath it. So the verdicts must be earned from the categories' own criteria, not asserted; a creature that is simply labeled Gregorian fails, and so does one whose description smuggles in the very internal modeling it is meant to lack.

Specifications:

1. A single biological species: not a human population, not a robot, not a language model or other engineered system. Those are the contested cases and would beg the question.
2. It must deploy mind-tools in the Gregorian sense — informational artifacts designed and cumulatively refined by the creatures' own ancestors, transmitted outside the genome, and taken up by individuals in a way that expands what they can discriminate, remember, or plan.
3. It must fail the Popperian test: the individual lacks a model of its circumstances rich enough to try candidate actions off internally, so that a defeated expectation is discarded as a belief rather than paid for as a corpse. Show this behaviorally — tell me what the creature does when the artifact's deliverances are violated or withdrawn.
4. Its performance must not be dismissible as merely Skinnerian: something it does resists explanation by operant conditioning layered on an innate repertoire.
5. Ordinary biology only — no exotic devices, no impossible sensory powers; the design should look like plausible convergent evolution. Keep the scenario to roughly two paragraphs.

Then audit your own case. State which element of the Gregorian characterization is satisfied and how; state the Popperian element that fails and how the behavior exhibits the failure; and isolate the single design feature doing the work. Say what happens to the verdict if that feature is removed. Finally, give the best reply a defender of strict nesting can make — for instance, that you have described a Skinnerian animal parasitizing a Popperian ancestor's design work, or that no creature could exploit the artifact without already harboring the internal model — and judge honestly whether that reply succeeds.
