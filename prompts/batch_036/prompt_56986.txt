I am preparing an application for an eighteen-month postdoctoral fellowship in ethics and social choice, and I need the complete proposal drafted to submission standard: roughly 1,500 words, with a month-by-month work plan. Please write the document itself rather than advice about how I might write it.

The project turns on a conjecture I want tested, not assumed. The familiar story is that the leveling-down objection is decisive against telic egalitarianism — a change that makes some people worse off and nobody better off can be better in one respect only on a view that treats a *relation* between people as intrinsically valuable — and that the priority view escapes unscathed, since it ranks outcomes by summing a concave transform of each person's well-being and so can never call a change better unless it is better for someone. My conjecture is that this immunity is an artefact of stating both views over certain outcomes, and that it does not survive the extension to choice under uncertainty. Two sketches, which I have worked out only loosely:

First, ex post prioritarianism — transform each person's realized well-being in every state, then take the expectation — can rank a safe prospect above a risky one even when every person strictly prefers the risky prospect by expected well-being. With the square-root transform, two people, a safe prospect giving each 50, and a risky prospect that gives one person 92 and the other 12 in one state and reverses in the other: each person expects 52 from the risky prospect, yet its expected prioritarian value is lower. The result is a prospect that is worse for everyone ex ante and better overall.

Second, ex ante prioritarianism — take each person's expected well-being, then transform — fails in a mirror-image way. Let the safe prospect again give each person 50 in every state, and let the risky prospect give one person 102 and the other 0 in one state, reversed in the other, so that each expects 51. Ex ante prioritarianism prefers the risky prospect, even though in every state its prioritarian value is strictly lower than the safe prospect's and in every state somebody ends up worse off than under the alternative. (In every state somebody also ends up better off, so this is a violation of state-by-state dominance in aggregate prioritarian value rather than of a Pareto principle.)

So, on my reading, the priority view secures its escape from the leveling-down objection only by choosing between two extensions, and each extension violates a structural principle that plays the same rhetorical role as nobody-is-better-off: the ex post extension violates unanimity about prospects, the ex ante extension violates statewise dominance and is, I suspect, exposed to the familiar sequential-choice or dynamic-consistency objection, on which a rule that ignores how things actually turn out can be made to reverse itself over time and thereby pump the decision-maker. If that is right, the leveling-down objection is not so much answered by prioritarianism as relocated.

The proposal must contain the following, in this order:

1. A precise statement of the central question, phrased so that it has determinate possible answers rather than being an invitation to survey the debate.
2. An account of why the question is open. Treat at least three deflationary responses and say what would remain of the project if each were right: that uncertainty is the wrong setting because well-being is only realized, not expected; that the choice of extension is already settled by the dynamic-consistency argument, leaving no substantive question; and that every aggregative distributive view faces a trade-off among structural principles, so nothing follows specifically against prioritarianism.
3. The method, with a real work plan. The early months should involve rebuilding my two sketches under explicit assumptions, checking whether they survive alternative specifications, and asking whether a third extension — for instance one that applies the transform to a risk-adjusted certainty-equivalent of each person's prospect, or one that weights states and persons in two distinct stages — is principled or merely ad hoc. I want the proposal to foreground a difficulty I have not resolved: what it is for a prospect to be better *for a person* when that person's good is itself risky, and whether a prioritarian may treat individuals as risk-neutral about their own well-being without begging the question against the objection.
4. The intended results and deliverables, described so that a reviewer can see what the eighteen months produce.
5. What would count as failure, stated candidly, including the possibility that the constructions are unstable or that the generalised objection turns out to be a restatement of a known impossibility result wearing philosophical clothing.
6. An honest assessment of scope: what I would cut at the six-month mark if the project is running too large, and a short paragraph answering the reviewer who asks why this belongs in an ethics fellowship rather than in a social choice theory seminar.
