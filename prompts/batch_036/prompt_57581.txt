I'm not a lawyer or a professor, I fix small engines for a living and somehow ended up treasurer of the winter shelter in our town (northern Michigan, about 8,000 people). We've got to decide something Thursday and I'm supposed to send the board a written recommendation ahead of it. Can you write that for me? Here's the situation, as straight as I can lay it out.

We run 22 cots, November through March, in a donated Legion hall. All volunteers except a part-time coordinator. Money comes about half from the county, half from church collections and one grocery chain. The fire marshal set our occupancy at 30 total including the two volunteers on shift, so 28 sleepers is the legal ceiling and we don't even reach that.

Last winter we were full 41 nights out of roughly 120 open. Six nights we turned people away at the door. In February a man we'd turned away was found dead of exposure in a stairwell about 400 yards from the building. I was the one who signed the log that night.

What changed: in June the county shut its 40-bed facility two towns over, budget reasons, and now there's a regional bus route that stops here. So the people who used to go there come to us, plus some from further out. Word travels. I expect us to be full most nights this winter, not six.

Three board members, three positions, and I dont think any of them is dumb:

- Hard cap at 22, doors at six, first come first served, everybody else gets the address of the mission 30 miles south. Honest, simple, we know what we're choosing.
- Never turn anybody away. Mats on the day room floor, and if the marshal shows up we deal with it then, because the alternative is that stairwell.
- Cap, but with a residency preference — county residents first, and the county that dumped its problem on us should be paying for the overflow.

One of the cap people keeps saying "we're a lifeboat, not a ferry," and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a way of feeling better.

Then there's the voucher thing and I don't know what to do with it. Two churches pool money for $25 motel vouchers for people we turn away; there's maybe eight nights' worth of money left for the whole season. One board member swears people show up late on purpose because a motel beats a cot. I don't think it's widespread but I can't disprove it, and if it's true we're paying people to not come in.

So: draft the memo. It goes to six people with strong feelings and no time to read. Put the recommendation at the very top — what the actual rule is, who gets in, what happens to the ones who don't, who pays for what, and when we reopen the question. Then the reasons, including the strongest honest case for whichever position you're arguing against, because if you strawman the never-turn-anybody-away crowd they'll just dig in. Say what to do with the vouchers. My own gut is cap-plus-vouchers, but I don't fully trust my gut given that I signed the log.

Under 450 words, hard, I mean it. And don't cite any laws, court cases or studies — I can't check them and neither can they. If something in the recommendation depends on a fact we'd have to go look up, just say so in plain words.
