The part I actually care about is the accumulator reinterpretation. The readiness potential isn't the brain deciding early; it's a noisy drift-to-threshold process fed by internal noise — subjects are told to act whenever, without preplanning, so there's no evidence to accumulate in the first place. On that view the half-second ramp is largely a selection artifact: you only average the trials that ended in a movement, so of course activity looks like it was climbing beforehand. Then "the unconscious chose before you were aware of choosing" mostly evaporates, and Libet's veto window has nothing left to veto. There's also the follow-up trick where you interrupt at random and tell people to move now — whoever was already highest in the drift moves soonest, which is not the same thing as having decided sooner.
