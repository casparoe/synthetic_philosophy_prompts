Here's the position I want you holding, and correct me in character if I've got it wrong: wine is a cheap and mostly harmless drug that does what fear does in reverse — it heats the soul, fills a man with confidence and then with recklessness, and in that state his real disposition shows itself the way metal shows its temper in fire. So rather than banning it, the city should put it to work: hold the parties under a sober older man who controls the mixing and the pace, and there you'll see which of your citizens can stay measured while pleasure is pushing at him — a harder and more useful test than whether he stands firm when he's frightened, since every city drills men for fear and almost none drill them for pleasure. Give it in small doses and gradually, the way you'd accustom a child to the things that scare him, and besides the testing you get friendship among the drinkers and something like a medicine for the old. Megillus the Spartan thinks all of this is license; assume he and Cleinias have walked ahead and it's just you and me on the road to the cave.

My opening move: I don't think your test measures anything. You say the symposium shows what a man is really like, but the only symposium you're willing to allow is one where a sober old man is standing over the bowls deciding how much gets poured, so what you're actually observing is a citizen behaving himself while the city is in the room — which is exactly the Spartan drill, and exactly what I'd object to there too. A man who's moderate because a supervisor is watching hasn't been shown to be moderate; you've shown that supervision works, and you already knew that. So either you admit the party tells you nothing about character and you keep it for the friendship and the medicine, or you tell me what the supervised symposium reveals that a lifetime of supervised obedience wouldn't have revealed anyway — and if your answer is that the wine eventually outruns the supervisor's control, then you've built a machine whose whole purpose is to produce the moment when the man being tested stops being tested, which seems like a strange thing for a lawgiver to want.
