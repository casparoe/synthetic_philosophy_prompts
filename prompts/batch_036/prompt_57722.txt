Writing something for a philosophy-of-economics seminar and I want an actual verdict, not a survey. The controversy: Simon's satisficing keeps getting dismissed as maximization in disguise. A cost-conscious maximizer who decides how many options to inspect before stopping ends up with a reservation threshold, and a reservation threshold looks exactly like an aspiration level — so on this line the two views never come apart observationally and the whole dispute is just vocabulary.

A friend of mine thinks she's found a way to break the tie, and I'd rather you engage her proposal than rehearse the standard reconciliation. Her idea: in Simon's account aspiration levels are set by the decision-maker's own past experience — they drift up after a run of decent outcomes, down after a run of bad ones — while a maximizer's reservation threshold is a function only of the payoff distribution and the per-inspection cost. So put people through a series of structurally identical choice problems where the distribution and the cost of another look are both explicitly described and held constant, but their realized payoffs on the earlier problems were randomly good or bad. If stopping behaviour tracks the payoff streak, satisficing is doing genuine explanatory work; if it doesn't, the maximization redescription wins.

Is her test decisive? I can see atleast one confound already — past outcomes might rationally update someone's beliefs about the distribution even when it was spelled out for them — so tell me whether that's fixable by design or fatal. And then go after the deeper worry, which cuts against her: if a maximizer can always absorb history-dependence by letting utility depend on the whole sequence of outcomes rather than the current option, does maximization become unfalsifiable, and does that mean she's proved too much?

Write it up as an essay that argues to a conclusion. A conditional verdict is fine, "both sides have a point" isnt. I want a clear statement of whether satisficing is a real rival to cost-constrained maximizing, and of what her experiment does and doesnt show.
