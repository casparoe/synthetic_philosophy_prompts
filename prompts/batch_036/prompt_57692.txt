The set-piece of my novel-in-progress is a breakfast argument in a communal house in 1971, over the single contraband copy of a scandalous book that has turned up on the table. The two principals are a priggish former schoolmaster and a libertine, and the house charter gives each resident sole authority over whether he himself reads it. The schoolmaster would rather see the book destroyed unread than watch the libertine enjoy it, but if someone must read it he would read it himself; the libertine most wants the schoolmaster forced through it, next wants to read it himself, and ranks destruction last. I gather this is Sen's liberal paradox about the impossibility of a Paretian liberal, and I want each voice at the table to defend a distinct way out of it, so that the scene has argumentative structure rather than ending in a shrug.

Build me a decision tree that carries a reader from that setup to one named exit. The leaves must include: waiving or trading one's right; restricting the admissible preferences so that nobody's taste about another person's reading counts at all; abandoning the Pareto condition rather than the liberty condition; treating a right as a limit on what may be done to a person instead of as a pair of entries in a social ranking; and the dissolution which says the outcome descriptions are too coarse to generate a cycle. If you think a sixth exit belongs, fold it into an item rather than appending a remark.

Give me exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no preamble, no summary, no closing note. Each item is one question phrased as a line a resident would actually say over breakfast, with two or three answer options, followed by the exit each option leads to: either the number of the next item or the name of the position. No item should run past three sentences, no question should betray the exit it steers toward, and wherever two of your leaves cannot actually be separated by the question just asked — or a combination of answers points at a position nobody defends — say so in a clause inside that same item.
