(1) process supervision is when a person reads and grades the model's intermediate reasoning, outcome supervision is when they only check the final answer. that's the entire difference between them.

(2) outcome supervision breaks down once the task gets harder than the grader. if you can't tell a correct proof from a broken one, checking the answer buys you nothing.

(3) i don't think a model's chain of thought is evidence of what it's really doing anyway. it's just more generated text, and generated text gets optimized to look good to whoever's reading it.

(4) but i still think process supervision is our best shot at supervising models that are smarter than us, cause even if you can't check a whole proof you can usually check one step. also those results where rating steps beat rating answers on competition math look pretty convincing to me.

(5) and honestly i think a model that gets right answers for dumb reasons is fine as long as it keeps generalizing to new problems. i don't care how it thinks, i care what it does.
