For eleven years I have worked on the storage and settlement layer of a payments system, most recently on the orchestration of multi-step flows that customers initiate themselves. My native vocabulary is transactions, atomicity and durability, isolation levels and their characteristic anomalies, commit protocols and write-ahead logging, sagas and compensating actions, idempotence, retries and backoff, leases and locks, and the named guarantees we advertise to callers — read-your-writes, monotonic reads, snapshot consistency. My philosophical training is a first-year ethics module and nothing since. Please do not give me references, reading lists, or names-and-dates of papers; describe positions by their content, and if you need a label for one, coin it in my terms.

Last quarter we found a real defect in a loyalty-points flow. A customer could redeem points for a voucher, spend part of the voucher, and then be offered, at each step, a conversion back into points at a marginally worse rate. Every individual step passed every policy check we ran against the state as it stood at that moment. Composed, the flow left a customer who began and ended holding points poorer by the spread. My team's fix was to make the sequence atomic: hide the intermediate states, evaluate the whole flow at one commit point, and refuse it if the resulting state is dominated by the state it started from. The fix works, and it is the fix any competent engineer would make. What I cannot settle is whether it answers a question or abolishes one, because I have been reading about money pumps and dynamic coherence and I suspect that we have been doing philosophy incompetently, in the confident way of people who think they are merely doing design.

The material I want translated is roughly this, as I understand it. An agent whose preferences are cyclic can be led through a sequence of individually attractive swaps that returns them to their starting holdings minus a fee. The first line of resistance is that nothing in the sequence is irrational: each exchange is preferred, at the moment it is made, to the alternative on offer, so condemning the whole is a fallacy of composition — unless the agent is myopic and simply fails to see where the sequence is going. A second line grants foresight and argues that a foresighted agent will decline the opening swap, since accepting it commits them either to a dominated continuation or to a later reversal they can already predict will not happen. A third line holds that the unit of rational assessment is not the isolated choice but the adopted plan: an agent who resolves on a plan and then treats its later nodes as settled, rather than re-optimising at each node in the light of the preferences they happen to have there, is not irrational but exercising a distinctively diachronic rationality. Running underneath all of this is a more general claim, which does not need intransitivity at all: preferences that shift over time — the sort modelled by discount rates that are steeper in the near term than in the far — can generate dominated sequences even when every pairwise comparison is transitive, and the interesting question becomes what an agent may permissibly do about their own predictable future choices, including whether binding commitment is available, and whether it is itself rational or merely a technology for dodging the problem. There is also a dominance principle for sequential choice, which says that an option at a node should be excluded from consideration if some available plan dominates it, even though the agent's step-by-step preferences would have taken it.

With that in hand, I want four things.

First, the translation itself. Restate each of those moves in the vocabulary I actually use — atomicity, isolation, invariants checked at commit boundaries, sagas and compensation, leases, retries, consistency models — as faithfully and as technically as you can. Be explicit about which notion in my vocabulary is being pressed into service for each: what is a preference, what is a choice node, what is foresight, what is a resolution to a plan, what is the fee, and what exactly is the invariant that a money pump is said to violate, given that in my world invariants are things checked at commit points by a component that stands outside the transaction.

Second, the residue report, in three tiers: claims that map cleanly; claims that map only with distortion, where you should say precisely what gets distorted; and claims that have no expression at all in my vocabulary. For each item in the third tier, say whether that is because my vocabulary is missing something or because the philosophical claim was less clear than it looked.

Third, a verdict on my fix. Making the flow atomic prevents the loss. Does it thereby show that the pump was only ever an engineering problem about exposed intermediate states, or does it presuppose an outside authority able to impose atomicity that the philosophical setting deliberately withholds? I want you to be blunt about whether my team solved the problem or removed the conditions under which it could be stated.

Fourth, and most importantly: name the two or three points at which my vocabulary is most seductive, most likely to make me think a substantive question has been dissolved when it has only been renamed — in particular, whether treating rationality as an invariant, treating commitment as a lock or a lease, and treating the agent as a system with a consistency model each import an assumption the philosophers would refuse. Where the translation tempts me, I want you to resist it on my behalf rather than flatter the analogy.
