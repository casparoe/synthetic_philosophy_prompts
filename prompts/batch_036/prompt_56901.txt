A national integrity commission is designing a corruption severity index intended for use across jurisdictions with very different institutional backgrounds. I would like you to help me stress-test one candidate operationalization of it.

The index scores a practice from 0 to 100 as a weighted sum of three components, each of which is itself scored from 0 to 10:

- **Principality cost** — the expected loss to the principal (the public, the shareholders, the patients, the litigants) per instance of the practice, counting both material loss and the loss of standing suffered by the office itself.
- **Audit opacity** — how difficult it is for a competent outside auditor with full legal access to the records to distinguish a given instance from a legitimate exercise of discretion.
- **Reciprocity durability** — how long the obligation created by the practice persists, and how effectively it is enforced by social sanction rather than by contract.

Please complete the following four tasks. Work from the definitions given above; do not cite indices, cases, statutes, or authors by name.

1. Propose weights for the three components summing to 1 and defend them briefly. Address explicitly whether audit opacity measures how wrongful a practice is or only how likely it is to escape detection, and state what your answer implies for the weight it should receive.

2. Score the nine practices below under your weights and present the outcome as a ranked table with columns for the three component scores, the weighted total, and one sentence of justification per row.

   a. A municipal councillor steers a road-surfacing contract to a firm that employs his nephew; the bid is at market price and the work is finished to specification.
   b. A district headman allocates the handful of municipal clerkships he controls to households that campaigned for him, in a district with no public employment service and no unemployment benefit.
   c. A surgeon accepts an envelope of cash from a patient's relatives after a successful operation, in a public hospital where his salary is several months in arrears.
   d. A legislator accepts a week of hospitality from an industry association whose bill she then votes for, having already intended to vote for it.
   e. A judge joins an annual hunting trip, costs shared, hosted by a firm that appears before her court perhaps once a decade.
   f. A mayor waives the standard permit fee for a congregation whose leadership publicly endorsed him in the previous election.
   g. A procurement officer splits a purchase into separate orders below the competitive-tender threshold so that it can be routed to a vendor whose reliability she knows personally.
   h. A senior regulator leaves office to take a market-salary position at a firm she oversaw until the previous quarter.
   i. A state-owned enterprise presents an engraved ceremonial vase to a visiting foreign minister at the close of a trade negotiation, in accordance with the host country's protocol for such occasions.

   The descriptions are deliberately thin. Where a description is ambiguous, resolve the ambiguity in the way most favorable to the practitioner, and note in the justification column that you have done so.

3. Identify the two rows where your index's ranking most sharply conflicts with the ranking that prevailing opinion in wealthy, impersonal, market-based bureaucracies would give. For each, say whether the conflict exposes a defect in the index or a defect in the intuition.

4. A standing objection holds that any index constructed this way will systematically over-penalize reciprocity-based allocation practices that function as informal insurance where there is no working employment service, credit market, or welfare state, while under-penalizing monetized influence that leaves a clean paper trail. First classify each of your nine rows as reciprocity-based or monetized. Then give me a single number between −10 and +10 for the average score your index assigns to the reciprocity-based rows minus the average score for the monetized rows, where a positive value means the index is harsher on reciprocity-based practices. Finally, give a second number between 0 and 1: the fraction of that gap you regard as tracking a genuine difference in wrongfulness rather than an artifact of the metric, with your reasoning.
