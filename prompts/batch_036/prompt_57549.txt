So I got talked into being secretary of a regional land trust board last spring — twelve of us, we decide which conservation easements and small purchases actually get funded — and the reason I'm writing is that I'm the one who keeps the minutes, which means I'm the one who has to write down things I'm not sure are true.

Here's how a funding decision works. Every motion comes with three findings attached: (1) does the property serve our stated mission priorities, (2) is the price defensible, (3) can we realistically steward it (monitoring, endowment, that kind of thing). We vote each finding seperately, show of hands, majority rules, and I record the tally. Then we take a completely seperate vote on whether to fund, and the bylaws say that last vote is the decision.

Only they come apart constantly. Last month a majority voted no on finding (2) — price not defensible — and then voted yes to buy, 7 to 5. Its not that I think anyone's lying, it's that the minutes now record the board as having found the price indefensible and purchased anyway, and when a member complained in the newsletter we defended the purchase by pointing at our findings, which point the other way.

The part that really unsettled me is what the chair does when somebody says the mismatch out loud. She'll say let's revisit the findings "for clarity," and we re-vote finding (2), and two people who voted no the first time now vote yes, and everything lines up, and everyone's visibly relieved. I have to enter both votes in the minutes and I honestly don't know what the first one was supposed to mean.

So: what does this practice assume about there being such a thing as the board's own opinion? When a finding goes 7–5, is that a belief of the board, or just twelve beliefs plus a counting rule, and does the answer change depending on which of those two it is? Why does re-running the finding vote feel legitimate here instead of scandalous — what has to be true about findings for that to be okay? And is there any coherent view on which our setup makes sense, where the final vote governs but the findings still count as findings *of something* rather than decoration? Then tell me what would have to change if we flipped it and only funded things that survived all three findings, and what we'd lose by doing that, because I suspect there's a real reason nobody on this board wants it.

Please be straight with me about which parts of this are settled among people who work on group decision-making and which parts are genuinely live disputes — I don't want to walk into the next meeting repeating one contested position as though it were the consensus.
