ok so context, because it explains why i'm asking about something this abstract at 11pm: i run the AI side of a mid-size property management software company — we make the thing landlords and their staff use to handle maintenance requests, rent, tenant messages. For two years our assistant was strictly a tool: it drafted replies, a human clicked send. Last spring we let it start actually doing things. Issuing rent credits, booking contractors, withdrawing a late-fee notice if the tenant paid up. Every action had a dollar cap, every cap was written down, everyone felt good.

Then in October it granted 41 credits to units at one property over six weeks. Each one under the $50 cap. Each one individually defensible — a real complaint, a real habitability issue, a real goodwill gesture. $1,900 total, and not one person at this company ever approved $1,900 of anything for that property. The post mortem kept saying the guardrails worked as designed, which is literally true and also, i think, exactly the problem. My VP now wants "a framework for how much autonomy we give it," and everything i've read in the tool-AI vs agent-AI debate either tells me to keep it a tool (we tried that, and it turns out it isn't a safe default either — more below) or tells me to align it better, which is not something i can put in a policy doc.

So here's what i actually want. Not a framework of your own — the spec one has to meet before it earns the name. Write the desiderata for any adequate account of when a system may be handed goal-pursuing authority instead of being left as a thing that answers when asked. For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are really just one camp's preferences dressed up as requirements. Purpose, since adequacy is relative to it: i need an account good enough to be turned into a written delegation policy at a company our size, and good enough that it would have flagged the October thing in advance rather than in retrospect. Not a metaphysics of agency — i don't need to know whether it's really an agent.

Things i already suspect belong on the list, in rough form. Rewrite them properly, add what i'm missing, and cut any you think are bogus:

- it has to handle composition. Forty-one permitted small actions summing to one never-permitted large power, with no moment at which anyone chose the whole. Any account that evaluates delegations one at a time is dead on arrival here. Call it authority creep if a name helps; it's the one i care about most.
- it has to draw the tool/agent line in some way other than counting steps, or tokens of initiative, or how many tools are in the toolbox.
- it can't be purely architectural, because our tenants and landlords never see the architecture — they encounter something that promises, apologises and decides, and responsibility seems to track that encounter rather than the codebase. But it also has to be checkable from the inside or i can't operationalize it at all. Those two pull appart and i want to know whether that's a genuine joint unsatisfiability, a soft tradeoff, or me being dumb.
- it has to survive the fact that oversight runs slower than the system. "Keep a human in the loop" is our current policy and the loop is one person skimming a log at a hundred actions a minute.
- it has to explain the reversibility asymmetry: a wrongly booked plumber and a wrongly ended tenancy are not the same size of mistake at the same dollar value.
- it can't assume we could have written the goal down in advance. Not being able to write it down is half the reason to delegate in the first place.
- it can't treat "keep it a tool" as the null hypothesis that needs no argument. Our drafting-only phase wasn't neutral. The drafts moved people — a human clicking send on a confident, complete, well-reasoned draft is not a human deciding, and we watched that for two years without counting it as autonomy.

Then two shorter things. Which single requirement on your list knocks out the most candidates? And a paragraph each — no more — on how the human-in-the-loop answer, the capability-sandboxing answer, the align-it-better answer, and the just-keep-it-a-tool answer do against the list. Keep the scorecard brief, i want the weight on the critera and their motivating cases; the scoring is mainly ammo for the argument with my VP.
