A friend of mine who works on alignment keeps reaching for Olds and Milner's rat to explain wireheading: the animal with an electrode in a self-stimulation site, pressing the lever until it collapses while food and water go untouched, is his picture of what any agent does once it has found a route to its own reward channel. He uses the image to support a design conclusion — that a reward signal an agent can reach is a reward signal that will be shorted out, so the fix is to make the channel unreachable, the way you would take the lever out of the cage.

I have found that picture persuasive for years and now suspect it is carrying more than it can bear. Could you audit it? Please lay the correspondence out explicitly — what plays the electrode, the lever, the cage, the implanted hardware, the food the rat walks past — and then mark where the mapping fails. Three worries of my own, which you should push on rather than accept: the rat is not modifying anything, since another agent installed the electrode and built the box; the rat has no representation of its own reinforcement and no means of acting on that representation even if it did; and I have read enough about incentive salience to suspect the lever-pressing is compulsive wanting rather than the serene satisfaction the thought experiment is supposed to depict. If the standard retelling of those experiments is overstated — rats pressing to death, indifferent to a receptive female — say so, and say what the correction costs the analogy.

Then separate the disanalogies that actually matter for his conclusion from the ones that are merely picturesque, and tell me whether "make the channel unreachable" survives once they are on the table, or whether the rat was quietly supplying the intuition that wireheading is a failure of agency rather than the successful execution of an agent's own values — which is what makes it disturbing in the first place. Finally, if there is a better source domain for reward tampering proper, as distinct from reward hacking, where the agent games the signal but leaves it intact, propose it and run the same audit on your own substitute: I would rather see where your analogy breaks than be sold on it.
