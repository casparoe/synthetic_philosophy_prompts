I'm one of nine trustees on the board of a mid-sized medical humanitarian NGO — mostly cholera and malnutrition work in the Sahel and around the Horn, about 400 national staff and maybe 30 internationals. I'm the only member who isn't a clinician or a former field director; I teach applied ethics at a university and I got onto the board four years ago basically as the token philosopher, which means people expect me to have opinions about cases like this one and I mostly dont. Here's the situation, compressed and anonymized a bit. Last year our country team wanted to open a cholera treatment centre in a district controlled by an armed group that isn't on any sanctions list but is thoroughly awful — arbitrary detention, forced recruitment, the works. Their condition for access was that we hand over the daily triage registers: names, ages, home village, date of arrival. The stated reason was "security and epidemiological coordination." Our protection policy, rewritten three years ago after an incident in another country where patient lists got used to trace people, flatly prohibits sharing individually identifiable patient data with any armed actor, and it says exceptions need prior board approval. The country director couldn't realistically convene nine people inside 72 hours during an outbreak, so he signed off himself. We shared registers for six weeks. The centre treated roughly 2,300 patients and the outbreak burned out. We have no evidence anyone on those registers was harmed and no realistic way to find out — the group already held the village-level population rolls. Now the same group is offering us access to a much bigger nutrition programme across two more districts, on the same terms, on an ongoing basis.

So Thursday's meeting has to settle two things at once and I've been asked to circulate something beforehand: whether we ratify what the country director already did, and whether the answer to the nutrition offer is yes, no, or yes-on-different-terms. The board is split in a way that doesn't track any usual lines. One trustee, a former head of mission, says there's nothing to decide — 2,300 people treated, the registers were a paper formality, and anyone who wants to call that "dirty hands" is being squeamish about a rule he's never had to trade against an actual epidemic. Another, who wrote the protection policy, says ratifying repeals it in practice: every field team will read this as permission, and the next armed group will open by demanding the same thing because they'll have learned the demand works. A third, who's from the region, says the communities already assume we're in the group's pocket, that our silence is doing the reputational damage disclosure would, and that whatever we decide has to include telling the affected villages what we handed over. When I asked our external adviser, he mumbled something about Walzer and about how the person who does this kind of thing is supposed to be punished even when he was right to do it, which I didn't follow and which sounds to me like it might just be a way for the organisation to feel better at somebody's expense.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate? Ground rules: it has to actually come down somewhere, not "the board should weigh the following considerations." It has to be usable by people who haven't opened the case file — bottom line up front, then the reasoning, no more than about 900 words. Argue from the case itself: no footnotes, no citations, no reading list, nobody in that room is going to look anything up and I don't want to have to check sources either. Treat the strongest version of the position you reject as seriously as the one you adopt, and be explicit about whether ratifying the past act and approving the future programme are one decision or two — my hunch is they're two seperate decisions but I can't say why in a form that would survive contact with the former head of mission. And if your recommendation involves sanctioning the country director, or not sanctioning him, say so plainly and give the reason, because that's what people will actually argue about. End with exactly one line in the form "Verdict: X / Y", where X is one of RATIFY, RATIFY WITH SANCTION, DO NOT RATIFY, DEFER, and Y is one of DISCLOSE, DISCLOSE LATER, DO NOT DISCLOSE.
