ok so I need a sanity check, and fair warning: zero philosophy training, I build distributed systems for a living (clocks, replication, consensus, the whole thing), and a few years ago I fell down the rabbit hole on what special relativity supposedly implies about time. I've come up holding a bunch of views that I now suspect don't all fit together, and I can't tell which ones are the problem. Here they are:

1. SR is right and there is no global "now". I feel this in my bones profesionally: simultaneity between distant events isn't a fact you can look up, it's a convention you pick so your nodes can agree on something. Lamport clocks, vector clocks, same lesson.
2. So I buy that argument — Rietdijk/Putnam I think, also told as the Andromeda one where two people passing on the street disagree about whether the alien fleet has launched yet — that if everybody's "now" is equally real and there's only one reality, then events in my future are already real. Block universe: past, present, future all just sitting there in the 4D manifold.
3. Except I also think the future is genuinely open, not fixed, not already written down. I lean Everett (no collapse, the wavefunction just does its unitary thing) *and* I think there's real objective chance in the world, not merely my ignorance of something.
4. And honestly I don't buy that no frame is special. Cosmology hands you one for free: observers comoving with the expansion, the frame where the CMB looks isotropic. If I had to name the universe's actual now, I'd use that slicing, and I think that's a fact about the world rather than a convention.
5. I've got my own name for the picture I actually hold, which is "append-only time": the universe is an append-only log, the block is the log, and becoming is just the fact that entries get appended at the head and nobody can read past the head pointer. I take the frame in (4) to be what supplies the head pointer. I tell myself this is fully compatible with (2) — same block, just an index into it.
6. Consciousness has a thick present, a couple hundred milliseconds of it rather than a knife-edge instant, and I want that window to be where the appending actually happens.
7. The past/future asymmetry is explained by entropy and records — there are traces of the past and none of the future — not by any metaphysical becoming. Though I can't tell whether that's actually a claim or just a redescription of (2).
8. None of this makes deliberation pointless. My deciding is part of the machinery that produces the log entry, so a fixed block and choices that matter are fine together.
9. And if the future is already real, nothing in my view rules out backwards time travel, so I kind of expect it to be possible in principle.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these genuinely conflict and which only look like they do? For each real conflict, rank them by how bad they are rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say what the cheapest edit is that repairs it and what I give up by making that edit. Then tell me the single change that would buy the most coherence across the whole set. I'm not going to go read papers, so no bibliography and no "go look up X" — argue it out with me directly. Use the systems analogy where it genuinely helps, but call me out if you think an analogy I'm leaning on is doing work the physics doesn't actually support.
