Write me a short story set in a compensation tribunal that settles injury claims by ranking how bad different people's suffering is. The tribunal's trick is a "carrier" — someone who's signed up to have each claimant's worst minute replayed inside their own nervous system, one after another, and then hand in an ordering. I want the story to actually collide with the problems in that setup rather than just narrate around them. Three I care about: the carrier's own scale seems to stretch or go numb somewhere around the fortieth playback, so the ordering comes out differently depending on what order they were played in; a woman who's had the same spinal injury for twenty years calls it manageable while a man with a clinically identical injury six weeks out calls it unbearable, and the tribunal has to decide whether those two are even claiming the same amount; and there's one pair where every test says the pains are equal but the carrier is certain one is worse, because of what it costs that person to keep going. End unresolved but not mushy — the tribunal still has to issue a ruling.

Around 900 words, no scene headings inside it. Then a section called "What I was doing" (300 words or so) saying which of the carrier's verdicts you meant to be trustworthy and which you meant to be contaminated, and how you handled the worry that this isn't interpersonal comparison at all, just a run of comparisons the carrier is making against their own earlier self. No references or bibliography, please.
