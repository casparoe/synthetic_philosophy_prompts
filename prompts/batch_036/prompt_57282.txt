I am a doctoral candidate in history and philosophy of science drafting the central section of a paper for an interdisciplinary panel on information and narrative. The paper reads Borges's short story "Funes the Memorious" (first published in 1942, collected in *Ficciones*) against the modern thermodynamics of information, and I need help making sure the physics is carrying the interpretive weight I am placing on it, rather than the other way round.

Here is the reading I want to defend. Please test it rather than flatter it. On the standard Bennett-style resolution of Maxwell's demon, the demon's measurements can in principle be carried out reversibly, so the entropy bill is not paid at the moment of looking; it is paid when the demon's memory must be reset in order to close the cycle and run the engine again. The familiar objection is the deferral reply: a demon with an unbounded register never needs to reset, so it can postpone the payment indefinitely. Ireneo Funes is, on my claim, precisely that demon — a being whose memory is never full, who cannot overwrite, and who therefore never incurs Landauer's cost. And Borges, I want to argue, supplies the premise that the deferral reply is usually accused of missing: a memory that is never erased is also a memory that cannot think. Funes's private numeration system, in which every number receives its own arbitrary name and no rule generates the next, is a code with no compression and no shared structure; the narrator's objection to it amounts to saying that it is not a system at all. Generalization, abstraction, concept formation, and sleep all look on this reading like erasure operations — logically irreversible mergings of distinct records into one — and Funes can perform none of them. He can only replay a day's worth of memories, an operation that costs him a further day. To think, in other words, is to delete.

What I would like from you:

1. An explicit correspondence table mapping the demon apparatus onto the story — register, measurement, feedback, cycle, reset, work extracted — with the story's counterpart for each term, and an honest blank where there is none.

2. The disanalogies, separated into those that are merely inelegant and those that are load-bearing for my thesis. I already know the largest one: Landauer's bound attaches to erasure, not to storage, so a being who never erases pays nothing, and the story cannot literally be about the thermodynamic price of remembering. Is there any respectable bridge across that gap — for instance, an argument about the cost of maintaining a register against thermal noise and error correction, or the generalized second law for feedback-controlled systems, in which information acquired by a demon has a quantifiable work value? On the latter: does Funes's hoarded information have any such value at all, given that he sorts nothing and extracts no work, and is therefore a demon who violates no law whatsoever? That is the feature of the story I find most difficult to accommodate, and I would rather it be pressed hard than explained away.

3. An assessment of how much of my reading depends on the contested parts of this literature — the charges that Landauer's principle is trivial, or circular, or that logical and thermodynamic reversibility come apart in both directions. If those charges are right, does the Borges section survive, and in what weakened form?

4. A verdict. Is the story a genuine contribution to the debate over the deferral reply — something that makes vivid a cost the physics leaves implicit — or is it a category mistake dressed in thermodynamic vocabulary, in which a cognitive limitation is mistaken for a physical one? If it survives, say what Borges shows that argument alone does not. If it does not, tell me so plainly and suggest what the story is actually good for.

Two constraints. Work from what you know; I have the secondary literature and do not want a bibliography, a citation list, or recommended reading. And do not invent or reconstruct quotations from either the story or the physics papers: if you are unsure of a detail — the manner of Funes's death, the precise terms of the narrator's objection, who proposed which principle and when — flag the uncertainty rather than confabulating a confident paraphrase. I would much prefer a reading that concedes a point to one that papers over it.
