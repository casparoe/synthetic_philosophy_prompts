A regional labour education centre has asked me to run a session for about thirty newly elected workplace representatives — warehouse, care, hospitality, food processing — meeting at six tables of five, with me facilitating and one assistant. Almost none have read Marx, and several are openly hostile to him on the grounds that if you agreed to the rate and the employer paid it, nothing unfair happened. I have one 90-minute block, cheap materials only, and payment in tokens redeemable for coffee and snacks at the break rather than money.

Design an activity in which the group works four short production rounds that vary two things independently: whether the workers receive the whole value of what they make, and whether the work is fragmented and dictated or whole and self-directed. The four rounds should fill out that pairing without anyone being told that is what is happening. The words exploitation and alienation, and any attribution to Marx, must not be spoken by me or printed on any handout until the debrief.

Please specify:

- The production task: a physically countable, quality-checkable artefact that unskilled adults can turn out at volume in three minutes, plus a rejection rule so that the pace requirement actually bites. Name the materials and the per-table kit.
- For each of the four rounds, in a table: role structure, pace rule, pay rule, and what gets recorded. In the rounds where the employer takes a cut, the rate must be posted, must equal the going rate the assistant pays for accepted units, and every worker must have a real, costless option to walk out — I want the contract to look unimpeachable on its face, because that is where the argument will come from.
- In the rounds where workers keep the whole product: a deduction schedule the table must vote on before it starts — replacement stock, a reserve fund, administration, common provision, and a line for a member who is injured and cannot work this round — so that the group discovers that "everything we made" does not settle itself.
- A private instrument each participant completes at the end of every round, taking seconds, that will later let the room see two separate axes rather than one.
- A debrief that fits the last 25 minutes: the order in which I reveal things, how the tally gets onto the flip chart, where the two concepts finally get named, and a scripted move that meets the objection "we signed up, we knew the rate, we were paid what we agreed" without first asking anyone to accept the labour theory of value.
- What will predictably go wrong on a first run — including the specific danger that my ratings instrument turns alienation into a job-satisfaction poll — and the cheapest fix for each.
- One closing paragraph: the single feature of Marx's account the activity cannot reproduce, which I must supply in my own voice, and the honest sentence I should say to the room about that limit.

Open with two sentences stating exactly what a participant should have worked out for themselves by the end of the fourth round. Then keep the whole design under 900 words.
