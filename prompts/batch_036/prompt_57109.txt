I have no academic training in philosophy; I have simply been reading about the mind on my own for several years, and lately I have become absorbed by higher-order theories of consciousness — the family of views, most often associated with David Rosenthal, on which a mental state is conscious not in virtue of anything intrinsic to it, but because some further state of the subject is directed at it.

Two details of this picture have lodged in my head, and I keep turning them over. The first is that the higher-order state is itself supposed to be unconscious, on pain of requiring yet another state to make it conscious, and then another, without end. The second is that the theory seems to permit the higher-order state to misfire: it may represent a first-order state that is not in fact present, in which case there would be a conscious state with nothing underneath it — a subject consciously furious without any first-order fury, say, or someone who has the experience of the dentist's drill a beat before the drill arrives. I may be exaggerating or mangling one or both of these points, and I would rather find that out from you than from a reader of the joke.

What I would like from you is a joke built out of this material, ideally one that leans on both features at once — for instance, by imagining some workplace in which the employee whose entire job is to keep track of what everyone else is up to is himself never tracked by anyone, and who one day files a report concerning a colleague's activity that never took place. I am not after a stock gag about philosophers being confused, or about consciousness being mysterious; the humor should land only for someone who recognizes the theory, so that the punchline depends on the regress or on the empty higher-order state rather than on any generic nerd humor. A few lines of setup and a clean punchline will do. Before writing it, please tell me briefly whether my two summary points are fair characterizations of the view, so that I am not circulating a joke that rests on a misunderstanding.
