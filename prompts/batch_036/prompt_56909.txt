A musician friend of mine wrote the programme note for a concert built around Schopenhauer, and glossed his aesthetics of music this way:

'Schopenhauer ranked the arts from architecture at the bottom to music at the top, the ranking tracking how close each art comes to the inner essence of the world. Music wins because, alone among the arts, it does not copy the Ideas — the fixed grades through which the will objectifies itself — but is a copy of the will itself, and that is why it moves us more powerfully than painting or tragedy ever can. This is also why music is the language of the emotions: a slow movement is sad in just the way a grieving person is sad, and the listener hears his own feeling in it. Since music and metaphysics have the same content, differing only in that the one addresses feeling and the other concepts, a symphony is in effect a treatise you can hear, and the composer knows precisely what he is saying. Wagner and Liszt saw the implication: if music is about the will rather than about appearances, it can carry a narrative, and the words of an opera exist to supply the particular events that the music on its own lacks.'

I found this compelling, but it sits badly with a vague memory that Schopenhauer thought the composer does not understand his own music, and I cannot tell which of us is confused. Take the note claim by claim and separate what a Schopenhauer scholar would accept as fair, what is a defensible simplification of something he did say, and what he explicitly denies. Then tell me which of its two load-bearing conclusions — that music sits at the summit of a single hierarchy of the arts, and that his view licenses narrative programme music with the libretto supplying content — survive correction, and whether the corrected doctrine makes his explanation of music's greater power stronger or weaker than the popularised version.
