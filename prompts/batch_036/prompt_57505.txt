I'm one of four people drafting the pay-differential rules for a worker co-op (about 200 members, very mixed skill levels), and every time we try to write down why some roles should be paid more, "deserves" splits into three or four notions that pull apart from each other. Before I read another chapter of the desert literature I want the spec.

Write the desiderata: what any theory of desert good enough to serve as the public justification for a written, applied pay rule has to deliver. Give the case behind each requirement, and make sure these show up somewhere in the list: two members with identical measured output, one of whom produced it while covering a sick parent and the other at ease; a member whose output is enormous only because of who she's teamed with, so there's no decomposition into individual contributions; a well-judged risky project that failed on bad luck sitting next to a reckless one that paid off; someone who front-loaded years of training at her own expense and wants to know whether that ever gets "used up"; and the flat conflict between what a member deserves for what he already did versus what paying him now would incentivize.

Then tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are one camp's priors dressed as criteria, which single one knocks out the most candidate theories, and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect pre-institutional desert plus contribution-as-the-desert-basis plus some responsibility condition is going to collapse, and I want the collapse argued rather then asserted. A couple of lines each, at the end, on how a contribution-based view, an effort/sacrifice view, and an institutionalist view do against the list.

Be straight about where you're least confident. If one of my five cases isn't actually hard, say which and say which requirement I'm over-reading into it. And tell me what would change your mind about the impossibility claim — what consideration would make you drop a requirement from the list as a prejudice instead of a desideratum.
