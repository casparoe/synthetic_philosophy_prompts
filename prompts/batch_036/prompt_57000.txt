For a book proposal I am cataloguing a colleague's verdicts on when an utterance of a slur derogates — that is, when it actually carries the contempt rather than merely mentioning a word that has contempt attached to it. Fix the word as a generic ethnic epithet; call it T. Her verdicts so far: a bigot's bare assertion that T are like that derogates; the same sentence delivered by an actor playing that bigot in a film that frames him as despicable does not; a philologist citing T in scare quotes does not; T embedded in the antecedent of a conditional — "if there are any T in this building, property values will fall" — derogates; an out-group comedian using T ironically to mock racists before a mixed audience derogates; an in-group member using T affectionately of in-group friends in their presence does not; and an out-group member who has been told by his in-group friends that he may use it derogates. The case she has not ruled on: an in-group member posts T affectionately, in its reclaimed sense, on an anonymous forum whose readers have no way of knowing she is in-group and mostly take the post as hostile. Predict her verdict. Lay out the two or more rules that fit her whole record equally well — noting which of them need a second factor, such as institutional framing, to survive the actor case — say what each predicts about the forum post, give me your best guess with reasons for preferring it, and name one further case that would split the surviving rules apart.
