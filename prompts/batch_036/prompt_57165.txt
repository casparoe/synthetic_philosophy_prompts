An assessment panel is drafting the uncertainty language for a report on a technology that has never been deployed at scale. It proposes two independent scales, in the manner of recent IPCC assessment reports: a likelihood statement about how probable each outcome is, and a separate confidence rating tracking how much relevant evidence exists, of what quality, and how far the experts agree. Two members of the panel are quarreling over whether that second scale is doing any philosophical work at all.

Write a dialogue between them. One is a Keynesian, who thinks Keynes's *A Treatise on Probability* already supplies the rationale for the two-scale convention: probability is an objective logical relation between a body of evidence and a hypothesis that is often not numerically measurable and not always even comparable across hypotheses, whereas "weight of argument" is a distinct dimension tracking the completeness of the relevant evidence, which can rise without the probability moving in any determinate direction. This member holds that identifying confidence with the width of a probability distribution gets the order of explanation backwards. The other is a Bayesian who concedes that a single number can mislead but maintains that everything Keynes wanted is recoverable inside probability theory — as imprecise credences, as hyperpriors over priors, or as the expected value of further information — so that weight is either a relabeling of spread or a disguised second-order probability.

Both should be equally fluent in Keynes's actual arguments and in the formal machinery the other invokes, and both should press hard on the points where their views yield different instructions for the report: whether to publish a bare interval or two graded numbers; whether the panel should recommend further research before any decision, and on what basis that recommendation could be computed if no probability is yet available; and what to write about the outcomes for which the panel sees no evidential ground for a number at all. Let each land genuine hits, permit concessions where they are warranted, and end where the disagreement actually runs out.
