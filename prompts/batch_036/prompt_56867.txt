I'm trying to write down, in advance, what any claim of the form "this culture / these values caused that economic outcome" has to be able to do before I'll take it seriously. Weber's protestant ethic argument is the canonical case but I want the rubric to be general, so it can be applied to any culture-as-cause story.

Give me the desiderata list, each requirement with the concrete case or worry that motivates it. At minimum it needs to deal with:

- the independence problem: if you only know a group had the relevant ethic because they did the thing the ethic predicts, you've redescribed the outcome, not explained it — so what does an independent measure of the value have to look like?
- reasons vs causes: Weber wanted an account to be adequate both on the level of meaning and causally, and it's not obvious you can have both at once when the actors' own avowals of the value are partly self-serving rationalizations of what they were doing anyway
- whose values: aggregate mean vs distribution, and the fact that it's often a minority of intense beleivers doing the causal work while the majority merely assents
- the retreat to elective affinity: does recasting the claim as an affinity between an ethic and an economic ethos preserve the causal content or quietly give it up?

Then mark which of your requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just the priors of one camp — institutionalists, rational choice types, cultural sociologists each demand different things and I don't want to smuggle in one camp's preferences as "adequacy." If some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly and show why; that's the part I most want to see. Finish with a couple of sentences on which single requirement does the most work ruling out otherwise attractive stories. No reading list.
