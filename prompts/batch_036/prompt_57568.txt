I spent thirty-five years measuring the low-temperature heat capacities of amorphous solids, and since retiring I have taken up the philosophy of physics with more seriousness than I expected of myself. This term the convenor of an interdisciplinary graduate seminar at a neighbouring university — a former colleague — has asked me to act as second marker on one of the written exercises, so that the marks can be moderated against an independent reading. The seminar is on the conceptual foundations of thermodynamics and information. I have read the submission twice and formed impressions that I would prefer not to share with you, since I want your marking to be independent of mine; my own confidence is weakest exactly where the physics shades into the philosophical literature, which is not my training.

The exercise was set as follows. It was to be written at home, in no more than 900 words, with library access permitted but not required.

> **Question.** In Szilard's one-molecule engine a single measurement appears to permit an amount of work *kT* ln 2 to be extracted from a heat bath at temperature *T*. (a) Explain why this does not, by itself, license a perpetual motion machine of the second kind. (b) Distinguish carefully the contribution that *measurement* makes to the thermodynamic accounting from the contribution that *erasure* makes, and say which of the two the modern treatment locates the cost in. (c) State whether Landauer's principle is a theorem of statistical mechanics or an empirical generalisation, and explain what would follow for the exorcism of the demon if it were the latter.

The candidate's answer, submitted in full:

> Maxwell's original demon appeared to violate the second law because it produced a temperature difference without expending work. Szilard reduced the difficulty to its smallest possible case: a single molecule in a box of volume *V*, in contact with a heat bath at temperature *T*. A partition is inserted, the demon determines which half the molecule occupies, and the partition is then used as a piston connected to a weight. Because the molecule is known to be on one side, it expands isothermally from *V*/2 to *V*, doing work *kT* ln 2 on the weight and drawing that energy wholly from the bath. Considered in isolation, this is a perpetual motion machine of the second kind: heat from a single reservoir converted entirely into work in a cycle.
>
> What prevents this is that the demon is itself a physical system and must be included in the accounting. For the arrangement to be cyclic, the demon must end each run in the same physical state in which it began; otherwise it is not a demon but a fuel tank. Szilard had already argued in 1929 that the step which must be paid for is the acquisition of the information itself, since a measurement is a physical interaction and cannot be had for nothing. Landauer's principle vindicates that diagnosis: erasing one bit held in a system at temperature *T* dissipates at least *kT* ln 2 of heat into the environment, and this minimum is attained only in the limit of an infinitely slow, quasi-static operation. The demon's determination of which half of the box the molecule occupies is therefore the irreversible act, and it must dump at least *kT* ln 2 into the bath, exactly balancing the work the demon gains. The subsequent need to reset the memory is a secondary matter: it guarantees that the demon cannot run up credit indefinitely, but the price has already been paid at the moment of looking.
>
> This accounting works because information and entropy are one quantity under two names. The Gibbs entropy of statistical mechanics and the Shannon entropy of information theory have the same functional form, −Σ *pᵢ* log *pᵢ*, and differ only in the base of the logarithm and hence in units. It follows that when the demon learns which half of the box the molecule occupies, the entropy of the gas is reduced by *k* ln 2, and that this is a genuine physical change in the gas rather than a change in the demon's state of mind. The erasure step later restores exactly this amount, so that the entropy of gas plus demon plus bath never decreases and the second law is unharmed.
>
> On part (c), Landauer's principle is a theorem and not an empirical generalisation. It follows from the one-to-one character of the microscopic dynamics: a logically irreversible operation, which maps two distinct memory states onto one, cannot be realised by a one-to-one map of the total phase space, so the lost distinctions must be exported into environmental degrees of freedom, and phase-space considerations then force the associated heat to be at least *kT* ln 2. The 2012 experiment in which the heat released on erasing a single bit held in a colloidal particle was measured therefore confirms a theorem, in the way that a Cavendish measurement confirms Newtonian gravitation. Those philosophers of physics who object that the principle is circular, or who suggest that the cost of erasure might be settled in some currency other than heat at temperature *T*, are in my view confusing the formal entropy with its physical realisation; the dissent, in any case, leaves the exorcism of the demon untouched.
