My sister and her mate Kwesi had a proper row at Sunday lunch and I've been chewing it over ever since. It started when our nan came up — she's got dementia, barely speaks these days, and Kwesi'd been reading about ubuntu and this idea that a person isn't simply born one but becomes one through the relationships they're in, so personhood is more something you achieve than something you have. My sister said that's a horrible thing to believe, because on that view someone like our nan slides down towards not-being-a-person as she loses the ability to relate, whereas the whole point of saying she's still fully a person is that nothing she does or fails to do can change it. Kwesi said he'd been misheard: the relationship isn't only hers to hold, the family is still in relation to her, so the personhood sits between them rather than inside her head, and her standing isn't up for a vote. She said that just moves the problem — now it turns on whether other people keep bothering, so if they stop, does she stop? He said no, you can't lose standing by being abandoned, since being abandoned is something done to you. She said he can't have it both ways: either the relationship is doing the work or the individual is, and if it's the relationship then a woman with nobody left has less of it, which he clearly doesn't accept. He said fine — but then a hermit who's opted out and a grandmother of twenty have to be exactly the same sort of thing, which he doesn't accept either, and there's a difference between saying personhood comes in degrees and saying people can be ranked. Neither of them would let it go, and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning. Can you take it move by move and say who's actually right at each point — not just who comes out ahead overall — and whether there's something neither of them has spotted?
