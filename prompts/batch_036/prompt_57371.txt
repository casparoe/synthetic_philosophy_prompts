I teach a first-year seminar, 24 students, 75 minutes, and they've only known each other a few weeks so the social stakes are still weirdly high. This week's theme is whether we can tell if someone arguing against us is playing straight. I don't want a discussion — I want a game they play first and name afterwards.

The thing I actually want them to feel: in public argument people collapse two seperate questions into one. Is this argument any good? And is this person sincere? Those come apart in both directions — an honest person can argue terribly, and someone playing for keeps can make a genuinely good point. And I think something worse happens once you start motive-hunting: you get worse at reading the argument itself, and the hunt becomes unfalsifiable, because whatever the other person says next gets re-read as further evidence of bad faith, including their denial. I want them inside that loop, ideally once as the hunter and once as the hunted.

One mechanic I already like, which you can build on or throw out: split the room in half, hand both halves the same set of written arguments, tell one half that every author is sincere and the other half that some fixed number of them are secretly arguing against their own views, then have each half steelman the arguments in writing and compare. My hunch is the suspicious half writes worse steelmen. If that's a bad hunch, say so and give me a better version of the same idea.

Then I need: full rules and roles, a minute-by-minute timeline, a payoff scheme that costs me nothing (no money, no candy budget — social stakes are fine), a materials list including whatever I have to prep the night before, and a debrief that maps specific moments from the run onto the argument-quality/arguer-sincerity split and onto the unfalsifiability loop.

Also tell me what will predictably break the first time I run it, including what to do if a student gets genuinely stung at being accused of bad faith, and whether I should keep the debate topics anodyne (parking, campus funding) or let them pick something they actually care about. And be frank about which part of real-world good-faith judgment the game can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. It should scale down to 14 if people are absent.
