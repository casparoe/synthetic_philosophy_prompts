So I finally read the careenium chapters of *I Am a Strange Loop* on a long flight and I've been arguing about it in a discord ever since, mostly with a friend who's a hard reductionist (physics PhD, thinks all the levels-of-description talk is poetry with extra steps). I tried writing down what I actually believe so I could tell whether I was just being slippery, and the list came out looking worse than I expected. Here's the package:

(1) causal closure — nothing but simms shoving simms, full stop. If you ask what made lever L move, the complete answer is a chain of collisions, and no simmball appears anywhere in it.
(2) but simmballs are real, not useful fictions. Real patterns in roughly the Dennett sense: the clumping is there whether or not anybody's watching, and it isn't made true by my deciding to see it.
(3) high-level claims are literally true, not just instrumentally handy. "The hunger-simmball triggered the foraging cascade" is true in the same way "the cue ball sank the eight" is true.
(4) the high-level causal graph earns its keep: it licenses counterfactuals — if that simmball hadn't formed, the lever wouldn't have moved — that the molecular description can't deliver in any tractable form, so it isn't just a redescription.
(5) functionalism: run a careenium long enough with the right couplings to a world outside it and you get a person with actual beliefs, not as-if beliefs.
(6) content is determinate: what a given simmball means is fixed by the whole causal role it plays, inside the chamber and out, so there's a fact of the matter about whether two simmballs in two different chambers mean the same thing.
(7) I reject strong emergence and I reject downward causation as a spooky extra force. My slogan, which I'm irrationally proud of: symbols don't shove the substrate around, the substrate shoves itself around in symbol-shaped ways.

The twist I keep getting pushed on is a variant he built me — call it the twin careenium. Two chambers, one full of magnetic simms and one a vat of colloidal blobs, radically different physics, coupled to the same outside world so their high-level graphs come out isomorphic. I want to say both contain simmballs with the same content, and that this isn't just an artifact of my choosing to read them that way. But he then pointed out that any chamber with enough moving parts can be decomposed into zillions of candidate "simmball" patterns, most of them garbage, and asked what picks out the real ones if not a compression scheme that is — awkwardly — relative to whoever's doing the compressing. I didnt have an answer, which is why I'm asking you.

So: audit it. Which of these genuinely pull against each other and which only look like they do? Rank the real tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say what has to give and what the cheapest revision costs me — including whether I get to keep the slogan in (7). Then tell me which single change would clean up the most damage, and which commitment you think I'm protecting without noticing.
