I've been asked to circulate a one-page statement of my position before a workshop on Wittgenstein's late remarks about certainty, and the plan is that someone senior spends the whole session trying to break it. I'd rather you break it first. Here's the package, roughly in the order I'd defend it:

(1) Hinge-type commitments — that there's an external world, that I have two hands, that the earth long predates me — are not things I know, and not things I'm justified in believing either. They're the scaffolding that makes justification possible. Demanding reasons for them isn't a hard question I'm failing to answer; it's a question that doesn't get asked at that level.

(2) Which is why the skeptic doesn't get refuted so much as diagnosed. Their global doubt isn't false, it's idle — it isn't hooked up to anything that could count as settling it.

(3) Ordinary justification is internalist. For garden-variety beliefs, what makes me justified is a reason I can actually cite and feel the force of, not some external reliability fact I have no access to.

(4) But hinge commitments are not all equally good. The flat-earther and the person certain the vaccines contain trackers are not just playing a different game — they're getting it wrong and it's worth telling them so. And the harder case: a community for whom an oracle's say-so settles empirical questions, where literally nothing we'd recognize as evidence would ever move them. I want to be able to say they're worse off, not merely different.

(5) Hinges are inherited, mostly by upbringing and training, and I don't think that's an embarrassment to admit.

(6) The river-bed picture is load bearing for me: which propositions are hinges isn't fixed forever, things migrate back and forth between the bed and the current.

(7) Conversion is possible, and it can be an improvement. Someone raised on a very different hinge-set can come over to mine, and when they do they haven't merely swapped one arational framework for another — something has gone better for them.

(8) I'm an anti-relativist. There's a fact of the matter about which hinge-set a person ought to have, independent of which community happened to install it in them.

So: go through these and tell me which ones pull against each other and how hard, and which pairs only look like they're in tension and fall apart once you spell them out — I suspect at least one of them does but I don't know which. For each real tension give me the cheapest revision and what it would actually cost me, keeping in mind I can't just walk away from the whole framework, that's the topic of the workshop. Rank the tensions by severity rather than handing me a list where everything is equally bad. I don't have a favorite among these and I'm not steering you toward one. And I'm not after the literature here — no bibliography, no who-says-what, just the structure of my own problem.

End with a single line reading exactly `Verdict: drop (n)`, where n is the number of the one commitment that has to go if I'm only allowed to sacrifice one.
