I would like a careful assessment of an arrangement I have made for the upcoming election, and of the objections a friend raises to it.

I live in a district where my party's candidate has won the last four races by margins of twenty-five points or more. In September I registered with an informal matching website that pairs voters like me, whose preferred candidates cannot lose where they live, with voters in districts where the race is genuinely even. My match and I have agreed on the following: on election day I will vote for the candidate she supports, whom I consider clearly worse for the country, and she will vote for the third-party candidate I actually favor. There is no mechanism by which either of us can confirm afterward that the other followed through; we have only each other's word, exchanged through a website that will delete the thread.

My reasoning is plain. My sincere ballot here cannot change anything; hers is one of a few thousand that could plausibly tip a close contest. By trading, each of us ends up with a ballot that might matter, cast where it might matter. For the first time in my life I feel my vote is doing something rather than merely being recorded.

My friend thinks the arrangement is corrupt, and offers three complaints. First, it does not reduce insincerity in the election, it doubles it: two ballots are now marked by people who reject the names on them, purely as payment for a favor from a stranger. The tally is supposed to tell the country what the electorate wants, and a practice of this kind would destroy that information while depending on nearly everyone's refusing to engage in it. Second, I am treating my ballot as a chattel to be bartered instead of as something I hold on behalf of others — a confusion she says is equally present when money changes hands, and no more excusable when the price is someone else's vote. Third, I will have helped elect a person I believe should not hold office, and I cannot launder that by pointing to the seat I gained elsewhere.

Please address the following. Is the trade permissible? Does her first complaint identify a genuine defect, or is she misapplying a test — and if the practice is self-undermining, what follows for the individual who participates in it now, when participation is rare? Is her distinction between a ballot I own and a ballot I hold for others a substantive claim, or merely a distaste for markets dressed up as principle? Does my causal contribution to a result in a district where I do not live make me complicit in a way that a voter who sincerely supports a doomed candidate in a close race is not? And does the unenforceability matter: if what I have really done is promise a stranger, is keeping the promise to her the weightier obligation, or the lesser one?
