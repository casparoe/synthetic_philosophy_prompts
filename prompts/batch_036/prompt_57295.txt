My formal epistemology group built this argument against van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I can't find where it breaks. Reflection says: if you know that at some later time your credence in P will be r, your present credence in P conditional on that knowledge should also be r.

(1) Rational credences are ones that can't be Dutch-booked across time — no bookie who knows your betting dispositions at both times can trade with you in a way that guarentees you a net loss whatever happens.
(2) Reflection is just an instance of (1). If your credence in P is .1 today and you know it'll be .9 tomorrow, today you'll sell a ticket paying $1 if P for 10¢ and tomorrow you'll pay up to 90¢ to buy it back — a sure 80¢ loss. So (1) requires C_now(P | C_later(P) = r) = r.
(3) But suppose you know your later opinion won't come from evidence. You've volunteered for a sleep-deprivation study, and you know that 40 hours in you'll be 90% convinced the researchers are withholding data from you. Right now you're 10% convinced of that.
(4) Nothing in (1) or (2) cares where the later credence comes from — only what bets you'll accept, and 40 hours in you'll accept them. So Reflection now requires you to be 90% confident today that they're withholding data.
(5) That's absurd. Adopting a belief because you know a distorting process will make you adopt it isn't rational, it's the opposite; the sane move is to leave yourself a note or precommit.
(6) So (1) fails as a general constraint. But (1) is the only thing holding up Reflection, and Reflection is essentially conditionalization run across time, so there are no real diachronic requirements on belief — only synchronic ones.
(7) Which is worse than it sounds: if nothing rationally links my beliefs now to my beliefs later, I can't criticize my future self's opinions, and gathering evidence now isn't a way of making my future self better informed.

I'd much rather keep (1) than swallow (6) or (7). Which step do you reject, and if the answer is that (1) needs a restriction, say exactly what the restriction has to look like and price it — which cases it now gets wrong, what else I lose.
