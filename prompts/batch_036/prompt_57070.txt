I'm on the standards committee for a farmed-seafood certification scheme — we audit maybe 200 sites, mostly salmon and trout, plus a handful of shrimp farms and one crab operation. Our own precedents: we banned ice-slurry chilling for warm-water species because it seems to prolong consciousness rather than end it; we allowed CO2 narcosis for trout only after a phase-in and only where the water's buffered; we've never certified a decapod site that ships animals live to market; and last year we approved a pilot outcome metric alongside the methods list — no corneal or vestibulo-ocular response within 20 seconds of stun.

Now a big member wants us to go fully outcome-based: scrap the approved-methods list, any slaughter method allowed as long as the site can demonstrate the metric at audit. The wrinkle is that nobody's got a validated unconsciousness indicator for shrimp or crab, so for those species the metric can't really be failed.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday. I want an actual recommendation (accept, reject, or accept with conditions, and which conditions), reasons the applicant can engage with rather than welfare boilerplate, and a serious treatment of their best point — that our methods list is already out of date and quietly locks in expensive kit only big producers can afford. Also tell me what we'd be commiting to on decapods either way, since I suspect that's the real decision hiding in this one.
