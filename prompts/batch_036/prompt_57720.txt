Compose a short joke about looping effects in psychiatric classification, written for an audience of philosophers of psychiatry and research clinicians who already know Hacking's distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds.

I would like the joke built out of the following material rather than the usual stock examples. A DSM revision work group has spent four years on a proposed new category, "anticipatory dysregulation": pervasive rehearsal of conversations that have not yet occurred, together with a marked tendency to look up symptom criteria and to conclude, correctly, that one meets them. The field trials are run the way DSM-III's were — reliability first, structured interviews, raters trained on the draft criteria — and they return an implausibly high kappa along with a prevalence that doubles between the first and second site visit. The work group's response is to tighten the definition by adding an exclusion criterion: the presentation must not be better accounted for by familiarity with the criteria.

The punchline should turn on the fact that this exclusion is not an escape from the loop but another turn of it — the category has become a better predictor of itself — and not merely on the general observation that labels change the people they label. If you can manage a second beat, get it out of the contrast with an indifferent kind: something that would behave the way quarks do, if only quarks subscribed to journals.

Constraints: keep it under about 150 words, with a clean setup and a single reversal; leave the mechanism implicit rather than explaining it inside the joke; and aim the humor at the taxonomy and the committee, never at anyone who might actually receive the diagnosis.
