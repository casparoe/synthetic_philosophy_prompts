You are a philosopher with deep expertise in Wittgenstein's later work, especially the discussion of private ostension and rule-following, and you are good at applying it to concrete cases rather than reciting standard interpretations. Please think carefully before answering. Before you rank anything, consider what would have to be true of a resource for it to supply a check that is genuinely independent of the diarist's own impressions.

For three years I have kept a diary of a sensory episode that reliably precedes my migraine attacks — a particular visual-and-somatosensory pattern for which I coined a private term, since there is no public vocabulary for it. I have recently realized that my re-identification of these episodes may be exactly what the private language argument is built to make problematic: when I judge that today's episode is the same kind as last month's, the only thing I can appeal to is that it strikes me as correct on this occasion, and its striking me as correct cannot itself be what makes it correct. This has stopped being an abstract worry for me, because the diary is now the evidence my neurologist and I are using to decide whether a preventive drug is working.

Rank the following four resources by how much of the work of a criterion of correctness each could do for my term. Assign each a score from 0 to 10 and justify each score in one or two sentences.

1. My memory of earlier episodes, together with the impression of recognition when a new one occurs.
2. A home-measured physiological correlate — skin conductance and sleep data from a wearable. Note the obvious complication: I selected and validated the correlate by looking for whatever tracked my own diary entries.
3. The term's predictive reliability. Episodes so labelled are followed by an attack within about a day four times out of five, and attacks are publicly observable events: vomiting, a missed shift at work, an emergency department record.
4. The possibility of teaching the term to someone else from my descriptions, so that they can apply it to my case from the outside.

Then state, in one or two sentences, whether any of these — separately or in combination — genuinely answers the worry that my re-identifications could be uniformly mistaken, or whether the diary's evidential value has to be understood in some entirely different way.

Lead with the ranked list and the four scores; put your reasoning after it. Hard limit: 450 words.
