For three years I've sat on my city's petition review committee: nine of us, all volunteers, who recommend to the council whether a citizen petition goes on the ballot. Our record so far, in order: a ban on single-use plastic bags, sent to the voters and passed; a petition that would have instructed our two state representatives to vote against a highway project, turned down because we cannot direct other bodies; a petition requiring a two-thirds council vote for any future tax increase, turned down as an attempt to bind councils that have not been elected yet; a petition requiring the city to publish its contracts online within thirty days, sent to the voters; a petition to fund a second ambulance by raising parking meter rates, turned down on the ground that spending is the council's business; a petition banning new distribution warehouses that by its own terms would take effect only if two neighboring towns adopted the same ban, turned down as "a measure that might never become one"; and an advisory question asking whether the city should explore merging its water system with the neighboring district, sent to the voters even though it bound nobody, because we wanted to know how people felt before deciding.

Next week we take up a petition to create a nine-member civilian board with power to subpoena internal police-affairs files and to require the city manager to explain publicly any decision not to discipline an officer. Its signatures are comfortably over the threshold. It appropriates no new money, using existing council chambers and up to forty staff hours a year, and it takes effect only once the city attorney certifies in writing that the subpoena power is lawful under state law.

Which way should we vote to be most consistent with our own record? Tell me which precedent is the closest analog, what more than one rule could be extracted from that record, what each of those rules says about this petition, and what principle we would be committing ourselves to by each possible vote — including if the honest answer is that we should send a narrower version instead. I voted for the advisory water question and against the warehouse ban and I still can't say what I was following when I did, so please tell me how confident you are in your recommendation and which single step in it you are least sure of.
