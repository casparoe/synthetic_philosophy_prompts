i'm in a metaethics seminar this term and our TA runs the discussion sections. she's been surprisingly consistent about which normative beliefs she thinks get wrecked by the evolutionary story and which ones survive it, and i want to work out her actual rule before next week's session.

her calls so far, as i've got them in my notes:

- "your own pain is bad" — survives. she said this is the one place where the selection pressure and the truth line up: damage really was bad for the animals that avoided it, so the mechanism wasn't fooling us.
- "parents owe their own kids special care" — survives, same reason.
- "sibling incest is wrong" — debunked. just a familiarity effect from being raised in the same house, she says, nothing to do with any moral fact.
- "inequality is bad in itself, over and above the suffering it causes" — debunked. status jockeying in small groups, and we'd have had the intuition even if inequality were perfectly fine.
- "you still have to keep a promise you made to someone who's since died" — survives, which shocked me. she said that one isn't a selected intuition at all, it falls out of principles you'd still endorse once you knew where your other intuitions came from.
- "gratitude: you owe something to someone who helped you at real cost to themselves" — survives, but she didn't say why, she just moved on.
- somebody asked about 2+2=4 and she said survives, we can check those and they aren't pulling much fitness weight anyway.

(she also says she isnt a moral realist, which i find confusing given how much tracking talk she does, so maybe that bears on what "debunked" even means for her.)
