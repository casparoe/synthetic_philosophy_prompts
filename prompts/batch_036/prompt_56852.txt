You're a philosopher who also writes deadpan comedy — basically the person a decision theorist would hire to write their satire. Before drafting, brainstorm out loud for three or four bullets: what does the internal logic of this contest actually force, where does the superrational reasoning bite, and what does the sponsor have to disclaim to protect himself? Then write the piece. American English, please.

Setup: a tech foundation is running a revival of the old Hofstadter-style "luring lottery" stunt, and I want their Official Rules document — numbered clauses, dry legalese, around 700 words, no winking and no footnotes explaining the philosophy. The mechanics, which the rules themselves should state: one grand prize of $500,000 split evenly across all valid entries, so the more people enter the less each gets; the foundation insists the ideal outcome is that a small, exactly-right number of superrational people enter once each, on the grounds that anyone who enters has thereby reasoned identically to everybody else who enters. Each entrant must write on thier card how many entries they're submitting and why that number is right.

The humor has to come from the fine print quietly taking real positions, so include: an eligibility clause defining a superrational entrant that undoes itself the moment you apply it; a provision for what happens if you're the only one who reasoned correctly and everyone else in the pool is a causal decision theorist; a Platonia clause disclaiming liability for the timeless Platonic versions of you that already contain a record of having entered, plus a waiver of any claim that your actually mailing the card added anything to the structure; a mixed-strategy section for people who entered with probability p; a tie-break rule for isomorphic copies of the same entrant; and a disputes clause naming a jurisdiction that can never convene.

Aim the satire at the logic, not at any real person — the foundation is invented. A reader should be able to tell what's being ironized without you saying so, and a specialist should feel the rules are fair rather than a strawman.
