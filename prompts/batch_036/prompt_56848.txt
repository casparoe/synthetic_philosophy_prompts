I build distributed monitoring systems for a living, and I've been reading around pain asymbolia: patients with insular damage who can localize a pinprick, rate its intensity, and describe it in ordinary pain vocabulary, yet report that it doesn't bother them and sometimes laugh during the examination. The philosophy around this case has been circling for the better part of two decades, and I can't see anyone moving anyone.

As I understand the positions, roughly: one camp, following Grahek, holds that asymbolics genuinely feel pain and that the case shows feeling a pain and being moved to stop it coming apart, which puts pressure on the idea that pain's badness just is its felt unpleasantness. Bain's evaluativist response is that what's missing is the negative evaluation of the body region, so asymbolic episodes aren't unpleasant at all. Klein's imperative account says the pain still carries its command, protect this body part, but the command has no authority over the rest of the system, as if an alert were routed to a queue no consumer ever reads. Then there's the more deflationary line, which I associate with Klein and Duval, that asymbolia may not be a clean single deficit but part of a broader syndrome of indifference, which would make it a poor thought experiment altogether.

My hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis and not something I want defended by default: the stalemate isn't about the patients but about the operational test for 'feels pain'. Each camp defines it by a different criterion, self-report, discrimination, avoidance behaviour, affective engagement, and since these patients pass some criteria and fail others, every side can claim the same datum. In my own terms, they disagree about whether pain is defined by the payload of the alert or by whether anything downstream acts on it.

Diagnose this rather than adjudicate it. Is the disagreement verbal, a case of one word doing phenomenological and functional work simultaneously, a hidden empirical question about whether the deficit is pain-specific, or two groups answering different questions under a single heading? Say what would count as progress on your diagnosis, or argue that none should be expected.
