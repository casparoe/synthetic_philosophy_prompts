I'm writing the final for an upper-level seminar on consciousness and I want one 45-minute essay question on the color phi phenomenon and the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction. The answer nearly every student gives is just "both stories predict the same verbal reports, so there's no telling them apart, and that's Dennett's whole point" — I want the question to make that move cost them something. So build it around a wrinkle: suppose that during a color-phi run, a forced-choice probe delivered immediately after the first flash shows above-chance discrimination of that spot's color, on trials where subjects insist they never saw a stationary spot at all, only a single spot moving and changing color halfway. Have them work out what each of the two accounts actually predicts about that result, whether the result discriminates between them, and what the side that comes out worse gets to say back. Then give me a model answer of about 700 words and a marking scheme out of 25 that seperates an A from a B by naming specific moves — I want real marks reserved for noticing that the probe result may be equally available to both stories, and for saying what kind of finding would genuinely favor one. Also flag the one or two mistakes almost everybody will make.
