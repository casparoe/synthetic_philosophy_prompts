I've been trying to figure out when two people are allowed to just keep disagreeing, going purely by my gut on cases. Here they are.

1. Two jurors, same trial, same testimony, they've sat in deliberations for a week and told each other everything. One's at like 0.9 the guy did it, the other's at 0.3. My verdict: that can't be okay. One of them is doing something wrong, and if they're both honest and both competent they should end up somewhere together.

2. Me and my wife, on whether our 13-year-old can take the train into the city to a concert with a friend. We have laid out literally everything we know: same crime numbers, same route, same kid, same two other parents' experiences. She's at maybe 0.15 something bad happens, I'm at 0.02. My verdict: totally fine. We could keep disagreeing like this for years and neither of us is being an idiot.

3. Two economists who have read all the same papers and run the same regressions, disagreeing about whether raising the minimum wage costs jobs. My verdict: fine, and I'd say they're both being reasonable even though I can't say what they're each getting at.

4. My friend who's a charge nurse and me, on whether her hospital will still have her position in six months. We've traded everything she's allowed to trade, and there's a bunch of internal stuff she genuinely can't tell me. Still disagree. My verdict: obviously fine — the gap is in what's sharable, not in how we think.

5. A public preprint war where both researchers post their numbers in the open, everyone reads everything everyone says, and after two years they haven't budged. My verdict: at least one of them is performing, not reasoning.

6. Me at a poker table against a guy who knows I know that he knows I know. We have opposite beliefs about whether he's bluffing. My verdict: fine, and unremarkable — I've got my cards, he's got his.

7. Two people who grew up in different religions, both smart, both have read the same arguments and the same history, neither is hiding anything. My verdict: they're allowed to keep disagreeing, and it would be arrogant to say otherwise.

8. My brother and me on whether our mom's cancer will respond to the treatment. Same oncologist, same scans, same survival figures read out loud at the kitchen table. I'm at maybe 0.6 it works, he's at 0.85. My verdict: I want to say one of us is fooling himself, but I can't say which, and I really don't want to say it's him.
