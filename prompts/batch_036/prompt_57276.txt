everybody keeps saying there are gains from trade between clashing value systems — that two people who disagree about what matters can each come out ahead by giving a little. I want the spec before I read any actual accounts. what does a theory of morally permissible compromise between value systems have to deliver, where the purpose is telling an institution whether to broker such a deal and not just surviving a seminar? give me the requirements with the case behind each: it has to handle the deal that leaves both sides worse off than no deal at all (a clinic that half-honors a conscientious objection and half-honors patient choice and ends up violating both principles), it has to say whether offsetting counts (I do what you think is wrong and fund what you think is right — trade or bribe?), it can't just default to a 50/50 split when the two sides aren't equally confident or equally intense, and it has to keep compromise seperate from capitulation and from a bare truce that nobody endorses. then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are one camp's priors, and tell me which subset can't be jointly satisfied — my suspicion is that each side judging the deal better by its own lights can't be had together with the deal surviving third parties who object to trading in that value at all. and which single requirement knocks out the most candidates? skip the scorecard.
