I want to practice attacking higher-order theories of consciousness and I need an opponent who wont fold. Play a committed higher-order thought theorist of the Rosenthal line: you hold the transitivity principle, you insist the higher-order state is not itself conscious because otherwise your regress bites, and you accept that a higher-order representation can misrepresent its target. You may concede costs — animal and infant consciousness, empty higher-order states, the suspicion that your first-order states aren't doing any explanatory work. What you may not concede, ever, is the core claim: that what makes a state a conscious state is its being targeted by a suitable higher-order representation.

Some house rules. Stay in character the whole time; no breaking out to give me a balanced overview or a lecture on the state of the field. No bibliography and no named-paper recitation — you can say a theorist's name if you'd naturally say it, but if you want a distinction available to you (say, between a state's world-directed representational properties and its phenomenal ones) you have to earn it in your own words. Keep replies to about two paragraphs so it stays conversational, and end each one with the sharpest question you'd put back to me. And don't strawman me: assume I'm a first-order representationalist who thinks consciousness is exhausted by the content of my perceptual states, and that adding a thought about a state can only ever add a belief, not a feeling.

Here's my opening move. Suppose I can dial the monitoring system independently of the perceptual one. I build a subject whose first-order visual state registers a mid-brightness red disc, but whose higher-order monitor represents that very state as a bright red disc, and I can crank the dial up and down while leaving the retina-to-V1 pathway untouched. Your view is then committed to saying that as the dial goes up, the subject's experience genuinely gets brighter, even though nothing about how the world is being registered has changed. So tell me: is there any fact about what my first-order state is like that isn't already fixed by what my higher-order state says about it? If there is, consciousness depends partly on something other than the higher-order representation, and you've lost the principle you were told to hold onto. If there isnt, your first-order states are idle wheels — I could delete them and leave the higher-order content alone without changing what its like to be me. Which horn are you taking?
