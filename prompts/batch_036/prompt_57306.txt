I sit on the four-member standards committee that maintains the answer keys for a university judgment lab's calibration app. The app runs short simulated vignettes in which a user plays an agent and reports a probability; points are awarded by a quadratic scoring rule against whatever key we publish, and the keys are also the lab's research instrument, since the reports are the dataset. Four of our vignettes turn on memory erasure and repeated questioning, and our rulings so far are these. In **Lamp**, a fair coin decides only whether a lamp shows red or green; the first release re-prompted the identical question three times in the tails branch and once in the heads branch, we keyed the answer at 1/2, we rejected a user petition to lower the key on the minute that being asked again is not being told something, and we then re-released the vignette with a single prompt in each branch. In **Two Doors**, heads means the agent is questioned once and the session ends, tails means she is questioned twice with the first questioning wiped from her memory; we keyed 1/3, marked 1/2 incorrect, and the chair's minute read that she should expect to be one of the twice-asked. One member dissented for 1/2 and predicted that, on his line, we would have to key 2/3 in any variant where the agent is told she is at her first questioning. In **Prelude** we put the same question, and offered the same one-point ticket paying on heads, before the Two Doors protocol begins, while the agent's memory is intact and she has read the whole script; we keyed both the probability and the ticket price at 1/2, and minuted that the key is indexed to the moment of asking, not to the vignette. In **Told First**, the agent is truthfully told at the questioning that it is her first; we keyed 1/2, the same member dissented again for 2/3, and the minute records only that the key changed upon the disclosure, not why it changed. (A postdoc maintains that the whole battery repackages a well-known puzzle from the literature, but the committee has never discussed that literature and our minutes cite nothing, so I would rather the analysis proceed from our own record.)

The case in front of us is **Ledger**. It uses the Two Doors protocol verbatim — same coin, same wipe, same question put at each questioning, same script shown to the user in advance — and the only change is in the payment engine, which a graduate student rebuilt over the summer for the mobile release. The new engine cannot score more than one report per session: it averages whatever the user reports at each questioning and scores that single number once, and a second setting discards all but the final report. The engineer's note says the agent's information is identical across all three settings, that the report maximizing a user's expected points is 1/3 under the old score-every-report engine, and that it is 1/2 under both new settings; he also says the old engine cannot be restored for Ledger alone without holding up the entire mobile release, and our freeze is in nine days. The committee is split evenly. One member holds that a key records what the agent ought to believe, that Ledger's agent has exactly the evidence Two Doors' agent had, and that if the rebuilt engine now pays users more for reporting 1/2 then it is the engine that is miscalibrated, not the key; she notes that a third of our users are clinical residents whose probability judgments the app is meant to train, and that keying Ledger at 1/2 would mean publishing a number we regard as wrong for the sake of a payment schedule. The other holds that we have never keyed anything except the number a user should be prepared to stake points on, that the Prelude minute says as much, and that consistency now requires 1/2 for Ledger together with a rewritten rationale line for Two Doors; he adds that on his reading our original Lamp key was simply an error, since the point-maximizing report under three prompts in the tails branch was 1/4, and that our having patched the vignette rather than the key shows we did not believe our own minute.

Please draft the recommendation I will circulate before the freeze. I need the candidate rules that could account for our record laid out explicitly — call them what you like, but show me which of them can explain Lamp, Two Doors, Prelude and Told First simultaneously, whether any of the four keys resists reconciliation under a single rule, and which of the four is the closest analog to Ledger and on what grounds. Then give me a concrete recommendation for Ledger: a single key, a two-part key that separates the probability the agent should hold from the number she should be willing to be scored on, a conditional release, or a decision not to release — argued so that the member on the losing side has something to answer other than a restatement of my own position. State plainly what principle the committee would be adopting with each option, including what becomes of the chair's Two Doors minute and of the rejected Lamp petition if we go one way, and what becomes of the interpretability of our calibration data if we go the other. Close with one further vignette we could pilot that would force the surviving rules apart at a point where our own intuitions about the key are firm enough to break the tie, and say what result would count for which rule.
