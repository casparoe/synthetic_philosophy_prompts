What I actually care about is an argument I ran into on the free Stanford encyclopedia site about this guy Frankfurt and his made-up case: there's a dude who's deciding whether to shoot someone, and somebody else is standing by who wants him to shoot, and that second guy is fully prepared to step in and force the outcome if he sees any sign the first guy is going to decide against it — but the first guy decides to do it on his own, so the second guy never has to lift a finger. The point is that the shooter had no real alternative, he couldn't have done otherwise, and yet everyone still thinks he's responsible for it. And the conclusion people draw is that "could he have done otherwise" was never the important question; what matters is whether the action came out of the person's own machinery — their own wants and their own weighing of reasons — instead of being shoved from outside. Then there's the pushback I also read, which is that the machinery doesn't come from nowhere: if somebody installed the desires, and installed the endorsement of those desires too, then saying "it came out of her own psychology" is just describing the crime, not answering it. History is supposed to matter.
