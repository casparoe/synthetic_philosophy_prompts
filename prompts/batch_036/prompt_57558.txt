Embarrassing backstory: my nine-year-old asked why her friend has leukemia and, without thinking, I said maybe his soul needed to work through something. Raised Hindu in Guyana, wife's family aggressively secular, and I've spent the week wondering if I actually believe that or was just homesick. So I want a diagnostic: a decision tree that starts at "I said something karma-flavored and don't know what I meant" and ends at a named position — or at "unnamed, nobody defends this, here's why not."

Build the branches from the logical space, not from a list of traditions. The splits I care about: does the mechanism need an administrator keeping a ledger, or does it run itself, like fire burning whoever grabs it; what carries over — the act, the intention behind it, or the disposition the act leaves in you; does it settle within one life or need rebirth to balance; can merit be handed to somebody else, say a dead parent, or is the account strictly non-transferable; and does any of this license a claim about why that particular child is sick.

Then locate the real positions in the grid, and say which cells come out empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unownable, or just unexplored. I've been calling one corner "hydraulic karma" — no judge, no ledger, consequence built into the act itself — and I suspect that's where I actually live, so make sure its reachable. Flag any pair of leaves your questions can't separate. American English please.
