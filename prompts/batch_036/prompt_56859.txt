I defend my dissertation in three weeks and my advisor sits on the committee. Chapter 4 reconstructs the position on moral demandingness she has defended for twenty years, and I have built the reconstruction entirely out of the verdicts she has given, in print and in seminar: wading into a shallow pond to save a drowning child at the cost of a ruined suit is obligatory, and so is wiring the same sum to an agency that saves a child on the other side of the world; giving a kidney to a stranger who will otherwise die is not obligatory, on the grounds that the body is not a resource one may be required to draw down; donating a year's discretionary income, roughly ten thousand dollars, is obligatory; choosing between a teaching career and a better-paying career taken up in order to donate a third of it is a choice morality cannot settle; forgoing a child so as to give the rearing costs away is not required, because a life stripped of the projects that make it the agent's own is not a life morality may demand; and being tired, or arriving late, is no excuse when you are the only person within reach who can carry water to a dozen people.

The case I put to her in Chapter 4 is one I invented. After a flood the state and nearly every other donor have defaulted, so the shortfall is enormous. The agent holds $150,000 in savings that she has repeatedly said carry no personal meaning. Giving all of it would save on the order of four hundred lives, and would leave her salary and way of life intact though without any safety net. Nothing non-fungible in her own sense — no body part, no relationship, no vocation — is touched. Her fair share, had everyone complied, would have been about $3,000.

I argue she is committed to saying the whole sum is required. Predict what she will actually say, and commit to a prediction rather than laying out the branches for me to choose among. Her verdicts look to me compatible with at least three principles: a ceiling on how much well-being morality may cost an agent; an exemption keyed not to the size of the cost but to the kind of good at stake; and a duty capped at one's fair share under full compliance, which others' defaults do not inflate. Say which she is running, what each predicts about the flood case, which of her earlier verdicts is doing the most work in your answer, and which further case put to her in the defense would separate the surviving principles most cleanly. Then tell me where my reconstruction is most likely to be wrong.
