In three weeks I am presenting a paper at a workshop on the history of democratic accountability, and I need a prep sheet for the question period rather than a defense of the paper.

My thesis is that the Athenian assembly's much-praised capacity to reverse itself was not a general institutional virtue but what I call cost-bearing reversibility. Revision occurred only where someone was willing to propose it and to absorb the political cost of proposing it; Athens's accountability machinery — prosecution of generals on their return, audits of officeholders, the standing risk of being charged with having deceived the people — concentrated that cost on precisely the men positioned to call for revision, and so suppressed revision exactly when the stakes were highest. Mytilene is the cheap case: the mood turned overnight, a second ship was dispatched, and nobody paid for the first decree. Sicily is the expensive case: Nicias resisted withdrawing while withdrawal was still possible partly because he dreaded the reception awaiting a general who came home without an order of the assembly behind him — a dread Thucydides has him articulate at length — and the expedition was destroyed in consequence. The trial of Socrates is the case in which a cheaper alternative was procedurally available at the penalty phase, the defendant declined to propose one worth taking seriously, and the second vote ran further against him than the first.

The room is mostly ancient historians: two who read Thucydides as a literary construction, one institutionalist who cares about decrees and procedure, plus two political theorists who use Athens as a case in democratic theory. Nobody there works on Plato as philosophy.

Give me the seven or eight objections this audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do, each phrased the way its raiser would phrase it; a two-minute reply to each; a note on where each reply is softest; what I should concede in my opening minutes to defuse the predictable ones; and the single reply that has to land for the paper to survive. End with the question I am hoping nobody asks.
