Ok so I'm deep in a novel and I've hit a problem that's really philosophy, not plot, and I figure you can untangle it faster then I can.

My narrator is unreliable first-person, she lies to herself constantly. I wanted a scene where the mask slips and she says something flat-out Moore's-paradoxical — "it's raining, but I don't believe it's raining." Obviously that's absurd to say out loud. What I can't get straight in my head is *why*. Both halves can be true. Somebody else could rationally believe both of them about her, no sweat. So the absurdity isn't in the proposition, it's somewhere else, and I don't know how to characterize that somewhere else.

Three things I need:

1. Why does it break when she says it but not when it's shifted? "It was raining, but I didn't believe it" sounds like a totally normal confession, and "it's raining but she doesn't believe it" is just gossip. Any account of the paradox has to explain why the first-person, present-tense, sincerely-asserted version is the only broken one. Does the usual explanation actually manage that, or does it quietly lean on the thing it's supposed to explain?

2. The omissive/commissive split — "p but I don't believe p" versus "p but I believe not-p." To my ear those go wrong differently, like one is almost tragic and the other is just incoherent. Is that a real difference with a real explanation behind it, or am I projecting because I like the sound of the first one?

3. Here's the actual novel problem. There's a stretch where she's demonstrably confabulating, inventing reasons for what she's just done in real time and believing them. If someone's self-knowledge is broken like that, does the paradox still bite? Is it secretly presupposing a unified agent with transparent access to her own beliefs — and if so, can I let her say the sentence sincerely and have it *not* be absurd? That would be a great scene but I don't want to cheat my way into it.

Bottom line up front: two or three sentences telling me whether I can write that scene honestly, then the reasoning behind each of the three. Hard cap 600 words, and no reading list at the end, I just want the argument.
