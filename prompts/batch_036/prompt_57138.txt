Whenever I raise Anscombe's pamphlet against Oxford's honorary degree for Truman, the discussion stalls on the same sentence: 'Truman had no choice.' I want to be clear what that sentence is even claiming before I argue against it. Set out the distinct theses it might express — that no alternative would have ended the war at a lower cost in innocent life; that the alternatives had already been foreclosed by earlier decisions, such as the insistence on unconditional surrender, which Anscombe herself calls 'the root of all evil'; that nothing else was politically or psychologically available to him in the summer of 1945; that declining to decide was itself a deciding — together with any readings I have missed, including ones on which the slogan is not about alternatives at all. For each, say whether it is true, what would settle it, and whether it would answer Anscombe's charge, which as I read it concerns the kind of thing Truman was willing to do rather than how good his options were.
