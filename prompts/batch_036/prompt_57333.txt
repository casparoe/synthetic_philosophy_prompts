I'm on the ops committee for a shared-instrument consortium — eleven labs across four universities that book time on a few very expensive machines. Each lab is supposed to log its usage, flag it when it leaves an instrument out of calibration, and credit other labs' students on papers coming out of the shared work. Compliance is patchy and everyone complains. Two proposals are up for the fall:

(A) a public ledger — every lab sees a running record of everyone elses flagged-vs-unflagged incidents and uncredited coauthorships, and any lab whose record falls below the consortium median loses booking priority for the following month.

(B) the same ledger, but a sanction lasts one month, is wiped clean afterward, and no lab can be sanctioned in two consecutive months.

Half the committee wants A because it's the only thing that makes repeat free-riding actually costly. The other half says the ledger will be noisy — incidents get misattributed, students graduate mid-investigation, instruments drift for reasons nobody caused — and that A will spiral into grudges where labs retaliate over accidents. One member thinks the whole debate is beside the point: labs join and leave the consortium and personnel rotate every couple years, so these are really just one-shot interactions and no ledger can change behavior.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before we vote. Recommend A, B, or some third concrete rule, explain the reasoning so the non-game-theorists on the committee can follow it, and give the strongest honest case for whichever option you're turning down.
