I'm a controls engineer, and for the last year and a half I've been the functional-safety person on a closed-loop insulin dosing project — the artificial pancreas kind of thing. Fluent in fault trees, HAZOP worksheets, barrier analysis, SIL targets, all of it. Philosophy background: roughly none.

Here's why this is suddenly urgent for me. We've hit a fork in the design. Either the controller actively commands insulin — it decides glucose is trending high and pumps a correction bolus on its own — or it only ever withholds and alarms, meaning it can shut off delivery and buzz at the patient but never initiates anything. On our models the expected patient outcomes are about the same either way. And I have this very strong intuition that the first option is a much bigger deal morally: if the controller commands a bolus that bottoms somebody out, thats the device killing them, and if it misses a bad trend and they crash anyway, that's the device letting them down. The ethicist on our review panel basically laughed at me and said the acting-versus-omitting distinction is worthless once you hold outcomes fixed, and that all I'm really describing is which element sits further upstream in the causal chain.

So here's what I want. Fault trees, top events, initiating events and failed barriers, demand mode versus continuous mode, single points of failure, detectability, ALARP, and the counterfactual you have to pick when you ask what the world looks like without the safeguard in it. Do the translation as faithfully as you can manage, and please don't smuggle moral premises into my jargon and hand it back to me labeled as translation.
