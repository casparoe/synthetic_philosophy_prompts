The distinction I want to build the project on (I'm aware I may have reinvented it, but it's mine for now): some period labels look like *boundary* terms, which pick out a stretch purely by what it sits between, so their content is mostly residual — after Rome, before the moderns. Others look like *trajectory* terms, which claim a direction of change and smuggle in an evaluation (rebirth, revolution, enlightenment), so they can't be neutral about whether what they name was good. And a third kind are *threshold* terms, which assert that some specific property switches on at a datable point — print, experiment, the mechanical philosophy, whatever you like. My hunch is that the deflationists and the defenders talk past eachother because they test all three kinds against the same criterion, roughly 'did the people at the time recognise it', and that's a fair test for threshold terms but a category error for boundary ones.
