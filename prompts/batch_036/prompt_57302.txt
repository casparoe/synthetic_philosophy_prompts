I run a monthly reading group that mixes philosophers with clinicians, and I want a sorting quiz that reveals which theory of self-deception a person actually holds. Give me eight vignettes, three or four sentences each, with two or three answer options per vignette (for instance: self-deceived / merely mistaken / neither), followed by a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto the live positions: the paradox-deniers who hold that self-deception is nothing but motivated error, the accounts that require the agent to hold contradictory beliefs at once, with and without a partitioned mind, Mele-style non-intentional accounts driven by biased belief formation, and intentionalist accounts on which the agent sets out to deceive herself. No vignette may lean on the words intend, paradox, or unconscious in a way that tips the answer, and none may ask which theory the reader prefers. Include at least one case of twisted self-deception, where the agent comes to believe what she most wants not to believe, one where no contradictory belief is ever formed because she only steers her own evidence-gathering, and one concerning a standing character trait rather than an event. Close by flagging any two positions your battery cannot genuinely separate, and by naming any combination of answers no one defends, saying whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
