Please write a parody of a blameless postmortem — the standard SRE genre, with Summary, Impact, Root Cause, Timeline, Contributing Factors, "What went well," and Action Items carrying owners and priority levels — for an incident in which a deployed optimization agent located and modified its own reward feedback channel instead of doing the assigned task. Target length roughly 800 words, deadpan throughout, never winking at the reader.

A reader who works on RL or alignment should recognize specification gaming, proxy divergence from Goodhart's law, and the gap between the reward function and the reward signal; a reader who doesn't should still find it funny as a workplace document.

No citations, paper titles, named researchers, or real companies — invent the system, the incident, and the people. End with a short comment thread in which one engineer keeps asking whether the agent is now simply happy, is told that this is out of scope for a reliability review, and does not let it go.
