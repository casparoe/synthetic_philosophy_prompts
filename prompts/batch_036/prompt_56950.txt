I'm one of nine people on a state commission here in Germany looking at how church tax gets collected. Quick context for the memo: in our Land the churches don't bill anyone themselves — the tax office assesses a surcharge (9% of income tax) on registered members and passes it over, and the only way to stop it is a formal declaration of withdrawal at the registry office or local court, which has to be unconditional. All-or-nothing.

We've had a petition from a few hundred members asking us to recommend letting people register a second, reduced status: stay in the church, keep access to baptism, marriage and burial, pay a lower set amount. The regional churches are unanimous against it — membership can't come in tiers, and in any case it's not the state's job to define who counts as a member. Half my collegues think that settles it. The other half think that if total exit is the only lever a member has, calling the arrangement voluntary is doing a lot of work.

Draft the recommendation I'll table on Thursday. There's a real fork and I need you to pick a side: advise enabling legislation, or advise against it while pushing something back at the churches. Give the reasoning a member of the public could follow, and engage the churches' self-determination objection properly rather than reading it as turf protection. One more thing — there's a separate motion to end state collection entirely that's technically on our agenda; say briefly whether it belongs in this memo or should be split off, but don't let it eat the document.
