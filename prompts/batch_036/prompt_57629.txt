"Aumann's theorem proves that rational people cannot agree to disagree. Its starting point is simple: two people who have been given the same evidence and who reason correctly must arrive at the same belief, so any disagreement between them is really just a difference in information. Now add the fact that each of them knows he is rational, and each knows that the other knows it. Then the moment they tell one another what they believe, their beliefs have to converge. Whatever is left standing after that exchange is not a disagreement but a symptom: one of them is lying, or refusing to update. Notice that this is not a claim about likelihood. It is a claim about possibility. Among people who are reasoning properly, an acknowledged disagreement simply cannot occur. That is why prices, which pool everyone's beliefs without anyone's having to argue, settle questions that debate cannot — and why the persistence of disagreement in politics and in religion tells us something about the participants rather than about the difficulty of the questions."
