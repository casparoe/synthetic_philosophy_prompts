**The anchor passage.** The line I keep returning to, in the Hong translation, is this: "The trend today is in the direction of mathematical equality, so that in all classes about so and so many uniformly make one individual." Read cold, that sounds to me like a claim about aggregation and effective sample size — N units behaving as one observation, so that a count of persons overstates the number of independent sources of judgment. Tell me whether it is that, or whether I am already distorting it before I have begun.

**Deliverable 2 — the translation.** Then render the claims one at a time into the vocabulary described above. For each: the closest available expression in my framework, a verdict of *clean*, *distorted*, or *no counterpart at all*, and, where distortion is involved, exactly what got bent and what the bending costs. A table is fine here. Four constraints on this section:

1. Treat "the public" as a question about what kind of thing it is. In my system every individual is identified and measured, and no aggregate has an identity, can be served a notice, or can be replied to. Kierkegaard's public is unlocatable and unanswerable, yet it is written about as though it could hold opinions and exert pressure. 2. Attend to the direction of construction. Our abstraction is built bottom-up from per-user data; his is authorized top-down by an organ that speaks in nobody's name and is nevertheless answered as though it spoke for everyone. Does the translation preserve that difference or flatten it?
