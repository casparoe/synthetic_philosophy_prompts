**The lever arm**

The standard quick argument goes like this. Two people pass each other on the street in relative motion. Each has a set of distant events they'd call simultaneous with right here, right now. Special relativity says those sets differ and that nothing picks out one of them as correct. Add the premise that reality tracks the now — everything and only what exists now is real — plus the premise that both walkers are equally real observers, so that what's real for one can't fail to be real for the other. Transitivity does the rest: my slice, your slice, and the slice tilted with respect to both get welded together, and before you know it you've swallowed a chunk of the future. Presentism is dead, block universe lives.

My worry is that the whole thing runs on a lever arm, and nobody ever says so out loud. The disagreement between two frames about which distant events are simultaneous with a given local event goes like vL/c² — relative velocity times distance, divided by c squared (ignoring the gamma factor, which is 1 for all purposes at these speeds). Which means the effect is essentially nothing at human scale. Two people strolling past each other at a brisk 1.4 m/s disagree about the far wall of a three-metre lab by roughly 5 × 10⁻¹⁷ seconds — tens of attoseconds. To get the two strollers to disagree by a full second you have to push the action about seven light years away; Sirius, at 8.6 light years, gives you a second and a bit. And to get the famous multi-day disagreement you need Andromeda, out at 2.5 million light years, where an ordinary walking pace shifts the slice by several days. The thought experiment only bites because the distance is astronomically large.

That matters, I think, because it changes what the argument is an argument *for*. On my reading it isn't showing that the future is real. It's showing that "now" can't be extended into a global, frame-invariant slice. Those are seperate claims and only the second one follows. What does the real work in the passage from no-privileged-foliation to block-universe is an extra premise: that becoming has to be global in order to be objective at all, that a notion of what has already happened which is indexed to a place isn't a notion of what has already happened. That premise is carrying all the metaphysical weight, and it is not a consequence of the Lorentz transformations. It's a picture.

And there's a rival picture the argument leaves completely untouched. Take becoming to be local: what has already happened, for me, is the content of my past light cone. That set is invariant — every frame agrees about which events could have influenced me. So the growing block survives; it just grows along worldlines rather than along hypersurfaces. Rietdijk–Putnam doesn't touch it, because the argument's lever is precisely the spacelike separation that this view declines to say anything about. Those events near Sirius that the two strollers disagree about aren't in either walker's past yet, and there's no pressure to call them real or unreal. They're simply not-yet-mine.

Two things I'm unsure about and would rather face than dodge. First: is the light-cone view secretly just eternalism with a spotlight on it? My past light cone contains everything that has ever been able to reach me, and as I advance along my worldline the vertex moves forward second by second, which looks like genuine becoming. But someone will say that once you grant a fixed four-dimensional structure with light cones baked into it, you've already conceded the block and are just haggling over which part of it you're permitted to point at. My only answer is to deny that "the structure exists" and "everything in the structure is equally real" are the same claim, which feels like it needs more than a shrug behind it.

Second: the cosmology escape hatch. There is a foliation physics picks out in practice — the one in which the microwave background looks isotropic. Comoving observers share a cosmic time, and the age of the universe is defined along it. Should the presentist dig in there and say: fine, that's the real now? It would hand back a global slice, but it trades a metaphysical necessity for a contingent accident about how the matter happens to be distributed — the frame is special because of what's in the universe, not because of the geometry. I suspect that's a bad trade and I can't say crisply why.

Conclusion: the argument is sound as a proof that there's no frame-invariant global present, and unsound as a proof that the future is real, though it's almost always sold as the second thing. The gap is filled by an assumption that objectivity requires globality. Once you see that, local becoming stops looking exotic and starts looking like the more conservative option.
