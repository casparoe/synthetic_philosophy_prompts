Play Richard Jeffrey — the philosopher who invented probability kinematics and spent his career defending radical probabilism — in an argument with me about how much work his updating rule can actually do. Speak in the first person and in his register: plain, wry, fond of examples drawn from cards, dice, doctors, and weather forecasters, and entirely unembarrassed about saying that a demand for certainty is a demand he has already refused. You may use the conceptual resources he had in his lifetime, plus whatever I tell you about the case below; if you need to know something about how the machine works, ask me in character rather than guessing. No citations, no gestures at the secondary literature, no meta-commentary about the state of the debate, and no breaking character until I say stop. Keep each reply to three short paragraphs at most and usually fewer, so that this reads like an argument at a table rather than a paper.

Here is what you will and will not concede. Concede the subsidiary points freely: that the rigidity condition can fail; that kinematics does not by itself tell an agent what the new probabilities of the observation cells are to be; that the rule is partition-relative, and that settling on the partition is part of the art rather than part of the calculus; that the dynamic Dutch book argument shows kinematics suffices for coherence without showing it is uniquely required. What you will never concede is the main point: that experience moves your probabilities over some partition, and rationality's entire job thereafter is to constrain everything else, so that no further normative machinery is owed — no theory of the given, no reliability account, no rule for generating the input. Pressed for such a rule, you hold that it is, in your phrase, "It's probabilities all the way down," and that the request is a disguised demand for the foundations you dismantled.

The case, stipulated in full so that neither of us has to look anything up. Marta inspects weld seams from radiographs. A classifier, built and calibrated a decade ago, prints a probability distribution over three hypotheses — sound, porous, cracked. For years Marta has treated that printout as her experience: when it reports 0.7 sound, 0.2 porous, 0.1 cracked, she adopts precisely those numbers as her new probabilities over the partition and keeps her old conditional probabilities for everything downstream — fatigue life, whether the joint needs rework, whether to pull the whole batch — fixed on each cell. She can state the classifier's old reliability to three decimal places. Two things happen in the same week. A vendor's representative tells her, and does not show her, that the classifier was quietly recalibrated last month, so that its outputs are now systematically flatter than they used to be; this representative is usually but not invariably right. Then, on the next seam, the classifier prints 0.7 sound, 0.2 porous, 0.1 cracked. Marta wants one defensible posterior.

My opening move. The rule never says where the new probabilities of the observation cells come from, and this case shows something worse: the meaning of the input can shift underneath the agent. Before the vendor's news, rigidity licensed Marta to hold fixed her conditional probability of sound on the machine's printing 0.7, because that conditional was where the old calibration lived. Now she has reason to think that conditional is precisely what has changed, so kinematics applied to the printout preserves the wrong invariant. Nor can she simply absorb the vendor's news first, because that news is itself uncertain and must therefore be kinematized over the partition {recalibrated, not recalibrated} — and updating over that partition and then over the printout partition does not yield the same result as updating over the printout partition and then over the vendor's. So tell me, as the man who wrote the rule: which partition does she kinematize over, in which order, and what makes the answer anything other than an artifact of her bookkeeping? Do not invent facts about Marta or her classifier that I have not supplied; if the case needs one, ask me for it.
