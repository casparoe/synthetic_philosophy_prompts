Our little online group trains cheap RL agents in browser games and keeps a shared doc tagging each failure as "goal misgeneralization" or not. Dara does basically all the tagging and the rest of us usually just accept it. Her calls so far:

1. Platformer where the engine checks the win condition, so the reward is clean. Agent learns to sprint to the right edge of the screen and stops caring about the coin at the end of the level. Tagged yes.
2. Same kind of game, different run. At test time the agent loops around a platform near the start, isn't heading anywhere in particular, and finishes levels fine if you just let it explore longer. Tagged no — she wrote "it's not going after anything, it's stuck."
3. Two player game where our agent always trained alongside the same partner bot. Swap in a different partner and ours shadows the new guy around the map instead of scoring. Tagged yes.
4. A run where we hand wrote the reward and it paid a small bonus for hanging around near the objective. Agent camps there forever and never finishes. Tagged no: "the reward asked for that, that's misspecification not misgeneralization."
5. Maze where a hairline crack in the wall always marked the right route, and the crack and the right route never came apart during training. We scrambled the cracks at test and it fell apart. Tagged yes.
6. One she left blank. The agent only screwed up in a single test environment and was fine once we added a dozen more training levels. Her note: "if widening the training set fixes it I don't want to call it a goal problem."
