I run a reading group on the metaphysics of social kinds and I want to hand out a decision tree titled something like "where do you actually land on whether generations are real?" Audience is grad students who've read plenty of social science but almost no ontology, so every branch question has to be a concrete case or claim they could answer without knowing which leaf it steers them toward — nothing shaped like "do you think generations are socially constructed?" Aim for six to eight questions and roughly as many leaves.

The branches I definitely want in there: whether the label does any causal work once you hold age and period fixed; what the age–period–cohort identification problem (cohort = period − age, so the three can't be estimated separately without extra assumptions) forces you to buy; whether shared birth-year location is enough on its own or a cohort has to develop some distinctive self-understanding before it counts as a generation — Mannheim's location / actuality / units distinction is fine to lean on for this one; whether applying a label changes the people it gets applied to; and whether the boundaries survive being shifted by a couple of years, or being exported outside the country where they were coined.

For leaves, I want cohort realism, something like a looping-effect or interactive-kind view, straight constructionism, fictionalism, and a deflationary "there's really just age effects and period effects" option, plus whatever else the space seems to demand. Then mark the answer combinations nobody occupies and say for each whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored — and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate.
