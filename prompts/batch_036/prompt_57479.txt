(1) Marcus opened by saying Solomonoff induction isn't a theory of reasoning at all, since the universal prior is uncomputable — you can enumerate it from below but never actually evaluate it — so its a benchmark no real agent can be measured against, and calling it *the* answer to induction is like saying the answer to driving is a route you can't steer on. He granted the invariance theorem — the gap is bounded by a constant that doesn't depend on the data — but insisted the constant is a fixed handicap measured in bits, so with only a few dozen bits of evidence in hand, a machine-dependent shift can flip which hypothesis comes out on top, and nobody has ever computed that constant for any realistic pair of machines.
