I am trying to settle what a theory of diachronic rationality would have to deliver before I would count it as adequate. The purpose I want it to serve is deliberative rather than technical: it has to be usable by an actual agent who chooses sequentially over a long horizon, who makes decisions now that will shape what she wants later, and who can sometimes be bled dry by a run of individually unobjectionable steps. A representation theorem that licenses a coherent agent in a one-shot gamble is not what I am after, and neither is a verdict delivered from nowhere at the end of a life.

The list should be built against a particular case, which I want you to treat as fixed rather than to redesign.

Wren, over roughly thirty years, repeatedly pays small sums — in money, time, and health — to acquire tastes she does not yet have. Each acquisition is informed and deliberate, and each makes her worse off in a currency she already recognizes while making her better off in a currency that arrives with the taste itself. She has, in advance and stably, endorsed the policy of letting experience reshape what she wants: she regards a life in which nothing is ever allowed to change one's desires as a worse life, and she holds this second-order commitment even when it costs her. At the end, the sequence turns out to have been closed. The last taste makes her want to give up what the first taste was purchased for, and giving it up returns her very nearly to her starting position, minus everything spent along the way. Nobody deceived her, there was no risk she failed to price, and no plan was visible to her from inside the episode that she could have seen as better than the one she followed. Her own retrospective judgment is ambiguous: she is poorer, and she is someone she would not trade back.

Given that case, write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of rational choice over time, for an agent whose preferences can change as a result of her choices, must be able to do. For each requirement, give the consideration or the case that motivates it, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a commitment of one camp wearing the costume of a criterion — in particular, whether immunity to money pumps is constitutive of rationality or only evidence of it, whether rationality must be expressible as a single transitive ranking over outcomes at all, and whether dynamic consistency is a demand on the agent or a convenience for the theorist. Include at least one requirement about whose verdict counts when earlier and later selves disagree, and at least one about whether the theory must be actionable from inside the episode rather than merely correct from outside it. Be attentive to which requirements smuggle in an assumption that the agent's currency of evaluation stays fixed, since in Wren's case it does not.

Then tell me where the list breaks. Flag any subset of the desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and show the case that drives the wedge — if the conjunction of assessment-by-the-agent's-own-values, immunity to exploitation as a criterion, and inside-the-episode usability is incoherent here, that is exactly the kind of result I want stated plainly, because then the list is doing philosophical work rather than expressing a wish. Say also which single requirement eliminates the largest number of otherwise attractive theories, and why.

Close with a short pass — a few lines, no more — at how the familiar stances do against the list: sophisticated choice, which has the agent anticipate her future self; resolute choice, which has her hold to a plan; and views that locate rationality in the agent's higher-order endorsement of her own motivational structure. Do not pad this section and do not supply references, attributions, or a reading list of any kind; I want the reasoning, not the scholarship.

Begin your answer with its bottom line in a single sentence — the requirement you judge decisive, or the incoherence you find at the core of the list — and then give the desiderata and the argument. No preamble, no restatement of the question, no framing remarks.
