im giving a talk at our department's methods workshop next week and they've asked me to put my historiographical position down as a numbered list beforehand so everyone can attack it. Here's roughly where I've landed, all of it filtered through the second Untimely Meditation, which I treat as a live methodological text and not a period piece:

1. Of the three modes, the one I want to practice is monumental history. A historiography that never hands anybody a model — someone who actually pulled something off against the odds — is politically inert, and I'd rather be accused of hagiography than write history nobody can act on.

2. Methodologically I'm a strict causal contextualist. An account that doesn't reconstruct the actual conditions and the chain of events that produced its subject isn't history. Treating past people as timeless types is the sin I think most of the discipline is guilty of.

3. I read critical history as Nietzsche's own preferred mode and the healthiest one: judge the past, condemn it, and build a second nature instead of revering the first.

4. I also hold that the standards you condemn a past by can only be inherited — you don't get critical traction from nowhere, and my own evaluative reflexes are products of the traditions I'm indicting.

5. Antiquarian history I don't see as a problem for me at all. I read it as archival piety aimed at a diferent object (where you come from, the local, the small stuff), so it can't really collide with 1 or 3.

6. All three are legitimate for the same reason, that they serve life, and I take serving life to be fully compatible with being truthful about the past.

Can you audit this instead of lecturing me on the essay? I want the pairs that genuinely pull against each other, ranked by how bad the pull is, and for each one what would have to be given up or watered down — plus, just as important, which pairs only look like a problem and dissolve once you're clear on what each mode is for. And then tell me the single cheapest revision that clears the most damage.
