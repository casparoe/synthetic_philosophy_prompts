I'm a philosophy-of-language PhD student helping to run a joint seminar with our law school on reference and legal interpretation, and one judicial practice keeps resisting my attempts to characterize it. When the Supreme Court must fix the sense of an ordinary term in a statute or constitutional provision, it commonly consults a dictionary published near the time of enactment — Johnson's for the eighteenth century, Webster's American Dictionary for the antebellum period — treating the entry as evidence of what the phrase meant to the enacting community. Yet the same Court, in Nix v. Hedden (1893), classified the tomato as a vegetable for tariff purposes: it conceded that botanically the tomato is the fruit of a vine, declined to treat as controlling either the Webster's and Worcester's definitions counsel had read into evidence or the botanical classification, and reasoned instead from how sellers and consumers of provisions ordinarily talk, adding that dictionaries are admitted "not as evidence, but only as aids to the memory and understanding of the court." In regulatory and evidentiary settings, meanwhile, these courts defer freely to expert classification of a substance, species, or drug.

What theory of meaning does the dictionary-lookup practice presuppose? Is it committed to descriptivism about sortal and natural-kind terms, and to the assumption that a lexicographer's entry samples a single community-wide competence rather than one register among many? If semantic externalism and the division of linguistic labor are correct, can an 1828 entry fix the extension of a term whose reference depends on the world and on specialist usage — or does the practice survive in some restricted role, fixing sense while leaving reference to the world? And is the pattern internally inconsistent, or can a single doctrine (ordinary meaning, judicial notice, deference to trade usage) accommodate all three of these behaviors? Please distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what merely sits comfortably with it, mark where it is underdetermined among rival semantic theories, and say what interpretive method would have to look like if the Court took externalism seriously.
