Here's the list:

1. Substantivalism is right: spacetime is a real thing in its own right, not just a way of talking about relations between material events. I dont buy relationalism.

2. But I'm not a manifold realist. The bare points of the manifold aren't what spacetime is. The metric field — the geometry — is spacetime, and a "point" is just a node in that structure, individuated by the field configuration as a whole.

3. Kretschmann convinced me that general covariance is cheap: any theory whatsoever can be written in generally covariant form, so covariance by itself doesn't tell you anything physical.

4. Even so, I think GR's invariance under diffeomorphisms is physically substantive. It goes together with there being no fixed background geometry, and I think that's where the hole argument gets whatever grip it has.

5. Leibniz equivalence: two models related by a diffeomorphism describe the same physical situation. Full stop. That's why I say GR is deterministic — the hole argument gets dissolved, not dodged.

6. The reason I believe 5 is basically Einstein's point-coincidence argument: all anyone can ever observe is coincidences of events, and diffeomorphic models agree on every coincidence, so theres no observable difference and therefore no real difference.

7. One honest case verdict. If you drag the metric along a symmetry so that nothing changes mathematically at all, obviously it's the same world, no worries there. But take a diffeomorphisim that's the identity outside some bounded hole region and shuffles things around inside: when I try to picture that one I can't shake the feeling that it's describing a genuinely different way the inside of the hole could be. I officially deny it. But the feeling is real and I don't know what to do with it.
