I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am currently assembling a long feature around a reframing that has quietly reshaped the consciousness debate: the suggestion, associated with David Chalmers, that the prior question is not whether the hard problem is genuine but why it seems genuine to us — why a gap between physical process and felt experience strikes people as intuitively unbridgeable, why a functionally identical zombie seems conceivable, why experience gets reported as ineffable and as richer than any description of it. Before I commission anything further from philosophers, I want a specification rather than a solution.

So: write the desiderata for an account of these problem intuitions. What would such an account have to deliver in order to count as solving the meta-problem, as distinct from restating it or from explaining something merely adjacent to it? Give me the requirements as a numbered list, each with the case, the kind of report, or the consideration that motivates it, so that I can see why it belongs on the list at all rather than being decoration.

Here is what I have from reporting, and I want the list to answer to it rather than around it. An anesthesiologist who says the intuition is most entrenched in people who have never watched an induction and least so in those who have; a man with aphantasia for whom the supposed gap has no intuitive purchase whatsoever; a twenty-year meditator whose introspective reports have grown more differentiated with training rather than less; a philosopher who denies the hard problem for a living and concedes that rereading his own arguments leaves the intuition intact; and an AI researcher who says the intuition evaporated once she was building systems that volunteered reports of having one. The intuition is neither uniform nor stable across people, nor stable within a person. Should an adequate account explain its variability, its trainability, its resilience after being explained away, or only its prevalence? State which of these you treat as requirements and which as optional, and say why.

Make the account answer for the asymmetries too: why the intuition attaches to experience specifically when comparable gaps — life, water, heat, heredity — no longer generate anything like it; why the people who find zombies conceivable do not find a digestion-zombie conceivable; and why the intuition persists in the ordinary speech of people who officially reject it.

Then the part I most want. Sort the requirements into those any adequate account must meet and those that are really the standing preferences of a particular camp — neutrality between physicalist and dualist readers, the demand that the explanation not beg the question against either side, the demand that it not explain away the very first-person reports it is constructed from, the demand that it be realizable as a mechanism in some cognitive architecture. If some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so explicitly and show me the conflict step by step; a specification that turns out to be unsatisfiable is a result I can publish. And identify the single requirement that excludes the largest number of otherwise plausible candidates.

Skip the scorecard of existing theories — I will handle that with my editor. And no references or reading lists, please; I want the structure of the problem, not the literature on it.
