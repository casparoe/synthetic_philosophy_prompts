I'm three-quarters through a novel whose narrator is a cosmologist, and in the last act she starts seriously entertaining the thought that she's a Boltzmann brain — a freak fluctuation in a vacuum that's already reached heat death, complete with a manufactured past and a manufactured body. My science reader thinks the whole chapter is a cheat, and his objection isn't the usual one about it being unfalsifiable. His line is that you cant actually get anywhere with the Boltzmann brain worry at all, because a theory that says nearly every thinking thing is a random fluke is also saying that nearly every chain of reasoning is garbage, so the reasoning that gets you to the worry discredits itself along the way. Best case, he says, you end up unable to trust either the theory or your rejection of it, which is agnosticism, not a finding.

I think he's wrong, and she's going to win the exchange, so I need the argument.

Make the strongest case you can that a cosmology predicting its own observers are overwhelmingly Boltzmann brains really is disconfirmed by the sort of evidence we have, and that the self-undermining reply doesn't rescue it. Stay in the weeds, not at slogan level. Specifically I want you to deal with what the typicality assumption in the objection is doing and whether it's doing any work the theorist is entitled to; why tidy, mutually consistent memories are supposed to count against a brain-heavy universe when a fluke brain can be manufactured with exactly those memories; whether counting flukes against ordinary observers is even a legitimate move here or a category slip; and what the difference amounts to between a theory being false and a theory being unusable. If the right answer to him runs through radical skepticism generally — that his move, if it worked, would block every empirical disconfirmation of every theory — say so and press it as hard as it goes.

Argue one side. No weighing-up at the end, no on-the-other-hand, and dont give me a bibliography or a list of papers, I've got no way to check any of it and it isn't what I'm after. If some step in his objection is just an equivocation, point at exactly which step it is and name the two senses it's sliding between. Call it 700 words.
