I'm a novelist, about halfway through a book set in a small fishing port, and I've built a lot of the plot around things that spread through the town the way people say ideas spread: a rumor about a drowning that nobody witnessed, a whistled tune that gets slightly misheard every time, a church collection ritual nobody alive can explain, a slogan the cannery stamps on its crates, a fish stew recipe, a superstition about not saying a certain name while offshore, a local insult, and a story about the lighthouse that gets longer every time its told.

I've been reading up on memetics to help me structure all this, and I've hit a wall — I can't tell which of my spreaders actually earn the right to be called replicators and which ones I'm just dressing up in evolutionary language because it sounds good in a notebook.

So here's what I want. Score each of the eight on a 0–10 scale for how gene-like it is as a replicator, where the score tracks four things at once: how discrete the item is, how much of it actually survives a round of transmission, whether it has a boundary you could point at, and whether the differences between its variants cause differences in how often they get passed on. Give me a ranked list with the numbers, plus a sentence or two each on what's driving the score.

Then the part I actually care about. Where's the cutoff, if there is one? Below what score does calling something a meme stop buying me anything explanatory, and for the ones that fall under it, say whether a selection-story that doesn't assume faithful copying still predicts something about them — or whether all that's left is a metaphor I should just own up to.

And flag any of the eight where you think my question is malformed: where the item isn't one thing that travels but something rebuilt from scratch each time, so that asking about fidelity is the wrong question in the first place. If you think that's most of them, say so, and tell me what I should be scoring instead.

I know this is a live fight — the reconstructive objection to memes, the people who keep the population-level maths but ditch the tidy little units, the replicator-versus-interactor business. Don't referee it politely from the middle. Tell me how confident you are in the numbers you hand me, and single out the one placement you're least sure about and why.
