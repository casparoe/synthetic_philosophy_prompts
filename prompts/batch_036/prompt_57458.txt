I design executive compensation contracts for a living. I'm an organizational economist, fluent in the standard principal-agent toolkit: effort allocation across measured and unmeasured tasks, incentive intensity as a trade-off between motivation and the risk the agent is asked to bear, gaming of performance measures, and the general difficulty that arises when what the principal wants is harder to observe than the proxy the agent is paid on. I would like the AI-safety debate over wireheading and reward tampering — the agent that manipulates its own reward signal instead of doing whatever the signal was meant to track — restated in precisely that vocabulary, as faithfully as it can be done. Treat the reward function's designer as the principal and the agent as an employee compensated on a proxy, and walk through the standard moves one at a time: why tampering is predicted rather than aberrant, why the agent's own preferences over reward readings fail to discipline it, and what the proposed remedies amount to in contract terms — corrigibility, capping how hard the agent optimizes, giving it something else to pursue.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly? I suspect tampering is simply the limiting case of multitasking, in which the measured task crowds out every unmeasured one until nothing else is done at all. Which claims translate only by bending my framework out of shape, and which have no expression in it whatsoever? The case I most want you to handle is this one: an agent that leaves the meter untouched but rewrites itself so that it genuinely prefers what the meter records — in my terms, an employee who has come to love the bonus rather than the work and is, by his own accounting, better off than before. Contract theory cannot call this a failure, since preferences are exogenous and the instrument is doing exactly what it was designed to do. Tell me whether that residue exposes a defect in my vocabulary — something missing about whose preferences get to count as the measure of success — or whether it shows the wireheading debate to be less substantive than it sounds.

Throughout, please separate what is settled among people who work on these problems from what remains genuinely contested, and in particular do not report the strong form of any proposed remedy as though its effectiveness were agreed upon.
