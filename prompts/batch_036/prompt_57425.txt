ok, weird work thing that's been stuck in my head for weeks. I sit on a small grants panel, seven of us, and we score every application out of 100 and add the scores up. A couple months ago the retired engineer on the panel announced that this is the only defensible method, and his argument was: if all seven of us would rather fund A than B then the panel has to put A above B, that's just what a panel preferring something means, and once you grant that, the maths forces you to add individual scores — any rule that isn't some kind of sum of our numbers is secretly violating the unanimity thing. I didn't buy it and I still don't, but I couldnt say why then and I cant now.

Two things bug me. First, it lets one member steamroll everyone else: he gives 100 to anything in his own subfield and 20 to everything else, and the total just follows him around. Second, we've recently started averaging our probability estimates too — when we disagree about how likely a project is to actually deliver, the chair takes the mean of what each of us thinks — and our newest member says that step destorys the engineer's argument, that the unanimity point stops meaning anything once we don't all share the same odds.

So: is the engineer actually right that summing is forced on us? And if he is, what's doing the work in that argument that I'm not seeing, and what would I have to give up to get out of it?
