**P:** A well-funded team runs one such study properly. They pick a single society with long continuous records (England/Britain 1500–2000, or France, or Han-through-Qing China). They pre-register everything: the instability index, the driver variables, the functional form, the estimation method. Two constraints are part of the setup, because they're doing most of the work in my version of the argument: (1) real-time information only — every indicator at year *t* is built only from data that would have been available by year *t*, and no indicator may be defined in terms that already contain the outcome, which is my worry about proxies for intra-elite competition: if you measure factional strife by counting party-line votes or newspaper words for elite conflict, you may just be measuring the thing you claim to predict; (2) they get compared to a dumb baseline — last-value-plus-trend, or a three-lag autoregression on the same index — and P is true only if a neutral statistician would call the gap real, not merely directionally encouraging.

So **P = the pre-registered model's 15-year-ahead out-of-sample forecasts of the instability index beat that baseline.**

My credence: 0.6. My co-author's: 0.2.

Here's the idea I keep reaching for, and I want you to actually push on it rather than nod at it. Call it the thin-panel problem — I made the name up over coffee, so don't go hunting for it. A secular cycle is roughly 250 years; a single society with genuinely continuous records gives you two or three cycles; so a three- or four-parameter model is being fit to about two periods of its own characteristic wavelength. Cross-society pooling is supposed to fix this, but pooling assumes the parameters are time- and place-invariant, which is precisely what the critics deny. Related distinction I don't see anyone making: hindsight identifiability versus foresight identifiability. Fitting a completed 500-year trajectory can pin parameters down well, because the shape of the finished cycle constrains them; standing at year *t* with 40 years of data you often can't tell which phase of which cycle you're in. Those are different epistemic situations, and I suspect a lot of the vindication talk slides quietly from the first to the second.
