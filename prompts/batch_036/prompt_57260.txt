Two weeks out from a workshop talk and I want to build the Q&A prep sheet now, while I still remember where the argument is thin. The room: about two thirds are formal social choice / judgment aggregation people who live inside the impossibility theorems and argue about conditions on rules; the rest are legal theorists who work on appellate practice and are pretty indifferent to the formalism but very interested in what courts actually do. No judges, no political scientists, nobody from CS.

My thesis: the discursive dilemma isn't a normative choice between premise based and conclusion based procedures for courts, because when a doctrine really is conjunctive a court that writes reasons has already committed itself to the premises — the outcome vote just isn't an independent input. So the stock three judge contract case, where majorities on each premise point toward liability but the outcome vote runs the other way, is a bad example: no real opinion-writing panel has those inputs. The impossibility results still bite, but only where the doctrine is disjunctive or weighted, or where which propositions count as the premises is itself contested. So the interesting question is agenda endogeneity, not procedure choice.
