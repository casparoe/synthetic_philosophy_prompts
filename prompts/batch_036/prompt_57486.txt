I am drafting the framing section of a dissertation chapter on the paradox of deontology, and before I survey proposed resolutions I want the specification rather than the survey: what would an account have to deliver in order to count as resolving the paradox, as opposed to restating it in more comfortable vocabulary? Please set out the desiderata — the phenomena such an account must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the structural conditions on its form — and give, for each, the case or consideration that motivates it.

So that we are working with the same puzzle, I take the paradox to be this: a constraint (do not kill, do not lie, do not break your promise) is ordinarily motivated by the badness of killings, lies and broken promises; yet if that is what motivates it, the constraint appears to forbid precisely the act that would prevent a greater number of the very same bad things, and the agent who obeys looks less concerned with the value the constraint was supposed to track than the agent who violates it.

Two cases I want any list to be able to handle, because they pull in opposite directions:

- A regulator is bound by an undertaking not to disclose a firm's confidential data. A failure in another agency's systems will shortly expose the data of a hundred firms; she can trigger the protocol that prevents this only by disclosing one firm's data herself. The violation-minimising intuition that generates the paradox seems notably weaker here than in the killing case, and an adequate account should explain why, or explain the difference away.
- The same agent may permissibly spend public money on education, policing and inspection to reduce violations committed by others, while being forbidden to reduce them by violating herself. Whether that is a datum a resolution must accommodate or a confusion it must dissolve is itself something the list should decide.

Among the requirements I would expect to see judged are whether the paradox can be shown to survive, or to evaporate, when the constraint's basis is restated without reference to outcomes at all — for instance in terms of not using a person merely as a means; whether the account distinguishes doing from allowing and, within doing, explains why killing one healthy patient to save five differs from killing an attacker to save five; what it says about regret, compensation and distress in the agent who rightly declines to violate; and whether whatever dissolves the paradox for an individual also governs the design of legal rules forbidding officials to infringe rights where infringement would prevent more infringements.

For each desideratum, mark whether it is non-negotiable for a resolution or is in truth a substantive commitment of one camp smuggled in as a criterion of adequacy — I suspect several are, and I want to know which. Then state plainly whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, in particular whether a formulation that owes nothing to outcomes can be combined with a demand that the account explain the constraint's resistance to numbers; if it cannot, the list is itself the result I need. Finally, identify which single requirement eliminates the largest share of the familiar moves, and give me a sentence or two on how each of these five fares against it: agent-relative value, side-constraints understood as non-teleological, threshold deontology, the doing and allowing distinction, and the deflationary claim that the paradox is merely verbal. No bibliography, please — I want the criteria, not the literature.
