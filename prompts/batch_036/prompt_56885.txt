QUESTION 3 (30 marks, ~35 minutes). State a closure principle for knowledge precise enough to survive the obvious counterexamples, and say what those counterexamples are. Then explain what Dretske's zebra case and Nozick's tracking theory each do to the principle you stated. Do the two accounts deliver the same verdict about the zebra? Finish with a short evaluation: is giving up closure a price worth paying for a tracking account, or should the tracking theorist be trying to keep it?

STUDENT ANSWER:

The simplest closure principle — if S knows that p, and p entails q, then S knows that q — is false as it stands. I can know that the café is open, and know that its being open entails that the lights are on, and still not know the lights are on, because I never believed it or never noticed the entailment. So the principle needs a belief clause and a basing clause: if, while knowing that p, S believes that q because she knows that p entails q, then S knows that q. That's the version worth arguing about, and it's the version both Dretske and Nozick target.

Dretske's zebra case is meant to break it. You're at the zoo, there's something in the zebra pen that looks exactly like a zebra, and you come to believe it's a zebra. That belief is knowledge, on his view: your evidence rules out the alternatives that are actually in play, like it being a horse or an antelope. But "it's a zebra" entails "it's not a cleverly painted mule," and you don't know that, because nothing in your evidence rules out the painted-mule possibility. So here's a case where you know p, you know p entails q, you believe q on that basis, and still don't know q. Closure goes.

Nozick arrives at the same place from a different direction. For him S knows that p just in case p is true, S believes p by some method M, and two counterfactuals hold: if p weren't true and S were using M, S wouldn't believe p by M; and if p were true and S were using M, S would believe p by M. The first of those is sensitivity and it's the one doing the work. He's a reliabilist like the rest of the externalists — what matters is that the belief-forming process is reliable — and sensitivity is just his way of spelling reliability out, since a reliable process is one that wouldn't have produced the belief if the belief had been false.

Applied to skepticism: my belief that I have hands is sensitive, because in the nearest worlds where I don't have hands — I lost them in an accident, say — I wouldn't believe I did. My belief that I'm not a brain in a vat isn't sensitive, because in the nearest vat world I'd believe exactly the same thing. So I know I have hands and I don't know I'm not a vat brain, even though the first entails the second. Closure fails, which stops the skeptic at the second premise rather than the first — the first is where Moore stopped it, by insisting he does know he's not a vat brain.

The zebra case comes out the same way for Nozick, which is a nice convergence between the two accounts. My belief that it's a zebra is sensitive: in the nearest worlds where it isn't a zebra, I don't believe that it is. But my belief that it isn't a cleverly painted mule isn't, because in the nearest world where it is a painted mule I'd still be standing there looking at it and thinking it isn't. So tracking reproduces Dretske's verdict exactly, and both get to keep the intuitive knowledge claim about the animal in the pen.

Method-relativization is what stops the account from collapsing. Nozick's grandmother case shows why M has to be held fixed: she sees her grandson and believes he's fine, but if he'd died the family would have told her he was fine to spare her, so her belief looks insensitive. The fix is to say the relevant method is seeing him rather than being told about him, and under that description the belief tracks the truth.

My evaluation: giving up closure is a real cost, but not a fatal one, because closure failures only seem to turn up when the entailment runs through a skeptical hypothesis or some far-fetched alternative. In ordinary cases, where p and q are about the same subject matter, a belief that tracks p is going to track the q you deduce from it. So the tracking theorist surrenders a principle we'd have liked to keep, but only at the edges, and that seems a reasonable trade for an account that lets me know I have hands.
