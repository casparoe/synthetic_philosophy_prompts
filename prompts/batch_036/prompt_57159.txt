Third-year musicology PhD here, and I'm well out of my depth on the logic side, so bear with me. I'm sitting in a cross-listed seminar on music and mind this term. Last week we did Hofstadter, and the convenor handed out a two-paragraph précis of the argument for those of us who hadn't read the book. It reads:

"Escher's Drawing Hands only looks like a paradox if you insist on finding a first cause inside the picture: each hand draws the other, so there is no top and no bottom. Hofstadter's claim is that the mind has the same structure — a strange loop, a hierarchy in which moving upward through the levels eventually deposits you back where you started. He calls the one level the loop never touches the inviolate level, and he cannot do without it; a tangle with no level outside it collapses into outright contradiction. This is precisely why Gödel's second theorem holds. A system cannot prove its own consistency, because that would require occupying a level the system cannot reach, so every system rich enough to describe itself has a blind spot. Gödel, Escher and Bach are making one point in three notations. Consciousness is what it is like to be such a system: to be a blind spot to yourself. The self, then, is a story the loop tells about a vantage point it never actually has — and that is the fatal difficulty. A fiction cannot be conscious, so a theory on which the self is a fiction cannot be anyone's theory."

Our guest that week (a philosopher of mind, extremely confident) treated the last two sentences as settling the matter and nobody pushed back. I half suspect they're a cheap shot, but I genuinely can't tell a cheap shot from a real problem at this level, and the middle is where I'm lost. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what a logician would nod at, what they'd wince at, and what a Hofstadterian would say simply isn't his view?

Two things I'm specifically unsure about. First, the inviolate level: the précis makes it sound like a defect in the system, a level it can't reach, but from the way the Escher is described it sounds more like the thing that keeps the picture from being vicious — the level the drawing is made from, which isn't in the picture at all. Are those the same notion, and is the move from there to "this is precisely why Gödel's second theorem holds" licensed, or is an analogy being sold as an explanation? Relatedly, does "collapses into outright contradiction" fit anything here, given that the whole selling point of a strange loop seems to be that it isn't vicious? Second, "every system rich enough to describe itself has a blind spot" — is that a fair statement of the incompleteness results, or is it doing something sneaky with the hypotheses?

And one thing from my own side that nobody addressed. The Musical Offering canon I'm fairly sure he leans on as his musical example is the modulating one, and it doesn't come back where it started: it ends a whole tone higher, so after six goes you're in the same key but an octave up. The loop only closes if you ignore register altogether. Does that break the strange-loop reading, or is the ignored parameter doing exactly the work the inviolate level is supposed to do — and if it is, does the précis's version of the inviolate level have any room for it?

So: where the summary is accurate, where it's a fair simplification, where it distorts, and whether the self-refutation conclusion survives once the distortions are cleaned up.
