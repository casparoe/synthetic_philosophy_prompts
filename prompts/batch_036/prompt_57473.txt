So I've been reading the radical interpretation stuff — Davidson and the people arguing with him — and there's a pattern I keep needing a word for and haven't found one. Let me try to describe it without the jargon, because I think the jargon is exactly what's making me miss it.

Two concrete things first.

One: my brother says our dad "never liked" a certain neighbour, and I'm pretty confident he did like her. Before I can even say "you've got that wrong," I catch myself working out what he'd have to mean by never liked — maybe never trusted, maybe never warmed to socially, maybe he's thinking of a different neighbour entirely. Every one of those readings turns him into someone who isn't mistaken. And I notice I basically never land on "he's just wrong."

Two: an old friend used to insist a certain record was the saddest thing ever written. I don't hear it that way at all. Same move available: I can say she's wrong about the record, or I can say we're using "sad" differently, one of us tracking the sound and one of us tracking the subject matter. The second option is always there, always easier, and never costs me anything.

The pattern is that apparent disagreement about the world can be refiled as a difference in what the words mean, and the refile is always available. And it only runs one way. I can't do the reverse — I can't take a real difference in meaning and refile it as one of us being mistaken about the world, because being mistaken about the world already requires the meaning to be settled. So the error ledger only ever goes down, never up.

Here's the bit I think is actually interesting, and where I suspect there's a name I'm missing. There's a ceiling on the trade. If someone turns out wrong about too many things at once, I can't keep refile-ing forever — but the outcome isn't "they're massively wrong about the world." It's "we don't share a language," i.e. I stop treating them as interpretable at all. So the verdicts on offer end up being something like *mostly right* or *not one of us*, and flat-out large-scale localised error gets squeezed out of the option space rather than discovered and then judged. I can't tell if that's a feature people have named or a bug I've invented.

So: does this have an established name? I already have charity in my toolbox and I know the multiple-equally-good-manuals point, and neither seems to be it — charity is a policy I adopt, and underdetermination is about there being several adequate ways to translate, whereas what I'm describing is about which direction a trade can run and what happens at the limit. If there's a nearest concept, tell me what it is and where exactly my description stops fitting it — I'd rather know the mismatch than get a label. If there's nothing, coin one, define it properly, and give me the boundary cases. In particular: what would a case look like where the refile genuinely isn't available and the interpreter has no choice but to record flat error? Is there such a case, or does the thing have no exceptions, and if it has none, is that a problem for it or the whole point of it?

Don't bother pointing me at papers or listing who said what, I can't chase those down right now — its the carving I want, not the bibliography.
