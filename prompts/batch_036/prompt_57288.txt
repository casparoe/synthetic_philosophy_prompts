im in a jurisprudence seminar and we just spent two weeks on the paradox of self-amendment — hart vs. kelsen and ross, plus suber's nomic appendix, the game where the players vote to change the rules they're playing under. for the last session everyone has to bring something that isn't a paper, and i want to write a poem that *is* a little nomic game.

so: stanza one lays down the poem's own rules — lines per stanza, rhyme, a refrain, whatever — and includes one clause entrenching itself, saying that clause can never be amended. partway through, a stanza proposes an amendment, the next stanza votes it through, and every stanza after that visibly obeys the new rule (you should be able to count lines and watch the change take effect). the last stanza amends the entrenched clause itself. call it 6-8 stanzas.

what it has to land: the whole sequence is a logical mess — a rule validating its own repeal, the exact self-reference ross says makes it void — and it goes through anyway, because the ground of the rules was never another rule, it's that everybody keeps going along. i want that felt in the form, not argued for. no names of philosophers anywhere in the poem, just the machinery doing the work.
