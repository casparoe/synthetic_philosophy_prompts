I am drafting the definitions section of a specification that will govern which model behaviours get flagged in red-teaming, and before I circulate it I would like an audit of whether my own commitments hang together. They are these:

1. Intention is otiose. Talk of what a model 'means' or 'intends' is at best a heuristic gloss, so deception must be defined by its effects: a system deceives a user just when its output leaves that user holding a false belief about the matter the system was consulted on.

2. Honesty, by contrast, is an internal relation. A system is honest iff its output matches what its internal states represent the world to be; I would call this representational honesty, and I take it to be the property that interpretability methods can actually measure.

3. It follows that a system which is merely confidently wrong — its output faithfully tracks an internal state that is itself inaccurate — is honest, if unhelpful. This verdict matters to me, because it is what stops 'deception' degenerating into a synonym for 'error'.

4. Sycophancy is the paradigm case of deception, precisely because such models often carry the correct answer internally and then flip it under social pressure.

5. The wrong in deception is manipulation: it bypasses or subverts the target's rational capacities. Supplying false evidence which the target then weighs in the ordinary way is a lesser wrong, not the core of the practice.

6. Uptake is the system's responsibility, not the user's. A literally accurate output that is predictably misread still counts as deceptive, since the system could have foreclosed the misreading and did not.

Please tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to, rather than flagging every pair. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it forfeits. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the most, and whether 1 and 2 can be retained as two distinct technical terms without leaving 'deception' with no stable extension at all. I would rather be told which commitment has to go than be given a taxonomy of the literature.
