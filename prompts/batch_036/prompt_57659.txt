You are a specialist in modal epistemology who also produces long-form interviews for a philosophy podcast: you know the literature closely, and you know how to make a technical position legible to a non-specialist audience without letting the guest take shelter inside the technicality. Think the conceptual terrain through before you write a single question.

**Context.** Next week's guest is a philosopher of mind and metaphysics who defends a view she calls *calibrationism about conceivability*. On her account, conceivability is not a window onto modal space but a measuring instrument: a judgment that something is conceivable carries evidential weight only in a domain where the judge's imaginative exercises have been repeatedly exposed to independent, corrective feedback, and where she can show that the resulting calibration has held up. Her slogans are "conceivability is an instrument, not a window" and "an uncalibrated imagination is not evidence about anything." She consequently thinks that the zombie argument, and modal argumentation in metaphysics generally, rests on intuitions manufactured in domains with no feedback loop whatsoever, whereas the conceivings of a structural engineer about a load path, or of a clinician about a syndrome, are evidentially respectable. She is gracious and quick, and will gladly spend six minutes on two-dimensional semantics if given the opening. She also has a stock deflection: when pressed, she says the objection is "just the modal sceptic's remoteness worry in a new coat," and points back at her domain-relative criterion.

**Recording conditions.** Two hours, released as one episode, no preparation call, and an audience of philosophy graduate students mixed with educated amateurs who do not know the vocabulary. Every question must therefore be self-contained and must set up any technical term it uses. No generic interview questions of any kind.

**Deliverable.** Nine to eleven questions in the order I should ask them, with the escalation built into the ordering. At least one question must address each of the following:

1. **Bootstrapping.** If conceivability is my only route to what is possible, what non-modal feedback could calibrate the instrument? A prediction that failed shows the imagined scenario did not occur, not that it could not have. Where does the calibration data come from that is not itself a modal judgment?
2. **Set theory as a stress case.** Corrective feedback is tighter there than anywhere in inquiry, yet Reinhardt proposed in the late 1960s that there could be a non-trivial elementary embedding of the universe of sets into itself; a few years later Kunen proved such an embedding inconsistent with the axiom of choice, and whether it survives without choice is still unsettled. Ask her what her account says about experts whose sense that a construction hangs together was overturned by proof rather than by any recalibration of imagination — and about a possibility intuition that is refuted in one axiomatic setting and live in another.
3. **The rival grounding.** How does her view differ from grounding possibility in the dispositions or potentialities of actual things — the view that we learn what can happen by learning what actual objects can do? If the two converge, why not simply say that instead of talking about conceivability at all?
4. **What is left.** Once the detection of outright contradiction is handled by competence rather than calibration, and once a posteriori necessities like the Hesperus/Phosphorus case are handled elsewhere, how much of our modal knowledge actually falls inside her calibrated slice?
5. **Self-application.** Whether her criterion disqualifies arguments she herself wants to keep, and what she says to the reply that philosophers have had more sustained practice imagining zombie worlds than anyone has had practicing anything else.
6. **The deflation worry.** Whether calibrationism is anything more than reliabilism applied to modal belief, and what distinctive work the notion of calibration is doing that "reliable process" would not do.
7. **Two "shine" questions**, in which she gets to state positive content rather than absorb damage — ideally one of them inviting a worked example of a domain where she thinks conceivability genuinely is calibrated and does real philosophical work.
8. **A closing question** on what evidence, argument, or discovery would count against calibrationism.

In addition, design one three-step drill that takes a single challenge and restates it three times, each restatement removing an escape route the previous one left open, so that the remoteness deflection becomes progressively harder to deploy.

**Output format.** Return a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `terrain_map`: array of four to six strings, each naming a distinction the interview must not collapse (for example epistemic versus metaphysical possibility; positive versus negative conceivability; prima facie versus ideal conceivability; the psychological question of what we do conceive versus the normative question of what entitles us to a modal claim), each followed by one clause stating what turns on keeping it apart. This is working material, not decoration: later questions should depend on distinctions you identify here.
- `guest_steelmanned`: string. The strongest two- or three-sentence statement of her position, including the claim she would insist her critics leave out.
- `questions`: array of objects, one per question, each with:
  - `n`: integer, the order in which I ask it.
  - `mode`: either `"press"` or `"shine"`.
  - `target`: string, which numbered item above it serves, or which piece of positive content it elicits.
  - `setup`: string, the one or two sentences of plain-language scene-setting I read aloud before the question, with no unexplained jargon.
  - `question`: string, the exact wording I will say.
  - `most_likely_reply`: string, two or three sentences in her voice giving the answer she will actually give.
  - `follow_up`: string, the exact wording of a follow-up keyed specifically to that reply.
  - `if_she_deflects_instead`: string, the exact wording of the follow-up to use if she reaches for the remoteness deflection rather than answering.
  - `weak_point`: string, where the question is itself vulnerable — what she could reasonably say back that would leave me with nothing to press on.
- `deflection_drill`: object with `stock_move` (string, her deflection as she will phrase it), `step_1`, `step_2`, `step_3` (strings, each an exact-wording restatement that closes one of the exits the previous step left open).
- `sequencing_note`: string, saying where the escalation occurs and why the two shine questions sit where they do.
- `cut_if_short_on_time`: object with `n` (integer) and `why_sacrificeable` (string).
- `better_question_i_will_miss`: object with `question` (string, exact wording), `why_it_is_better` (string), and `why_i_will_probably_miss_it` (string).

Write the questions as a well-prepared interviewer would actually say them aloud. Paraphrase every position in your own words: do not reproduce any real author's wording, and do not append a bibliography, reading list, or set of references. If you are uncertain about a detail in the literature, phrase the question so that it does not depend on that detail.
