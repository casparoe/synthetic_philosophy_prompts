A friend and I spent most of an evening on the family of views that locates normativity in idealized deliberation — what an agent would endorse under fuller information, more vivid appreciation, sounder reasoning, or after the sort of cognitive psychotherapy Brandt prescribed — and we made no progress, because she held that such a view is empty until the idealizing conditions are spelled out, and I held that they can be spelled out without smuggling in the answer. Before we resume, I want the specification rather than another round: what must an account of idealized deliberation deliver in order to count as substantive instead of a placeholder?

Give the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. I expect at least: the choice of starting materials and why the verdict is not an artifact of that choice; whether the list of idealizing parameters is closed; non-question-beggingness, in that the conditions may not be selected by consulting the very normative facts under explanation; recognizability, in that the actual agent must be able to see the output as hers rather than a stranger's; and consistency with what we know about reflection, which does not reliably improve judgment. Add whatever further requirements you judge genuine. I particularly want your verdict on three I suspect are load-bearing: idempotence, meaning that feeding the output back into the procedure should not move it; the monotonicity of partial idealization, meaning whether a bounded approximation is better or worse than none at all; and the unbounded-parameter problem, since more information and more coherence have no maximum, so the account must say what it picks out — a limit, a stability plateau, or nothing.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of a camp (I suspect unique convergence is a realist prejudice and recognizability an internalist one), and say which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, that is the result I most want, since it would mean I lose for a better reason than hers. Fix your purpose explicitly: an account adequate to settle a metaethical dispute, or one adequate to serve as the target a machine could be pointed at, as in coherent extrapolated volition and indirect normativity, and note where the list would differ between the two. Close with two or three sentences scoring the leading contenders — ideal observer theory, Brandt, the full-information accounts of welfare, and the alignment descendant — against the list.
