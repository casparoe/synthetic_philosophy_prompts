I'm the deputy head of our national statistics office and on Friday I'm giving the after-dinner speech at a reception for the fact that we just cracked the top five of the ladder-based world happiness ranking for the first time ever. The room will be about a third ministers and journalists who want a victory lap, a third local mayors who are already talking about "the wellbeing target", and a third survey people who know exactly what the ladder question asks and how much of our score is just log GDP per capita and social support doing the heavy lifting. I need seven minutes that doesn't lie to anybody, doesn't kill the party, and doesn't hand the mayors a number to optimize — because the second a ranking becomes a target you get Goodhart plus a pile of people quietly gaming a questionnaire.

One thing I haven't decided: on the experienced-wellbeing measures, how people say their yesterday actually felt, we're somewhere around fortieth. Should that go in the speech, and if so how do I land it without telling a room full of citizens their lives are worse than they think?

Write the speech, then afterwards tell me which theory of wellbeing I ended up smuggling in and where the seams are, so I know before the philosophers in the room do.
