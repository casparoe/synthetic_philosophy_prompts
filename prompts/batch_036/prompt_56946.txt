I sit on a three-person panel at our state benefits agency that has to sign off before any behavioral intervention goes out to claimants. Here's the proposal in front of us Thursday, roughly as the program team wrote it: instead of the usual "your coverage ends soon, please renew" letter, they send a message saying the person is "provisionally approved" for the next period and just needs to tap a button to confirm, with a date after which that status lapses. The eligibility check doesn't actually run until after they tap — so the provisional approval isn't a fact about thier file, it's a device for getting them to open the thing. Our charter has two tests: the intervention must not mislead, and the recipient must not mind having had it done to them once it's explained to them. The pilot roughly doubled renewal rates in the hardest-to-reach group, which is the whole point of the program, but that same group also had by far the most people confirm, get denied, and then get chased for overpayments. One colleague says the wording is literally true and the push is toward something people already want; the other says it's engineered ambiguity and disqualifying whatever the numbers say.
