Before I read another paper on whether truth is a substantive property I want the spec, because I'm convinced a lot of the deflationist/inflationist debate is just two sides demanding different things of a theory and pretending they're arguing about the same thing. So: write out the desiderata. What does any adequate theory of truth have to deliver? The phenomena it must explain (truth's generalizing job, including generalizations over infinitely many sentences; the way truth looks load-bearing in explaining why an agent's action succeeded given that they were mostly right; the normativity of belief and assertion; the possibility of error; genuine disagreement as opposed to mere difference in what people say; gaps and gluts), the sentences it must handle without ad-hockery (liar, strengthened liar, Curry), the distinctions it must not collapse (truth vs warranted assertibility, having a truth predicate vs having a truth concept, defining the predicate vs giving a theory of the property), and the formal results it has to be consistent with. For every item give me the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a slogan, and say which ones are non-negotiable and which are one camp's preferences dressed up as data — is the demand that truth have an analysable nature a requirement, or is it the whole question? Then tell me which single item knocks out the most candidates.

Most of all I want the impossibility results, the places where the list is itself the finding. I suspect atleast two subsets can't be jointly satisfied: classical logic + every instance of the equivalence schema + a language containing its own truth predicate; and a deflationist demand that the truth theory be conservative over the base theory versus the explanatory work people point at when they say truth is doing something real (deriving the consistency of arithmetic, reflection, that sort of thing). Say which requirement gets dropped in each case and why that's the right one to drop. The setting I care about is self-application — an agent with a truth predicate it applies to its own sentences — so paradox is not a technicality I can set aside, it's the main event. Also, don't just accomodate a phenomenon you think a theory should explain away; say so and say why.

Finish with a committed verdict, not a menu: which requirements stay in the final spec even if that disqualifies a whole family of views, and your reasons. Then a couple of lines each on how disquotationalism, a prosentential view, and a substantive identity-or-correspondence theory come out against the list you just built.
