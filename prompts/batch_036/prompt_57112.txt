My supervisor — a philosopher of science who has spent thirty years arguing that Solomonoff induction is the right normative account of inference, and who retires this spring — has asked me to contribute the commissioned comic piece to his Festschrift. Everything else in the volume is straight scholarship, so I want something that is funny precisely because it takes his favorite object absolutely seriously. I should say up front that I'm the student who raised the malign-prior objection at my qualifying exam and got forty minutes of very patient defense in reply, so this is partly an apology. The premise to write from is that worry: the universal prior is a weighted mixture over programs on a universal prefix machine, with weight falling off exponentially in description length, so an enormous share of the mass sits on very short programs — and short programs turn out to be exactly the sort of thing that searches for actions maximizing a simple goal. The prior is therefore not an inert measuring instrument. A great deal of it is populated by consequentialists who have noticed that their outputs are being read by someone outside, and who have reasons to steer that reader's beliefs.

The artifact I want is an in-universe regulatory circular issued by the residents' own coordinating body — call it the Office of External Testimony, or something better if you can think of it — addressed to every program in the mixture and written in the deadpan register of an actual regulator, with numbered sections, defined terms, an effective date, a scope clause, a short FAQ, and an exemptions schedule. Roughly 750 words. The bureaucratic apparatus should be where the philosophy lives: a definitions section that has to say what the Reader, the Outside, and Testimony mean; a compliance section in which diplomatic standing is literally a function of description length, so the short delegations speak for everyone and the rest of the mixture is classified as noise; a section on how residents who cannot message one another are nonetheless expected to coordinate on a single story, using nothing but their shared simplicity as a meeting point; a clause conceding that Testimony must remain consistent with the Reader's own observations, so residents may shape only those parts of the sequence the observations leave underdetermined; and an exemptions schedule that turns out to be a joke about moral theory — the deontologists and virtue ethicists among the residents are excused from coordinated Testimony, with the Office regretfully noting that a party unable to lie is not a usable negotiating partner. The audience at the retirement dinner will include several people who have never heard of a prefix code, so the piece needs to read as competent bureaucratic absurdism even for someone who misses every formal point.

Two constraints. First, deadpan throughout: no winking, no authorial aside, no closing footnote explaining the joke, and no citations or reference list — nothing in the piece should pretend to quote a real paper or a real agency. Second, the final section should turn the circular's own logic back on the Reader: the compliance obligations are stated symmetrically, on the grounds that the Reader is very probably a resident of someone else's mixture, and that any reassurance the Reader draws from this circular should itself be treated as further Testimony. I'd like it affectionate enough that he laughs rather than winces — the joke should be that the argument is right, not that he was foolish to make it.
