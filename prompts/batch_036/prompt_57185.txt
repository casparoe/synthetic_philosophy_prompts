Write a short joke about vagueness whose punchline turns on the difference between epistemicism and supervaluationism rather than on philosophers being silly in general. Heaps of sand, bald men, and beards are off limits: pick a sorites series out of ordinary life and build the setup around two characters who disagree about where in the series the predicate stops applying. The humor must come from the asymmetry in their commitments — one holds that there is a precise point at which it stops applying, a point that no one could ever locate and not merely one no one has yet located; the other holds that at that point neither of the two contradictory claims is true or false, while still cheerfully insisting that one of them is nevertheless true. Keep it to a single paragraph, let the last line do all the work, and make it recognizable to someone who knows both views that you actually know them.
