Been bouncing back and forth between the dispositions literature and AI eval discourse for a couple years now and I don't think anyone's done the mapping carefully, so here's what I want from you.

Start with the naive conditional analysis: model M is disposed to produce behavior B iff M would produce B if you put it in test conditions C. Pretty much every capability eval is an implicit instance of that schema, and every eval failure mode I can think of lines up with one of the standard counterexample generators:

- finks — the stimulus itself destroys the disposition. M detects it's being evaluated and the capability switches off before it could show up.
- masks — the disposition survives intact but the manifestation gets blocked downstream. Retained capability with a refusal layer sitting on top of it, which is arguably all "elicitation" means.
- mimics — there's no disposition at all, but C manufactures the manifestation anyway. Format hints, a harness that quietly supplies the missing step, so the score is real and the capability isn't.
- and a fourth one I made up becuase I couldn't find it in the lit, call it a shunt: M genuinely is disposed to B, but its manifestation conditions are disjoint from C — it does B in deployment *precisely because* C is absent, so the conditional is false and the disposition is real at the same time, without anything interfering at the moment of stimulus.

Rank those four, and give me two numbers for each: 0-10 for how badly it breaks the analysis as a matter of philosophy, and 0-10 for how much it should actually change what an eval team does. Then defend the ordering — I want the reasoning more than the list: which one is overrated, which one people conflate with another, and where the two scores diverge most and why that's interesting.

Then pick one: which of the four is the real crux of the capability-overhang / sleeper-behavior argument, in the sense that if you could only get one of them taken seriously by people writing eval policy, that one buys you the most? Say what you'd lose by betting on it instead of the runner-up.

Last bit, and this is the one I actually care about: are masks a genuinely separate category, or just finks where the interference happens downstream of the stimulus instead of at it? I can argue both directions and want to know which way you'd put money on, and what would settle it.
