My two housemates have been having the same argument for about two years and I've given up trying to referee it, so I'm outsourcing.

Background: Marcus stopped eating beef roughly two years ago and now eats mostly chicken and some fish, on the theory that he's causing less suffering. Ines says he's got it backwards and that the swap probaby increased the total.

Round 1 (Ines): it's just arithmetic. One steer gives you a couple hundred kilos of meat — hundreds of meals. A broiler gives you maybe a kilo and a half, five or six meals. So per unit of protein he went from a fraction of one animal to dozens or hundreds of them. And broilers are the most miserable animals in the whole system — bred to lay down muscle so fast their legs can't carry them, slaughtered at six weeks. He traded a small number of animals with decent lives for a huge number with awful ones.

Round 2 (Marcus): headcount isn't the metric. If what you care about is suffering, what matters is how much bad experience there is, and there's no reason 200 lives of 40 days contains more of it than one life of 20 months. Counting individuals regardless of how each life goes is a weird view — it implies you make the world better by bringing more animals into existence at slightly-positive welfare.

Round 3 (Ines): then you can't have it both ways. If total welfare is the metric and a cow's life is net positive, the cows who never exist because you stopped buying beef are a loss, not a saving — by your own logic your boycott made things worse. Either lives are net negative, in which case say so about cattle too, which have some of the least bad lives in agriculture, or existing is a benefit, in which case my objection to your chicken goes away but so does your defense of yourself.

Round 4 (Marcus): fine, I'll say it. Most broilers' lives are net negative and most cattle's aren't, especially on pasture. I'm summing welfare, not counting heads, and the sum comes out where it comes out. Also, the beef I gave up was supermarket grain-finished beef, so it was never the good case to begin with.

Round 5 (Ines): then explain the feed. Broilers convert grain at something like 2:1 and feedlot cattle much worse, and growing that grain kills field mice and voles and who knows how many insects — plowing, harvest, rodenticide. His chicken is crops plus a bird. Once field deaths are on the ledger, the plant-heavy diet stops being the low-death option everyone assumes, and the ranking might come out pasture beef, then plants, then chicken.

Round 6 (Marcus): unmoved. Per calorie, crops kill fewer animals than crops run through a bird, because the conversion is lossy — stacking an animal layer on top of agriculture can't reduce agriculture's toll. And her ranking needs pasture's own footprint — fencing, whatever habitat was there before, the whole managed ecology — to be zero, which it isn't. Ines: your argument proves too much. If rodent and invertebrate deaths are big enough, the kindest diet is the one using the least cropland, and you'd end up eating steak for the animals' sake. Marcus: I'd bite that bullet, it's just a very small one — nobody has established insect sentience, the rodent numbers are wildly contested and depend heavily on tillage practice, and I can cut those deaths with no-till and hedgerows without raising cattle.

Neither of them will move. Can you go through it move by move — for each of the six rounds, say who's right, who's partly right, and exactly where the move fails if it does — and then say who's ahead overall, if anyone? And separately: what should one of them have said that neither did, either to settle it or to show it can't be settled on these terms?
