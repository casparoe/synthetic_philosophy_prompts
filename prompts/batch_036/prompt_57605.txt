So here's a Everettian position I've more or less talked myself into, and I want you to try to break it rather than explain it back to me.

The view: the Born rule doesn't need deriving from anything spooky. Decoherence hands you a stable quasi-classical branch structure; right after a measurement and before you look, there are several copies of you, all equally real; a rational agent in that position should spread her credence by indifference over the copies — except that the "number of copies" isn't an integer headcount, it's the decomposition-invariant measure decoherence supplies, i.e. squared amplitude. That's roughly the self-locating-uncertainty / epistemic separability shape (the Sebens–Carroll line), and I want to lean on envariance-style swap arguments to explain why the measure has to be that one rather than a branch count.

The bit I most want pressure applied to: I think branch-counting isn't merely unmotivated, it's ruled out, because you can always re-decompose an unequal split into arbitrarily many equal-amplitude sub-branches, so a count has no invariant meaning at all.

What would have to be true for this to fail? Rank the defeaters, fatal down to merely awkward. In particular:

- Does the derivation need unitarity, or conservation of total measure? Picture a variant world with very slightly nonlinear dynamics, so total squared amplitude creeps up a bit at each branching event. Does my argument survive there — and if it does, is that a point in its favor or a sign it isn't saying anything?
- Is there anything in the formalism that actually forbids weights going like amplitude to the power p for p ≠ 2, or does the whole thing quietly rest on Gleason-flavored noncontextuality assumptions that are the real postulate wearing a theorem's clothes?
- What does my position commit me to that a collapse theorist or a Bohmian isn't committed to?

For what it's worth, what would move me most is a concrete finite-dimensional case where indifference and squared-amplitude weighting come apart and counting still looks defensible. And be honest about whether I've insulated the view with qualifications — "the measure is supplied by decoherence" is doing an awful lot of work and I'm not sure that's a derivation or a relabeled postulate. Tell me which objections a defender would themselves concede as biting and which they'd wave off, and why.
