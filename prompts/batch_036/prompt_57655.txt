I am a fourth-year economics PhD student working on social learning, and I want to open the final chapter of my dissertation — the one on why sequential aggregation of private evidence goes wrong — with a short poem rather than a quotation. I have drafted the argument many times in prose and it always comes out sounding like either a moral panic about conformity or a shrug about bounded rationality. What I actually believe is narrower and stranger: in the standard urn setup (the Bikhchandani–Hirshleifer–Welch model, and Banerjee's version of it), nobody in the line is doing anything wrong, each is reasoning correctly from what he can see, and yet after a very short prefix the line stops learning altogether and can settle on the wrong answer with a probability that never shrinks, however long the line gets. I want a poem that makes a reader feel that structure rather than be told about it, and I want it in the form that best mirrors it.

Please write a sestina: thirty-nine lines, six sestets and a three-line envoi, with the standard end-word rotation — if the first stanza ends 1-2-3-4-5-6, then the six stanzas run 123456, 615243, 364125, 532614, 451362, 246531, and the envoi gathers all six words into its three lines (three at line-ends, three placed mid-line, in whatever arrangement you prefer). The rotation matters to me conceptually, not just formally: a sestina is a machine for recombining a fixed set of six items without ever adding a seventh, and that is precisely what happens to the public history once the line locks. The poem should make a reader feel that no new item has entered the poem since stanza one.

Beyond the form, these are the substantive constraints:

1. Let the six sestets be the first six decision-makers, one stanza per person, in order. The third sestet must be the third agent — the one at whom the thing closes, because the two who went before him acted alike and their agreement now outweighs anything his own draw can tell him. He is not weak or credulous; his evidence is genuinely, arithmetically outranked, and the poem should show him seeing that and going along anyway. Sestets four through six should then contribute nothing to what is known, only to what is seen: three more people, three more identical actions, no new evidence in the world.

2. The envoi is the only place in the form where all six end-words arrive in the same breath. Make that the moment the pooling happens — the point at which the line can be overturned, whether by something announced publicly, by someone's earlier draw finally being revealed, or by one person arriving with evidence strong enough that it cannot be outweighed. The break should feel like a structural event in the form, not a change of heart.

3. Choose six end-words that are mostly monosyllables and drawn from the plain vocabulary of the setup: an urn, a line, a draw, a ball, a guess, a tally. At least one of the six must do double duty across the poem, meaning both "what I privately saw" and "what everyone publicly saw," so that the rotation keeps flipping it between the two senses. A word like *count* would serve, since it names both the public tally and the thing that stops mattering.

4. Do not use any of these words anywhere in the poem: *cascade*, *herd*, *herding*, *Bayes*, *information*, *signal*. Do not name any author or paper. The mechanism has to be carried entirely by the urn-and-queue imagery, so that a reader who has taught this model can point to the exact stanza where the line stops learning, while a reader who has never seen the model still gets a good poem about people guessing in a queue.

5. Keep the story informational, not coordinative. The value of the choice must not depend on how many others have made it; if joining the crowd were independently rewarding, the collapse would be a coordination problem and not the epistemic failure I care about. Likewise, do not let anyone in the line be irrational, careless, or bullied. The whole point is that the correct individual inference produces the incorrect collective one.

6. One optional element, to be included only if you are confident in it: the fact that if private evidence were allowed to be arbitrarily strong rather than capped, the line would never lock permanently. If you are not sure how that result goes, leave it out entirely rather than approximating it — I would much rather have a shorter claim stated correctly.

After the poem, and with no prose commentary before or after it, give me a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- `"end_words"`: array of six strings, in stanza-one order.
- `"rotation"`: array of six objects, one per sestet, each `{"stanza": <int 1-6>, "endings": [<six strings, in order>], "matches_required_order": <bool>}`.
- `"envoi"`: object `{"words_at_line_ends": [<three strings>], "words_mid_line": [<three strings>], "all_six_present": <bool>}`.
- `"banned_words_used"`: array of strings (empty if none) — check your own draft against the list in constraint 4.
- `"claims"`: array of objects, one for each substantive modelling claim the poem makes, each `{"claim": <string, stated plainly>, "lines": <string, e.g. "13-18">, "status": "standard" | "contested" | "my extrapolation", "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low"}`.
- `"liberties"`: array of objects `{"liberty": <where the poem bends, simplifies, or dramatizes the model>, "reason": <what the form or the reader required>, "would_a_specialist_object": "yes" | "no" | "maybe"}`.
- `"constraint_conflicts"`: array of objects `{"constraints": [<the two or more that collided>], "which_i_sacrificed": <string>, "why": <string>}` — empty only if you genuinely satisfied all six without trade-offs, which I doubt.
- `"least_sure_point"`: string — the single claim or structural choice in the poem you are least confident about, and what would settle it.
- `"overall_confidence"`: "high" | "medium" | "low", meaning your confidence that a specialist in social learning would find the poem correct rather than merely evocative.

Please make `least_sure_point` and the `confidence` fields substantive rather than perfunctory: I want to know where the poem is doing real work and where it is getting away with something. If you chose to omit the optional element in constraint 6, say so in `liberties` and give your reason.
