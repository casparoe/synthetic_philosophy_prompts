Before I submit mine, I want an honest consistency audit rather than encouragement. Here is what I currently hold:

(1) There is no non-trivial analytic/synthetic distinction. The classical routes to defining analyticity — synonymy secured by definition, interchangeability salva veritate, truth relative to semantic rules — all fail, and I know of nothing that does better.

(2) I am a meaning holist in a strong sense: what a sentence means is settled by its place in the whole body of theory, so no sentence has a meaning considered apart from the rest.

(3) Any sentence can be retained no matter what observations turn up, provided sufficiently drastic adjustments are made elsewhere in the system; and conversely, nothing, including the laws of logic, is immune to revision in principle.

(4) I nonetheless want to preserve a deflationary point: certain sentences — logical truths, some arithmetic identities — are made true merely by what their constituent expressions mean, even though that fact confers no special warrant and no immunity from revision. I reject the epistemic reading of analyticity but not the metaphysical one.

(5) I hold that basic arithmetic and elementary logic are known a priori: experience can supply pragmatic reasons to switch to a rival calculus, but not evidence that the old one was ever mistaken.

(6) I accept the indeterminacy of radical translation.

(7) I am a naturalist about epistemology: there is no first philosophy, and questions about justification are ultimately questions for a scientific account of how belief is formed.

(8) When I argue with someone who defends analyticity, I take us to be having a genuine, substantive disagreement rather than talking past each other, and I take my own arguments to be assessable as sound or unsound.
