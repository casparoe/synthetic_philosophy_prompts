In a philosophy-of-physics reading group I belong to, we got to the Principal Principle, and one member waved it away with "probability is in the eye of the beholder." His gloss: if chances are nothing over and above credences, then a principle instructing me to set my credence equal to the chance either tells me nothing new — it merely aligns one of my attitudes with another — or it quietly presupposes the objective chances it was supposed to explain. I suspected a slide somewhere but couldn't locate it on the spot.

Could you separate the precise theses that one sentence might be carrying? I can dimly see at least a metaphysical claim that there are no chances in the world, a semantic claim about what probability statements mean, an epistemic claim that chance can bear on belief only through evidence, and a normative claim about what coherence requires — but please build the menu yourself rather than following my sketch, ordered from platitude to radical, and for each entry say whether it is true, what it would take to establish it, and whether it licenses his dismissal. Then tell me what becomes of the triviality charge on each reading: does the Principal Principle degenerate into an internal consistency constraint on my own beliefs, or does it stay a substantive bridge from the world to credence — and does that turn on how the clause about inadmissible evidence is understood?
