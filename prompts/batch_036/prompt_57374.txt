I'm about three quarters of the way through a novel and I've hit the wall on the scene I've been dreading and looking forward to in equal measure. Setup, quick, so the drafts make sense: an invented country, call it Karst. Thirty-odd years ago the government relocated a highland minority into "model villages" and took their children to boarding schools where the language was beaten out of them, and a certain number of adults went into the interior ministry and never came out. Karst has changed regime twice since. My prime minister is going to stand up in the parliament and issue a formal apology on behalf of the country for all of it.

I've written six versions of the speech and my editor says she can't tell which one is supposed to be the good one, and honestly neither can I anymore. They've all gone grey on me. So I need an outside read.

**A.** "What happened in the highlands happened in a different Karst, under a different flag, by men who are mostly dead. This government did not order it. We inherited it, the way we inherited the roads and the debt. But your grief is real, it is here, and it is in this room, and for the grief we are sorry. We will build a museum on the site of the Vilno school and we will fund the keeping of your language, so that what was taken from your grandparents is given back to your grandchildren."

**B.** "The state took children from their parents. The state killed some of those parents. There is no context that makes this anything but a crime, and the state that committed it is the state I lead today — same seal, same treasury, same name. We are sorry. We will establish a compensation fund, we will open the files, and we will prosecute where prosecution is still possible. In return — and I use that word deliberately — we ask the families to withdraw the suits now pending, and to accept that this matter is closed, and to forgive, so that Karst can heal."

**C.** "Mistakes were made. Harm occurred. People suffered in ways that no one should suffer and it is right that we say so here. We regret that this harm occurred. No one alive in this chamber intended it. Let us now turn to the future, which is the only place any of us can still act."

**D.** "Yes, the state took children. We say it plainly, and we are sorry. But we should also say plainly that in those years nobody's hands were clean. The highland brigades killed too — ask the families of the conscripts at Serratt bridge. Violence belonged to all of us. So let us not sort this country into the guilty and the innocent. Let us apologize to each other, mutually, and be done with it."

**E.** "Between 1961 and 1974 the state of Karst removed eleven thousand children from their families and placed them in schools where they were forbidden their language, and it detained or killed an unknown number of their parents, and the number is unknown because we destroyed the records that would tell us. I am not going to say the times were different. I am not going to say both sides. I am not going to ask you to forgive us, and I am not going to ask you to let this be closed, because it isn't ours to close. You are entirely within your rights to hear this apology and refuse it, and if you refuse it that will not make you unreasonable and it will not make this speech untrue. Some of you were beaten by men who draw pensions from this building, and I draw a salary from it too. Whatever you tell us to do next, we will do, and I have not come here with a price already set on it."

**F.** "Before I am prime minister I am a man of sixty-eight who took a job in the ministry in 1969 because it was the only job offered to me, and who signed things without reading them. I have read them now. So I will not stand here and make my country apologize, because my country is a large abstraction and I am not certain it is capable of remorse, and I will not put that word in the mouths of people who were not there. I can only tell you what I did and what I did not do, in my own name, and ask you for nothing at all."

Here's what I want from you:

1. Rank all six, most to least adequate *as a state apology* — not best written, not most likely to be popular, but which one a survivor sitting in the gallery could actually receive as an apology and which ones are counterfeits wearing apology clothes. Score each out of 10 on that dimension and give me a sentence or two each on the specific thing it does or fails to do.
2. Name the failure mode each one is committing. Invent your own labels if you like, but use them consistently, and say which of them you think are fatal versus which are survivable.
3. Tell me which one I should put in the mouth of my sincere character and which one in the mouth of the cynic — and be aware I might use the same draft for both, depending on how you read it. If one of them works as either, say so and say what flips it.
4. For whichever you rank first, give me the strongest objection a survivor in the room could still make to it. I don't want this scene to let the apology off easy, and I'd rather find the crack now than have a reviewer find it later.

I don't need sources or a bibliography, this is a novel and not a dissertation — I just need your judgement, argued for. When you're done, put the final ordering on its own last line in this exact form, e.g. \answer{E > B > F > A > D > C}, using your actual ranking.
