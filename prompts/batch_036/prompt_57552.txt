I'm in a philosophy of psychiatry seminar and we're on Szasz this week. A guy in the class who's pre-med kept pushing back with what seems to be the standard line in these debates now: depression is like diabetes. Same underlying structure, he said -- an organ isn't doing its chemical job, the patient didnt choose it, blaming them for it is as cruel and as stupid as blaming someone for not making enough insulin, and the fix is a pill that corrects the imbalance. Once you see that, Szasz's whole "myth" thing collapses, because nobody thinks diabetes is a metaphor. The prof just said the analogy is doing a lot of work and moved on, which helped me exactly zero.

So can you audit it? Tell me what plays the role of the pancreas, the insulin, the blood sugar reading, the lesion -- and then where the mapping actually holds versus where it comes apart, separating the breakdowns that kill his use of it from the ones that are just cosmetic. I especially want to know what the diabetes case is quietly lending him that the depression case doesn't have: a lab test that settles who's in and who's out, an uncontested account of what the organ is for, a deviation from normal that everyone agrees is a bad thing, a patient who's the same before and after treatment. And the other direction too -- where do you think Szasz himself overreaches, if he takes the disanalogies to show that these people aren't ill at all and are just having problems in living? I don't buy that either, and I'd like to know what the analogy's failure does and doesn't license.

Don't give me a balanced both-sides list. I want an actual verdict -- does the diabetes analogy survive the work he's making it do, or was it carrying his conclusion the whole time -- with your reasons on the table. Five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up at the end.
