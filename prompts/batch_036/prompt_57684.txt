so me and my friend D got into it last night, roughly 11pm to 2am, about commitment races — and of course the thing I'm supposed to teach on friday is commitment races. great timing. the session: 90 minutes, twelve new research fellows at a small AI-policy-ish org, all smart, mostly CS and econ, no philosophy whatsoever, and at least three of them have enough game theory to be insufferable about it. the term as we use it internally means something like: a situation where it's individually sensible to stake out a binding, visible commitment early — a threat, a promise, a public "we will do X come what may" — but the competition to be the one who commits first drags everybody to a worse place than if nobody had been able to commit at all. D thinks that's a prisoner's dilemma wearing a trenchcoat: two players, dominant strategy to defect, mutual defection is bad, nothing new to say. I think the structure is genuinely different, because the disaster isn't caused by anyone reneging, it's caused by the players successfully pulling off the move the theory tells them is smart, and the ones who end up worst off are the ones who moved too slowly to bind themselves. neither of us budged.
