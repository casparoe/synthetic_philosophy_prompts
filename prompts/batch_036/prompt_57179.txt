I put up the two Allais pairs, someone agrees their choices clash with the independence axiom, and then rather than drop the axiom they say the problem statement left something out: the certain option also buys you not having to explain yourself afterwards, or there's a small chance the organiser goes bust so both options can pay nothing, or the winnings land in different tax years so they aren't really the same amounts. Same thing occured with the sure-thing principle in a medical example — the doctor says the test result also tells her how the patient will respond, so the two acts weren't the same acts after all. So the pattern is: an axiom about choices gets treated as incapable of being violated, and any apparent violation is read as evidence that the description of the options was incomplete.
