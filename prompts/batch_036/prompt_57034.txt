Okay so a little context before the actual ask: I'm a junior and I do an independent study in philosophy of science, and on weekends I volunteer on a dig the state university runs in a river valley, Bronze Age-ish, and crucially no writing survives from there. The two people supervising my trench have been having basically the same arguement for like a year now and it keeps leaking into the comments of the draft site report. Dr. A (the professor) thinks you can make disciplined inferences from objects to beliefs and institutions if you're careful about sampling and about actually listing the rival explanations, and her whole thing is that if we can't do that we can't say anything about the past at all. Dr. B (the postdoc) thinks that the step from a material regularity to a belief or an institution is exactly where the archaeologist's own concepts sneak in, and he'd sign off on us documenting what people did but not on what they thought they were doing or what any of it meant to them.

The thing they're fighting over concretely: we've got roughly forty sites spread along maybe 600 km of the valley, and at nearly all of them the stone weights come in the same ratio series, same handful of sizes over and over, for centuries. No palaces, no archives, no seals with any kind of authority mark on them, no pictures of anybody enforcing anything. Dr. A wants a section saying the weight standard is evidence of some institution that kept the standard stable, possibly backed by an authority, and she thinks the alternatives are worse explanations. Dr. B wants the report to say only that the weights were standardized, full stop, and that "institution" and "authority" are our imports. I'm changing some numbers and leaving the site unnamed because the report isn't public yet, so just work with the case as I've described it rather than trying to figure out which valley it is.

For my symposium project I want to write the document they should have produced instead, so: draft the joint methodology note that could actually go at the front of the site report with both their names on it. I want the claims they can genuinely both sign — about preservation bias, sampling, what absence of evidence licenses, what analogy with other societies is worth, that kind of thing. Then the residual disagreements, each one worded so the person holding it would accept the wording about their own view instead of a caricature, in a two-column layout so I can see them side by side. Then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one of them, and for each crux a case or comparison or test that both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance like a pre-registration. Two test ideas they've both half-accepted but never put in writing: comparing the valley against places where texts do survive so you independently know whether standardization there came with enforcement or with merchant convention; and something I'd call the norm problem, whether a regularity that's stable and rarely violated can ever support "this was required" instead of just "this was usual," where maybe the test is whether exceptions get marked in some costly way — push back on that one if its bad, I made it up on the bus. Finally, tell me which parts of this fight are just about words.
