So I had this argument last night with my friend Priya (she's doing a phil of science PhD, I just read this stuff for fun) about whether Woodward's interventionist theory of causation actually gives us anything Lewis's counterfactual theory didn't already give us, just repackaged with arrows and structural equations. My point was that "intervention" is defined in explicitly causal terms (an intervention on X w.r.t. Y has to change Y only via X, no fiddling with other causes) so the whole thing is circular and doesn't reduce causation to anything more basic, wheras at least Lewis is trying to ground causal claims in the non-causal machinery of similarity across possible worlds. Priya's comeback was that Woodward never promised reduction, just non-triviality, and that Lewis's account falls apart on ordinary preemption cases (Billy and Suzy throwing rocks at the bottle) in a way interventionism just doesn't, since you can build the backup causal path directly into the structural equations. We got stuck arguing about Hitchcock's train-and-loop-track case and neither of us could agree on whether an interventionist can give a principled non-ad-hoc answer about what counts as "the" cause there without secretly smuggling in counterfactual intuitions about the loop track anyway.

Can you write a dialogue between a committed Lewisian counterfactual theorist and a Woodward-style interventionist hashing this out? I want them to actually engage with each other's specific objections (circularity charge vs. preemption/switching cases) rather than just restating their own view, and I'd like the train-loop-track case or something structurally similar to come up as a concrete test case they both try to handle. Neither should just cave at the end - if there's a real resolution have them find it, but it's fine if they end up locating the actual crux of their disagreement instead.
