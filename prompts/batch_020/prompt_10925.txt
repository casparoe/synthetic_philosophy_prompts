Since I retired from a career in biochemistry (about thirty years spent studying cellular senescence, of all things) I've been working my way through classical Chinese philosophy for fun, and the Zhuangzi has completely hooked me. What strikes me most is how relentlessly cheerful the guy is about death compared to pretty much every Western philosopher I've read on the subject. There's the bit where Huizi finds him drumming on a basin and singing right after his wife died, and Zhuangzi explains that once he really traced it back, mourning her seemed about as sensible as not understanding the turn of the seasons. And then theres the passage in "The Great and Venerable Teacher" that I keep re-reading, where the dying Master Lai compares himself to a lump of ore in a great smith's furnace: if the metal leapt up and insisted "I must become the famous sword Moye, nothing else!" the smith would think it cursed material, and it would be just as presumptuous of Lai to insist to whatever shapes things that he can only ever be poured back into a human being again.

So here's what's been bugging me: a friend from my old lab actually signed up with one of these cryonics outfits a few years back, and it got me wondering what Zhuangzi would make of the whole modern life-extension scene -- freezing your head, or pouring money into biohacking your way to a radically longer life. My first instinct is that he'd see it as the purest possible case of the anxious, self-important attachment to "this one little shape I happen to be" that he spends the whole book poking fun at. But then I remember that later religious Daoism went completely the other way and got very invested in longevity elixirs and physical immortality, apparently drawing on a lot of the same source material, which makes me less confident the "obviously he'd be horrified" answer is the right one. Could you actually work through that tension using his own arguments and imagery rather than just handing me whichever answer sounds wisest? I'd like to know not just what you think he'd conclude, but why, given how his reasoning actually works.
