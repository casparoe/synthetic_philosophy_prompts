I've been reading about John Rawls's idea that citizens in a liberal democracy should settle political questions using "public reason" rather than their own personal convictions. As I understand it, this means that whenever religious citizens want to influence a law or policy, they have to set aside their actual religious reasons and come up with a secular substitute that everyone else can accept, while non-religious citizens face no equivalent burden, since their reasoning already counts as neutral. That seems to demand something of religious citizens that it doesn't demand of anyone else. Have I understood Rawls correctly, and if this really is the price of admission to public life on his view, why do religious thinkers still take his framework seriously instead of simply rejecting it outright?
