So this has been rattling around in my head for like two weeks now. My friend Dana sat me down and told me, pretty seriously, that I need to stop flaking on plans last minute because its inconvenient and kind of hurtful to people. And fine, I probably do that more than I should. Except Dana cancels on people constantly too, if anything more than me, she's just smoother about making it sound like a real emergency every time so nobody calls her on it. When I brought this up she said something like "that doesn't make me wrong, my flakiness and your flakiness are two seperate issues," and she also made it pretty clear she doesn't even see her own cancellations as the same category of thing as mine, apparently hers are always for legit reasons and mine aren't. Logically, sure, two wrongs don't make a right, so what she actually said to me could still be completely correct. But it still feels like there's something off about HER specifically being the one to say it, like she loses some kind of right to bring it up even though nothing she said was technically false.

I brought this up with my roommate and he thinks I'm just grasping for an excuse not to deal with valid criticism, which, ngl, might be true. But I keep noticing this exact same shape of argument show up everywhere, like when a country gets called out over human rights stuff and people just respond "you're no better," and I can never tell if that's a cheap dodge or if it's actually pointing at something real about who gets to say what to who. So here's my actual question: is there a real, meaningful difference between whether someone's criticism of you is true, and whether they had any standing to be the one saying it to your face in the first place? And if hypocrisy really does mess something up here, what exactly is it messing up, if not the truth of what they're saying?
