I teach a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind, and this week we're covering Tononi's Integrated Information Theory alongside global workspace and higher-order theories. While preparing the session, I invited a colleague from computer science to sit in, and he raised a point I haven't been able to shake since. IIT identifies the degree of consciousness a system has with its integrated information, Φ, standardly glossed as the extent to which the system's causal power as a whole exceeds what its parts could achieve operating in isolation. My colleague pointed out that a transformer-based language model integrates information on a scale nothing biological approaches: at every layer, every token's representation is recomputed as a weighted function of every other token's representation across the entire context, simultaneously, through billions of parameters, so that the network's output at any point is thoroughly non-separable from the joint state of the whole system. If that is what "integration" amounts to, it looks like current language models should already qualify, on IIT's own terms, as highly integrated systems, and therefore as bearing a significant degree of consciousness -- quite possibly more than a mouse, or even a human, with the amount only increasing as models scale further.

Most of the seminar is treating this as an obvious reductio of the theory rather than a genuine discovery about machine consciousness, but I'd like to be able to give them a more precise diagnosis than "that sounds absurd." Does the actual mathematics behind Φ license the inference my colleague is drawing from this informal description of integration, or is there some principled feature of how IIT is defined that blocks the move from "combines a great deal of information" to "has a great deal of integrated information" in Tononi's technical sense? And separately, since the inference seems to presuppose that consciousness tracks a purely computational or informational profile of a system, I'd be grateful for your view on whether that presupposition even sits well with IIT being, as I understood it, a rather strongly anti-functionalist theory.
