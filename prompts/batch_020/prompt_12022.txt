I spent my career as a neuroscientist studying reward and impulsivity, and since retiring I have been reading through the philosophical literature on weakness of will with real interest in how it connects to that work. One picture that has stayed with me is George Ainslie's picoeconomics. Because hyperbolic discount curves, unlike exponential ones, are guaranteed to cross, a person's preference between a smaller-sooner and a larger-later reward reverses as the smaller reward draws near. Ainslie treats the person, accordingly, not as one continuous agent but as a succession of momentary selves, with self-control, where it exists, emerging from a precarious bargain among them, policed by private "personal rules" whose breach threatens an entire future series of choices rather than just the present one. I would like a short story that dramatizes this specific model of akrasia rather than the more familiar picture of self-control through external constraint: no contracts, locks, or other people should do the restraining, only the negotiations among a character's own successive selves, with the crossing of two discount curves as the hinge on which the plot turns.
