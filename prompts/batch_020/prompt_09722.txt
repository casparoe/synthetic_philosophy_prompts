Evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) is basically the claim that in a universe (or multiverse) that's absurdly big, you should still act cooperatively toward value systems you don't hold and will never causally interact with, because your choice is evidence about what other similarly-reasoning agents scattered across that huge world are choosing too, and that nets everyone a better expected outcome. This is obviously a descendant of Hofstadter's old superrationality argument for why you should cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma even with no communication or binding contract between you and the other player. So whats actually new here? Is ECL just superrationality scaled up and given a coat of decision-theory jargon, or does the "huge world full of wildly different value systems" part do real conceptual work that plain superrationality can't pull off on its own? Try to pin down exactly where, if anywhere, the two genuinely part ways.
