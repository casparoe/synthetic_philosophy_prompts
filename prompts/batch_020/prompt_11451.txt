My daughter's bat mitzvah is in three weeks and her parsha is Vayikra, so her whole d'var is about animal sacrifice, of all things. Our rabbi told her to read some Maimonides to make it less weird, and now I'm the one who's obsessed: in the Guide he basically argues korbanot were a concession - God let the Israelites keep sacrificing so he could wean them off idol worship gradually instead of banning it outright. Except in the Mishneh Torah he writes, totally straight-faced, that sacrifices start right back up the moment the Temple's rebuilt, which seems to gut his own explanation, since presumably nobody's tempted by idols anymore by then. My father-in-law, who's leading Musaf that day (the part where we literally pray for the sacrifices to come back), is already side-eyeing me for "Rambam-ing" everyone at Shabbat dinner, and he's firmly Team Ramban, who tore into Maimonides for this exact explanation more than 700 years ago. Can you write the two-minute intro I have to give right before her speech - something honest about what Maimonides actually argued, that doesn't make sacrifice sound like a primitive embarrassment we're stuck apologizing for, and doesnt contradict my father-in-law or whatever more traditional spin she ends up putting on it?
