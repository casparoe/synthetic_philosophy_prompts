Cosmologists have noted that in a universe with a positive cosmological constant, random thermal fluctuations over a sufficiently long future will eventually generate far more "Boltzmann brains" — observers who fluctuate briefly into existence, complete with false memories of an orderly past, before dissolving back into chaos — than there ever are or were observers produced by ordinary cosmic and biological history. Since I have no way of telling from the inside which kind of observer I am, and Boltzmann brains are supposed to vastly outnumber genuine ones on such models, ordinary anthropic reasoning about my place among all observers seems to imply that I am far more likely to be one of these fluctuation-produced brains than a real product of that history. Given that, why do physicists who take this argument seriously keep refining their cosmological models and trusting the reasoning behind them, rather than concluding that all of it, including the argument that led to this very conclusion, is most likely just meaningless noise?
