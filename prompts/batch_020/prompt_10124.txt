So I'm part of a smallish forecasting collective — a handful of LW/ACX-adjacent people who got really into Tetlock-style calibration training a few years back and now informally consult for a couple of orgs that want probability estimates on messy stuff (regulatory outcomes, project timelines, whether some negotiation falls through). This whole thing landed on my desk because my housemate and I got into an argument about whether two people who disagree should just always split the difference on their credences, and it turned out our group has never actually nailed down a real policy on this even though it keeps coming up — so now I've been asked to write something up before our next retro.

The case that's forcing the issue: two of our strongest forecasters, A and B, did a full double-crux session on a live question (something like "does this merger get approved by regulators within 18 months") and are still about 25 points apart after finding what they both agree is the actual crux — they just weigh the same underlying uncertainty differently, and each is convinced the other is misjudging it. It's not a one-off either; they've diverged like this on three other questions this year. To complicate things, A has the better calibration score over our whole tournament history, while B has the better track record specifically on regulatory-type questions like this one, and nobody can agree on wether general calibration or category-specific track record is the one that should actually count.

Half the group wants a standing rule: whenever two forecasters can't converge after an honest double-crux, the team's official number defaults to some kind of average (maybe weighted by track record) instead of either person's number standing alone — the argument being that persistent disagreement between people who've genuinely done the work to find their crux is itself strong evidence that neither of them has it fully right, and that this is basically what Aumann-agreement-style reasoning should push us toward. The other half thinks this rule would be actively harmful: it'd punish exactly the people willing to hold an unpopular number, reward regression to the mean over actual insight, and it quietly assumes A and B are still "peers" in the relevant sense even after you condition on the track-record split. Could you write the short memo I'll circulate before the retro? It needs to land on an actual mechanism for handling this case and others like it going forward, not just "it depends," and it has to take the loosing side's best argument seriously instead of just gesturing at Aumann and moving on.
