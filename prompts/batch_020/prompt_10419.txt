So this week one of my coworkers mentioned, completely casually, that he's been microdosing psilocybin before big sprints to help him focus, which is apparently a whole thing in tech now (nootropic stacks, modafinil, all of it). It sent me down a random rabbit hole into what Plato actually thought about drinking and altered states, since the only thing I remembered going in was that the Symposium is set at a drinking party. Turns out that's barely the interesting part. In the Republic he uses what surfaces in dreams — especially after a night of overdoing it on food and wine, which is literally the example he gives — as proof that everyone's got a lawless, appetite-driven "beast" inside that's normally kept on a leash by reason and shame, and he leans on that to explain the psychology of a tyrant.

Then in the Laws he does this whole other thing: he spends a big chunk of the first two books defending regulated drinking parties as actually good for civic education. There's supposed to be a sober "president" running things specifically so it doesn't get out of hand, some age below which you're not even allowed to touch wine, and the idea that getting a bit drunk under the right supervision is basically a stress test that reveals whether someone has real self control or just looks like they do when nothing's tempting them. At first that felt like a flat contradiction with the Republic thing, but now I think it's more like the same mechanism (wine loosens reason's grip on appetite) counting as either a bug or a feature depending on whether anyone competent is actually supervising it.

Which is what made me think of the microdosing thing. So: given both of those texts, what do you think Plato would actually say about Silicon Valley's whole cognitive-enhancement-drug scene — would he file it under the Republic's "you're unleashing the beast, you just don't realize it yet" warning, or would he recognize the Laws' "properly supervised, this is actually fine and useful" structure in it? And here's the part I can't work out myself: microdosing is explicitly designed to stay sub-perceptual, the whole point is not losing control even a little, which seems like the opposite of his symposium model, where the entire value of the exercise depends on people actually loosing enough inhibition that their real character shows through. Does his framework even have anything useful to say about a drug practice that's built around never crossing that line? Don't just give me an "everything in moderation" answer — actually work through the tension.
