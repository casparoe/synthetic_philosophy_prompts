The student's answer, transcribed as written (I only fixed illegible handwriting, not grammar):

"Van Inwagen's Special Composition Question asks: under what conditions do some objects, the xs, compose a further object y? Since van Inwagen is a nihilist, he thinks that strictly speaking there are no chairs, tables, or statues -- only particles arranged chairwise, tablewise, etc. His actual answer, Organicism, is that the xs compose something exactly when their activity constitutes a life, so composition happens only among the parts of living organisms.

Van Inwagen rules out answers like contact, fastening, and cohesion because they make composition a matter of degree, and he thinks this would mean it's sometimes vague whether a given y exists, which is absurd -- existence doesn't come in degrees. Lewis and other universalists take that exact same premise (no vague existence) and run it the other way: any restricted answer to the SCQ, including "life," will have sorites-style borderline cases -- is a virus alive? what about the exact moment a cell divides? -- so if you rule out vagueness you can't have a restricted answer at all. Since nihilism is too costly, Lewis concludes composition must always occur: unrestricted universalism.

Merricks reaches Organicism too, just from a different starting point. Instead of arguing from vagueness, he argues from causal overdetermination: if a baseball and the particles arranged baseball-wise both cause the window to break, the baseball's causal contribution is redundant, so by a version of Ockham's razor the baseball doesn't exist. But that same overdetermination argument would eliminate any composite object whose causal powers are exhausted by its parts', which is every composite object except ones with genuinely emergent, non-redundant causal powers -- and living things have exactly that, since biological life involves higher-level causal processes (metabolism, self-repair) that aren't fully redundant with the sum of micro-level particle interactions. So Merricks and van Inwagen end up in the same place: organisms compose, nothing else does."
