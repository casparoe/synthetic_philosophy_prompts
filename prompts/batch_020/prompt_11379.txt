Over the last couple of years I've spent a lot of time on poker and sports-betting forums, where the Kelly criterion comes up constantly: the rule that a bet should be sized to maximize the expected logarithm of your bankroll rather than its expected value, since that is what maximizes your long-run compound growth rate. More recently, while reading through some old LessWrong posts, I came across the term "geometric rationality," which treats that same move — using a geometric mean instead of an arithmetic one — as an instance of a much more general principle, one that gets extended to situations quite far from repeated betting, such as how two parties should split the gains from a negotiation, or how to combine several people's conflicting probability estimates into a single number. I want to understand exactly how these two ideas relate, rather than simply assuming they are the same trick wearing two different hats. Please compare Kelly betting with this broader idea of geometric rationality. In which respects is Kelly betting merely a narrow special case that ordinary expected-utility reasoning, applied to a repeated gamble, could already justify on its own — and in which respects does geometric rationality rest on a genuinely different foundation that Kelly's reasoning alone does not establish?
