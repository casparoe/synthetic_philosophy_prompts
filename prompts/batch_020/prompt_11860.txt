I'm always stealing real arguments for my novels, and two friends of mine got into one on the long drive back from a wedding that I still can't stop turning over. Elena's doing a philosophy PhD, Jonah just likes to argue, and it started when Elena brought up Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: you act the way you do because of how you are, you never chose how you are (genes, upbringing, plain luck), and any move to reshape yourself just expresses a self you already had, so it regresses forever and nobody's ever ultimately responsible for anything. Jonah said great, so he's not responsible for anything either, then asked how that squares with everyone, her included, blaming each other constantly. Elena said fair, but funnily enough Galen Strawson's own father, P.F. Strawson, wrote a much earlier paper people now treat as the standing answer to exactly this: whether determinism's true is beside the point, since blaming and resenting isn't hostage to metaphysics, its just how humans relate to each other. Jonah said that's not an answer, its a description of what we'll do regardless, and doesn't tell you if it's fair to punish someone because they deserve it. Elena said the 'deserves it, ultimately, from nowhere' standard he wants was never what our practices ran on — we already excuse toddlers and the coerced for lacking specific local capacities, not for flunking some cosmic self-authorship test. Jonah, grasping a little, asked if quantum randomness in the brain might at least give someone a toehold of origination outside the chain of luck. Elena said no — Strawson's own answer is that a partly random act isn't any more yours, if anything less so, so chance just relocates the luck instead of removing it.

Could you go through their exchange move by move and say who's actually right at each step, not just who won overall, and flag anything important both of them are missing?
