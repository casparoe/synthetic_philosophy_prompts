I sit on the committee overseeing my hospital's clinical ethics consultation service. Consultants now work on a "facilitative" model, helping the treating team and family reason toward their own resolution rather than issuing a verdict. Administration wants to pilot a "directive" model for one recurring dispute: a surrogate refusing to stop ICU-level care that the team and consult service agree is non-beneficial. Under the pilot, the consultant's recommendation would enter the chart as an order the attending follows unless they formally document dissent. Some committee members call this no different from deferring to a specialist; others argue that an attending who complies with a verdict they neither understand nor endorse has not thereby acted well, and that this defeats the point of having ethics consultation at all. Draft the committee's policy memo on whether, and under what safeguards, to adopt the directive model for this dispute type, and engage the second objection on its merits rather than dismissing it as mere resistance to efficiency.
