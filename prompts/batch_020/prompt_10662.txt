I'm a novelist, and a small press has asked me for a cover blurb on a debut I read last month. I found it merely competent: careful prose, but the book as a whole left me cold. Since then, three readers whose literary judgment I trust more than my own — a former editor, a longtime critic, and a novelist whose last several recommendations all turned out to be right — have independently told me the book is quietly extraordinary, pointing especially to its handling of time in the middle section. I went back and reread those pages and still felt nothing remarkable, but their track record beats mine, and part of me wants to just write the blurb they would write. Still, putting my own name to a verdict I don't hold feels different from recommending a mechanic to a friend on someone else's say-so, and I can't tell whether that difference is real or whether I'm just being precious about my own authority as a reader. Is there an actual problem with calling a book good on other people's word rather than my own response to it, or is this simply an ordinary case of deferring to better-informed judgment?
