I'm the community member on my hospital's patient relations committee, and I keep turning over a question my colleagues would rather not name directly: how do we tell a patient's legitimate preference from a bias we shouldn't accommodate? We already grant same-sex requests for intimate procedures without debate, but race-based requests are multiplying, and they don't come in one flavor: a family refusing any Black nurse for their newborn, a Black patient requesting a Black physician after his pain was dismissed on a prior admission, and patients who simply want someone who shares their background, with no specific incident behind it. The committee is split between full accommodation on request, a flat ban on sorting staff by race, and a case-by-case test that nursing leadership calls unworkable on a busy floor. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to next month's meeting: commit to one workable policy, and make the strongest possible case for the option it rejects.
