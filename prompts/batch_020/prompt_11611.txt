my mom died two weeks ago and i'm giving her eulogy this saturday. she was a philosophy of mind professor for almost 30 years, and her whole career was basically one long fight against jaegwon kim's causal exclusion problem -- she was a non-reductive physicalist who defended what's called the "subset view," the idea that a mental property's causal powers are literally a subset of the causal powers of its physical realizer, so the mental never has to complete with the physical for causal credit, they're not two rivals. after the wake my friend dee (also philosophy-trained) and i argued for like two hours over whether that actually solves anything or whether it's just epiphenomenalism wearing a costume -- if all the mental power ever does is a subset of what the physical power already does, it's not adding anything, so why even call it causation. neither of us gave ground and i'm honestly still not sure who won.

now i actually have to write this eulogy and i'm kind of stuck. i've got about ten minutes, and half the room is her old department, people who will absolutely clock it if i quietly give up the store to dualism ("her love watches over us," "her spirit still causes things") or to eliminativism ("it was really just neurons, the love part is a story we tell ourselves"). the other half is family who don't care about any of that and just want to hear that she mattered, that her love for us was real and actually did something in the world. can you write it? it needs to land emotionally for the people who aren't philosophers, without quietly caving to either side of the fight she spent her career refusing to give ground on.
