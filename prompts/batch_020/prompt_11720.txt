So I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole the last few weeks reading about Al-Ghazali's occasionalism, mostly because I got into an argument with some guy on a forum about whether Islamic theology is "what killed science" in the Muslim world, and it turned out neither of us actually understood the view beyond the fire-and-cotton example. So now I want to actually get it right instead of just parroting that back at people.

Here's where I get stuck though. I get the basic move - fire touching cotton doesn't cause the burning, God directly creates the burning each time, the fire is just the "occasion," not a real cause. Fine. But Ghazali doesn't want the world to be totally chaotic either - he still talks about a "custom" (I think the Arabic word is "ada"?) that keeps things regular enough for us to cook food and build houses and whatever, and that custom only gets broken for an actual miracle. What I cant wrap my head around is what's doing the work of keeping that custom stable if there's genuinely no necessary connection underneath it. Like, if nothing is actually binding cause to effect, what in his own framework stops the "custom" from breaking constantly instead of once in a blue moon for a prophet? Does he actually earn that regularity somehow, or is he just quietly helping himself to it?

The other thing nagging me is how close this all sounds to Hume, who also says we never perceive necessary connection, only "constant conjunction," and that its custom/habit that makes us expect the effect once we've seen the cause enough times. So is Ghazali basically doing Hume's move hundreds of years early, just with God doing the habit-forming instead of our brains doing it? Or is "custom" actually pulling totally different weight in each account - like one being a claim about how God happens to behave and the other being a claim about how human minds happen to work - such that it's not really the same move dressed up in different clothes at all? Could you pull that comparison apart properly, and then use it to answer my actual question: does occasionalism, taken on its own terms, really leave room for something like careful empirical science, or does denying necessary connection quietly wreck that no matter how much "custom-talk" gets layered on top?
