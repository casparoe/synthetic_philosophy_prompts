Ok, random one, but it's been stuck in my head while I'm reporting on knowledge graphs. RDF's reification mechanism -- where, if you want to attach a further predicate to a whole subject-predicate-object triple (like 'who asserted this' or 'how confident are we'), you have to turn the triple into its own object and bolt three more relations onto it, and even then the reified version doesnt strictly assert the original triple is true -- always struck me as Bradley's regress, rebuilt in miniature, in software. If you caught F.H. Bradley up on how RDF and the alternatives people use now instead (RDF-star, named graphs, whatever) actually handle this, would he say they've walked straight into the trap his regress argument describes, or that they've found a real escape his own reasoning in Appearance and Reality would have ruled out? I want his actual argument doing the work here, not just a caricature of a baffled Victorian idealist confronting a database.
