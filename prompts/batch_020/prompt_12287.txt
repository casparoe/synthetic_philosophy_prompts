He thinks it's one of those lazy hot takes people repeat without having read past the Wikipedia summary, kind of like how everyone says Nietzsche just meant "atheism" by "God is dead." I think there's actually something real there if you get specific about it instead of just going "ooh, 19th century guy complains about gossip, spooky."

Where we got stuck is the actual mechanics. Kierkegaard's whole thing in Two Ages / the Present Age essay is that "the public" isn't a real entity like a crowd or a congregation, it's basically a phantom that only exists through the press, made up of people who relate to everything through idle chatter and comparison instead of direct commitment, plus that leveling process where everyone gets reduced to the same anonymous nobody, and reflection replaces actual passion and decisive action. Jonas's counter was that a Twitter/X pile-on or a subreddit mob is almost the opposite of Kierkegaard's "public," because it does organize and act (dogpiling someone, getting them fired, brigading a post), whereas Kierkegaard is pretty explicit that the public's whole non-identity comes from the fact that it never assembles or acts as a body, can't be reviewed like an army, has no representative you could ever put on trial. Honestly I didn't have a great comeback for that.

I don't want the "he basically predicted it" cop-out answer, I want to know exactly where it holds up and where it breaks down.
