I'm a novelist drafting a scene in which an economist aunt tries to talk her nephew out of standing in a long line to vote in a tight mayoral runoff, arguing from cold probability rather than apathy. I want her arithmetic to be genuinely sound rather than gestured at: for a hypothetical runoff with about 90,000 votes cast, work through an honest order-of-magnitude estimate of the probability that his single vote decides the outcome, and use it to state the paradox of voting properly — why ordinary means-end rationality seems to make voting a bad bet at that scale. Then tell me what her nephew, who has read a little political science, could fairly say in reply: of the standard replies to the paradox — expressive value, civic duty, a Kantian appeal to universalizability, minimax regret, and so on — which one actually supplies a rational justification for voting, rather than merely a psychological explanation of why people do it anyway.
