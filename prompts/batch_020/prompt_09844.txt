As a journalist who covers the intersection of psychology and philosophy, I'm preparing to interview a perceptual psychologist known for arguing that fear and desire can penetrate visual experience itself, not just the judgments we base on it. Her signature findings include spiders that look larger to people who fear them, hills that look steeper to the fatigued and to those carrying heavy loads, and desired objects, such as a bottle of water to someone thirsty, that look closer than they actually are. Draft eight interview questions that go beyond letting her restate these results. Include at least one question that raises the objection, associated with Firestone and Scholl, that such effects are really memory, judgment, or response bias rather than genuine perception; one that asks her to locate the effect relative to Pylyshyn's distinction between an encapsulated "early vision" and later, belief-laden processing; one that presses Susanna Siegel's worry that a cognitively penetrated experience can become question-begging evidence for the very belief that shaped it; and one that asks how her work bears on the older philosophy-of-science debate over the theory-ladenness of observation. For each question, add a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
