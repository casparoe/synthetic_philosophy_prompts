Had a long argument with my friend last night about vagueness and I don't think either of us won. She's an epistemicist -- thinks there's an exact number of grains of sand that separates a heap from a non-heap, we just can't ever know which one it is. I said that's crazy and told her I lean supervaluationist instead, since that view doesn't commit you to some spooky sharp cutoff. Except then she pointed out that supervaluationism still makes it true that 'there's some number of grains such that removing one turns a heap into a non-heap' -- so isn't my view saying there's a sharp cutoff too, it just won't tell us where it is? I didn't have a good comeback for that. Is there an actual difference between 'true, but unknowable which one' and whatever supervaluationism is doing, or did I basically loose the argument?
