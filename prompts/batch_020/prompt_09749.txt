A colleague of mine who works in experimental psychology and I have been arguing for weeks about whether a well-known finding in her field will hold up when a large-scale replication is published next month. We've both read the same pre-publication materials and the same commentary, so it isn't a matter of one of us knowing something the other doesn't. She's confident enough that the finding won't replicate that she's offered to bet me $1,000, at even odds, against my view that it will. My instinct is that if I really believe I'm right, I should be glad to take her money. But as a philosophy professor, I'm supposed to be good at noticing when an instinct like that is missing something, and I can't shake the feeling that her eagerness to bet is itself telling me something — in a way that goes beyond ordinary nerves about losing. Should the fact that she wants to bet change how confident I am in my own view, and if so, does that mean two people who trust each other's judgment should almost never be willing to bet against one another in the first place?
