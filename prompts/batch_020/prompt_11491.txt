In the "grudge informer" case discussed in Hart and Fuller's 1958 exchange, a German woman denounced her husband to the Nazi authorities for disparaging remarks about Hitler that he had made while home on leave; invoking a wartime statute that made such remarks a criminal offense, the authorities had him imprisoned. After the war, a German court convicted the wife of unlawfully depriving her husband of his liberty, holding that a law so iniquitous could not be recognized as valid law at all. Write a dialogue between a legal positivist following H. L. A. Hart and a natural lawyer following Lon Fuller, debating how the case should have been decided. The positivist should hold that the Nazi statute was valid law however odious, so that convicting the wife legitimately requires an openly retroactive criminal statute rather than the pretense that the enactment was never law; the natural lawyer should hold that the enactment failed even to qualify as law, since it violated what Fuller calls the inner morality of law. Each should press the other on whether procedural virtues such as generality, publicity, and prospectivity are genuinely moral achievements or merely conditions for efficacious rule-following, and each should engage with the other's actual objections rather than simply restating their own position.
