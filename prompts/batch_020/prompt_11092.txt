About fourteen months ago, my former business partner and I dissolved a small catering company we had built together over several enjoyable years, and we have been contesting the terms of the wind-down in court ever since. The dispute is nominally about who keeps a delivery van, a walk-in cooler, and an established client list, together worth perhaps twenty thousand dollars, but between the two of us we have already paid our lawyers a combined sum closer to sixty thousand, with no settlement in sight. Both of our attorneys have hinted, as diplomatically as they can, that at this point the fight has stopped being about the van or the cooler.

I recounted all of this to a friend last week, expecting some sympathy, and instead received a lecture: she told me I was falling for a classic sunk-cost mistake and should simply write off the legal fees and walk away. I agreed that ignoring sunk costs is usually sound advice, but applying it here felt too quick, and we ended up arguing about it for most of the evening. My thought was that if I stop now, I do not just lose the money I have already spent; I also hand my former partner the van, the cooler, and the client list outright, with nothing at all to show for what I have paid, whereas if I keep contesting I retain some chance of recovering part of it, and I suspect he is weighing his own choice to continue in exactly the same terms. That feels different from an isolated error in judgment, since each of us seems to be responding in a way that is locally sensible given what the other is doing, even though we can both see that the total being spent has long since dwarfed anything either of us could plausibly win.

I would like help thinking through whether that intuition actually holds up. Is there a meaningful difference between an ordinary sunk-cost mistake and a standoff like this one, where two parties are each escalating in direct response to the other's escalation and where dropping out first guarantees a total loss while the other side gets everything by default? And if that difference is real, what does it imply about whether it can ever be rational, from the inside of a situation like this, to keep going, and what would genuinely sound advice about when to stop look like, as opposed to advice that just tells me to stop caring about money I cannot get back?
