I'm a PhD student working on the philosophical foundations of criminal punishment, and this term I've been auditing a graduate seminar on free will, where we recently spent two sessions on Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against ultimate moral responsibility. As I've reconstructed it, the argument runs roughly as follows: you act as you do because of how you are, mentally speaking; to be truly responsible for your action, you would have to be truly responsible for how you are, at least in the relevant mental respects; but you could only be responsible for how you are if you had earlier chosen, on the basis of some prior character or set of values, to be that way; and since that prior character would itself need to have been chosen on the basis of a still-earlier character, and so on, the chain of responsibility can never terminate in the kind of self-causing self (a causa sui, in Strawson's own phrase) that full, ultimate responsibility seems to require.

In discussion, a classmate of mine, who works mainly on Kane-style event-causal libertarianism, argued that this regress is really just a repackaged version of hard determinism. On his reading, the argument tacitly assumes that each stage of your character is the causally necessitated product of the stage before it, so that the only way to stop the regress would be to locate some point at which your character-forming choices were not fully fixed by your prior character -- which is exactly what indeterminism, and in particular something like Kane's self-forming actions, is supposed to supply. I found this quite persuasive: if a person's values at some early, formative stage were settled by a genuinely undetermined event rather than by an earlier self, it is hard to see what is left for the regress to feed on. Our instructor dismissed this quickly, insisting that Strawson explicitly intends the Basic Argument to hold whether determinism or indeterminism is true, but she did not have time in class to explain why. I cannot reconcile that "independence" claim with the actual structure of the regress as I have laid it out above, so I would like help understanding whether there is a real argument behind it -- some specific reason indeterminism at the character-forming stage fails to do the work my classmate and I think it does -- or whether this is a case where a slogan about the Basic Argument has outrun what Strawson actually showed.
