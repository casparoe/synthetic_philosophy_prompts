I'm on the small committee at my company that runs our internal prediction market -- fake points redeemable for a modest bonus pool, used to forecast stuff like ship dates. I've spent way too many hours reading Robin Hanson's blog over the years, so I was thrilled when we set this up, but now we've hit a real dispute and I need to write something up before next week's meeting. Right now anyone can trade on anything, including the two engineers who obviously know more than the rest of us about whether the migration actually ships this quarter. Half the committee wants to ban people from betting on their own project, since it's basically insider trading and it's demoralizing for everyone else who just looses their points to the specialists every time. The other half says that's backwards -- the whole point of a market is to pull informed people's beliefs into the price, and banning them just gets you a thin, useless market. I keep getting stuck on the no-trade theorem, the idea that rational people with common priors shouldn't even want to bet with each other on private info in the first place, since my willingness to take your bet is itself evidence I should update on -- so maybe we literally can't have a market that's both fair and informative unless we subsidize a market maker to eat the losses, a la Hanson. Can you draft the committee's decision memo? I want an actual ruling on the trading rules, not just a list of pros and cons, and it should take seriously whichever side it ends up ruling against.
