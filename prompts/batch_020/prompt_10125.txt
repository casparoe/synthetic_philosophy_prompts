So this semester my school is piloting a new thing called Peer Panels -- basically instead of getting written up for stuff like cheating on a quiz or dress code and phone violations, you go in front of a small group of fellow students who talk it over with you and decide the consequence (community service, written reflection, whatever) instead of it going straight to the assistant principal. I'm the only sophomore on the student government committee that has to actually write the rules before the pilot launches in three weeks, and we're deadlocked on panel size and how panelists get picked. It somehow turned into an argument about the Condorcet Jury Theorem, which one of the policy debate kids brought up, and I tried reading about it myself last night and now I'm even more confused than before.

Here's the actual disagreement: the debate kid says its basically mathematically proven that bigger panels chosen totally at random beat small ones, so we should just pull like 15 names out of a hat every time. The kids who did mock trial want the opposite -- small panels of 3 or 4 people who've actually been trained for this, since they think random sophomores won't be any good at figuring out what actually happened or what's fair. There's also beef over whether panelists should each write down their decision privately before talking, or just hash it out loud as a group, which the teacher running the pilot said "changes whether the theorem even applies" but wouldn't explain further. And I don't think anyone's noticed that "what happened" and "what's a fair consequence" aren't the same kind of question -- one has a real answer and the other kind of doesnt -- which feels like it should matter for which parts of this the theorem can even speak to.

Can you write the memo I'm bringing to our meeting Thursday? I need an actual recommendation -- panel size, how people get selected, and whether/when they deliberate together versus vote independently -- not just a list of pros and cons for each side. If you think we should build in some kind of check after the pilot's first few panels to see whether it's actually working, say when that should happen and what we'd be looking for. And please take the "big random panel" argument seriously on its own terms instead of just shooting it down, since half the committee will get defensive if it reads like we're just dismissing it.
