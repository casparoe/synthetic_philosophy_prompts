My friend Mara is getting married in a few weeks. She wrote her dissertation on perdurantism -- the four-dimensionalist view that a person isn't wholly present at every moment (like endurantists think) but is really a series of temporal parts stitched into one long 'space-time worm.' She also goes back and forth between worm theory and stage theory, something about whether future Maras are parts of that same worm or just separate stages linked by a counterpart relation, which I don't totally get. Half the wedding is her old philosophy cohort, who'll catch every reference; the other half is family who've never heard the phrase 'temporal part' in their life. I'm giving the toast and I want to actually use the idea, not just drop it in as a punchline -- like, if shes right, what does it even mean to promise to love the same person forever, when its really only the part of her standing at the altar that can make any promise at all? Can you write me a warm, kind of funny, five minute toast that takes that seriously without turning into a metaphysics lecture, and that still lands for people who have zero idea what any of this means?
