Working on a piece about old philosophical arguments colliding with modern science, and this one's been stuck in my head. In the Discourse on the Method, Descartes argues the real test for a rational soul isn't cleverness but language — he specifically singles out parrots and magpies as creatures that can spit out words but never truly converse, wheras even the dumbest human can string words together to answer whatever's said to them. Which got me thinking about Alex, the African grey parrot Irene Pepperberg spent three decades studying, who could label objects by color, shape and material, handle brand-new combinations of those questions, used "none" for zero, and supposedly once asked what color he was after seeing himself in a mirror. Given that parrots were literally Descartes' chosen example of "talks but doesn't really talk," what do you reckon he'd say if he saw Alex's actual transcripts — would he have to grant the bird some kind of mind, or is there a principled Cartesian move that explains it all away as organs plus instinct? Walk me through the reasoning, not just the verdict.
