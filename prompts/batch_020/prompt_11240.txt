I'm working on a feature about philosophical "family businesses" for a science-and-ideas outlet, and one case keeps nagging at me: Peter Strawson and his son Galen Strawson ended up on opposite sides of the free will debate, despite sharing a surname, a discipline, and presumably more than a few dinner-table arguments over the years. Peter Strawson's 1962 essay "Freedom and Resentment" tried to defuse the whole determinism-versus-responsibility standoff by relocating the basis of moral responsibility in our reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, and the like — treating the metaphysical question of whether determinism is true as almost beside the point for creatures constitutionally built to react to one another as agents rather than as mere objects. Decades later, his son's "Basic Argument" seems to reopen precisely the wound that essay was designed to close, contending that no one can be truly or ultimately morally responsible for anything, because that would require being causa sui — the cause of oneself — with respect to the mental character from which one's actions spring, and nothing can be causa sui in the relevant sense.

For the piece, I'd like the philosophical mechanics right, not just a human-interest gloss on a family disagreement. Could you reconstruct the Basic Argument premise by premise, and then tell me whether it genuinely engages Peter Strawson's naturalistic, reactive-attitudes picture, or whether the two arguments really operate at different levels of description and end up talking past one another? I'm especially curious whether someone could consistently hold both views at once — accepting the reactive-attitudes account as an accurate description of our social practice of holding one another responsible, while still granting the Basic Argument's claim that no such practice could ever amount to the kind of "true," desert-based responsibility that retributive punishment seems to require. And if you had to judge which Strawson comes closer to actually dissolving the problem, rather than just redescribing it at a different level, which would you pick, and why?
