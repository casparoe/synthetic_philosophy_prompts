so in my epistemology seminar we just spent two lectures on Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument and honestly its been living in my head ever since. the basic idea (correct me if im butchering it) is that a condition counts as "luminous" if whenever you're in that state, you're automatically in a position to know you're in it -- and Williamson tries to show basically nothing clears that bar, not even something that feels as transparent as "I feel cold right now." the argument leans on this margin-for-error move: your introspective abilities aren't infinitely fine grained, so if you count as knowing you feel cold at one instant, you'd have to also count as knowing it a split second later (since to you that instant is indistinguishable from the first one), and if you chain that reasoning all the way across a long, gradual slide from clearly-cold to clearly-not-cold, you end up "knowing" you feel cold even once you've obviously warmed up -- which is absurd, so something has to give, meaning you can't always know you're in the state you're actually in. our prof mentioned that the exact same trick is what Williamson uses to take down the KK principle (Kp -> KKp, knowing entails knowing that you know), since "knowing" itself apparently isn't luminous either.
