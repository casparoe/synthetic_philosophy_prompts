After a career in evolutionary biology, I have spent much of my retirement reading philosophy of religion and philosophy of science, and keep returning to the fine-tuning argument for the existence of a cosmic designer. What has caught my attention lately is Lee Smolin's hypothesis of cosmological natural selection, which tries to explain the very same appearance of fine-tuning in purely naturalistic terms: universes reproduce through the formation of black holes, their physical constants mutate slightly with each generation, and those constants that happen to maximize black hole production come to dominate the population of universes over many generations. As a biologist, I am curious whether this borrowing of Darwinian logic does genuine explanatory work or merely lends the hypothesis an unearned air of scientific respectability. Could you compare the fine-tuning argument directly with Smolin's proposal, addressing two things in particular: whether cosmological natural selection actually dissolves the fine-tuning problem or merely relocates it to the meta-laws governing how universes reproduce and vary, and how the hypothesis fares against the more familiar multiverse response on the specific question of falsifiability?
