After forty years in experimental particle physics, I have spent my retirement finally reading the moral and political philosophy I never had time for. One preoccupation from that reading, prediction markets as a mechanism of governance, has collided with a live dispute on the beamtime-allocation committee for a shared detector facility jointly funded by several universities, on which I still serve. Inspired by futarchy -- vote on values, bet on beliefs -- one faction wants to replace our peer-review scoring with a two-step scheme: agree on a single metric to maximize (say, significant results per beamtime-hour over five years), then let a real-money market of qualified physicists allocate time to whichever proposal traders judge most likely to move that metric, since traders risk their own money where reviewers risk nothing. Opponents counter that this would starve slow, unglamorous work -- calibration runs, null-result searches, replication attempts -- that rarely excites traders yet keeps the field honest, and would hand influence to whoever has spare cash and time to trade. Please draft the committee's decision memo on whether, and in what modified form, to pilot this next year, including a concrete design if you recommend adopting it, and a serious response to the strongest objection on the side you reject.
