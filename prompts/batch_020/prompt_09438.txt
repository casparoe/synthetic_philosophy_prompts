I'm preparing a graduate seminar on Kuhn and the historiography of science, and I want to get the dialectic around incommensurability exactly right. The Structure of Scientific Revolutions is usually read as holding that successive paradigms are incommensurable, yet historians of science plainly do compare paradigms, explain why scientists switched allegiance, and reconstruct superseded theories in careful detail — activities a strong incommensurability thesis seems to forbid. In response to precisely this worry, Kuhn's later work narrowed the claim to a 'local' incommensurability confined to a small taxonomy of interdefined kind terms (his 'lexicon'), within which no two terms at the same level may overlap in their referents; a paradigm change restructures this taxonomy (as when the sun moved from the kind 'planet' to the kind 'star' after Copernicus) in a way that blocks term-for-term translation, even though the surrounding language stays fixed and an interpreter can still become 'bilingual' across both taxonomies. Please explain how this maneuver is meant to secure a genuine, principled untranslatability without undoing the very comparability that motivated the retreat from Kuhn's earlier, stronger formulations, and assess whether it actually meets Davidson-style objections that thoroughgoing conceptual incommensurability is unintelligible, or simply redraws the boundary at which that objection resurfaces.
