So this whole thing started at my cousin's place a few weeks ago, after my uncle's funeral, when a bunch of us were sitting around not really knowing what to say to each other, and my cousin Mira (shes been way into physics youtube lately, Sean Carroll type stuff) said something like "he's not really gone, he's just not *now* anymore, the same way Australia doesn't stop existing just because you're not looking at it." Her husband Sam told her that was a weird thing to say right then, and it turned into a proper argument that went on for like half an hour. I didn't say a word the whole time but I've been chewing on it ever since, so I wrote down roughly how it went, because I honestly can't tell who came out ahead.

1. Mira's opening point was that "now" is just like "here" -- the present isn't specially real while the past and future aren't, it's just that "now" means whenever this particular conversation is happening, same way "here" means wherever you happen to be standing. Every time is equally real, we just each experience our own little slice of it, like being at one spot on a map.
2. Sam said that's a cop-out because it makes actual change impossible -- if past, present and future are all just sitting there equally real like one giant frozen sculpture, then nothing is really happening, it's all just laid out, which flat out contradicts the fact that something is obviously going on right now that isn't going on in, say, 1990.
3. Mira came back with the relativity thing: two people moving at different speeds relative to each other will genuinely disagree about which faraway events count as happening "at the same time" as right now, so there's no single universal "now" for physics to back up, which is a real problem for treating the present as objectively special rather than just where you happen to be standing.
4. Sam's comeback had two parts. First he said maybe there really is one true "now," we just can't measure it because of how light and clocks work -- Mira called that an unfalsifiable rescue, basically inventing a hidden fact the moment the physics gets inconvenient. But then he turned it around on her: if every time is equally real, she owes an explanation for why the past feels completely fixed and done while the future feels wide open, why we can remember last Tuesday but not next Tuesday, and "it's just a psychological illusion" struck him as exactly as convenient a dodge as his hidden clock.
5. Mira's last move was that presentism has an even worse version of this same problem: if only the present exists, what makes it true right now that dinosaurs ever existed, or that my uncle was ever alive -- there's nothing left for that to even be about. Sam said maybe the present just comes with built-in properties like "having-been-such-that-dinosaurs-existed," no different from how a thing can have the property "five feet from the fridge" without the fridge needing to be made of some special universe-cheese to explain it. Mira said that sounded exactly as made-up and convenient as his hidden reference frame, and Sam never really had a comeback for that one.

Neither of them budged and I've gone back and forth in my head all week without landing anywhere.
