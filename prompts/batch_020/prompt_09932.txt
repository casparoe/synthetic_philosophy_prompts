I'm taking an upper-level metaphysics seminar this term on persistence and identity, and we've just finished a unit setting endurantism against perdurantism as rival answers to how an object survives change over time. On the endurantist picture, a persisting thing is wholly present, numerically one and the same, at every moment at which it exists; on the perdurantist picture, what exists at any given moment is only a temporal part, or stage, of a larger four-dimensional object that is spread across time the way an ordinary object is spread across space. Much of our discussion centered on Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics — the puzzle of how a single enduring thing could be bent at one time and perfectly straight at another — which perdurantists standardly present as their best argument for the view, and on the related oddity that, strictly speaking, it was never quite true that I was a child, only that an earlier temporal part of the four-dimensional whole that includes me was.
