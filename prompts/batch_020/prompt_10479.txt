So I'm about two-thirds of the way through a novel where a big chunk of the plot hinges on a memory-wiping sleep experiment that's basically the Sleeping Beauty problem made flesh — my protagonist gets put under, a coin gets flipped by the lab, and depending on how it lands she either wakes up once or twice (with her memory of the first waking wiped before the second), and she never knows which case she's in the moment her eyes open. There's a scene I keep chickening out of writing where two of the researchers — one a died-in-the-wool "halfer," the other a "thirder" — are watching her through the observation glass and start arguing about what her credence that the coin landed heads *should* be right when she wakes up. I want to get the argument actually right before I put words in there mouths, so could you write that dialogue for me?

The thing I really don't want is the version where one of them just says "well obviously it's still 1/2, nothing informative happened" and the other says "obviously it's 1/3, there's just more waking-up-moments where it's tails" and they leave it at that — that's the pop-philosophy-podcast version and I've read it a hundred times. I want the thirder to actually run something like Elga's move (the bit where you get her to agree that, conditional on it being Monday, heads and tails have to be equally likely, and then show what that commits her to once you fold in the fact that a Monday-tails-waking and a Tuesday-tails-waking have to be equally likely too), and I want the halfer to have a real Lewis-style comeback rather than just stamping their foot on the prior — I know his reply basically has to accept the weird-sounding consequence that learning "it's Monday" would bump your credence in heads up to 2/3, so I want to see the halfer bite that bullet convincingly instead of dodging it. Bonus points if one of them brings up what happens once you crank the tails-branch up to, say, a hundred awakenings instead of two, since that's the version that always seems to make the halfer position feel most unbearable. Keep it maybe a page, sharp and a little combative — these two clearly enjoy needling each other and have had this fight before — but don't strawman either of them; I need both sides to sound like the smartest possible version of themselves, since one of them is going to turn out to be right by the end of my book and I honestly haven't decided which yet.
