My mother has just been diagnosed with a rare, fast-moving brain disease that will kill her within months. Her medical team has offered her a place in an experimental program: a machine would record the exact state of every cell in her brain, then use that record to build, in a new and healthy body, an exact copy of her that retains all her memories and personality, while her own body and brain continue to fail over the following days, just as they would without any treatment. My brother believes that agreeing to this is really just accepting her death while comforting ourselves with a stranger who happens to believe she is our mother, and that calling it treatment does not change this. My sister believes he is being sentimental, since we already accept that people survive anesthesia and organ replacement as the very same person, and this is no different in kind. What unsettles me most is a line in the consent form: for two or three days, the healthy copy and the dying original would both be alive at once, each equally convinced of being her. Before I say anything to my mother, I need to know whether my brother is onto something real, whether my sister's confidence is actually warranted, or whether they're both missing the point.
