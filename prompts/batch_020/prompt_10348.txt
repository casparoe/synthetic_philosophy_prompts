I'm the service chair of Interact, my high school's Rotary-affiliated community service club, and I have to write the decision memo our executive board votes on this Tuesday. Two months ago we ran 'Miles for Maya,' a fundraiser for a freshman with leukemia after her family's insurance denied the recommended treatment; local businesses pledged to match every donation made 'for Maya,' and we ended up raising $18,400, well beyond our original $6,000 goal. Before we hand the family a check, one member circulated Peter Singer's drowning-child argument alongside GiveWell's cost-effectiveness numbers, arguing that neither distance nor a familiar face makes one life worth more than another, so we're obligated to redirect at least the windfall above our goal to a charity like the Against Malaria Foundation, where the same money would save several times as many lives. Other members think redirecting money that businesses matched specifically for Maya would be a betrayal, whatever the math says. Please draft the memo I'll bring to the board: it should reach a clear, concrete recommendation with a timeline for talking to the family and our donors, and it should take whichever side it doesn't land on seriously rather than dismiss it as naive number-crunching or mere sentimentality.
