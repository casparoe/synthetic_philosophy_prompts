So I've been down a rabbit hole this week rereading Pearl's Book of Why and some old LessWrong threads on causal decision theory, and it made me realize something that I don't think gets discussed enough outside of the philosophy-of-causation literature: the two leading theories of causation -- Lewis's counterfactual account and the Woodward/Pearl interventionist picture -- have each been accused of smuggling in the very causal facts they're supposed to explain, just from opposite directions. Lewis needs his similarity ordering between possible worlds rigged just right (big miracles are worse than small ones, matching particular fact matters more than avoiding small miracles, no backtracking allowed) so that counterfactual dependence lines up with the direction of causation, and the standard complaint is that this looks less like deriving the causal asymmetry from something more basic and more like just building it in by hand. Meanwhile Woodward's interventions are themselves defined using causal vocabulary (an intervention variable has to be a direct cause of X, has to "break" all the other arrows into X, and can't affect Y except through X), so on his own account interventionism isn't really a reductive analysis of causation in non-causal terms either -- its more like relating one bunch of causal facts to another bunch, which is basically what Cartwright's old "no causes in, no causes out" jab was aimed at.

Anyway, I want to see this as an actual argument instead of me just listing complaints. Could you write me a dialogue between a Lewisian counterfactual theorist and a Woodward-style interventionist where each one presses the circularity charge against the other's theory and then has to defend their own view against basically the same charge thrown right back at them? I don't want them to just stay at "ur theory is circular, no ur theory is circular" -- the counterfactualist should say something real about why the similarity ordering isn't just reverse-engineered to spit out the right verdicts, and the interventionist should have an actual answer for why a non-reductive theory is still worth having (e.g. what it buys you for telling causation apart from mere correlation that a regularity theory can't touch). Bonus points if it comes down to a concrete case at some point, like Suzy-and-Billy-style late preemption, with each of them claiming their framework handles it more honestly than the other's. Neither one should be a pushover or get the last word for free -- I want to genuinely not be able to tell who's "winning" by the end.
