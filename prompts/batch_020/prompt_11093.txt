I've been chewing on Goodhart's Law lately, that famous line 'when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure,' which I saw people say was proved by the economist Charles Goodhart back in the 70s. Once it clicked for me it kinda blew my mind, because if an actual economist mathematically proved that literally any measure breaks down the second you turn it into a target, that's basically a law of nature for organizations, not just some rule of thumb. So why do governments and companies keep merrily setting KPIs, inflation targets, standardized test scores and whatnot like the law doesn't even apply to them? Are they all just choosing to ignore established economics, or is their some clever trick to dodge Goodhart's Law that I'm missing?
