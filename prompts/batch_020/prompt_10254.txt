Had a long, kind of pointless argument with my friend last night about vagueness that never got resolved. I was defending epistemicism (the Williamson-ish view that "heap" or "tall" really do have a sharp cutoff, we just can't know where it is), and she was defending supervaluationism, insisting her view doesn't need sharp boundaries because truth is just truth-on-every-admissible-precisification. My comeback was that this doesn't actually buy her anything: the sharpness just moves up a level, to which precisifications count as admissible, or to the "definitely" operator and higher-order vagueness, so there's still a hard edge somewhere, she's just relocated it and stopped looking. She wasn't having it, and we also got stuck on whether "supertrue" is even the same thing as ordinary true, given that on her view a sentence about some genuinely borderline guy who's five-eleven, say, isn't true or false at all.

Can you write a dialogue between an epistemicist and a supervaluationist hashing out exactly that -- whether supervaluationism actually avoids commitment to sharp boundaries or just relocates them, and what that costs for ordinary truth-talk about borderline cases. Keep them evenly matched, actually responding to each other's points rather then talking past one another, and it doesnt need a tidy ending -- ours sure didn't have one.
