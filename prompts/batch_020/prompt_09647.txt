So I've been down a rabbit hole about why psychiatric diagnoses (adhd, autism, you name it) seem to shift shape over the decades, and I ran into this idea from a philosopher, Ian Hacking I think, called "looping effects." As I understand it: when doctors invent a category to sort a certain kind of person, the people sorted into it become aware of the label and start acting and thinking in ways shaped by it, which changes what the category looks like in practice, so the diagnosis quietly gets revised, which changes the people again, and round it goes. He makes a big deal about how this is totally different from classifying rocks or stars, since a rock doesn't start behaving differently once you slap a label on it.

This freaks me out more then most sci-fi premises once you actually sit with it, and I'd rather see it dramatized than have it explained to me again. Could you write me a short story (a page or two) built around a looping effect like this, a person and a diagnosis basically warping each other over time? Just please don't reach for multiple personality disorder/the Sybil case, that's the example literally every write up on this uses. Bonus points if somewhere in there is a doctor or researcher who slowly realizes they're not just observing the category, they're half creating it.
