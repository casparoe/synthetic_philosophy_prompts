I'm working on a piece about modern attempts to resolve the Liar paradox, and I keep running into what logicians call the "revenge problem": the complaint that nearly every proposed fix ends up generating a new paradoxical sentence out of the very machinery used to solve the original one. Rather than have this explained to me in the abstract, I'd like to see it play out directly. Please write a dialogue between two logicians: one defends a Kripke-style fixed-point theory of truth, on which the Liar sentence comes out neither true nor false; the other defends a dialetheist, paraconsistent theory on which it comes out both true and false. Have the dialetheist press the Kripkean on how the fixed-point account handles the strengthened liar sentence "This sentence is not true"; let the Kripkean offer the strongest available reply; then have the Kripkean turn the question around and ask whether the dialetheist's own framework can consistently handle a sentence like "This sentence is false but not true." Neither logician should concede more than their position actually allows, and each should respond to the specific point the other has just made rather than simply restating their own view.
