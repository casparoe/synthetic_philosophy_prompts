I've been falling down a rabbit hole reading old MIRI and LessWrong stuff on self-modifying AI, and I keep running into this thing called the Lobian obstacle, which shows up whenever an agent tries to build or sign off on a successor version of itself. I think I've got the bare logic down: Lob's theorem says that if a formal system S can prove "if S proves P, then P is true" for some statement P, then S can already prove P outright, so no consistent system as strong as basic arithmetic can adopt a blanket rule like "whatever I prove is true" without that system already deciding everything (apparently this is basically a souped-up version of Godels second incompleteness theorem, which is just the special case where P is a contradiction). What I'm fuzzy on is why this cashes out as such a deep, structural problem for self-trust and self-improvement specifically, rather than just being a neat fact about arithmetic. A friend of mine whose into formal epistemology insists it's the same basic shape as the old regress problems about justifying your own faculties, like the Cartesian circle, and that the standard workarounds in the tiling agents literature (each successor being strictly weaker than its parent, infinite descending towers of theories, parametric polymorphism, Christiano-style probabilistic reflection instead of flat-out proof) are all just different ways of ducking the issue rather than actually resolving it.

So: what do you think are the most important implications of the Lobian obstacle for any agent, AI or otherwise, that needs to trust its own future reasoning or construct and vouch for a successor that reasons roughly the way it does? I'm especially interested in whether this is a genuinely deep limit on self-trust in general, one that would show up in some looser form even for a human trying to fully certify their own future judgment, or whether it's mostly an artifact of insisting on formal proof as the standard of trust and mostly dissolves once you allow anything probabilistic. And if you've got a view on which of the standard workarounds actually gets closest to dissolving the obstacle, rather than just relabeling it, I'd love to hear that too.
