I teach a graduate seminar on metaethics, and this term's unit on value theory covers fitting-attitude analyses of value (including the influential buck-passing version), according to which something is good just in case it merits a pro-attitude such as desire or admiration. These views face the wrong kind of reasons problem: a demon who threatens to make you suffer unless you admire it thereby gives you a reason to admire the demon, but plainly not the kind of reason that would make the demon actually admirable. For an icebreaker in this week's class, write me a joke that turns on the gap between reasons that make a pro-attitude fitting and reasons that merely make it prudent to have. It should be funny to someone who genuinely grasps the distinction, not land as a generic joke about pedantic philosophers.
