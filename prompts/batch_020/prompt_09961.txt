As a novelist, I recently agreed to write a cover blurb for a close friend's forthcoming novel, which her publisher is marketing as "a true story" drawn from her late grandmother's diaries. I have never read the diaries myself and have no independent means of checking them; I know the story only through my friend's account of what her grandmother wrote. I trust my friend's honesty without reservation, and on the strength of that trust, I do believe the events really happened. She has asked that my blurb say so outright, in my own words, rather than simply praising the writing, and the other novelists who have already blurbed the book seem to have agreed without a second thought. I feel no hesitation calling the prose "luminous" or the pacing "masterful," since those are just my own impressions, but putting my name to the flat claim that the events actually occurred feels like a different kind of commitment, though I cannot quite say why. Am I drawing a real distinction here, or is this just squeamishness on my part, given that I do sincerely believe her?
