So this week in my metaphysics seminar (we're doing a unit on powers/dispositions and how they relate to laws of nature) we went through basically the entire history of people trying to patch up the conditional analysis of disposition ascriptions, and by the end of two hours I honestly couldn't tell if anyone had made progress or if we'd just watched an elaborate game of whack-a-mole. You start with the naive "x is fragile iff x would break if struck," then C.B. Martin's electro-fink kills it (the wire hooked up to a machine that flips the wire's conductivity the instant a conductor touches it, so the wire counts as live even though it would never actually conduct), then Lewis patches things up in '97 by requiring that the same intrinsic causal basis persist through the stimulus, and then you get hit with the mask/antidote cases — the arsenic-plus-antidote case, or a fragile glass so well bubble-wrapped that it would never break even though its just as fragile as ever — which seem to survive Lewis's fix, since the causal basis is completely untouched and its only something extrinsic getting in the way of the manifestation. A few of us ended up arguing about this after class over coffee, and my professor had already hinted this could go on forever, new analysis, new gerrymandered counterexample, repeat, so now I can't decide if that means dispositions just can't be given a conditional analysis at all or if its a solvable problem and we just haven't hit on the right formulation yet (something more like the proportion-of-cases analyses people like Manley and Wasserman have proposed).
