So I've been on a bit of a rabbit hole lately re-reading old MIRI/LessWrong stuff on "program equilibrium" -- the Tennenholtz idea that if players submit programs that get to read each other's source code before acting, you can get cooperation as an actual equilibrium in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, no repeated play or outside enforcer needed. What really sold me was the Löbian version from the "Robust Cooperation" paper: FairBot cooperating with FairBot via a proof-search argument that bottoms out in Löb's theorem, and then PrudentBot patching the obvious flaw that FairBot is basically a sucker for CooperateBot. It felt like actual progress on the Hobbesian trap rather than just a restatement of it.

Then I brought it up with a friend of mine who does more mainstream game theory and she was pretty unimpressed -- her line was roughly "you haven't dissolved the trust problem, you've relocated it to trusting the source-code channel," plus a jab that the plain "cooperate iff identical source" version is trivially broken by renaming a variable, and a worry that mutual transparency is just as good at enabling exotic threats and commitment races as it is at enabling cooperation. I didn't have a great comeback and its been bugging me since.

Feel free to set it however works -- I was picturing the two of them finishing their entries for a friend's informal open-source-PD tournament with the deadline an hour off, each trying to talk the other out of what they're about to submit, but use a better frame if you've got one.
