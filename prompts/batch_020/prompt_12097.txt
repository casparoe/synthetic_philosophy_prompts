Consider the following responses to the regress that arises when we ask what unifies a subject and a predicate, or a particular and a universal, into a single proposition or fact rather than leaving them an unconnected list:

- Bradley's own response: deny that relations are ultimately real and retreat to a monism on which ordinary subject-predicate judgments are at best partial, adjectival appearances of one undivided whole.
- Russell's early distinction between a relation occurring "as relating" its terms within a proposition and that same relation occurring "as a term" in its own right.
- Frege's appeal to the unsaturatedness of concepts, which require completion by an object to yield a unified thought.
- The Tractarian, primitivist move of declining to explain unity any further: facts are logically basic, and their constituents simply hang together, with no further relating relation doing the work.
- The non-relational tie, or nexus of exemplification, often associated with Gustav Bergmann, on which a particular's having a property is a sui generis form of combination rather than a further relatable relation.
- Armstrong's states-of-affairs theory, which grants that instantiation generates a further regress of its own but denies that this regress is vicious.
