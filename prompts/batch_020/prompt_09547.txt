I'm a backend engineer, and last month I was on call during a major production outage. Our postmortem has turned into a drawn-out argument about who's actually responsible. I didn't restart a degraded service for almost forty minutes because I was absorbed in an unrelated ticket and missed the page, and I'm now getting most of the blame. But you could just as easily point to whoever built that service years ago without a retry mechanism, to the infrastructure team that never set up alerting to escalate to a backup when the primary doesn't respond, or to management, which has kept our on-call rotation understaffed despite repeated requests to expand it. Everyone agrees my inaction made things worse, but it bothers me that we're pinning so much on what I failed to do, when several earlier decisions, had they gone differently, would also have prevented the whole incident. Is there a principled way to say my omission counts as more of a cause than those other ones, or am I just the easiest person to hold accountable?
