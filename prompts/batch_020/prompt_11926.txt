I read that money-pump arguments show a rational agent must act like an expected-utility maximizer, since otherwise a sufficiently clever trading partner could walk the agent in a circle back to its starting point while draining its money at every step. As I understand it, this means the agent has to be able to rank any two options against each other -- so treating two options as truly incomparable, rather than just hard to compare (like choosing between two architectures, one faster but harder to maintain and the other slower but simpler), can never be a legitimate rational stance, only a bias to be corrected. What's a sound way to start ranking cases like that instead of shrugging and calling them incomparable?
