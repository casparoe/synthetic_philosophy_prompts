I'm interviewing a philosopher for a feature on autonomous weapons. She defends the 'responsibility gap' thesis: because an autonomous weapon selects and engages its own targets, no one -- not the programmer, the manufacturer, the commanding officer, or the weapon itself -- can be justly held responsible when it kills wrongfully, and she takes that as grounds for banning such weapons outright. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the thesis: I want one question each on why ordinary doctrines like negligence can't simply be stretched to fit these cases, on whether pinning the blame on a human operator who lacked real control is scapegoating rather than genuine accountability, and on whether 'meaningful human control' requirements close the gap or merely relocate it -- and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
