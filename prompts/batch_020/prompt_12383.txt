I got stuck on the bit numbered 341 to 343, where he compares certain propositions to the hinges of a door. He says our doubts "are as it were like hinges on which those turn," and a little later says that if you want the door to turn at all, the hinges have to "stay put." Fine, I get the general vibe -- some stuff (like the claim that the earth existed for ages before I was born) is too basic to actually question, its just assumed in the background of everything else we think or doubt about.

Heres where im actually confused though: in a totally different section (204) he says its not knowledge thats at the very bottom of everything, its "our acting, which lies at the bottom of the language-game." So now idk if hinges are supposed to be things we still kind of believe, just at some crazy high level of certainty, or if theyre not beliefs at all, more like unspoken rules that show up in what we do rather than things we hold to be true.
