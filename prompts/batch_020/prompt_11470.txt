At work a few of us built a little internal play-money prediction market, kinda like Manifold, where people bet on stuff like sprint deadlines or whether a migration will go smoothly. Someone wants to add a conditional question like IF we ship the new auth system by Q3, THEN will on-call load go down, and actually let it trade as its own live contract instead of just voiding the bet whenever the IF part never happens (which is what we do now). My plan was to price it as whatever fraction of our shipped-by-Q3 worlds also have on-call load going down, recomputed live off the other markets — basically the normal way you'd estimate a conditional probability from data. A teammate with a math PhD insists this is fundamentally broken, not just annoying to build, something about how a genuinely tradeable if-then contract can't have its price always equal the right conditional probability without the whole thing collapsing into nonsense. I dont really see it, the formula looks totally standard to me — can you explain what's actually going wrong here, and whether we're just stuck voiding IF-fails bets forever?
