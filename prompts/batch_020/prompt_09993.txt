Random question that's been bugging me since a rough on-call week, and a couple of us went back and forth on it at lunch without really agreeing on anything. I was on a long overnight incident, up from around midnight to 6am, slowly narrowing down a nasty bug. A few times during the night I hit a point where I felt sure - like "ok, THIS is actually the root cause" - and told the team to stand down while I shipped a fix. At 2am I was right. At 5am I was just as sure, in exactly the same way it felt from the inside - and I was completely wrong, we reopened the incident twenty minutes later. What's stuck with me isn't just that I made a bad call while tired - everyone already knows fatigue makes you worse at your job. It's that my feeling of "I've got it, I actually know this is the cause" didn't change at all between the correct 2am call and the wrong 5am one. No internal warning light went off. And there's no specific moment in between where I could've caught myself switching from reliable to not, either, because minute to minute I never noticed any change in how sure I felt. Whatever happened, happened somewhere in those six hours, but not at any point I can actually point to.

My coworker Priya thinks I'm overthinking a pretty boring problem: fatigue degrades judgement, thats well known, so just add outside rules (mandatory handoff after N hours, no solo "stand down" calls overnight) instead of trusting your own read of your own reliability. Fair enough as advice, but it kind of assumes the deeper thing I'm asking about is fixable in principle if I just introspected harder or had more discipline, and I'm honestly not sure that's true. If at every single minute I can't detect any change from the minute before, it seems like I should never be able to notice myself crossing from "actually knows the cause" into "just feels like it knows the cause," even in ideal conditions, because the mismatch has to creep in somewhere I can't see it happening - if I could see it, it wouldn't be a mismatch anymore. So how is it even possible, in real time, to know that I currently know something rather than just feeling like I do? Is Priya right that this is basically an ordinary practical failure of self-monitoring under fatigue, or is there something about trying to catch yourself doing this specific thing that doesn't actually work even for someone doing everything right?
