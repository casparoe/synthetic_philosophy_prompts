I was naturalized as a U.S. citizen three weeks ago, and preparing for the civics portion of my interview sent me back to the Constitution itself, and from there to how fiercely that document was argued over by the very abolitionists who could not yet claim its protections. One disagreement in particular has stayed with me: the split between Frederick Douglass, who broke with William Lloyd Garrison in 1851 to insist that the Constitution, honestly read, was an anti-slavery document, and Garrison, who never abandoned his description of it as a "covenant with death, and an agreement with hell." Please compare the two positions, and say whose reading of the constitutional text as it actually stood before the Reconstruction Amendments is better supported, and whose depends more on explaining away clauses that cut the other way. Justify your answer with reference to specific constitutional provisions, not just each side's general rhetorical posture.
