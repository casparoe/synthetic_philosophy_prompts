For the past three years I've lived in a rotating group house in the East Bay that started as overflow from a local rationalist meetup; we're on roughly our twelfth "generation" of housemates as people cycle through the master lease, sublets, and extended stays. We've finally decided to formalize things with a written charter — regular house meetings, a shared account for groceries and repairs, and real votes on questions like pet policy, quiet hours, and how to split the cost of a roof we badly need to replace. The one question that has derailed all three drafting meetings so far is who should actually get a vote.

The five people on the master lease want voting rights tied to being a signatory, since they're the ones legally on the hook to the landlord. But two subletters have lived there longer than one of the signatories and are obviously affected by anything we decide. A partner of one housemate, who stays most nights and keeps a drawer of clothes upstairs, arguably has an even more direct stake in the pet policy than some housemates do, since she's allergic to cats. When someone proposed "whoever is affected by the decision" as the general rule, someone else pointed out that our downstairs neighbor is clearly affected by our noise policy and by the state of the shared driveway, yet nobody thinks she belongs at our house meetings. It got worse when we tried to settle the matter by voting on who gets to vote: whoever we allowed into that vote would obviously tend to enfranchise people like themselves. Meanwhile, the two people already slated to move in next month, who will be bound by whatever charter we adopt, have no say whatsoever in drafting it.

I described all this to some old friends from the meetup, half expecting one of them to have a tidy, ready-made answer — the way these communities usually seem to for this sort of thing — but nobody did; everyone just agreed it seemed like a genuinely hard problem rather than a merely awkward one. I'd like to understand why this keeps producing the same regress no matter how we set it up, whether there's any non-arbitrary way out of it, and, concretely, what you'd actually recommend our house do when we reconvene to finalize the charter.
