I somehow ended up on the volunteer case-review panel for a small restorative-justice diversion program in my city (a friend needed extra people and I had Tuesday evenings free, that's basically the whole story of how) — minor offenses get routed to us instead of court if both sides agree to try it. We've got a meeting in two weeks on a case thats splitting the panel and I need to walk in with an actual written recommendation, not just my gut feeling.

Here's the case: a guy in his mid-20s shoved and threatened a shop owner during what his therapist's letter calls a dissociative episode tied to PTSD, and he says he barely remembers the middle part of it. Our program's own training leans hard on a "trauma-informed, no-blame" model, so about half the panel wants the agreement to skip anything like an apology or acknowledgment of wrongdoing — the thinking being that making him perform remorse for something "he" wasn't even fully present for is dishonest, maybe even re-traumatizing. The rest of us aren't so sure. The shop owner has said that if we just tell her "there's no one to really be upset at, it was the trauma talking," it feels like she's the one getting written out of her own story. Honestly this keeps reminding me of some old essay I read years ago, by a British philosopher, I think the name was Strawson, about how stuff like resentment and gratitude aren't just primitive reactions we're supposed to reason our way out of once we understand the causes behind someone's behavior — and how excusing someone for one particular act is a totally different move than deciding they're just not the kind of being you have those reactions toward anymore.

Could you draft the memo I'll actually bring to the panel? Keep it to about a page, have it recommend concretely what the agreement should say and on what timeline, whether there's any acknowledgment-of-harm piece and what that should look like given the facts as reported, and please engage the "no-blame" side properly instead of just steamrolling them, because I don't think they're crazy for believing what they believe, I just think they might be missing something.
