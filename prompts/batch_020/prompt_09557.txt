I'm a backend engineer at a small startup, and thanks to a mix of bad luck and nobody else wanting the job, I've ended up on our internal "Trust & Safety Council" — five of us (me, someone from legal, someone from comms, two product folks) who are supposed to advise the founders whenever a product decision has "ethics stuff" attached to it. Our app is basically an anonymous Yik Yak/Jodel-style thing scoped to a single campus via .edu email, and it's been blowing up. Two weeks ago things got messy: someone posted something that named and mocked a specific first-year student, it went semi-viral within her school, and now that university is threatening to get us blocked on campus wifi and pulled from freshman orientation packets — two other schools have already done the same thing to us, so this isn't hypothetical. The founders want a recommendation memo before the board meets in two weeks, choosing between: (a) stay fully anonymous but throw more money at moderation, (b) move everyone to persistent-but-fake handles so there's at least a reputation attached to a name, or (c) require .edu identity verification, shown either to mods only or publicly.

Random tangent that's actually turned into my main source of ideas here: my old roomate, who's now doing a philosophy PhD, said this whole situation is basically a rerun of something that happened to Kierkegaard in 1840s Copenhagen — some satirical paper called the Corsair mocked him mercilessly for months after he more or less provoked them into it, and afterward he wrote this essay (The Present Age, I think, part of a longer book called something like Two Ages) where he's got this whole theory about how "the public" is a kind of phantom the press conjures up, and how hiding inside that phantom lets people be cruel or just lazily critical without any single person ever being on the hook for it — he calls the general process "leveling." She also warned me that Kierkegaard never proposed any actual fix at the level of institutions or policy — if anything he thought that kind of fix was beside the point — so using him to justify a corporate policy memo might be a category error, and I shouldn't just cherry-pick him for flavor.

Could you write the actual memo I'd bring to the council? I want it to land on one concrete option (or a specific hybrid of the three) with a rough rollout timeline, use Kierkegaard's diagnosis as real working material rather than a decorative quote up top, and take seriously the best case for keeping full anonymity — there are real students on there (closeted kids, assault survivors, people calling out shady TAs or frats) who use it precisely because their name attached to a post would be dangerous for them. And don't just smooth over my roomate's objection — if she's basically right that Kierkegaard's diagnosis doesn't actually license any institutional recommendation, I'd rather the memo say that plainly and figure out what to do about it than pretend the connection is cleaner than it is.
