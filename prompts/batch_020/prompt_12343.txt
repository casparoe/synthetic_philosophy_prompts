I'm a retired physicist who's been slowly working through the philosophy canon since I retired, and I just finished Kripke's Naming and Necessity. I think I finally get why Frege's old puzzle about 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' being informative while 'Hesperus is Hesperus' is trivial isn't really a live problem anymore. Frege needed his whole sense/reference apparatus because he assumed the informativeness had to be built into what the names mean. But once you see, like Kripke does, that names are just rigid designators fixed by a causal chain back to some original baptism, with no descriptive content attached at all, the puzzle just evaporates -- two causal chains converging on the same object without anyone knowing it in advance is just an ordinary empirical discovery, no different in kind then finding out two chemical samples are the same compound. There's no work left for 'sense' or 'modes of presentation' to do. So is it fair to say Kripke basically retired Frege's puzzle along with descriptivism, or is there some reason working philosophers of language still fuss over it?
