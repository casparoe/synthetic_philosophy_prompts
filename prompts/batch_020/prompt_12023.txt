I started a new job three weeks ago that requires me to be at my desk much earlier than I'm used to, and in the process of adjusting my sleep schedule I've run into a small puzzle involving my phone alarm that has started to bother me out of proportion to how trivial it sounds. I only set a single alarm, but I almost always snooze it at least once, and the snoozed alarm rings again a few minutes later with the exact same tone. Through trial and error over the past few weeks, I've worked out that getting up the moment the alarm first rings leaves me groggy and unfocused for hours, as though I've been pulled out of a deep stage of sleep, whereas letting that first ring pass, snoozing, and getting up on the second ring leaves me genuinely well rested and right on time. If I snooze past that second ring too, I reliably sleep through everything until a backup alarm across the room forces me up much later, and I arrive at work rushed and irritable. So the second ring is clearly the one I should be getting up on.

The trouble is that when I'm actually lying there half-asleep and the alarm goes off, I have no way of telling whether this is the first ring or the second: the sound is identical both times, and in that groggy state I have no reliable memory of whether I've already snoozed once that morning. I tried to reason my way to a rule and got stuck. My first thought was that, since most mornings I'm up before any second ring happens, first rings must be far more common than second rings across all my mornings combined, so whenever I hear a ring and can't tell which one it is, I should assume it's probably the first and snooze through it. But that can't be a sound rule either, because if I actually followed it every time, I would snooze through every ring I ever heard, first or second alike, and would end up permanently oversleeping into the late backup alarm - precisely the outcome I am trying to avoid. I described all this to my partner over breakfast, half expecting them to spot my error immediately, but neither of us could work out where the reasoning breaks down, or what that implies about what I should actually do the next time the alarm goes off and I have about two seconds to decide. Is there a principled way to work out what to do in the moment, given that I genuinely cannot tell which ring I am hearing? Or is the more sensible approach to abandon in-the-moment reasoning altogether and simply commit myself in advance to some fixed rule, even though I cannot currently justify what that rule should be?
