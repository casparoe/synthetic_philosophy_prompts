So last night my friend Priya and I got into it again about Bell's theorem, over pizza instead of over text for once (this might be the third time this exact fight has happened). I was defending what I think of as the boring mainstream take: Bell's theorem plus the actual experimental violations, including the loophole-free ones, means nature really is nonlocal and thats basically forced on us. Priya says I'm being too quick — her point is that nonlocality is only forced if you also grant "measurement independence" (aka the "no-conspiracy" or "free choice" assumption), the idea that the hidden variables can't be correlated with whatever settings the experimenters end up dialing in. Deny that instead, she says, and you can go superdeterministic, or even let the later measurement choice reach back and shape the particle's earlier state (retrocausality), and locality survives just fine. We were both already well past the "what is entanglement" stage, so neither of us wants a 101 explainer — we want the actual fight, the one where you already accept that CHSH is violated exactly as QM predicts and you're just arguing about which assumption has to give.

Could you write that fight out properly, as a dialogue between the two positions? Doesn't have to be literally me and Priya, name them whatever works. I want the nonlocality side to press hard on the conspiracy/fine-tuning worry against superdeterminism — that denying measurement independence doesn't just cost you "logical possibility," it seems to wreck the whole rationale for running a controlled experiment at all, and maybe bring up how physicists have tried to close that exact loophole using light from distant stars or quasars to pick the settings. And I want the locality side to have a genuine comeback rather than just shrugging and saying "still logically possible" — and to get a real turn to press back on why nonlocality/nonseparability isn't its own unexplained brute fact dressed up as an "explanation." Both sides should be sharp and actually responding to what the other just said, not just restating their own talking points, and it's totally fine if it doesn't resolve neatly, since it sure didn't for us.
