Van Fraassen's Reflection Principle holds that my current credence in a proposition, conditional on the supposition that my credence in it at some later time will be r, must itself equal r. This looks to me like nothing more than a restatement of ordinary Bayesian conditionalization: if I am certain that, between now and then, I will only ever revise my credences by conditionalizing on whatever evidence comes my way, then Reflection simply falls out as a theorem, with no further assumptions required. That would make the Dutch book argument van Fraassen builds around the principle, and the accompanying "treat your future self as an expert" motivation, superfluous machinery layered on top of a fact already secured by ordinary diachronic coherence. Given this, why do formal epistemologists still treat Reflection as a distinct, independently controversial principle—one requiring its own restrictions to rule out cases of anticipated memory loss—rather than simply treating it as conditionalization under another name?
