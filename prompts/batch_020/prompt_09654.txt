A conversation with some friends from my local rationalist meetup recently reopened the old debate over Hempel's raven paradox, and it left me wanting a clearer sense of how the various technical responses that have accumulated since the 1940s actually stack up against one another, rather than simply knowing that they exist. The setup, for context, is the familiar one: by the equivalence condition, "all ravens are black" is logically equivalent to "all non-black things are non-ravens," so by Nicod's criterion, observing a red herring (an object that is both non-black and a non-raven) should confirm the raven hypothesis exactly as observing a black raven does — which strikes nearly everyone as absurd on its face. What gets discussed far less outside specialist circles is how differently the major responses handle the underlying logic, and some of them strike me as considerably more successful than others at genuinely dissolving the paradox rather than merely relabeling it.

I'd like you to rank the following eight approaches by how successfully each one resolves the paradox, as opposed to merely restating it, changing the subject, or biting the bullet without real explanatory payoff: (1) Hempel's own response, which accepts the conclusion and attributes the felt paradox to tacit background assumptions; (2) the "standard" Bayesian/proportionality solution, anticipated by Hosiasson-Lindenbaum's pre-Hempel probabilistic treatment, on which non-black non-ravens confirm the hypothesis but only negligibly, given how heavily non-ravens outnumber ravens; (3) I. J. Good's counterexample, which constructs background knowledge under which observing a black raven can disconfirm "all ravens are black"; (4) Quine's proposal to restrict Nicod's criterion to natural-kind or "projectible" predicates; (5) Goodman's treatment of the paradox as a special case of the grue problem of projectibility; (6) the Scheffler–Goodman "selective confirmation" account; (7) Vranas's argument that the standard Bayesian solution smuggles in an independence assumption that fails in general; and (8) the Popperian move of rejecting the entire confirmation-theoretic framing in favor of falsification and corroboration. Feel free to fold in any other well-known approach you think deserves a place.
