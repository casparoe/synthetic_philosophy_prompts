I cover philosophy and science for a magazine, and I'm preparing for a recorded interview with a philosopher who has built her career defending Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility: the claim that no one can be truly or "ultimately" responsible for what they do, since responsibility for an action would require responsibility for the character and motives that produced it, and that requirement generates a regress nothing can close, whether determinism turns out to be true or not. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond a bare restatement of the argument: I want at least one question on whether the causa sui regress is doing any more work than Nietzsche's old point about the incoherence of a self-caused self, one on why she holds that indeterminism doesn't rescue libertarian free will, given that undetermined choices look like luck rather than control, one on the tension between her position and "Freedom and Resentment," the essay in which Galen Strawson's own father, Peter Strawson, argued that the truth of determinism is beside the point for our reactive attitudes, and one on the standard compatibilist rejoinder that "ultimate" responsibility was never the kind our ordinary moral practices actually needed. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and close with a question about what, if anything, could count as evidence against the Basic Argument.
