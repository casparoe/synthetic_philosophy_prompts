I chair the Q&A that follows our department's philosophy of biology colloquium, and next week's speaker is presenting a paper that defends a strict etiological, selected-effects account of biological function -- in the line of Wright, Neander, and Millikan -- against causal-role rivals descended from Cummins. In the draft she has circulated, whenever a commentator raises a case in which a trait plainly does something beneficial but lacks the right selective history, she reaches for the same move: she says the trait "simply has no function yet, only an effect," and treats that bit of bullet-biting as closing the matter. My graduate seminar has spent the last few weeks working through the function literature, and I would like the Q&A to model something more than a restatement of the position for the audience's benefit.

Please draft eight to ten questions for the session that make that deflection progressively harder to use, rather than something raised once and set aside. Within that arc, include at least one question about a trait, or an entire organism, assembled with no evolutionary history behind it at all, so that "not yet" has no future episode of selection left for it to refer to; one about exaptation, where a structure's current effect and the effect it was originally selected for have already come apart; one asking her to explain why malfunction is supposed to be easier for her account to accommodate than for a causal-role theorist's, given that causal-role theorists have their own proposals for handling breakdown and abnormal cases; and one raising the fact that biologists doing physiology or comparative anatomy seem, in practice, to hand out function-talk on causal-role grounds without consulting anyone's selective history. For each question, add a brief note on the reply she is likely to give and a sharper follow-up keyed to that particular reply, and end the sequence with a question asking what finding or thought experiment would actually lead her to abandon the etiological analysis rather than qualify it once again.
