Write a short story built around the discovery of a single, well-documented exception to what had always been treated as an exceptionless law of nature. Let the plot's tension come from two different ways the characters make sense of the anomaly. One character holds a Humean view of laws, in the spirit of David Lewis's best-system analysis: laws are nothing but the axioms of whichever true deductive system best combines simplicity and strength in systematizing the total mosaic of otherwise unconnected particular facts, so an isolated exception simply calls for adopting a different "best system" to summarize the facts, not for discovering anything further about the world. Another character holds an anti-Humean view, in the spirit of D. M. Armstrong's account of laws as relations of natural necessitation between universals: a genuine violation of a law is metaphysically impossible, so the anomaly must be a clue to some deeper, still-unrecognized law. Dramatize this disagreement through what each character expects to happen next and how they act on that expectation, rather than having them simply announce their positions.
