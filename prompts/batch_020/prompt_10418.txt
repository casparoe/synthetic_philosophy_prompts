My younger brother died three weeks ago at twenty-six, the same age Frank Ramsey was when he died, and Ramsey's 'Epilogue' -- his 1925 talk to the Cambridge Apostles -- was something my brother reread every year since university. What he loved in it was the claim that feeling humbled by the universe's vastness is a category error, since significance was never about physical size: his own picture of things was, like Ramsey's, 'drawn in perspective, and not like a model to scale,' right down to the joke that the heat death of the universe is a future cost so heavily discounted it has almost no present value. What he couldn't stand was the standard eulogy move of conceding we're each a speck in an indifferent cosmos and then insisting love or memory is 'big enough' to outweigh it -- he thought that quietly conceded the very premise Ramsey denied. I'm giving his eulogy Saturday, to a room mostly of family and old friends who've never heard of Ramsey, plus a few people from his philosophy MPhil at Cambridge who'll wince if this collapses into 'he mattered despite being small.' Please write it: about five minutes, no false comfort, and whatever comfort it offers has to be earned from Ramsey's own argument, not the cliché he spent his life arguing against.
