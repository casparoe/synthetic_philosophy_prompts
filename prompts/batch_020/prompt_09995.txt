I have been asked to give the toast at the wedding of my former doctoral student, who wrote her dissertation defending David Lewis's counterpart theory, to her fiancé, a fellow modal metaphysician who has built his career defending transworld identity along broadly Kripkean and Plantingian lines. By department legend, the two of them first met while arguing opposite sides of the Humphrey objection at a graduate conference, and I happen to know that their vows include a promise to love one another 'in every possible world.' Roughly half the room will be philosophers who will notice at once that this vow cannot mean quite the same thing for a committed counterpart theorist as for a committed transworld-identity theorist; the other half is family, who will simply find it moving. Please write me a five-minute toast, built around one good joke about counterpart theory versus transworld identity, that earns a genuine laugh from the metaphysicians without losing anyone else, and that reads, in the end, as affection for two people rather than as an adjudication of their dispute.
