I only took up philosophy after retiring from a career as a field geologist, mainly to follow my daughter's work: her dissertation argues that Bradley's regress is still unsolved, and that the standard fixes -- Fregean unsaturation, primitive non-relational ties -- all quietly assume the very unity of the proposition they are meant to explain. She marries next month and has asked me, her father, to give the toast. Half the room will be metaphysicians from her department who will wince if I reach for the usual cliché about two people 'becoming one' or 'bound together' -- exactly the unearned unity her work exists to question -- while the rest have never heard of F. H. Bradley and just want to hear that I love my daughter and her new spouse. Please write me that toast, about four minutes long, with one genuine regress joke the metaphysicians will actually laugh at, rigorous enough that my daughter couldn't fault it on the merits, and warm enough that everyone else simply hears a father who loves his daughter.
