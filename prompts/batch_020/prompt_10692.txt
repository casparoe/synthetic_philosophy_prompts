Two of my graduate students got into a substantive disagreement after our seminar on the epistemology of testimony, and I haven't been able to settle it to my own satisfaction.

(1) Renata mentioned that she routinely accepts factual claims from her research assistant without checking them herself, since he has been reliable in the past. (2) Julian said this is only justified because she already has independent, non-testimonial evidence -- built up over many past checks -- that this particular source tracks the truth; on his view, testimonial belief is never itself a basic source of justification, only ever parasitic on perception, memory, and the inductive inferences built from them. (3) Renata replied that this can't be right as a general account, since hardly any of what she or anyone else believes on someone's word has actually been personally and independently verified; if that kind of independent evidence were really required, almost nothing anyone believes on testimony would count as justified. (4) Julian answered that she was attacking a stronger thesis than his own: reductionism doesn't require a personally verified dossier on every source, only that listeners monitor each report for local cues -- the speaker's apparent competence and sincerity, the claim's antecedent plausibility, its fit with what is already believed -- and it's this monitoring, not a global track record, that does the inductive work. (5) Renata answered that this just pushes the regress back a step, since judgments of competence, sincerity, and fit are themselves normally made using background beliefs that were acquired testimonially in the first place; she added that small children trust what they're told long before they're capable of monitoring anyone's competence or sincerity, which suggests such trust is basic rather than a product of monitoring.

Could you go through moves (1) through (5) and say who has the better of it at each stage, then close by noting what, if anything, each side's position is missing that the other's isn't?
