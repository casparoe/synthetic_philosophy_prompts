State its premises explicitly, and explain why each one is meant to be independently plausible on its own. Explain what conclusion follows from the premises, and why this poses a distinctive problem for non-reductive physicalism specifically, as opposed to reductive physicalism or substance dualism. Finally, state briefly how Kim himself proposes to escape the problem."

And here, in full, is my partner's answer:

"Kim's argument rests on four claims. First, mental properties supervene on physical properties: any two physically identical worlds are mentally identical, so there is no mental difference without a physical difference. Second, the physical domain is causally closed: every physical event that has a cause at a given time has a sufficient physical cause at that time, so nothing 'outside' physics ever needs to be invoked to explain a physical occurrence. Third, the causal exclusion principle says that if an event already has a sufficient cause, no distinct event occurring at the same time can also count as a cause of it, except in rare coincidental cases like two snipers firing on a target at once; systematic, wholesale overdetermination of this kind is not something we should accept as a general feature of the world. Fourth, and this is really the premise that makes the argument bite specifically for non-reductive physicalists: because mental properties like pain are multiply realizable across different physical substrates, they cannot be identical to any one physical property, so the non-reductive physicalist is committed to mental properties being distinct from their physical bases while still wanting them to do real causal work.

Putting these together: suppose a mental property M causes a physical effect P by way of its physical realizer P*. Since P* is already a sufficient physical cause of P (by closure), and M is distinct from P* (by multiple realizability), exclusion says M cannot also be a genuine cause of P unless we accept the kind of systematic overdetermination premise three rules out. So M turns out to be epiphenomenal: real, but causally idle. This is a special problem for non-reductive physicalism specifically because a reductive physicalist can just say M is identical to P*, so there is no distinct competitor to exclude, and a substance dualist was never committed to the physical domain being causally closed to begin with, so premise two is not even available to run the argument against them.

Kim's own way out is to accept that the mental cause and the physical cause systematically overdetermine their effects together: since they co-occur every single time rather than by mere coincidence, he argues this regularity actually makes the overdetermination unmysterious, so mental properties keep their causal powers after all without physicalists having to give up their non-reductive commitments."
