I've followed the anthropics debates on LessWrong and Overcoming Bias for years, so I thought I had a handle on every move in the Self-Sampling-Assumption-versus-Self-Indication-Assumption dispute. Then two people in my in-person reading group, partway through Bostrom's *Anthropic Bias*, had an exchange that left me unable to say who came out ahead, and I'd like a neutral, move-by-move read on it.

(1) Sasha opened by rejecting SSA because it delivers the Doomsday Argument: merely conditionalizing on your own birth rank among all the humans who will ever have lived apparently licenses a real shift in credence toward near-term extinction, with no new empirical input at all. A theory that lets you learn something like that about civilization's future purely by introspecting on your place in a list, she said, has to be broken somewhere.

(2) Marcus replied that this cuts both ways, since it's SIA that generates the presumptuous philosopher: given two cosmological theories that are empirically indistinguishable except that one implies a vastly larger population of observers, SIA tells you to be almost certain of the bigger-universe theory before the physicists even finish building their experiment. If settling cosmology from an armchair is absurd, he said, that is exactly what SIA licenses.

(3) Sasha answered that the cases aren't symmetric: the presumptuous philosopher asks you to compare two entire possible universes across a contrived, astronomical gap in their observer populations, while the Doomsday Argument asks you to predict the future of the one universe you already know you're in, from the position you already occupy in it. An implication confined to a gerrymandered comparison between hypothetical worlds, she said, is much easier to live with than one that reaches into your own timeline.

(4) Marcus said that distinction doesn't survive contact with Sleeping Beauty: that problem splits people into the same halfer and thirder camps that track SSA and SIA, using one coin, one experiment, and no comparison between different possible universes whatsoever. If the rift shows up entirely within a single timeline, he said, it has nothing to do with hypothetical universes versus one's own future, and Sasha's distinction just gerrymanders the problem space to fit the conclusion she already wanted.

Please go through moves (1) through (4) and say, for each, who has the better of it and why -- not just who wins overall -- and close by flagging anything you think is important to this dispute that neither Sasha nor Marcus raises.
