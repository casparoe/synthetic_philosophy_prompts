Spent 35 years doing statistical mechanics before I retired, so I thought I already understood what ergodic means, until I fell down a rabbit hole of "ergodicity economics" (Ole Peters and that crowd) a few months back. Their pitch is that Kelly betting -- maximizing the expected log of your wealth, i.e. the long-run geometric growth rate -- isn't just expected utility theory with a log-shaped utility function bolted on, its actually a different and more defensible notion of rationality, since ordinary expected-value reasoning quietly averages over a bunch of parallel copies of you that never existed instead of tracking what happens to the one real you across time. Samuelson thought this was nonsense and apparently even wrote a whole rebuttal paper using only one-syllable words just to mock the log-utility crowd, insisting it's just log utility dressed up in a physics costume, nothing deeper going on. I can't tell who's right here, so can you make the strongest possible case FOR the ergodicity/Kelly camp: that maximizing time-average growth is a genuinely distinct, and often superior, standard of rationality for repeated or compounding bets, not merely expected-utility maximization wearing a disguise?
