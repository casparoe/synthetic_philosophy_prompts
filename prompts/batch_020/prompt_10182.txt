So I'm teaching the falsifiability unit in my undergrad phil of science seminar this week, and one of my students dug up this bit from Popper's intellectual autobiography where he flatly says the theory of natural selection isn't a testable scientific theory at all, just a "metaphysical research programme" -- his reasoning being that "survival of the fittest" is circular, since fitness just gets defined as whatever traits happen to survive and reproduce more, so the whole principle collapses into "those who survive, survive," which no observation could ever falsify. I'd half remembered Popper saying something like this once but had never actually sat down with the argument, and honestly, stated that baldly, it's a pretty clean bit of logic.

Now the student (who I'm fairly sure lifted this from some intelligent-design site, though they wouldn't cop to it) is pushing the line that if the guy who invented the demarcation criterion himself says Darwinism flunks his own test, biologists don't really get to just wave that away. I've got a vague memory that Popper walked the claim back later on, but the student pre-empted that by pointing out that recanting under pressure from an angry biology establishment isn't the same as actually rebutting the tautology point, and I realized I didn't have a good comeback for that either. So: is there a real, substantive answer to the tautology charge itself, or did Popper basically hand opponents of evolution a legitimate objection that the field just sort of collectively agreed to stop talking about? Trying to get my head around it properly before Thursday's seminar so I don't embarass myself in front of 20 sophomores.
