I'm putting together a session for our grad philosophy of mind reading group next month, and the guest we've got lined up is a committed naive realist disjunctivist -- one of those people who'll happily tell you that a veridical experience of a red apple and a subjectively indistinguishable hallucination of one are not two instances of the same fundamental kind of mental state, full stop. We spent the last few weeks working through the classic argument from illusion and the usual "highest common factor" responses (sense-datum theory, intentionalism), so the group aready knows the setup cold and is itching to see the view get pushed on rather than explained again for the fourth time.

Can you draft me 8-10 questions for the Q&A that actually engage the substance instead of just inviting her to restate the position? I want at least one question that presses the causal/"matching hallucination" worry -- the case where a hallucination is produced by directly stimulating the visual cortex so the proximal causal chain is type-identical to the veridical case, which seems to strain the claim that these are different in kind rather than degree. I also want one on the "screening off" problem for epistemological disjunctivism, McDowell/Pritchard-style: if the subject can't tell from the inside whether shes in the good case or the subjectively identical bad case, how is her supposedly factive justification meant to do any work for her, first-personally? And one on the old time-lag point, since light and neural transmission both take time and naive realism wants something like unmediated access to a presently existing object. For each question, tack on a quick follow-up keyed to whichever of the three standard moves she reaches for (deny the common-kind assumption, bite the bullet on the causal case, or retreat to a purely disjunctive concept of experience) -- I don't want any of those to go unchallenged.
