So this argument happened at Thanksgiving and its been stuck in my head since. Backstory: my company just started doing these quarterly "culture pulse" surveys where you get a $50 gift card if you submit a written testimonal about how much you admire our CEO's leadership. I filled one out (free fifty bucks) even though honestly I don't feel much of anything about the guy one way or the other. I brought it up at dinner as a funny work story and it somehow turned into a whole back-and-forth between my uncle and my aunt that nobody could settle.

(1) My uncle said there's nothing philosophically weird going on: the gift card gives me a reason to admire the CEO, period. A reason is just a consideration that favors having some attitude, and getting paid obviously favors having it.

(2) My aunt said no -- the gift card gives me a reason to want to admire him, or to act like I do, but it can't be a reason that makes admiring him actually fitting, since nothing about his real leadership qualities changed the moment the gift card showed up.

(3) My uncle said that's a distinction without a difference. If I have sufficient reason to do something, I have sufficient reason to do it, and you don't get to disqualify some reasons as not "really" counting just because you don't like where they came from.

(4) My aunt said that proves too much -- by that logic a mobster threatening your kneecaps also gives you a reason to admire him, which is insane. So there has to be some real difference between reasons that track what the CEO (or the mobster) is actually like and reasons that are just about the consequences of having the attitude. She admitted she wasn't sure how to state that difference without it sounding like she was just making up a rule to get the answer she wanted.

Neither of them budged, and I'm the one who actually cashed the gift card so now I can't stop thinking about it. Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step (not just who wins overall)? And is there an actual principled way to draw the line my aunt's gesturing at, or is my uncle right that it's rigged from the start?
