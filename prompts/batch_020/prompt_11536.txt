So a coworker linked an old AI alignment forum post in our team's random Slack channel a few weeks ago, and I went down a total rabbit hole from there (I'm a backend dev, not a philosopher, so no idea if I'm even using the jargon right). The thing that's been stuck in my head ever since is this idea people call the "commitment races problem." As I understand it: if two agents can both make binding commitments, whoever locks theirs in first gets leverage over the other, kind of like the classic negotiating trick where being unable to back down is actually a strength. But that means both sides are incentivized to commit as early and fast as possible, before they've had proper time to reason it through, because reasoning takes time, and time is exactly what you don't have if the other guy might commit first. So you get this race to the bottom where being careful gets punished, and the "winning" move is to slam down some threat or ultimatum half-baked -- which is obviously a recipe for disaster if both sides do it and lock into incompatible demands that neither can walk back later.

I'd love a short story that makes a reader feel that dynamic in their gut rather than have a character just explain it out loud, kind of like how the Trisolaran chapters in The Three-Body Problem make the game theory itself feel dreadful. Doesn't have to be aliens though -- could be two probes from different civilizations meeting in deep space for the first time, two trading AI's on opposite sides of a merger negotiation, or literally anything else you think sells the idea better, your call. The one thing I really want is for the plot to hinge on the actual tradeoff (committing fast and dumb vs. committing slow and safe-but-weak) rather than just namedrop it, and if you happen to remember any of the "ways out" people in that literature propose, I'd love for a character to stumble onto something in that spirit for the ending instead of a clean "one side blinks first." A page or two is plenty, and it doesnt need to be textbook-accurate on every detail, I just don't want the central tension fudged.
