Two weeks ago I had my appendix removed after a fairly dramatic middle-of-the-night trip to the ER, and while I was recovering on the couch, my surgeon mentioned, almost in passing, that nobody is entirely sure what the appendix is actually for. That comment sent me down a genuine rabbit hole of reading. I learned that some immunologists now hypothesize that the appendix functions as a kind of reservoir, or "safe house," for beneficial gut bacteria, helping to repopulate the gut after an illness wipes it out. What I did not expect was to end up, a few nights later, on the phone with my sister, who is finishing a philosophy PhD, discovering that there is a whole unresolved debate in philosophy of biology about what it would even mean for the appendix to have a "function" in the first place. She walked me through the difference between etiological (or "selected-effect") accounts, in the tradition of Larry Wright, on which a trait's function is whatever effect explains why it was selected for and has persisted in the population, and causal-role accounts, in the tradition of Robert Cummins, on which a trait's function is simply whatever it currently contributes to some capacity of the containing system, with no essential reference to evolutionary history at all. Apparently my appendix is something of a textbook case for prying the two views apart, since the "safe house" story is a perfectly good current causal contribution that almost certainly was not what the structure was originally selected for.

Now that I have had this crash course, I would like to do something more interesting with it than simply boring my coworkers. Could you write me a short, genuinely funny joke that turns on the real substance of the etiological-versus-causal-role distinction? I do not want a generic biology pun, or a generic "philosophers argue about everything" joke with the labels swapped in; I want the punchline to actually depend on grasping the difference between "what a trait was selected to do" and "what a trait currently does for the system it is part of," in the same way that a joke about functionalism and type-identity theory in philosophy of mind only really lands if you understand what is at stake between the two. You are welcome to use my appendix as the running example if it helps, but the main requirement is that the joke should land with someone who already knows this debate, not just someone who enjoys biology puns.
