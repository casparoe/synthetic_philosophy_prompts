So I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal epistemology, and this week's early modern seminar sent me down a bit of a rabbit hole I havent been able to shake. We were on section 6 of Leibniz's Discourse on Metaphysics, the bit where he points out that if you scatter a bunch of points on a page at random, you can always find some mathematical curve that passes through every single one -- so a world being lawful can't just mean some equation fits the data, since literally any data fits some equation. His fix is that God picks the world that's simplest in its laws while being richest in phenomena. A friend of mine doing theoretical CS took one look at that passage and said it's basically Kolmogorov complexity and algorithmic randomness, just centuries before anyone had the math for it -- and now I can't stop thinking about it, especially since this is also the guy who invented binary arithmetic and dreamed up the characteristica universalis and calculus ratiocinator, a universal symbolic language plus calculus that could supposedly settle any dispute mechanically.

What I'm actually curious about: if you sat Leibniz down and walked him through Solomonoff induction properly -- universal prior over all programs on some fixed universal Turing machine, hypotheses weighted by 2 to the minus their program length, the whole thing being formally uncomputable -- what do you think his actual reaction would be? Not the lazy "he liked simplicity so he'd probably approve" answer, but something that engages with what he actually believed: would he take Solomonoff induction as vindicating the Principle of Sufficient Reason and his simplest-laws-richest-phenomena story about creation, or would he be bothered that the choice of universal Turing machine is arbitrary -- there's no sufficient reason to pick one machine over another, and different choices can rank the same hypotheses totally differently -- and that the ideal predictor he's being handed is literally impossible to run? Feel free to bring in the monadology or the Arnauld correspondence if its relevant, I just want it grounded in the actual texts rather than vibes.
