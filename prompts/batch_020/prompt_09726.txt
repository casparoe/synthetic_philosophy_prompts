In *On Certainty*, Wittgenstein describes propositions such as "the earth has existed for many years" as "hinges": our practices of doubting and testing beliefs against experience presuppose them, so they themselves are held fast rather than tested. Since no empirical discovery could overturn a hinge proposition, doesn't this mean Wittgenstein treats hinges as a special class of necessary truths, unlike ordinary empirical claims that remain open to revision? And if that's right, how does this square with his own image of a shifting river-bed, where what counts as a hinge at one time can later become an ordinary, testable empirical claim, and vice versa?
