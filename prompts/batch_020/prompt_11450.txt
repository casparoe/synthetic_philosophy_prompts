He had been reading Peter Woit's and Lee Smolin's critiques of string theory and come away convinced that the field has quietly given up on any Popperian standard of science: after several decades it still hasn't produced a single risky, falsifiable prediction, and a "landscape" of something like 10^500 possible vacua seems tailor-made to accommodate almost any observed value of, say, the cosmological constant. I pushed back that falsifiability was never a terribly stable criterion in the first place. Lakatos already showed that no theory is tested in isolation, only as part of a research programme wrapped in a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, so what actually matters is whether a programme's adjustments are progressive (generating novel, confirmed predictions) or merely degenerating (ad hoc patches with no independent payoff); and Laudan went further still, arguing in "The Demise of the Demarcation Problem" that the whole project of drawing a principled line between science and pseudoscience is confused and should be dropped in favor of just asking whether a belief is well warranted.
