Ok, here's one for the pile: I'm sick of quantum interpretation essays that just rehash the cat-in-the-box thing and then shrug at the end. Write me one that actually weighs Everett, Copenhagen, and Bohmian mechanics against each other and lands on a verdict -- no "reasonable people disagree" cop-out allowed. Make sure you actually engage with: (1) whether the Everettian can really get Born-rule probabilities out of a theory where every outcome happens -- is the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic move legit, or are Albert, Kent and Price right that its basically smuggling probability in through the back door; (2) whether Bohm's outright nonlocality, and the headache of reconciling the pilot wave with relativistic invariance, is a bigger cost than people give it credit for; and (3) whether Copenhagen is even a real interpretation, or just "shut up and calculate" with a lab coat on. Pick a horse -- a fourth option like GRW or QBism is fine too, as long as you actually argue for it rather than just gesturing at it.
