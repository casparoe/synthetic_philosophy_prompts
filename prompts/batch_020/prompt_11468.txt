I'm giving the toast at my sister's wedding next month. She's a philosopher who works on judgment aggregation, and though I never studied philosophy myself, I've read enough of her papers over the years to follow the arguments, if not reproduce them. The discursive dilemma -- the fact that a committee can accept every premise of an argument by majority vote and then, voting on the conclusion directly, reject the very conclusion those premises imply -- has become something of a family joke. Roughly half the guests are her fellow judgment-aggregation people, who will catch a reference to premise-based versus conclusion-based procedures before I finish the sentence; the other half is family and old friends, who won't. Please write a five-minute toast built around one genuine discursive-dilemma joke -- about merging two people's separate judgments into a single shared life -- that lands as a tribute to her marriage for both halves of the room, without misstating what the result actually shows just to make the joke work.
