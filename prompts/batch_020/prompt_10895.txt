I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent a long time thinking on my own about the passage in the Zhuangzi where he dreams he is a butterfly, and I have grown unconvinced by how it is usually explained. Nearly every popular account treats it as a cousin of Descartes' worry that we can never be sure we are not dreaming, and reads Zhuangzi's closing remark as just another way of confessing that same uncertainty. But Zhuangzi shows none of Descartes' anxiety about the question, and the passage does not end by declaring the matter undecidable — it ends by noting that Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly are simply two forms joined by a transformation. Please argue for the strongest version of the view that the butterfly dream is not a Cartesian-style skeptical challenge at all, but a claim about the fluidity of identity that never has to settle whether Zhuangzi is really a man or really a butterfly. Please also address the complication that some serious interpreters still call this a kind of "skepticism" — just one aimed not at "am I dreaming right now" but at whether any waking perspective can ever count as final.
