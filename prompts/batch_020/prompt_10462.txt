I'm a novelist writing a book whose protagonist is a decision theorist, and as research I've arranged an interview with a real specialist who has built her career defending evidential cooperation in large worlds: the claim that agents who can never causally interact should still act cooperatively toward each other's values, because their decisions are evidentially correlated. I want the exchange to give me sharper material than an encyclopedia paraphrase, so draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. Include at least one question each on why mere evidential correlation, absent any causal channel, is supposed to license cooperation rather than wishful thinking; on how she could ever gather enough evidence about causally inaccessible agents' values to know what compromise she's even acting on; on how the view differs from Hofstadter-style superrationality between twins and from Kantian universalizability, since people keep collapsing the three; and on whether resting everything on a very large or infinite world lets tiny probabilities of enormous payoffs dominate the reasoning, Pascal's-mugging style -- and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give.
