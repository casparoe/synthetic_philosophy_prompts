Since retiring from a career in physics, I have been reading systematically through decision theory and its recent applications in ethics. I recently came across "evidential cooperation in large worlds" (ECL), an idea I gather traces back to Caspar Oesterheld's work on multiverse-wide superrationality. As I understand the argument, a sufficiently large or infinite universe must contain vast numbers of agents whose reasoning correlates with our own even without any causal contact; evidential decision theory then gives us reason to act cooperatively toward their values, since our own disposition to cooperate is itself evidence that they are disposed to cooperate too. My question concerns the physics the argument relies on. As far as I can tell, the "large world" it requires is really just a many-worlds cosmology, so the whole case for cooperation stands or falls with the Everett interpretation of quantum mechanics. Given that interpretations of quantum mechanics are observationally equivalent, with no empirical way for physicists to adjudicate between them, shouldn't we treat this cooperative argument as resting on untestable metaphysics rather than sound inference?
