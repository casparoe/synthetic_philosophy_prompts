I've been slogging through the Hart–Fuller material for a jurisprudence seminar, and weirdly the part that's actually tripping me up isn't the grudge informer case everyone always brings up — it's the later stuff about Fuller's "inner morality of law." Fuller lays out those eight procedural desiderata (generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, no contradictions between rules, not demanding the impossible, reasonable constancy through time, and congruence between the rules on paper and what officials actually do — he's even got that parable about King Rex botching each one in turn) and insists the whole package amounts to a genuine morality, just an internal/procedural one rather than a morality of substantive justice. Hart is not buying it in his review of The Morality of Law — his basic line is that its all just efficacy conditions for getting purposive, rule-guided behavior out of people, no different in kind from the "internal standards" of any skilled goal-directed activity, good or evil (I recall he's got some pointed analogy for this, maybe involving a poisoner or maybe carpentry, honestly cant remember which).

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed Fullerian and a committed Hartian working this out properly? Neither one should be a pushover for the other — the positivist should press the efficacy-conditions point as hard as it goes, and the Fullerian needs an actual comeback, not just restating the stuff about reciprocity between ruler and ruled. Somewhere in there I'd like them to test it against a concrete case: a legal system that nails all eight desiderata perfectly — crystal clear rules, properly published, prospective, stable, enforced exactly as written — but uses all that procedural rigor to run something monstrous, like a rigidly enforced caste system. Does the Fullerian have to grant that such a system achieved some real moral good just by being so procedurally clean, and is that a bullet they can bite gracefully? It'd also be good if at some point they got into whether the two of them actually disagree about anything at the level of fact, or whether this is one of those disputes that's ultimately just about which things get to wear the label "morality."
