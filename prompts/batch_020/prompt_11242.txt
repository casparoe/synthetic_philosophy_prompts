So last night I got into this long, kind of heated argument with my friend Priya about split-brain patients — the corpus callosotomy people from the old Sperry/Gazzaniga experiments, where they sever the connection between the two hemispheres to control epilepsy. It kicked off because she'd read something about a patient whose hands apparently fought each other, like one buttoning a shirt while the other undoes it, and she was convinced that basically proves there's two separate people stuck in one skull after the surgery. I thought that was way too fast a conclusion — these patients walk around, hold down jobs, don't report feeling like two people day to day, so it seemed weird to say the operation literally doubled the number of subjects of experience in there.

We went back and forth for ages and neither of us budged. She kept bringing up the classic lateralized tests, where you flash one word to the left visual field and a different word to the right, and the patient will say one of them out loud but grab the object matching the other word with their left hand, like they don't even know why they're doing it — to her that screamed "two separate minds, each with its own access to information." I countered with something I half remembered about newer research (a Dutch group, I think the lead researcher's name was Pinto?) that apparently found split-brain patients could still respond correctly to stuff across their entire visual field on certain tasks, not just their own half, which lead me to think there's still just one subject whose access gets fragmented under specific lab conditions, not a literal second person popping into existence. She accused me of hiding behind "common sense" and ignoring what the data shows, I accused her of over-reading a couple of dramatic anecdotes into a huge metaphysical conclusion, and we honestly probably butchered some of the details on both sides.

I'd love to see this argued out properly by two people who actually know what they're doing. Could you write a dialogue between two characters who hold these opposing views — one defending the idea that (at least during standard lateralized testing) a split-brain patient contains two distinct subjects of experience, and the other defending the idea that there remains a single, unified subject the whole time, even though perceptual access sometimes gets split. Make them equally sharp, no strawmen on either side, and have them genuinely respond to each other's specific points rather than just restating their own — the hand-conflict cases, what "unity of consciousness" actually requires, whether mere functional/informational separation between hemispheres is enough on its own to generate a second subject, the classic lateralized-word evidence, and whatever those newer integration-style findings would mean for either side. I don't need them to land on some neat resolution at the end — given how the argument with Priya went, an unresolved but genuinely sharp back-and-forth would feel more honest anyway.
