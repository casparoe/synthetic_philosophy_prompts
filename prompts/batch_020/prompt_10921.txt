I'm researching a novel set in the aftermath of the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, and I asked two philosopher friends, Naomi and Devon, to read Voltaire's Candide alongside excerpts from Leibniz's Theodicy so I could get the theological debate right. Over dinner they ended up arguing about something I hadn't expected: whether Candide actually refutes Leibniz, or only mocks a caricature of him.

Devon said Pangloss's response to every catastrophe -- the syphilis, the shipwreck, the earthquake itself -- shows that 'the best of all possible worlds' is compatible with literally anything, which makes it either empty or obscene. Naomi said that isn't actually Leibniz's claim: he never says each event is good on its own, only that the whole compossible system beats every alternative complete system, and that you can't subtract the earthquake while holding everything else fixed, since on his view each substance's very identity is bound up with every other's. Devon replied that a thesis able to absorb any horror by appeal to some incomprehensible package deal is unfalsifiable and explains nothing -- which is exactly what Voltaire's satire dramatizes, argument or no argument. Naomi countered that unfalsifiability cuts just as hard against secular views on the ultimate meaninglessness of suffering, and that Leibniz isn't improvising an excuse after the fact: from the Discourse on Metaphysics onward he builds in the claim that finite creatures see only a tiny corner of an infinitely interconnected whole, so local ugliness is exactly what we should expect even from the best possible arrangement. Devon shot back that this quietly assumes there's a coherent pattern to be discerned from some vantage point at all -- the very thing Candide puts in doubt, not a premise Leibniz gets to help himself to for free.

Neither budged, and I honestly couldn't tell you who came out ahead. Could you adjudicate the exchange move by move -- who has the better of it at each step, not just overall -- and say what, if anything, you think both of them are missing?
