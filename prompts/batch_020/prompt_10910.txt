For twenty years I have written a detective series that has become the mainstay of my career. My publisher now proposes that it continue indefinitely after I retire: a language model trained on my backlist has produced chapters that blind readers cannot distinguish from my own drafts. The books would keep appearing under my name, and, as my editor keeps pointing out, nothing about any reader's experience would change. I have used human co-writers on other projects without a second thought, yet I find myself unwilling to sign this contract. I cannot name a single sentence in these new chapters that would be worse for having been produced this way. My editor thinks I am simply being sentimental about my own byline. Is she right, or would something real be lost even if no one ever noticed the difference?
