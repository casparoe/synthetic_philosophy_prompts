I've always found puzzles about responsibility and control more interesting in the abstract than in real life, but a situation from my time on a nonprofit's board has been nagging at me for two years now, and I'd value an outside perspective on it. The board convened to vote on whether to remove our executive director after financial irregularities came to light; I deliberated for days and ultimately cast the deciding vote in favor of her removal. I've since learned that our largest funder had privately arranged with legal counsel to invoke a clause in its grant agreement and force her removal unilaterally within the week, had the board instead voted to keep her — the funder was simply waiting to see whether the board would act on its own first. So however I had voted, the eventual outcome would have been exactly the same, even though at the time I reasoned my way to my vote entirely on my own, in complete ignorance of the funder's arrangement. Does it still make sense to hold me responsible for what happened to her, given that the outcome was fixed regardless of my choice — or does the fact that I deliberated honestly and chose for my own reasons mean the responsibility is mine all the same? I'd like to understand what's actually doing the work here, rather than just be told which reaction to trust.
