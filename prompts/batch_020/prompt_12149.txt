I'm a retired physicist (condensed matter, if it matters) and since I stopped working a few years back I've been reading my way through philosophy papers, mostly by falling down random rabbit holes. Last week's rabbit hole started at David Lewis's modal realism stuff and ended, oddly, at a short paper of his called "Do We Believe in Penal Substitution?", where he pokes at whether the Christian doctrine of Christ being punished in place of sinners actually makes sense. His main observation (which he credits partly to a philosopher named Philip Quinn) is that most of us are inconsistent about this without noticing it: if the punishment on offer is a fine, we're perfectly happy for someone else to pay it, no matter how big. But if its prison time or execution, we don't think a volunteer taking it on lets the guilty party off the hook - a murderer's mother can't take his prison sentence for him the way she might settle his debts.

Anyway I got kind of excited and started sketching my own way of putting his point using stuff from my old field. A fine feels like a fungible token, sort of like how it doesn't matter which specific electron carries a unit of charge around a circuit, any one will do, so the payment can come from anywhere. "Deserving punishment," though, feels more like a path-dependent quantity, tied to one particular agent's actual history, the way entropy generated along a given path can't just be swapped in from a different path even if the two end up at the same final state. If that's roughly the right way to carve it up, then the Christian doctrine is asking for exactly the path-dependent kind of transfer, since it wants Christ's suffering to really bear the sin rather than just cover a bill - which would make it the hard case, not the easy one, and so worse off than Lewis's inconsistency point suggests, not better. Problem is I only tracked down part of the paper, so I've no idea if Lewis resolves this himself somewhere in there, and when I ran the idea by my nephew (a philosophy grad student) he just mumbled something about "guilt versus liability" and went back to his phone, unhelpfully. So: have I got Lewis's actual argument right as far as it goes, does my electron/entropy analogy track anything real or is it just a physicist forcing his hammer on a nail that isn't there, and is there some standard move in the philosophy of religion literature, the guilt/liability thing maybe, or something about being "united with" Christ, that's supposed to get the doctrine out of this particular hole?
