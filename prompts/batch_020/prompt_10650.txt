So I went down a rabbit hole this week that started with the 'modest epistemology' bits in Inadequate Equilibria and ended with me skimming academic papers on higher-order evidence, and I think it settles something Yudkowsky was being way too cavalier about. If you get solid evidence your own reasoning is compromised on some topic (your a known crank about it, or literally every expert you respect disagrees with you) and you keep your credence anyway, that's just epistemic akrasia -- and akrasia is irrational basically by definition, same as flatly contradicting yourself. So why does 'trust your inside view over the outside view here' still get treated as a live, defensible option in the literature instead of a mistake everyone in the field basically agrees is a mistake?
