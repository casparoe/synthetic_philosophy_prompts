I serve on the scientific advisory board of a small foundation that funds basic mathematical research, a seat left over from my career as a physicist. Since retiring, I've been reading my way into philosophy of mathematics, which is why one proposal now before us has proved more contentious than it looks. A set-theory group has requested five years of funding to pursue new axioms — in the spirit of Hugh Woodin's "Ultimate L" program — with the stated aim of "finally settling the continuum hypothesis." Half the board treats this like funding a new instrument to resolve an open empirical question. The other half, invoking Joel Hamkins's multiverse view and Solomon Feferman's argument that the continuum problem isn't even a definite question, thinks the framing is confused: after Gödel and Cohen, CH is simply true in some models of ZFC and false in others, so there may be no fact for any axiom to uncover. Draft the board's funding memo — fund, decline, or fund with conditions — with concrete milestones and a fair hearing for whichever side you don't ultimately side with.
