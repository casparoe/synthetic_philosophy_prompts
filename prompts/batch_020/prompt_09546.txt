So a few weeks back I went down a rabbit hole reading old posts on this AI-safety forum (LessWrong, dunno if you know it) about how two super advanced AIs might end up negotiating with each other someday, and I got completely stuck on this idea people call the "commitment race" problem. From what I pieced together: in a lot of bargaining setups, whoever manages to lock themselves into a hardline position FIRST basically wins, kind of like the old Dr. Strangelove doomsday-machine logic, or the guy who rips the steering wheel off in a game of chicken so the other driver has no choice but to swerve. So the argument seems to be that if two agents both understand this dynamic, each one wants to be the one to commit first and as fast/hard as possible, before they've even properly thought the situation through - because the agent who hangs back to reason carefully is the "sucker" who ends up accepting the worse deal, or worse, both sides just plow into each other because they committed to incompatible hardline stances. What I don't get is WHY this has to turn into a reckless race instead of the two sides just... coordinating on committing at the same time, or agreeing to think it over together first. It seems like something a genuinely rational agent should definitly be able to route around, so I feel like I'm missing a piece of the logic.

There's also this other bit I saw mentioned almost in passing that I can't stop chewing on: that even the act of REASONING about your bargaining strategy can itself count as a kind of commitment, so an agent can't necessarily protect itself just by deciding to "think it over carefully before committing to anything," because the thinking itself might already lock something in. My friend, who's read way more decision theory than me, said this connects to some stuff about updateless decision theory, but that lost me completely. Could you walk me through, in plain terms, why the incentives actually push rational agents toward this hasty race rather then calm coordination, and also unpack that recursive "reasoning is already a commitment" worry for me - like is that a real, load-bearing part of the argument, or am I misremembering something I half-read at 1am?
