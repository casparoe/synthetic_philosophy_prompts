So I fell down a rabbit hole a while back reading about Hugh Woodin's "Ultimate L" program and it's been rattling around in my head ever since, to the point my friends are sick of me bringing it up at dinner. Quick recap as I understand it (correct me if I'm garbling it): back around the late 90s/2000s Woodin built up this whole Omega-logic apparatus and used it to argue CH is probably *false* -- that any "reasonable" extension of ZFC with enough large cardinals would end up forcing CH false, and it got treated as maybe the strongest "new axiom" case anyone had made for settling an independent question. Then over the following decade he basically reverses himself: he starts developing "Ultimate L," a souped-up L-like canonical model that's compatible with large cardinals (unlike plain old V=L), and since L-ish models satisfy CH, he ends up arguing more or less the opposite conclusion. Meanwhile you've got the Hamkins multiverse camp, who look at that reversal plus the general zoo of forcing constructions that can push the continuum to basically whatever aleph you like, and conclude the whole premise is confused -- there's no privileged background universe for CH to be true or false *in*, just a multiverse of equally legitimate models, and "new axioms" are really just us picking which universe we like rather than detecting facts about one. Kind of surprised this doesn't come up more on LW/ACX given how much that crowd loves a good realism-vs-anti-realism fight.

I want to see these two camps actually argue it out instead of me reconstructing the debate solo from SEP and old Quanta articles. Could you write me a dialogue between a "universist" character -- who thinks there's one true cumulative hierarchy of sets, CH has a determinate truth value, and Woodin-style axiom-hunting (occasional embarassing reversals included) counts as genuine, fallible inquiry into that fact -- and a "multiversist" character in the Hamkins mold, who thinks the reversal itself is evidence there's no fact to hunt for, and that once you've mapped out the forcing extensions you've basically said everything there is to say about CH. Don't let either one win by the other just going quiet: the multiversist should really press on stuff like what would even count as evidence for Ultimate L being "correct" rather than merely elegant, and why we should trust intuitions (Freiling's dart-throwing symmetry argument, Godels own hunches) that have flipped before; and the universist needs actual comebacks, not just reasserted Platonism -- e.g. analogies to ordinary scientific theory change, or pushing the multiversist on why some models (ones satisfying PFA or Martin's Maximum, say, which pin the continuum at aleph-2) still seem to strike multiversists as more "canonical" than others even on a pluralist view. Keep it tight, a couple pages, and make sure they're actually responding to each other's last point each round instead of delivering pre-packaged monologues.
