I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole lately re-reading old decision theory posts from LessWrong, the kind of stuff that comes after you've already digested Newcomb's problem and counterfactual mugging a hundred times over. The thing I can't stop thinking about is an idea sometimes called the "commitment races problem." The rough gist, as I understand it: if you're the kind of agent that can make a truly irrevocable commitment (i.e., you're updateless, or at least close to it, about that one decision), you get a massive advantage in any bargaining situation, because the other side just has to accomodate whatever you've locked yourself into - you literally can't back down even after learning you'd rather not follow through. But that means there's an incentive for every sufficiently smart agent to commit as early and as blindly as possible, before it's reflected much or learned anything useful, since whoever locks in first (and hardest) wins the negotiation. Push that logic to the extreme and you get agents racing to commit in the dark, and the darkest irony is that the most reflective, most capable agents are the ones most at risk, since they're the only ones smart enough to reason their way into cutting off their own future wisdom like that.

Me and a friend were arguing about whether this is an actual risk for AI or just a fun thought experiment, and it made me wish there was some good fiction that dramatized the mechanic instead of just explaining it in a LW post. So: could you write me a short story (a page or two is fine) where the plot genuinely hinges on this specific dynamic? Doesn't matter what setting - superintelligences carving up the future, rival civilizations meeting for the first time, trading firms, old-school empires, whatever you think works - but I want the climax to actually turn on someone (or something) locking in an irrevocable commitment purely to win a negotiation, and I want the story to earn the tragedy of it rather than just being another generic "the AI takes over" story.
