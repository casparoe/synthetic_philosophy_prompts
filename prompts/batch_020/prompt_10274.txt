(2) Marcus came back with the Mach line: it's not absolute space doing the work, it's rotation relative to all the rest of the matter in the universe (the "fixed stars," as the old phrase goes), and if you deleted everything else in the cosmos there'd be nothing left for the water to spin relative to — so the bucket by itself can't tell absolute space apart from rotation-relative-to-everything-else. (3) Ilana's comeback was that general relativity actually settles this against Mach, since GR allows a single rotating body out in otherwise empty space to still drag inertial frames around with it — no distant matter required — so "relative to what other matter" can't be the whole story, and something like spacetime structure itself has to be doing real work. (4) Marcus granted that but said it doesn't hand the debate to old-fashioned substantivalism either, because of the hole argument: if spacetime points are genuine individuals in their own right, independent of what's happening at them, GR's diffeomorphism invariance lets you generate two "different" solutions out of the exact same physical setup, which is a vicious kind of indeterminism — so anyone who wants to keep believing in substantival spacetime after GR has to retreat to some thinner "sophisticated" version, and that's a seperate debate from the one about rotation that Ilana thinks she just won. (5) Ilana's closing shot was that a thinner substantivalism is still substantivalism — spacetime is still a real thing over and above bodies and their relations — so the goalposts moved, but Marcus still hasn't actually vindicated relationism about anything.
