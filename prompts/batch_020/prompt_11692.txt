I cover science and ideas for a living, which apparently made me qualified to be the "community" seat on my university's human research ethics board. We've got a case I cant stop turning over: a genetics team wants to build a biobank from an entire villiage in rural KwaZulu-Natal for a heart-disease study, and individual consent forms are all drafted and approved as usual -- except the local induna won't let researchers near a single household until the community council gives its own collective sign-off first. His reasoning, relayed through the translator, is basically ubuntu 101: nobody's blood or data is theirs alone to give away, since it says as much about their kin as it does about them. Half the board wants to just log the council's blessing as a nice extra on top of individual consent as normal; the other half says requiring council approval before anyone's even approached turns "consent" into permission-with-extra-steps, since good luck refusing in front of your chief. I need a short recommendation memo to bring to the full board next week -- an actual position, not "let's consult more" -- that takes the relational-personhood argument seriously as a reason individual consent alone might not cut it here, but doesn't dodge what we'd be trading away if the council can effectively consent on people's behalf. Can you draft it?
