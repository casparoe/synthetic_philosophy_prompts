As a software engineer, I recently came across Ned Block's "China Brain" (or "Chinese Nation") thought experiment: the proposal that if an entire country's population were organized -- each citizen given a simple rule to follow and a radio to relay messages to a few designated others -- so as to reproduce, message for message, the pattern of neuron firings that occurs in a human brain during (say) an episode of feeling pain, then functionalism seems committed to holding that the resulting system has genuine mental states, including conscious experience. This struck me as essentially a distributed system simulating a neural network, implemented in people rather than silicon, with radio transmissions standing in for synaptic signals. Write a short story set inside such a system, narrated either by one of the "neurons" or by the emergent mind itself, that dramatizes the tension between the functionalist verdict that the system is conscious and the strong intuition that it is not. Rather than resolving the question explicitly, let the narrative itself imply a position on just how far multiple realizability can be stretched before it breaks down.
