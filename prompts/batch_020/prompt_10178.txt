Two friends from my rationalist-adjacent reading group had a dinner argument I couldn't settle, prompted by something real: one friend's cousin, Mara, had promised her dying grandfather she'd scatter his ashes at a lake he loved rather than use the family plot he'd already bought. No one else knew about the promise, he could never find out either way, and keeping it cost Mara real friction with the rest of the family. Owen and Nadia disagreed about why, if at all, she was still bound.

(1) Owen said it obviously binds, for the reason any promise binds: giving your word creates an obligation to the person you gave it to, whether or not they ever learn what became of it.

(2) Nadia objected: if Mara had privately resolved, in the same terms, to scatter the ashes there but never told her grandfather, no one would call that a binding promise -- so it must be what her words did to him, not her resolve alone, that generates the obligation.

(3) Owen granted this but denied it helps Nadia: whatever her words did to him, they did it while he was alive -- they gave him real comfort, and reneging now retroactively makes that comfort rest on a lie. The obligation was fixed the moment he believed her; his dying afterward shouldn't cancel it any more than a creditor's death cancels a debt, which just passes to the estate.

(4) Nadia replied that this backfires: a debt passes to the estate because the estate is a determinate successor, worse off if unpaid. Nothing plays that role here -- if Mara broke her word in secret, no one living or dead would be worse off in any nameable respect, yet both agreed she must still keep it. That, she said, suggests the real ground is something like a duty not to betray trust even when the betrayal is undetectable in principle -- a stranger, stronger claim than either meant to defend.

Could you work through (1)-(4), saying who has the better of it at each step rather than just who wins overall, and flag any premise either leans on that doesn't hold up? I'd also be curious which existing account of why promises bind, if any, comes out looking best.
