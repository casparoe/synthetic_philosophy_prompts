I've spent a good deal of time trying to think through updateless decision theory on my own, mostly by turning the counterfactual mugging thought experiment over in my head until it stopped feeling like a trick question and started feeling like a real dilemma. The core of it: a perfect predictor flips a coin, and because of how it landed, you are asked to hand over a modest sum of money for no reason that will do you any good now — the only justification is that had the coin landed the other way, you would have received a much larger reward, but solely on condition that you were the sort of agent who would pay up in exactly this situation. Nothing is left to cause or reveal at the moment of the request; only a policy fixed in advance is doing the work, and the question is whether you honor it anyway. I'd like a short story of around 800 to 1,000 words set inside that moment, ideally involving an artificial agent that was deliberately designed to reason this way, so the story can also carry the unease of whoever built something that keeps its word even when nothing remains to be gained and no one is left to notice. Please don't let it turn into a dialogue that simply explains the theory to the reader — I'd rather the dilemma show up in what the character does and what it costs.
