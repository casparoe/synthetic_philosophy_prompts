I'm working on a novel with a Vienna Circle-adjacent subplot, and one of my characters is basically Otto Neurath with the serial numbers filed off. Digging through the background reading for him turned up something that bugs me and that none of my source books really tackle head-on. Neurath's most famous line is the boat metaphor: we're sailors rebuilding the ship at sea, no dry dock, no plank safe from being pulled up and replaced -- there's no neutral, foundational sentence anywhere in science. But for years he was also building Isotype, his picture-statistics system, on the explicit selling point that images dodge the vagueness and emotional baggage of words and just gives you a cleaner, more universal look at the facts. Can you argue those two sides of him are actually in real tension -- that his own anti-foundationalism should've made him more suspicious of pictures getting you closer to "just the facts," not less? Don't let him off easy with "Isotype was only ever meant as a supplement, not a replacement" -- I want the version of the argument that survives that reply.
