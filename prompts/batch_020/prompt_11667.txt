A friend and I got into a long argument after our decision-theory reading group last night about program equilibrium, and neither of us left satisfied. We agreed on the starting point, which goes back to Tennenholtz's original work on the subject: if players in a game submit programs rather than strategies — programs that can inspect one another's source code before acting — then cooperative outcomes that are unreachable in ordinary Nash equilibrium can suddenly be sustained as equilibria in their own right. My friend's illustration of this was the crude version: a program that cooperates only when it detects that the other program's source code is an exact, or near-exact, copy of its own, and defects otherwise. I objected that this feels like a cheap trick rather than a genuine model of mutual reasoning, since it is fragile to any cosmetic rewriting of the code and gives no reason to think it would generalize to programs that reason about each other in more open-ended ways.

Where we really got stuck was on the fix I half-remembered for this problem: instead of checking for a matching source code, each program searches for a formal proof that its counterpart cooperates with it, and cooperates if and only if such a proof turns up. I know this is supposed to be the more principled solution, and I recall it being tied to Löb's theorem from provability logic via something people call a "Löbian handshake," but I could not reconstruct for my friend why the whole scheme doesn't just collapse into circularity: for program A's proof search to succeed, doesn't it need to reason about program B's proof search, which is itself trying to establish something about A's behavior, and so on without end? Neither of us could say what work Löb's theorem is actually doing here, as opposed to its being a convenient piece of formal machinery that happens to make the conclusion pop out at the end. Could you explain, as intuitively as possible and without leaning too heavily on the formal logic, what actually blocks the regress, and in what sense the resulting mutual cooperation is genuinely secured rather than simply assumed into existence?
