For three years, five other novelists and I have run *The Long Armistice*, a subscription serial published one chapter at a time, with each month's installment written by whichever of us holds the "turn." We drew up a short charter early on — quickly, and a little giddily, the same evening we agreed to try this — meant to keep our private plans from colliding on the page. One clause says that no author may retcon or foreclose another author's established plot thread without consensus at our monthly plotting call; a later clause says that each author retains final creative authority over any character they originated. Nobody has ever had to ask which clause would win if they conflicted, because until now they never did.

They've now conflicted. Elena, who created and has always written the soldier at the center of one of our viewpoint threads, submitted this month's chapter without raising it on the call: in a single paragraph, it reveals that he secretly died off-page several installments ago. Two of our other writers have spent the past year building a redemption arc for him out of their own characters' storylines, and both threads are closed by a sentence neither of them saw coming. Elena's position is that the character was always hers to end, charter or no charter, and that the consensus clause was only ever meant to catch scheduling conflicts and continuity slips, not decisions an originating author makes about her own creation. The other two say that signing the charter meant none of us could unilaterally decide, on our own authority, which of its clauses actually bound us. I sit on the three-person council our charter empowers to settle exactly this kind of dispute, and I chair it; the chapter is due to subscribers in nine days.

Could you draft the council's ruling? I'd like a specific recommendation — publish the chapter as submitted, hold it for revision, or something else — with a deadline attached, and I'd like it to take Elena's reading of the charter seriously on its own terms rather than dismiss it as an obvious violation, since I'm honestly not certain our own document settles who's right.
