My colleague Helen is retiring this spring after almost forty years, and somehow I've ended up giving the toast at her dinner. Here's the wrinkle: Helen's whole research career has been on standing to blame -- when you actually get to call someone out and when you should just keep quiet -- and for a good chunk of that career she was also the person in our department who called people out, formally, including me once, in writing, for being chronically late to a whole semester of faculty meetings. Tonight I'd like to gently roast her for being chronically late to everything herself, which means the philosophers in the room are going to clock my hypocrisy problem before I even finish the sentence, and Helen -- this is the annoying part -- is going to be sitting right there doing the standing-to-blame math in real time, because its literally her specialty. I want the toast to take that on directly rather than dodge it -- Cohen's old point that a hypocrite can still be dead right about the charge even if they've got no business making it is roughly the shape I'm imagining -- and still land as affection instead of a seminar. Half the room is philosophy faculty who'll be tracking every move, the other half is her family and a couple of deans who just want a nice five minutes. Can you write me that toast?
