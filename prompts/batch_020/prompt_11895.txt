As a novelist, I was recently offered a considerable sum by a university archive for the marked-up typescripts and notebooks behind my last three books: pages full of crossed-out lines, margin notes, and the occasional coffee ring. The offer moved me more than any review has. My agent thinks I'm being sentimental: since everything I write begins on a word processor, these particular sheets are just one printed pass among many, and a fine enough scan of each page — cross-outs, stains, and all — would preserve everything a future scholar could want. Suppose such scans were made and the pages themselves were then lost in a fire: would the archive have lost anything truly worth caring about, or only some sentimental props of mine? I can't decide whether my attachment to these sheets tracks something real about creative work and its history, or whether it's simply a prejudice I've never had reason to examine.
