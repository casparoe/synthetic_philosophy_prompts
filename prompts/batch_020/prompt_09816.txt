A few months ago I fell down an internet rabbit hole about where the little pictograms on things like flat-pack furniture manuals, hazard labels, and airport signs actually came from, and that eventually led me to Otto Neurath and the ISOTYPE project he ran in Vienna in the 1920s and 30s. I don't have any background in philosophy — I got into this purely because I design packaging for a small outdoor-gear business I run — but once I started reading his own essays on what he called "international picture language," I realized his interest went well beyond graphic design. He was making fairly pointed philosophical claims about the differences between picture language and word language: that pictures have no real grammar of their own, that they struggle to convey things like negation, tense, or connectives such as "if…then" and "or," and that a picture, unlike a word, can never stay fully general or abstract because it always has to depict something concrete and particular. Interestingly, he still thought picture language was worth developing seriously as a supplement to words rather than a rival to them, even for fairly technical and statistical information.

I'm currently trying to design a set of wordless assembly and safety instructions for one of my products, intended to work for customers regardless of what language they read, and I keep running headlong into the exact issues Neurath apparently flagged: how do you show "do not do this," or "do this step only if that other thing is true," or a general rule as opposed to one particular case, using only pictures? Could you set out what you take to be the most important implications of Neurath's views on the limits of picture language for a project like mine? I'd especially value your sense of which of the limitations he pointed to are genuinely unavoidable features of any picture-based system, and which ones might really just be artifacts of 1930s printing technology and could plausibly be worked around today with things like sequential panels, colour, or simple animation.
