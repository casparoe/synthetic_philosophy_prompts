Make sure to explain why Tim is in one sense able to kill Grandfather and in another sense unable to."

Here's the answer one of my students turned in, can you grade it:

"According to Lewis, 'can' statements are always relative to a background set of facts we're holding fixed, and the Grandfather Paradox only looks like a contradiction because it quietly switches between two different such backgrounds. Tim, our time traveler, goes back to a point before Grandfather sired Tim's father, gun in hand. In one sense Tim can kill Grandfather: he's a decent shot, has a clear opportunity, and nothing in the immediate situation is stopping him. In another sense Tim can't: it's already a fact that Grandfather lived long enough to have a son, so any scenario where Tim succeeds isn't compossible with the rest of the actual facts about Tim's own ancestry (a term Lewis coins to describe when two or more things can jointly hold true of one possible world). Both claims are true - they're just relativized to different sets of held-fixed facts, Tim's personal skills and situation versus the entire fixed course of history. So there's no real contradiction, just an equivocation on 'can'. Lewis's solution requires that the instant Tim pulls the trigger, his personal timeline branches off from the original one: in the new branch Grandfather dies, while the original timeline (the one where Tim gets born) stays intact and unaltered. That's what lets both the 'can' and 'can't' be true without contradiction. He also distinguishes 'personal time' - the time Tim experiences, ticking forward through the trip - from 'external time', the ordinary timeline of the world, which is how he explains why Tim meeting his younger self isn't paradoxical either."
