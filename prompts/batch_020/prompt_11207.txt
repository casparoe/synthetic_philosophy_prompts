Been down a rabbit hole for a piece on AI doomerism and keep bumping into von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay 'Can We Survive Technology?' What strikes me is that his big worry wasn't really nukes specifically -- he treats the bomb as just the first case of a general problem, and spends a good chunk of the essay on the idea that weather/climate control could be an equally dangerous technology, partly because it'd be so much easier to weaponize quietly than a bomb is. He's also pretty dismissive of the idea that treaties or prohibition can save us, partly because the globe had gotten too 'small' (fast travel and communication meaning there's no buffer zones between rivals anymore), and he frames the whole situation as a permanent condition we just muddle through with patience and flexibility, rather then a problem that has a final fix. Given all that, what do you think are the most important implications of von Neumann's actual argument (not just 'technology is scary') for how we should evaluate today's proposals to pause or heavily regulate AI development? Trying to work out whether, on his own logic, he'd have been more of a pause guy or an accelerationist, basically.
