In my seminar on formal epistemology and AI safety, we've been discussing the Löbian obstacle facing self-modifying AI systems, which arises because Löb's theorem generalizes Gödel's second incompleteness theorem. Since Löb's theorem shows that a consistent formal system can never prove, from within itself, that everything it proves is true, doesn't this mean an AI agent can never have any principled basis for trusting a successor that reasons in the same formal system—making self-trusting formal agents impossible in principle? If so, I don't understand why researchers keep proposing workarounds like proof-system hierarchies or bounded reflection schemes instead of treating the question as closed.
