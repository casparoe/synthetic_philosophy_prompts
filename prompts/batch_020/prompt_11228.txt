Three weeks ago, an at-home DNA test I took mostly out of curiosity about my family history revealed that the man who raised me, whose surname I still carry, is not my biological father. Nothing about my day-to-day life has changed: my memories, relationships, and sense of self feel exactly as they did before I opened the results. A close friend has been reassuring me that this is all the discovery amounts to — a new true belief about myself, no different in kind from learning some obscure fact about a great-grandparent I never met. But when I picture things having gone differently at conception, with my mother instead conceiving with the man I grew up calling my father, it doesn't seem like I would simply have ended up with different paperwork; it seems like I would never have existed, and someone else would be living the life I call mine. If that's right, then which man is my biological father starts to look like the most basic fact there could be about me, which makes my friend's reassurance feel too quick. I can't tell whether she's right and I'm overthinking this, or whether my own reaction is tracking something her explanation overlooks — which of us has it right?
