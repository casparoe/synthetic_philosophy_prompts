A friend and I argued long into the night about inverse reinforcement learning, and I think I won, though they wouldn't concede it. Their position was that no amount of behavioral data could ever pin down a person's true preferences, even in the limit of infinite observations; the best defense they could muster was the vague claim that "people aren't perfectly rational," which struck me as weak given that IRL methods already exist to accommodate noisy or boundedly rational behavior. If a rich enough behavioral record, paired with a sensible model of how preferences translate into action, converges on the true reward function the same way any well-specified statistical model converges as samples accumulate, then inferring what someone wants from what they do is ultimately a data-and-engineering problem rather than a principled limitation. So why do people in AI alignment keep treating reward inference from behavior as fundamentally underdetermined, rather than as a matter of eventually collecting rich enough behavioral datasets?
