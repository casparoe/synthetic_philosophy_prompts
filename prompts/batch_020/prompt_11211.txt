Here's the question:

Richard Jeffrey generalized Bayesian conditionalization to handle evidence that doesn't come to us with certainty. (a) State Jeffrey's updating rule and the "rigidity" condition it depends on. (b) Use Jeffrey's own cloth-by-candlelight case to illustrate how this differs from ordinary conditionalization on some proposition E. (c) If an agent updates on two separate uncertain observations, one after the other, does the order she updates in change her final posterior? Justify your answer.

And here's my partner's answer:

Jeffrey's rule says that when an uncertain observation shifts your credence in E to some new value P'(E), your new probability for any hypothesis H should be P'(H) = P(H|E)P'(E) + P(H|¬E)P'(¬E). This generalizes ordinary conditionalization, which is just the special case where P'(E) = 1. The rigidity condition is that P(H|E) and P(H|¬E) have to stay fixed across the update - only your credence in E itself is allowed to move, not how H depends on E.

For the cloth case: Jeffrey's agent examines a piece of cloth by candlelight and it looks green, but the light's bad enough that she can't rule out blue or violet - say she ends up 0.7 confident it's green. She can't conditionalize on "the cloth is green" because she's never certain of it; instead she plugs her new credence for green straight into the weighted-average rule above to update whatever else she cares about, like whether it'll go with a jacket she already owns.

Since Jeffrey conditionalization is just a weighted-average version of regular conditionalization, and regular conditionalization is order-independent (conditionalizing on E1 then E2 gets you the same posterior as conditionalizing on E2 then E1), the same has to be true here. So no, order doesn't matter - once she's incorporated both observations her final credences are the same either way, which is part of what makes it a rational updating rule instead of just a heuristic.
