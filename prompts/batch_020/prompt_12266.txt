My friend and I had a long argument last week that I still have not been able to resolve. A mutual friend of ours, who is Black, told us about being called a racial slur by a stranger, and in recounting the story she used the actual word rather than a euphemism. A few days later, my friend retold the same incident to someone who had not heard it, and he too spoke the word out loud, verbatim, insisting he was merely quoting what had been said. I found his retelling considerably harder to sit through than hers, even though the sentence itself was identical in both cases and both of them were simply reporting something that had actually happened. When I said as much, my friend argued that my reaction was inconsistent: if quoting the slur to report an incident is acceptable, it should not matter who is doing the quoting. Is he right, or is there a principled reason to treat the two retellings differently?
