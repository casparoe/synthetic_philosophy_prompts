Since retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have recently been working my way through the literature on formal epistemology and game theory, and I keep returning to Robert Aumann's proof that common knowledge of rationality forces the backward-induction outcome in any finite game of perfect information. In the centipede game, this implies that the first mover, if genuinely rational, is required to end the game on the very first move, even though a few further rounds of mutual cooperation would leave both players considerably better off. Yet the experimental record is unambiguous: most human subjects continue passing for several rounds before taking the money, rather than defecting immediately as the theorem demands. Given such a sharp theoretical prediction alongside a robust, well-replicated experimental failure to meet it, shouldn't this count as about as clean a demonstration of systematic human irrationality as the behavioral sciences have to offer?
