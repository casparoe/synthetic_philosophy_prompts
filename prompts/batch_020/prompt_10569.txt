I quit my analyst job back in March to try to make an actual living playing poker, and it's been going ok so far, my win rate at 2/5 live cash has held up over a decent sample. Recently an old college friend who's now a quant at a hedge fund offered to stake me: he'd front 40% of my buy-ins for the next six months in exchange for 40% of whatever I win (or lose). Separate from that offer, he's also been giving me a hard time about how I size my bankroll in general. Like most players I know, I stick to the rule of thumb of never having more then about 5% of my total bankroll in play at once, but he thinks thats way too timid given my actual winrate, and that anyone who really wants to maximize their money would play noticeably bigger.

The argument he keeps making is that if you imagine a thousand copies of me all playing the exact same way over the exact same stretch of hands, the copies playing bigger end up ahead on average by a mile, so playing smaller than that is basically just leaving money on the table out of nervousness rather than for any good reason. I don't have a sharp comeback to this, but it also bugs me, partly because I've personally watched two solid players go completely bust playing sizes that were, on paper, still favorable bets. I tried looking around online for a real counterargument and mostly found either bankroll "rules of thumb" with no justification given, or finance papers way over my head. So: is my friend basically right that the "imagine a thousand parallel yous and average the outcome" way of thinking is what a rational person should use to decide how big to play, or is he missing something by applying it to one actual person playing hand after hand? And does taking him up on the staking deal change what size I should be comfortable playing, independent of whatever the "rational" answer turns out to be?
