Here is my classmate's answer in full:

"Fitch's paradox of knowability is a proof in modal epistemic logic showing that a seemingly modest thesis collapses into an absurd one. Let K stand for 'it is known by someone at some time that,' and let ◊ be the usual possibility operator. The Knowability Principle (KP) says every truth is at least knowable in principle: ∀p(p → ◊Kp). The Non-Omniscience assumption (NO) says merely that not everything true is actually known: ∃p(p ∧ ¬Kp) — there is at least one true proposition nobody ever comes to know.

The proof starts by establishing Fitch's Lemma: for any proposition p, it's impossible to know that 'p is true and p is unknown.' Suppose, for contradiction, that someone knows p ∧ ¬Kp. Since knowledge distributes over conjunction, Kp and K¬Kp both hold. But knowledge is factive — if you know something, it's true — so K¬Kp guarantees ¬Kp. Putting these together gives Kp and ¬Kp, a flat contradiction. So K(p ∧ ¬Kp) can never hold, for any p, and neither can ◊K(p ∧ ¬Kp).

Now apply KP to NO. NO says some specific true proposition has the form p ∧ ¬Kp. If KP is right, that truth should be knowable: ◊K(p ∧ ¬Kp). But Fitch's Lemma just ruled that out. So once you run the argument all the way through, KP together with NO actually forces the much stronger conclusion that every truth is known, ∀p(p → Kp) — which is far more than anyone signed up for.

Realists are the ones who want to hold on to the Knowability Principle, since it's realism, after all, that insists truth has to answer to the way things actually are rather than to what we happen to have evidence for; the paradox therefore works as an internal critique of realism, not of the anti-realist or verificationist picture it's usually paired with. That's what makes it a 'paradox' rather than a boring disproof: a thesis its own proponents find attractive turns out to lead somewhere nobody wants to go.

The most influential attempt to block the argument is due to Neil Tennant. Tennant accepts the logic of the derivation but denies that knowledge is really factive in the problem case: he argues the link between K and truth should be suspended specifically for Moore-paradoxical conjunctions like p ∧ ¬Kp, which lets him keep an unrestricted Knowability Principle everywhere else while blocking the slide into universal omniscience."
