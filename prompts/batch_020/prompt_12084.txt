So my friend Jae and I got into an argument last night that spiraled way further than it should have (it started because her coworker got chewed out for being late by a manager who's notoriously even later to everything himself). It turned into a two-hour back-and-forth about whether hypocrites actually loose their "standing" to blame people, or whether that's just a nice-sounding idea people use to wave away criticism from folks they don't like. I've been reading Cohen's stuff on "silencing" and something like Wallace's equal-standing response to him for a seminar this semester, so I kept trying to frame it in those terms, and Jae basically ended up defending Wallace's side: blaming someone for X while doing X yourself, unrepentantly, isnt just unfair or annoying - it makes the blame fail as an act of moral address, full stop, regardless of whether X is actually wrong. On her view the blame doesn't even "land," independent of its truth.

My position was that this is overkill - there's no extra deontic status called "standing" that gets stripped away on top of everything else. All the work is really done by two much more boring things: (1) the hypocrisy is its own separate wrong (unfairness, exempting yourself from a rule you enforce on others), and (2) it's decent evidence you don't actually sincerely hold the norm you're invoking. The content of the blame itself - "you shouldn't have done X" - stays exactly as true and exactly as authoritative no matter who utters it; we're just (rightly) annoyed at the messenger for reasons that have nothing to do with the message. Jae's best counterexample, which I still haven't found a clean answer to: picture a guy who cheated on every partner he had for a decade, has now been faithful for five straight years (so by any reasonable measure he's sincerely converted to actually believing cheating is wrong), but has never once acknowledged to any of his exes that what he did was messed up - and he now goes around lecturing his friends about "loyalty." Jae's intuition is that this guy clearly still lacks full standing, even though under my view both boxes are already checked (sincerity: check, the wrong itself: safely in the past). I couldn't explain that gap without basically conceding that standing is doing real, independent work after all.

Can you build the strongest possible version of my side of this - that "standing to blame" reduces without any leftover to (separate wrong of the hypocrisy) + (evidence of insincerity) and nothing else - and make sure it actually engages with the unapologetic-reformed-cheater case instead of dodging it? And then, honestly, tell me which way you'd bet if you had to referee: does my reductive story survive that case, or does something like Jae's "equal standing" picture end up necessary after all to explain why the missing apology still seems to matter?
