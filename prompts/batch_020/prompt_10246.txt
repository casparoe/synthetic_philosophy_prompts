Hey, so I'm on my university's honor council (the student panel that hears academic integrity cases), and this week we ran into something that's basically the discursive dilemma from my social choice theory seminar - I couldn't believe it.

Our code says a student is responsible for a violation iff (a) they submitted work that wasn't their own, and (b) they knew or clearly should've known that was against the rules. Three of us heard a case and split:
- Me: a=yes, b=no -> not responsible
- Panelist 2: a=no, b=yes -> not responsible
- Panelist 3: a=yes, b=yes -> responsible

Vote on the final verdict and it's 2-1 not responsible. Vote issue by issue instead and both (a) and (b) get a majority yes, which by the code means responsible. Same three votes, opposite result depending how you count them. Our bylaws don't say which way we're suppose to vote, and now the council's split between doing case-by-case from now on vs. always issue-by-issue.

Can you draft the memo I'd bring to next weeks meeting? I need an actual recommendation, not just "it depends," it has to take the strongest argument for whichever option it rejects seriously rather than hand-wave it, and it should say whether we redo the vote on the case we already heard or only apply the fix going forward.
