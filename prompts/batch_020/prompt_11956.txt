Ok this is going to sound very "extremely online rationalist" but bear with me. A few people from my decision theory reading group (I'm doing a PhD in philosophy, mostly formal epistemology/decision theory) got into it again over Roko's basilisk last week, and everyone just recited the same canned answer without really engaging with it, so I want an outside take that actually takes the argument seriously.

The "official" LessWrong dissolution goes something like this: a sufficiently rational agent running something like Yudkowsky's TDT or Wei Dai's UDT would, as a matter of policy, just refuse to ever respond to acausal blackmail, and since a rational future AI could predict that refusal in advance, it would never bother constructing the threat to begin with, so the whole basilisk unravels before it even gets going. One friend kept invoking Parfit's hitchhiker as the "clean" version of the underlying logic, which I get. But when you object "hang on, I've already heard the argument, so how can I retroactively adopt that policy now?", the standard comeback is that updatelessness is a logical, not chronological, notion, so choosing right now to refuse is just as much "implementing the policy" as if you'd committed to it from birth.

That comeback has always felt slightly too convenient to me though, like its trading on an equivocation between an idealized agent optimizing over a fixed prior from the start, and actual bounded reasoners like us, who are, factually, already mid-update by the time we encounter the argument. So here's what I'd like: please argue, as rigorously as you can, that this standard "just precommit to ignore blackmail" rebuttal does NOT actually succeed in defusing Roko's basilisk for agents like us, and that the "logical not chronological priority" move is either question-begging or covertly changes the subject away from the psychological/practical problem the basilisk was originally supposed to pose. Feel free to engage with the strongest version of the LW response rather than a strawman of it - I already know the weak version fails, I want the real fight.
