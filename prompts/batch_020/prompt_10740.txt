A friend and I spent most of dinner last night arguing about risk-weighted expected utility theory, and neither of us gave much ground. He thinks Buchak's risk function is just Quiggin's rank-dependent utility in normative dress -- take the probability-weighting curve that Kahneman and Tversky treated as evidence of bias, rename it a rational 'global' attitude toward a gamble's shape, and you've redescribed the Allais paradox rather than explained why it's rational. I argued that separating this 'global' sensitivity to a gamble's overall shape from the 'local,' outcome-by-outcome curvature of the utility function is a genuine advance, not a relabeling. Neither of us settled the dynamic-choice worry: once a gamble whose shape you supposedly care about is cashed out as a sequence of smaller choices spread over time, does avoiding the resulting money pump cost the theory more than it buys.
