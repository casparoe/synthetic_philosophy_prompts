I'm giving the toast at my philosophy professor's retirement dinner; I'm a student in her seminar on ancient economic thought this term, which happens to be the last course she'll ever teach. She has spent nearly forty years teaching Aristotle's *Politics*, above all his argument that lending money at interest is the most unnatural of all ways of getting wealth, since the Greek word for interest, *tokos*, literally means "offspring" -- money, unlike livestock, isn't supposed to breed. A good number of the guests are former students of hers who now work in finance and will catch a joke built on *tokos* immediately; the rest of the room, including her family and colleagues from other departments, won't know a word of Greek. Write a five-minute toast built around one good joke about money "breeding" that works on both levels at once, and that lands as real affection for her rather than as a dig at anyone's career choice or a recap of the seminar.
