I'm a citizen member of my state's gaming control board, on the subcommittee that runs the voluntary self-exclusion program for problem gamblers. Participants can bar themselves from every casino in the state for one year, five years, or life, with no way back once the lifetime option is chosen. A man who chose it two years ago, after his gambling had cost him his marriage and savings, has now petitioned for reinstatement, citing a completed treatment program and a psychologist's assessment that he no longer meets the criteria for gambling disorder. Some subcommittee members want to grant the petition and build a formal early-release process for similar cases; others argue this defeats the entire point of offering a lifetime option, since a person in the grip of the disorder will always feel recovered enough to gamble safely again -- precisely the self-assessment the program exists to override. Please draft the subcommittee's decision memo: rule on his petition, recommend whether an early-release process should exist going forward and on what conditions, and give a fair account of why the strongest objection to ever allowing one might still be right.
