My uncle passed away in August and left a chunk of money to a tiny family foundation he'd run out of his study for like 20 years, and somehow I've inherited his seat on the board -- its just me, my cousin, and his old buisness partner Hal. We have to decide by our call on the 30th where this year's roughly $80k in grants goes, and it's come down to two finalists: Against Malaria Foundation (bednets, the GiveWell darling, extremely well-evidenced cost per life saved) or a scholarship fund for kids in our uncle's hometown. My cousin's dead set on AMF because the evidence is so much stronger. Hal keeps pushing back that we actually have no idea whether saving lives via bednets is even net good once you look downstream -- more surviving kids means more people consuming stuff, more animals farmed, more emissions, who knows -- and that nobody can honestly sign the net effect, so treating "lives saved" as the bottom line is kind of a shell game. My cousin's counter is that the same objection applies just as much to the scholarship fund, which also has totally unknowable ripple effects down the line, so it can't actually favor one option over the other -- Hal's basically just found a fancy way to justify doing whatever he already wanted. The foundation's one-page charter says we should fund "the most effective, evidence-backed" causes, and now all three of us read that phrase differently. Can you write the short memo I'll send Hal and my cousin before the call -- it needs an actual recommendation (fund one, fund both, split some other way, whatever), a next step with a deadline, and it has to take Hal's objection seriously instead of just waving it off, since "AMF has better RCTs" is exactly what's in dispute.
