I've spent a while working through the "coherence arguments" debate that periodically resurfaces on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum: the argument, most associated with Yudkowsky's old "Coherent Decisions Imply Consistent Utilities" post, that any agent whose choices can't be modeled as maximizing some utility function is exploitable via a money pump or Dutch book, so that sufficiently capable, self-improving agents should be expected to converge on expected-utility-maximizing behavior. Rohin Shah's reply that this move smuggles in more than the bare representation theorem delivers — since a utility function can be trivial, e.g. one that simply assigns the maximum to whatever the agent already does, and still satisfy the relevant axioms without the agent being "goal-directed" in any worrying sense — has stuck with me too, and I've since seen people push the same kind of skepticism further by asking whether an agent with genuinely incomplete preferences (one that declines to rank certain options outright, rather than ranking them cyclically) might dodge the pump altogether. I've never seen the two sides of this actually argue it out face to face, as opposed to trading blog posts six months apart, so I'd like to see that.

Please write a dialogue between two people who have just left the same seminar on this topic and are continuing the argument on their walk to the train. NADIA holds that the money-pump/Dutch-book argument really does the work its proponents want from it: any agent robust against exploitation must be representable as an expected-utility maximizer with respect to some utility function, and this is exactly why we should expect advanced, self-modifying systems to end up with stable, world-directed goals rather than a shifting hodgepodge of preferences. THEO thinks this conclusion is oversold, and presses Nadia on two fronts: whether the bare theorem licenses anything beyond a possibly trivial utility function, and whether an agent can maintain incomplete, rather than cyclic, preferences and thereby escape the standard pump without covertly having completed its preference ordering after all. Have Nadia engage both objections on their own terms rather than simply re-asserting the pump argument, have Theo concede whatever turns out to be genuinely right in her replies rather than shift ground to avoid it, and let the two of them go several real rounds — neither should be a pushover, and by the end each should have landed at least one point the other cannot fully answer, even if the dispute doesn't neatly resolve overall.
