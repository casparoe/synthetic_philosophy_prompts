So my AI safety seminar has been covering commitment and bargaining this week -- the idea that an agent who locks itself into a strategy before anyone else does gets a real edge, but racing to be the one who locks in first, before you've actually thought it through, is exactly what makes it dangerous ("commitment races"). Two people in my discussion group got into it afterward over whether that means you should always try to commit first and hardest.

(1) Owen said if commitment power is real, holding back just gives away value -- whoever locks in a hardline demand first effectively moves first in a game where moving first wins, so the rational move for any agent (company, country, AI) is to commit as fast and hard as possible. (2) Sasha said that's backwards once you notice both sides can reason the same way: you can end up with two incompatible hardline demands locked in before either side knows what the other wants, like two drivers in a game of chicken who've both welded the steering wheel straight -- great if only one of you does it, a guaranteed crash if you both do. (3) Owen said fine, but "wait and think it through" isn't safe either, since while you're being careful the other side just commits first and you're stuck reacting to whatever they already locked in -- refusing to race doesn't save you from it, it just means you loose. (4) Sasha said the fix isn't "never commit," its that some commitments are dumber than others: commit to a rule built so it can't leave you worse off than not committing at all, instead of a flat ultimatum, and you keep the deterrent without betting everything on guessing their position right. (5) Owen said that just pushes the race back a level, since now you're racing to get your "safe" rule on the table before the other side locks in their ultimatum.

Neither budged. Can you adjudicate this move by move -- score each point from -2 (clearly wrong) to 2 (clearly right) as a JSON object like {"1": <score>, "2": <score>, "3": <score>, "4": <score>, "5": <score>}, then briefly justify each score and say what, if anything, they're both still missing?
