I've been reading L.A. Paul's stuff on 'transformative experience' -- the idea that some choices (she uses becoming a parent, or the classic thought experiment of turning into a vampire) can't really be evaluated ahead of time with normal expected-value reasoning, because you literally cannot know what the post-choice version of you will experience or even value until you've already gone through with it. It's this weird bind where the 'you' making the decision and the 'you' who has to live with it might not even share the same preferences anymore, so the usual advice-seeking, weigh-the-pros-and-cons approach kind of breaks down. Got into this with some friends over drinks recently and we ended up stuck on how strange it is as a problem for rational choice -- like what are you even supposed to be optimizing for going in?

Anyway, I don't want another vampire story, that ones been done to death (no pun intended, ok maybe a little intended). Could you write me a short story, maybe a page or so, centered on a character facing a decision that's transformative in both of Paul's senses at once -- epistemically, in that they genuinely can't know in advance what the resulting first-person experience will be like, and personally, in that it stands to rewrite their core values or identity, not just their circumstances. I'd love it if the scenario itself was something a bit unexpected rather than the standard becoming-a-parent or religious-conversion examples, and if the story's structure or voice did some of the philosophical work itself -- so the reader actually feels the gap between the 'before' self and the 'after' self, rather than a character just explaining the concept out loud.
