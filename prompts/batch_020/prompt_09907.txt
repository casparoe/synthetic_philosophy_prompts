As a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, I felt a jolt of recognition when I first came across Alfred North Whitehead's concept of "inert ideas" in his writings on education -- ideas absorbed into the mind but never put to use, tested against reality, or recombined with anything else -- because it so perfectly describes the algorithm patterns people cram for whiteboard coding interviews. Whitehead's point was not simply that such ideas are useless; he argued they are actively corrosive to a person's capacity for thought. Write a joke about inert ideas whose punchline turns on that specific claim rather than being a generic joke about interviews being tedious -- it should land for someone who has actually crammed for a coding interview, yet fall completely flat for anyone unfamiliar with what Whitehead actually meant by the term.
