So this came up at a dinner thing and it's been bugging me since. My friend Nadia puts a chunk of her paycheck every year into one of those charities that plants trees in east Africa. Her boyfriend Colin started needling her about it, saying she has no real idea whether it does any good at all: the trees could get logged again in twenty years, the whole project could just push local farmers to clear forest somewhere else instead (he called it 'leakage'), or planting that many trees could shift rainfall patterns in the region for decades in ways nobody could ever trace back to her donation. Nadia said sure, you can never know EVERY effect of anything, but that's not a reason to just freeze up -- you go with your best guess about what you can actually foresee (more trees, more carbon soaked up, income for locals) and treat the stuff you can't foresee as basically a coin flip thats just as likely to help as hurt, so on average it cancels out and doesn't change what you should do. Colin said that's a convenient assumption with nothing behind it, she has no actual reason to think the unknown effects are 50/50 like that, and if anything there's way more ways for messing with a whole region's farming or rainfall to go badly than to go well. Nadia shot back that if you take that seriously you'd have to say the same thing about literally everything anyone ever does, including recycling or donating blood, which seems like a nuts place to end up. Neither of them budged. Can you go through it step by step and say who's actually right at each point, not just who wins overall, and whether they're both missing something bigger?
