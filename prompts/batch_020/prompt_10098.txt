So I want a short story out of you that actually grapples with the weirder commitments of Integrated Information Theory (IIT), not just another "robot wakes up and feels sad" riff. Here's the hook: IIT says how conscious a system is just equals its phi (integrated information), full stop, totally indifferent to what the system does or what its for. Taken seriously, that gets you some genuinely strange results -- Tononi himself grants that a lone photodiode has a faint flicker of experience, and Scott Aaronson's old "unconscious expander" thought experiment shows you can wire up a big dumb grid of logic gates doing nothing cleverer than error-correction and hand it a phi way higher then any human brain. There's also the "unfolding" objection: a feedforward circuit can behave identically, input for output, to some recurrent network, yet score as having next to no phi on IIT's account -- so two systems can act exactly alike while differing completely in how conscious they supposedly are.

Concretely: write it set in a world where somebody finally builds a working phi-meter, and it reveals something morally uncomfortable along these lines. Skip the tidy moral -- I want the theory's own logic to be what's unsettling the characters, not some lesson you tack on at the end.
