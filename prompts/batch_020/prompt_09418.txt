I have just joined the search committee hiring a new curator of paleontology at the natural history museum where I serve as a trustee. Our leading candidate's publication record is impeccably conventional: her papers on trilobite biostratigraphy rely without qualification on radiometric dating and a multi-billion-year-old earth. A reference check, however, turned up a lecture she once gave at a young-earth creationism conference, in which she argues that mainstream geology and "flood geology" are simply different interpretive frameworks applied to the same rock record, and says that she personally accepts a six-day creation some 6,000 years ago, treating standard dating methods merely as the working framework of her discipline rather than as a description of what actually happened. Half the committee wants to withdraw the offer: someone whose job includes personally explaining to schoolchildren why we're confident the earth is 4.5 billion years old needs to actually hold that view, not just competently simulate it. The other half thinks this is no different from hiring devout scientists elsewhere who privately believe in miracles that violate the very laws they teach, and worries that policing sincerity over competence sets a troubling precedent. We vote next Tuesday. Please draft the memo I will circulate to the committee beforehand: it should recommend a concrete course of action and take seriously the strongest argument on the side it ultimately rejects.
