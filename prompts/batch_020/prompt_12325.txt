A friend of mine keeps a public log of a few hundred predictions every year, each tagged with an explicit confidence level, and he is genuinely well-calibrated: across several years, the predictions he has marked at 90% confidence have come true almost exactly 90% of the time. Lately, though, this has started to unsettle him. Ask him about any single prediction on this year's list tagged 90% or higher, and he will flatly assert it as something he simply believes, not merely something probable. Yet he is equally comfortable believing that, across the whole list, a handful of this year's high-confidence predictions are almost certainly false, even though he can't say which ones. When I pointed out that he seems to believe every item in a large set individually while also believing the set as a whole contains a falsehood, he shrugged it off as just what good calibration across many independent judgments looks like, and said that wanting anything tidier would itself be the mistake. I can't decide whether he has dissolved a genuine puzzle or just talked himself into a comfortable contradiction. Is there a real inconsistency in his position, or is he right that this is simply what rational confidence has to look like once you're tracking hundreds of independent judgments?
