I spent about thirty-five years as an observational cosmologist before I retired (mostly a laptop and a lot of fighting for telescope time, not exactly a lab-coat kind of job), and one thing that always sat at the back of my mind is that our best flat-universe measurements from the CMB are perfectly consistent with space being genuinely infinite, not just "really, really big." Now that I've got time to read actual moral philosophy, a friend from my old department pointed me at this literature on "infinite ethics" a few months back -- turns out that if the universe (or the multiverse, if you like eternal inflation) really is spatially infinite, then on any garden-variety aggregative view like utilitarianism it already contains an infinite amount of both good and bad, so the usual trick of totalling up the consequences of an act and comparing sums just breaks: every act comes out either infinitely good, infinitely bad, or flat-out incomparable to every other act. I gather Bostrom coined the term "infinitarian paralysis" for this in a paper from around 2011, which is definately a good name for it.

What I can't tell from the papers I've dug up is which of the proposed fixes people actually take seriously versus which ones only get mentioned so the author can shoot them down in the literature review.
