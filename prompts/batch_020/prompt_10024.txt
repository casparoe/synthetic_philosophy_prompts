Quick recap in case it helps: you're dying of thirst in the desert, a driver says he'll only give you a lift into town if he's confident you'll pay up once your safe, and he's basically got a lie detector, so bluffing won't work. The catch is that once you're actually in town, the "rational" thing by ordinary lights is not to pay, since paying can't undo a ride you already got -- meaning a purely rational hitchhiker never gets picked up to begin with (I had to reread that bit of Reasons and Persons like three times before it clicked). Parfit apparently called the fix for this "rational irrationality": it can be rational to make yourself disposed to do the locally irrational thing.
