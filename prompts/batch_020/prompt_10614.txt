so for my early modern political philosophy seminar (its basically a Hobbes-vs-everybody unit) this week was Pascal's Pensees and the discussion prompt is kind of a beast: is Pascal's whole justice-and-force thing basically cynicism in a nicer coat, or does he actually think theres a "real" justice out there that we just can't get at?

there's the famous fragment - "justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical... unable to make what is just strong, we have made what is strong just" - which reads like straight up might-makes-right. but there's also the bit where some guy asks why he's being killed and basically gets told it's because he lives on the other side of the river, plus the "truth on this side of the Pyrenees, error on the other" line, and my prof keeps hinting these connect to his three orders stuff (body/mind/charity) - like maybe real justice does exist, in the order of charity, and we fallen humans just have zero access to it through force or unaided reason, so leaning on custom is damage control, not a claim that might equals right.

can you actually write the paper? go through both readings using that material and don't hedge at the end, pick whichever one actually fits Pascal better and defend it. around 700 words is probably right.
