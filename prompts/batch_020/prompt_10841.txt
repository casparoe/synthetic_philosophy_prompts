So this happened at our grad reading group last week and I haven't been able to stop turning it over. We were on the sorites paradox and two of my PhD students, Marcus and Elif, got into a real back-and-forth about epistemicism vs supervaluationism that never actually got resolved. Roughly how it went:

(1) Marcus said "bald" has to have a sharp cutoff -- some exact number of hairs where you flip from bald to not-bald -- we just can't know which number it is, because our use of the word is too coarse to fix it any more precisely than that. Classical logic stays fully intact, we just bolt on an epistemic constraint.

(2) Elif said that's a worse bullet to bite than anything supervaluationism asks of you: it means there's some unlucky guy for whom loosing a single hair makes a determinate difference to whether he's bald, and not even an omniscient being could point to which hair, since no pattern of use ever fixed a fact that precise. Meaning is made by us, she said, and we never did anything fine-grained enough to encode a cutoff like that.

(3) Marcus said supervaluationism doesn't dodge this, it just relocates it: now you need a boundary for where "definitely bald" shades into borderline, and by Elif's own lights that boundary is just as vague, so she's stuck needing a "definitely, definitely" operator, and then one of those, and so on -- and that hierarchy either bottoms out somewhere (arbitrary, same problem) or goes on forever (no semantics could actually be like that). Neither option is obviously better than his one unknown cutoff.

(4) Elif said higher-order vagueness is exactly what you'd predict if vagueness comes from our sloppy, finite practices rather than from hidden precise facts -- so it's confirmation of her picture, not a cost. Marcus's cutoff, by contrast, is only there to rescue classical logic and has zero independent motivation.

I'd love a real referee on this since I'm too close to both of them. Could you go through moves (1)-(4) and say who has the better of it at each step -- not just an overall winner -- and point out anything important that both of them are overlooking?
