I had a long argument with a friend last night about what Bell's theorem actually rules out, and I came away thinking I had won. My claim was that Bell can only derive his famous inequality by first assuming that hidden variables fix measurement outcomes deterministically; if the hidden variables instead fix only probabilities, as in a genuinely stochastic local theory, the derivation doesn't go through, so the theorem leaves indeterministic local hidden-variable theories completely untouched. If that's right, I don't see why physicists and philosophers of physics keep describing Bell's theorem as ruling out local realism in general, rather than just the narrower, more classical thesis of local determinism. Was I right, or was there a flaw in my reasoning that I'm not seeing?
