Having spent a career in experimental physics before retiring, I've recently been working through Derek Parfit's *Reasons and Persons*, and his teletransporter cases have taken up more of my retirement than I expected. Quantum teleportation, as I recall from the physics, only ever transmits a state via entanglement and destroys the original in the process; Parfit's scan-and-rebuild device is a rather different animal, and it is the metaphysics, not the physics, that has me stuck. In his Branch-Line Case, a malfunctioning teletransporter leaves the original alive on Earth for a few more days while an exact Replica already lives on Mars. From our expected equanimity about this scenario, Parfit infers that identity itself is not what matters in survival, and that only "Relation R" (psychological continuity and/or connectedness, with any cause) deserves our concern. Yet his reductionism about personal identity — the claim that identity just consists in continuity facts, with no further fact over and above them — strikes me as logically separable from that stronger conclusion. Please argue that one can accept Parfit's reductionism about personal identity while still holding that identity itself, and not merely Relation R, is what matters in survival, and that the Branch-Line Case does not, by itself, establish otherwise.
