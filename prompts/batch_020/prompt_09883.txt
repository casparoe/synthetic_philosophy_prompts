I've been working through the anti-luminosity argument in Timothy Williamson's *Knowledge and Its Limits* for a reading group on self-knowledge, and while I can follow the basic mechanics of the argument, I'm stuck on exactly how it is supposed to bear on the KK principle rather than just on introspection of sensations. The core case is something like this: a subject's sense of, say, feeling cold changes gradually and continuously over a long stretch of time, from clearly cold to clearly not cold; their introspective discrimination is coarse enough that any two moments a fraction of a second apart are indistinguishable to them from the inside; and a margin-for-error principle — roughly, that a belief doesn't count as knowledge if the method producing it could easily have delivered the same verdict in a very similar case where that verdict would have been false — then forces a sorites-style chain running from a case where the subject clearly knows they feel cold to one where they clearly don't, which is absurd unless luminosity fails somewhere along the way. I can reconstruct that much on my own.

What I can't reconstruct is the further step from "feeling cold is not luminous" to an actual counterexample to KK. Is the idea that "S knows that p" is itself a graded, margin-sensitive condition — varying continuously with the strength of S's evidence — so that exactly the same sorites machinery can be re-run with "believing p on evidence e" in place of "being at some exact temperature," this time yielding cases where S knows p but is not in a position to know that S knows p? And if that is the structure, how strong a conclusion does it actually license: does it show that KK fails somewhere in any sufficiently long sequence of cases (leaving it open that KK holds for clear, non-marginal instances of knowledge), or does Williamson think it shows something much stronger — that no instance of knowledge whatsoever, however central, comes with guaranteed knowledge of itself? I'd like a precise, step-by-step account of how the generalization to KK is supposed to work and which of those readings of the conclusion the argument actually supports.
