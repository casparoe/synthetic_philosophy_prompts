So i went down a rabbit hole a while back into this old book called The Querist, written by Berkeley -- yeah the "if a tree falls in the forest" guy, turns out later in life he wrote a ton about banking and money too. His big idea seems to be that money isnt wealth in itself, its just a ticket or counter for keeping track of who can claim what from everyone else, and that gold and silver coins only "work" because people agree to treat them that way, not cause the metal itself is special. He even floats the idea of a bank just printing paper to do the same job.

Then i found out that a generation or two earlier, Locke was on the complete opposite side of basically this same question, during that huge fight in England over reminting all the worn down silver coins. His whole position was that the silver content IS the value, full stop, and that fiddling with that (even just relabeling coins to be worth more) was cheating people out of something real, no matter what everybody agreed to call it.

Could you write me a dialogue where the two of them actually hash this out face to face, like whether money is fundamentally about the stuff its made of or just a trust/ticket system that works as long as people go along with it? I want real back and forth, where they're responding to each others specific points and not just repeating their own talking points past eachother, and neither one should come off as the dumb one. Dont feel like you need to crown a winner at the end, i honestly cant tell who's definately right.
