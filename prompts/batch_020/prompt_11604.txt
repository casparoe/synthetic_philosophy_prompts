Hey, I'm drafting some notes for my phil of math seminar next week and want a sanity check before I flesh the argument out properly. My core claim is that "intuitionistic logic" is actually a label for at least three different packages that get run together, and I think conflating them is why students (and honestly some papers) end up confused about why anyone would reject excluded middle.

Package 1 is Brouwer's own view: rejecting LEM falls out of a broader idealism about mathematical objects as mental constructions built up in inner time-consciousness, so an undecided statement like Goldbach's conjecture just doesn't have a determinate truth value yet, since no one's constructed a proof or refutation of it.

Package 2 is Dummett's semantic argument, which doesn't need any of Brouwer's mysticism at all: if meaning is constituted by verification conditions rather than by truth conditions we can't in general recognize as obtaining, manifestability worries push you toward giving up bivalence, and intuitionistic logic falls out as the correct logic for a thought whose content is exhausted by its proof conditions.

Package 3 is the purely instrumental use you get in type theory and CS (Martin-Löf and co), where people adopt intuitionistic logic because BHK proof terms give a nice computational reading via Curry-Howard, with zero commitment to either Brouwer's metaphysics or Dummett's semantics.

I want to argue these are genuinely seperable - you could take 2 without 1, or 3 without either - and that a lot of hand-wavy classroom talk about "the intuitionist thinks mathematical objects are mental constructions" is actually smuggling in package 1 to justify a move thats really only motivated (if at all) by package 2. Does this trichotomy actually survive scrutiny or am I flattening some real connections between the packages? And is there a more standard way of carving this up in the literature that I'm just reinventing badly?
