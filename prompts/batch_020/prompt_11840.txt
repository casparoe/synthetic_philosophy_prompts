Me and my friend Dan got into it for like two hours last night (on a work night, no less) arguing about sports team identity, of all things. His childhood team relocated to a different city years back, got a new name and colors, and had basically a whole new roster within a couple seasons -- but he still says stuff like "we've won three championships," counting titles the franchise picked up back when it played in his hometown under the old name. I said thats kind of nuts: if none of the players, coaches, or even the fans overlap anymore, in what sense is it "the same team" just because some league office ruled the trophies carry over with it? He argued identity isn't about the particular people involved at all, its about the continuous organization persisting through change, kind of like how a country or a company stays "itself" even though basically everyone in it eventually turns over. We ended up googling around and found out leagues don't even agree with each other on this -- some let a relocated team take all the old stats and banners with it, others split things so the name and history stay behind with a "new" team while the relocated one starts from zero. Which honestly just made me suspect there's no real fact of the matter here, just different leagues picking different conventions and calling it a day.

So here's what I actually want help with: when Dan says "it's the same team" and I say "no it isn't," are we disagreeing about something real, or have we just been using "same team" to mean two different things and arguing past each other this whole time? And if it's the second thing, was the argument pointless, or is there an actual way to figure out whose notion of "sameness" is better here, instead of just throwing up my hands and going "eh, semantics"? I feel like this exact shape of disagreement comes up everywhere -- whether a renovated old building is "the original," whether a friend group is "the same" after everyone in it has changed, whether you're "the same person" you were at 15 -- and I'd love some actual way of thinking it through instead of my usual move of just getting tired and agreeing to disagree.
