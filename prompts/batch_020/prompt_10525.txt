I'm putting together a short story collection built around odd philosophy-of-mind puzzles, and there's one I can't shake: the idea that what a thought or signal is "about" comes down to the biological function it was shaped to perform, not just whatever happens to trigger it (I think this is called teleosemantics, or something close to that). The textbook example is a frog that snaps at flies but will just as happily snap at a bb pellet flicked past it — so is its fly-detector actually about flies, or just about "small dark moving thing," and is the frog wrong about the pellet, or doing exactly what it evolved to do? I want to steal that unease for a story, but swap the frog for some modern AI system (a vision classifier, a targeting system, whatever fits) — not a "the machine turns evil" story, more one that just sits with the question of whether the thing can ever really be wrong about what it's looking at, or whether "wrong" stops meaning anything once you dig into what fixed its "aboutness" in the first place. Could you write that story for me? I'd rather it stayed small and unsettling then turned into an action scene.
