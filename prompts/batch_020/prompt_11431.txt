Been low-key obsessed with MIRI's old "embedded agency" stuff lately (the split into embedded world-models, decision theory, robust delegation, subsystem alignment) and I think I found a real-world hook for it from my day job. Want to turn it into a blog post but I'm worried I'm just forcing a metaphor that doesn't actually hold up. These are rough bullet notes, not real prose yet:

- The classic agent/environment split (the "Cartesian boundary") assumes clean sensor/actuator inputs and a world-model that's bigger than the agent itself. Embedded agents don't get that: they're made of the same stuff as the world and can't fully model themselves.
- Our autoscaler at work is basically a toy version of this. It watches cluster load and scales pods up or down, but its own scaling actions change the load it measures a few minutes later, and under heavy load the autoscaler's own pods are competing for the same CPU it's trying to allocate. It's not observing an external environment, its observing a system that includes its own recent decisions.
- Want to push it further into "subsystem alignment" too - our autoscaler is really three seperate heuristics (CPU, queue depth, a cost-budget cap) bolted together, and they fight each other in ways nobody designed for, kind of like the worry about sub-processes inside an agent not sharing the outer objective.
- Where I'm genuinely unsure the analogy holds: the tiling-agents / Löb's-theorem obstacle is about a formal system being unable to fully trust proofs from a version of itself, and I don't think "v2 of the config trusts v3" is really the same kind of self-trust problem - might just be normal software versioning dressed up in fancier language.

Can you give me an honest read? Specifically: (1) is the Cartesian-boundary comparison actually sound, or am I pattern-matching two things that just both happen to involve feedback loops, (2) is my instinct right that the tiling-agents bit doesn't transfer to normal software versioning, and if so why exactly, (3) is there a sharper example from distributed systems I'm missing that would map more precisely onto one of the four sub-problems. Not fishing for encouragement, I want someone to poke real holes in it before I post this anywhere.
