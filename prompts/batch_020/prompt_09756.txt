I'm the philosophy prof on my university's committee that reviews outside labor contracts (don't ask how I landed that one), and we've got a genuinely hard case this week. A student group wants us to cancel the contract with the firm that staffs the dining halls, arguing the arrangement is exploitative in Marx's technical sense -- not just "underpaid" but literally extracting surplus value from workers who have no real alternative to selling their labor. The contractor's rebuttal, forwarded by the provost's office, is that every worker signed up voluntarily, the wage clears legal minimum, and each of them is strictly better off than their next-best option, so "exploitation" is just activist rhetoric here. The committee's split down the middle, and one member keeps steering us toward alienation instead, which I think is smuggling in a different complaint rather then settling this one. Can you draft the memo I'm bringing Thursday? It needs an actual recommendation (renew, cancel, or renew with conditions) and a rough timeline, and it has to seriously engage the "voluntary and mutually beneficial" defense rather than just dismiss it -- plenty of people who work on exploitation (Roemer, Wertheimer, whoever) think mutual benefit doesn't automatically get you off the hook, so I want that argued through, not just asserted.
