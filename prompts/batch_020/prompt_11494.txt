I am drafting a novel whose central character is a superintelligent AI who claims that, in principle, she could learn any truth about the world she wished, even though at any given moment there remain plenty of truths she simply has not yet learned. I have always understood Fitch's paradox of knowability as a result about the predicament of finite, human knowers, and so I assumed it has no real purchase on a being with unlimited computational power and all the time in the world to think. Before I hinge a pivotal plot point on that assumption, I would like to know whether this is in fact a fair reading of the paradox, and if it is not, exactly where my reasoning has gone wrong.
