I'm currently taking a graduate seminar on Frege and the origins of analytic philosophy, and a few weeks ago we spent a session on something I hadn't encountered before: the private diary Frege kept in 1924, filled with virulently antisemitic and antidemocratic opinions, and Michael Dummett's visibly pained reaction upon learning of it. The pain was sharpened by the fact that it was Dummett's own scholarship that had done more than anyone's to canonize Frege as the founding father of analytic philosophy of language. Our department is bringing in a visiting historian of philosophy next month to give a talk built around the claim that the diary is not merely an unfortunate biographical footnote: she argues that Frege's insistence on a rigidly objective "third realm" of thoughts, sealed off from psychological or cultural contamination, and his decades-long campaign against psychologism and relativism about logical truth, spring from the same intolerance of pluralism that surfaces in the diary's politics — not identical to it, but expressive of a kindred temperament.

I've been asked to help draft questions for the Q&A, and apparently she is well rehearsed in the standard reply to this line of attack: that Frege's formal results stand or fall on their own logical merits regardless of the character of the man who discovered them, so none of this amounts to more than unflattering trivia about the discoverer. Could you draft eight to ten questions that push past that move rather than simply inviting her to restate it? I'd like at least one question that pins her down on the "third realm" passages in "Der Gedanke" and the anti-psychologism of the Grundgesetze introduction, asking her to specify concretely what the supposed link to the diary amounts to beyond a loose analogy; at least one on the awkward chronology, since most of the logical work she leans on long predates 1924, so I want her forced to say whether her thesis requires the animus to have been latent all along or to have hardened only late in his life, and what would tell us which; and at least one that uses Dummett's own response — as both Frege's great scholarly champion and a lifelong anti-racist campaigner — as a test case for whether compartmentalizing the two really is the evasion she takes it to be. For each of these, please add a short follow-up keyed to the deflection she is most likely to reach for, and close the set with a question asking what evidence would actually count against her thesis, since as described it sounds like it could accommodate almost anything.
