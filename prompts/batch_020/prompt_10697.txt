So my school's independent research program just started making anyone who wants to use live animals in their science fair project get approved by a little student-run "ethics committee" first, basically our tiny version of the review boards actual scientists have to go through, and I somehow got roped into being on it along with two other juniors and our research teacher, Mr. Alvarez. The case we're stuck on is from my friend Priya: she wants to run mice through a maze with a mild foot-shock to test whether stress before a trial wrecks or actually sharpens their memory of the route afterward. Its a totally standard setup, the kind real neuroscience labs run constantly, and her data would genuinely be strong for states, but a couple of us feel weird approving pain on something that can't say yes or no to it, especially when we'd never let her run an equivalent stress study on human classmates without a mountain of consent forms first.

The catch is Mr. Alvarez wants us to reason it through using the rights/side-constraints framework from Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia, since thats what our philosophy elective just covered, instead of just doing a straight pain-vs-scientific-benefit calculation. And the annoying thing about Nozick, once you actually dig into that book, is that he doesn't really have a clean answer for animals — there's a footnote where he floats something like "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people," basically letting animal suffering get outweighed by aggregate benefits while human side-constraints stay near-absolute, but he sounds genuinely unsure about it, like he knows that drawing the line at species membership needs a justification he never actually gives.

Can you draft the memo I'm supposed to bring to our meeting Monday? I need it to land on an actual recommendation (approve, reject, or approve with specific changes to Priya's protocol), argue for it mainly from that side-constraints angle rather than a generic "animal testing good/bad" essay, and take genuinely seriously how someone applying Nozick's own view might still end up approving the project instead of just dismissing that option.
