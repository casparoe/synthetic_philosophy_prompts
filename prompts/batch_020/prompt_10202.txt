Last year I had two different foundation repair companies come inspect some cracking in my basement wall. The first contractor spent maybe twenty minutes looking around and told me the cracking was just normal settling, and that anything beyond a cheap cosmetic patch would be a waste of money. The second contractor was much more alarmed, said the wall was at real risk of failing, and quoted me a very expensive underpinning job. When I mentioned what the first contractor had said, he had a specific rebuttal ready for every point, and always seemed to know exactly why the other guy was wrong or had missed something. I don't have any background in construction or engineering, so I basically just sat there for an hour listening to each side answer the other's objections, trying to judge who was running out of good responses and who wasn't.

I did eventually pay for an independent structural engineer's report to settle it, but the experience left me thinking about something more general. A lot of situations in life seem to boil down to a non-expert having to referee two confident, articulate people who disagree and who are each mainly motivated to win the argument rather than to be neutral, whether that's contractors, lawyers in a courtroom, or competing financial advisors. My instinct is that just watching each side rebut the other's weak points should, at least eventually, tend to surface the truth, since the correct side should always have a good comeback available and the wrong side should eventually run out of them. But I can't really justify that instinct to myself beyond it feeling plausible, and I'm not sure whether it actually holds up, or whether there's some deeper reason this kind of back-and-forth could reliably steer even a careful, patient judge toward the wrong conclusion. Is there something real behind my hunch, or am I missing a deeper problem with trying to find the truth this way?
