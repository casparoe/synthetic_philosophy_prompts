In my physics class, we recently covered the EPR paradox and Bell's theorem, and my teacher explained that Bell's theorem proved hidden variables cannot exist, which is why quantum randomness has to be fundamental rather than a reflection of our ignorance of some deeper, deterministic reality. Curious to learn more, I did some outside reading and found that Bohmian mechanics (pilot-wave theory) is still treated by a number of physicists as a serious, internally consistent interpretation of quantum mechanics, even though it explicitly relies on hidden variables. If Bell's theorem already closed the door on hidden variables decades ago, why do physicists still treat Bohmian mechanics as a live option instead of something the theorem ruled out from the start?
