For my graduate seminar on modal metaphysics, write a dialogue between a committed Lewisian counterpart theorist and a philosopher who holds that ordinary individuals are strictly and numerically identical across possible worlds. Have the identity theorist open with Kripke's Humphrey objection -- that in saying Humphrey could have won the election, we are talking about Humphrey himself, not about some other-worldly counterpart that Humphrey has no reason to care about -- and press the counterpart theorist to explain why her semantics for de re modal claims isn't simply changing the subject. She should meet that challenge on its own terms and then turn the dialectic around by pressing him with the problem of accidental intrinsics. Neither view should go unchallenged: each move should respond to the specific point just made rather than retreating into a restatement of the general position, and the dialogue should close with each character naming the cost of their own view that they find hardest to dismiss.
