I spent almost forty years doing experimental particle physics before I retired, and for the last year or so I've been filling the gap with philosophy, mostly metaphysics since its the closest thing to my old turf. Our local philosophy discussion group has been working through a book on persistence through time, and last week's chapter used special relativity to argue for perdurantism over endurantism -- the idea that persisting things are really four-dimensional "worms" made of temporal parts, rather than three-dimensional things that are "wholly present" at every moment they exist. It's been nagging at me ever since, seeing as relativity is supposed to be my home turf and I feel like I should be able to sort out whether the argument actually holds up.

The argument, as I understood it, runs like this: endurantism needs a privileged, frame-independent fact about which spacetime points count as "the object, complete, right now," which just amounts to a privileged way of carving spacetime into instants. But relativity of simultaneity says there's no such privileged carving -- what counts as simultaneous with what depends on your reference frame, full stop. Perdurantism, on the other hand, just posits a four-dimensional worldtube sitting in spacetime, and different frames slice that same worldtube into "temporal parts" differently, but the worldtube doesnt care which frame does the slicing. So, the book concludes, perdurantism fits comfortably with relativity while endurantism needs a kind of fact relativity rules out.

My trouble with this is that it seems too easy to block: why can't the endurantist just relativize "wholly present" to a frame, exactly the way we already relativize simultaneity, length, and duration? Nobody treats relativizing simultaneity as a cheap trick, so why would relativizing "wholly present at t" be one either? I suspect the comeback is that the object then turns into a bundle of frame-indexed stages, which just is perdurantism wearing a trenchcoat, but I can't make that worry rigorous on my own, and the book didn't really engage with it. Please make the strongest case you can that this relativizing move actually saves endurantism -- that it lets three-dimensionalism survive the relativistic challenge intact, without quietly collapsing into perdurantism and without just being an ad hoc patch -- and take on that collapse worry directly rather than waving it away. I'd rather have that than the pop-physics "relativity proves the block universe, so four-dimensionalism wins" line, which I've run into a dozen times now and which I think skips right past the hard part.
