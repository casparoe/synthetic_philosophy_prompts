A friend and I spent most of the drive home from a holiday party arguing about a game we'd just played, and neither of us has been able to let it go since. Each guest had been handed two identical sealed envelopes and told that one contained exactly twice as much cash as the other. I opened one and found $60 inside. My friend argued that I should swap for the still-sealed envelope: since it was equally likely to be the smaller or the larger of the pair, it had to hold either $30 or $120 with equal probability, for an expected value of $75 — comfortably more than the $60 already in my hand. What nags at me is that his argument never used the number $60 in any special way: the same reasoning would have told me to swap whatever amount I had found, and, if repeated, would tell me to swap back once I was holding the new envelope, and so on indefinitely. That can't be sound reasoning, but neither of us could explain exactly where it breaks down. Was my friend right that swapping was the better bet, and if not, where precisely does his calculation fail?
