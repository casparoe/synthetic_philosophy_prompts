Okay so this has been rattling around in my head since a comment thread on ACX about "code is law" a while back. I ended up actually reading the Hart–Fuller exchange properly (the 1958 Harvard Law Review pieces, plus enough of The Morality of Law to get the eight desiderata and the fable of Rex the incompetent king), and I can't stop thinking about how well it maps onto the DAO hack. You remember that one — 2016, Ethereum, a reentrancy bug that let someone drain tens of millions of dollars worth of ETH out of "The DAO," followed by a huge fight over whether the community should hard-fork to reverse it. One camp insisted the drain was just as valid a transaction as any other, since the code just IS the agreement; the other camp said that's obviously not what anyone signed up for and the fork was basically mandatory.

What gets me is its basically the grudge-informer case again, except with the moral valence flipped and the formalism cranked to eleven: instead of a wicked law being followed to the letter, you've got a payout mechanism with no discernible purpose whatsoever being followed to the letter. So here's what I want: a dialogue where Hart and Fuller get briefed on smart contracts and the DAO fork and basically pick their old argument back up. Hart should make the positivist case — the drain was fully valid under the only rule of recognition this particular system had, and intellectual honesty requires admitting the fork overrode a valid outcome rather than "discovering" the hack was never really authorized. Fuller should push back with the inner-morality-of-law stuff, especially congruence between rule and administration, to argue that code incapable of purposive interpretation was never entitled to the presumption of validity Hart wants to hand it.

Don't let either of them win too easy. Hart should get a real dig in about Fuller's "not really law" move being awfully convenient for reaching whatever conclusion he already wanted; Fuller needs an actual comeback to the standard "you're just smuggling morality back in under a new label" charge, not just a restatement of the eight desiderata. I'd rather it end without a tidy resolution than have either one quietly fold — which, now that I think about it, is honestly how their real exchange felt too.
