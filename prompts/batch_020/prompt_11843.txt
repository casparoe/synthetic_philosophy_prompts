For the past few months I have been choosing between two very different paths for my career: a research fellowship abroad and a permanent position in the city where my family lives. Neither option seems better than, worse than, or equally good as the other, and the difficulty doesn't feel like a symptom of missing information: even perfect information, I suspect, would not settle it. In trying to make sense of this, I came across Ruth Chang's proposal that such cases involve a fourth basic value relation, "parity," distinct from the standard trichotomy of better, worse, and equally good. What interests me most is her further claim that parity is also distinct from mere rough or imprecise equality, a claim her small-improvement argument is meant to secure. I find the thought that there is no hidden fact I'm failing to grasp genuinely comforting, but I suspect I only find it comforting because I want it to be true. Please argue that the small-improvement argument fails to establish this distinction, so that what I'm facing might, after all, be nothing more than a case of rough or imprecise comparability.
