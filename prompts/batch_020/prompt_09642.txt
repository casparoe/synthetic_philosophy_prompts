Me and my girlfriend have been doing this thing for about a year now and my brother recently told me it's basically hypocritical on both our parts, so now I can't stop turning it over in my head. She's vegetarian for animal welfare reasons, thinks factory farming is genuinely one of the worst things humans do to other creatures. I don't actually think eating meat is wrong, but I also fly a fair amount for work, and it turns out that bothers her way more than my eating meat bothers me, cause of the environmental impact. So somewhere along the way, without really sitting down and planning it, we landed on this arrangement: I stopped eating meat around her and cut back generally, and in exchange she stopped giving me grief about flights and doesnt push the whole take-the-train-instead thing anymore, even though it still bugs her a little. Neither of us actually changed our mind about the underlying moral question, we just both ended up doing more of what the other person cares about, since it cost each of us less than it was worth to the other one.

My brother thinks this proves neither of us really believes our own position. His argument is that if I genuinely thought eating meat was fine, no amount of "flight credit" should be able to talk me out of it, and if she genuinely thought factory farming was a serious moral wrong, she shouldn't be okay with me still doing it sometimes just because she got something else out of the deal. He says morality isn't supposed to work like splitting rent or trading chores, you can't horse-trade on stuff that's actually right or wrong, and the fact that we're doing it means at least one of us is faking our convictions. Part of me thinks he's got a real point, but another part of me thinks this is just what two people who disagree and still want to make it work end up doing, and it doesn't seem obviously worse than one of us just steamrolling the other. I've been going back and forth on this by myself for weeks now and I genuinely can't tell if there's something inconsistent about handling a moral disagreement this way, or if my brother's making it into a bigger deal than it actually is. Who's actually got this right?
