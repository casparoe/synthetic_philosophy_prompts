I'm doing a podcast episode with a guest who defends the knowledge norm of assertion: you're only allowed to assert p if you know p. From his older interviews I've noticed his go-to dodge -- whenever you hand him a case where someone asserts something on terrific evidence that turns out false, or true only by luck, and the assertion still seems totally fine, he just says it was improper but excused, so it doesnt actually count against the norm. Can you draft me 8-10 questions that make that dodge harder to reach for, plus a follow-up ready for when he repeats that line? I want at least one built on the lottery case -- why does the asymmetry (fine to believe your ticket lost, not fine to assert it) require knowledge itself, rather than just much stronger justification -- one asking flat out whether "improper but excused" can absorb literally any counterexample thrown at it, and what that does to the view's testability, and one on whether the oddity of "p, but I don't know that p" might just be conversational implicature rather than proof of a real constitutive norm. End with a question that makes him actually describe the one case that would get him to drop the knowledge norm for something weaker.
