So this happened to a friend of mine a few weeks ago and it's been rattling around in my head since (I write about science and philosophy-adjacent stuff for a living, so naturally everyone now assumes I have some tidy answer for this — I dont). She's the de facto manager of the small walk-up she lives in, not the actual landlord, just the person tenants go to because she's organized and has everyone's numbers. For weeks a tenant had been posting in the building group chat that one of the hallway smoke detectors was doing the low-battery chirp. My friend saw those messages but didn't personally go swap the battery, figuring that's literally what the landlord's maintenance guy (who reads the same chat and is contractually the one responsible for that stuff) was for. Then there was a small grease fire in one unit — put out fast, nobody hurt directly by the fire itself — but the hallway filled with smoke for several minutes before any alarm went off, and two older tenants ended up needing oxygen because of it.

Now there's a whole building meeting where everyone's trying to work out who's actually to blame, and my friend has more or less convinced herself she caused the smoke inhalation, since if she'd just walked over with a 9-volt battery none of this happens. Meanwhile the maintenance guy, who had the actual contractual duty and saw the identical texts, is barely getting mentioned — and by that same logic you could just as easily blame every other tenant who read that chat and shrugged, or even whoever designed the building without hardwired detectors to begin with. Something about singling her out doesn't sit right with me, but I can't quite articulate why. Is she actually right that her not acting "caused" this in any meaningful sense, the same way actually disabling the alarm would have? And if not her specifically, is there a principled reason to land the blame on the maintenance guy instead, or are we all just picking whoever happens to feel the guiltiest?
