I've spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums -- Overcoming Bias, the LessWrong diaspora, Astral Codex Ten -- and a debate broke out in my old reading group's chat last week that I think is more subtle than either participant realized. Someone posted a video of a mutual friend's wedding proposal (a rented stadium jumbotron, a hired string quartet, the works), and two group members, Sasha and Theo, spent the next hour arguing over whether this kind of display is well explained by Zahavi's handicap principle -- the idea, originally developed to explain traits and behaviors like the peacock's tail or a gazelle's stotting in front of predators, that a signal can be trustworthy precisely because it is too costly for a low-quality signaler to fake.

The exchange went roughly like this. (1) Sasha argued that the jumbotron proposal is a clean instance of the handicap principle: spending that much money and public dignity on a single afternoon is exactly the kind of costly, hard-to-fake gesture that separates a genuinely committed partner from a less committed one, on the same logic that makes the peacock's tail an honest signal of fitness. (2) Theo objected that this misidentifies the mechanism: the tail works because carrying it is differentially more costly for a parasitized or weak bird than for a healthy one -- the cost tracks the very quality being signaled. Renting a jumbotron costs the same whether or not the proposer is secretly ambivalent, so there is no differential cost separating the sincere from the insincere, provided both can afford it; it's just an expensive display, and expense alone was never Zahavi's criterion. (3) Sasha replied that the differential cost is real but shows up on a different axis than money: it's the risk of public humiliation if the relationship later collapses, and someone who privately doubts the relationship is taking on a higher expected cost from a loud, hard-to-walk-back public display than someone who's actually sure -- so the jumbotron screens on private information about relationship quality much as the tail screens on parasite load. (4) Theo answered that this move is cleverer but proves too much: on this logic, any sufficiently embarrassing public act would count as a signal of relationship confidence, yet people obviously stage such events for reasons that have nothing to do with private certainty -- social pressure, competitive in-laws, wanting the photos -- so some further story is needed about why this particular costly convention, rather than costly display in general, is the one actually doing signaling work, and neither of them had supplied it.

I couldn't settle who was right, and I'd like an adjudication that goes move by move rather than a verdict that just declares a winner.
