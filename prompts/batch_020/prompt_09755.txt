For the final meeting of my high school's computer science club this year, each member has to give a short "lightning talk" on something outside the normal curriculum that we found interesting, and I've chosen a game-theoretic idea I came across while reading about AI safety called program equilibrium. I follow the individual pieces well enough to explain them seriously in my talk, but I want to open with a short joke to get people's attention before the technical part starts, and comedy has never been my strong suit.

Could you write me a short joke about program equilibrium -- a one-liner or a brief exchange would both work -- provided that the punchline actually depends on how the idea works, rather than being a generic joke about robots or computers that just happens to name-drop game theory? My hunch is that the funniest material is hiding somewhere in the source-code-reading setup itself, or in that strange, seemingly-circular "I will cooperate with you only once I can prove you will cooperate with me" reasoning that the Löb's theorem trick is supposed to rescue -- but I'll leave the exact angle up to you, as long as the person in the room who actually knows some game theory and a little logic ends up laughing hardest.
