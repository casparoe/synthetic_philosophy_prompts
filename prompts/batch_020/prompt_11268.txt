I picked up chess again a few months ago after nearly a decade away, and most evenings now I play a couple of online games with an old university friend while we keep an engine - Stockfish, mostly - running in the background to dissect our mistakes afterward. Lately, though, something about how we use that engine has started to bother me more than the chess itself. When we pause a tense position and have the engine evaluate it at a shallow search depth, it might report that White is, say, 70 percent likely to win from there; if we simply let it keep calculating on that very same position for another thirty seconds, the figure can swing down to 35 percent before climbing back up to 95 percent once it finds some hidden resource. Nothing about the position itself changes while any of this happens: assuming perfect play from both sides going forward, that position was always strictly a win, a loss, or a draw before either the engine or we ever looked at it, and it stays exactly that way the whole time the number bounces around. On reflection, that makes it feel strange to call these shifting figures "probabilities" in anything like the sense we'd use for a coin flip or a hand of cards, since there is no actual matter of chance left for a probability to be about.

My friend, who is usually the more careful thinker of the two of us, has taken to arguing that the "win percentage" is therefore a kind of useful fiction: a way of dressing an internal evaluation score up in probabilistic language for our benefit, but not something we should treat as a genuine degree of rational belief, since strictly speaking nothing remains uncertain once you fix the position and assume perfect play from that point on. I find this hard to accept, even though I can't quite say what's wrong with it. It still seems obviously more reasonable to feel confident in a won position once the engine has searched deeply and consistently confirms it than after a single shallow, soon-to-be-overturned pass, and that difference in justification feels like exactly the kind of thing "probability" is supposed to capture in the first place.

We've even started half-jokingly wagering small amounts on shallow-depth readings before letting the engine finish thinking things through, which has only made the disagreement more pointed between us. So I'd like to know: is my friend right that talk of probability simply stops applying once a question has a fixed, determinate answer that we merely haven't computed yet, or is there some legitimate sense in which our shifting, imperfect confidence about the position can still count as rational, and even as tracking something real?
