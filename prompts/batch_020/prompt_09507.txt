I'm a retired developmental biologist, and since retiring I've gotten pretty into reading philosophy for fun. I still sit on my university's stem cell research oversight committee -- the group that signs off on anything involving human-animal chimeras. We've got a proposal to make pig embryos with a substantial human cell contribution, aimed at eventually growing transplantable organs; human contribution to brain and germline tissue stays capped near zero under our rules, but contribution to organs like kidney or pancreas isn't limited. Two members want to reject the proposal or delay it indefinitely because, in their words, it strikes almost everybody as viscerally wrong -- they keep invoking that old Leon Kass line about repugnance being a kind of wisdom that reason can't fully spell out. Two other members say that's just pathogen-avoidance psychology firing at something novel, which is basically the same fight as the evolutionary debunking arguments I've been reading about lately -- on their view the gut reaction is evidence of nothing but our evolutionary history, so it shouldn't count for anything. Neither side is really engaging with the other's best point, if you ask me. Can you draft the short memo I'll bring to our next meeting? It needs an actual recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions and a review timeframe -- and it has to take the repugnance side seriously rather then just diagnosing it as bias and moving on.
