Over the last year or so I've spent a lot of my spare time reading effective altruism and rationalist blogs and forums. I never studied philosophy in school -- a friend got me into the existential-risk material, and I've just followed my own curiosity from there -- but lately I can't stop returning to one particular corner of it: "infinite ethics."

What hooked me is the way ordinary moral bookkeeping seems to break down, in at least two distinct ways, once you allow that the universe might genuinely be infinite, which apparently a fair number of cosmologists take seriously. First, since an infinite quantity of value is left completely unchanged by adding or subtracting any finite quantity, whatever good or harm any single person manages to do in a lifetime technically leaves the total value of the universe exactly as it was -- people on these forums call the resulting shrug-at-everything conclusion "infinitarian paralysis." Second, and this one unsettles me even more: apparently you can take an infinite list of finite good and bad events and simply reorder it -- the way a conditionally convergent series in calculus can be rearranged to sum to any number at all -- and get a completely different grand total for the universe, so that whether the cosmic ledger reads as glorious or catastrophic might hinge on bookkeeping order rather than on anything that actually happened.

I don't want a generic "look how silly philosophers get about infinity" joke; I want the punchline to genuinely depend on understanding either infinitarian paralysis or the reordering trick (either is fine -- pick whichever makes the better joke rather than forcing both in), so that someone who has never encountered the idea might find it merely a bit odd, while someone who has spent time with it would feel a small jolt of recognition underneath the laugh. If it isn't too much trouble, a brief note afterward on why the joke works would be welcome too, mainly so I can check that I've actually grasped the underlying issue rather than just repeating a phrase that sounds clever.
