Hobbes thought geometry was basically the one field of human knowledge where real certainty was possible, and he leaned on that to argue his civil philosophy (the Leviathan stuff on sovereignty and the social contract) should count as just as certain, since a commonwealth, like a triangle, is something we construct ourselves rather then just observe in nature. Funny thing is, Hobbes was also a notoriously bad geometer: he spent decades insisting he'd squared the circle, getting publicly demolished for it by the mathematician John Wallis over and over, and never once backing down. Write an essay on whether that whole saga actually undermines Hobbes's argument for the certainty of civil philosophy, or whether his mathematical incompetence and his epistemological claim are basically separate matters. Don't hedge -- land on an actual verdict and defend it.
