Next month our department is hosting a colloquium talk by a philosopher of psychiatry whose current book project extends Ian Hacking's looping-effects account of human kinds to the recent boom in adult ADHD diagnoses, arguing that the category does not simply pick out a pre-existing population but has helped bring a new kind of person into being whose self-understanding then reshapes the diagnosis in turn. I'll be chairing the Q&A and want eight to ten questions that push past a restatement of the framework. Please include at least one question on each of the following: whether talk of looping is compatible with ADHD's substantial heritability and neurobiological correlates, or risks being heard as a debunking move; whether the distinction between "interactive" human kinds and "indifferent" natural kinds survives once one notices how much diagnostic change owes to clinical politics and pharmaceutical marketing rather than to patients' altered self-understanding; whether social-media self-diagnosis is really the same looping mechanism Hacking described or something categorically different, since it bypasses clinicians almost entirely; and whether the framework has any predictive bite, or whether looping is only ever visible in hindsight. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the hedge or deflection she is most likely to reach for, and close with one further question asking what she would count as evidence against the whole picture.
