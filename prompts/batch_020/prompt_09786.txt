so i've been slowly working through Hobbes (started with Leviathan, mostly for the "nasty, brutish and short" stuff everyone quotes) and got kind of sidetracked by something I don't see people talk about much: how obsessed the guy was with geometry. Not just "geometry is a good example of clear thinking," more like he thought it was basically the only real science humans had ever managed. Then I fell down a rabbit hole and found out he had this insane decades-long feud with a mathematician named Wallis, where Hobbes claimed he'd squared the circle and just never, ever backed down, even after getting torn apart in print over and over, basically up until he died in his 90s.

Here's a theory I've been chewing on, tell me if im onto something or just connecting dots that don't actually connect. My read is that Hobbes thinks we can only be TRULY certain about things we made ourselves — geometry counts because we're the ones defining a line as "a point moved" and so on, so there's no hidden fact we could get wrong, we built the thing from scratch. And I think that's also the real reason he tried to set up his political philosophy the exact same way (strict definitions first, then deductions, Euclid-style) — because if the commonwealth is also just something people construct, through the covenant, then "civil science" gets to be just as certain as triangles are.

So my actual idea is: this is exactly why he couldn't let the circle-squaring thing go, even when he was obviously wrong. If your whole system rests on "I can be certain about geometry because I'm its author," then having a professional mathematician show the world you botched a proof doesn't just embarrass you, it kind of pulls the rug out from under the whole epistemology. So it's not plain stubbornness, or not only that — being shown wrong about geometry specifically was threatening to him in a way it just wasn't for Wallis.

Is this actually close to how Hobbes scholars read him, or have I built a "just-so" story out of like two half-remembered sources? Also not 100% sure I'm even describing the "we can only really know what we make" idea correctly, so feel free to correct that part too. Don't sugarcoat it, I mainly want to know if there's a real throughline here or if I'm forcing two separate facts about the guy together.
