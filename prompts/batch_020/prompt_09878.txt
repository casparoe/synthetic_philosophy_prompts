My brother Dan is getting married in about six weeks and I've somehow ended up on the hook for a speech. Backstory that matters, I promise: a few months ago he was going on about "Ramsey numbers" from some math video (something about how at a party of 6 people you're guaranteed either 3 mutual strangers or 3 mutual friends, whatever that means), and when I went to google it so I could nod along, I fell down a totally different rabbit hole into the actual guy it's named after -- Frank Ramsey, a Cambridge philosopher/mathematician/economist who apparently casually invented like four fields before dying at 26 in 1930, from some botched operation after he got sick swimming in a river. Buried in there was this essay called "Epilogue," and there's a bit near the end I can't stop thinking about, where he basically refuses to feel small or humbled just because the universe happens to be physically enormous -- his point is that size isn't the kind of property that should matter to anyone, stars can't think or love so who cares how big they are, and that his "picture of the world" is drawn in perspective rather than to scale, with actual people huge in the foreground and the stars shrunk down to about the size of an old coin (he actually says "threepenny bits," had to look that up too).

Here's the part that really got me though: since the guy was also literally an economist, later in that same passage he does this deadpan bit where he applies the exact same logic to time instead of space -- something like, sure, the universe will eventually run down and everything will die, but that's so far off that its "present value at compound discount" is basically nothing, so its no reason to feel bad right now. Which cracked me up once I found out that in an actual serious economics paper a few years later he apparently called discounting future generations like that "ethically indefensible" and blamed it on "weakness of the imagination" -- so the same guy thought discounting the future was almost immoral in one paper and used discounting as a punchline about the heat death of the universe in another. Not sure that's even resolvable, but I love it.

Anyway. This is so obviously my brother's whole deal. He's the type to have genuine 1am spirals about how nothing matters because the universe is huge and cold and everybody dies, and his fiancée Priya, without ever reading a word of this guy, is basically a walking demonstration of Ramsey's point -- she's the one who drags him back to "ok but right now, this, us, is good" every single time. Problem is, Dan is a pretty hardcore athiest/materialist, and he will physically wince if my speech has anything in it about fate, destiny, "written in the stars," soulmates, the universe bringing them together, any of that -- which is annoying because that's like 80% of the normal vocabulary of a wedding speech. But I think the Ramsey stuff might let me flip that instead of just avoiding it -- like, the whole point isn't that the stars arranged this, it's that the stars are irrelevant, and that's supposed to be the comforting part, not the depressing one. I just can't get the tone right myself, it either comes out as a philosophy lecture or generic hallmark-card mush. Could you write me the actual speech (5ish minutes, so maybe 700 words) using this as the real spine of it, not just a quote dropped in for flavor -- something that still works for my great-aunts who have no idea who Frank Ramsey is and just want a nice cry, as much as for his old university friends who'll clock the reference immediately? If the "compound discount" line can be snuck in somewhere as a joke for the finance people at the wedding without needing a footnote to explain it, even better, but don't force it if it doesn't fit naturally.
