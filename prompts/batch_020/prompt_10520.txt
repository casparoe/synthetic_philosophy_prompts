I keep running into the same issue whenever contextualism and pragmatic encroachment (subject-sensitive invariantism) are taught side by side: the contextualist camp (DeRose, Cohen, Lewis) and the encroachment camp (Hawthorne, Stanley, Fantl and McGrath) both reach for the very same low-stakes/high-stakes bank case, as though it settled things in their own favor. But the ordinary bank case varies the subject's practical stakes while also, through the way describing high stakes makes error-possibilities salient, plausibly shifting the attributor's own conversational standards at the same time -- so it's not obvious that the case isolates what is actually doing the theoretical work. Could you construct a scenario, or a minimal pair of scenarios, that pulls attributor context and subject context apart from each other -- holding one fixed while varying the other -- so that contextualism and pragmatic encroachment deliver genuinely different verdicts on the truth of some specific knowledge attribution? Please indicate which verdict belongs to which theory, and briefly explain why the standard bank case fails to achieve this sort of isolation on its own.
