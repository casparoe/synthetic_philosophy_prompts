So this has become weirdly relevant to my life over the last month: I'm doing a CS master's, and for fun (masochism, really) a few of us started formalizing the department's intro real analysis and algebra courses in a little Lean-style proof assistant that one of the PhD students built as a side project. It grew enough that there's now a 3-person "foundations" working group that makes calls about the core library, and I somehow got roped into it. We've got an actual vote in about two weeks and I've been asked to write something up beforehand, which is why I'm bugging you about it.

The fight is over whether to bake classical logic into the core. Right now the whole library is built on intuitionistic type theory -- no excluded middle, no choice, unless you invoke them explicitly -- basically like Coq's default. Half the group, mostly the people formalizing analysis, are sick of it: they keep hitting walls where something is trivial classically (a nonempty finite set of reals has a max, equality on some custom type is decidable, whatever) but takes real contortions to prove constructively, and they point out that Lean's mathlib just said screw it and allows classical reasoning pretty much everywhere, and it's the most successful formalization library that exists, so who cares. The other half, mostly people with a programming-languages background, are horrified: they say the entire point of a constructive core is that an existence proof hands you an actual algorithm, which is what would let us eventually compile some of these lemmas into checked code for the algorithms course, and if you add excluded middle or choice globally you loose the ability to tell, lemma by lemma, whether it still computes anything -- you just quietly end up with half the library marked "noncomputable." Someone also mentioned that the "compromise" a couple of people floated, adding choice but not full excluded middle, doesn't actually dodge the problem, because of some theorem (Diaconescu's, I think?) that gets you excluded middle back out of choice anyway. I didn't really follow the proof, just that it's apparently a known result.

Could you draft the memo the working group is expecting from me? I want an actual recommendation with reasoning, not just "constructive purity good" or "classical convenience good" -- and I especially don't want it to dodge the strongest version of whichever side it ends up not fully agreeing with, since I'll have to defend this to both camps in person.
