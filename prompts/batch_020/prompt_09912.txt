Last spring, a pipe burst behind an upstairs bathroom and damaged part of the wall and ceiling below. Once the immediate mess was cleared, I hired a contractor to cut open the drywall, remediate any mold, replace the damaged framing, and close everything back up. He's well reviewed and has done this kind of work for many years, and by every outward sign he did a careful job: he sent photos at each stage, walked me through what he'd found, and the finished wall now looks and smells completely normal. The trouble is that once the new drywall went up, I lost any independent way of confirming that the mold was actually all removed or the framing properly repaired, short of cutting it all open again, which would defeat the point of hiring him in the first place.

I mentioned this to a friend, who said this is simply the ordinary cost of hiring a specialist for work you can't do yourself, and that picking someone reputable is all you can reasonably do. That hasn't fully settled it for me, though, because "reputable" is built entirely out of how someone's past work held up on the parts that were checkable, and says nothing about problems -- regrowth, weakened framing -- that might not surface for years, if they ever do, by which point there would be no way to trace them back to this particular job anyway. I have no actual reason to think this contractor cut corners; the trouble is that an honest contractor and one who quietly cut corners would hand me the exact same photos and the exact same reassurances, so I can't see what more references or paperwork would really establish. Is there some principled way out of this kind of bind, or is trusting someone's word about things you structurally cannot check just something you're stuck doing on faith?
