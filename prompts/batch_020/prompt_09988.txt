I've been reporting a piece on "full discretion" arrangements — basically any setup where you hire someone, hand them real authority to just run with a plan, but agree up front that you (the client) can call the whole thing off at any moment and they have to stop, no argument. Talked to a high-end renovation contractor last week who said something that's been bugging me since: the better and more genuinely invested a contractor is in their design, the LESS that "stop anytime" clause actually protects you, because a contractor who really believes in the plan has every reason to front-load the irreversible stuff — demo the wall, order the custom cabinetry, pour the foundation — so that by the time you'd ever want to use your right to stop, theres nothing left worth stopping. And if you tried to halt her mid-project anyway, she'd push back hard, not because she's scamming you but because from where she's standing, stopping really would be the mistake.

That feels like more than just a renovation-industry thing to me — it seems like a general problem with any arrangement where you give someone real competence and a goal to chase, plus a button you can press to override them whenever you want. Is there some deeper logic for why capability and "stoppability" seem to trade off against each other like this, even when the person genuinely has your interests at heart and isnt just being shady? And is it actually fixable, or is there something structurally self-undermining about wanting an agent that's both highly autonomous/effective AND perfectly indifferent to being overridden? Would love an actual philosophical take on what's going on here, not just "get a better contract."
