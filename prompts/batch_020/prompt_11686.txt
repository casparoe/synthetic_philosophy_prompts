So I'm a backend engineer, and I promise this connects to philosophy even though it starts with software architecture diagrams. At my last job we had this recurring arguement about what the arrows in our service diagrams actually meant: half the team drew "X depends on Y" to mean "X imports Y's package," another chunk meant "X calls Y at runtime and breaks if Y goes down," and someone else meant "X is configured by Y." Everyone agreed each arrow was tracking something real, we just could not agree on wether it was fundamentally the same relationship everywhere or five different things we'd sloppily lumped under one word because they all felt vaguely dependency-shaped. I mentioned this to a friend doing a philosophy PhD, mostly as a joke, and she said "oh, that's basically the whole fight about metaphysical grounding" and now I've fallen down a rabbit hole.

From what I can piece together (please correct me, I'm sure I'm mangling parts of this): there's a kind of orthodox camp in metaphysics, associated with people like Kit Fine, Jonathan Schaffer and Gideon Rosen, who think there's a single, primitive "grounding" relation - the "in virtue of" relation - running across totally different cases: between the physical and the mental, between a lump of clay and the statue it makes up, between a set and its members, between natural facts and moral facts. That unified relation is supposedly what lets you state something as general as "physicalism just is the claim that everything is grounded in physics." Then apparently Jessica Wilson wrote a pretty influential paper (something like "No Work for a Theory of Grounding"?) making basically the same argument I was making about the arrows: there's no single big-G Grounding, just a pile of perfectly respectable small-g relations (realization, composition, set membership, the determinate/determinable relation...) that don't all behave the same way logically, and bolting one all-purpose "grounding" on top either collapses into whichever small-g relation is doing the local work, or is dead weight that doesn't explain anything extra.

Could you write me a dialogue between two metaphysicians who are equally sharp and don't strawman each other - one defending the unified Grounding relation, one on Team Wilson - working through a specific case (the statue and the clay is fine, or swap in a better one if you've got one) instead of staying abstract? I want genuine back and forth where each one is actually responding to what the other just said, not two monologues stapled together, and somewhere in there each side should have to admit the other has landed a real hit, rather than it reading like a clean win for whichever view you personally find more convincing. (For what it's worth, my instinct from the software case is that "depends on" turning out to be five relations in a trenchcoat isn't actually a scandal, so I might already be leaning Wilson - feel free to talk me out of it.)
