A friend of mine and his business partner started a small sports-betting syndicate a few months ago, built around a pricing model for lower-tier tennis markets that they're confident gives them a real edge. I liked the pitch enough to put some of my own savings into their pool, so I get their weekly notes and have an actual stake in how carefully they reason about this. After a long winning stretch confirmed the edge wasn't just variance, the two of them got into a running argument, still unresolved, about how much to stake per bet, and I'd like a considered outside read on it.

(1) Jonas argued that now that the larger sample has narrowed their uncertainty about the edge, they should move to full Kelly staking: betting any fixed fraction below the Kelly fraction just sacrifices long-run growth for nothing, since the Kelly fraction is the one strategy that will almost surely outgrow every other fixed-fraction strategy over time.

(2) Fiona replied that "almost surely outgrows in the long run" is exactly the kind of argument economists have accused Kelly enthusiasts of abusing: winning eventually with probability one doesn't make full Kelly the rational stake, since that conclusion just restates what maximizing expected log wealth happens to entail, and nothing forces them to have log utility rather than some other attitude toward risk.

(3) Jonas answered that this gets it backwards: log wealth isn't privileged by an assumed utility function, but by the fact that their bankroll compounds multiplicatively, so the ordinary expected value of a betting sequence is dominated by absurdly lucky paths that essentially none of their actual futures will resemble; optimizing the growth rate of the one trajectory they will really live through, rather than an average over hypothetical parallel syndicates, corrects a mathematical mismatch rather than smuggling in risk-aversion.

(4) Fiona countered that this proves too much: if what matters is the trajectory they'll actually live through, then it also matters that a bad enough drawdown ends the syndicate before any "long run" arrives -- I lose confidence and pull my stake, or they do -- and once that's priced in, the same trajectory-based reasoning recommends staking below full Kelly, not at it.

(5) Jonas responded that this just collapses into the ordinary reason for fractional Kelly, hedging against error in their edge estimate, and has nothing to do with ruin, since their edge is now precise enough that estimation error isn't the binding concern.

(6) Fiona countered that the two reasons for staking below full Kelly don't compete with each other, and that Jonas still hadn't said why full Kelly, rather than some smaller fraction, is the privileged number, apart from its being what the formula outputs.

Could you go through this move by move and say who has the better of it at each point, rather than just declaring an overall winner, and note what -- if anything -- both of them are still missing? Since it's my money in the pool too, I'd also like to know whether their exchange actually settles which staking rule is sounder, or whether something further would need to be resolved first.
