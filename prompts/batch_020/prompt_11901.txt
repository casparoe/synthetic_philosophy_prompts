I was recently appointed to my university's research ethics board, and my first case has sent me back to physics I haven't touched since undergrad. A physics PhD candidate has proposed a dissertation experiment built on the "quantum suicide" thought experiment: a device wired to a quantum-random trigger that, on each trial, either does nothing or kills the operator instantly, with no other outcome possible. The candidate intends to be the device's sole subject, testing it on themselves repeatedly, and argues that if the many-worlds interpretation is correct, their continued survival across trials is subjectively guaranteed and would itself constitute evidence for that interpretation over collapse theories. Several board members want to reject the proposal on safety grounds alone, but I think the candidate deserves an answer on the merits. Please draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before next week's board meeting: a clear recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with reasoning that takes the candidate's argument seriously, in its strongest form, rather than resting only on the risk of death.
