Question:
Consider the standard two-person bargaining problem (S,d). Both the Nash solution N(S,d) and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution K(S,d) satisfy Pareto-optimality, symmetry, and invariance to positive affine transformations of utility (scale invariance).

(a) [5 pts] Identify the further axiom that seperates the two solutions -- the one satisfied by K but not N, and vice versa -- and state precisely whichever of the two you find less familiar.
(b) [4 pts] Give the formula defining K(S,d) in terms of d and the ideal ("utopia") point a(S,d).
(c) [6 pts] Kalai (1977) also proposed an "egalitarian" solution E(S,d), the Pareto-efficient point with u1-d1 = u2-d2. How does E relate to K, and does it satisfy the same axioms that K does?

Student's answer:

(a) N satisfies Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA): if S shrinks down to a smaller set T that still contains N(S,d), then N(T,d) = N(S,d). K gives up IIA in exchange for individual monotonicity, which says that enlarging the feasible set can never make a player worse off -- if S⊆S', both players should do at least as well under K(S',d) as under K(S,d). This tracks the intuition that Nash's solution can behave perversely, where giving a player strictly more options can actually lower their own payoff, and KS was built specifically to rule that out.

(b) K(S,d) is the point on the Pareto frontier of S lying on the segment connecting d and a(S,d), i.e. the point x with (x1-d1)/(a1-d1) = (x2-d2)/(a2-d2), where a_i(S,d) = max{x_i : x∈S, x_j≥d_j}.

(c) E is basically an "un-normalized" version of K -- instead of equalizing gains as a fraction of the distance to the utopia point, it just equalizes the raw gains ui-di. Since E is defined the same way as K, just without dividing by (ai-di), it satisfies the same list of axioms K does, including scale invariance: rescaling a player's utility rescales their gain ui-di by that same constant, exactly like it does for K, so the equalized point just transforms along with it.
