Been chewing on this for a piece on "post-truth" politics I'm drafting, and I keep bumping into people treating Hannah Arendt's writing on political lying and Harry Frankfurt's "On Bullshit" as basically two words for the same phenomenon. A friend who's finishing a poli-sci PhD insists their basically interchangeable, just different vocab for the same slide into truth-indifference. But when I went back and reread "Truth and Politics" plus the Pentagon Papers essay, Arendt seems to be drawing a pretty specific line between old-fashioned lying (where the liar still has to privately track the truth in order to hide it) and a newer, "organized," image-making kind of lying that tries to remake the factual world wholesale instead of just concealing a piece of it. Frankfurt's bullshitter, on the other hand, is supposedly defined mainly by indifference — he's not tracking truth or falsity at all, just aiming at some other effect entirely.

So which is it: is Arendt's modern political liar basically just a Frankfurtian bullshitter with a fancier vocabulary, or does her account of organized lying require something that Frankfurt's framework genuinely can't capture? I'd love a proper side-by-side of the two, and then a verdict, with reasons, on which lens cuts closer to what's actually going on with modern spin doctors and comms operations. Bonus points for flagging where you think Arendt and Frankfurt would actually push back on each other if you sat them down in a room together. Trying hard not to flatten two genuinely different ideas into one soundbite for this piece, so the more precise you can be about where they diverge, the better.
