Random question from deep in my dissertation research on du Châtelet, but here goes. In the Discours sur le bonheur she argues illusions are actually necessary for happiness rather than something reason should clear away — her go-to example is that you shouldn't wander backstage at the opera and stare at the machinery running the flying effects, because once you see how the trick works the pleasure's gone. But the illusions she has in mind are ones we choose to protect for ourselves, like deciding not to peek behind the curtain. So what do you think she'd say about Instagram/TikTok, where the "highlight reel" illusion is manufactured for you by a platform thats profiting off keeping you scrolling, rather than something your quietly guarding for your own pleasure? Would she still count that as the good kind of illusion her theory needs, or does it being manufactured by someone else for profit actually break the analogy? Try to reason it out from her actual view and not just vibes about what some 18th century philosophe would say about phones.
