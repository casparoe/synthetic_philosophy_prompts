I'm several drafts into a novel that follows the deliberations of a "population council" aboard a generation ship, tasked with setting fertility and resource-allocation policy for a crew that won't reach its destination for another nine generations. One of my viewpoint characters is the ship's resident population ethicist, and I want her private reasoning — rendered mostly through memos and marginalia she drafts ahead of council votes — to reflect genuine, informed wrestling with the field rather than a simplified "the repugnant conclusion is bad, so reject total utilitarianism" gloss. I've read enough population axiology to know that Parfit's original conclusion is really just the entry point: average utilitarianism, critical-level utilitarianism, critical-range views, variable-value (or variable critical-level) theories, and person-affecting views each avoid the repugnant conclusion only by running headlong into some other conclusion that seems just as damning — the sadistic conclusion, the very repugnant conclusion, various forms of the non-identity problem, and so on — and I understand that Arrhenius's impossibility results are usually read as showing that this isn't a failure of imagination on anyone's part, but a structural feature of the problem itself.

For the scene I'm drafting, my ethicist needs to hand the council a one-page memo ranking the major families of views by overall defensibility, given that each of them is going to cost her something. Could you rank the following six positions from most to least defensible, all things considered: total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, critical-level utilitarianism, critical-range views, variable-value theories, and person-affecting views? Please give me the answer as a JSON array ordered from most to least defensible, where each entry names the view and gives a one-to-two-sentence verdict centered on which specific conclusion(s) it is forced to swallow in exchange for avoiding the others, formatted roughly like this:

```json
[
  {"view": "...", "verdict": "..."},
  {"view": "...", "verdict": "..."}
]
```

My critique group keeps flagging the philosophy-heavy chapters as info-dumps unless the character's reasoning carries real stakes, so I'd rather have your honest, considered ranking than a diplomatic refusal to choose — by the end of the chapter she has to recommend one framework to the council, fully aware that whichever she picks, someone will eventually point out the monster living in its basement.
