I'm working on a feature about how generative AI is reshaping white-collar work, and one pattern from my interviews keeps nagging at me. A copywriter told me that the ads she ships are technically hers—she wrote the prompts and chose the final draft—but she couldn't say what, if anything, of herself was actually in them anymore. A software engineer described his day as mostly reviewing code an AI had written, and said it left him feeling like the supervisor of a stranger rather than someone who builds things, even though his output hasn't dropped. Both have quietly stopped sharing early drafts with colleagues or asking for feedback, since there's less a shared craft to discuss now than a personal knack for prompting that feels worth guarding. None of this is about pay, hours, or being shortchanged by their employer; by most measures, they're doing better than before. But there's a common thread of something like hollowness that I can't quite name. Is there something real underneath that feeling, or is it just the ordinary discomfort of learning a new tool?
