Been lurking on LW/ACX-adjacent stuff for like 15 years now and I finally wrote up a little pet theory I've been chewing on, wanted some outside eyes on it before I put it up as a shortform or maybe a full post. Here's the rough version:

Wallach and Allen's old "Moral Machines" taxonomy splits machine ethics approaches into top-down (hand-code the ethical theory as rules), bottom-up (learn it from examples/experience), and hybrid. Their standard critiques were: top-down chokes on the frame problem and on rule conflicts nobody pre-specified for; bottom-up is opaque, inherits whatever biases are baked into the training data, and has no principled way to handle genuinely novel cases; hybrid was supposed to fix this but Wallach and Allen themselves admit nobody really knows how you're supposed to integrate the two halves.

My claim: this 15-year-old taxonomy maps almost embarrassingly well onto the current alignment landscape, and it basically predicted our problems in advance.

- RLHF is bottom-up ethics, full stop. It's literally "learn the reward function from a pile of human comparisons." So of course it's opaque and inherits rater bias and does weird stuff off-distribution (reward hacking, sycophancy, the model telling you what it thinks you want to hear).
- Constitutional AI is the hybrid case almost by design: you write down explicit top-down principles (the constitution), and bottom-up RLAIF is what actually enforces them. Which means it should inherit hybrid's classic unsolved problem: how do you verify the model is actually applying the stated principles instead of learning some cheaper proxy that happens to score well on them in training? I don't think anyone's solved that, we've just gotten better at not noticing.
- CEV is the interesting one. It's not top-down in the "hand code the theory" sense, it's top-down about a *process* (extrapolate everyone's volition, then aggregate). So it dodges the frame-problem critique of old-school top-down. But it inherits a new version of the same problem one level up: who picks the extrapolation procedure, and how do you aggregate across billions of extrapolated volitions when they conflict? And, kind of unfairly, CEV gets to look clean specifically because nobody's ever actually tried to build it. RLHF and CAI have battle scars because people shipped them.

Punchline I want to land on: the old taxonomy wasn't wrong, we just built the bottom-up and hybrid versions and left the top-down one on the whiteboard, and thats part of why it still looks so clean.
