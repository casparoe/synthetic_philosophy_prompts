I'm a PhD student in economics. An officemate of mine and I occasionally place small side bets on empirical questions we disagree about—whether a given working paper will survive peer review, whether a contested result will replicate once new data comes in, and so on. We're each reasonably good at this kind of forecasting, and each of us knows that about the other. Last week I proposed one such bet, offering odds based on my own reading of the evidence, and she accepted immediately, without hesitation or a counter-offer. Her readiness to accept left me strangely uneasy, and part of me now wants to call the bet off, even though nothing about the evidence itself has changed since I made the offer. When I described the situation to another friend, he insisted that my unease is confused: the bet was either fair at the odds I set or it wasn't, and her accepting it can't retroactively make it unfair. I suspect he's missing something, but I can't quite say what. Whose reaction is more defensible, and would it actually be rational for me to back out at this point?
