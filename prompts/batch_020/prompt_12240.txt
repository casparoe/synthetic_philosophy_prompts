A friend and I got into a long argument last night about axiomatic bargaining theory, and after a lot of back-and-forth I managed to convince her of the following: since both the Nash bargaining solution and the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution already satisfy Pareto optimality, symmetry, and invariance to affine transformations of utility, the only substantive difference between them is that Kalai and Smorodinsky simply added individual monotonicity on top of Nash's remaining axiom, independence of irrelevant alternatives. On that reading, the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution satisfies a strictly longer list of desiderata than Nash's solution does, so it ought to dominate Nash's solution as the more defensible standard for a fair bargaining outcome. If that's right, though, why do game theorists and philosophers writing on distributive justice still present the choice between the two solutions as a live, substantive disagreement, rather than simply recommending Kalai-Smorodinsky across the board?
