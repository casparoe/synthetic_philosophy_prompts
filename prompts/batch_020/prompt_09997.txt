My sister's been trying to get pregnant for a few months, and her neurologist wants her to wait another couple months after switching seizure meds before conceiving, since the drug she was on carries a small but real risk of birth defects if she gets pregnant while it's still in her system. She and her husband are 36 and 38 and feeling the fertility clock though, so they've decided not to wait.

This came up at my mom's birthday dinner and turned into a whole debate between my uncle Ray and my cousin Beth, who never agree on anything.

Ray's position: if the baby ends up with some issue that waiting would've avoided, my sister will have harmed her own kid by rushing it. Beth said that doesn't hold up, because waiting means a totally different egg and sperm combo - a genetically different kid, not "this kid but healthier." The kid born now literally couldn't have existed at all if they'd waited three more months, so as long as his life is worth living, there's no version of events where that specific kid exists AND is better off - waiting was never something that could've helped him. She said shed looked into it, and apparently this exact dilemma is a known thing in philosophy called the nonidentity problem.

Ray called that a technicality dressed up as an argument: by that logic, a woman who drank heavily through a pregnancy she didn't know about yet could say the exact same thing about her kid's fetal alcohol syndrome - "if I'd known and waited, I'd have had a totally different kid anyway" - and that's obviously monstrous, not clever. Beth admitted that's a real bullet people who work on this stuff have to bite, but said some of them get around it by arguing you can wrong someone without harming them: carelessly causing a kid to exist with an avoidable bad condition can just be wrong on its own, even though that kid can't say "you made me worse off than I'd otherwise be," since theres no otherwise-him to compare to.

Ray wasn't having it - if it's not about making a specific kid worse off, what stops "wronged but not harmed" from covering literally any less-than-ideal choice a pregnant woman makes? Beth didn't have a clean comeback and just said "somewhere," which Ray pounced on.

Nobody at the table settled it, and my sister looked pretty uncomfortable the whole time, understandably. Could you go through Ray and Beth's argument move by move and say who's got the better of it at each point? Also tell me the biggest thing you think is missing from their exchange, and end with one line just saying which of them came out ahead overall.
