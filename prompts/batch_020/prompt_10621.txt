Quine argued for the inscrutability of reference: even once every fact about a speaker's dispositions to verbal behavior is fixed, incompatible translation manuals — construing an utterance of "gavagai" as "rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," or "rabbit-stage" — can fit that behavior equally well, so that there is no further fact about what the term really refers to. Suppose Quine were confronted with contemporary mechanistic interpretability research, in which sparse autoencoders isolate "features" in a language model's activations that researchers then label with specific referents on the basis of what inputs activate them — for instance, a feature in Claude 3 Sonnet that Anthropic's researchers described as representing the Golden Gate Bridge. Would Quine hold that his argument extends to this case, so that the realist claim that such a feature "really" represents the Golden Gate Bridge, rather than some behaviorally equivalent alternative, is exactly as empty as a claim about the one true translation of "gavagai"? Reconstruct the argument he would most plausibly give, and note any disanalogy between the two cases that a defender of interpretability realism could exploit.
