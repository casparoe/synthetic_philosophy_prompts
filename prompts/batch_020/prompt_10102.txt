I'm putting together a collection of short stories loosely themed around "impossible knowledge," and I've fallen down a bit of a Molinism rabbit hole thanks to a friend whose doing a PhD in philosophy of religion. We got into a long argument a few weeks back about whether God, on the Molinist picture, really "chooses" anything at all, given how constrained he supposedly is by the counterfactuals of freedom, and it's left me with an idea for a story I can't shake.

Here's the setup I want you to run with: a novelist character discovers she has something like scientia media for her own fiction, not full omniscience, just exhaustive, unchosen knowledge of exactly what any version of any character she could write would freely do in any scene she puts them in. At first this feels like a superpower, no more guessing, she can just survey the counterfactuals and pick the best combination. But she runs headfirst into the two problems that make Molinism so contested. First, the grounding problem: these counterfactuals are true before she's written a single word and independent of her will, so what on earth makes them true? Second, and more painful for her personally: she's trying to write a redemption arc for a character modeled closely on her estranged brother -- one where he finally forgives a character modeled on herself -- and discovers there's no feasible version of the story, no consistent set of circumstances she has the power to arrange, in which he does. Every feasible world she tries, he won't budge. It's basically transworld depravity, aimed at one person she actually loves.

I don't want the story to resolve this into a neat lesson -- I'd rather the metaphysical bind stay genuinely unresolved and just sit there, doing real work on the plot rather than functioning as window dressing. Something like 800-1200 words, third person, is roughly the shape I have in mind, but don't sweat the exact count. Can you write it for me?
