I spent thirty-five years as an experimental particle physicist before retiring, and have since been making up for lost time on the philosophy front, mainly through a small metaphysics discussion group that meets monthly at my old university. We recently spent two sessions on the dispute between endurantism and perdurantism over how objects persist through time, and I have not been able to stop translating it into the one piece of physics I know better than anything else, special relativity. I have written up the connection below to bring to our next meeting, and before I do, I would be grateful for a frank assessment of whether it actually works.

As I understand it, endurantists hold that a persisting object is wholly present at every moment of its existence: the same three-dimensional thing, all its parts included, located anew at each successive time. Perdurantists deny this: on their view, what occupies any single moment is merely a temporal part, an instantaneous stage, and the persisting object itself is the four-dimensional sum of all such stages — no more wholly present at any one time than a journey is wholly present at any one milestone.

It strikes me that special relativity weighs heavily on the perdurantist side of this dispute. "Wholly present at t" only has content if there is a fact about which events, across the entire spatial extent of an object, count as occurring at t, and for anything extended in space that requires a way of slicing Minkowski spacetime into a stack of simultaneous "nows." But the relativity of simultaneity tells us that for spacelike-separated events — those lying outside each other's light cones — that slicing is not a feature of the world but of a chosen reference frame: two observers in relative motion will draw the boundary of "the object exactly as it is right now" through different, non-overlapping sets of events, and nothing in the physics says which observer is correct. Whatever the endurantist means by an object being wholly present all at once, it seems to hold only relative to a frame, never simpliciter. The four-dimensional whole the perdurantist posits has no such difficulty: it is the same invariant object under every frame, with different frames merely carving it into different stages for our descriptive convenience.

I would rather have this taken apart than politely praised. First, have I stated the two views fairly, or made endurantism an easier target than it deserves? Second, is the inference actually sound, or have I smuggled in an assumption somewhere? Third, I gather the relativity of simultaneity is already used to argue from special relativity toward eternalism about time, the so-called block-universe picture — is what I have written above simply that argument wearing different clothes, or is it doing separate work specific to the endurance/perdurance question, and does the move already have a name and a standard rebuttal I ought to know before I present it as my own? And finally, a worry I have not settled to my own satisfaction: doesn't the perdurantist's own notion of an instantaneous temporal part of an extended object — the table exactly as it is at noon today — require exactly the same choice of simultaneity slice, and so face exactly the same frame-relativity? If so, I wonder whether I have found a genuine asymmetry between the two views at all, or merely relocated one problem from the object to the stage.
