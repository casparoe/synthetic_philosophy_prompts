ok this has been bugging me and I feel like im missing something obvious. The Meno problem -- why knowledge is supposed to be better than mere true belief -- always struck me as a pseudo-problem once you think about it in track-record terms, kind of like the whole LW thing about making your beliefs pay rent. Take Plato's own example: the guide who's actually walked the road to Larissa and the guide who's never been but happens to guess right both get the traveler to Larissa just fine, on that one trip. But who cares about that one trip? The whole reason we call one of them "knowledge" is that it comes from a process that reliably lands on Larissa across many trips, while the lucky guess is just noise that happened to pay off this one time. So "knowledge > true belief" reduces to "reliable track records beat one-off noise," which is obviously true and not some deep mystery -- so did epistemologists just dress up something simple, or am I missing why this is supposed to be a hard problem?
