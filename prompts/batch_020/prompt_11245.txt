So this happened at a happy hour after we shipped a big release a couple weeks ago and it's been stuck in my head since. Two coworkers, Dave and Priya, ended up in a genuinely heated argument about whether anyone actually deserves credit or blame for anything, kicked off because Dave had been listening to a philosophy podcast about some guy named Galen Strawson and his "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility. Dave's version went roughly: you act the way you do in any situation because of the way you are, your personality and values and whatever; so to be truly responsible for an action you'd have to be truly responsible for being the kind of person who'd do it; but to be responsible for being that kind of person, you'd have had to deliberately make yourself that way earlier, and that act of self-making was itself just something you did because of how you already were before that, so you never actually reach a point where you authored yourself from scratch — its an infinite regress backwards. He threw in that this holds even if determinism is false, because if some of your choices are just undetermined/random, that's not you authoring yourself either, that's just luck. So nobody's ultimately responsible for anything, full stop.

Priya said fine, that indeterminism move was actually pretty clever, she hadn't heard that angle before, but the conclusion is still nuts: it puts a toddler's tantrum and a serial killer's murder plan in the exact same moral boat, and there's obviously a real difference between someone acting on reasons they can reflect on and revise versus someone acting on raw impulse, and that difference has to matter for responsibility. Dave said sure, there's a real psychological difference, but that's not what "truly responsible" is tracking when people say a criminal actually deserves to suffer in the full retributive sense — if nobody authors their own character from nothing, the psychological differences she's pointing to are just different flavors of being shaped by stuff nobody chose. Priya shot back that it sounds like he's quietly built an impossible self-creation requirement into the definition of "responsible," proven nobody meets it, and is now presenting that as some deep discovery about the ordinary concept — nobody on the street means "literally caused myself to exist" when they say "she's responsible for this." Dave's comeback was that it's not some made-up technical sense — its actually what's doing the work every single time someone's gut says a criminal truly deserves to suffer and not just "deserves" in a watered-down, socially-useful sense, and that the reaction is tracking the ultimate notion even if the person could never state the argument.

They ended up just agreeing to disagree and getting another round.
