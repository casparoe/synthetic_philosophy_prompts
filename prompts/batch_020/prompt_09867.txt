I'm on my state's professional engineering licensing board, and we've got a tough one. An engineer who stamped falsified safety-inspection reports for two years is fighting license revocation with a defense that's split the board: he says the firms culture made refusing basically impossible -- push back and you got frozen out of projects -- and, almost in the same breath, insists none of this reflects who he really is as an engineer, just what the job demanded. Half the board calls that real mitigation, the other half calls it self-serving to lean on both excuses at once. Write the memo I'll circulate before the vote: a recommendation on his license and a timeline, one that treats the leniency side as more than 'he's lying' and actually grapples with whether 'the firm left me no choice' and 'that's not who I am' can both be true -- or whether needing both is itself a sign he could've refused the whole time, whatever it cost him.
