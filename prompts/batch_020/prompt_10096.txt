Last weekend a friend of mine, who is several years into a PhD in logic and delights in defending unfashionable positions, spent the better part of an evening trying to convert me to Graham Priest's dialetheism on the strength of its solution to the Liar paradox. When I raised the obvious worry about triviality, he dispatched it easily enough by pointing out that paraconsistent logics are built precisely to block explosion, so a single true contradiction needn't infect the whole theory. Where I thought I had finally landed a real objection was in recalling, from something I had read years ago, that Priest's own three-valued logic LP apparently fails to validate modus ponens once "both true and false" is allowed as a value for a conditional's antecedent. My friend granted the point but waved it off as old news with a known fix involving relevant logics and a properly primitive conditional connective, without being able to reconstruct, on the spot, either why the failure arises or how the fix is meant to work. We ended the night having agreed to disagree about how serious this really is.

I would like your help settling the matter properly. Could you explain, at the level of someone who has had a first course in formal logic but nothing in non-classical systems, exactly why a semantics built to let the Liar sentence come out both true and false forces modus ponens to fail once the conditional is read as material implication, ¬A ∨ B — ideally by giving me the actual three-valued assignment on which the premises both come out true (or "both") while the conclusion comes out simply false, rather than just asserting that this happens. I would then like to understand, in equally concrete terms, what it means to replace that conditional with a genuinely relevant one instead, and why this is supposed to restore modus ponens without quietly reintroducing the explosion that going paraconsistent was meant to avoid in the first place. I am not asking for a verdict on whether dialetheism is ultimately defensible, only for this one piece of machinery to be made fully transparent to me, since neither a philosophy generalist nor a logic PhD student could reconstruct it from memory at the dinner table.
