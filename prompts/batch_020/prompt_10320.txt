A friend of mine, Theo, is really into float tanks -- the sensory deprivation pods, pitch dark and silent, water at skin temperature so you can barely feel where your body ends. After a session he was convinced it proves something: strip away literally everything -- sight, sound, touch, even the felt edge of your own skin -- and there's still a "you" in there, aware that it exists, even though its aware of nothing about the body. So the self must be something other than the body.

My other friend Naomi, who's doing a phil PhD, told him he'd basically just reinvented Avicenna's "flying man" argument, badly -- a float tank doesn't actually get you to zero bodily sensation. You still feel your breathing, your heartbeat if you attend to it, gravity where your back meets the water, and your thoughts are happening via a brain, which is a body part too. So whatever the tank shows, it isn't what he thinks it shows.

Theo said fine, forget the tank then, treat it as a pure thought experiment: imagine someone who from their very first instant has zero bodily sensation whatsoever. Wouldn't they still know they exist? Obviously yes, and that alone shows self and body are two different things.

Naomi said that's actually the weaker move, because now the whole argument rests on what's imaginable, and imagining X without Y doesn't mean they can really come apart -- people could once imagine water turning out not to be H2O, and that didn't make it possible. So even a flawless version of his thought experiment would only show our concept of "self" doesn't obviously include our concept of "body," not that a self could actually exist without one.

They went back and forth on this for like an hour and neither budged. I don't know enough philosophy to referee it -- can you go through it move by move, say who's got the better of it at each point, and whether they're both missing something?
