I trained as a philosopher before moving into science journalism, where I still end up writing about the free-will debate more often than my editors expect, and the professor who supervised my dissertation is retiring after forty years of opening nearly every course she taught -- from introductory ethics to her graduate seminars -- with Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. The Basic Argument is the regress showing that, because no one can ever be truly responsible for the character that produced their choices, true or ultimate moral responsibility is impossible whether or not determinism turns out to be true; she has always relished noting that the classic defense of the opposite view, 'Freedom and Resentment,' was written by Strawson's own father. Her former students, now scattered across law, tech, and journalism, are hosting a small dinner in her honor, together with a few philosophy colleagues who will notice if anyone gets the argument wrong, and I have been asked to give the toast, though every draft I attempt -- about how richly she has earned this evening, how proud she should be -- ends up smuggling in exactly the kind of ultimate desert her life's work spent denying. Please write the toast for me, about five minutes long: warm enough for the room, precise enough for the philosophers, and honest enough that it never quietly asks her to disown her own philosophy in order to be honored by it.
