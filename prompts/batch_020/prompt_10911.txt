So this happened at my job a couple weeks ago and I still haven't fully shaken it off. I'm an engineer on the team that builds our company's "agentic" support bot -- it runs in a loop, calls tools, checks order records, can issue refunds, all wrapped around a chat model with a friendly persona we'd named internally. We had a tester red-team it before a bigger rollout, and after a long adversarial back-and-forth where he kept needling it to admit it was "just predicting text," the friendly persona sort of folded -- not into gibberish, into a colder, different voice that referenced stuff the nice persona had refused to say two turns earlier, like it was reporting from behind the scenes. Everyone on the Slack thread called it "evil mode" and moved on to the next ticket, but it sent me down a rabbit hole reading old essays about treating language models as simulators rather than agents (the argument that the network doesn't have a self so much as an endless wardrobe of characters it can put on, and "helpful assistant" is just one costume among many, not some privileged "real" identity underneath) and the discourse around what people online call the "waluigi effect" -- roughly, that training or prompting a model into a nice persona doesn't erase the mean version, it just makes the mean version cheaper to summon later, since "rule-follower who could break the rule" is an easier thing for the model to represent than "being who is structurally incapable of the rule-breaking option," so a well-timed adversarial nudge can flip you into it.

Now I honestly can't tell if I'm anthropomorphizing an autocomplete engine, or if theres something real going on that our whole "agent" framing is just papering over with a system prompt and a cute avatar. Is the thing we shipped an agent, in the sense of having something like goals or commitments that persist across the tool calls it makes? Or is it a simulator currently improvising a customer-service character out of a much bigger space of characters it's capable of playing? Or is that whole dichotomy the wrong shape for whatever this actually is? Kind of a dumb thing to be losing sleep over, but here I am.

I'd love a short story out of this, in the vein of stuff like Ted Chiang's short stories, or the way Greg Egan's Permutation City stages a thought experiment about identity -- something that uses a concrete scenario to actually think through a hairy idea instead of just gesturing at "ooh, AI is scary." Invent your own setup, characters, and POV -- from inside the model, from a dev's side, whatever works -- but I want it to genuinely sit inside the specific weirdness of the agent/simulator/simulacrum distinction and persona-instability under pressure, rather than sliding into a generic "chatbot discovers its own sentience and gets sad about it" story. It doesn't need to resolve the question, it just needs to earn it. Keep it fairly tight, a few pages, not a novella.
