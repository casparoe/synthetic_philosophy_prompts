A consumer genetic test recently gave me a 12 percent lifetime chance of developing the neurological condition that runs in my family, and the report explicitly notes that this figure is based only on a fixed panel of genetic markers, not on family history. Three of my four grandparents had this condition, and part of me feels that my real chances must be higher than 12 percent, but a friend argues that once you have the scientifically measured probability, adjusting it upward because of a feeling about relatives is just letting anxiety override good data. Is my friend right that 12 percent is simply what I should believe about my own chances, or is there a principled reason, and not merely anxiety, for thinking my actual chances are higher than what the test reports?
