My father died two weeks ago, and I've been asked to give his eulogy this Saturday. In his twenties he read Franklin's autobiography and spent the rest of his life running his own version of Franklin's scheme for moral perfection, right down to the ruled chart with a black mark for every day he fell short. Sorting through his desk, I found forty years of these notebooks, and, just like Franklin's own, the row for Order is by far the most marked up. He loved telling us the story of the man who chose to keep a speckled axe rather than go on turning the grindstone, and he was fully aware of how funny it was that Humility made his list too. I don't want a eulogy that catalogues his failures, but pretending he mostly succeeded would falsify the very thing I admired most in him: something close to Franklin's own conclusion that the trying mattered more than the arriving. Please write the eulogy, about five minutes long, for relatives and coworkers who mostly won't know Franklin's project, so his unfinished self-scoring reads as the most loving thing said about him, not something to explain away.
