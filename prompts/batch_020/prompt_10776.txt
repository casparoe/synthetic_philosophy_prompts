One of my former advisees, Priya, is getting married in a couple weeks and asked me to give a toast, which is an honor but also a bit of a puzzle given how she and her fiancé Theo actually got together. They met in my dissertation seminar, and their first real fight was over the moral worth literature: Priya was defending something close to Barbara Herman's line that an act only has genuine moral worth if the motive of duty is doing the actual work in producing it, even when nicer motives are around too, and Theo was arguing the opposite corner, very much in the Arpaly/Michael Smith vein, that this basically amounts to a kind of fetishism -- that Huck Finn helping Jim escape down the river is worth more, morally, for NOT being done "because it was the right thing to do." I'm told the argument that actually got them talking afterward, over drinks, was about whether Kant's bit on the philanthropist whos gone numb to everyone's suffering but keeps helping people anyway purely from duty is the most admirable passage in the Groundwork or the most repellent one.

That argument, as far as I can tell, never really ended, it just became how they flirt. Apparently theres a running bit where one of them will be doing something completely mundane, like making the other person coffee, and the other will ask, dead serious, "is this out of love or out of duty," and they will actually stop and litigate it for ten minutes.

I want the toast to use that disagreement as its actual backbone, not just a "these two met being nerds, cute right" aside -- the tension between loving someone from duty and loving them from pure inclination should be doing real work in the speech, the way it would in one of their papers, not just decorating it. Here's the constraint that's got me stuck: I was Priya's advisor, not Theo's, and my own sympathies run pretty Kantian, so Theo will absolutely notice if the toast quietly stacks the deck for Herman's side, and I don't want to do that to him on his own wedding day. Plus half the room is Theo's family and Priya's aunts, who have never heard of Kant in their life and just want to hear that these two people love each other. Could you draft me something like a four-minute toast that has at least one moment with actual teeth for the philosophers in the room, without losing everybody else, and that doesn't quietly settle their argument in my favor?
