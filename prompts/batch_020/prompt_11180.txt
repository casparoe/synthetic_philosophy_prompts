I've spent nearly twenty years writing novels, and a pattern in my working life has started to trouble me enough that I'd like someone else's perspective on it. At the start, I was sure that finishing a manuscript would feel like an arrival. It didn't, so I decided the real threshold must be signing with an agent. That came and went the same way, and so did the first book deal, the first serious review, the first foreign edition, and, two years ago, a prize shortlisting I had spent the better part of a decade privately telling myself would finally settle the question of whether any of this amounted to something. In every case the satisfaction lasted about a week before my attention slid forward to whichever threshold came next, so that the sense of having arrived never actually settles anywhere — it is always just past the next achievement.

I described this at a writers' residency last spring to a far more celebrated friend, who told me the fix was obvious: aim higher. Stop measuring the work against shortlists and start writing "for the ages," with an eye on how it might be read in fifty years. Something in me resists this advice, though I could not, at the time, say what. The only moments when I feel no such restlessness are oddly minor ones that have nothing to do with any of these milestones: an afternoon spent on a scene I have no plan to publish, or, further back, a shoebox of stories I wrote at fifteen for the sole entertainment of one friend, with no thought of any other reader. Those hours felt complete in a way the shortlisting never quite did, even though nothing came of them and no one was keeping score.

I would be grateful for help thinking this through properly, since I suspect I am too close to it to see the shape of the problem myself. Is my friend right that the remedy for this restlessness is simply a bigger, more distant goal, or is there something wrong in principle with locating the point of the work in any future milestone at all, however grand? And if it is the latter, what should I make of the fact that the only episodes that felt finished in themselves were exactly the ones with no goal attached to them?
