My uncle Walt died twelve days ago, pretty suddenly, a heart attack, and I got asked to give the eulogy this Saturday, which I am extremely not qualified for. Walt was a machinist his whole life, never set foot in a college classroom, but he read constantly, and sometime in his sixties he got completely fixated on this British guy named Frank Ramsey, who I guess was a philosopher/mathematician type who died crazy young (26, some kind of surgery that went wrong, back around 1930) but before that wrote an essay about the meaning of life that Walt basically treated as scripture. There's a bit in it about how he refuses to feel small next to the stars because stars can't think or love and that's what actually matters, and something about drawing the world "in perspective" rather than "to scale," and a joke about taking no credit for his own body weight the same way he takes no credit for the size of the universe. Walt used to do his own version of that last one, subbing in whatever he currently weighed, and it killed every single Thanksgiving for like twenty years.

Here's my problem. Walt told me straight up, more than once, sober and completely serious, that he did not want anyone standing up at his funeral saying he's "in a better place" or "watching over us" or that "everything happens for a reason" — he thought that stuff was just false and he didnt want to be lied about at his own funeral. But he was equally insistent that this was NOT supposed to come out sounding bleak, like life doesn't matter because the universe is huge and indifferent and we're all just specks. He always said that specific move — big universe, therefore small pointless us — was exactly the mistake his favorite essay was arguing against, and he'd get weirdly heated about it for a guy who fixed lathes for a living. Honestly Im not 100% sure I even follow why it doesn't follow, but he was completely sure, and I don't want to get it backwards in front of people.

The room is going to be maybe fifty people, and forty-some of them are cousins and old coworkers who've never heard of Frank Ramsey in their life, but there's a cluster of about six guys from his old "lunch table philosophy club" at the plant who will absolutely notice if I mangle the actual argument. Can you write the eulogy? I'd like it to run about five minutes out loud, work the stars/body-weight bit in as an actual laugh line rather than a quote just sitting there, and do the real work of explaining — in plain talking-to-your-family language, not a lecture — why "the universe doesn't care about us" doesn't get you to "so nothing we do matters," so the room walks out comforted without me saying a single thing Walt would've called a nice lie.
