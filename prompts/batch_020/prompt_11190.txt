I write literary fiction, and I've lately been drawn to stories whose entire architecture is borrowed from a philosophical argument rather than from a conventional plot — the idea does the work a conflict usually does. I'd like to test the method on a genuinely resistant case.

Write a short story, roughly 1,000 to 1,500 words, built on Schopenhauer's account of music in Book Three of The World as Will and Representation (§52): his claim that music alone among the arts is not a copy of the Platonic Ideas but a direct copy of the Will itself, and so stands beside the phenomenal world rather than depicting it. The story must put two more specific pieces of that argument to structural use, not just mention them: his analogy between the layers of a musical texture — the slow bass, the inner voices, the melody — and the hierarchy running from inert matter through plant and animal life up to self-conscious human striving; and his treatment of a suspended, delayed dissonance as the musical counterpart of a desire whose satisfaction is withheld. Let a single character (composer, performer, or listener, as you prefer) carry these ideas through what happens to them, and resist the easy move of having anyone simply muse that music feels "transcendent."
