Hi - I spent about 35 years doing experimental condensed matter physics, and now that I'm retired I've fallen down a philosophy-of-physics rabbit hole about the arrow of time. Lately I've been reading about David Albert's "Past Hypothesis" - the idea that since the microdynamics are time-symmetric, you have to posit, as an extra law bolted onto everything else, that the universe started out in an incredibly low-entropy macrostate.

Here's a worry that's been bugging me, and I honestly can't tell if it's a real problem or if I'm just going in circles. Boltzmann entropy only gets defined once you've picked a partition of phase space into macrostates - i.e., a choice of wich variables count as "macroscopic." But that choice isn't handed to you by the dynamics itself - it's a modeling decision, presumably one we make because those are the variables that are convenient, measurable, or relevant to creatures like us. So when someone says "the early universe had low entropy," aren't they leaning on a partition that was picked, at least partly, because its the one that makes the arrow come out the way we already know it goes? It feels uncomfortably close to defining "downhill" as whatever direction balls happen to roll, and then acting impressed that balls roll downhill.

Rough notes, not a real essay:
- The dynamics are time-symmetric, no built-in direction there.
- The 2nd law needs the Past Hypothesis (an asymmetric boundary condition) to get any arrow out of that dynamics at all.
- But "low entropy" already presupposes a macro-partition, and that choice is a second, much less discussed place where an asymmetry could sneak in.
- If the partition isn't chosen innocently, doesn't the Past Hypothesis end up explaining the arrow partly by assuming it?

Is this an actual known objection I've just never run into, or does the standard picture have a solid answer for why the choice of macrovariables isn't arbitrary in the relevant sense? Please be blunt if the argument doesn't hold up - I'd much rather be corrected than politely humored.
