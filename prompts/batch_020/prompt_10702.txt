I work on the policy team at a small AI lab, and a question that felt purely academic a month ago now has my name on a memo due Friday. Our assistant was accused, in the same week, of left-wing bias by one set of users and right-wing bias by another over the same contested policy question, and leadership wants a durable principle for whose values a deployed model should encode, not another one-off patch. Two colleagues have staked out opposing camps that I need to pressure-test before drafting my recommendation. One wants the model to act on some aggregate of humanity's actual or suitably idealized values, in the spirit of Harsanyi-style utilitarian aggregation or something like coherent extrapolated volition. The other wants the model to behave like a Rawlsian liberal institution: neutral among reasonable comprehensive doctrines, confined to reasons that could form part of an overlapping consensus, and unwilling to aggregate substantive conceptions of the good at all. Write a dialogue between these two colleagues in which each is given their strongest arguments and each genuinely engages the other's objections rather than talking past them. I especially want the aggregator to confront the worry that something like Arrow's impossibility theorem makes principled aggregation across people's values incoherent, and the neutralist to confront the worry that neutrality is either unachievable or itself a substantive value stance once a system has to make constant first-order content decisions.
