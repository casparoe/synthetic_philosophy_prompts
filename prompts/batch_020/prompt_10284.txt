I TA a seminar on philosophy of mind and AI, and — like a lot of people who came to this material through rationalist blogs and forums rather than through a philosophy department — I've been following the "do LLMs understand language" debate since the octopus test was first making the rounds. One of the short-answer questions on this term's take-home exam was:

"Bender and Koller's 'octopus test' is meant to show that a system trained only on the statistical distribution of linguistic form could never achieve genuine understanding, no matter how much data it sees. (a) Reconstruct the thought experiment and the conclusion it is meant to establish. (b) It has been argued that findings like the Othello-GPT experiments — in which a transformer trained only to predict legal move sequences was found to encode an internal representation of the actual board state — complicate this conclusion. Explain why, and say whether you think the complication is serious. (c) Relate the octopus argument to Harnad's symbol grounding problem, and explain whether Harnad's own proposed solution to that problem would satisfy Searle."

Here is one student's answer, verbatim:

"(a) In this thought experiment, which comes from Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and Shmitchell's paper 'On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots,' two fluent English speakers, A and B, are marooned on separate islands and can communicate only via an underwater telegraph cable. A hyper-intelligent octopus, O, taps the cable and, despite knowing no English to start with, becomes so good at modeling the statistical patterns in their exchange that it can generate plausible responses of its own. O manages to impersonate B for a while without A noticing, but when A is chased by a dangerous animal and urgently cables over asking how to improvise some kind of defense from whatever is lying around, O has no way to answer usefully, because it has only ever had access to the words for the objects and the animal, never to the things themselves. The upshot is that fluent, statistically well-formed output is not evidence of understanding: no matter how much text O sees, it never gets past form to meaning.

(b) The Othello-GPT result is relevant because the model was never given anything beyond sequences of move-tokens, yet it turned out to encode something that functions like the actual state of the board internally. That is evidence that training on form alone can, at least in a narrow and fully specified domain, produce internal structure that tracks the thing the tokens are about rather than just the surface statistics of the tokens themselves. Even so, I don't think this seriously undermines the octopus argument: Othello is a closed system in which the rules fix, once and for all, what a legal move is and what it does to the board, so there is no further question about what the symbols refer to beyond the game itself. Natural language is open-ended and refers to a world that is nowhere specified in the rules of the language, which is exactly the gap the octopus is supposed to illustrate. So the finding complicates the strongest version of the claim that statistics can never track anything beyond statistics, but it does not show that a system trained only on human text is thereby grounded in the world that text is about.

(c) Harnad's symbol grounding problem is the problem of explaining how the symbols in a purely symbolic system can be about anything, given that, taken on their own, each symbol only connects to other symbols rather than to the world — Harnad illustrates this with the case of trying to learn Chinese from a Chinese-Chinese dictionary alone, where every definition just points to more undefined symbols. Harnad's proposed solution is essentially the same move as the 'Robot Reply' that Searle considers in the Chinese Room paper: ground the symbols by connecting the system to the world through sensors and motors, so that at least some symbols are tied directly to sensorimotor experience rather than only to other symbols. But Searle already answers the Robot Reply in his original paper: his point is that wiring the room up to cameras and mechanical arms does not change anything, because the person inside is still just pushing symbols around according to their shape, now with a few more uninterpreted symbols coming in from the 'eyes.' Since Harnad's solution just is the Robot Reply, it fails for the same reason: transduction buys you causal contact with the world, but it does not yet buy you meaning."
