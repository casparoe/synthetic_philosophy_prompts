I've been down a rabbit hole on trolley-problem literature ever since a debate with some friends about whether our gut reactions to these dilemmas are even worth taking seriously, and I keep running into this unresolved fight between Joshua Greene and F.M. Kamm that I can't quite adjudicate myself. Greene's dual-process pitch is basically that the switch/footbridge asymmetry (fine with diverting the trolley onto one guy, not fine with pushing him off a footbridge onto the tracks) comes down to "personal force" — physically pushing someone, or being in direct bodily contact with the harm — tripping some alarm-like emotional response that evolution built for face-to-face violence, not for coordinating impersonal group welfare. The kicker for him is stuff like the trapdoor-type variants, where you hit a switch that drops the guy through a trapdoor instead of shoving him with your hands: same intention, same causal role for the body, but way less personal force, and people's judgments shift back toward "fine." For Greene that's basically a smoking gun that the footbridge intuition is a bias, not a moral discovery.

Kamm obviously doesn't buy this, and a big chunk of her Intricate Ethics-era work (the Loop Track case, the Doctrine of Triple Effect, all that) is basically an attempt to show that what looks like noise to a psychologist is actually a real, fine-grained moral structure — stuff like whether someone's death is something you do "in order to" achieve your goal versus merely "because of" the way you achieve it. Her whole approach implies that you can't debunk an intuition just by finding its proximate psychological trigger, since pretty much any moral judgment has *some* psychological trigger, including the ones we're confident are tracking real principles.

So: who do you think wins this one, at least on the footbridge case specifically? Does Greene actually succeed in showing personal force is a bias we should discount, or does Kamm's more fine-grained apparatus show theres a genuine principle there that a reaction-time-and-fMRI methodology just isn't equipped to see? Feel free to bring in trolley variants beyond the classic two if that helps you make the case either way.
