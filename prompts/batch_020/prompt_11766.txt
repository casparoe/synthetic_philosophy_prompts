I'm a retired field geologist, and since hanging up my rock hammer a few years back I've gotten hooked on philosophy almost by accident -- my daughter is partway through a philosophy MA and keeps leaving books around the house when she visits, and one of them was David Lewis's old book on convention (the one from '69, called, uncreatively, "Convention"). It's been rattling around in my head all week because of something happening at the little science museum where I volunteer on the exhibits committee.

We're redoing the permanent solar-system hall, and the committee has to settle, for real this time, what the new Pluto signage says, before the hall reopens in six weeks. We've actually flip-flopped on this once already: took Pluto off the "planet" wall back in 2007 following the IAU's decision, got a genuinely startling amount of hate mail over it (including, I'm told, one boycotted kids birthday party), and a couple years later quietly softened the language without really resolving anything. One faction on the committee says the matter is closed: the IAU voted in 2006, "planet" now has an actual definition with three clauses, Pluto fails clause three, done. A second faction -- and this is the bit that connects to the Lewis book -- points out that "planet" was never a term anybody had explicitly defined before that; it was just whatever bodies people had, by long habit and precedent, gotten into the way of calling planets, and that a resolution passed by something like 424 astronomers still left in a Prague conference hall on the last day of a meeting (out of some ten thousand IAU members, and a public that was never consulted at all) doesn't obviously have the standing to just overwrite all that by itself, especially given how many people, including plenty of working planetary scientists, never fell in line. A third faction wants to duck the whole fight with a placard that just narrates the controversy, which our director says is a cop-out for an institution who's job is to teach people things, not shrug at them.

I got asked to draft the committee's recommendation ahead of next month's vote, mostly because I'm the closest thing we have to a "science person" on hand, even though I keep telling everyone this particular fight isn't really about astronomy anymore. Could you write that memo for me? I want an actual recommendation, not a list of the three options -- concrete guidance on what the sign should say and how we'd handle another round of angry letters -- and I especially want it to take the second faction's objection seriously on its own terms, rather than just concluding that the IAU are the experts so that settles it. If there's something to the idea that a vote like that can declare a new rule without thereby actually changing what the word conventionally means, at least not right away, I'd like the memo to grapple with that honestly rather than wave it off.
