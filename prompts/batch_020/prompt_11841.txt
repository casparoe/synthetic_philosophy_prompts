Ok so my friend Marco and I had this argument last night that's been bugging me ever since. Backstory: we both started messing around on one of those prediction market apps (like Kalshi) a few months ago just for fun, and lately Marco's gotten it into his head that he can actually make money off it. Last night he goes "I'll bet you $150 the Fed doesn't cut rates in March, I think the market's mispricing it." Thing is, Marco reads way more finance and macro stuff than I do — like three podcasts a week, follows a bunch of econ people on twitter, the whole deal. My first instinct was not to take the bet, but when he pushed me on why, all I had was "because your smarter than me about this," which felt like a cop-out and not an actual reason.

Then at some point I said, half-joking, "wait, if you really do know more than me here, and you know that I know that, why would you even want to bet me? Shouldn't the fact that you're offering it at all tell me something?" He said I was way overthinking a friendly $150 bet between friends and that I should just take free money if I really believed my own read. We argued about it for like an hour and never landed anywhere. I keep feeling like there's a more general puzzle buried in here that isn't really about Marco or the Fed specifically — like, anytime two people who each think of themselves as reasonably rational and reasonably informed still want to bet against each other, isn't there something a little fishy going on, almost no matter what the bet is actually about? Was I right to hesitate, and is there an actual principled reason for it, or was I just being paranoid? I don't want a "trust your gut" answer — I want to know if there's real logic underneath the hesitation, and if so, whether it means I should basically never take bets like this, or just this one.
