I am taking a course on autonomy and paternalism this term, and a situation in my family has me grappling with a problem we have so far only discussed in the abstract. My aunt has bipolar disorder. Two years ago, during a stable period, she wrote out detailed instructions asking that if she ever refused hospitalization again during a manic episode, her family and psychiatrist should override her objections and have her admitted regardless. She is now in the midst of a manic episode and is refusing hospitalization, but she is lucid, articulate, and meets every ordinary clinical standard for decision-making capacity. So this is not a case of an incapacitated person requiring a proxy decision-maker; it is a case of two apparently competent versions of the same person, at two different times, wanting opposite things. Some relatives believe that honoring her earlier instructions is precisely what respecting her autonomy requires, since that is what she asked for while reasoning clearly; others believe that overriding a lucid adult's present refusal is precisely what respect for autonomy forbids, whatever she said two years ago. Is there a principled basis for deciding whose judgment should prevail, or does "respecting her autonomy" simply pull in two directions here, with nothing left to adjudicate between them?
