Two members of a small investing club I belong to got into a disagreement after one of them brought up an economic result called the no-trade theorem: roughly, the idea that if two rational people share the same starting beliefs, one offering to trade purely because they believe they know something the other doesn't should be self-defeating, since the other can infer as much from the offer itself and decline.

(1) Derek argued that this means trying to beat the market through your own research is pointless, since any trade you would want to make for purely informational reasons is exactly the kind of trade a rational counterparty should refuse to make with you.

(2) Naomi objected that this cannot be right as a description of real markets, since enormous volumes of stock change hands every day and some investors clearly outperform others over long stretches, so whatever the theorem proves, it can't be describing actual trading.

(3) Derek replied that almost none of that volume is the kind of trade the theorem is even about, since most of it comes from rebalancing, index funds, and investors meeting their own liquidity needs rather than from a pure bet about who is better informed.

(4) Naomi answered that even setting liquidity aside, two careful, honest analysts can look at identical public financial statements and reach different valuations simply because they weigh the same assumptions differently, and that kind of disagreement involves no private information on either side.

(5) Derek said that "weighing things differently" cannot really be its own category: two ideally rational people who had truly processed the same evidence would reach the same conclusion, so any lasting difference between them must come down to one of them holding some further piece of information, or one of them making a mistake — meaning "we just have different priors" is only a polite name for one of those two things.

Neither would concede the point. Could you go through the five moves and say who has the better of the argument at each one, rather than just declaring an overall winner, and point out anything important that both of them seem to be missing?
