I read enough Bayesian-flavored blogs that I instinctively analyze arguments in terms of priors and updates, which is how I ended up stuck on a disagreement with a colleague this week. For two years our service has had an unexplained latency spike every day at 2:37pm, give or take a minute, fully logged and never in doubt; last week a new engineer proposed a mechanism — tied to a vendor's batch-job schedule rather than anything in our own logs — that predicts a spike within a minute of that same time and also accounts for two older anomalies we had separately written off. My colleague argues that none of this counts as evidence for the new theory, since our credence in the spike was already essentially at 1 before it was ever proposed, so there is nothing left for conditionalizing to do; I suspect she is technically right and that this still cannot be the whole story. Who is correct, and is there a principled way to decide, or does one of us simply have to abandon an intuition?
