A friend and I spent an entire evening arguing over the hole argument in the philosophy of general relativity, and neither of us left sure we actually disagreed about anything. I was defending substantivalism, but only in its "sophisticated" form: to block the indeterminism that the hole argument otherwise generates, I had already conceded that a substantivalist must give up haecceitism about spacetime points, so that two models related by a hole diffeomorphism — agreeing on everything outside the hole, but disagreeing on which metrical properties sit at which points inside it — represent one and the same possibility rather than two distinct ones. My friend, a committed relationalist, seized on this concession: once primitive identity for spacetime points is gone, she argued, nothing remains in my view that isn't already in hers, just dressed up with superfluous talk of "points." I wasn't ready to grant that, though I struggled to say exactly why. Write a dialogue between the two of us — a sophisticated substantivalist and a relationalist — in which we try to settle whether our positions are genuinely distinct or merely verbal variants of the same view, pressing each other on things like modal commitments, explanatory priority, and the charge of carrying superfluous "surplus structure," and let the argument end wherever it honestly leads rather than in a tidy draw.
