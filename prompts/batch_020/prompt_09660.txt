Our student council is drafting a new rule that would let the school discipline students for legal, private things they do outside school — such as getting a tattoo or posting about their relationships online — whenever enough parents and teachers find them morally objectionable, even though no other student is affected. The rule's supporters argue that a school only functions as a real community if its members share certain values, and that tolerating open disregard for those values, even in private, threatens that community. This argument sounds persuasive, yet I remain uneasy about it without being able to say exactly why. Is it actually sound, or is there a flaw in it that I'm not seeing?
