I'm a novelist and I sit on the small board that runs a literary quarterly a few of us started -- we've always let readers comment on reviews anonymously, figuring it's more honest that way. Last month an anonymous pile-on turned a mixed review of a local debut author into a running joke for weeks, digging up an old blog post of hers; she's gone quiet online since. The boards split: two of us want real names required going forward, two think that just protects well-connected authors and scares off honest critics who cant afford to be identified. I keep half-remembering a college class on Kierkegaard writing about exactly this -- the press, "the public," all that -- after some Danish satirical paper did basically this to him, but I never got past the lecture summary. Could you draft the actual memo I'll bring to Thursday's meeting -- a real recommendation with next steps, not a shrug, that takes his argument seriously but still gives the anonymity side its due?
