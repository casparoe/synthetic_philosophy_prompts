Solomonoff induction is often presented as a rigorous vindication of Occam's razor: it assigns hypotheses prior probability according to their Kolmogorov complexity and provably converges on the truth for any computable data-generating process. Yet Kolmogorov complexity is invariant across choices of universal Turing machine only up to an additive constant — one that is independent of the string being described, but can still be made arbitrarily large by choosing a sufficiently pathological machine — and the Solomonoff prior itself is uncomputable, so the convergence guarantee is asymptotic and says little about performance on any particular finite body of evidence. Write an essay assessing whether Kolmogorov complexity and Solomonoff induction really supply a non-circular justification of Occam's razor, or whether their dependence on an arbitrary choice of description language means the framework merely relocates, rather than resolves, the original problem of justifying a preference for simplicity. Weigh the strongest considerations on each side, but close with a definite, defended verdict rather than a noncommittal "it depends"; a qualified conclusion — for instance, that the framework vindicates only a language-relative, comparative notion of simplicity rather than an absolute one — is acceptable, provided it is argued for rather than merely asserted.
