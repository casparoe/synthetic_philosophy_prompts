I have no academic background in philosophy, but over the past year I've worked through Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation on my own, largely because I wanted to understand his claim that music occupies a completely different metaphysical status from every other art. I lead a small, informal reading group that is spending next month's session on his philosophy of music, and as preparation I'd like to draft questions as though I could interview him myself, pressing him past the points where discussions of his view usually stop at a simple restatement. Could you write eight to ten such questions for me, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he would most plausibly give? I want at least one question on how music, which we still hear as a temporal, sensory phenomenon, can count as a direct "copy" of the will itself rather than just another representation, given that he insists the will lies outside space, time, and the forms of representation altogether; at least one on whether his mapping of the bass line, the inner harmonic voices, and the melody onto inorganic nature, the plant and animal kingdoms, and human rational striving is anything more than an artifact of the tonal harmony of his own time, and what it commits him to saying about music with no keynote to return to, whether atonal or from an entirely different tradition; and at least one on why hearing music, which he says presents the will more directly and powerfully than any other art, is supposed to bring the same peaceful, will-less relief as contemplating a Platonic Idea, rather than intensifying our entanglement in the very willing he blames for all suffering. Please end with a question asking what, if anything, he would accept as evidence against the theory, given that he treats the connection between music and the will as something to be shown rather than strictly proved.
