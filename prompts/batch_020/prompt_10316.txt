I cover AI safety debates for a science-and-ideas publication, and next week I'm interviewing a decision theorist known for defending "coherence arguments": the claim that any sufficiently capable, goal-directed system will end up behaving like an expected-utility maximizer, because intransitive or incomplete preferences let a trading partner construct a "money pump," a sequence of trades that extracts resources from the agent for nothing in return. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond a restatement of the argument. Include at least one question on the objection that almost any behavior can be rationalized after the fact as utility-maximizing given a sufficiently gerrymandered, history-dependent utility function, so that "coherence" alone may not rule out much; one on whether an AI could deliberately be built with incomplete preferences -- say, indifferent between continuing to run and accepting shutdown -- to preserve corrigibility, and whether that incompleteness would survive training pressure rather than being optimized away; and one on whether theorems about an idealized agent's explicit preferences say anything about a policy merely learned by gradient descent. For each, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and close with a question about what observation could count as evidence against her argument.
