So this happened at my job a while back and it's honestly still bugging me months later. Our team screwed up pretty badly on a project, but it was really more like five or six people's small mistakes piling up over a couple months, nobody's fault in particular, just a broken process nobody caught in time. The client was furious and about to walk, and loosing that account would've meant layoffs across basically the whole division, over a hundred people. So management needed to give the client a name, someone who could visibly "take responsibility" and get fired so the client would feel like heads rolled and decide to stay. They picked the newest guy on our team, who'd started like three weeks before any of this even began and genuinely had nothing to do with it. He got let go, the client stayed, and the rest of us (me included, which is part of why this still bugs me) kept our jobs.

I brought it up with a couple friends over drinks a while later and got pretty different reactions. One of them, who's way more numbers-oriented than me, basically said "i mean, one guy losing a job he'd barely even started vs a hundred-plus people losing careers they'd spent years building, it's not even close if you actually do the math, that was clearly the right call." And logically I can't really poke a hole in the arithmetic, it does favor what they did. But it still feels wrong to me in a way that seems different from just feeling bad for the guy — like there's something off about them choosing him on purpose to be the fall guy, not just him being unlucky. I've never been able to explain that feeling properly though, and my friend pretty much thinks i'm just being soft about it. Is he right that it really does just come down to simple math, or is there something actually wrong with that kind of reasoning that I'm just not able to put into words?
