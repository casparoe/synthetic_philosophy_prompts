I'm a software engineer on the data-visualization standards committee at the county health department where I work. Our dashboards' icon charts (one syringe icon per 10,000 vaccine doses administered, say) have always followed a rule inherited from Otto Neurath's Isotype method: show a larger quantity by repeating more icons of a fixed size, never by enlarging a single icon. A new hire wants an exception for the mobile dashboard, where there's no room for dozens of tiny repeated icons on large counts; she'd rather scale icon size, with exact figures available on tap. Half the committee calls this a reasonable concession to a medium Neurath never designed for; the other half says it guts the point of the rule. Please draft the short memo the committee will vote on Monday: recommend keeping the rule, dropping it, or carving out a narrow exception, and make sure the reasoning engages with why Neurath insisted on repetition over resizing in the first place, not just the modern perceptual-research case against scaled icons.
