Write a dialogue dramatizing a real fault line in the corrigibility literature on the Alignment Forum and LessWrong: whether the shutdown problem can be solved by an expected-utility maximizer at all. One speaker defends the classical MIRI-style position that coherence arguments (in the spirit of Yudkowsky's "Coherent decisions imply consistent utilities") push any sufficiently capable planner toward complete preferences, so that the best available fix is something like Armstrong's utility-indifference construction, accepted as a leaky patch rather than a genuine solution. The other defends the position associated with Elliott Thornley's work on the shutdown problem: that the desiderata for shutdownability — no incentive to either hasten or prevent shutdown — can only be jointly satisfied by an agent with incomplete preferences over trajectories that differ in shutdown timing, and that such an agent can resist the standard money-pump argument for completeness by choosing resolutely rather than sequentially. Have the two actually engage rather than talk past each other: the first should press on whether resolute choice genuinely blocks exploitation or merely relabels it, and the second should press on whether utility indifference is reflectively stable at all, i.e., whether the agent has any reason to preserve the correction term in its successors. Neither position should be a strawman, and the dialogue need not end in agreement — let it stop wherever the disagreement actually bottoms out.
