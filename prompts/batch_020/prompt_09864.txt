Quick question for a column I'm noodling on about weird decision-theory thought experiments. I finally read up on Kavka's toxin puzzle and I don't really get why anyone finds it puzzling? The setup is: a billionaire will drop a million bucks in your account by morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink some nasty toxin tomorrow at noon that'll make you sick as a dog for a day but leaves no lasting harm. So why not just tell her you intend to, bank the money, then skip the toxin the next day since by then theres no upside to actually going through with it? Seems like the whole "paradox" just boils down to "people can say things they don't mean," which isn't exactly a profound insight into rationality. Am I missing something, or is this one of those puzzles that's more famous than it deserves to be?
