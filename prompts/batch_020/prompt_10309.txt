So over the weekend I fell down a philosophy rabbit hole after a coworker linked some paper in our team's random-chat Slack channel (as one does), about this idea in metaphysics called "grounding" - the notion that some facts hold "in virtue of" other facts, like how a vase's fragility is grounded in facts about its molecular structure, or a whole is grounded in facts about its parts. As a backend engineer, everything I'm reading about this maps disturbingly well onto a dependency graph: grounding facts sound like edges saying "this fact is explained by / depends on that one," and it's all supposed to bottom out in some set of "fundamental" facts that aren't grounded in anything further - basically leaf nodes with no imports. Except every source I read is weirdly insistent that grounding is NOT the same as causation, NOT the same as logical entailment, and NOT even the same as supervenience - apparently even though mental facts supervene on physical facts (no mental difference without some physical difference), that alone still doesn't entitle you to say the physical grounds the mental.

That's the bit I can't get my head around. If A and B always co-vary and A is obviously the "lower level" one, what is grounding adding on top of that which isn't just necessitation plus some hand-wavy talk about "priority"? And then theres this other wrinkle where people worry whether the grounding claim itself ("A grounds B") needs its own ground - which sounds exactly like worrying about a circular import, or a build dependency chain that never actually bottoms out at a real base case. Do people working on this have an actual principled story for why that regress stops (or why it's not vicious even if it doesn't), or does it eventually just come down to shrugging and declaring some facts "brute"? Could you walk me through, for someone with zero formal philosophy training, what work "grounding" is really doing that causation, entailment, and supervenience don't already do on their own, and how the field actually handles this grounding-of-grounding regress problem?
