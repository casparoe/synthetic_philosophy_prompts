I've got the headline idea of modal realism down fine, but one specific move of Lewis's has been bugging me for a week. He says worlds can't share parts, since a world is just everything thats spatiotemporally hooked together and isolated from everything else -- so the same guy can't literally exist in two worlds, which is why he needs counterpart theory instead of plain trans-world identity. Then I ran into Kripke's "Humphrey" complaint: if some other fellow who merely resembles Humphrey wins the election over in another world, that doesn't seem to be a fact about what Humphrey himself could've done, its a fact about a stranger who looks like him. Digging further, Lewis's comeback seems to be that which counterpart "counts" depends on whatever similarities are contextually relevant, not some fixed relation -- but that just sounds like it makes "Humphrey could have won" true or false depending on what we feel like talking about, rather than a fact about Humphrey. Can you walk me through whether that's a fair reading of Lewis, and whether it actually answers Kripke or just relocates the problem one level down?
