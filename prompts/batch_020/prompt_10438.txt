I'm my high school's valedictorian this year and have to give the graduation speech this spring to a class of about two hundred, plus all our families and teachers. For an AP Seminar research project, I read past the "we rise together" version of ubuntu that everyone already knows and into Ifeanyi Menkiti's much harder claim that personhood itself is not given at birth but achieved gradually through participation in community life and moral growth -- he actually describes early childhood as a kind of "it-status" that only becomes full "person-status" later. I want to build the speech around that idea, but it creates a real problem: I'm being singled out for an individual academic ranking, and Menkiti's own gradational language makes it easy to imply, without meaning to, that classmates who aren't up on this stage are somehow less fully realized as people. Swinging the other way and insisting the honor really belongs to everyone but me would be just as dishonest, since plenty of people worked as hard as I did without getting the title. Two classmates did the same research seminar and read Kwame Gyekye's "moderate communitarian" reply to Menkiti along with me, so they'll notice if I flatten this into a slogan, but almost nobody else in the room has heard of either philosopher and just wants a speech that feels true. Could you write it, about five minutes long, in a way that actually uses the disagreement between Menkiti and Gyekye to earn the ending, instead of just decorating an ordinary graduation speech with the word ubuntu?
