So this happened at a barbecue two weekends ago and its been rattling around in my head since. Two friends of mine, Marcus and Elena, got into it after someone made an offhand joke about whether an LLM "really" decides anything. Marcus did a neuro PhD before jumping ship into ML infra, and Elena did a philosophy minor before she became a backend dev, so both of them know more than the average person about this stuff, they just ended up in totally different places and neither one budged. I'm just a Python/Go dev with zero philosophy training, so I couldn't referee in the moment, but I wrote down the gist of it on the drive home because it felt like there was something real at stake and not just people talking past each other.

Here's roughly how it went. (1) Marcus opened with: neuroscience tells us every action already has a full, sufficient physical cause in the brain, and physics is "causally closed," meaning nothing non-physical ever needs to be added on top to produce a physical effect. So if you also want to say beliefs and desires are something distinct from brain states, not literally the same thing, then they can't be doing any actual causal work — they're just riding along for free. Your only real options, he said, are that the mind reduces to the brain, or the mind is causally inert. (2) Elena said that's nuts, because then "the null pointer deref caused the crash" wouldn't be a genuine causal claim, just a polite fiction, even though that's literally what she gets paid to diagnose and everyone treats it as true — the low-level physical story being complete doesn't make the bug-level story false, it's just a different level of description. (3) Marcus said the bug analogy backfires on her: a bug just is a pattern of physical states, redescribed at a higher grain, so pointing at it isn't adding a second cause, it's renaming the one cause that's already there. But the entire reason to call the mind "distinct" from the brain, instead of just another name for brain stuff, is to deny yourself that redescription move — she can't have the mind be genuinely separate from the brain and also just a relabeling of it. (4) Elena bit the bullet: fine, say this particular belief in this particular brain just is this particular neural firing, so there's no second cause and no overdetermination — while the type, "believing it's raining," can still get realized by completely different physical stuff in different brains, or chips. So mental and physical were never two competing causes to begin with, just one event sorted two ways. (5) Marcus said that gives up the exact thing she was trying to protect: if only the specific physical token ever does any causing, and the mental type as such carries no causal weight of its own, then generalizations like "beliefs cause actions," or her own "this class of bug causes this class of crash," aren't causal claims at all, just handy summaries — which means, by her own logic, her field isn't describing anything causally real either.
