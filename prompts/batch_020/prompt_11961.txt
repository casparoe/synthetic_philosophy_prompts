Question: Explain how, according to the Second Essay of On the Genealogy of Morality, "bad conscience" originates and is subsequently moralized into the feeling of guilt (Schuld). Situate this account in relation to the slave revolt in morality described in the First Essay, and address what, if anything, Nietzsche thinks follows for the legitimacy of a practice like punishment once we trace it back to such an unflattering origin.

Student answer: Nietzsche's account begins from the etymological kinship between Schuld (guilt) and Schulden (debts): the feeling of owing a moral debt derives from the very concrete, contractual relation between creditor and debtor, in which a creditor unable to collect payment was entitled to extract compensation in the coin of the debtor's suffering, and the infliction of cruelty was itself a kind of pleasurable spectacle that made up for the loss. This is of a piece with punishment more generally, which has always relied on pain, since, as Nietzsche puts it, only what continues to hurt stays in the memory; early societies effectively branded their rules onto forgetful human animals through a mnemotechnics of pain. Bad conscience proper is best understood as a continuation of this same dynamic: it is what happens when the ressentiment behind the slave revolt in morality, unable to discharge itself outward against the strong, is turned back against the self, producing the self-torment Nietzsche calls bad conscience. As human communities came to feel indebted not only to living creditors but to their tribe and ancestors, and eventually to God as the ultimate creditor, the debt swelled to infinite, unpayable proportions, and bad conscience was moralized into the properly Christian sense of sin and guilt. Because Nietzsche traces this whole apparatus back to a history of cruelty, resentment, and the creditor's right to inflict suffering, he concludes that guilt and the punitive practices resting on it are thereby exposed as illegitimate — a moral technology we are entitled to discard now that its shameful origin has been laid bare.
