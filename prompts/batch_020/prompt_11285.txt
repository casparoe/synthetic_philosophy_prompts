Throughout my career as a field ecologist, a recurring irritant in my statistical work was the fact that the "survival probability" I reported for any single tagged animal depended entirely on which comparison group I used to compute it. A three-year-old female otter's estimated annual survival differed depending on whether I pooled her records with every otter in the study, with otters of her sex and age class only, or with the handful of animals trapped at her particular site in that particular year — and each of these groupings was statistically defensible, yet they yielded different numbers for what was supposedly one fact about one animal.

Since retiring, I have been reading a good deal of philosophy of probability, and I have learned that this has a proper name: the reference class problem. As I understand it, this is standardly presented as a specific embarrassment for the frequentist interpretation of probability, since frequentism defines a single case's probability as a limiting relative frequency within some reference class, and any individual belongs to indefinitely many overlapping classes that give different frequencies, with no principled way to choose among them short of narrowing all the way down to a class of one, where frequency becomes trivial or undefined. It seems to me that this whole difficulty simply dissolves once one adopts a subjectivist or Bayesian view of probability instead, since on that view a probability is just a suitably rational agent's degree of belief, and such a degree of belief is never defined by appeal to a reference class in the first place — so the Bayesian owes no one an answer to "which class?"

If that is correct, I am puzzled as to why philosophers and statisticians continue to discuss the reference class problem as though it were a deep, unresolved difficulty for the interpretation of probability in general, rather than as a specific and by-now-curable defect of frequentism that the subjectivist simply never inherits. Am I missing something that would make this a live problem even for someone who has already abandoned frequentism in favor of personal or rational credences?
