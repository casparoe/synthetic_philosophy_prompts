I've only really gotten into philosophy since retiring a few years back, so bear with me if this has an obvious answer. I spent forty years as a physiologist telling medical students that adrenaline is basically what fear is, which is why last week has been bugging me. I'm on a beta-blocker for an arrhythmia, and on the drive to school a truck ran a red light and nearly hit us with my granddaughter in the back seat. Twenty years ago that would've left me shaking and sick for an hour afterward. This time: nothing, no pounding heart, no sweat, the drug won't allow it. But I swerved and braked exactly right, knew exactly how dangerous it was, and drove the rest of the way overly careful - though I honestly couldn't tell you if I felt anything in the moment or not. My wife insists I was obviously still afraid, just not showing it. My cardiologist says the drug did exactly what its meant to and theres nothing more to it. Neither is really answering what I want to know: if the bodily side of fear was switched off but the 'this is dangerous' judgment was fully intact, was there fear in that car or wasn't there? And if the honest answer is 'not really' - should that worry me, still driving my grandkids around?
