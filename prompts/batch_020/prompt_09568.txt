I've been working through Luis de Molina's concept of "middle knowledge" for an independent project in my philosophy of religion elective, and I'd like to see the strongest version of the debate it provokes rather than a summary of it. Could you write a dialogue between a Molinist and a Calvinist (a theological determinist) arguing over whether counterfactuals of creaturely freedom can really do the providential work Molinists assign to them? Have the Molinist explain how such counterfactuals let God providentially order a world of free creatures without determining their choices, since they are true logically prior to God's decree to create. The Calvinist should press the grounding objection as sharply as possible: if nothing about the creature or the circumstances necessitates the counterfactual, what makes it true rather than false, or true rather than simply lacking a truth value? Have the Molinist respond with a specific, defensible move rather than just restating the position, and have the Calvinist engage with that exact move rather than repeating the objection. Somewhere in the exchange, get them to address whether appealing to transworld depravity actually distances God from creaturely evil, or just relocates the problem to God's choice of which feasible world to actualize, given that middle knowledge means God knew all along which evils that choice would bring. Keep both sides equally sharp, with each turn responding to what the other just said rather than talking past it.
