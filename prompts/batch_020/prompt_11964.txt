So this happened at my friend's engagement party, of all places, and I haven't been able to stop turning it over -- partly because I think I'm going to steal the shape of it for a scene in the novel I'm drafting (there's a sociologist character my writing group keeps telling me to cut, and this might be her redemption arc). Two people I'd only just met, Dana and Marcus, ended up in a long back-and-forth about whether saying "race and gender are socially constructed" is really the same kind of claim as saying "money is socially constructed," and I was sober enough to remember most of it but not sober enough to tell who was actually winning.

It went roughly like this. (1) Marcus opened by saying that if "social construct" means anything, it has to mean race and gender are basically collective fictions, like made-up rules, so that in principle we could just stop enforcing them and they'd dissolve. (2) Dana said money refutes that: it's entirely made up, yet completely real and non-optional -- you can't pay rent by declaring dollars fictional -- so "constructed" clearly can't mean "not real" or "easy to drop." (3) Marcus answered that money was constructed on purpose, by mints and treasuries and laws, while nobody voted race into being, it was just imposed by force, so the two aren't built the same way. (4) Dana came back that plenty of currencies were also imposed, by conquest or colonial administrations or tax law backed by state violence, so "agreed-to vs. imposed" doesn't actually separate money from race the way he wants it to. (5) Marcus's last move was that there's still an asymmetry in staying power: kill belief in a currency and it can collapse in a year or two, but structural race and gender effects keep grinding on for generations after most people say they've dropped the underlying beliefs, which suggests something less optional is going on than with money.

Neither of them budged and the party moved on to cake before it got resolved, which is probably for the best, but I definately want a real answer now. Could you go through moves (1) through (5) and say who's actually right at each one, not just who's ahead on points overall? I'm especially curious whether Marcus's move (5) is a genuine disanalogy that sinks the whole money comparison, or whether there's a decent reply available to Dana that she just didn't happen to make on the spot -- and whether they're both missing some more basic distinction that would clear this up. End with a line that says exactly "Verdict: Dana" or "Verdict: Marcus", whichever you think is more right overall.
