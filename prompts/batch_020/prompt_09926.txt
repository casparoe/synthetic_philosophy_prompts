Last night a friend and I spent the better part of an hour arguing about a disagreement from my workplace, and neither of us came away satisfied. A colleague and I each pushed an update to our company's billing system on the same afternoon; it later emerged that either update, entirely on its own, would have been sufficient to corrupt the same batch of customer records, and the two updates had nothing to do with each other beyond their timing. In the review that followed, my colleague argued that neither of us was actually responsible for the corruption: undo only his update, and the records are still corrupted because of mine; undo only mine, and they are still corrupted because of his. On that basis, he concluded that neither update made any real difference, so neither of us caused anything. My friend was adamant that this reasoning was a dodge but couldn't say exactly why, and I wasn't able to settle it either. If the corruption would have happened regardless of what I specifically did, in what sense did my update cause it, and is there a principled way of thinking about cases like this?
