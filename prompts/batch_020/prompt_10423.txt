Since retiring from a career in physics, I've been reading my way through formal epistemology, where I've been struck by a wrinkle in Richard Jeffrey's treatment of uncertain evidence — what he called probability kinematics. I had always treated it as almost self-evident that Bayesian updating shouldn't care about the order in which independent pieces of evidence arrive, but successive applications of Jeffrey conditionalization to two different partitions generally fail to commute, even though the rigidity condition is, in effect, automatically satisfied at each individual step. I can reconstruct the failure with a small worked example, but I'm missing the underlying intuition for it. Could you explain why rigidity holding locally, at each step, doesn't suffice to guarantee path-independence globally — and whether some natural, non-question-begging condition relating the two partitions would restore commutativity?
