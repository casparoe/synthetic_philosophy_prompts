So I've been down a probability-theory rabbit hole this week and keep getting stuck on the Judy Benjamin problem in a way where I want an outside argument rather than more of my own circular thinking. I believe this is originally a van Fraassen puzzle aimed at knocking down relative-information-minimization as a general updating rule, but I could be misremembering the source. Quick recap in case its not front of mind: Judy gets dropped somewhere in a region that's half Red territory and half Blue, and each territory is itself split 50/50 into a "Headquarters" area and a "Second Company" area, so all four cells (Red-HQ, Red-2nd, Blue-HQ, Blue-2nd) start out at 1/4 apiece. Command radios her, the message is garbled, and the one bit that survives is "if you're in Red, it's 3:1 odds you're in the HQ area" -- so the new information is just P(HQ | Red) = 3/4, nothing at all is said about Blue.

Do the "obvious" thing and you hold P(Red) = P(Blue) = 1/2 fixed, since the message is silent on that, and just redistribute inside Red to 3:1: Red-HQ = 3/8, Red-2nd = 1/8, Blue-HQ = Blue-2nd = 1/4. But if you instead treat P(HQ|Red) = 3/4 as the only constraint and minimize cross-entropy against the flat prior over the four cells (which is what Jaynes and the max-ent crowd say you should actually do), you get something noticeably different: Red-HQ ≈ .3505, Red-2nd ≈ .1168, Blue-HQ = Blue-2nd ≈ .2663 apiece. So P(Red) actually drops to about .467 and P(Blue) rises to about .533, even though the Blue-internal split stays exactly even. A message that's ostensibly only about the inside of Red territory ends up moving your credence on Red vs. Blue itself, and that's the part everyone I've brought this up with on various forums finds fishy.

Here's what I want from you: argue, as rigorously as you can, for the harder position -- that Judy is in fact rationally required to move off P(Red) = 1/2 here, that the "obvious" partition-preserving update is actually the unprincipled one, and that none of this amounts to manufacturing information out of nowhere. While you're at it, don't dodge the standard objection that minimizing cross-entropy isn't invariant under how you carve the space into cells to begin with (a change of variables can change what counts as the "flat" reference measure), so nothing guarantees uniform-over-these-four-cells was ever the privileged prior -- I don't think that objection is fatal here but I've only ever seen it gestured at, never actually met head on. Feel free to get technical; I'd rather have a tight, correct argument than a hand-wavy one.
