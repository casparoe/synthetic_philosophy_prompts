This came up in a reading group I'm part of that has spent the last few weeks working through the standard rationalist-blogosphere material on bet sizing: Thorp's old papers, the relevant stretch of Poundstone's Fortune's Formula, and a handful of LessWrong posts that fold the Kelly criterion into Scott Garrabrant's "geometric rationality" framework. Between all of that, I think I finally have a solid grip on why maximizing expected log-wealth is the right response to repeated favorable bets, rather than naively maximizing expected wealth itself. What's still tripping me up is the standard advice I keep running into, from poker forums to EA-adjacent posts about sizing prediction-market positions, to bet only a fraction of full Kelly — half-Kelly, say — rather than the full amount.

As I understand Breiman's classic result on proportional betting, a full-Kelly bettor will, with probability one, eventually pull permanently ahead of anyone running a different fixed-fraction strategy, and will reach any sufficiently large wealth target faster, in expectation, than any other strategy could manage. If the theorem really delivers that, betting less than full Kelly looks like it can only be an ad hoc concession to risk aversion, tacked onto a criterion that was already supposed to be the objectively correct answer to the risk-aversion question — there shouldn't be any further growth-rate-relevant consideration left for fractional Kelly to be tracking. And yet Thorp himself, who was about as rigorous a Kelly bettor as has ever existed, spent much of his career recommending fractional Kelly in practice, and I gather this whole dispute traces back to Samuelson's objections to Kelly enthusiasts, which I've never seen spelled out clearly.

So: given that Breiman's theorem already identifies the strategy that wins almost surely, why do so many people who clearly understand the math — Thorp very much included — treat fractional Kelly as the more defensible position rather than as a compromise for those who haven't fully internalized the proof? And does Garrabrant's "geometric rationality" reframing change anything substantive about this debate, or is it best read as restating, in more philosophical language, a question that Breiman's mathematics had already closed?
