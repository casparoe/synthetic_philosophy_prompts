I've spent years reading LessWrong and its offshoots, and I now sit on the three-person council of an income-sharing group house. Our charter's 'equal surplus' clause pools and evenly splits any discretionary income beyond a living stipend, however earned, among all seven residents — adopted unanimously at founding to keep money and status from distorting house dynamics. One member, a freelance contractor, has spent the past year doing well-paid work directly for several housemates who sought him out and paid voluntarily; he now invokes Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument, noting that since the starting holdings were just and each transaction was a free exchange, the resulting inequality is just regardless of the charter's pattern. Recognizing the reference hasn't resolved anything, though: the clause has only ever covered outside income and windfalls, so its text doesn't say whether payments between members count the same way. Other council members add that several of his 'clients' only had spare cash because they'd quietly cut their own house contributions, so it isn't obviously true that non-participants are unaffected. Please draft the council's short written decision: rule on whether the clause applies to this income, propose a concrete resolution for this month, and take the contractor's strongest argument seriously rather than simply asserting that the charter must be honored as written.
