So last night my friend Priya and I almost placed a bet and then spent like two hours arguing about whether we even should, and neither of us backed down, so now I want a neutral third party (you) to do it justice. Context: we were at a bar with a game on, and she was about to put $50 on the under because her cousin works with the team's training staff and told her their center's ankle is worse than the injury report says. Right as we were about to shake on it I said "wait, hold on, doesnt the fact that you specifically want to bet ME on this, at these odds, already tell me something?" and that derailed the entire night.

Priya's basic view is Hayekian: odds/prices are close to epistemically magic, just everyone's scattered private info compressed into one honest number, and trading is exactly the mechanism that keeps the number honest — so if you think the line is wrong, the right move is to bet, and if someone offers YOU a bet, the right move is usually to update toward thinking they know something, not to refuse. I countered with the no-trade theorem thing I half remember from a game theory elective — Milgrom and Stokey, building on Aumann's result that two Bayesians with a shared prior can't knowingly agree to disagree — where if you actually run the logic, a purely information-driven offer to trade should unravel completely before it ever happens (the "Groucho Marx" move: wanting to trade with someone is itself bad news about the trade). So two rational, risk-averse people who share a prior shouldn't be able to make that bet at all once they think it through carefully enough. Given how much real trading and betting obviously happens, my point to her was that something in that story has to be false — either people don't really share priors, or alot of that volume is hedging/liquidity/overconfidence rather than information — and either way we should be a lot less reverent about "the odds must be right." She thinks I'm using a cute theorem to dodge a plain empirical fact she sees confirmed every time she checks a market.

Can you write the dialogue we should've had instead — two characters (call them whatever you like), standing right at the bar with the fifty still on the table and a phone open to the betting app, actually engaging with each other's strongest point instead of talking past it? Neither of us was bluffing or being dumb, so I don't want either side to be the pushover here — Priya's position is basically the mainstream "markets are wise" one, mine is the theory nerd's "well, actually, here's why that can't be quite right" one, and I think both deserve to be played as stronger than people usually give them credit for. Extra credit if the argument over whether to actually make the bet ends up illustrating whatever conclusion the two of them reach.
