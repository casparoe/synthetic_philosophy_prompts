For a term paper in my seminar on Newton, where we've been reading his theological manuscripts alongside the Principia, I want to compare episodes of failed prophetic date-setting and pair Newton with William Miller. In an unpublished 1704 manuscript, Newton reasons from the 1,260 "days" of Daniel's prophecy, read as years and measured from the year 800 AD, when Charlemagne was crowned Holy Roman Emperor, to fix 2060 as the year the world will end; this is essentially the method Miller used more than a century later with the 2,300 days of Daniel 8:14 to arrive at 1844. Since both men treated Daniel's day-counts as a precise, decodable timetable and both landed on a specific terminal year, I'm puzzled why historians of science treat Newton's calculation as a forgivable curiosity in an otherwise rational career, while Miller's nearly identical reasoning is remembered as the paradigm case of religious delusion that produced the Great Disappointment. Shouldn't intellectual consistency require us to either rehabilitate Miller's method or apply the same skepticism to Newton's?
