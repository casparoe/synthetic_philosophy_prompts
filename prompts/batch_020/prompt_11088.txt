Ok so I went down a rabbit hole on the "Knobe effect" — that finding where people say a chairman 'intentionally' harmed the environment when he only cared about profit and the harm was just a side effect he didn't care about, but they won't say he 'intentionally' helped the environment in the flipped version where the side effect is good instead of bad. Same guy, same indifference, opposite verdict on intentionality, just depending on good vs bad. Messes with your head a bit once you sit with it.

Thing is, I can't tell from the blog posts which explanation for this actually wins. Knobe himself seems to think it shows moral judgement is genuinely baked into our ordinary concept of "intentional action" itself. Other people say thats overselling it and its really a performance error — blame or anger bleeding into peoples answers, or some pragmatics thing about how the word "intentionally" gets used in conversation. And apparently theres also a version where its not about morality at all, just a general cost/benefit asymmetry, since the same pattern shows up even in non-moral versions of the setup.

Can you write me an essay that actually picks a side instead of hedging? Go through the main candidate explanations, weigh them against each other and against the original data, and land on a real verdict — both on what's actually driving the effect, and on whether it tells us something deep about the concept of intentional action or is 'just' a bias we should discount.
