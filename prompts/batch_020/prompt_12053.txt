Random tangent that's been bugging me. A buddy of mine and I are both software engineers who mess around with investing on the side, and he's really into leveraging his positions — 2x/3x leveraged ETFs, or just borrowing against his brokerage account to buy more index funds. His argument is that since the market has positive expected return over time, and leverage just scales up your exposure to that return, more leverage should always mean more expected money, as long as you dont get margin called. To sanity check this I hacked together a quick simulation: a stock that goes up 30% or down 20% each year, 50/50 odds, so obviously positive expected return every single year. Once I cranked up the leverage past a certain point though, the leveraged version almost always ended up worth less than the plain unleveraged one after 50 years, sometimes by a lot, even though its expected value was actually higher than the unleveraged version's the whole time. I feel like I'm missing something about why "higher expected value" doesn't mean "better bet" here. Is my friend's reasoning actually flawed, or is my sim just buggy?
