Our CI pipeline automatically blocks any pull request that would drop test coverage below 80%, a threshold a former tech lead picked years ago that nobody has revisited since. A teammate argues that the rule doesn't hold up: a PR that leaves us at 79.9% coverage gets blocked, while one that leaves us at 80.0% sails through, even though that tenth of a point reflects no real difference in how well-tested the code actually is. He points out that the same complaint applies at every other point on the scale, so by his logic there is no non-arbitrary place to draw the line, not even at 80%, which would mean the whole policy rests on nothing. My instinct is that we obviously shouldn't ship code with, say, 0% test coverage, so a line has to exist somewhere, but I can't come up with a principled reason to prefer 80% specifically over 79% or 81%. Is my teammate right that any such cutoff is indefensible, or is there a genuine justification for picking and enforcing one anyway?
