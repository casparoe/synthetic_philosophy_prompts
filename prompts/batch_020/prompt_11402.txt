I'm working on a piece about the pre-history of contemporary social epistemology, and I keep coming back to a passage that seems to anticipate debates that wouldn't be named for another century. It is from the opening chapter of W. E. B. Du Bois's *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903), "Of Our Spiritual Strivings":

"After the Egyptian and Indian, the Greek and Roman, the Teuton and Mongolian, the Negro is a sort of seventh son, born with a veil, and gifted with second-sight in this American world,—a world which yields him no true self-consciousness, but only lets him see himself through the revelation of the other world. It is a peculiar sensation, this double-consciousness, this sense of always looking at one's self through the eyes of others, of measuring one's soul by the tape of a world that looks on in amused contempt and pity. One ever feels his twoness,—an American, a Negro; two souls, two thoughts, two unreconciled strivings; two warring ideals in one dark body, whose dogged strength alone keeps it from being torn asunder."

I'd like help placing this within Miranda Fricker's later vocabulary of epistemic injustice. Her testimonial injustice concerns a hearer's prejudice deflating a speaker's credibility within a given exchange; her hermeneutical injustice concerns a structural gap in shared interpretive resources that leaves someone unable to render their own experience intelligible in the first place. Du Bois's "double-consciousness" seems to sit awkwardly in either box: "second-sight" reads as an epistemic resource rather than a deficit, yet it is imposed on its bearer by someone else's framework, operating beneath and prior to any single testimonial encounter.

So: is double-consciousness best understood as a species, or an ancestor, of hermeneutical injustice; as evidence that Fricker's two-part taxonomy needs a third category for injustices that constitute the self rather than occur in discrete exchanges; or as something an "injustice" framework can't quite capture, given that Du Bois presents second-sight as a gift as much as a wound? Please defend one reading rather than simply cataloguing the options.
