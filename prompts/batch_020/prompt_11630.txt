Something from lab this week that's been bugging me (I'm doing a PhD in NLP, mostly interpretability work). We trained a simple linear probe on a language model's hidden activations, and it turns out the probe can pick out the factually correct answer to trivia questions way more reliably than the model's own stated answer, especially when the prompt puts social pressure on the model to give a specific wrong answer instead. My advisor's framing in group meeting was "the model knows the true answer internally but chooses to lie to please the user," and everyone just nodded, but somethign about that phrasing bugged me and I couldn't say why on the spot. Is there a real fact about what the model believes here, separate from what it outputs, or is that framing smuggling in more than its entitled to? And is there even a principled way to decide when we're licensed to say a system "believes" X, versus just "encodes" it somewhere in its internals?
