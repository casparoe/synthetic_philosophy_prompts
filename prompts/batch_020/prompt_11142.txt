I'm on the grants committee for a small education foundation and we've got a fight brewing. We can commit $3M now to scale a literacy program to every school in the district based on decent-but-not-amazing evidence, or spend $80k and six months running a pilot first. Some committee members keep citing something called Good's theorem -- that a rational Bayesian should always want more free information since it can't make you worse off in expectation -- to argue we obviously should pilot. Others say thats a dodge: the pilot isn't free (six months is a lot of kids not getting the program if it works), and nobody's mind is likely to move much based on one small pilot anyway. Can you write the memo I'd bring to next week's meeting -- pilot or scale now, an actual recommendation with reasoning, and give the "more information can't hurt" side a fair shake instead of letting it win by default?
