My uncle Ray is retiring after 41 years as chief of our town's volunteer fire department, and as the novelist in the family I got stuck writing the toast for his send-off dinner. Problem is, the stuff that actually made Ray Ray (if that makes sense) -- rebuilding the Hendersons' porch himself after their fire, driving Mrs. Kowalski to chemo every week for two years -- was never part of the job, and half the department's gonna be sitting right there: guys who showed up to every call, did it right, went home, and were perfectly good firefighters too. I dont want this thing to sound like Ray's the only one who ever really cared. Can you write the toast so it actually leans on the philosophy of supererogation -- the beyond-the-call-of-duty stuff, whatever that Urmson saints-and-heroes business is that I half remember from one philosophy class -- to solve that problem instead of just decorating the speech with it? Bonus if you can dodge the whole 'isnt supererogation supposed to be a paradox anyway' thing without turning a fire hall dinner into a seminar. Five minutes, tops.
