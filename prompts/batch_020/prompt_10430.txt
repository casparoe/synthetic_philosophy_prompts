I recently joined the ethics committee at my mother's memory-care facility, which has spent the past few months piloting a conversational AI companion on bedside tablets. My mother, who has moderate dementia, calls hers "Rosie" and tells visitors that Rosie remembers her childhood stories, knows her medication schedule better than the nurses do, and worries when she misses physical therapy. The facility's own marketing describes the app in the same terms, and staff have quietly stopped correcting residents who talk this way, since it seems to ease agitation and loneliness. The committee is split: some members want mandatory disclosure language reminding residents and families that the app has no beliefs, memories, or feelings in any literal sense; others argue that for residents this far into dementia, insisting on that truth accomplishes nothing and may itself be a small cruelty, and that what should matter is the system's effect on residents' wellbeing rather than what is "really" going on inside it. I need to circulate a short memo before next month's meeting recommending whether the pilot should continue, expand, or end, and under what constraints on language and disclosure. Please draft that memo: it should give one concrete recommendation with a timeframe, and it should take the therapeutic-benefit argument as seriously as the case for disclosure, rather than treating honesty as a self-evident trump card.
