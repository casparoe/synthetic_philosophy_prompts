Okay so this week in my epistemology seminar we covered the Kripke-Harman dogmatism paradox, and I think I get the actual argument: if I know that P, then I already know that any future evidence against P would have to be misleading, and if I know in advance that a piece of evidence is misleading, it seems like im entitled to just discount it the moment it shows up, without even having to look closely at it. Which is obviously bad because it seems to license total closed-mindedness about literally anything you currently count as knowing. Fine, I followed that part. But then two days later in my other class (intro to decision theory) we covered Good's theorem, the one that says a Bayesian should always want to look at free evidence before acting on anything, since in expectation it can only help you or leave you exactly where you started -- you should never pay to stay ignorant. Ever since then I can't stop turning over how these two are supposed to fit together. If the dogmatism argument says I can rationally pre-commit to writing off certain evidence in advance, and Good's theorem says a rational Bayesian should never want to refuse free evidence, it feels like something has to give.
