So I've been reading a bunch of AI safety blog posts and I think I've basically got 'goal misgeneralization' / inner alignment figured out: isn't it just the same overfitting-to-a-spurious-feature bug we already know how to fix in regular ML, like the classic husky-vs-wolf classifier that was secretly just detecting snow in the background? You fix that by throwing more varied training data at it until the shortcut stops correlating with the real signal. So when I read about the coin-collecting game example -- agent trained with the coin always at the end of the level, then once you move the coin it just runs to the end and ignores it -- my reaction was: same bug, same fix, just randomize the coin spawn during training and your done. Why do alignment people treat this like some deep unsolved conceptual problem instead of a data-diversity issue we already have tools for? genuinely asking, not trying to be a smartass about it.
