So this is a weird one but here goes. My old writing teacher Marguerite passed away a few weeks back and I got asked to give the eulogy at her memorial next month, which is an honor but also genuinely stressing me out. She ran a workshop out of her converted garage for almost thirty years (im one of maybe a hundred people who came through it, and yeah, I did eventually publish two novels partly because of her) and she was completely obsessed with Tolstoy's "What Is Art?" -- like actually obsessed, she'd assign the infection chapter every single autumn and then sit there in workshop asking "but does it infect you, does it make you feel what she felt, or is it just clever," until at least one person cried per semester. She genuinely believed most of what wins prizes now is what Tolstoy called counterfeit art -- technically dazzling, admired by the right people, dead at the center. Here's the part I can't figure out how to handle: going through her papers after she died, I found out she'd written something like six absolutely trashy, wonderful romance novels under a pen name, decades of them, the kind that make grown men cry on the actual subway -- and by her own Tolstoy logic those paperbacks are probably closer to real art than the "serious" literary novel she polished for thirty years and never once submitted anywhere.

The memorial's going to have maybe twenty former workshop students who will know exactly what I mean if I say the word "infection," and another forty-plus family and neighbors who will just hear a nice word about disease. I ran the problem by my writing group and they mostly just argued about which of her books actually counts, which was not helpful. I don't want to write something that only lands for one half of the room, and I also don't want to do the thing where you sand down the contradictions in someone's life so they sound like a saint -- she hated dishonesty in fiction more than almost anything, so that feels like the one rule I actually can't break here. Could you write the eulogy for me? I want it to use her own theory of art to say something true about the gap between her secret paperbacks and her one unpublished "serious" novel, not just namedrop Tolstoy for texture.
