A friend from my philosophy of physics reading group and I spent most of dinner last night arguing about the hole argument in general relativity, and by the end she'd largely talked me into her view: that the hole argument shows general relativity is just as indeterministic as quantum mechanics, since the field equations leave what happens inside an empty, matter-free region completely undetermined, even once you fix the complete state of the universe outside and before it. If she's right, I don't understand why general relativity keeps being held up in physics courses as the paradigm case of a deterministic classical field theory rather than being treated as no more deterministic than quantum theory. Which of us should have had the better of the argument, and why?
