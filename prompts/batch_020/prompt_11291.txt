Last night a friend and I spent hours arguing about Wittgenstein's On Certainty without either of us conceding ground: she maintains that Crispin Wright's entitlement theory of hinge propositions is just foundationalism restated in more palatable terms, and I could not give her a fully convincing reason to think otherwise. I am interviewing, for my podcast, a philosopher who defends a broadly Wrightian entitlement account of hinges against both classical foundationalism and a rival, non-doxastic 'quietist' reading inspired by Duncan Pritchard's work, and I would like eight to ten interview questions that press her beyond a restatement of the view. Include at least one question on whether entitlement without evidence is genuinely distinguishable from the arbitrary stopping points for which foundationalism was criticized, one on why certain hinges (say, 'I have two hands') seem far more secure than others (say, which authorities or records deserve our trust), and one on whether Wittgenstein's river-bed image -- on which hinges harden and soften over time -- commits her to a relativism about which hinges count as reasonable, a consequence she would presumably want to resist. For each question, add a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give.
