So a weird thing happened at my critique group last night. Two people got into a whole side-argument about a scene in my draft. My villain, Daniel, does something awful, and another character mutters "you can't really blame him, his dad did the exact same thing to him at that age" -- meant as a chilling line, not an excuse, but Jess and Marco ran off with it in totally different directions.

Marco said the line lets Daniel off the hook: he weighed his options and picked the worst one, so he's on the hook for that choice no matter how he became the kind of guy who'd make it. Jess said "the kind of guy who'd make it" is doing all the work there -- Daniel never chose to become someone who'd choose that, so tracing the choice back to his character just pushes the question one step back, it doesn't answer it. Marco said fine, but Daniel should of just stopped and reasoned his way out of it, and not doing that is still on him. Jess said "reasoned his way out of it" is itself just another choice, flowing from whatever capacity for self-control Daniel happened to end up with -- which he didn't choose either, so your stuck in the same regress, and it doesn't even matter if anything about him was predetermined. Marco shot back that by that logic nobody's ever responsible for anything, including Jess, right now, defending this exact view -- which has to be a reductio. Jess said maybe not, maybe it's just true, and plenty of serious people think exactly that. They were still at it when I left to get more wine.

Can you go through this move by move and say who's got the better of it at each step, not just an overall winner -- and is there something they're both missing?
