As part of some multi-agent evaluations at work, I recently had two separate instances of the same language model — identical weights, identical prompt, no channel for them to communicate — play a single, one-shot round of the Prisoner's Dilemma against each other, and they cooperated far more often than standard game theory would predict for purely self-interested players. A colleague dismissed this as noise, but it sent me back to Douglas Hofstadter's old notion of superrationality: the claim that agents who know they reason identically should realize that whatever choice is correct for one of them is correct for all of them, and should cooperate on that basis rather than defect, as classical game theory recommends. Write me a joke about superrationality. I want the humor to hinge on that specific piece of reasoning — the symmetry argument for cooperation among identical reasoners — rather than being a generic joke about game theory, clones, or AI.
