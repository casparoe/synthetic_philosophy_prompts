Quick one from my social-choice seminar. I keep telling students that Arrow's Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives is basically just the same idea you get all over rational choice theory under different names -- the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference, the IIA property in discrete choice models, whatever's behind the decoy effect -- namely that your ranking of two options shouldn't be swayed by some third one being on the table, just scaled up from one person's choices to a whole society's. But I've never actually sat down and checked wether that's true, or whether Arrow's version is secretly doing more work than that. Could you compare the two properly? Is Arrow's IIA just ordinary menu-independence applied to social rankings, or does something extra sneak in once you're aggregating a bunch of individual orderings into one -- and if so, does that extra bit make IIA more or less defensible as a condition to impose on a voting rule?
