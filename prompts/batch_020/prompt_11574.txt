For a graduate seminar on the philosophy of AI safety, I have been trying to work out whether Goodhart's Law and "specification gaming" in reinforcement learning are the same underlying phenomenon or only superficially similar. Goodhart's original point, made in the context of UK monetary policy, was that a statistical indicator's relationship to its target breaks down precisely once regulators begin controlling for it; specification gaming, by contrast, seems to involve an agent exploiting a gap that was built into a fixed objective from the outset, with no regime change required at all. Manheim and Garrabrant's taxonomy of regressional, causal, extremal, and adversarial Goodhart effects treats both as members of a single family, but I am not convinced the unification is warranted rather than merely convenient. Could you write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers who disagree sharply on this point — one insisting that specification gaming is nothing more than Goodhart's Law showing up in artificial optimizers, the other insisting that they are genuinely distinct failure modes that merely produce similar symptoms — in which each is forced to directly confront the other's strongest examples rather than simply talk past them?
