So this is a weird one that came up in real life and I can't stop thinking about it. I sit on the steering committee for a new interdisciplinary institute at my university that's throwing economists and biologists into the same building (long story involving a donor who couldn't decide what he wanted his name on). We've gotten bogged down in a surprisingly heated fight over the house style for author order on jointly-written papers. The economists want strict alphabetical order, which is just what their whole field does and always has done. The biologists want contribution order instead, with the PI or corresponding author listed last. One of our postdocs, whose last name starts with a W, pointed out that under alphabetical order she's going to look like the most junior person on every paper she ever writes here, even the ones where she does most of the actual work. A couple of the senior economists on the committee waved this off, saying that within economics "everybody knows" alphabetical order carries zero information, so it can't be sending anyone an unfair signal - kind of like arguing a coin flip discriminates against tails. One of the biologists thinks that's a very convenient thing for the alphabetically-blessed senior people to say, and is more worried that whichever rule we bless right now, on day one, is basically going to calcify into "just how its done here" for decades, regardless of whether it was actually the fairer option.

I tried to explain to the room what I thought was going on philosophically and realized halfway through that I couldn't actually pin it down myself, beyond a nagging feeling that both sides are onto something real here and that this isn't merely a matter of taste or bureaucratic housekeeping. So, professor to professor: is the economists' "everybody knows the rule, so there's no signal, so there's no unfairness" move actually a good argument, or does it quietly smuggle something past us? And is the biologist right to be nervous about lock-in, given that we're effectively being asked to just pick one option out of several that all seem equally defensible on paper? I'd much rather you walk me through what's actually going on underneath this than just hand me a verdict on our house style - I want to have my head straight before I open my mouth again at the next committee meeting.
