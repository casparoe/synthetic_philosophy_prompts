Last month a bug I introduced in a pull request caused a six-hour outage in our checkout service, and the postmortem has turned into an uncomfortable argument about who is actually responsible for it. The bug got past code review because the reviewer skipped the edge-case tests, it went undiagnosed for twenty extra minutes because the on-call engineer silenced the alert before investigating, and none of this would have mattered if our tech lead had prioritized the input-validation work he'd been postponing for months. Had any one of these three acted differently, the outage simply wouldn't have happened, just as surely as if I'd never written the bug at all. Yet leadership wants to hold the reviewer primarily accountable, on the grounds that catching this kind of bug is specifically their job, while treating the other two lapses as background noise. That feels reasonable to me but also strangely arbitrary: if failing to catch the bug is what makes someone responsible for the outage, shouldn't everyone who could have caught it and didn't be equally responsible? Is there an actual principle behind singling out one person's inaction over the others', or are we just choosing a scapegoat and calling it accountability?
