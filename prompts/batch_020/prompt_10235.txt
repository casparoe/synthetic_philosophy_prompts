Three months ago my company merged with a similarly sized competitor, and I now co-chair the resulting Handbook Integration Committee, which governs the internal assistant that chains rules from both legacy handbooks to answer staff questions. Legal has confirmed one direct conflict, applying to the same merged staff: one handbook allows remote work without approval, the other requires a manager's sign-off, and formal harmonization is six to nine months out. Our engineers want to rebuild the assistant's inference engine on a paraconsistent logic, so the contradiction can't contaminate answers to unrelated questions the way it can now; asked about remote work directly, it would flag the conflict instead of silently picking a side. Others on the committee object that outputting "both rules hold," even flagged, functionally asserts a contradiction to staff, who will simply cite whichever half suits them, and would rather the assistant refuse any question touching the disputed rule until harmonization is final. I need the committee's decision memo for next week: which approach to adopt for the interim, or what conditions to attach, with a rollout timeline and reasoning that takes the strongest objection to our choice seriously.
