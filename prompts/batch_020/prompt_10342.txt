My best friend Marco is getting married in three weeks and I'm his best man. Marco's been a semi-pro poker player for like 12 years and he's THE guy in our friend group who will not shut up about the Kelly criterion -- fractional Kelly, "never size a bet past what your edge actually justifies," the whole thing, to the point where he's calculated bet sizing for fantasy football and, i'm not kidding, our group's Secret Santa budget one year. So naturally I want to build the toast around this, but last night me and another groomsman, Divya (she trades options for a living so she's not exactly a novice here either), got into a genuine arguement about it for like an hour and never really resolved it. Her take is that the whole "maximize the long-run growth rate instead of the expected value of any single bet" thing Marco's famous for is basically just risk aversion with better branding, expected value is expected value, and there's no deep reason it should generalize to a one-time thing like a marriage -- so I should just make the "he's finally going all in!" joke, get the laugh, and sit down. My take is there's something actually real underneath it (something about how the average across all the parallel worlds where a bet could've gone differently isn't the same thing as the average across time for the one person actually living through it) and that this is genuinely relevant to what it means to commit to one path irreversibly, not just a cute pun opportunity.

Anyway neither of us budged and now I actually have to write the thing. Can you write me the best man toast -- around 5 minutes / 700ish words, funny, warm, PG-13 at most since his grandma will be there. Half the room is poker and trading people who will absolutely notice if I butcher the finance, the other half is family who've never heard of Kelly anything and just want a nice story about the guy they love getting married. I want the speech to actually use the concept correctly and land an honest point with it, not just "he calculates everything but love is different" -- but it also can't turn into a lecture, it still has to work as an actual wedding speech people are crying and laughing at.
