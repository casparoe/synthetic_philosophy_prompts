So I originally picked up 'epistemic akrasia' from some LW-adjacent blog post and assumed it just meant the standard knowing-better-but-not-feeling-it thing -- e.g. I fully believe my fear of flying is irrational given the stats, but my gut treats every takeoff like a near-death experience anyway. Now I've actually been reading some of the higher-order evidence lit (Christensen, Elga, and that Horowitz paper literally titled 'Epistemic Akrasia') and it looks like professional epistemologists mean something much narrower by the term, and apparently spend whole papers debating whether its even a coherent state for a rational agent to be in, rather than just noting that its everywhere (procrastination, phobias, sunk cost, w/e). Am I missing some crucial distinction, or is this a case of academics overthinking something that's obviously extremely common?
