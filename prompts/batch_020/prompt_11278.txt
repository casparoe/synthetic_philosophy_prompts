I've spent a long time reading around the rationalist blogosphere -- Overcoming Bias in its early days, then LessWrong and Astral Codex Ten -- and about a year ago that turned into helping produce a small interview podcast in roughly the same vein, mostly on decision theory and population ethics. A couple of us from an old LessWrong meetup have argued on and off for years about infinite ethics without ever quite resolving anything, so I was glad when a philosopher willing to defend an unusually hard line on it agreed to come on next month.

Most people working in this area treat Bostrom's "hyperreal" proposal from his 2011 paper "Infinite Ethics" the way he himself does -- as the most powerful of the aggregation-rule patches for infinitarian paralysis, yet still an imperfect one. My guest instead defends it as an outright solution. Her view: assign each canonically infinite world a hyperreal number built by summing local value over an expanding sequence of regions, then fix one non-principal ultrafilter once and for all to get a complete ordering over these hyperreals. On this basis, she thinks total aggregative utilitarianism can keep an unrestricted domain and ordinary expected-value maximization fully intact -- no temporal or spatial discounting, no retreat to one's future light cone, no lexical-priority patch bolted onto the decision rule.

I don't want her just re-explaining the proposal, since she's given that talk before. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that push past a restatement and into the specific weak points Bostrom's own paper flags for this approach. I want at least one question on the arbitrariness of the ultrafilter choice -- the fact that, on his own example of a perfectly flat world compared against an oscillating one, either world can come out ahead, or they can tie, purely depending on which ultrafilter is selected; at least one on what happens when there is no privileged point to center the expanding regions on, so that the construction's very first coordinate has nothing non-arbitrary to latch onto; and at least one on whether the view can keep faith with the classical utilitarian thought that nobody's position in an infinite population should carry any fundamental ethical weight, given that merely relabeling who sits where -- with nobody's own welfare actually changed -- is enough to flip which world the ordering favors. For each of these three, add a follow-up question keyed to the answer she's most likely to give (for instance, if she bites the bullet on the ultrafilter worry by insisting the residual indeterminacy simply mirrors a genuine indeterminacy in our own intuitions, press on what that concession costs her in cases where our intuitions are not indeterminate at all). Close the list with a separate question asking her to say, concretely, what -- short of a mathematical proof that the universe is finite -- could ever move her off the hyperreal program, given that Bostrom explicitly warns that any view granting lexical priority to infinite considerations, hers included, risks the same Pascal's-mugging-shaped fanaticism problem that afflicts cruder infinite-ethics patches.
