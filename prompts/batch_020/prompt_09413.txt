I've been reading up on AI alignment in my spare time, and I think the famous "shutdown problem" in corrigibility research is actually already solved. Hadfield-Menell, Dragan, Abbeel, and Russell's 2017 off-switch game shows that a Bayesian agent that stays uncertain about its own reward function will always have an incentive to let a human switch it off, since disabling the switch can only lose expected value compared to deferring to the human's judgment. That seems to give a clean, general recipe: just make sure the agent's uncertainty about its reward never collapses, and shutdown-resistance stops being a possibility. So I'm confused why MIRI and other alignment researchers still treat corrigibility as a major unsolved problem, publishing new formalizations and impossibility results, instead of treating the off-switch game as having closed the question. Am I missing some reason this doesn't actually generalize, or is this mostly researchers overcomplicating a problem that already has a clean solution?
