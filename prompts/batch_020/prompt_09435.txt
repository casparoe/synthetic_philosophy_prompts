Question: Explain Goodman's grue paradox and his proposed resolution via entrenchment. Does entrenchment solve Hume's original problem of induction, or is it doing something else?

Answer: Goodman's puzzle is about how ordinary induction works. Say we've examined a bunch of emeralds before some future time T (e.g. Jan 1 2050) and they've all been green. Define "grue" so that something counts as grue if its examined before T and green, or examined after T and blue. Every emerald we've looked at so far is green, and every one of them is also grue, because they were all examined before T. So the evidence we have supports "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" equally well under simple induction by enumeration - but the two hypotheses give opposite predictions for emeralds examined after T. Since we obviously think green is the right thing to project and grue isn't, Goodman says the real problem isn't Hume's old question of whether induction is justified at all, but a new one: which predicates are legitimately projectible from observed to unobserved cases.

His answer is entrenchment: green is a much better-entrenched predicate than grue because it (and predicates coextensive with it) have actually been used again and again in our past inductive practice, while grue is a novel, artificially constructed predicate that's never really been projected before. Entrenchment gives us a principled reason to prefer projecting green over grue, since grue is really just a disguised way of predicting a change partway through, hidden inside a single made-up predicate - once you see that, its arbitrariness next to a genuinely natural property like green is obvious. And because entrenchment tells us which regularities are lawlike rather than accidental, it actually resolves Hume's problem too: Hume worried we can't justify projecting the past onto the future, but once entrenchment tells us which predicates are the legitimate ones to run that projection on, the inference from "all observed emeralds are green" to "all emeralds are green" is justified after all.
