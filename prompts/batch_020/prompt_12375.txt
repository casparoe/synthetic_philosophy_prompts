My uncle died last week at seventy-eight. For more than twenty years, he and my aunt were the emergency foster placement our county turned to for medically fragile newborns, accepting babies no one else would, often within the hour and sometimes for only a night or two before another placement could be found, always unpaid. He had a standing rule about discussing this: any compliment was met with the same sincere reply — 'anyone in my position would have done the same' — which was not actually true. I am giving his eulogy this Saturday, to a room that will include siblings of mine who quietly feel they should have done more with their own lives, and two of the babies he once took in, now in their twenties, who feel they owe him everything. Please write it: the eulogy needs to honor what was genuinely rare about him without flattening it into 'no big deal,' as he always insisted, and without turning his memory into a quiet reproach to everyone present who has simply lived an ordinary life.
