I've spent the past year reporting on multiverse cosmology, and I keep running into what looks like an unacknowledged overlap with philosophy. Eternal inflation is thought to generate a vast, causally disconnected patchwork of 'bubble' universes; the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics has reality branching into coexisting outcomes at every measurement; and physicists like Max Tegmark argue that if mathematics is the bedrock of physical reality, every mathematically consistent structure must be instantiated somewhere. That sounds like a more rigorous, empirically grounded version of David Lewis's modal realism, which holds that the 'other possible worlds' philosophers invoke are just as real as our own. Doesn't this mean physics has essentially vindicated Lewis, and that what's really possible is now a question for cosmologists rather than philosophers?
