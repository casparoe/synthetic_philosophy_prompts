I'm a philosophy PhD student and the graduate-student representative on the acquisitions committee for a university press's crossover imprint for general readers. We've received a proposal arguing that the pages on diet, climate, and recreation in Ecce Homo's "Why I Am So Clever" are the practical center of Nietzsche's late philosophy rather than autobiographical color, and using that claim to license a specific eating and lifestyle regimen the author markets as "the Nietzschean diet." Half the committee wants to reject the proposal as wellness content trading on Nietzsche's name that flattens his physiological idiom beyond recognition; the other half thinks the author has noticed something real that the secondary literature underplays and wants to acquire the book subject to revisions. Please draft the committee's decision memo -- accept, reject, or accept with specified revisions -- for circulation before next week's meeting. It should identify the concrete interpretive commitments the author would have to defend, including how a one-size-fits-all regimen squares with Nietzsche's own insistence that there is no general answer to the nutrition question, only the question of how you, in particular, must eat to reach your maximum strength -- and it should give whichever side of the committee loses this argument reasoning it could actually engage with, not a token concession.
