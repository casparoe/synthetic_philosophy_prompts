I'm a retired field ecologist who's gotten into philosophy since retiring, which is maybe why an old memory still bugs me. On a bird walk years back, someone mentioned a rare warbler, well outside its normal range, had been seen nearby that morning. Twenty minutes later I was sure I caught the diagnostic wing-bars on a bird that flushed from a hedge before anyone got binoculars on it, and I logged it as the rarity. Another birder, who'd drifted off down the trail and missed that whole conversation, glimpsed the same bird a second earlier and just logged it as a probable common yellow warbler -- said she hadn't gotten a good enough look to be any more definate. I still don't know whether hearing the report actually changed what I saw in that half-second, or just made me quicker to read an ambiguous glimpse the way I wanted to. It feels like a confound I can't control for: is there even a real difference between those two, and if there is, shouldn't it make me trust my own field notes less than hers?
