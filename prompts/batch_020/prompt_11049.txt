Three weeks ago I walked away from a car accident that, by any reasonable reckoning, should have killed me -- a few inches either way and I would not be writing this. I have not been able to stop replaying the counterfactual since: I could have died that day. A friend midway through a philosophy PhD pointed out that this ordinary-sounding thought rests on a genuine, unresolved dispute in the metaphysics of modality. On David Lewis's counterpart theory, no individual exists in more than one possible world, so my claim is made true by a distinct other-worldly individual -- one similar enough to count as my counterpart -- who dies in the crash instead of me; on the rival view, transworld identity, it is literally me, the very same individual, who perishes in that other world, with no counterpart relation needed to broker the connection. She also mentioned Kripke's well-known objection that the counterpart theorist seems to change the subject: whatever relief I feel at having survived is relief that I did not die, not relief that some mere lookalike died in my place. Could you write a dialogue between a committed counterpart theorist and a committed transworld-identity theorist who try to settle, using my accident as their working example, what I actually mean when I say I could have died -- each pressing their strongest considerations and directly answering the other's, rather than simply restating their own position?
