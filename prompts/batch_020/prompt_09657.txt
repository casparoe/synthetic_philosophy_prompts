My aunt died last week. Twenty years ago she gave a kidney to a stranger she'd found through a transplant registry, and for the rest of her life she volunteered for that same registry, helping pair willing donors with strangers who needed a kidney. At the wake, an old friend of hers and I argued for the better part of an hour: he insisted that calling this "above and beyond the call of duty" is a comforting fiction, since if a donation is really as low-risk and high-benefit as the surgeons claim, morality arguably demanded it of all of us, and "supererogatory" is just the word we reach for so we don't feel implicated. I think the beyond-duty description is basically right, but I never managed a clean rebuttal. I'm giving a five-minute eulogy on Saturday to a room split between her fellow donors, who will notice immediately if I get the framing wrong, and family who mainly want to grieve. Write the eulogy: it needs to honor what she did as genuinely exceptional without implying that everyone who kept both their kidneys failed some duty, and without shrinking her sacrifice down to something merely nice.
