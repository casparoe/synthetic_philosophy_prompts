My mum died three weeks ago after a long stretch with ovarian cancer, and I'm doing her eulogy this Saturday. She left school at 16 and was a bookkeeper her whole life, zero science background, but somewhere in her fifties she fell down a proper rabbit hole with pop physics books, especially one of those Sean Carroll ones on many-worlds, and ended up a genuine, thought-through believer in quantum immortality - like, properly believed that because the universe keeps branching, there's no version of events where she actually experiences being dead, she just keeps finding herself alive in whichever branches she happens to survive in, over and over, forever. It really did seem to make the cancer easier for her to carry, she was weirdly calm right up until the end, and she used to joke, pretty darkly, that somewhere out there she's a 150-year-old woman hooked up to increasingly ridiculous machines, and thought that was funny rather than something to be scared of.

Here's the actual problem: a few weeks before she died she made me promise, really specifically (she even scribbled a couple of lines about it on a card so I'd remember exactly how she put it), that I would NOT let the eulogy turn into "mum's still out there somewhere" or "she lives on" as some kind of comfort for US, because she was adament that isn't what her belief even means - the branches where she keeps surviving aren't branches we're in, so however much the idea helped her personally, she said it does nothing for the people left behind in this branch, the one we're all actually sitting in on Saturday. She didn't want her real, carefully worked-out view getting mushed into the same vague afterlife stuff she used to gently take the mick out of when other people said it at funerals. At the same time she definitely did not want it left out, since it was such a massive part of who she was near the end and honestly made her braver about dying than anyone I've ever known. I'm not a philosopher or a physicist, just her kid who listened to this for years, so I don't fully trust myself to keep that distinction straight while I'm stood up there crying in front of maybe 40 people who, apart from my cousin who's actually doing a physics PhD and will 100% clock it if I get it wrong, have never heard of "many-worlds" in their life and do not want a lecture. Can you help me write it? Something that's honest about what she actually believed, including the bit where she insisted it wasn't supposed to comfort us, but that still works as a proper eulogy and leaves the room in a better place than if I'd just said nothing about any of it.
