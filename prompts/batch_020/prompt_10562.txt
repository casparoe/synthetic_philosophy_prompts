So I went down a rabbit hole a while back after seeing a headline that the WHO actually tried to officially add "old age" as a disease category in their big international disease code book (ICD-11, i think its called), gave it its own code and everything, and then quietly walked it back a few years later after a bunch of doctors and old-people's advocacy groups pushed back hard. My dad's the kind of guy who takes twenty supplements a day trying to "cure" aging, so this whole thing got me and him arguing about whether aging could even count as a disease in the first place, as opposed to just being what happens when you're alive long enough.

Thinking about it more, I realized the disagreement is actually about something bigger than aging specifically. Some people seem to think whether something's a disease is basically a biology/stats question -- your body part is either working within the normal range for someone your species/age/sex or its not, no opinions needed, full stop. But then here's the weird part: since literally everyone's body changes the same way as they get older, that decline IS the normal range for their age group, so by that logic aging could never really be classified as a disease no matter how much it wrecks you, which feels like its dodging the real question rather than answering it. Other people say that whole "just describing normal ranges" framing is fooling itself, that somebody always has to first decide what "functioning well" even means, and that decision is a value judgment about what's good for a person, not a neutral fact — so under their view aging (or stuff like ADHD, or grief that never really lifts, or menopause) can absolutely count as disease if its bad enough for the person, no matter how common or "on schedule" it is.

I don't think this is just people arguing over a word, it feels like it actually changes real things, like whether insurance should ever cover anti-aging drugs, or whether my dad's "treating" a disease or just being a bit silly. Could you write me a dialogue between two people who each hold one of these views, really hashing it out specifically over whether aging counts as a disease? I want them to be equally sharp and neither one a strawman, actually responding to what the other just said instead of talking past each other, and I especially want the "it's just statistics" person to have to deal with the weird aging loophole I mentioned instead of dodging around it.
