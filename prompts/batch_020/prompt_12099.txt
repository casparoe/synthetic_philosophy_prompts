So im taking a rational choice / decision theory class this semester (cross-listed with econ, but it's taught out of the philosophy department), and we've been going through all the standard assumptions that are supposedly required for your preferences to even count as "rational." It made me think back to this housing thing from last spring that has honestly been bugging me a little ever since.

I was choosing between three summer sublets. One was cheap ($650) but a 25 min walk from campus, one was a bit pricier ($720) with a 15 min walk, and the fanciest one was $790 but basically next door at 10 min. When I compared the cheap one to the middle one, the $70 difference felt small enough that I basically just went with whichever was closer, so the middle one won. Comparing the middle one to the fancy one, same story: $70 felt small, closer won again, so the fancy one beat the middle one. But then comparing the cheap one directly to the fancy one, the rent gap was now $140, which was definately real money this time (idk why $70 was my cutoff and not, like, $140, but it was), so the cheaper one won even though it meant an extra 15 min walk. So: middle beats cheap, fancy beats middle, but cheap beats fancy.

My friend, who's a math major and never lets an opportunity like this pass, told me this pattern means my preferences are basically "circular," and that in theory, if someone knew this about me going in, they could've kept offering me trades between the three places, each one looking like a totally reasonable upgrade in the moment, and walked me around in a loop right back to the apartment I started with — except now I'd have paid a moving fee three times over for nothing. She says that alone proves I was being irrational, full stop, regardless of what actually happened. Here's what I can't settle though: even if there's technically a loop hiding in there, once I actually notice it, it seems pretty obvious that I wouldn't really let someone walk me through that whole cycle for real money — I'd just bail on the last trade once I saw where the sequence was heading. So who's right here, me or her — does noticing the pattern in advance basically get me off the hook, or is there something already irrational about having preferences shaped like that, even before anybody actually tries to exploit them?
