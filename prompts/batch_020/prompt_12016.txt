So I've been reading through the coherence-theorem stuff on LW and the alignment forum -- the "if you're not VNM-rational you're leaving money on the table" argument, like in Yudkowsky's "coherent decisions imply consistent utilities" -- and also the pushback on it, where I think it was Thornley (or someone in that corner of the alignment forum) arguing theres no actual theorem that forces completeness, since an agent can supposedly keep incomplete or even cyclic preferences and still dodge money pumps just by having enough foresight. Want a real argument about this, not a survey. Setup: an agent has a preference cycle -- it strictly prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A -- and a trader offers it the standard sequence of trades, each one a strict local improvement, that walks it around the cycle and leaves it strictly poorer every time it completes a loop. Write a dialogue between two philosophers watching this happen, each trying to convince the other what rationality actually requires here. One holds that the only real fix is for the agent's preferences themselves to become transitive (and complete) -- patching the exploitability with a clever policy while keeping the "irrational" preferences underneath isn't a fix, its a band-aid. The other holds that the agent can keep exactly the preferences it has and never end up worse off, just by "resolutely" committing at the outset to a plan that doesn't walk the whole cycle -- and that not being exploitable is all rationality could ever coherently demand of it. Push them to really engage with each other's strongest move: the classic defense of self-binding is Ulysses tying himself to the mast, but Ulysses did that because he knew his future preferences would be corrupted by the Sirens -- whatever's behind the cyclic agent's later choices isn't corrupted like that, it's just its ordinary preferences, so what licenses ignoring them? And if the agent can bind its future choices anyway, hasn't it effectively adopted consistent preferences over plans instead of outcomes -- coherence by another name? Keep it tight, a handful of exchanges rather than a full essay.
