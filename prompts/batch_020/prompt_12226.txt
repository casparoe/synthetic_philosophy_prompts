Molinism holds that, logically prior to deciding what to create, God possesses "middle knowledge" of true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (CCFs), such as "if Peter were placed in circumstances C, he would freely deny Christ." The grounding objection, pressed most influentially by Robert Adams, denies that any such counterfactual could be true at that logical moment: the Peter in question does not yet exist to serve as a truthmaker, God has by hypothesis left the matter to Peter's free choice, and circumstances C are compatible with his doing otherwise. Adams sharpens the point by arguing that, absent anything to settle the matter, at most it could be true that Peter might deny Christ in C — not that he would, which is exactly the determinacy on which God's providential planning is supposed to depend. Molinists standardly reply that the demand for "grounds" either begs the question against libertarian freedom or presupposes a truthmaker theory no one is obliged to accept.

Write a dialogue in which a Molinist and a defender of the grounding objection try to determine whether "brute" counterfactuals of freedom are a principled metaphysical position or merely an ad hoc rescue of the theory. The objector should press the would/might worry specifically, instead of resting on the bare demand for grounds, and the Molinist must explain why libertarian freedom yields a determinate brute truth rather than mere indeterminacy, rather than simply insisting that some truths need no grounds.
