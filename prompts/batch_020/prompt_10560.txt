A friend and I spent much of a long dinner arguing about whether Bradley's regress and the problem of the unity of the proposition are really the same difficulty under two different names, or whether treating them as identical obscures a genuine disagreement about what actually needs explaining. Neither of us was persuaded, and I said I'd look for a more disinterested opinion. Please compare Bradley's regress, understood as the argument that a relation cannot unite its terms without some further relation to relate it to them, with the unity-of-the-proposition problem as it confronted Frege and the early Russell in their respective accounts of predication. Does Frege's treatment of concepts as "unsaturated" genuinely dissolve the regress, or does it merely relocate the mystery into an unexplained notion of saturation? And did Russell's later multiple-relation theory of judgment actually meet Bradley's original objection, or was it designed to solve a different problem, the status of falsehood, that only looks in retrospect like an answer to Bradley? Please say which of the two thinkers' treatments of unity strikes you as least question-begging, and justify your answer by reference to how each one handles predication.
