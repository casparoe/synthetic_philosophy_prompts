For some time now, I have been trying to work out, largely through my own reading, whether a religious person can preserve genuine libertarian free will while still holding that God knows the future with complete certainty. Molinism, with its doctrine of middle knowledge, seemed for a while like the answer: prior to deciding which world to create, God is supposed to already know exactly how every possible free creature would choose to act in every possible circumstance. The more closely I examine this, however, the more I run into what is called the "grounding objection," and I have yet to see it explained in a way that truly satisfies me: most treatments simply assert that these "counterfactuals of freedom" lack anything to make them true, without ever spelling out why that would actually be a problem for God's knowledge of them. Please explain in detail exactly what critics think is missing here, and what the most serious Molinist responses actually say in reply.
