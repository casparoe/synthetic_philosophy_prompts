I've recently been working through the lineage that runs from Thomas Schelling's essays on self-command, through Jon Elster's *Ulysses and the Sirens* and its notably more skeptical sequel *Ulysses Unbound*, to Yudkowsky's "Schelling Fences on Slippery Slopes" and the rationalist community's general enthusiasm for commitment devices (Beeminder pledges and the like). One thing that struck me on this pass through the material is a distinction Elster leans on far more heavily in the later book than the earlier one: an individual binding his own future self (Odysseus and the mast) is, rationally and ethically, a different kind of act from a constitution-making body binding future majorities who never agreed to the constraint in the first place. The first is unambiguously the same agent constraining itself; the second is one group locking in a rule that some later, non-consenting group has to live under, which raises the question of whether "precommitment" is even the right word for it, as opposed to just a more polite name for entrenched power.

This got me wanting to actually test how well specific, real precommitment and entrenchment mechanisms hold up once that distinction is taken seriously, rather than just noting it in the abstract. I'd like your help ranking the seven devices below from most to least robust, where "robust" means: resistant, both in principle and in whatever the historical record shows, to being watered down, interpreted around, or formally circumvented by the very body or agent the device is meant to constrain.

1. Odysseus having his crew bind him to the mast and stop their own ears with wax, so that his hearing the Sirens' song could not, even via his own later pleading, change the ship's course (Homer's original case)
2. Germany's Basic Law, Article 79(3), the "eternity clause" placing human dignity and the federal, democratic, and social-state principles beyond the reach of any amendment procedure whatsoever
3. The U.S. Constitution's Article V entrenchment of each state's equal suffrage in the Senate, which cannot be amended without that state's own consent
4. France's Fifth Republic Constitution, Article 89, which declares the republican form of government not to be a permissible object of any amendment
5. India's judge-made "basic structure doctrine," from Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), holding that Parliament's amending power does not extend to the constitution's basic structure, despite there being no textual unamendability clause to interpret
6. Turkey's constitutional provision declaring its founding articles on the republic's secular, democratic character unamendable and not even open to a proposal to amend them
7. An ordinary rationalist-style personal commitment contract: a Beeminder pledge with an escalating financial penalty for missing a self-set goal

Please give your answer as a JSON array of objects ordered from most to least robust, with each object containing a "device" field and a one-sentence "vulnerability" field naming the main way that device could, in principle or in fact, be gotten around by the party it constrains, formatted like:
