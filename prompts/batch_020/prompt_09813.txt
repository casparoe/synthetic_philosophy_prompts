I'm the philosopher on my university's Behavioral Insights Ethics Panel, which reviews administrative initiatives built on behavioral science before they launch. Ahead of next week's vote, the panel needs a decision memo on an HR proposal to raise staff retirement-plan participation by combining opt-out default enrollment with automatic annual contribution increases, monthly loss-framed reminder texts ('you are on pace to retire with $140,000 less than your target'), a peer-comparison statistic shown only to employees below the median contribution rate, and a cancellation process that requires a phone call during business hours rather than a self-service form. The panel is split: some members see this as ordinary libertarian paternalism, no different in kind from the retirement defaults employers have used for years to help people act on ends they already hold; others think several of these elements are manipulative, since they are engineered to move behavior regardless of whether the employee would endorse them on reflection, and since the selective statistic and the asymmetric friction are never disclosed to those affected. Please draft the memo -- approve, reject, or approve with specific modifications -- with reasoning HR could actually act on, and engage seriously with the strongest version of whichever side you end up rejecting.
