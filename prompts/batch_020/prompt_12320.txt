Quick one for you — I write about science for a living and must've used the phrase "paradigm shift" a thousand times without ever really reading Kuhn properly, and a philosopher I interviewed last week gently implied I've been butchering it. My (and I think most journalism's) take is basically: paradigms are so incommensurable that there's no rational way to compare them, so science doesn't really progress, it just changes — which always struck me as a bit relativist. But apparently Kuhn spent a chunk of his later career walking that back: he insisted incommensurable doesnt mean incomparable, and narrowed incommensurability down to something more local/"taxonomic" — specific interdefined terms not translating cleanly between theories — rather than two whole worldviews being mutually unintelligible, partly (I think) because of Davidson-style objections that total untranslatability would make it impossible to even recognize two paradigms as rival accounts of the same world. Can you walk me through what Kuhn's actual mature position on incommensurability was, and how "incommensurable but still comparable" is supposed to hold together without being incoherent? Enough precision that I can stop hand-waving about it in print would be great.
