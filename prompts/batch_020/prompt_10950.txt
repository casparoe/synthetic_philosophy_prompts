Write a dialogue between a conciliationist and a steadfaster about peer disagreement in epistemology. Rather than rehearsing one of the standard motivating cases (splitting a restaurant bill, reading a bathroom scale, forecasting an election), have the conversation focus on a reflexive problem for conciliationism itself: the view is a hotly contested position among thoughtful, well-informed epistemologists, so shouldn't consistency require its own proponent to significantly lower confidence in it? Have the steadfaster press this point relentlessly, and have the conciliationist try to find a principled response that doesn't simply exempt conciliationism from the very standard it recommends applying to everyone else. Neither character should fully concede by the end.
