Ok so this is a weirdly specific committee problem, but I could use actual outside help thinking it through instead of just venting to my partner about it again. I sit on the steering committee for a mid-size open source foundation -- think the kind of nonprofit that hosts a database or build-tool project you've probably used at some job. About three years ago our flagship project had a huge blowup over a goverance disagreement (doesn't matter what, honestly, it was dumb) and the community split into two camps. One camp happened to be holding the admin keys to the github org and the package registry name at the time, so they kept the original name -- let's say "Corvid." The other camp had to rename to "Magpie" since they lost that fight.

Here's the annoying part. Over the three years since, Corvid quietly rewrote the entire codebase from scratch in a different language for performance reasons, so literally 0% of the original lines survive, and every single original core maintainer eventually got fed up and left to go work on Magpie instead. Magpie, meanwhile, kept the old codebase (which has obviously evolved too, but maybe 35-40% of the original lines are still in there somewhere) plus the entire original governance process, mailing list, issue tracker history, and community. So Corvid has the name and basically none of the original substance, and Magpie has the substance and none of the name.

Both are now petitioning our foundation for official "top-level project" status ahead of their 1.0 releases (the board votes on this in two weeks), which comes with a yearly infra grant and, more importantly, is what a big cloud provider is waiting on before they'll announce a managed offering for "the real one." Our bylaws say top-level status and rights to the original name go to "whichever project constitutes a continuation of the original," but whoever wrote that clause never bothered defining "continuation." I somehow got voluntold to draft the recommendation memo, because apparently I "write clearly," which in committee-speak means nobody else wanted to touch it. My gut says Magpie, since they kept the people and the lineage, but a colleague pointed out that everybody in the wild still just calls Corvid "Corvid" out of habit, so thats functionally what the name refers to now no matter what we decree, and picking Magpie just creates years of confusion for ordinary users who don't know or care about our internal drama. Its the ship-of-Theseus thing, except messier, because instead of comparing one rebuilt ship against a pile of discarded planks, we've got two complete, currently-sailing ships, and each one is only really entitled to half of what people usually mean by "the same ship." Can you draft the actual memo I'd bring to the board -- I need a real recommendation, not a shrug, keep it to a page since nobody reads long docs, and it has to seriously engage with whichever side we don't pick, not just wave it away.
