I'm writing a piece on how a century-old thought experiment from general relativity still unsettles philosophers of physics, and I keep coming back to Einstein's 'hole argument,' revived in its modern form by John Earman and John Norton in the 1980s. As I understand it, the argument shows that Einstein's field equations fail to fix a unique metric inside a small, matter-free region of spacetime even when every fact about the rest of the universe is held fixed, which seems to establish that general relativity itself is indeterministic as physics, not just indeterminate depending on how philosophers choose to redescribe the mathematics. If that's right, why do most physicists, and even many philosophers, treat this as a curiosity to be managed through interpretation rather than as a genuine crack in the theory's predictive credentials, on a par with the measurement problem in quantum mechanics?
