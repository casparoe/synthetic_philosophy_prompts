I spent something like thirty-five years running a comparative physiology lab, mostly poking around the cardiovascular and respiratory systems of birds and reptiles, and "function" was just a word we tossed around all day without thinking twice about it -- the function of the sinus venosus, the function of avian air sacs, whatever came up that week. Now that I'm retired I've been reading philosophy of biology on the side (my daughter's doing a philosophy PhD and keeps sending me papers), and it turns out theres a real fight over what we even mean when we say a trait "has" a function. On one side is the etiological camp -- Wright's original 1973 account, tightened up later by Neander -- where the function of a trait just is whatever it was selected for, so a function claim ends up being basically a historical claim about past selection pressures. On the other side is Cummins' causal-role approach, where the function of a part is just whatever it contributes to some capacity of the containing system that happens to interest the person asking, no history required. Amundson and Lauder even argued this second view is closer to how working biologists actually talk, which stung a little since its basically how I always used the word in the lab.

Two cases keep nagging at me though, and I don't think either account handles both of them cleanly. First, malfunction: a heart with a busted valve still has the function of pumping blood even though it's failing at doing it, and etiology explains that easily enough (selection history doesn't care that this particular token heart happens to be broken), but causal-role seems to struggle to say what the "function" even refers to once the actual causal contribution isn't happening anymore. Second, co-option: feathers apparently started out doing something like insulation or display and only later got recruited for flight (this is Gould and Vrba's old "exaptation" idea), so depending on whether you look at the original selection pressure or the selection pressure currently maintaining the trait, you get different answers about a feather's "real" function -- while causal-role just shrugs and says a feather has several functions simultaneously, relative to whichever capacity you happen to pick, which feels a bit too easy to me. Could you write me an essay that actually wrestles with both of these cases, lays out the strongest version of each account, and then commits to a real verdict -- pick a side, or some specific hybrid, whatever you think is right -- rather than just concluding that "both accounts have their merits"? I want something I can actually argue with over coffee.
