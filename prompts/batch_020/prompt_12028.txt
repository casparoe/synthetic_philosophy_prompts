Over the past couple of years, a close friend and I have developed a habit of taking turns doing favors for each other, with each favor a little bigger than the last: it started with watering each other's plants, moved on to airport pickups, then to week-long house- and pet-sitting stints, and most recently to lending each other fairly substantial sums of money, always on the understanding that the other would even things out with something comparable when their turn came around, rather than simply paying cash. We both know this arrangement has a natural end in sight, since my friend has accepted a job abroad and will be moving away for good early next year, after which this kind of ongoing back-and-forth will no longer really be possible.

My partner, who studied economics, finds the whole arrangement puzzling once that ending is taken seriously. Her argument runs roughly as follows: whoever ends up doing the last favor before the move has no way of ensuring anything ever comes back to them for it, since there will be no further turn left in which the other could reciprocate, so that final favor should never actually happen in the first place. But if it is never going to happen, then the favor before it is just as pointless to offer, since whoever would be offering it can already see how things will end. Carrying that reasoning all the way back to the very beginning, she concludes that two people who could see this coming from the outset should never have gotten past the smallest, most trivial favors, let alone built up to lending each other real money.

I have turned this over in my head a great deal since she first made the point, and it still bothers me, because our arrangement has plainly worked out well and it does not feel like we have simply been lucky. I have no formal background in this sort of thing myself, but her argument seems to me to be missing something about what it would really take for two people to reason things through that thoroughly, rather than showing that arrangements like ours are doomed from the outset. I would like to know whether my partner's reasoning is basically sound, so that we are just a fortunate exception, or whether there is a genuine flaw somewhere in the chain of argument itself.
