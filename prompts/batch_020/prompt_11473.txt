So for about the last nine years, a loose group of us from an online rationality/decision-theory forum have done an unofficial long weekend retreat at somebody's cabin - no university involved, nobody's getting paid, basically grown-up summer camp for people who never stopped arguing about Newcomb's problem. I've handled the logistics (booking the place, food, schedule) since year two, even though I never went to college and have zero formal background in any of this - I just fell into the rationalist-blog rabbit hole years ago and never really climbed back out. The guy who normally gives the little welcome speech on the first night had to drop out at the last minute, and everyone half-jokingly decided I should do it instead, so now I'm stuck actually doing it.

This year's retreat theme is common knowledge - not just knowledge, but the specific weird thing where it matters not just that everyone knows something, but that everyone knows that everyone knows it, all the way up. I've been low-key obsessed with this since I first worked through that puzzle where a bunch of islanders can all see each others eye color but it's never mentioned out loud, and then a visitor says something totally obvious that everyone already knew, and somehow that one public sentence starts a countdown that ends with people leaving days later - like saying it out loud in front of everybody did something that everyone just privately knowing it couldn't do. A bunch of the old-timers at this retreat have spent actual years arguing about the harder version of that, like whether the Aumann thing about honest people not being able to agree to disagree really means what everyone thinks it means, or whether "common knowledge of rationality" really forces perfect players to defect immediately in the centipede game, or if that whole argument quietly falls apart the moment anyone deviates from it. Meanwhile every year a few people bring partners who've never heard any of this and are honestly just there for the lake. I tried explaining the eye-color puzzle to mine last week and completely butchered it, which is a big part of why I'm nervous about this.

Could you write me the opening-night remarks? Something like 4-5 minutes out loud. It has to work as an actual warm, funny "welcome, here's the vibe" speech and not a lecture, but I'd really like it built around one real idea about common knowledge - the eye-color thing, the Aumann thing, whatever you think works best - that lands as a nice joke for the veterans who'll immediately clock what it's doing, while still making total sense to someone hearing all of this for the first time. Extra credit if it somehow gets at why we bother flying out and saying this stuff to each others faces once a year instead of just posting it on the forum, without turning into a TED talk about it.
