This semester I'm taking a seminar on formal theories of distributive justice, and we recently covered Harsanyi's aggregation theorem. As I understand the result, if every individual's preferences over uncertain prospects satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and the social preference ordering satisfies those same axioms together with the Pareto principle, then the social welfare function is forced to take the form of a positive weighted sum of the individual utility functions. Since almost no one wants to deny the Pareto principle, it seems to follow that anyone who rejects utilitarianism in favor of prioritarianism or strict egalitarianism must, whether they realize it or not, be committed to violating Pareto somewhere. For my final paper, I want to spell out this argument carefully and use it to show that prioritarian and egalitarian objections to utilitarianism are ultimately confused. Could you help me anticipate the strongest objections a prioritarian might raise against this line of argument, so that I can address them in advance?
