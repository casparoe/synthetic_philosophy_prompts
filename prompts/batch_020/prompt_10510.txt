I'm on the standards committee at the outlet I write for, and we've got an annoying case in front of us. A piece our science desk ran stated flat-out that social media is causing the rise in teen depression, no hedge, leaning on the same correlational and quasi-experimental evidence Haidt uses in The Anxious Generation. A few academics in the Odgers/Orben camp complained that effect sizes are tiny and causation's nowhere near established, so we owe readers a correction. Half the committee thinks we overclaimed; the other half thinks the reporter had plenty of good evidence and that hedging everything with "some scientists believe" is it's own kind of dishonesty, not more honest, just mushier. We meet thursday and I need an actual memo: what to do about this piece, plus a real rule reporters can use for how much evidence is "enough" to assert a contested causal claim outright instead of attributing it. Don't split the difference, and give the anti-hedging camp a fair hearing -- committees like ours usually wave them off too fast.
