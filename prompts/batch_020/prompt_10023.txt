Okay so this has been rattling around in my head for a couple weeks now and I don't really have anyone in my life to hash it out with, so here goes. I've always gotten "akrasia" in the basic everyday sense - weakness of will, the classic case being you know full well that eating the whole bag of chips at midnight is a bad idea, but the craving wins out over your better judgement anyway and you do it. Totally intuitive, everyone's lived it. But I was listening to a philosophy podcast in the car last week and the guest brought up something called "epistemic akrasia," which from what I could gather is when you keep on believing something is true while AT THE SAME TIME believing that you shouldn't believe it - like your convinced of something even though you also think your own evidence doesn't actually back it up. There was also this other term he kept using, "higher order evidence," basically meaning evidence about whether you can even trust your own reasoning in the first place, if that makes sense. The example that stuck with me was something like a scientist who gets told, reliably, that a pill she just swallowed (thinking it was just a vitamin) is known to mess up people's logical reasoning in a big chunk of cases, and even once she's been told this about herself she still finds her own conclusion just as convincing as before. So now she believes her conclusion AND believes "I have good reason to think my judgement right now can't fully be trusted." Apparently thats supposed to be a real head scratcher for philosophers, maybe even close to an impossible or irrational state to be in, but honestly it sounds like something people (me included) do all the time.

What I can't shake is whether this "epistemic" akrasia is actually the same underlying failure as regular weakness of will, just moved over from actions to beliefs, or if using the same word for both is kind of misleading. Like with the chips, theres an obvious force pulling against my better judgement - the craving, my lousy self control, whatever you wanna call it. What's supposed to be playing that same role when the "action" in question is just holding onto a belief instead of eating something? Is there some equivalent tug of war going on in the belief case, or is it a totally different kind of animal that just happens to have gotten the same name slapped onto it. Could you really compare the two properly for me, and say which one strikes you as the clearer, more "genuine" case of irrationality, and why?
