Ok so I've been chewing on the paradox of voting again -- the thing where the odds you're literally the tie-breaking vote in any real election are so vanishingly small that under plain old causal decision theory, voting basically never pencils out once you weigh that against the cost of showing up. But the move I keep seeing pop up on rationalist-adjacent blogs is to bring in superrationality / evidential or functional decision theory instead: your vote isn't just one causally isolated act, its more like evidence about (or even "the same computation as") what everyone who reasons the way you do will end up doing, which changes the expected value math completely. Could you write me a short story, just a few hundred words, where a character actually works through that CDT-vs-EDT/FDT tension in their own head on the way to the polls? I don't want a philosophy lecture wearing a story as a costume -- I want the decision theory to come through in what the character notices and decides, not through exposition dumps.
