I'm taking a seminar this term on the epistemic foundations of democracy, and this week's session centered on the Condorcet Jury Theorem: if each voter is more likely than not to be right, and voters judge independently of one another, majority rule converges on the correct answer as the electorate grows. It strikes me that those same two premises seem to license opposite institutional conclusions, and I'd like to see that tension dramatized. Write a dialogue between two political theorists who run into each other in the lobby after a public lecture on electoral reform: one holds that the theorem is the strongest argument available for one-person-one-vote majority rule, the other that it is just as clearly the strongest argument for restricting the franchise to those who can demonstrate the relevant competence. Have them press each other on whether the theorem's independence and competence conditions could genuinely be secured under either arrangement, rather than letting the exchange collapse into a generic dispute about fairness versus truth.
