Write a dialogue between Thomas Aquinas and the twelfth-century political thinker John of Salisbury, author of the Policraticus, on the question of tyrannicide. Have them work through at least two contrasting cases: a ruler who seized his throne by force or fraud and never held legitimate title to it, and a ruler who inherited his throne lawfully but has since turned to cruelty and plunder. Each figure should argue from his own actual views on tyranny, including how much weight he gives to legitimate title versus tyrannical exercise of power, and what role he assigns to public versus private authority in removing a ruler. Rather than talking past each other, they should press one another on the weakest points of their respective positions.
