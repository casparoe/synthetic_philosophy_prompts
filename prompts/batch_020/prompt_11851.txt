Over dinner recently, a friend and I fell into a long, unresolved argument about the regress problem in epistemology. Her position was that infinitism and coherentism are, at bottom, the same view described in two different vocabularies: both give up on the idea of a privileged stopping point for justification, so whether one calls the resulting structure an endless chain or an interconnected web is a matter of framing rather than substance. I objected that Peter Klein's infinitism and the coherentism defended by Laurence BonJour are structurally quite different: Klein explicitly rules out any repetition among the reasons in the regress, whereas coherentism seems to depend on exactly this kind of mutual, reciprocal support among beliefs. She replied that Klein's own fallback position -- that a believer need not actually possess infinitely many reasons, only have further reasons "available" on request -- has to bottom out somewhere in the believer's existing stock of beliefs, which is just holism under another name. I had no good answer for that at the time. Could you compare infinitism and coherentism as responses to the regress problem and adjudicate the specific point we couldn't settle: is there a principled structural difference between the two views, or does Klein's appeal to "availability" quietly reintroduce the same holistic picture that coherentists rely on?
