I was hanging out with a couple of my grad students after our decision theory seminar last week (we'd just finished going through Newcomb's problem and Hofstadter-style superrationality for the umpteenth time) and one of them brought up acausal trade - the idea, popular in some corners of decision theory and AI safety circles, that two agents who can never causally affect each other in any way whatsoever might still strike a bargain and actually keep their end of it, purely because each has correctly worked out what the other would do. The classic cases are things like agents in causally disconnected regions of an infinite universe, civilizations in different Everett branches, or a mind that only exists as a very accurate simulation someone else is running. I find the whole idea fascinating but also kind of vertiginous - there's something genuinely strange about the thought of being bound by a "deal" with someone you'll never meet, never observe, and who will never even know whether you held up your end.

Anyways, I'd love it if you could write me a short story that gets at this weirdness from the inside, rather then just being a plot where "it later turns out the distant aliens secretly cooperated with us." I want something that actually sits with the reasoning and the psychology of an agent (human, alien, AI, whatever you like) working out that it should honor a bargain nobody can ever verify or enforce - and then goes through with it anyway. Feel free to invent the setting, I'm not fussed about that part, but I do want the mechanics of the trade (why they believe it's rational, what's at stake if the reasoning turns out to be wrong, whatever doubt creeps in along the way) to be load-bearing for the plot rather than just window dressing. Doesnt need to be long, a few thousand words is plenty - I'd rather it be philosophically honest than sprawling.
