Okay so I've been chewing on this thought experiment for weeks and I can't shake it. Picture a universe with literally nothing in it except two spheres - same size, same material, same temperature, everything, sitting some exact distance apart, and thats genuinely all that exists anywhere. The part that gets me is that because there's nothing else around, even relationships can't tell them apart, like "distance to the nearest other object" is the exact same fact for both of them. So it's not just that they look alike, literally every single true statement about one is also true of the other. I guess this traces back to Leibniz, who had this rule that if two things share every property they're actually the same thing, and then some later philosopher (Black? I think) used the spheres to argue that's wrong, there's obviously two of them. But then what actually makes them two? Is there some "thisness" to each one that isn't a property at all, which kinda feels like cheating? I'd love a short story that actually sits inside that problem instead of just explaining it to me - doesn't have to literally involve floating spheres, it can be set wherever, but I want it to really wrestle with two things being totally indistinguishable, even relationally, and still somehow being two seperate things.
