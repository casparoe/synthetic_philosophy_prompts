So I just learned that Gottlob Frege — the guy who basically invented modern predicate logic and all the sense/reference stuff — left behind a political diary from near the end of his life (1924, he died in '25) that's full of hardcore antisemitic, anti-democratic, nationalist views: wanting Jews stripped of political rights, despising the Weimar republic and parliamentary democracy, all of it. Fine, tons of historical figures held ugly views, that's not shocking on its own. What I'm actually curious about is a specific argument I've seen floated: that this isnt just unrelated personal ugliness, but actually connects to his logical Platonism and his rejection of psychologism — like the same impulse that made him insist logical truth is fully objective and mind-independent, not reducible to anyone's subjective psychology, is somehow the same impulse behind rejecting democracy, which treats political truth as just whatever the aggregate of individual opinions says. Is there a real, serious version of that argument, or is it just a cute-sounding analogy that falls apart once you push on it?
