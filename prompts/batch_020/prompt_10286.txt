I've been re-reading Menkiti and Gyekye for a paper on relational personhood and want to sanity check where they actually diverge, since students always flatten them into "ubuntu says community matters." Menkiti's version is the strong one -- personhood is achieved gradually through moral maturation and incorporation into community, its a matter of degree, and on his view you can basically fail to become a full person. Gyekye thinks this concedes too much and proposes a "moderate" communitarianism that keeps community as constitutive of the self but holds onto some innate, pre-social capacities (rationality, will) so individual rights don't just dissolve. Can you compare the two and tell me whose view handles hard cases -- infants, people with severe cognitive disabilities -- better? Specifically, does Gyekye's appeal to innate capacities actually solve that problem, or does it just quietly reintroduce the individualist premises he's supposedly rejecting? Push back on my read of either philosopher if you think I'm being unfair to them.
