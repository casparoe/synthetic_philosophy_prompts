In my grad mechanism design seminar we spent a whole session on the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem, and I came away thinking the takeaway was pretty clean: with three or more alternatives, any voting rule is either a dictatorship or someone can benefit from lying about their preferences, full stop, no exceptions. Felt like a nice tidy impossibility result, kind of like Arrow's but for strategic behavior instead of just aggregation.

Except then in the weeks after, the same professor kept casually calling other mechanisms "strategy-proof" without blinking -- the VCG mechanism once we got to auctions, then the median-voter rule for siting a public good when everyone's preferences are single-peaked, and even a throwaway line about how "serial dictatorship" mechanisms for allocating dorm rooms or course seats are strategy-proof too. My study group got kinda stuck on this: either non-dictatorial strategy-proof mechanisms exist or they don't, so how can the theorem and all these later examples acutally be true at the same time? One guy in the group insists the later ones must be quietly cheating somehow (extra assumptions, redefined terms, whatever), but none of us could pin down what the trick actually was. Is there a real reconciliation here, or is "strategy-proof" just being used loosely across different parts of the course?
