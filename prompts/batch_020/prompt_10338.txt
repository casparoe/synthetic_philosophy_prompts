Since retiring after forty years in experimental condensed-matter physics, I've taken up philosophy, and I now hold a seat on my university's task force reviewing a proposed Composite Research Impact Score — combining field-normalized citations, grant income, and top-quartile publication counts — meant to replace narrative peer review for annual merit raises and, eventually, tenure decisions. A pilot in the chemistry department already shows predictable side effects: papers split into minimal publishable units, reciprocal-citation arrangements between cooperating labs, and a drift away from ambitious or null-result work toward safer, more citable projects. Half the task force wants to patch the formula; the other half wants to scrap quantification and return to narrative review, which administrators say was slower and let personal networks quietly bias outcomes. Please draft the memo I'll bring to the full task force: a specific recommendation for next year's review cycle, reasoning both sides could actually accept, and a serious account of why the metrics were proposed in the first place, rather than simply repeating that metrics get gamed.
