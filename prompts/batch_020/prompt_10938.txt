I've been chasing a story that grew out of that open letter from last year where a long list of consciousness researchers piled on to call Integrated Information Theory pseudoscience, and it sent me down a rabbit hole into some of the older fights over the theory, especially the one between Giulio Tononi and the computer scientist Scott Aaronson. From what I remember of Aaronson's old blog post (feel free to correct me on the details, I read it years ago), he built some kind of densely connected grid of simple logic gates, the sort of structure you'd use for error correction, and showed that if you actually run IIT's math on it you get a phi value that dwarfs any serious estimate of phi for a human brain, even though the thing isn't doing anything anyone would call thinking. The whole point was a reductio: if phi says that grid is more conscious than you or me, so much the worse for phi. Tononi's answer, at least as its usually retold to me, amounted to something like "fine, then it's conscious, that's what the theory predicts and I'm not fudging the math just because the conclusion sounds silly."

Everyone I've mentioned this to treats Tononi's answer as him being too stubborn to admit the theory broke, and honestly I was leaning that way too, but for the piece I want to actually test the opposite reading. So here's what I'd like from you: argue that Tononi's bullet-biting isn't stubbornness at all, but is actually the only response available to someone who takes IIT's explicit rejection of functionalism seriously, and that the "this is absurd" reaction, mine included, is quietly smuggling in exactly the assumption IIT was built to reject, namely that whether a system is conscious has anything to do with what it does or computes rather than its own intrinsic causal structure.

Don't hedge or give me a split-the-difference answer, commit to that case as hard as you can. And once you've made it, take on the obvious comeback, that you don't have to be a functionalist to think a glorified parity-check circuit is a bad candidate for experience, and tell me whether that comeback still ends up depending on functionalist premises through the back door, or admit if it doesn't.
