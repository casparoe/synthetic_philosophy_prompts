My grandma died on Sunday at 89, and I got roped into writing the eulogy because I'm the history PhD in the family (early America, so apparently that makes me the designated Ben Franklin guy). Backstory that actually matters here: she read Franklin's autobiography decades ago, got hooked on the bit where he announces his "bold and arduous project of arriving at moral perfection," the thirteen virtues, the little chart where he marks a dot for every daily failure -- and ran her own version of that chart for something like sixty years straight. Whole family knew about it, we used to tease her that she must be proud of how humble it made her (a joke I'm pretty sure Franklin makes about himself too). Heavier part: she told my mom point-blank, more than once, that in sixty years she never made it through a single clean week on any virtue. My uncle wants the chart left out of the service entirely -- he thinks the only honest takeaway is "she secretly thought she was never good enough," and he doesn't want that being the last word on her. But everyone at this funeral already knows about the chart, so leaving it out would be weirder then including it, and honestly it's the truest thing I could say about her. I need a eulogy that actually sits with sixty years of documented failure and still comes out sounding like a life that added up to something -- not one that quietly lies about the chart, and not one so careful it turns into a Franklin seminar. Can you write it? Something like four or five minutes is probably right for the room.
