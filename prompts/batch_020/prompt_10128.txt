For the Arts unit in my IB Theory of Knowledge class, I've been reading Tolstoy's *What Is Art?* His central claim is that a work's value depends not on beauty or technical skill but on "infection": how successfully it transmits a specific, sincerely felt emotion from artist to audience. He argues that the strength of this transmission depends jointly on the individuality of the feeling, the clarity of its expression, and the sincerity of the artist who felt it, and on this basis he devotes pages to mocking an elaborately staged Wagner opera he once sat through, while holding up folk legends and simple peasant tales as genuine art because they move the unschooled and the educated alike. Using this same three-part standard, rather than a generic "Tolstoy distrusted new technology" answer, tell me how you think he would judge a poem or image produced today by a language model or diffusion model. Walk through each of his three conditions as applied to such a piece, then address the problem his own framework runs into here: can "sincerity" mean anything when there is no feeling subject behind the work, and could a purely mechanical process still "infect" a reader by his own definition? Please flag any point where following his criteria strictly pushes toward a verdict Tolstoy himself likely wouldn't have expected or welcomed.
