So this actually happened to me and I still don't quite know what to do about it. Part of my job covering science and ideas is that I get roped into judging a smallish science-journalism award every year -- five of us on the panel, call it the Beacon Prize, that's not its real name, I'd rather not out my fellow judges -- and the prize's rules say a piece can only win if the panel decides, by majority, that it clears two seperate bars: factually airtight, and genuinely original rather than just beautifully written.

This year the sentimental favorite was a big investigation into deep-sea-mining startups, and something strange happened when we tallied things up. Judged bar by bar, it cleared both: three of the five of us voted yes on accuracy, and three of five voted yes on originality (mostly, not entirely, the same three people). But earlier in the meeting, before we split things into those two separate questions, I'd also asked everyone for a straight gut yes-or-no on whether the piece deserved the prize outright, and only two of five said yes. Same five people, same actual views as far as I can tell -- I checked everyone's reasoning afterwards, and every single person's yes-or-no matched exactly what you'd predict from their own two votes, nobody flip-flopped -- and yet the two ways of tallying the very same opinions hand you opposite winners.

I mentioned this to a friend who's an academic philosopher, does something with logic and voting theory, and she got weirdly excited and said it sounded like "Arrow's theorem for verdicts," then had to jump on a call before she could explain what she meant. So now I'm fairly confident this is an actual documented Thing and not just our panel being sloppy, which is reassuring and unsettling in about equal measure. What I actually need is the memo I'm supposed to send the other four judges before we reconvene next Tuesday to lock in this year's results. I don't want it to just lay out the puzzle -- I want it to take a real position: it should say whether the deep-sea-mining piece won or not, recommend which way of tallying votes we should adopt as our standing rule going forward so we're not relitigating this every year, and seriously engage with the best argument for whichever method it ends up rejecting -- at least two of my co-judges already have strong opinions about which one we should use, and I'd rather the memo had already met their best objection than get shot down in the room.
