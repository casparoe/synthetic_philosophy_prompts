I'm writing a novel that turns on a scene in which an infantry captain, four years into an otherwise unblemished career, petitions her branch's conscientious objector review board for discharge -- not because she has come to reject war as such, but because she has concluded, on grounds she can defend in detail, that the specific war she has been ordered back to is unjust. She has committed no infraction and says she would deploy without hesitation to a war she believed just. The regulation the board applies exempts only those conscientiously opposed to "participation in war in any form" and says nothing about objecting to a single conflict, so her petition sits awkwardly outside the category the rule was written for. For the chapter depicting the board's deliberations, I need a realistic memo recommending a disposition -- approval, denial, or something in between -- that engages honestly with the reasons armies have traditionally refused to let soldiers choose which wars to fight, and with the strongest argument for why her case might deserve an exception.
