I'm the philosophy department's representative on my university's research-integrity committee, and we're deadlocked on a case with almost no paper trail. A postdoc alleges, from memory alone and months after leaving the lab, that her supervisor told her to omit an inconvenient outlier before publication; the supervisor denies it flatly, and there is no email, notebook entry, or second witness -- though routine data-purging in the lab means that absence proves nothing either way. Half the committee thinks a finding requires evidence independent of her word, since sincere testimony can still be honestly mistaken; the other half fears that such a standard would shield exactly the private misconduct that leaves no trace, and would rather weigh her testimony on its own merits -- consistency, specificity, absence of any apparent motive -- as we would weigh any witness denied corroboration through no fault of her own. Please draft the memo I need to circulate before next week's meeting: recommend a finding, propose a standing evidentiary standard for future single-witness cases, and take the strongest version of whichever side you don't adopt seriously rather than wave it away.
