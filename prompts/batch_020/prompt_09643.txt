My physics teacher, Mr. Halvorsen, is retiring after 34 years and a few of us seniors get to speak at his goodbye dinner. His whole teaching philosophy boiled down to one line he'd repeat every time a theory we'd learned got replaced by a better one (Newton to Einstein, whatever) -- "that's fine, we're just getting closer to the truth." It was basically his catchphrase. I was procrastinating studying for finals and fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole and found out the actual philosophy term for that, verisimilitude, got kind of demolished in the 70s -- two guys named Miller and Tichy proved that under Popper's own definition of it, you can never actually show that one false theory is closer to the truth than another false theory, and there's apparently a whole unsolved "problem of verisimilitude" about patching this that nobody's agreed on since. So now it feels weird to just get up there and repeat his line like its some settled, proven fact. Can you write me the speech? The room's gonna be mostly parents, siblings, and other teachers who have zero clue about any of this, plus a handful of his old AP physics kids who'll definitely clock a good reference if there is one, and it needs to feel warm and genuine, not like im quietly correcting him at his own retirement party.
