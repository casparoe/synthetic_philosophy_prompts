At the rehearsal dinner, the groom and I fell into a long argument about the regress problem in epistemology. He thinks any regress that doesn't terminate in something self-justifying is a dodge, whether it's coherentism's web of mutual support or infinitism's endless chain, and I think that verdict rests on a bad objection to the latter: the idea that a finite mind would have to consciously hold infinitely many reasons at once, when the position only requires a further reason to be available on demand. I'm giving the best man speech tomorrow, and I want to finish the argument in front of him: write the toast the way a committed infinitist would build the case for why he and his partner belong together, so that every reason I offer is immediately met by a further reason behind it, never resting on one bedrock anecdote, while still building to a genuine emotional payoff rather than sounding like I'm stalling. It has to work first as an actual wedding toast — funny and warm, and completely legible to the two-thirds of the room who have never heard the word "infinitism."
