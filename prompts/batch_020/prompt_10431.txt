I am taking a seminar this term on theories of well-being and rational agency, and a real situation in my family has me wishing I could apply something from the course, if only I could tell what. My aunt is now in an irreversible coma after a severe stroke, with no realistic chance of regaining consciousness. Fifteen years ago, while part of a religious community that emphasized preserving life at any cost, she signed a directive requesting that she be kept on life support "as long as there is any chance at all." In the years since leaving that community and reconciling with the rest of the family, however, she used to say, half-jokingly, that she could not imagine anything worse than being "kept around as a vegetable." Some relatives think the signed document settles the matter, since it is the one thing she actually put in writing; others think we should honor what she would want now, given how much she changed, rather than hold her to a version of herself she had already moved past. I do not know how to weigh her one explicit, formal decision against our various guesses at an updated set of wishes she never got to state herself. Is there a principled way to decide whose claim on her "actual" wishes should win out?
