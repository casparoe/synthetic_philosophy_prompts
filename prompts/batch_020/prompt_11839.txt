I picked up The Selfish Gene at a used bookstore last year and that sent me down a rabbit hole into the whole "units of selection" fight in evolutionary biology - genes vs individuals vs group selection. One thing that's been stuck in my head is a toy example (I think its from Sober or somewhere in that multilevel selection literature) where you split a population into groups, an altruistic trait actually loses to selfishness in EVERY single group, and yet the trait still goes up in frequency across the whole population because the more altruistic groups produce more offspring overall - some kind of Simpson's paradox thing. Group selectionists treat that as proof group selection is a real, separate causal force in nature. The hardcore gene's-eye-view crowd says it's not a new force at all, just kin selection dressed up in a different accounting scheme so it looks novel. Can you write a dialogue between one person from each camp hashing this out using that exact example? I don't want them just trading taglines - I want them actually responding to each other's specific moves, and trying to settle whether this is a genuine disagreement about what's causing what in nature, or just two bookkeeping systems for the same underlying numbers.
