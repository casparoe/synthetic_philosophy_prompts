I've been an on-and-off reader of the EA Forum and LessWrong for the better part of a decade, and I recently found myself needing to put some of that reading to direct, practical use. I sit on the small grants committee of an effective-altruism-aligned foundation that has, for several years, renewed a standing grant to an organization distributing insecticide-treated bednets in a high-transmission region of sub-Saharan Africa. The case for renewal has always rested on an uncontroversial cost-effectiveness model: well-replicated RCT evidence on child mortality, a defensible cost-per-life-saved figure, and no real dispute about the near-term facts on the ground.

At last month's meeting, though, one committee member, who has been reading Hilary Greaves and others on what's sometimes called "complex cluelessness," argued that this model quietly assumes something we can't actually justify: that the near-term mortality effect is a reasonable proxy for the grant's all-things-considered value. Averting child deaths changes population size, which in turn ramifies into economic growth, land use, and any number of further downstream effects that we have no principled way to sign, let alone weigh against the near-term benefit we can measure. She wants us to pause the renewal pending further analysis, or at least heavily discount the cost-effectiveness figure we've relied on. A second member countered that this objection, taken seriously, would apply with equal force to every other use of the money, including making no grant at all, so treating it as a reason to single out this particular grant is arbitrary. Pressed on the point, though, he conceded he couldn't say why the argument actually fails, as opposed to just gesturing at how absurd its implications would be if generalized.

We meet again in two weeks and have to decide whether to renew as before, renew with modified terms, or pause the grant pending further work. Could you draft the short decision memo I'll circulate ahead of that meeting? I'd like it to recommend one specific course of action with a concrete next step and timeframe, to take the cluelessness worry seriously enough to explain why it does or does not warrant changing our behavior on this particular grant rather than either waving it away as a debater's trick or treating it as a reason to stop making any comparative judgments at all, and to give the committee member who raised it reasons she could actually engage with rather than just a restatement of the standard cost-effectiveness case.
