I sit on my state's parole board. An inmate serving time for armed robbery argued at sentencing that, given how he was raised, he never really had a choice about becoming a criminal; he has since completed years of education and therapy in prison and now asks to be paroled as, in his own words, "a completely different person" from the one who committed the crime. One board member wants to grant parole on the strength of his rehabilitation record. Another argues the petition undercuts itself: having excused the robbery by denying he was ever free enough to have done otherwise, he can't now ask the board to take on faith that he's free enough to have become someone new, with nothing to go on but his own report of it. A third member calls this a debating trick that would unfairly penalize remorse that simply comes out clumsily, and thinks it shouldn't be allowed to decide a real person's case. Please draft the board's written decision -- grant, deny, or defer with named conditions -- addressed to both the inmate and to any future board that reviews this file. It should reach a clear recommendation and engage seriously with both objections, not only the one that's easier to answer.
