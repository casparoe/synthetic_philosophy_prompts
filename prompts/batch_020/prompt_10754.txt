So in my philosophy of religion seminar we just spent two weeks on the kalam cosmological argument, working through big chunks of Ghazali's Tahafut al-Falasifa, and theres a thing from that unit that's been stuck in my head since. Ghazali's whole defense of the causal premise (everything that begins to exist has a cause) is bundled together with his occasionalism from the 17th discussion, where he denies that fire actually causes cotton to burn - he says the connection between them is just God's habitual custom ('ada) of making the two co-occur, not some necessary causal power sitting in the fire itself, and he even brings up Abraham surviving the furnace to show the "natural" effect can be switched off anytime God wills it. So on his view no created thing ever really does causal work; God is the sole true efficient cause acting moment to moment, and what we call natural laws are just very reliable habits God happens to keep.

Then in discussion section my TA brought up how modern defenders of kalam, like William Lane Craig, get hit with the objection that quantum events (radioactive decay, vacuum fluctuations popping into existence) seem to have no determinate physical cause, and that this supposedly undercuts premise 1. A friend in my class thinks that objection is basically fatal to the whole argument no matter what, but I'm not convinced that's true specifically for Ghazali's version, since he never thought physical antecedents were doing any real causal work to begin with. Couldn't he just say "sure, no necessitating physical cause, there never was one, God's doing that directly too, same as everything else"? Or does quantum indeterminism actually mess with something deeper in his setup, like the 'ada mechanism itself, which seems to need pretty reliable customary patterns to even get off the ground in the first place? I guess what I'm asking is: if you reconstruct this on Ghazali's own terms rather than Craig's modern version, would he treat quantum indeterminacy as a non-issue he's basically already accounted for a thousand years early, or as a genuine problem for his picture, and why?
