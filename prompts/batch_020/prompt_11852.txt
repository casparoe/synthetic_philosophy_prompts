So I've been tinkering with a little hobby project on the side — a toy bot for a game with hidden info, where it updates its beliefs about where stuff is hidden based on hints, except none of the hints are ever 100% reliable. Like the bot might go from "50% sure the item's in room 3" to "80% sure" after a clue, but never all the way to certainty. I looked into how to do this properly instead of hacking it, and it turns out this is basically what's called Jeffrey conditionalization — updating on "uncertain evidence" instead of the textbook Bayes rule where you condition on something you now know for sure.

Here's what's bugging me though. With normal Bayesian conditionalization, I always understood that order doesn't matter — if you learn fact A and then fact B, you land in the exact same spot as if you'd learned B then A, since conditioning on both is just conditioning on "A and B," and that's symmetric. So I just assumed the same had to be true here, just with soft partial updates instead of hard facts. But when I coded up two successive uncertain updates for my bot and ran them in both orders, I got two noticeably different final probability distributions out the other end. I burned a whole evening assuming I had a normalization bug somewhere before it occured to me that maybe the math is just built that way. Is that actually right — does the order you feed in uncertain evidence genuinely change your final beliefs under Jeffrey's rule, even when the two hints are about totally separate things? Because if so, that seems like a pretty damning property for something that's supposed to be THE rational way to update beliefs, and I don't get how anyone uses this stuff for real decision-making without it bugging them constantly. Am I missing something basic, or is this an actual known issue with the whole framework?
