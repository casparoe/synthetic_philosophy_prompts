This is the one fight in EA-adjacent philosophy that never actually seems to get argued out properly, everyone just retreats to their own camp. Camp one: the wild-animal-suffering people (Ng's old "welfare biology" paper, Tomasik, Horta) -- point out that most wild animals are r-selected, huge broods, almost all of them die young and badly, so if you actually add up the hedons nature is running a massive deficit and just leaving it alone isnt some neutral default, its a choice. Camp two: ecological holists in the Callicott/land-ethic tradition, who think that whole framing is a category error -- the "biotic community" isn't reducible to a sum of individual welfares -- and are basically willing to eat Tom Regan's "environmental fascism" charge as the cost of taking ecology seriously.

can you write me a dialogue between one person from each camp? put them on the advisory board of a rewilding project (reintroducing wolves, say) that they actually have to vote on, knowing it means a lot more predation than the status quo. I want real back and forth, not two monologues -- the welfarist should have to face the "is aggregate hedonic tone across a trillion sentient-ish insects even the right unit of account" objection, and the holist should have to actually answer whether they're just biting the fascism bullet or if there's a principled way out. neither one should fold, but by the end they should land on roughly where the actual crux is.
