I'm taking a graduate seminar on formal epistemology this term, and this week's problem set centers on van Fraassen's "Judy Benjamin problem," which we came to right after covering Jeffrey conditionalization. A soldier is dropped somewhere in a region that two independent, perpendicular borders divide into four equally likely quadrants: Red-1, Red-2, Blue-1, and Blue-2. Command radios her a single message: "If you're in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that you're in Red-2 rather than Red-1." Everyone in my study group agreed on the arithmetic: if you update by finding the distribution that minimizes relative entropy against the uniform prior, subject to that one constraint, Red-1 and Red-2 do end up in a 3:1 ratio, Blue-1 and Blue-2 stay exactly equal to each other, but her credence that she's in Blue territory at all rises from one half to just over 0.53. What we couldn't agree on is what to make of that. Two people in the group, Owen and Nadia, kept arguing about it after the session broke up, and I've been turning their exchange over ever since without landing anywhere:

(1) Owen said this shows relative-entropy minimization is simply the wrong update rule here. The message is entirely about how to divide up Red territory; it says nothing about Red versus Blue, so a rational update has to leave her credence in Blue at exactly one half. Since minimizing relative entropy doesn't do that, so much the worse for minimizing relative entropy.

(2) Nadia replied that this moves too fast. The message "says nothing about Red versus Blue" only if you read it as the material conditional "not-Red-or-Red-2," which is true no matter how the Red/Blue split falls and so really is silent on it. But nobody is conditionalizing on that. Judy is learning a fact about a conditional probability, that P(Red-2 given Red) equals 3/4, and there's no general reason a constraint like that has to leave some "unrelated" proposition untouched. Owen's intuition was smuggling in the material-conditional reading.

(3) Owen granted the point about the material conditional but came back with a challenge: name a different, equally principled update rule that both incorporates the 3:1 fact and leaves Blue at one half, without just stipulating that outcome by hand. If no such rule exists, he said, that isn't evidence in favor of treating conditionals this way; it's a reason to distrust handing conditional information to a formal updating rule at all, and to reason about cases like this directly instead.

(4) Nadia said Owen had just conceded her actual point without noticing. If every formal rule disturbs some "irrelevant" proposition when conditionalizing on a conditional, then the disturbance isn't a defect of relative-entropy minimization specifically, which is what he'd originally attacked; it's a general discovery about conditionals, namely that they aren't, in general, statements purely about the internal structure of their own antecedent, however much they resemble one on the surface.

Could you go through moves (1) through (4) and say who has the better of it at each step, rather than just declaring an overall winner? I'd also like to know whether their exchange is missing some further option from the literature on this problem that neither of them raises — something that might dissolve the dispute rather than simply settle it in one direction or the other.
