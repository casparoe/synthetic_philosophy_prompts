Ok this is going to sound like a weirdly specific request but here's the backstory. I do biz-dev at a smallish logistics startup, and a couple months ago we let our contract-negotiation bot loose against a bigger competitor's bot to hash out shipping rate terms automatically, no humans in the loop. In one test run the two bots got stuck in this scary escalating loop, each one locking in bigger "walk-away" penalty clauses to look more committed than the other, kind of like a game of chicken where whoever throws their steering wheel out the window first "wins." The outside consultant we'd brought in called it a textbook "commitment race" - apparently a real thing people worry about in AI safety/game theory circles, where agents that can predict or verify each others strategies have an incentive to lock in extreme commitments before the other side does, and if both sides race to do it you end up way worse off than if neither had committed to anything. She said the fix was having both bots defer to what she called a "safe pareto improvement": basically a redesigned bargaining protocol that guarantees each side does at least as well, on average, as they would've under the free-for-all approach, without either company having to reveal the private stuff (our actual walk away point, how much we were bluffing, etc) that the other side could exploit if we'd just been transparent about it instead. Apparently this is based on actual published research and not just a term the consultant made up, which honestly makes it scarier. Anyway I have not been able to stop thinking about how this exact dynamic could play out with way higher stakes than shipping contracts.

So: could you write me a short story that takes this seriously? I want the tension to come from an actual commitment race spiraling, and the resolution (good or bad) to hinge on whether a safe pareto improvement gets found in time, not just generic "AI turns evil" vibes. Think more hard-sci-fi-takes-decision-theory-seriously, like Greg Egan or Ted Chiang, where the characters (human, AI, whatever) actually have to reason through the bargaining problem on the page instead of just talking around it. Doesn't need to be corporate bots like my situation, could be nation-states, AI diplomats, whatever setting works, as long as the underlying logic is genuinely doing the work in the plot. Don't worry about making it long, a few pages is plenty.
