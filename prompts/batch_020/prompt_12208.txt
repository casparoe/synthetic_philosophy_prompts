I organize my department's colloquium series, and next month's invited speaker is a decision theorist who has built her reputation defending updateless decision theory (UDT) as an alternative to the standard causal and evidential approaches. I will be moderating the Q&A that follows her talk, and experience from past sessions has taught me that the open floor tends to produce questions that simply re-elicit the textbook motivation for the view -- dynamic consistency, resistance to money-pump arguments, and so on -- rather than pressing on the places where I suspect it is still shaky. I would like to come in with eight to ten sharper questions of my own to prime the discussion, pitched so that she cannot simply answer by restating the case for the view.

At minimum, I want one question that moves past the standard motivating example -- an agent that hands over a sure sum in exactly the branch of a coin flip where paying can do it no good, solely because being disposed to pay in that branch is what makes it true that a reliable predictor would have rewarded it handsomely had the coin landed the other way -- and pushes her to say why this counts as rational commitment rather than a cleverly constructed exception that only pays off against a very particular kind of predictor. I also want a question on where the prior that "updatelessness" is relativized to is supposed to come from, given that the agent is meant to fix its policy before updating on any information at all, apparently including its own logical or mathematical uncertainty -- and it is not obvious what it would even mean for a fixed program to be uncertain of its own output. A third question should press on the worry -- sometimes labeled a "commitment race" -- that surfaces in the more game-theoretic corners of this literature: if being updateless rewards whichever party commits earliest and most irrevocably, doesn't the view give every bargainer a reason to commit before doing any deliberation whatsoever, which looks like precisely the wrong moral to draw about rational commitment? And I want a fourth question on how the view is meant to handle self-locating uncertainty -- Sleeping-Beauty-style cases, or cases where a single policy is being carried out by more than one copy of the agent at once -- since I have seen the updateless treatment advertised as dissolving the halfer/thirder dispute, and I would like her account of why that is not simply a relabeling of it.

For each of these four, and for whichever others you propose in order to round the set out to eight or ten, please add a short follow-up question keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that I have a next move ready from the chair rather than having to improvise one.
