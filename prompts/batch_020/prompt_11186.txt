I recently went through carrier screening with my partner before we start trying to conceive, and I've since fallen down a rabbit hole reading about what the results actually mean. Both the genetic counselor's handout and the pop-science books I picked up afterward describe my DNA as an 'instruction manual,' a 'program,' or a 'code' carrying 'information' about everything from eye color to disease risk. I'd like to know whether that language is doing real theoretical work or is just convenient shorthand for ordinary chemical causation.

Write me an essay that takes a stand on this question: is the genome a code, or an information-bearer, in some robust and non-metaphorical sense -- or is 'genetic code' talk ultimately dispensable? Engage seriously with both sides rather than settling for the pop-science gloss. In particular, address (1) whether being a genuine code requires an arbitrary, convention-like mapping rather than one fixed by physical or chemical necessity -- the way smoke's correlation with fire isn't a code -- and how Crick's 'frozen accident' hypothesis about the origin of the codon-to-amino-acid assignments bears on that, given that some researchers have also argued for partial chemical affinities behind those same assignments; and (2) whether the sense in which DNA carries 'information' differs at all from the cheap sense in which tree rings carry information about a tree's age, in which case maternal cytoplasm and the embryo's environment would seem to count as equally 'informational.' Defend a specific verdict rather than concluding that it's complicated.
