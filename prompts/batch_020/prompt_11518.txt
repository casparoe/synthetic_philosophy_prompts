I've followed rationalist blogs and forums for years, and the Dutch Book argument keeps coming up as the decisive reason a person's credences must satisfy the probability axioms: if they don't, a bookmaker can construct a set of bets that produces a net loss for that person no matter which outcome occurs. But this seems to be exactly what real bookmakers do to every customer already, rational or not, by pricing both sides of a line (say, -110/-110, so each side's implied probability is above fifty percent) so that the house's margin, often called the vig, is locked in regardless of outcome. If a fixed margin alone lets a bookmaker guarantee itself a profit off anyone, coherent or incoherent, what additional work is the bettor's incoherence supposed to be doing in the philosophical argument? Doesn't the existence of the vig already show that no credence function, however coherent, is truly safe from a Dutch Book?
