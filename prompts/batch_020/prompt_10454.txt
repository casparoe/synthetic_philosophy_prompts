I'm a junior on my high school's Honor Council, a student-and-faculty board that hears academic-integrity cases and sends the Dean of Students a written recommendation for each one; I'm the council's secretary this year, which means I'm the one who actually drafts these memos. We're deadlocked on a case we have to close out by Friday's meeting, and I think the real disagreement is that the two sides on the council are quietly relying on different ideas about what makes someone responsible for what they did, so I was hoping you could help me put together a first draft of the recommendation.

Here is the case, anonymized the way our paperwork always is. A sophomore, M., was caught with a phone showing answers during a chemistry final. At her hearing she explained that a senior, T., had been threatening for weeks to tell the swim coach that she had skipped a mandatory practice, which carries an automatic one-meet suspension, unless she let T. copy her problem sets, and that on the morning of the final T. slid her the answer key under the desk "so there wouldn't be an argument about it," which she then used. That would normally count as coercion under our honor code, which allows a reduced sanction when "the student's capacity for voluntary choice was substantially impaired." But a friend of M.'s testified, unprompted, that M. had told her the week before that she planned to cheat on that exam regardless, because she was failing the class and was more afraid of her parents finding out than of anything T. could do to her. So T.'s threat was genuinely hanging over her, but nothing else in the record suggests it is actually what made her cheat.

The council has split down the middle on how to read our own guidelines. Some members think that because the threat existed, and refusing it would have cost her a spot on the team, her choice was constrained enough that she could not really have done otherwise, so the reduced sanction, a warning plus a retake, should apply no matter what actually motivated her that morning. Others think that since the threat did no work in producing her decision, it is beside the point, and she owes the standard sanction, a zero on the exam plus a semester of probation, exactly as she would if T. had never said anything to her. Could you draft the short memo I would bring to Friday's meeting? I would like it to take a clear position on which reading should govern, recommend one specific sanction along with a timeline for the retake or for the start and length of the probation, and give a fair, non-dismissive account of the strongest reason the other side of the council has for reaching a different conclusion.
