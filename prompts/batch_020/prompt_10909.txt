I'm taking a seminar on philosophy of religion this term, and this week's unit covers Gödel's modal ontological argument, in the version reconstructed from his notes by Dana Scott. The handout gives the following definitions and axioms (P is the second-order predicate "is a positive property"):

Df. 1 (God-like): G(x) ≡ (∀φ)[P(φ) → φ(x)] — x is God-like iff x has every positive property.
Df. 2 (Essence): φ Ess x ≡ φ(x) ∧ (∀ψ)[ψ(x) → □(∀y)(φ(y) → ψ(y))] — φ is an essence of x iff x has φ and φ necessarily entails every property x has.
Df. 3 (Necessary Existence): E(x) ≡ (∀φ)[φ Ess x → □(∃y)φ(y)] — x necessarily exists iff every essence of it is necessarily exemplified.

Ax. 1: [P(φ) ∧ □(∀x)(φ(x) → ψ(x))] → P(ψ)
Ax. 2: P(¬φ) ≡ ¬P(φ)
Ax. 3: P(G)
Ax. 4: P(φ) → □P(φ)
Ax. 5: P(E)

From these, the notes derive in sequence that any positive property is possibly exemplified, that God-likeness is therefore possibly exemplified, that whatever is God-like has God-likeness as its essence, and that whatever is God-like therefore necessarily exists — and then close the argument by invoking the S5 principle that whatever is possibly necessary is necessary, to conclude that a God-like being exists necessarily.

I can follow each individual line, but two things aren't clicking for me. First, I don't see exactly what work Axiom 4 and that final S5 step are doing — the modal machinery feels like it's happening somewhere I can't quite locate. Second, I keep running into commentary claiming that this system forces a "modal collapse," where every truth turns out to be a necessary one. Could you walk me through both points, and explain why the collapse worry is supposed to be a problem specifically for Gödel's argument, rather than a general problem with S5 modal logic?
