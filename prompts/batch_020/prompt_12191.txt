I'm taking a seminar on just war theory this semester, and this week's assignment asks each of us to role-play a member of a military conscientious-objector review board considering a specific petition. The petitioner, C, has no quarrel with war in general — C would fight without hesitation to repel an invasion or stop a genocide — but has become convinced that the particular operation to which C has been assigned lacks just cause, and requests conscientious-objector status on that basis alone, raising no complaint about how the operation is being conducted. Current policy, like that of most Western militaries, only recognizes objection to war 'in any form,' not to one war in particular, so C's petition would ordinarily be dismissed without reaching the merits. Please draft the board's memo: rule on C's petition as policy currently stands, and recommend whether the policy should be revisited for objectors like C. Whichever way it comes down, make sure the memo seriously engages the strongest argument on the other side — either Walzer's case for treating combatants on both sides as moral equals regardless of the justice of their cause, or McMahan's liability-based challenge to that view — rather than just gesturing at both.
