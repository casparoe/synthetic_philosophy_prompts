I am drafting a historical novel narrated from within Johannes Kepler's household during his years in Prague and Linz, and I need to portray his relationship to astrology with more precision than the usual caricature of either a closet skeptic humoring wealthy patrons or a credulous stargazer indistinguishable from the almanac-peddlers he sometimes disparaged. Historians disagree sharply here: some treat his astrological output as essentially mercenary — a way to keep his family fed through court horoscopes and annual prognostica while his real convictions lay in celestial physics — while others argue that the "reformed" astrology he worked out in texts like Tertius Interveniens and De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus was sincere and continuous with his broader cosmology, including the harmonic theory of planetary aspects and the ensouled earth that also produced the Harmonices Mundi. Write an essay that lays out the strongest case for each reading, weighs the evidence — his private correspondence, his rejection of zodiacal signs and houses alongside his retention of aspects, his precarious finances — and defends a specific, committed verdict (not merely "a bit of both") about which picture should govern how I write his inner life, including how confident or embarrassed he ought to seem in a scene where he casts a horoscope he privately doubts.
