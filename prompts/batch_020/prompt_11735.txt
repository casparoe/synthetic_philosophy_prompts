I started a new job about a month ago as basically the first data person at a small startup, and something from this week is bugging me way more than it probably should. We tested two versions of our signup page: old one got 0 signups out of 20 visitors, new one got 4 out of 35. Our rule is we need to be at least 95% sure the new version is actually better before rolling it out everywhere, so I ran the numbers assuming that, before seeing any data, every conversion rate from 0-100% was equally likely, and got about 91%. My cofounder redid the exact same calc but started from a different "neutral" assumption he'd learned about in a stats class (some kind of Jeffreys prior thing) and got 96%, so now he wants to ship it and I don't. Were looking at the identical data and both of us insist we're the one being truly unbiased and just "letting the data speak," but somehow we land on opposite sides of our own cutoff. It's honestly kind of messing with my head that "assume nothing going in" doesn't pin down a single answer. Is one of us doing something wrong here, or is there just no such thing as a neutral, assumption-free starting point?
