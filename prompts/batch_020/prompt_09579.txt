"Objection nine is the strangest of the nine, because rather than dismissing ESP as pseudoscience, Turing actually grants that the statistical evidence for telepathy is 'overwhelming,' and worries that once you accept it, it becomes hard to resist the other three items on his list — clairvoyance, precognition, and psychokinesis — along with the 'ghosts and bogies' he says come bundled with them. He calls it, in his own words, 'quite a strong' objection, and his first line of defense, that plenty of scientific theories go on working fine in practice despite nominally clashing with ESP, he immediately admits is cold comfort, since thinking is exactly the kind of process where telepathic interference seems most likely to intrude.

The concrete problem is illustrated with a card game: the interrogator asks a witness to name the suit of a card, and a telepathic human witness gets it right about 130 times out of 400 tries, comfortably above the chance rate of 100, while the machine, limited to its random-number generator, stays near chance at around 104 and so gets correctly flagged as the machine — not because it thinks any less, but because it lacks a paranormal channel to information it has no sensory route to. Turing pushes the thought experiment one step further: he notes that if the machine relies on an internal random-number generator to answer such questions, that generator could itself be bent by a sufficiently psychokinetic opposing witness, so that the machine's scores start beating chance for reasons that have nothing to do with thinking — which would only make it harder, not easier, for the interrogator to tell the two apart.

Turing's proposed fix is architectural rather than theoretical: house the parties in a 'telepathy-proof room,' on the model of preventing someone from listening at the wall, and the confound disappears. But because Turing never retracts his claim that the evidence for telepathy is overwhelming, and offers the room only as a practical patch rather than a refutation of ESP itself, this objection is really the one contrary view in Section 6 that he leaves standing rather than defeats — a strikingly candid admission, for a founding document of AI, that the paper's own test might rest on a phenomenon its author believes to be real but cannot explain."
