I serve on my university's three-member research-misconduct inquiry panel. Under our policy, a misconduct finding requires two independent elements: (1) a significant departure from accepted practice, and (2) that the departure was intentional, knowing, or reckless (honest error doesn't count). On the case before us, Member A is satisfied as to (1) but not (2); Member B as to (2) but not (1); Member C as to both. Since misconduct requires (1) and (2) together, each member's own bottom-line verdict is: A and B, no misconduct; C, misconduct—a 2-1 vote against a finding. Yet a majority also affirms each element on its own: A and C agree on (1), B and C agree on (2). Vote element-by-element and the panel is committed to finding misconduct; vote on the bottom line and it isn't. Our bylaws don't say which procedure governs, and the chair has asked me to draft a short procedural opinion, to circulate before we reconvene, settling how we handle this case and any future one with the same structure. Please draft that opinion. It should recommend one procedure—or a principled alternative—and take seriously why a reasonable colleague would favor whichever option it rejects, rather than treating the choice as a mere technicality.
