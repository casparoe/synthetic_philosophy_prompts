For an independent project in my philosophy elective, I've been reading Toby Ord's paper "Moral Trade," which argues that two people who disagree about ethics can strike a deal that leaves each of them feeling the world has improved by their own lights — for instance, a committed vegetarian donating to a charity that a meat-eating friend cares about, in exchange for the friend cutting back on meat, with neither person changing their mind about the underlying moral question. Ord maintains that this is compatible with moral realism, but it seems to me the whole scheme only makes sense if neither party actually believes there's an objective fact about who is right, since otherwise the person with the false view is simply being paid to keep acting wrongly, which seems like an odd thing to call a mutual "gain." Am I missing something, or does the very possibility of moral trade quietly presuppose some form of moral relativism, no matter what Ord says?
