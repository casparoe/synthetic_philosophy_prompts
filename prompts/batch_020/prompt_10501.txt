"Weil argues that a political party is essentially a machine for manufacturing collective passion. On her account a party has three defining features: it generates a collective enthusiasm that overrides individual thought, it pressures each member's mind to fall in line with a whole pre-set index of opinions rather than just the one issue the party originally formed around, and its own indefinite growth becomes its only real purpose, since there's no natural ceiling on how much power a party should want. Because members sign onto a whole platform instead of judging each question on its own merits, party membership corrodes people's capacity for truthful, independent judgment - you stop asking "is this true or just" and start asking "what's my party's line." She's especially worried about extremist, totalitarian parties like the single parties of fascist states, which take this tendency to its extreme; more moderate parties competing within a pluralist system are comparatively safe, since competition between them still leaves room for individual judgment to operate.
