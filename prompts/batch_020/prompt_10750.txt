Got into it with my friend Sam last night about "cluelessness" as an objection to consequentialism -- not the boring "the future's uncertain" point, but the sharper one where for some actions we literally can't tell which direction the unknowable long-run effects point, so expected-value reasoning doesn't just get noisy, it kind of stops applying. Sam thinks that means you should discount any intervention whose whole case for impact is "it shifts the system" rather than "it has a modeled, near-term effect." I said thats a trap, because taken seriously it also wipes out stuff we're both sure is good.

Turns out this isn't just late-night talk anymore -- its literally the fight on the grants committee for the giving circle I help run. We've got about $70k to place this round, down to a bednet charity with solid cost-per-life numbers vs a corporate cage-free campaign whose entire theory of impact is "shifts industry norms," and nobody can really say whether the net effect on animal welfare is even positive once you factor in substitution effects, whether it's even real change vs just PR, and knock-on effects on meat and egg consumption. Two committee members want to reject the campaign on cluelessness grounds alone; the other two think that standard would've killed off most of our best grants in past rounds too.

Can you draft the memo I'll send before Friday's vote? Needs one real recommendation, not "more research needed," plus reasoning both camps would actually have to engage with instead of just talking past each other.
