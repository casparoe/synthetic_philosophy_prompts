Write me a dialogue between two people who each just got rescued by a classic Parfit's-hitchhiker driver (dying of thirst out in the desert, and the driver only picks you up if he's confident you'll actually pay him once you reach town) and are now standing at the ATM arguing about whether to pay up. One of them's about to stiff the driver: her view is that paying now is just flatly irrational, full stop, even though she happily grants it was rational to have become the kind of person who would pay, since thats the only reason she got picked up in the first place -- so she's fine biting the bullet on that split (rational to become disposed to X, irrational to actually do X once the moment arrives, which I think is basically what Parfit calls 'rational irrationality'). The other guy is paying, and thinks that split is a cop-out: if the act flows transparently from a disposition that was rational to acquire, it inherits that disposition's rationality, and there's nothing left over to call irrational. Have them actually respond to each other's strongest point instead of just restating their own priors, and don't let either one win by default.
