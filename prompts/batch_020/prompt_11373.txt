Random philosophy tangent while I'm outlining my new novel. There's a scene where a teleporter malfunctions and instead of moving my protagonist it produces two of her, identical down to the last molecule, and I want them to argue over which one is the "real" one. Digging around for inspiration I found this old Max Black thought experiment about two spheres floating alone in an empty, perfectly symmetric universe, matching in absolutely every property, which he uses to show that the identity of indiscernibles is false — basically, that two things can be totally identical and still count as one and the same object. If that's right it kind of solves my plot problem for free: my two duplicates, being indistinguishable in every property, are technically just one person standing in two seperate bodies, not two people. Does that logic actually check out, and if so, how would you have the characters stumble onto it mid-argument?
