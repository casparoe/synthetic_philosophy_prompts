So last night my friend Alex and I got into it for like two hours after our AI safety reading group about whether "coherence arguments" actually prove anything about future AI systems. His position is the classic one: if your preferences can't be represented as maximizing some expected utility function, you're exploitable, money-pumpable, and anything smart enough to notice that will patch itself up until its coherent - which is basically why we should expect sufficiently advanced AI to end up looking like a ruthless expected-utility-maximizer, dangerous utility function included. I pushed back with this thing I half-remembered about incomplete preferences: that an agent can dodge the classic cyclic-preference money pump just by adopting the policy "never accept an option worse than one I already turned down," and that this works without the agent ever becoming "complete," let alone a fully coherent EU-maximizer. Alex's comeback was that this trick doesn't actually get you out of it, it just relocates the cost onto memory and bookkeeping, and an agent that has to track every option it's ever declined just to stay "incomplete" starts looking suspiciously like one that's smuggled in a utility function through the back door anyway (and it'll get outcompeted by agents that don't bother with all that bookkeeping). We went around in circles on this for a while and never actually landed anywhere, which is annoying because I feel like one of us has to be missing something.

Could you write the rematch as a dialogue between two people hashing this out properly? Set it somewhere fun and concrete, like the two of them standing in front of a fruit stand or vending machine selling three things they both have circular preferences over, so the money-pump move is actually on the table instead of just described in the abstract. One character should argue that coherence pressure is real and that patched-up incompleteness is at best a temporary or illusory escape from it; the other should argue that incomplete preferences plus a sane non-exploitation policy really is a stable way out, and that coherence theorems don't establish what people in AI safety often assume they establish. I want genuine back and forth, not two people delivering monologues past each other - so have the first character press specifically on whether the bookkeeping is just a disguised utility function and whether such an agent gets competed away, and make the second character actually answer that instead of changing the subject, even if the honest answer involves a partial concession somewhere. It doesn't need to end in a tidy resolution, but neither one should come off as the strawman.
