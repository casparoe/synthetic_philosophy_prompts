I sit on my university's IACUC (the commitee that signs off on animal research protocols), and we're stuck on what standard to even use. Half of us want to basically run Nozick's old split from Anarchy, State, and Utopia: weigh animal suffering against expected benefit like a straightforward utilitarian calculus, the way we'd never allow for human subjects, where certain protections just aren't up for trade no matter the payoff. The other half thinks that human/animal line is doing way more work than Nozick's own reasoning for it (something about the capacity to shape a life by your own lights) can actually support, especially once you get to chimps or dolphins. We've got a real protocol up this cycle to make it concrete -- a repeated early-life-stress paradigm in macaques modeling PTSD, with a plausible clinical payoff. Write the memo I'd bring to the committee: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and make sure whatever standard you use is actually defended against the other side's strongest objection.
