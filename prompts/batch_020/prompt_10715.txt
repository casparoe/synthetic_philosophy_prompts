My old friend Renate is retiring after forty years running the gravitational physics group, and I've been roped into giving the toast. Shes spent her whole career as an unrepentant substantivalist -- keeps a battered galvanized bucket on her windowsill as a little shrine to Newton -- and it drives her up the wall when some visiting speaker breezes through the "well Einstein basically proved Mach right, space isnt a thing" line, as if the hole argument and everything since just settled itself over coffee. I'm a retired spectroscopist myself, not a relativist, and only got serious about philosophy of physics after I stopped working, so I don't fully trust my own instincts here. Half the room will be physicists and philosophers of science who'll know exactly what she means when she mutters about the hole argument; the other half is her kids and old university friends who think a bucket is just a bucket. Could you write me a five-minute toast that uses the substantivalism/relationism fight -- ideally with one real joke built around that bucket -- to say something warm and true about her, without repeating the pop-science line she can't stand, and without turning into a lecture that leaves half the room behind?
