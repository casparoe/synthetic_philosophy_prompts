I am a PhD student in cognitive science, and for the past several months I have been trying to decide whether to take a year's leave from my program for an extended stay at a strict Theravada meditation monastery in Southeast Asia, the kind that keeps foreign students silent and cut off from outside contact for months at a time. Two friends who did this a few years ago tell me it changed what they wanted out of life so completely that they cannot really put the change into words; when I press them on what it was actually like, they just say I would have to go through it myself to understand. I have tried to reason about this the way I would about any major decision, weighing what I stand to gain against what I would give up, but I keep getting stuck, because I do not know how to weigh my current goals and relationships against the preferences of whoever I would become, especially if that person would not even miss the things I care about now. Is this simply ordinary indecisiveness on my part, or is there something about choices like this one that makes the usual way of weighing pros and cons break down?
