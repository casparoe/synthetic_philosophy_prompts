A former student of mine, now practicing as a clinical psychologist, recently told me about something that has stayed with me. Over the fifteen years she has worked at her clinic, the intake checklist used for a particular diagnosis has been revised twice, each revision folding in phrasing drawn from how earlier patients had described their own symptoms; meanwhile, newer patients increasingly describe their symptoms in almost exactly that checklist's language, having encountered it online well before any formal assessment, and diagnosis rates have risen steadily across this whole period. One of her colleagues treats this pattern as evidence that clinicians are finally recognizing a genuine condition that used to be missed; another treats it as evidence that the "condition" is largely an artifact of the checklist, propagating through the patient population much as a rumor might. My former student suspects both colleagues are being too hasty, but she cannot quite say why. Which of them, if either, is right?
