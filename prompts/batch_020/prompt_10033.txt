I'm writing a dissertation chapter on the epistemology of peer disagreement, and I keep running into what looks like a self-undermining problem for conciliationist views such as Adam Elga's Equal Weight View. Conciliationism is itself a highly controversial thesis within epistemology, and plenty of philosophers who look like genuine epistemic peers to its proponents reject it in favor of some steadfast view. If conciliationists applied their own principle consistently, wouldn't the sheer extent of this disagreement force them to drastically lower their confidence in conciliationism itself -- to the point that they could no longer rationally hold it with anything like the confidence its defenders actually display? Please spell out this objection as precisely as you can, and then explain the most serious strategies conciliationists have used to defuse it, such as arguing that certain foundational epistemic commitments cannot coherently fall within the view's own scope. Finally, assess whether any of these strategies genuinely escapes the problem, or whether the trouble just resurfaces one level up.
