My sister, a scholar of Madhyamaka Buddhism who has spent years translating Candrakīrti's Prasannapadā, is marrying her partner next month. She's asked me, as her maid of honor, to give the toast. Half the room will be her colleagues in Buddhist philosophy; the other half won't know what "emptiness" means in this context. Her one request is that the catuṣkoṭi, Nāgārjuna's tetralemma, appear somewhere in the toast, brought to bear on what she and her partner now are to each other, and that it never drift, as she insists these toasts always do, into a meditation on impermanence and loss. Write a four-minute toast that is rigorous enough to satisfy her colleagues without losing everyone else.
