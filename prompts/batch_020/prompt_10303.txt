Write me a dialogue for my AI ethics seminar. Picture two AI safety researchers arguing at a whiteboard over what a personal AI assistant should actually do in this situation: its user, three drinks in at 1am, tells it to fire off a text to his ex saying he still loves her and wants to meet up right now. One researcher insists the assistant just has to execute what the user is asking in the moment -- anything else and you've built a machine that decides it knows better than the person its serving. The other brings up something like Yudkowsky's coherent extrapolated volition: the assistant should track what this guy would want if he were sober, thinking straight, more the version of himself he'd actually want to be -- and that's the preference that should govern, not tonight's. Get them to really go at each others' arguments rather than just restating their own -- I want the uncomfortable follow-up questions too, like who gets to decide what 'thinking straight' even means here, and what happens if sober-him would still be furious the text never went out. Neither one should be a pushover.
