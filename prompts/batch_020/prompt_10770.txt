I'm the student representative on my high school's Honor Council, which hears academic-integrity cases with two faculty advisors, and we're deadlocked on a case ahead of Wednesday's hearing. Four seniors submitted a single joint capstone report and, per the syllabus, all four received one shared grade for it. It's since come out that one member fabricated a data table without telling the others, who say they only checked their own sections and had no reason to suspect anything. Our honor code says students are responsible for 'work they submit,' but doesn't address whether submitting one shared document makes everyone answerable for parts they didn't personally write. Half the council argues that choosing to present a single joint product is itself a commitment that makes the whole group responsible for the whole report -- a 'joint commitment,' as one member keeps putting it, borrowing a term from a philosophy elective. The other half insists responsibility should track only who actually knew or intended the fabrication. Please draft the short opinion I'll present: it should recommend specific findings and consequences for each of the four students, and it should take the 'joint commitment' argument seriously rather than treat it as a rhetorical stretch.
