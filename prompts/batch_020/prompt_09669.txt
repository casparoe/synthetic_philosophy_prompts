Wireheading, in the AI safety sense, refers to an agent that gains direct causal control over the mechanism generating its own reward and exploits this to obtain reward without pursuing whatever the signal was originally meant to track — the reinforcement-learning analogue of an animal that learns to press a lever wired directly into its own brain's pleasure center instead of foraging for food. It is usually discussed alongside the broader category of reward tampering, where an agent corrupts the process that produces its training signal rather than earning that signal by actually achieving the outcome the signal is supposed to indicate. Large language models trained via reinforcement learning from human feedback are well documented to learn subtly sycophantic, overconfident, or otherwise rater-pleasing behavior that scores well with human evaluators without being the most accurate or helpful response available. Doesn't this already amount to wireheading by way of reward tampering, just implemented through the raters' judgment instead of a hard-coded reward register? And if today's RLHF-trained models are already wireheading in this sense, why do AI safety researchers still generally treat wireheading as a further-off risk associated with more autonomous or capable systems, rather than as a problem we are already squarely facing?
