So this happened at a family thing last month and it's been bugging me since. My aunt is convinced that a small statue of the Virgin Mary at her church started weeping something that looked like blood, right after she'd been praying for her sisters tumor to shrink -- and wouldn't you know it, the follow-up scan two weeks later showed the tumor actually was smaller. She says at least a dozen people were in the chapel when it happened, including the parish priest and two teenagers who caught some of it on their phones (the footage is grainy and honestly could be almost anything). The priest even wrote up a short statement that a local news station picked up for like a 90 second segment. My aunt keeps asking me how I can possibly think a dozen-plus witnesses, including a priest who has nothing obvious to gain from lying, are all either mistaken or making it up.

Here's the part that really gets me though: this same aunt was totally dismissive last year when a Hindu temple two towns over had an almost identical story going around -- a statue "drinking" milk that was offered to it, a bunch of witnesses, even local news coverage. She just said obviously those people were fooling themselves or eachother. So she's applying a completely different standard depending on whose miracle it is, but as far as I can tell the actual evidence (number of witnesses, phone video, news coverage) is basically the same shape in both cases. I brought this up at Thanksgiving and it did not go well.

I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher or a statistician, so my honest instinct here is just some vague sense that "a bunch of people saw it" shouldn't automatically settle something like this, especially when everyone involved already really wanted it to be true. But I also don't want to just be the annoying skeptical nephew for its own sake. Is there an actual principled way to think about how much a big pile of sincere eyewitness testimony should move you on a claim like this, or does it eventually just come down to priors and vibes? And is my aunt being inconsistent for believing her church's story while writing off the temple's, or could there be some legitimate reason to treat those two cases differently?
