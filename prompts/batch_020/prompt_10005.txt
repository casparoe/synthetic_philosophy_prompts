I help run the methodology side of a smallish forecasting tournament -- basically a scrappy Metaculus-style thing some of us started after getting way too into the superforecasting scene. We've hit a real fight over interpretations of probability, and it's not academic anymore since it affects prize money and the leaderboard. Half the commitee are frequentists who say you can't really assign a meaningful probability to a one-off event like 'will this specific ceasefire hold past June,' since there's no reference class of repeated trials to average over -- sure, you can still compute a Brier score for one question, but you can't say a single 73% call was actually good, just lucky, and real calibration only shows up over hundreds of questions. The other half are Bayesians who think probability just is credence, happy to score and rank every question on its own, 'best call of the year' prize included. Somebody brought up propensity interpretations too and it just stalled everyone out. I lean Bayesian myself but think the frequentists have a real point I can't just dismiss. can you draft the memo I'll bring to committee -- an actual recommendation for how we score and talk about singular, non-repeatable questions, one that takes the reference-class worry seriously instead of waving it away.
