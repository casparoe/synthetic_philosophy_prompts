So my friend Sam and I spent way to much time at dinner last night arguing about Kierkegaard - specifically the stuff in Two Ages / "The Present Age" about the public and the press, and whether it actually says anything about Twitter/social media or if it's just a bitter old man theory. Quick bit of backstory we both already knew going in: this is the guy who, after a sleazy satirical paper called The Corsair praised him, wrote an article basically daring it to attack him instead, ending with the line "the indignity of being immortalized by The Corsair is too much to bear" - and then got mercilessly caricatured in it for years afterward (the crooked back, the too-short trouser legs, all of it). It's only after living through that that he writes the big theory: that "the public" is a phantom that never assembles, never decides, and lets every member disown their own opinion, and that "leveling," driven by exactly that kind of anonymous chatter, flattens real individuality far more effectively than any actual mob ever could.

Sam's take: this is obviously a rationalized grudge, the public/crowd distinction falls apart the second you look at an actual online pile-on (which very much can act, and can ruin someone's career or life), and the whole complaint about anonymity is rich coming from a guy who published half his major works under made-up names like Johannes Climacus and Victor Eremita. My take: the pseudonym thing isn't remotely the same move, since he always stood ready to own it and used it to force the reader to decide for themselves instead of leaning on his authority - basically the opposite of anonymous chatter - and the fact that a modern "public" can occasionaly coordinate into a pile-on doesn't refute the impotence point, it just means a crowd borrowed the public's mask for a night. Neither of us budged, and we ran out of wine before we ran out of arguments.

Could you write out the dialogue we wish we'd actually had? Kierkegaard himself on one side, and on the other a genuinely sharp modern defender of wide-open, anonymous, participatory public discourse - not a strawman techno-optimist, but someone who actually knows the real arguments for why disaggregated, anonymous speech can beat small-town, face-to-face accountability rather than just lose to it. I want Kierkegaard arguing from his actual concepts rather than generic old-man-yells-at-cloud stuff, and I want the modern defender to really press him on the pseudonym point and on whether "real community" can end up just as suffocating as any anonymous public. Something like 800-1000 words is fine, and it doesn't need to end in agreement, but I'd like it to land somewhere sharper than a shrug.
