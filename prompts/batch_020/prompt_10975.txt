So I've been having these nights where I get kind of stuck on how mind-bogglingly big and old the universe is, how the sun's gonna burn out and eventually everything runs down to heat death, and it spirals into me feeling like nothing I do actually matters. A while back I found this old passage by a guy named Frank Ramsey, a Cambridge mathematician/philosopher from the 1920s who died crazy young (like 26), where he says he refuses to feel small next to the universe because his "picture of the world is drawn in perspective" instead of to scale — people take up the whole foreground and the stars shrink down to the size of coins, since stars cant think or love and that matters way more to him than sheer size does. Then he does this second move where he applies the same trick to time: sure the universe will eventually go cold and die, but thats so far off that, like a payment way out in the future, its basically worth nothing today, so he doesn't see why that should bother him now either.

That second move is the part that's been rattling around in my head. If I actually adopted Ramsey's reasoning as my own outlook on life, what are the most important implications, especially of that "discounting the far future" trick? Like if I'm allowed to shrug off the heat death of the universe just because it's so far away, doesn't that same logic also let me shrug off stuff like climate change, or how my kids' kids will have it, since that's not close enough to me either? I want the actual implications of taking this seriously, not just whether the quote feels comforting.
