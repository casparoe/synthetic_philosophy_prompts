The homeowners' association in my subdivision recently voted to close one of our internal streets to through traffic and install speed humps, in response to complaints that outside drivers were using the neighborhood as a shortcut around a congested intersection. The predictable effect is that most of that traffic will now be pushed onto the public street bordering ours, which serves an entirely separate, adjoining neighborhood. Only dues-paying members were entitled to vote on the closure; the residents who will now absorb the displaced traffic had no say, since they belong to no association with any claim on the matter. When I raised this at the meeting, I was told, reasonably enough, that voting rights simply track membership rather than exposure to a decision's effects — that is what it means to be a voluntary association governed by its own bylaws. Everyone else found this a sufficient answer; I did not, though I have had trouble putting my objection into precise terms. Is there a principled basis for confining the vote to association members in a case like this, or does my discomfort point to a genuine problem with how the boundaries of the deciding group were drawn?
