Been down a rabbit hole on evidential cooperation in large worlds lately (Oesterheld's old multiverse-wide superrationality writeup, back when it was still an FRI/CLR paper - might be misremembering the timeline). Theres a specific worry about it I keep running into that I havent seen anyone really argue through properly. The case for ECL needs two things to both hold: (1) your decision procedure is correlated with a bunch of causally disconnected agents who have different values than you, so acting cooperatively is "evidence" they do too, in the classic superrationality/EDT sense, and (2) those agents' values are different enough from yours that there's real surplus from each side shifting toward some compromise utility function. But the most plausible story for why your decision procedure would correlate with some alien civilization's in the first place is that you both converged on similar reasoning - similar epistemics, similar decision theory, maybe similar meta-ethics. And if that's the source of the correlation, isn't it also reason to expect converging values, which guts the gains from trade? So the more correlated an agent is with you (i.e. the more ECL says to weight them), the less there should actually be to gain from cooperating with them.

Can you make the strongest case you can that this is a genuine problem for ECL and not just a surface-level confusion on my part? Feel free to engage with the obvious reply that correlation can live at the level of decision-procedure/meta-reasoning while terminal values stay independent, and say why that reply does or doesn't fully work.
