I got elected to my town's select board last month, and last week the board voted me in as chair for the year -- first woman to chair it in the town's history, no pressure. My first meeting running the show is next Tuesday, and I'm supposed to give a few minutes of opening remarks before we dive into the actual agenda. My book club has been working through Spinoza's Theological-Political Treatise all spring, and I got kind of obsessed with his argument for why democracy is the most "natural" form of government -- basically that it's the one regime where people don't really hand their natural right over to somebody else, they just pool it, so everyone stays about as equal and free as they were before any government existed. I want that to actually be the backbone of my remarks, not just a quote dropped in. Only catch: I also know that in the unfinished Tractatus Politicus, the very last thing he was writing before he died was the chapter on democracy, and it breaks off with him mid-argument that women shouldn't get full political rights in it -- such a betrayal of his own logic its almost funny, except it's not, since it's me up there. I don't want to pretend that bit doesn't exist, but I also don't want four minutes of remarks turning into a Spinoza seminar or a takedown -- most of the room has never heard of the guy and just wants to get through the zoning agenda. Can you draft me something short (3-4 minutes spoken) that uses his real argument as the backbone, is honest about that wrinkle without dwelling on it, and still lands as a warm, genuine opening for a room that's almost entirely non-philosophers, plus like two people (including my book club friend) who will absolutely clock the reference?
