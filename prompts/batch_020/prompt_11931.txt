I write about science and ideas for a living, which means my whole career runs on a habit I've never examined very closely: taking experts at their word. I have never run a particle-physics experiment or sequenced a genome, yet I'll happily report that the universe is 13.8 billion years old, or that a given mutation raises someone's cancer risk, because researchers I trust said so and nobody I trust has said otherwise. Even the fact-checking process at the outlets I write for mostly means phoning a second expert, not re-deriving the result myself. This arrangement has started to nag at me because of an ongoing argument with a close friend, a data scientist who has become almost evangelical about what she calls "epistemic self-reliance." It started, oddly enough, with a family story: her grandmother always said she crossed a certain border alone as a teenager, fleeing on foot, but a cousin's account of the same event, which surfaced only after the grandmother died, disagreed with the grandmother's on several key details, and nobody left alive could settle which version was accurate. Since then my friend has adopted a rule that she won't count anything as known unless she could, in principle, verify it herself from first-hand evidence; she has stopped saying she "knows" the moon landing happened, or that a given historical atrocity unfolded the way the textbooks describe, on the grounds that all she actually has, in either case, is somebody else's word, passed along at several removes.

Part of me admires the rigor, but the position also seems to prove too much: someone who genuinely refused to believe anything she hadn't personally checked would end up knowing almost nothing at all, including most of what persuaded her that checking mattered in the first place, since presumably even that lesson was taught to her by someone else. Yet it also seems obviously wrong to say all secondhand claims deserve equal trust -- I don't believe every press release that lands in my inbox just because someone signed their name to it. I would like help figuring out where the real, defensible line falls between my friend's position and my own comparatively unreflective habit of taking people's word for things, and what should determine, in a given case, how much independent corroboration a claim needs before it is reasonable to accept it. Is she onto a discipline the rest of us are too intellectually lazy to practice, or has she reasoned her way into a position that isn't even coherent?
