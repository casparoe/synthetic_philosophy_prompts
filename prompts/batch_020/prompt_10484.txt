Turns out the whole thing technically started with a guy named Peirce before James ran with it, so I tried reading him directly too, but his writing is honestly brutal, so most of what I know about his side comes from secondhand stuff like blog posts and a couple of youtube videos rather than the actual texts.

Anyway, my friend asked me to explain the difference between the two of them since I'd been going on about it so much, so I wrote up this short thing for her. Before I send it over though I want a sanity check, because I've got this nagging feeling I'm smoothing over something important, especially with Peirce since I never read him firsthand. Here's what I've got:

"Pragmatism started with Peirce, who said an idea's meaning basically comes down to whatever practical difference it would make if it were true. He carried this over to truth itself too: a belief is true if it's the one that keeps working out for you the more you act on it. He dressed this up in fancier language by saying truth is whatever opinion the whole community of investigators would eventually settle on if they kept at it forever, which once you strip away the jargon is really just consensus by another name. James took that same basic idea and made it famous, except he put it in more personal terms - for him, an idea is true if believing it is useful and satisfying to you, if it has good "cash value" for your life. So really the two of them were saying the same thing, truth equals what works, Peirce was just the drier first draft and James was the popularizer who said it better and with more feeling, and my understanding is Peirce was glad to have James spread it to a wider audience."

Does that basically check out, or am I flattening something important / getting it backwards somewhere?
