Problem: Prove that every proper ideal of a commutative ring R with 1≠0 is contained in some maximal ideal of R. State Zorn's Lemma precisely and verify that its hypotheses hold in this case.

Submitted answer:
Let I be a proper ideal of R. Let P be the set of all proper ideals of R containing I, partially ordered by J1 ≤ J2 iff J1 ⊆ J2. P is nonempty since I ∈ P.

Zorn's Lemma: if (P, ≤) is a nonempty poset in which every chain has a least upper bound in P, then P has a maximal element.

Let C be a chain in P and let J be the union of all K in C. Since C is totally ordered, J is an ideal: given a, b ∈ J, both lie in some common K ∈ C (whichever of the two containing ideals is bigger), so a − b ∈ K ⊆ J, and rK ⊆ K ⊆ J for any r ∈ R. Also 1 ∉ J, since every K ∈ C is proper (so 1 ∉ K), and 1 ∈ J would force 1 ∈ K for some K. So J is a proper ideal containing I, i.e. J ∈ P, and J is an upper bound for C.

By Zorn's Lemma, P has a maximal element M. If M ⊆ K ⊊ R for some ideal K, then K ∈ P (its proper and contains I), so K = M by maximality of M in P. Hence M is a maximal ideal of R containing I, and its the unique maximal ideal containing I.
