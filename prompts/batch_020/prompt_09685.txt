I'm drafting a novel whose central character is Deaf but wasn't identified as such until age seven, because her hearing family didn't realize she needed sign language; she therefore grew up with no accessible language at all, spoken or signed. For the chapters set before that discovery, narrated tightly from her point of view, I've been rendering her inner life as essentially pre-conceptual—rich in sensation and feeling, but without real categories, propositions, or inference—on the theory that structured thought only comes online once language does, which is what I took Fodor's language of thought hypothesis to establish. Before I commit to another eighty pages written that way, how sparse can I actually make her interior monologue before it stops reading as psychologically informed and starts reading as just thin characterization?
