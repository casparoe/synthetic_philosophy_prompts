So this happened at a rationalist-adjacent meetup I go to and I still can't stop chewing on it. One of the regulars, Eli, told a story about some contested empirical claim he'd gone down a rabbit hole on the night before -- stayed up till 3am reading primary sources (that week it was some specific claim about a supplement-drug interaction, the kind of thing that blows up into a 300-comment ACX thread, but honestly the exact topic doesn't matter), and landed at like 90% confidence in his answer. A friend of his who takes forecasting seriously heard the story and just said, flatly, "people who reach 90% confidence after one solo all-nighter on one topic are wrong most of the time, that's just the base rate for that pattern, doesn't matter what the topic is," and left it at that. Eli agreed that was fair and said he'd report his number as more like 60% going forward -- but he also said that walking through his own reasoning step by step still feels completely airtight to him, and that he keeps catching himself arguing for the 90%-answer whenever it comes up in conversation, he just mentally appends the discount afterward. He didnt see a problem with that combination.

Two other people there, Kavita and Ben, ended up going a few rounds over whether that combination is even coherent:
(1) Kavita said this is a clean case of epistemic akrasia -- you can't say "the argument is airtight" and "it's only 60% likely" in the same breath when the higher-order evidence is specifically about your reliability at judging arguments like this one. The object-level assessment and the meta-level evidence aren't independent inputs, so "the reasoning still feels solid, I've just discounted the number" is really just refusing to update where it actually counts.
(2) Ben said the forecaster's remark is just peer-disagreement-flavored evidence, and ordinary peer disagreement doesn't require torching your inside view down to nothing, only weighing it against an outside view. There's nothing incoherent about an argument still striking you as valid, step by step, while your all-things-considered credence sits lower once a base rate about people-like-you gets folded in.
(3) Kavita replied that's exactly the move these cases are supposed to rule out: if your going to take the meta-evidence seriously, you can't also keep leaning on the very faculty it indicts and call that humility -- that's just using the discredited process to certify itself, which is having it both ways.
(4) Ben said full deference would make Eli's object-level reasoning permanently idle -- he'd never again be allowed to trust a night of real research, no matter how good it actually was, and that seems like too high a price. At some point the actual first-order reasons have to be let back in the room.
