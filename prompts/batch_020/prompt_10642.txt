Okay so this has been bugging me for like two weeks now and it started with something kind of dumb. My roommate and I were hauling a couch up to our new place and at one point she just stopped pushing for a sec because her phone buzzed, and I got weirdly annoyed at her, like she owed it to me to keep pushing right then, not just that I was hoping she would. That sent me down a whole rabbit hole about what actually makes two people doing something count as a joint action instead of just two people each doing their own thing that happens to line up. Like if we're both separately intending "I want us to get this couch upstairs," and we each know the other one wants that too, and we're both adjusting our own pushing/steering around what the other is doing, is that basically the whole story? Or does really being in it together require some extra ingredient, like a commitment we actually make to each other, which is the thing that gives me standing to be annoyed rather than just disappointed, the way I'd be disappointed if a vending machine ate my dollar.

I mentioned this to a friend who did some philosophy in undergrad and apparently there's a real disagreement about exactly this. Something like: Bratman thinks shared intention basically comes down to a bunch of individual intentions and beliefs about each other stacked up the right way (plans that mesh, common knowledge, all that), while Gilbert thinks you need something more, an actual joint commitment the two of you create together, which is the thing that generates real obligations to each other and the right to call the other person out for bailing. Could you write me a dialogue between one philosopher who holds the Bratman-ish view and one who holds the Gilbert-ish view, using the couch (or any similarly mundane joint task, doesn't have to be that exact one) as the example they keep coming back to? I don't want either of them to be the obvious loser here, I want to see the strongest version of both sides actually going back and forth and responding to each others real points, especially on this obligation/entitled-to-be-annoyed thing, since thats the part I can't shake.
