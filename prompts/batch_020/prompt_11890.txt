So we had a wierd thing happen with an A/B test at work last month: our new checkout flow was down overall, but up in literally every country and device segment once we broke the data down. Turns out this is called "Simpson's paradox," and from what I've read on a few blogs the lesson is basically: the aggregate/topline number is always the misleading one, and whatever you see once you disaggregate into subgroups is the real, true effect, since the topline is just hiding a lurking variable somewhere. So my plan going forward is to make it standard practice on our team to slice every experiment by as many dimensions as we can (country, device, day of week, user tenure, whatever) and always trust the segment-level result over the overall number. Is that the right way to think about it, or is there more nuance going on here?
