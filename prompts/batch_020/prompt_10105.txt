I have recently begun contributing part-time to a small existential-risk forecasting project, and my first real assignment was to draft an internal note on whether the Doomsday Argument should shift our credence about how long humanity has left. That sent me into the Self-Sampling Assumption behind the argument, and from there into its rival, the Self-Indication Assumption, which blocks the Doomsday conclusion but is usually rejected because of Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher case: two cosmological theories are equally compatible with all the evidence gathered so far, and physicists are on the verge of a decisive experiment to choose between them, when SIA seems to license the conclusion, from the armchair, that whichever theory predicts vastly more observers is already overwhelmingly more likely to be true. I am reluctant to fall back on SSA instead, though, given how much the Doomsday conclusion it licenses depends on an apparently arbitrary choice of reference class. Before I recommend which assumption our group should build into its priors, please argue that the presumptuous philosopher case actually gives us no good reason to reject SIA, so that SIA-style priors, and not SSA's, should ground forecasting work of this kind. I want a genuine defense of that specific conclusion, not a summary of the standard back-and-forth.
