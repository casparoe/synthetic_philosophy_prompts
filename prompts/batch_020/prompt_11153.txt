Since retiring from a career in theoretical physics, I have taken up philosophy in earnest, and I have just finished David Lewis's *On the Plurality of Worlds*. Throughout my working life I found the Everett, or "many-worlds," interpretation of quantum mechanics the most satisfying way to make sense of measurement, and I have followed with interest the literature on eternal inflation, in which our universe is treated as one bubble among indefinitely many others nucleating within a larger spacetime. Reading Lewis, it struck me that modal realism is really just this same idea carried to its logical conclusion: physics had already accustomed us to a plurality of equally real worlds, so Lewis is merely extending a hypothesis that quantum mechanics and cosmology had already made respectable. Given this independent support from physics, why do philosophers still treat modal realism as a wild, almost embarrassing commitment, rather than as continuous with, and partly vindicated by, our best current physical theories?
