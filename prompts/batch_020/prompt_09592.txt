"Dawkins' gene's-eye view holds that the gene, not the organism, is the fundamental unit of selection, since genes are the things that actually get copied across generations — so evolution is best described as competition between alleles for representation in the next generation. On this view, sterile workers are explained through kin selection: because haplodiploidy means full sisters share 3/4 of their genes (vs. 1/2 with their own offspring), a worker increases her genetic representation more by helping her mother produce more sisters than by reproducing herself. Dawkins formalized this logic in his famous inequality rB > C, where r is relatedness, B is benefit to the recipient, and C is cost to the actor — if relatedness-discounted benefit exceeds cost, the 'altruistic' gene spreads even as the individual carrying it reproduces less.

Sober and Wilson, on the other hand, treat the colony itself as a vehicle (even a kind of organism), with selection acting directly at the colony level: colonies with better cooperation out-reproduce colonies with worse cooperation, the same way fitter individuals out-reproduce less fit ones.
