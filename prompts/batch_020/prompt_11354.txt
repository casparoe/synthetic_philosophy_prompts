Over the last couple months I've gotten way more serious about sports betting (proper bankroll, tracking closing line value, all of it), and my buddy who trades options for a living keeps insisting that Kelly staking isn't just a good method, its basically THE mathematically correct one, and flat betting or gut-feel sizing is just leaving money on the table. Went down a rabbit hole reading about this and found out theres a genuinely heated old fight between the Kelly crowd and mainstream economists over that exact claim -- apparently Samuelson was so annoyed by it that he once wrote an entire paper attacking "maximize expected log wealth" using only one-syllable words, arguing it's only the right rule if you happen to have log utility, and that the standard "but it wins almost surely given enough bets" defense is basically a sleight of hand. But Thorp and the other big Kelly guys insist you don't need to assume any utility function at all, since the growth-rate optimality is a fact about probability-1 outcomes, not about anyone's preferences. Except then I noticed actual practitioners like Thorp bet half or quarter Kelly, never full, which sounds like a quiet admission that "optimal in the limit" isn't the same as "the right amount to risk." Can you help me pin down the actual crux here -- is the "you don't need a utility function" defense of Kelly betting a real free lunch, or does it just smuggle in a value judgement of its own (like only caring about far-future outcomes, or shrugging off how ugly the bad paths look along the way)? And is fractional Kelly in practice basically a quiet concession that Samuelson had a point?
