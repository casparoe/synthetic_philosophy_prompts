So I've been reading up on Otto Neurath for a piece and somethings been bugging me. He's the guy everyone cites for the "boat" line -- you can't dry-dock the ship of science and rebuild it from a blank slate, your stuck patching planks while still at sea, no neutral bedrock, everything's revisable. Classic anti-foundationalist stuff. But this same guy spent like two decades of his career building Isotype, the pictogram system from his Vienna museum days with the artist Gerd Arntz -- the one where you show "twice as many workers" by drawing two identical little worker icons side by side instead of one bigger worker. And the whole sales pitch for Isotype was that it'd be this internationally legible, basically neutral way to hand people facts, cutting across language and education level. That seems to assume the opposite of the boat thing though -- like there IS a stable, transparent, roughly theory-free way to just display "the facts" to anyone, anywhere. Am I crazy, or is there a real tension between Neurath-the-epistemologist and Neurath-the-picture-language-guy here? Give me the strongest case you can that these two halves of his thinking don't actually fit together.
