Random question that's been bugging me while I research a character. I'm writing a lapsed-seminarian type for my new book, and I went down a rabbit hole reading the actual letters between Augustine and Jerome about that bit in Galatians where Peter dodges eating with Gentiles and Paul calls him out for it. Jerome's theory was that Paul's rebuke was basically staged, a pious fiction for the Judaizers benefit, and Augustine gets really worked up about it. Theres this bit of his argument I can't quite unpack:

"For if you once admit into such a high sanctuary of authority one false statement as made in the way of duty, there will not be left a single sentence of those books which, if appearing to any one difficult in practice or hard to believe, may not by the same fatal rule be explained away, as a statement in which, intentionally, and under a sense of duty, the author declared what was not true."

Is that just a slippery-slope move, or is there tighter logic underneath it? And how does it connect to Augustine's harder line elsewhere that even an "officious" lie (one told to help someone) is still a real, damnable lie? Want my character to actually be able to defend this position instead of just sounding like he's repeating a talking point.
