I spent almost forty years doing condensed matter physics before I retired a couple of years back, and if I'm honest the only reason I got into philosophy at all is that a friend at a dinner party told me David Lewis's "possible worlds" were basically the philosopher's version of the many-worlds arguments I used to have back in grad school. That's not quite right, I've since learned, but it was definately close enough to hook me, and I've now spent a good chunk of this past month with Lewis's Plurality of Worlds plus some Kripke on the side, trying to get straight on the actual difference between counterpart theory and transworld identity. I've got the headline version down, I think: Lewis says no single thing is ever literally part of two worlds, you've just got counterparts linked by resemblance, whereas Kripke's happy to say it's literally you, numerically the very same guy, who shows up de re in some other world.

Two finer points are still nagging at me though. First, there's the so-called problem of accidental intrinsics: if I'm sitting down in the actual world and my counterpart over in some other world happens to be standing up, it seems off to say my sitting is just a relational fact involving a counterpart, rather than a plain old intrinsic property of me. Second, since the counterpart relation runs on similarity, and similarity famously depends on which respects you care about, it looks like I could have several different, even incompatible, counterparts in one and the same world depending on which resemblances get weighted -- so "I might've ended up a chemist" and "I might've ended up in prison" could both come out true relative to different counterpart relations, without anything actually being indeterminate about me.

None of this has produced a single decent joke yet, despite my conviction that it's extremely joke-shaped material, and my old reading group (mostly other retired-scientist types who have zero patience for cleverness that dosent pay off) have started ribbing me about it. So here's what I'm asking: write me an actual joke, with a real setup and a punchline, where the humor genuinely turns on the difference between counterpart theory and transworld identity -- ideally hanging off one of the two wrinkles above, the accidental-intrinsics business or the multiple-incompatible-counterparts business, rather than just being a generic riff on "possible worlds" that anyone who sat through one modal logic lecture could've come up with.
