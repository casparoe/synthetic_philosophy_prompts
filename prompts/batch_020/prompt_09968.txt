My friend Theo is getting married in a couple weeks and asked me to write his best man speech, which is already a lot of pressure, except Theo used to play poker professionally before he became a quant, and he's spent basically his whole adult life organizing decisions around the Kelly criterion -- you size every bet as a fraction of your bankroll based on your edge, and the entire point is you never go "all in," because over a long enough run the guy who occasionally shoves everything busts eventually, no matter how good his edge is. It's not about the expected value of any single bet, its about the growth rate of your wealth over time, which he insists is a genuinely different thing (he's tried to explain the ergodicity stuff to me, something about time-averages vs. averaging across all the possible worlds where the bet could've gone differently, and I mostly just nod). I've also been quietly stealing his mannerisms for a side character in the novel I'm drafting, so if I botch this speech I suspect he'll never let me pick his brain again.

Here's the annoying part. Half the room is going to be poker and finance people who will absolutely clock it if I get the logic wrong, and the other half is our families, who think "bankroll" is a type of bread. And Theo pulled me aside last week, dead serious for once, and said he'd genuinely hate it if the speech leaned on the "love is the one bet you go all-in on" cliche, because that's the exact opposite of how he thinks about this -- he says the reason he's confident about marrying her has more to do with why he never once shoved his whole stack even on great hands, and I could tell it actually mattered to him, wasn't just a bit.

So could you write me the speech? Five minutes, funny enough that nobody's uncle checks his phone, but I want it built on the real logic of Kelly betting and this "geometric" way of thinking about rationality, so the "all-in" joke lands backwards -- so it actually argues, correctly, why a good marriage looks more like disciplined position-sizing over a whole lifetime than like pushing your chips in on one hand. Bonus points, only if it actually fits without derailing things, for working in that unhinged fact he loves to bring up about some famous economist supposedly writing an entire paper trashing the Kelly crowd using nothing but one-syllable words.
