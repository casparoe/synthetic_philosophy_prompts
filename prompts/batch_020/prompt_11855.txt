In my seminar on decision theory and AI safety, we spent last week on coherence arguments for expected utility maximization, and two classmates picked the debate back up over coffee afterward without settling anything. I was just listening, so I'd like an outside opinion on who had the better of it at each step.

(1) Naomi opened by arguing that any agent whose preferences can't be represented by a utility function is exploitable via a money pump, so a sufficiently capable AI that can detect and correct its own flaws will eventually converge on being an expected-utility maximizer -- which is exactly the kind of relentless, goal-directed behavior that makes misaligned AI dangerous.

(2) Callum objected that this skips over completeness. An agent with merely incomplete preferences -- one that doesn't rank every pair of outcomes against each other -- isn't forced into a money pump at all, since it can simply refuse any trade that doesn't strictly improve on whatever it currently has. Declining to trade away from a status quo unless you're sure the alternative is better isn't incoherence; it's just caution, and it costs the agent nothing.

(3) Naomi replied that this is too easy a way out. If you fix that "refuse unless it strictly improves" policy across every situation the agent could face, the whole policy can itself be redescribed as maximizing a utility function that assigns top value to following the policy -- so Callum's cautious agent turns out to be a hidden expected-utility maximizer after all, just over a stranger domain than outcomes.

(4) Callum answered that this move proves too much: if any fixed policy whatsoever can be redescribed after the fact as maximizing some utility function, then every possible agent -- a thermostat, even a rock that never does anything -- counts as an expected-utility maximizer, which empties the original claim of content. For coherence arguments to say anything specific about why advanced AI will be dangerously goal-directed, "utility function" has to be restricted to something like preferences over world-states rather than over which policy was in fact followed -- and once that restriction is in place, his incomplete agent from (2) is a counterexample again.
