So I've been reporting on the aftermath of a satellite that ended up in the wrong orbit last year, and one detail from the failure review keeps bugging me (no pun intended). Two subcontractors each shipped a guidance-software bug, completely independent of each other, and investigators confirmed that either bug on its own would've been enough to send the satellite off course. Now each subcontractor's internal report says almost word-for-word the same thing: 'our bug did not cause the failure, since the satellite would have ended up off course anyway because of the other team's bug.' Each statement seems fine on its own, but if you accept both of them then apparently nobody caused the failure, which seems nuts given that if neither bug had existed the satellite would of reached the right orbit just fine. Is there a principled way to say both bugs actually were causes of the failure? And if so, what exactly is wrong with each team's individual defense?
