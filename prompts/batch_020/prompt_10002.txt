In my formal epistemology seminar, we've been working through Richard Jeffrey's generalization of Bayesian conditionalization to cases where the evidence itself is uncertain. Since Jeffrey's rule is supposed to reduce to ordinary strict conditionalization whenever the new probability of the evidence proposition reaches 1, and since strict conditionalization on a sequence of certain observations always yields the same posterior no matter what order the observations arrive in, it seems to me that the same order-independence has to carry over to successive Jeffrey updates — otherwise the rule would hardly deserve to be called a generalization of Bayesian updating. When I worked through a two-step example for this week's problem set, though, updating on two different uncertain observations in reverse order left me with a noticeably different final distribution, which I took to mean I had mishandled the rigidity condition somewhere. Could you go over the reasoning with me and confirm that Jeffrey conditionalization must be order-independent in the way ordinary conditionalization is?
