On my own time I've been reading about the Condorcet Jury Theorem: the result that a majority vote among independent judges who are each just somewhat more likely right than wrong becomes more reliable as their numbers increase. I sit on the review panel for a small neighborhood mutual aid fund, where three volunteers, drawn from a rotating pool of about a dozen, discuss each emergency-grant case aloud in a fixed order (newest member last) before voting yes or no by show of hands. A board member now wants every case reviewed by seven or nine people instead of three, reasoning that more reviewers can only mean fewer mistakes -- but it strikes me that our fixed speaking order and small, like-minded pool may already violate the independence that reasoning depends on, so more voters could buy us confidence rather than accuracy. Please draft the short memo I'd bring to next month's board meeting: it should recommend concretely whether and how to change the panel's size or its process, and it should take the "more reviewers can only help" view as seriously as it deserves rather than simply dismiss it.
