My older sister Priya is getting married in a few weeks and she asked me to give one of the toasts at the reception, which is stressing me out because I'm sixteen and apparently half the guest list is her and her fiance Daniel's philosophy grad program, so those people will 100% notice if I say something dumb. Over Thanksgiving Daniel spent like twenty minutes explaining David Lewis's book on convention to me (the philosopher, not the Narnia guy), and it's been stuck in my head since — the idea that a convention is basically whatever solution to a recurring coordination problem happens to stick, just because everyone expects everyone else to keep doing it and everyone knows that everyone knows that, even though some other solution would've worked exactly as well if we'd all landed on it instead. He used this example about how if a phone call gets cut off, the person who originally called is just supposed to be the one to call back — not because thats the objectively correct thing to do, but because that's the equilibrium we all silently coordinated on.

Priya and Daniel have this running joke where they say they don't believe in soulmates, they believe in "coordination equilibria," and they've specifically told me not to get mystical about fate or destiny in my toast. So here's what I actually need: can you write me a toast, around three minutes long, that takes that bit seriously instead of just using it as a punchline — something that actually uses the common-knowledge, arbitrary-but-self-sustaining idea of convention to say something real and moving about why their relationship works, in a way that lands for the theorem-proving crowd at their table without leaving my grandma totally lost.
