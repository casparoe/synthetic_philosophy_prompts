I spent my career as an observational astronomer, and since retiring I've stayed on my former institute's time-allocation committee while finally taking up philosophy in earnest. A case before us turns, I think, on Aumann's agreement theorem. Two senior reviewers, both careful Bayesians, examined the same dataset behind a proposal to follow up a claimed anomalous signal, spent a day walking each other through their reasoning, and still returned posteriors of 0.8 and 0.2 that the signal is real -- which, as I understand the theorem, shouldn't be possible once those posteriors are common knowledge to one another. One committee member wants to treat the standoff as proof that one reviewer is reasoning badly and should be overruled; another insists the two were never entitled to a common prior to begin with, since their subfields treat this class of claim so differently. We allocate the contested block of time in three weeks. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate beforehand: it should recommend a concrete course of action, with a timeline for any further steps before the vote, and it should engage the second view on its merits rather than dismiss it as special pleading.
