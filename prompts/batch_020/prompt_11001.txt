A few months ago a forum argument about whether it is fair to blame a shy person for staying quiet during a crisis sent me down a rabbit hole I still have not climbed out of, even though I have never taken a philosophy class in my life. It started with the compatibilist idea that "you could have done otherwise" just means "you would have done otherwise, if you had chosen to," which sounded reasonable to me until I learned that this style of conditional analysis breaks down even for something as ordinary as calling a glass "fragile." Apparently there are two distinct ways the simple test of "would it manifest if triggered" can be fooled. In a finkish case, the very trigger that would normally set off the manifestation also causes the underlying disposition to switch on or off at the last instant, so that, in the standard illustration, a wire that is genuinely dead can be hooked up to a device that makes it live for exactly as long as someone is touching it, tricking the counterfactual test into calling the wire "live" when it really is not. In a masked (sometimes "antidoted") case, nothing happens to the disposition itself; it stays fully in place the whole time, but something separate steps in between the trigger and the manifestation, the way a fragile glass wrapped in enough padding will survive a fall without ceasing to be fragile.

What I cannot find spelled out anywhere is what these two phenomena would look like for a personality trait rather than a wire or a glass, even though that seems like the version that should actually bear on the free-will question I started with. Could you invent one original scenario in which a person's disposition toward something like courage, honesty, or a short temper is finkish in the strict sense described above, and a separate scenario in which a person's disposition toward some trait is merely masked, making sure neither one is just the wire or the glass with new names painted on? For each, spell out precisely which feature makes it a fink rather than a mask, or vice versa, since that is exactly where I keep losing the thread. Then, so that I can check whether I have actually absorbed the distinction rather than just nodding along, give me one further scenario involving a character trait that looks at first as though it must be a fink or a mask but is in fact neither, and explain what is really going on in it instead. Finally, I would be curious to hear your take on which of the two phenomena, finks or masks, would actually cause a genuine problem for the "could have done otherwise" analysis I started with, and which one that analysis can comfortably absorb.
