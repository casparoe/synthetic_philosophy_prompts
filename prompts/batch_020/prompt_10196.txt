I've been mulling over something that came up in my writing group, and I'd value an outside perspective, since everyone else involved has a stake in the answer. Two of us — a close friend, M., and I — turned out, quite independently, to have written novels built around the same formal gamble: a narrator who spends nearly the whole book earning the reader's trust, only for a late chapter to reveal that she has been lying, or lying to herself, throughout, so that everything before it has to be re-read in a different light. Neither of us knew the other was attempting this until we traded manuscripts. My book happened to go out first; an influential critic called the ending "a devastating, fully earned recontextualization," and the whole gambit got talked about as evidence of nerve and control. M.'s book, finished only weeks later and if anything more carefully seeded, lost its original editor to an unrelated restructuring at the press, was eventually self-published, and drew exactly one review, which dismissed the identical device as "a bait-and-switch that insults the reader." M. isn't bothered so much by the difference in sales as by the fact that people now discuss her choice as a lapse in judgment, and mine as a mark of skill, when we made the same decision, for the same reasons, with the same information, at the same time.

I keep telling her that this is just how publishing works, but that doesn't really settle my own unease. Whatever separated our receptions afterward — which editor was on duty that month, which critic happened to pick up the book, what else readers had recently read — was not up to either of us at the point where the actual authorial decision was made. So I'm not sure what to make of the fact that people now praise or fault us for that decision, rather than for whatever they can trace to that later, luck-dependent divergence. Is there some principled sense in which one of us really does deserve more credit or blame for the choice itself, or is the whole business of calling one version "bold" and the other "self-indulgent" just luck wearing the costume of judgment? I'd like your honest, considered view on whether my discomfort here tracks something real, and if it does, how you'd suggest I put it to M. in a way that might actually help her see the gap in reception as something other than a verdict on her as a writer.
