Ok so this has been living in my head since I reread Turing's 1950 "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" a few weeks back. Near the end, going through objections to the imitation game, he gets to "the argument from extrasensory perception" (he even lists the four flavors worth taking seriously -- telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, psychokinesis) and just... accepts it. He says the statistical case for telepathy specifically is basically overwhelming and he's reluctantly forced to believe it, then seriously proposes running the whole test in what he literally calls a "telepathy-proof room" so ESP can't contaminate the results. No extraordinary-claims-extraordinary-evidence moment, no flagging it as a wild prior, nothing -- the guy whos normally the poster child for rigor just folds.

I want a short story built around that specific bit of cognitive dissonance, not a generic "what if psychic powers are real" premise. Give me a character (human, machine, whatever) who has to design a test for telling minds apart in a world where the statistical case for ESP really is as strong as Turing feared, and who has to sit with same discomfort he clearly felt. Don't make the twist "and then it got debunked" -- stay inside that weird epistemic spot the whole way through. ~1500 words, any genre that fits.
