For the past year I have been teaching myself epistemology through the Stanford Encyclopedia and whatever journal articles I can track down, and I have come to think that Rawls's reflective equilibrium and van Fraassen's Reflection Principle are really the same procedure described in two different vocabularies: both instruct you to keep adjusting your beliefs against themselves — principles against particular judgments in one case, credences against anticipated future credences in the other — until everything settles into a stable, coherent whole. If that is correct, the standard complaint against reflective equilibrium, that mere internal coherence can never certify that one has latched onto the truth, should apply with equal force to van Fraassen's principle. Why, then, do formal epistemologists not treat this as the central objection to Reflection, in the way that critics of Rawls treat it as the central objection to his method?
