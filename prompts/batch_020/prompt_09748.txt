Since virtually every theory science has produced, including Einstein's, is presumably false in some respect, doesn't the Miller–Tichý result mean philosophers had to abandon all "closer to the truth" talk and settle for saying only that later theories predict better? If so, I'm puzzled why philosophers of science still routinely call later theories more truthlike than the ones they replaced. Is that just an unexamined habit of speech, or is there a real answer to Miller and Tichý that I'm missing?
