I'm the token philosopher on the committee that oversees my university's 'Rapid Response' press line, where reporters get a quote from whichever faculty expert is on call and it goes out under the university's name within the hour, before anyone can review it. During last spring's meningitis scare, our on-call microbiologist told reporters the outbreak strain was one the standard vaccine covers - the best reading of the genomic data every lab had at that point - and a week later a state lab found out that it wasn't, by which point it was too late for some people to get the newer vaccine instead. Half the committee wants to reprimand her and adopt a rule that Rapid Response experts may only assert things that turn out to be true, full stop; the other half says that standard can't be met in real time and she's blameless because she beleived it for perfectly good reasons at the time. Draft the short decision memo I need before Monday's meeting - one concrete recommendation with a timeline for her case, and one for the policy going forward - and make sure the side you don't land on gets a fair hearing rather than a strawman.
