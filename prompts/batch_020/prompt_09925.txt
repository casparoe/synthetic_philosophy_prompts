I'm taking a seminar on philosophy of religion this term, and after our session on Gödel's modal ontological argument, two classmates got into an extended disagreement while the rest of us just listened. Nora argued that the proof is undone by the modal collapse problem: because Gödel's axioms stipulate that exactly one of any property and its negation is positive, and that a God-like being has all and only positive properties as essential features, adding the further axiom that being God-like is itself positive lets you derive that whatever happens to be true is true of necessity, leaving no room for genuine contingency; she treats this as a reductio of the axiom set rather than a cost worth accepting. Theo answered that this was already handled: Anderson's 1990 emendation rewrites exactly the definitions that let Sobel's collapse argument go through, loosening how a God-like being's essence relates to its positive properties, so that a repaired version of the proof survives without entailing collapse. Nora replied that the repair isn't free: once the link between God-likeness and its properties is loosened enough to block collapse, it's no longer clear that "being God-like" is what the positive-properties axiom was ever meant to track, so Theo has rescued the theorem by hollowing out the intuition that made the axioms attractive in the first place. Theo's rejoinder was that collapse is only scandalous if you assume, independently of any theological commitment, that necessitarianism has to be false, and that a theist already committed to strong providence, in the Leibnizian mold where God necessarily creates the best of all possible worlds, has independent reason not to be troubled by a world in which every truth flows necessarily from God's nature.

Could you go through this move by move, saying who has the stronger position at each stage, and note anything significant that both of them are overlooking?
