As a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or cognitive science, I would like to get past the usual "stochastic parrots" talking points about whether large language models really understand anything. I recently came across a distinction that cognitive scientists, including Kyle Mahowald and Evelina Fedorenko, draw between "formal linguistic competence" (fluency with the grammar and statistical patterns of a language) and "functional linguistic competence" (the capacity to use language for reasoning, world-modeling, and problem-solving). Apparently these two capacities are dissociable in the human brain: some aphasia patients lose language almost entirely while their non-verbal reasoning remains sharp, while others show roughly the opposite pattern. Assuming this framework is basically sound, please describe the most important implications for how much weight I should give to an LLM's fluent, confident, natural-language explanation of its own reasoning — for instance, when it explains why it structured a piece of code in a particular way — as evidence that genuine reasoning actually produced that output.
