Write a dialogue between a conditional analyst and a dispositional essentialist debating whether dispositions can be analyzed in terms of counterfactual conditionals. The conditional analyst defends a broadly Lewisian "reformed" analysis that handles C. B. Martin's electro-fink cases—where a fink causes a live wire to stop conducting the instant it is touched, or a dead wire to start conducting the instant it is touched—by requiring the disposition's intrinsic causal basis to persist through the stimulus, and tries to extend this strategy to Bird-style masking cases, such as a poison whose lethal disposition is masked by a simultaneously ingested antidote. The essentialist objects that masking defeats the reformed analysis even when the causal basis remains fully intact, that interfering factors can never be exhaustively excluded by a ceteris paribus clause, and that dispositionality must instead be treated as a primitive, unanalyzable feature of properties. The two should respond directly to each other's specific examples and proposed fixes rather than restating general positions, with the conditional analyst pressing the essentialist on whether "primitive dispositionality" is genuinely explanatory or merely a refusal to explain.
