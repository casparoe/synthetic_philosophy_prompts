So I went down a MIRI-papers rabbit hole again this week — rereading Yudkowsky and Herreshoff's old "Tiling Agents for Self-Modifying AI, and the Löbian Obstacle" writeup, plus the Christiano-et-al probabilistic reflection paper and some of Garrabrant's logical induction stuff — and it turned into a whole argument with a friend from my meetup group about whether the Löbian obstacle is actually telling us something deep about self-trust or whether its mostly a self-inflicted wound from insisting that trust has to run through formal provability. Figured this would make a good dialogue rather than me just re-litigating it in my head.

Here's the setup: one character, call her the Prover, is a proof-theoretic purist in the tiling-agents tradition. Her view is that Löb's theorem is a real wall, not a technicality — a system can't in general prove "if I can prove P, then P" for arbitrary P without thereby already proving P, so no fixed formal system can fully vouch for a successor reasoning the same way it does. She thinks the honest response is the ordinal-hierarchy / parametric-polymorphism move, where an agent only ever certifies successors running on a strictly weaker system than its own, all the way down, and that this "no free lunch" structure is exactly what an agent's self-trust should look like. The other character, Bayes, thinks that whole apparatus is a beautifully engineered fix for a problem you get mostly by demanding binary provability in the first place — once you let the agent hold calibrated credences instead (reflective probability à la Christiano et al., or Garrabrant's logical induction), a lot of the sting goes out of it, since a well-defined probability distribution can talk about its own future outputs without the liar-paradox-style blowup that provability runs into.

Write me a meaty back-and-forth between them, maybe 10ish exchanges, where they're actually responding to each other's specific moves rather than restating their opening positions — I want Bayes to push the Prover on whether her descending hierarchy is really a solution or just a polite way of shoving the regress up one level forever (and whether it's weird that trusting a successor apparently requires that successor to be strictly weaker than you), and I want the Prover to push back on whether Bayes's calibration guarantees, which as I understand them are asymptotic / in-the-limit rather than available at any given finite time, actually buy you the kind of self-trust you'd want before doing something irreversible like a self-modification. Both of them should be genuinely sharp and know the material cold — I don't want either position strawmanned — and it's totally fine if they end in a real stalemate rather than a tidy resolution, as long as it's clear exactly where the disagreement now lives.
