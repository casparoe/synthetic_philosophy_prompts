So I'm a phil PhD student (formal epistemology track, plus an epistemic logic reading group on the side that meets every other week), and this week we finally did the surprise exam paradox -- teacher tells the class there'll be a surprise test sometime next week, the students reason their way backward from Friday ("if it hasn't happened by Thursday, we'd know it has to be Friday, so Friday's out"), keep eliminating days the same way, "prove" no day can possibly work, and then the test happens on, say, Wednesday and everyone's still genuinely surprised. I feel like I've got a decent handle on the classic moves here -- the self-referential "announcement is basically a Moore's-paradox sentence" reading, Sorensen's blindspot-proposition framing from that 80s book of his, that kind of thing.

What I can't shake is an offhand comment our prof made, which is that on Williamson's picture the real culprit isn't self-reference at all, its the KK principle failing (the idea that knowing P puts you in a position to know that you know P) -- that the backward induction secretly needs the students to not just know but know-that-they-know-that-they-know, all the way up, on every single day, and that this is basically the same phenomenon as his margin-for-error stuff, the examples about not being able to tell purely by introspection that you're not sitting right on the edge of some threshold (exactly how many hairs make you bald, whether it's cold enough that you'd say "I'm cold"). I genuinely don't see the bridge between those two things -- one side is about perceptual or introspective vagueness and the other is a clean little logic puzzle about an announcement, no fuzziness in sight. Could you walk me through, fairly intuitively and without leaning too hard on the symbolic apparatus, why iterated knowledge is supposed to be doing the real work in generating the paradox, how a margin-for-error story is even supposed to get traction on a case with no perceptual component, and why someone would think this KK-failure diagnosis is a deeper solution than just stopping at "the announcement is a blindspot proposition, no real contradiction, done"? Also fully prepared to hear I'm garbling the Williamson attribution and this is actually from a paper responding to him rather than from the man himself.
