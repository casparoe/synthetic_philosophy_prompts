My friend and I got into a whole thing last night about whether my wedding ring is "the same object" as my grandmother's old brooch, since the goldsmith melted the brooch down to cast the ring. He's doing a metaphysics PhD and wouldn't drop it -- kept going on about Gibbard's example, a statue called Goliath and the lump of clay it's made of, "Lumpl," which share literally every atom and boundary right now but still aren't identical, because the lump could survive being squashed flat and the statue couldn't, so by Leibniz's law they're discernible and therefore distinct. Same deal with my ring and the gold sitting there composing it, apparently. I couldnt actually beat him on it, which is annoying.

Anyway -- the goldsmith who's done this for our family for 40 years, melting down worn heirlooms and recasting them for the next wedding or christening, is my aunt Teresa, and shes retiring. I'm giving the toast at her dinner this weekend, to a room that's half her craft-guild friends and my insufferable philosophy friend, half family who just want something warm about a woman who's had her hands on four generations of our jewelry. Can you write it? I want the constitution-vs-identity thing to be the actual backbone of the toast -- something true and a little moving about what does and doesn't survive when she remakes a piece -- not a lazy "ship of Theseus, am I right" line, and it can't turn into a lecture or grandma's side of the table will start checking their phones.
