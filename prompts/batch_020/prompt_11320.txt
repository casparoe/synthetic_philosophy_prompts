I'm about two-thirds through a novel set in Paris during the Terror (working title is something clunky like "The Mathematician's Widow," that'll change), told partly from Sophie de Grouchy's point of view while her husband Condorcet is in hiding at a widow's boarding house — a Madame Vernet, if the accounts are right — under a false name, in the last months before he's caught. For research I've been reading his 1785 essay on the probability of majority verdicts, the jury theorem stuff, where he tries to show mathematically that a group of voters who are each just slightly more likely to be right then wrong will, given enough of them voting independently, almost certainly land on the correct verdict. My economist friend keeps saying this makes Condorcet basically the patron saint of "futarchy," Robin Hanson's proposal where a legislature votes on one measurable value to maximize (GDP, life expectancy, whatever) and a speculative betting market, not politicians or citizens, decides which actual policy gets enacted, on the theory that people with money on the line will aggregate evidence better than any debate could.

That comparison has been stuck in my head and I want to build a late scene around it: an argument between Sophie and Condorcet in his last weeks of hiding, where she presses him on whether he'd really put his name to something like this. So I need his actual reasoning, not just "Enlightenment rationalist loves math, therefore yes." Given the specific conditions his own theorem needs to work at all (independence of voters, each one individually more reliable than not, which is exactly why he was so fixated on public instruction as a precondition for citizenship), given his 1790 pamphlet arguing women had to be recognized as competent political judges, and given that Sophie's own philosophy, via her Smith translation, runs through sympathy rather than calculation — would Condorcet see futarchy as his project finally realized, or is there something in the mechanism that actually betrays what he was after? I don't need a clean yes or no. I need the specific spot where he'd start to squirm, since that's the argument I have to be able to put in his mouth instead of mine.
