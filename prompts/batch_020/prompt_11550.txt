Last week a friend and I spent an entire dinner arguing over whether Schopenhauer's philosophy of music can survive contact with twelve-tone composition, and neither of us gave ground. Her position was that his account in The World as Will and Representation ties music's whole metaphysical standing to a tonal hierarchy: the bass, inner voices, and melody map onto the successive grades of the will's objectification, and the melody's constant departure from and return to the keynote, together with devices like suspension, supplies the audible image of desire delayed and then satisfied. On her reading, a technique expressly designed to abolish the tonal center should leave him with nothing coherent to say, or force him to deny that such music is really music at all. I countered that his pessimism is elastic enough that a will which never resolves might be the more faithful image, keynote or no keynote. Reasoning strictly from what Schopenhauer actually commits himself to, rather than from anachronistic sympathy, how do you think he would have judged Schoenberg's method: as music that has lapsed out of its metaphysical vocation entirely, as an unexpectedly apt image of a will that never finds satisfaction, or as a case his own framework has no resources to adjudicate? Work through the strongest case for each option before you settle on one.
