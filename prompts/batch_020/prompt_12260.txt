A close friend and I spent much of last night's dinner arguing in circles about a decision I've been putting off, and I still don't know which of us was right. My cousin is being released from prison in a few weeks after a five-year sentence, and I've offered him my spare room while he gets back on his feet, along with a lead on a steady construction job through a contact of mine. On the face of it this looks like an easy call: stable housing and steady employment are among the best-documented predictors of whether someone reoffends, so it seems as though I'd be doing him, and probably everyone else, a real favor.

My friend didn't dispute any of that, but she pushed back hard on whether I should feel confident that this is the right thing to do. Her point was that the moment he moves in and starts that job, the effects branch outward in ways neither of us could trace, let alone weigh against one another: maybe the job would otherwise have gone to someone who needed it more, whose life goes somewhat worse as a result; maybe the stability lets my cousin meet someone and have children whose lives ripple forward for generations, for better or worse; maybe having him around changes the course of my own life in ways I would never be able to trace back to this decision at all. She argued that these unforeseeable knock-on effects vastly outnumber the handful of effects I can actually see and reason about, that there is no particular reason to expect them to cancel out on balance, and that I have therefore just arbitrarily stopped counting consequences at the point that happens to make me feel good about my choice. I countered that this cannot be the right way to think about it, since it seems to show that we could never be justified in believing any action to be good, which feels like the wrong lesson to draw from a case this mundane. We went back and forth for the better part of an hour and never resolved it.

I would be curious to get an outside read on this: is my friend pointing to a genuine problem with how I am reasoning about this decision, or is there a flaw in her argument that we were both missing?
