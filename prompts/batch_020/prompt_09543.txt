My younger brother is getting married in three weeks, and as his best man I owe the room a toast at the reception. Since I offered a while ago to be the one driving people home afterward, I'll also be the only sober adult in the building that night, which turns out to matter for what I want to ask you to write.

For the past couple of years I've been making my way through Plato on my own, evenings mostly, with no class or teacher behind it, and somewhere in the Laws I ran into an argument that stopped me: that a wine party, properly run by someone who stays sober and keeps order, can actually be used to test and even train a person's self-control, because wine strips away the composure people normally perform and lets their real character surface, in something like the way real fear shows who is actually brave. It came with conditions attached -- the young kept away from wine altogether, older men more or less encouraged toward real drunkenness at festivals, as a kind of medicine against the stiffness that sets in with age. I know Plato says colder-sounding things about drink elsewhere -- Socrates is famously the one guest at the Symposium who never loses himself no matter how much wine goes around -- but it's the Laws passage I care about here, because it happens to describe, almost exactly, the job I'll be doing in three weeks. My brother, who has been our family's designated wine person since he was about nineteen, thought this was hilarious when I first sent it to him, and we've been half-jokingly calling me the "sober president" for the wedding ever since.

Please write the toast itself: about five minutes long, built around this idea, for a room where my brother and one or two of his old philosophy-minded friends will catch exactly what I'm doing, while his fiancée's family, our grandparents, and most of the other guests just need it to land as warm and genuinely funny with no context required. It should use the idea seriously enough that being "the one who stayed sober enough to say what the wine revealed" is the actual license I'm using to tell a couple of slightly unflattering stories about him over the years -- but it can't tip over into an actual roast, since the whole point of Plato's sober president is to use what he notices to look after people and correct them gently, not to expose them. Close with the toast proper, glass raised.
