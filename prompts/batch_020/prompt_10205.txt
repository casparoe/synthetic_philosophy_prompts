So a while back the whole AI boom got me curious enough that I actually sat down and read Alan Turing's original 1950 paper, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," instead of just absorbing the usual pop-culture version of "the Turing test" you see everywhere. I don't have any background in philosophy or computer science, I just like going to the source when something bugs me. Anyway, near the end there's a section where he runs through objections to his imitation game, and one of them is literally about extrasensory perception - telepathy, that sort of thing. He talks about the statistical evidence for it being "overwhelming" (I'm guessing this is tied to those old Duke University card-guessing experiments from the 30s/40s, could be wrong), and then he actually proposes sticking people in what he calls a "telepathy-proof room" so a psychic contestant couldn't cheat the test by reading the interrogator's mind. I nearly fell off my chair - this is the guy who basically invented theoretical computer science, and hes taking seance-adjacent stuff seriously enough to build a fix for it right into his thought experiment.

What I actually want is help understanding the logic of that bit, not just "wow weird huh." Like, does ESP being real actually pose a genuine threat to the imitation game as a test of machine intelligence, in a way that needs a real patch? Or is the "telepathy-proof room" just a cheap workaround that doesn't touch anything deep about the argument? And is there maybe a more generous reading where him taking this seriously actually shows something coherent about how strictly behavioral his whole test was meant to be - basically any channel at all that could give a human contestant an edge unrelated to intelligence has to get closed off, no matter how outlandish, because the test only cares about observable behavior? Or is that too charitable, and its really just a smart guy absorbing some bad scientific fashions of his own moment in a way that doesn't tell us anything deep about his philosophy of mind? I've gone back and forth on this on my own for a while now and would love an actual unpacking instead of a one-line shrug.
