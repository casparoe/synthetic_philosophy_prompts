Been down an old GOFAI rabbit hole lately and think I finally get why everyone made such a fuss about the frame problem. But it also kinda seems like LLMs just... solved it? The original McCarthy-and-Hayes version was about how you formally represent all the stuff that *doesn't* change when an agent acts, without hand-coding a seperate axiom for every fact, and Dennett's whole point in "Cognitive Wheels" was that any general reasoner is doomed to either miss some obviously relevant side effect or get stuck endlessly deducing irrelevant non-changes. Modern LLMs clearly do neither of those by default, they just handle implicit non-effects fine with zero hand-coded frame axioms. So was Fodor's argument that this shows "central cognition" can't be modeled computationally basically just a failure of imagination about what scale and gradient descent could pull off? Feels like the whole deep mystery evaporated once we threw enough compute at it, but curious if I'm missing something, since nobody who actually works on this stuff seems to talk like it's a closed question.
