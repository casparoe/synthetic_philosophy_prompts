During a graduate seminar on free will and moral responsibility, we spent an entire session on the relationship between Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility and the position his own father, P. F. Strawson, had defended decades earlier in "Freedom and Resentment," and I came away unsure whether the two views are genuinely in conflict or simply talk past one another. The Basic Argument tries to show that ultimate, or "true," moral responsibility is impossible whether or not determinism is true: to be truly responsible for what one does, the argument runs, one would have to be truly responsible for the way one is, and this in turn would require having authored one's own formative character in some prior act; but any such act would itself express a still-earlier nature one did not choose, so that the demand for self-authorship regresses without ever reaching a non-arbitrary starting point. P. F. Strawson's essay, by contrast, argues that our reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt) are simply given features of ordinary interpersonal life that neither require nor could receive any external metaphysical license, and that the "objective attitude," which treats a person as something to be managed or treated rather than blamed or praised, is reserved for special exempting cases such as childhood, illness, or compulsion, rather than something the mere truth of determinism would force on us wholesale.

Please compare these two positions in some depth, and then take a definite stance on the following question: does the elder Strawson's naturalistic, practice-based strategy actually defuse the force of the Basic Argument, precisely because it declines to engage with the metaphysical question it answers in the negative, namely, whether anyone is ever truly, ultimately responsible? Or does the Basic Argument, if sound, undercut the naturalistic strategy from within, by showing that the reactive attitudes it treats as bedrock are fitting responses only to a picture of persons as self-originating sources of their own character, a picture the regress is meant to discredit, so that naturalism at best explains why the attitudes persist without ever vindicating them? Please defend a specific answer, engaging with the strongest version of whichever side you end up rejecting, rather than presenting the two views as simply incommensurable.
