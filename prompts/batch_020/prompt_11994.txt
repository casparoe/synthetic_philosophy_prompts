In my philosophy elective this semester, we spent a few weeks on Wittgenstein's later work, reading selections from the Philosophical Investigations on language games, the rule-following paradox, and the private language argument. Around the same time, a debate broke out in my friend group about whether models like ChatGPT actually understand the language they produce, or whether they are just extremely sophisticated autocomplete with no real grasp of meaning ("stochastic parrots," as one of my friends kept putting it, borrowing the phrase from the famous paper). I already know the standard talking points on both sides of that debate, so what I'm actually curious about is something narrower: what would Wittgenstein himself have said, reasoning strictly from his own framework and not from anything written about artificial intelligence?

Could you reconstruct his likely position, working through three things in particular? First, his idea that a word's meaning is constituted by its use: does this cut in favor of ascribing understanding to a system trained on nothing but patterns of use, or does it actually raise the bar in a way that's hard for such a system to meet? Second, his insistence that language games are only intelligible against a shared "form of life": how should this bear on a system that has no body, no needs, and no participation in human customs and practices? Third, the rule-following considerations and the private language argument that grows out of them: the puzzle there is that no finite stretch of behavior can, on its own, fix which rule is being followed, and Wittgenstein seems to think only a public, communal practice can do that work. Would this count against an LLM ever being said to genuinely follow a linguistic rule at all, or does the fact that its outputs are constantly checked and corrected by a whole community of human users and evaluators actually give it something like the public foothold his argument requires? I'm less interested in a tidy summary of each idea on its own than in how the three interact, and possibly pull against each other, once you actually apply them to this case. Please end by telling me which way you think Wittgenstein would ultimately have leaned, and be honest about the weakest link in your own reconstruction.
