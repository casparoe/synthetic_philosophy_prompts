Last spring I agreed to serve as one of three judges for a regional prize for debut novels, and the experience left me with a philosophical puzzle I have not been able to set aside since. During our deliberations, my two co-judges and I disagreed sharply over several of the finalists, and at one point one of them remarked, half in jest, that whichever novel the three of us eventually settled on would simply become "the best debut of the year" by virtue of our verdict, since the prize just is whatever its judges decide, with no further fact of the matter to get right or wrong. This struck me as troubling, since it would seem to imply that had the panel been composed of three different people with different sensibilities, a quite different and perhaps far weaker novel would have been just as entitled to the title. Surely, I objected, we were meant to be tracking some genuine excellence already present in the prose, rather than simply manufacturing it by fiat through our vote.

The same co-judge then asked me to say what this independent excellence actually consisted in, apart from our own reactions and those of the critics and readers we happen to admire, and I found I had nothing to offer that did not eventually bottom out in somebody's judgment: mine, hers, some reviewer's, some tradition's. Every criterion I reached for, such as psychological depth, control of sentence rhythm, or structural daring, turned out itself to be something we had learned to value through prior acts of judging, rather than a feature we could point to independently of all judges, the way one might point to a novel's page count. The question has stayed with me partly for self-interested reasons, since I am now well into my third novel and cannot quite shake the suspicion that its fate will have less to do with what is on the page than with who happens to be reading it. I would very much welcome help thinking the matter through: is one of us right and the other simply confused, or is there some third way of understanding literary merit that would spare me from having to choose between these two uncomfortable options?
