I am a PhD student in philosophy working mainly on practical reason, though I have recently been pulled into the metaethics literature on the wrong-kind-of-reasons (WKR) problem for fitting-attitude analyses of value. Write a short dialogue, set in the question period after a colloquium talk, between the speaker -- who has just defended the view that for something to be good just is for there to be reason to favor it -- and an audience member who argues that the analysis cannot survive WKR-style cases, such as a demon who credibly threatens an agent with terrible harm unless she comes to admire something thoroughly unremarkable. The speaker should attempt some principled, non-question-begging way of separating the 'right kind' of reasons for favoring an object from the 'wrong kind,' while the questioner presses on whether any such line can be drawn without either presupposing the very notion of value at issue or simply misclassifying some intuitive cases. Have the two engage directly with each other's strongest moves rather than talking past one another, and do not let either side win too easily.
