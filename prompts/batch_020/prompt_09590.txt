I have no background in philosophy, but I've been a serious amateur violinist for most of my life, and over the past year I've been working through Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation on my own, mostly because I kept running into the claim that Wagner got his ideas about the special status of music from him. I've been stuck for weeks on the paragraph in Book Three, section 52 (I'm reading the old Haldane and Kemp translation), where he spells out the analogy between the parts of a piece of harmony and the "grades" by which the will objectifies itself in nature. The part I keep re-reading is this:

"Now, further, in the whole of the complemental parts which make up the harmony between the bass and the leading voice singing the melody, I recognise the whole gradation of the Ideas in which the will objectifies itself. Those nearer to the bass are the lower of these grades, the still unorganised, but yet manifold phenomenal things; the higher represent to me the world of plants and beasts. The definite intervals of the scale are parallel to the definite grades of the objectification of will, the definite species in nature. [...] Indeed, even the impure discords, which give no definite interval, may be compared to the monstrous abortions produced by beasts of two species, or by man and beast. But to all these bass and complemental parts which make up the harmony there is wanting that connected progress which belongs only to the high voice singing the melody [...]. The disconnected course of all the complemental parts, and their regulation by definite laws, is analogous to the fact that in the whole irrational world, from the crystal to the most perfect animal, no being has a connected consciousness of its own which would make its life into a significant whole, and none experiences a succession of mental developments, none perfects itself by culture, but everything exists always in the same way according to its kind, determined by fixed law."

What I can't tell is whether the specific musical facts he leans on here are doing real argumentative work, or are just vivid illustrations of a conclusion he already holds on other grounds. Is a discord "monstrous" for the same reason a hybrid animal is, namely that it crosses two otherwise distinct kinds, or is the comparison really just trading on a vaguer sense that both are somehow "impure"? And is that point meant to support, or merely restate, the claim right after it, that the middle voices lack the melodic "connected progress" that would give them anything like a unified inner life? I would like to understand this well enough to know whether Schopenhauer thought facts about harmony were themselves evidence for his picture of nature, or whether the music was only ever meant to make an independently held metaphysics easier to feel.
