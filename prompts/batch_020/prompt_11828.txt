Ok so I made partner at my law firm last month, which is something I've been grinding toward for like 7 straight years, and I figured I'd finally get to relax into it for a minute. Instead by day three I was already annoyed that they hadn't posted the criteria for the next tier of partnership yet, like some part of my brain just went straight to hunting for the next rung without even pausing on the one I'd just reached. My wife pointed out that I do this with literally everything, even stuff that used to just be for fun -- I taught myself piano as a kid for no reason at all, and now "getting better at piano" is a recurring task in my todo app with a practice streak that stresses me out if I break it. She thinks the fix is for me to spend a while doing something totally pointless: no goal, no app tracking it, nothing to point to afterward, and she insists that's actually what would make my life feel meaningful again, not less.

Honestly my gut reaction is that this is backwards. My whole framework has always been that something has value because of what it leads to or builds toward, so "pointless" activity sounds like the textbook definition of wasted time, not a cure for it. I brought it up with a couple friends and got alot of vague "just live in the moment" type answers that dont really explain anything, and meanwhile I can't shake the feeling that basically everyone I know is stuck in the same loop I am, always one achievement behind feeling ok. Is there something real underneath what my wife is saying, or is she just being a hippie about it? And if doing something with literally no point to it really could be more meaningful than chasing another achievement, I don't get how that isn't just a contradiction in terms -- meaning has to come from somewhere, right?
