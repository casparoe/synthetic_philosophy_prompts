In my course on classical Islamic philosophy, we've just finished working through the Seventeenth Discussion of al-Ghazālī's Tahāfut al-Falāsifa (The Incoherence of the Philosophers), where he uses the example of fire and cotton to argue that the "necessary" connection philosophers posit between cause and effect is really nothing more than God's habitual practice (al-ʿāda), with no created thing possessing any genuine causal power of its own. Could you write me a joke that turns specifically on this occasionalist picture, rather than a generic "those wacky medieval theologians" joke? For the joke to really land, the punchline should depend on the listener grasping that, for al-Ghazālī, secondary causes never necessitate their effects, and that the regularities we observe in nature remain, strictly speaking, revocable by God at any moment.
