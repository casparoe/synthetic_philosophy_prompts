I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and one recurring move in that milieu is to treat the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, independence especially, as though they belong in the same category as transitivity: deny them and you're basically volunteering to be money-pumped. The Allais paradox has always struck me as the sharpest test of that stance. The classic pattern -- preferring a certain $1 million to a gamble giving $5 million with probability 0.10, $1 million with probability 0.89, and nothing with probability 0.01, while also preferring a 10% shot at $5 million to an 11% shot at $1 million once the common 89% chance of $1 million is stripped out of both pairs -- doesn't feel, even to the people who exhibit it, like the same kind of mistake as getting caught in a preference cycle. By most accounts, even Savage gave exactly that pattern of answers when Allais first put the choice to him directly, and only afterward concluded that it was his own intuition, rather than his sure-thing principle, that needed correcting.

What I'd like from you is an essay that actually settles this rather than just surveying it. Lay out the strongest case for treating independence as non-negotiable, including the dynamic-consistency and money-pump arguments meant to show that any systematic violation of it can be converted into a sequence of trades that leaves the violator strictly worse off for certain. Then weigh it against the most serious case that Allais-type preferences can be fully rational: the objection that independence quietly assumes an outcome contributes the same value no matter what it is mixed with, which looks doubtful once disappointment or regret is allowed to attach to "almost winning $5 million," and at least one worked-out alternative theory -- rank-dependent or cumulative prospect theory, or Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility -- that tries to accommodate the Allais pattern without licensing a money pump. Please don't hedge at the end: tell me whether independence should be kept as a bedrock rationality constraint, rejected outright, or replaced with something more specific, and defend that verdict against the sharpest objection to it that you raised along the way.
