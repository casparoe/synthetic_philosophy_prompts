Then explain how 'level-splitting' responses are supposed to defuse the tension, and say briefly whether you think they succeed."

And here's the student's answer, word for word:

"Epistemic akrasia is a state where an agent's first-order belief about the world and their higher-order belief about what their evidence supports come apart -- e.g. believing it's raining while also believing your evidence doesn't support that it's raining. This is a straightforward contradiction, since it amounts to believing both P and not-P at once, and most epistemologists think rational agents should never end up this way.

The tension comes from cases like the sleep-deprived doctor: she works out a medication dosage and is confident in the arithmetic, then learns she's just come off a brutal overnight shift and that doctors that sleep-deprived get this kind of calculation wrong noticeably more often, without being able to tell from the inside whether they're one of the ones who messed up. Intuitively she should now be less sure of her answer. But her original evidence (the calculation itself) hasn't changed, and it still supports the same dosage exactly as much as before -- being tired doesn't change what follows from what. So if she lowers her confidence in the dosage while still believing the calculation supports it, she's now akratic in exactly the sense described above.

Christensen's level-splitting response is that this is fine: the first-order and higher-order levels can just come apart, because higher-order evidence about your reliability doesn't actually defeat what your first-order evidence supports, it only makes it reasonable to doubt yourself. On this view the doctor can rationally keep believing the calculation is sound while also rationally lowering her credence in the dosage being right, and the Non-Akrasia requirement turns out to be less secure than it looks. I think this is basically the right way to handle these cases, since demanding perfect coordination between what we believe and what we believe about our own reliability is asking too much of fallible creatures like us."
