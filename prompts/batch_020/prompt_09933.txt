I co-host a podcast for an audience steeped in the rationalist blogosphere, and next episode's guest is a philosopher who defends a conciliationist, "equal weight" view of peer disagreement in the spirit of Elga and Christensen. Our listeners have spent years hashing out Aumann's agreement theorem and the Yudkowsky–Hanson modesty debate, so I don't want her just restating the equal-weight view. Please draft eight interview questions that push past that: I want one on the self-undermining objection, i.e., whether widespread, competent rejection of conciliationism should, by her own lights, lower her confidence in conciliationism itself; one on whether Christensen's independence condition can be met without covertly relying on the very reasoning under dispute; one on whether the famous restaurant-bill-splitting case actually generalizes from arithmetic slip-ups to deep philosophical disagreements, where what counts as "reasonable" is itself contested; one on how her view relates to Aumann's theorem once you drop common priors and common knowledge of posteriors, which real disputants never share; and one on whether iterating conciliation across many controversial domains collapses into a global skepticism she would presumably want to resist -- and for each question, a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give.
