I fell down a rabbit hole recently reading rationalist / AI-safety stuff about "acausal threats"—the idea that some agent you have zero causal contact with (a future misaligned AI, some alien superintelligence, whatever) could still extort you right now by threatening to hurt something you value later, kind of in the same family as the old Roko's basilisk thing but treated way more seriously in actual decision theory writing. The proposed defense that keeps showing up is "surrogate goals": you pick some cheap, made-up goal ahead of time and precommit to reacting to threats against it exactly as hard as you'd react to threats against your real values, the theory being that this redirects the threat onto the decoy instead of the stuff you actually care about.

Here's what I don't get though. If the goal is openly fake and the threatening agent knows perfectly well it's just a decoy invented for this exact purpose, why on earth would a sufficiently intelligent threatener not just ignore it and go straight for you're real values anyway? Seems like this could maybe trick a dumb adversary who doesn't notice the substitution, but every version of this scenario I've read is explicitly about superintelligent, near-perfectly rational agents, so shouldn't the surrogate goal strategy just fall apart the moment the adversary is smart enough to clock what's going on? I mentioned this to a friend who's way more into decision theory than me and she insisted the whole strategy is specifically built to survive the threatener knowing full well it's a decoy, which sounds backwards to me—isn't the entire point of a decoy that it has to fool someone in order to work?
