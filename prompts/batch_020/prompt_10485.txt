Apparently one of his big arguments was that from a totally impartial "point of view of the universe," WHEN someone's happiness happens shouldn't change how much it counts — like a person's wellbeing a thousand years from now matters exactly as much as someone's wellbeing today, and treating it as less important just because its farther away in time isn't really defensible (being less sure about the future is a totally separate issue from that, according to him).

This has been stuck in my head because I also spend way too much time watching youtube videos about effective altruism and "longtermism," and they use basically this exact same no-discounting logic to argue we should care enormously about stuff like AI extinction risk over nearer-term problems, since if you don't discount future people at all, there could eventually be trillions of them and their wellbeing ends up outweighing everyone alive right now. Will MacAskill's whole book is kind of built around this idea.

So here's my question: if you somehow plopped Sidgwick down in 2025 and showed him how his own "don't discount the future" argument gets used to justify prioritizing speculative stuff like AI risk over more concrete present-day suffering, what do you think he'd actually say?
