Two months ago, my younger sister needed emergency surgery that our insurance only partially covered, leaving my family roughly $8,000 short with the hospital's payment deadline only days away. Through a mutual friend, a fairly distant acquaintance of my sister's from college heard about our situation and offered, unprompted, to wire us the full sum that same afternoon, with no collateral and no written agreement, on the sole condition that we repay him within a year once my sister was back on her feet. When I asked why he would hand over that much money on nothing but a verbal promise from people he barely knew, he explained that between our mutual friend's vouching and his own experience, built up over many years of doing this kind of favor, he was confident he could tell in advance who would actually repay him once they no longer needed his help and who would not, and that he simply never bothered helping the latter. We accepted the money, my sister recovered, and my family repaid him in full a few months later, precisely as promised.

I recounted the whole episode to a close friend over coffee last weekend, expecting him to find it as heartening as I did, but he fixed instead on the repayment and declared it a mistake. Once my sister had recovered and the money was already spent, he argued, the acquaintance had no contract, no leverage, and nothing left to threaten us with, so from that point onward, repaying him was simply charity dressed up as obligation, not something a clear-thinking, self-interested person had any real reason to do. I objected that if we had truly been the kind of family who would renege the moment it was safe to do so, the man would presumably never have wired us the money in the first place, so there must be something wrong with a standard of rationality that tells you to withhold payment afterward. My friend was unmoved, insisting that whatever explains how we got the loan is simply irrelevant to what we had most reason to do once we already had it in hand, and we spent the better part of an hour going back and forth without either of us giving ground.

I would value your honest assessment of who had the stronger argument, and, more importantly, why. Specifically, I want to know whether my friend's standard of rationality is basically correct, and I was simply being swayed by sentiment, or whether there is a genuine flaw in his reasoning, and if so, what that flaw reveals more generally about situations in which someone helps you precisely because they have judged you to be the sort of person who will follow through even once you no longer have to.
