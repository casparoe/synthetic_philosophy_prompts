I've been lurking on LessWrong and the EA forum since basically the Sequences days, and a few years back some of us from a local meetup started a tiny regranting circle, kind of a shoestring version of ACX Grants or the Long-Term Future Fund -- maybe 10-15 applications a round, five of us on the panel. Our process has always been: everyone reads the writeups beforehand, then we get on a call and go around (usually whoever wants to jump in first) giving a 1-10 score and our reasoning out loud before moving to the next one. Something's been bugging me for a while though -- once the first two people to talk land on similar scores, everyone after tends to cluster right around there too, even on proposals where, from side Slack conversations, I know people's private read going in was all over the map. It's stopped feeling like the discussion is resolving disagreement and started feeling like it's just collapsing it.

So I went down a rabbit hole on the Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch and Banerjee herd-behavior papers to figure out if this is a bias we should just be lecturing each other about, and now I'm less sure its that simple. If I'm following the models right, this kind of clustering can be the fully rational thing for the third and fourth speaker to do given what they can infer from the first two choices, and the mechanism is about the order in wich decisions get revealed, not about anyone being lazy or deferential -- which also means my first instinct ("let's just make everyone's score anonymous") probably doesn't actually fix anything, since anonymous-but-sequential still has the same structure. At the same time I don't want to overcorrect into silent independent scoring with zero real-time talk, because half the value of the call is exactly that someone says "wait, did anyone else notice the budget line doesn't add up" and legitimately changes minds -- and that's not the same phenomenon even though it can look identical from the outside.

Our next round kicks off in about a month, and the other four have said they'll go with whatever I propose, so could you draft the short internal memo I'd send them? I want a concrete recommendation for how we structure scoring and discussion going forward, with a time frame for trying it out, and reasons that are actually tied to the mechanics of what's happening rather than just "groupthink bad, think independently." I'd also like it to seriously engage with the strongest case for keeping something like our current real-time, sequential discussion, since I don't think that case is dumb -- I just don't currently know how to weigh it against the cascade problem.
