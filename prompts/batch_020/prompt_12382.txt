So this is going to sound niche but bear with me. I'm on the board of my building's tenant co-op (it's a converted old warehouse, like 40 units) and every spring we vote on how to spend whatever's left over in the maintenance reserve once the boring repairs are covered. There's usually four or five things on the table - this year it's new gym equipment, redoing the roof deck, bike storage, a small solar pilot, or just rolling the money into next year's pot. We've always done plain plurality voting (most first-place votes wins), and every single year there's drama about vote-splitting, like two of the "green-ish" options splitting the vote so something almost nobody actually wanted ends up winning. A few of us have been pushing to switch to ranked-choice as part of the bylaws rewrite we're doing this year, on the theory that it would basically fix the strategic voting problem for good.

Then I fell down a rabbit hole reading about voting theory and ran into this thing called the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem (hope I'm even remembering that name right), which as far as I can tell says pretty much any voting rule - ranked-choice included - can be gamed by someone voting dishonestly, as long as it isn't a flat-out dictatorship and there's three or more options in play. That kind of deflated my whole pitch. Now one guy on the committe is arguing we should just put everyone's favorite in a hat and draw one at random, since apparently that's the one setup that's actually impossible to game, but that feels crazy for a decision involving real money. I'm the one who has to write the actual recommendation memo for our committee meeting next Tuesday, and I don't want to oversell ranked-choice as "the fair one" now that I know that's not really true, but I also don't want us to just shrug and keep plurality, or go full lottery either. Could you draft that memo for me - doesn't need to be long, a page or so - that names an actual voting method for these reserve-fund votes, is upfront about what this impossibility result does and doesn't rule out, and gives the committee a real argument for picking your recommendation over the other options people have floated?
