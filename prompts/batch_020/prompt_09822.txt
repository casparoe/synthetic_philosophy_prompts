Write an essay on how to respond to Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals. The basic idea is that if indicatives express ordinary propositions, and it's probability always has to match the conditional probability P(B|A) (the "Equation," basically Adams's thesis), things collapse into triviality. So somethings gotta go: either conditionals don't really express propositions after all (the Adams/Edgington route), or the Equation just doesn't hold in full generality. Weigh the considerations on each side (a third way out is fine too, if you think there's one) and actually commit to a verdict -- don't just conclude that both sides have a point.
