So I've been buried in Simone Weil's essay on abolishing political parties for a dissertation chapter on anti-party political thought (the one she wrote in London for the Free French right before she died in '43). Her argument is basically that a party is defined by three things: it's a machine for manufacturing collective passion, a machine for exerting totalitarian pressure on the minds of its own members, and its real goal underneath whatever program it claims is just its own unlimited growth. Obviously she never lived to see anything like modern partisan media. Here's the thing bugging me: political scientists now describe US parties as organizationally hollow -- weak, can't even discipline their own members half the time -- while partisan tribalism is stronger than its probably ever been, manufactured mostly by cable news and algorithmic feeds rather than the party apparatus itself. Given her actual three-part definition, would Weil say that's the same disease she diagnosed just running on new hardware, so her proposed cure of abolishing the parties is now beside the point since the parties aren't even the ones doing the damage anymore? Or does something in her framework break once the organization itself stops being the vector for the collective passion? Give me your actual read on this using her specific argument, not a 'she'd probably have hated polarization' hand-wave.
