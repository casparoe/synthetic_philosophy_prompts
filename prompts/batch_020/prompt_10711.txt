I'm about two-thirds through drafting my third novel and I've run into a snag that's more philosophy than plot, so bear with me. My protagonist, Nadia, is stuck choosing between three job offers she's been sitting on for months: a curator gig at a small gallery, a teaching post at her old art school, and a conservation/restoration job at a museum. The twist (and the thing my writing group keeps flagging as "unrealistic") is that her preferences genuinely cycle — she'd take the gallery over teaching, teaching over restoration, but then, if you put restoration and the gallery job side by side, she'd actually pick restoration. My brother, who has an econ PhD, told me this is a textbook case of irrationality because of the money-pump argument: if she really prefers in a circle like that, in principle someone could walk her through a sequence of "upgrade" trades, charging a small fee at each step, and end up right back where she started except poorer. He says this settles it, full stop — cyclic preferences just are irrational and exploitable, and there's no way to write Nadia as both rational and stuck in that loop without it being a plot hole I need to fix.

Part of me doesn't want to sand the cycle out of her, since it's doing real thematic work (she's weighing genuinely incommensurable things — money, prestige, craft — and I think that's why the comparisons don't line up neatly). Before I go back to my brother, though, I want to actually get the philosophy right: is it true, no exceptions, that having cyclic preferences makes you irrational and exploitable? Or does it matter how Nadia would actually behave if faced with that sequence of trades — like whether she's naive about the pattern versus someone who'd see the whole loop coming and refuse to play along? I have a vague memory from an undegrad seminar of a phrase like "resolute choice" being relevant to this exact kind of case, but I never got the full story on what it actually does to the argument. Would love a real answer before I email him my very smug rebuttal.
