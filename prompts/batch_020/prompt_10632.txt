Two competing theories of how slurs actually work, semantically: Christopher Hom's "combinatorial externalism," where a slur's literal content bakes in an entire racist ideology (so it ends up true of nobody, since the ideology itself is false) — vs. the "prohibitionist" line from Anderson & Lepore, where a slur and its neutral counterpart have the exact same content, and all the offensiveness is just a social prohibition on the word itself, not on anything semantic. Compare the two: which one actually does a better job explaining stuff like in-group reclamation, or the fact that a slur still feels gross even embedded under negation or inside a hypothetical? Feel free to bring in Dummet's old "Boche" case too (he thought it had the same reference as "German," just a different "tone") if it's useful. Which theory do you actually buy, and why?
