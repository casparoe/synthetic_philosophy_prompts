Been prepping a session for my moral psych seminar and got stuck on how to present the relationship between old-school akrasia and the newer hyperbolic discounting stuff (Ainslie, Laibson, that whole literature) without just hand-waving "economists found a model that fits the data, mystery solved." What I want my students to actually sit with is wether the hyperbolic discounting story dissolves the classical philosophical puzzle of weakness of will, or just redescribes it while leaving the hard part completely untouched.

Here's the crux as I see it. On one reading, once you say the "now" self and the "later" self each have locally coherent (if myopic) preferences given their own discount curve, there's no genuine akrasia left over -- nobody's acting against their own considered judgment at the moment of choice, because "their judgment at the moment" just is whatever the current self's discount-weighted preference happens to be. But on the classical picture -- Aristotle's attempt to rescue the phenomenon against Socrates' flat denial that anyone ever knowingly does the worse thing -- real weakness of will requires a single agent who, right up until the moment they cave, still judges that the other option is better. Hyperbolic discounting looks like it explains that judgment away rather than vindicating it as a genuine bit of practical irrationality, and I can't decide if thats a feature or a bug of the model.

Anyways, could you write me a dialogue between two people on opposite sides of this? Maybe set it at like 1am in a kitchen, with one of them holding a slice of cake they very explicitly told themselves at breakfast they wouldn't touch. One character should think the whole "akrasia is its own distinctive kind of irrationality" picture is basically an artifact of bad folk psychology once you take discounting seriously; the other should think that move is a sleight of hand that just changes the subject instead of solving anything. Don't let either of them just restate their own view over and over -- I want real back and forth, so e.g. the "dissolutionist" should get pushed on why we should privilege the momentary self as the seat of "true" preference at all, and the "traditionalist" should get pushed on what's supposedly left of "judgment" once you admit it's produced by a discount function like everything else upstairs.
