I'm a senior currently drafting my IB Extended Essay in philosophy, and I've settled on Gödel's modal ontological argument as my topic after a term in our school's logic elective spent reconstructing the axioms on positive properties, God-likeness, and necessary existence on the whiteboard with our teacher, who did graduate work in modal logic before he started teaching. What has kept me more occupied than the proof itself is its aftermath: Jordan Howard Sobel's 1987 result that Gödel's own axioms, if true, entail that every actual truth is a necessary one (the so-called modal collapse), and C. Anthony Anderson's 1990 "emendation," which revises the definitions of "God-like" and "essence" specifically so that Sobel's derivation no longer goes through, later refined by Anderson and Gettings in response to Hájek's objections.

My teacher treats the Anderson fix as more or less closing the matter, and that is exactly the part I want to pressure-test before I commit to it as my essay's thesis. Could you write the strongest argument you can for the position that Anderson's emendation, even granting that it technically blocks Sobel's specific derivation, fails to rescue Gödel's argument, because the revised definitions have no motivation beyond the need to dodge collapse: they are read off the conclusion Gödel wanted to avoid rather than defended on independent grounds, so the repaired argument no longer earns its axioms the way the original claimed to. I would like the argument to engage with the actual mechanics of how Sobel's derivation runs and what Anderson changes, rather than gesturing at "ad hocness" in the abstract, and I would like it to address the obvious rejoinder that almost any revision made in response to a discovered problem could be called ad hoc by that same standard, so something more has to be said about why this particular repair is illegitimate.
