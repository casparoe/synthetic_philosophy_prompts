I've been down a bit of a Frege rabbit hole for a phil of logic class, and came accross something that's been bugging me. Apparently in 1924, the year before he died, Frege kept a private diary that's basically full-on hard-right politics: ranting against the Weimar constitution and parliamentary democracy, hoping some kind of strong "leader" would sweep it all away, going after the French and the Catholics, and spouting genuinely ugly antisemitic stuff, including musing that Jews shouldn't have equal political rights. It sat in his papers for decades and only got published sometime in the 90s, and apparently even Michael Dummett -- basically the guy who made Frege central to 20th-century analytic philosophy -- has said discovering it genuinely rattled him. But Dummett's official line (and I think most people's default move) is that this is just sad biographical trivia: Frege the man was a bigot, sure, but Frege's actual philosophy -- the logic, the sense/reference stuff, all of it -- stands completely untouched by that, because good arguments don't care about the character of whoever makes them.

That separation feels too clean to me though. Frege spent his whole career being fanatically anti-psychologistic about logic -- insisting that logical laws and "thoughts" (his Gedanken) exist objectively in a mind-independent realm he actually calls something like the "third realm" (drittes Reich) in the original essay, totally unaffected by what any person or society happens to believe, and treating any attempt to ground truth in what people actually think, individually or (worse, to him) collectively, as a corrosive relativism to be stamped out wherever it shows up. That's obviously a weird coincidence of terminology and not itself an argument, so set it aside. The real point is that this is someone with a deep temperamental commitment to truth being unaccountable to popular opinion. So: please construct the strongest argument you can that this exact impulse is what's running underneath both the anti-psychologism in his logic and the contempt for parliamentary democracy and equal citizenship in the diary, such that the diary counts as real evidence about the deep structure of his thought and isn't just embarrassing trivia that happens to share an author with the Grundgesetze. Don't just hand-wave "well, both come from a rigid personality" -- spell out the actual mechanism connecting the two. You'll also need to handle the obvious objection: plenty of committed logical and mathematical platonists, before and after Frege, were nowhere near authoritarians, so whatever connection you're proposing can't be a strict entailment -- so what kind of connection is it, exactly?
