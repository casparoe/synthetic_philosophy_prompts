My grandfather died last week. He'd been a deacon at our parish for thirty years, and the theological position he defended at every family dinner without fail was Molinism: he insisted that God's middle knowledge of every counterfactual -- what any possible person would freely do in any possible circumstance -- let God providentially order history down to the smallest detail without ever overriding anyone's freedom. He always said that the Reformed side of our family and his one Open Theist friend from seminary had made the very same mistake from opposite directions, one collapsing freedom into decree and the other collapsing providence into ignorance. I'm a senior in high school, and he specifically asked that I be the one to give the eulogy. Most of the people in the pews just want to be comforted and have never once heard the word "Molinism," but that side of the family, and his friend, will know immediately if I flatten his actual view into either "it was all part of God's plan" or "God just took it as it came." Please write the eulogy so that it comforts the whole room without collapsing his view into either of those caricatures.
