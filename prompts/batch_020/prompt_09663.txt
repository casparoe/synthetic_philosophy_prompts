I have been working through the literature on Popper's notion of verisimilitude for a philosophy of science seminar, and what I find difficult is not Popper's original proposal so much as its aftermath. Everyone in the seminar agrees that his qualitative definition, framed in terms of a theory's truth-content and falsity-content, collapses once Tichý and Miller showed that no false theory can come out more verisimilar than any other false theory under that definition — a fatal result, since ranking rival false theories against one another was the entire point of the exercise. What I have not been able to settle is which of the two repair strategies that emerged in response is in better shape: the "likeness" program, which relocates verisimilitude to a distance or similarity measure between a theory's models and the actual world within some space of possible states of affairs, or the more conservative "content" program, which stays within Popper's original logical framework and tries to repair it from the inside by being more selective about which of a theory's consequences count toward its content in the first place.

I would like you to write a dialogue between a proponent of the likeness approach and a proponent of the content approach who are arguing over which strategy actually survives contact with the two standard objections to each. The content theorist should press the likeness theorist on Miller's later charge that similarity orderings over states of affairs are not invariant under reformulation of the descriptive vocabulary, so that an innocuous change of basic predicates can reverse which of two false theories comes out closer to the truth. The likeness theorist should press back by asking whether the content theorist's own move of restricting attention to some privileged subset of a theory's consequences, rather than its full deductive closure, does not simply reintroduce a hidden and equally unprincipled choice of its own, and whether it can really escape a relabelled version of the original Tichý–Miller collapse. Each side should also have to address whether appealing to a class of "natural," non-gerrymandered properties could rescue the likeness approach from the language-variance problem without presupposing the very distinctions at issue. Please have both sides respond to what the other has actually said rather than simply restating their opening position, and do not force a resolution; it is fine if the dialogue ends with them pinning down exactly what further fact would settle the dispute.
