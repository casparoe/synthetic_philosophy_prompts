Ok so this has been living in my head for like two weeks and I need to get it out of my system. I'm not a programmer or a math person by trade, I just like messing around with home networking stuff, and I went down a wiki rabbit hole trying to figure out why setting up a TCP connection takes three steps instead of one, which led me to this old thought experiment called the Two Generals' Problem. Two armies on separate hills need to attack the same city at the exact same time or they both get slaughtered, and they can only talk by sending a messenger through enemy territory who might get caught. The part that broke my brain is that you can actually prove there is no number of "got your message" / "confirming I got it" / "confirming I got your confirmation" messages that ever makes both generals fully certain the other one is really going to charge, not even if a thousand messengers get through safe in a row, theres always one more layer of doubt underneath. Then I found out this connects to this whole philosophy/game-theory idea called common knowledge (not just both of us know a thing, but I know you know it, and you know that I know that you know it, forever down the chain), and apparently there's an even nastier version economists made up, sometimes called the email game, where two people's computers just auto-confirm back and forth, and it turns out that no matter how many hundreds of confirmations go through, the only stable outcome is that neither of them ever risks the coordinated move. The uncertainty just never rounds down to zero, and I guess it technically can't, structurally.

I told my friends about this at a bar and they just kind of blinked at me, so now I'm bringing it to you instead. I keep thinking there's a genuinely good, quietly devastating short story hiding in here, about the gap between "basically sure" and "sure," and how that gap never fully closes no matter how hard two people try to close it. I don't want literal generals on a hilltop, that's just the textbook version of it, so pick whatever setting actually earns the idea, could be smugglers, could be two people each planning to finally walk out on something on the same day so neither one is left exposed alone, could be something totally different. I'm not looking for a story that explains the math to me, I've basically got that part now, I want one that makes a reader actually feel why that last little bit of doubt is so unkillable, even after message forty, even after message four thousand.
