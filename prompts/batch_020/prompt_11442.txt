So a few weeks back my brother and I were having one of our usual late night arguments, this time about whether the "many worlds" quantum thing means theres a version of us out there thats happier, richer, whatever, living out all the choices we didn't make. I went down a rabbit hole afterwards trying to figure out if thats actually what physicists believe, and somehow ended up reading about a totally different idea called "modal realism," from a philosopher named David Lewis, and honestly it kind of wrecked my brain more than the quantum stuff did. As far as I can tell he's not talking about physics at all, he's saying that literally every way things could possibly have gone is just as real and solid as the world we're in, not just quantum branches but every coherent way reality could be, dragons and all, and that "actual" doesn't pick out anything special about our world, it just means "the one we're in," kind of like how "here" doesn't mean a special place, just wherever you happen to be standing. Even weirder, from what I read he didn't think you or I literally exist as duplicates in these other worlds making the different choices, there's just other people who happen to resemble us really closely (I think he called them "counterparts"), and none of these worlds are reachable from ours, no wormhole, no portal, because they're not even connected to us in space or time in the first place.

I'd love it if you could write me a short story that actually sits with this version of the idea instead of doing the usual parallel-universe/portal story we've all read a hundred times. Maybe follow someone who becomes properly convinced this is literally true (through reading, an argument, a weird experience, whatever works for the story) and has to grapple with the consequences: that they'll never be able to visit, contact, or prove the existence of any of it, that nothing they choose today changes how much reality there "is" in total since every option is realized somewhere regardless, and that when they die, it's not like a luckier version of them keeps going in some other world, its just some stranger elsewhere who happens to have lived a very similar life up to that point. I don't need a full explainer of the philosophy in there, I just want the existential weirdness of really believing this to come through in how the character thinks and feels, and if you can naturally make it clear along the way why this is a genuinely different claim than the usual quantum multiverse thing, that'd be great, since apparently a lot of people (me included, until recently) mix those two up.
