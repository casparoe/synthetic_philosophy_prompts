Since the start of this school year, I've been part of my high school's robotics team, and something that happened at one of our recent meetings has been bothering me in a way I haven't quite been able to untangle. Our team captain, Jordan, called an emergency meeting to lecture two of us, myself included, for missing a build session without letting anyone know ahead of time, saying it showed a lack of respect for everyone else's time during a busy stretch of the competition season. What bothers me is that Jordan has skipped at least three sessions himself this year without any warning to the rest of us, and when another member brought this up afterward, Jordan said his situation was different because he already knows the robot inside and out and doesn't need the practice time the way newer members do.

I mentioned all this to a couple of friends later that week, and we ended up disagreeing about how to make sense of it. One friend argued that since Jordan does exactly what he criticized us for, he loses any right to be upset with us at all. My other friend thought that was beside the point: missing a session without telling anyone really is inconsiderate to the rest of the team, and that's true no matter who happens to be saying it, so Jordan's own behavior shouldn't change how fair his criticism actually was. I can see the appeal of both positions, but they seem to be pulling in opposite directions, and I would rather understand the actual principle at work than just pick whichever explanation sounds more persuasive in the moment. Does someone's hypocrisy genuinely undermine their ability to fairly call out the same behavior in others, or does it just make them look bad personally while leaving the truth of what they said completely untouched?
