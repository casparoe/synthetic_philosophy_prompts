Ok so I'm about a third into a new novel and hit a snag that's more metaphysics than plot -- wanted to run my notes by you before I write myself into a corner.

Setup: my protagonist Ilsa gets in a bad accident at 19, wakes up with severe amnesia and a totally different affect -- flatter, colder, weirdly funnier than she used to be. The rest of the book follows her at 45. I've been drafting the two timelines as alternating chapters, and in my own notes I keep calling them Before-Ilsa and After-Ilsa, like they're two people sharing a body and a name rather than one person straightforwardly.

Which sent me down the endurantism/perdurantism rabbit hole. As I understand it: the endurantist says Ilsa's a 3D thing, wholly present at 19 and wholly present at 45, numerically one object that's just warm-at-19 and flat-at-45 rather than warm and flat full stop (that's the standard dodge around Lewis's temporary-intrinsics problem, right?). The perdurantist says she's really a 4D worm and 19-Ilsa and 45-Ilsa are litterally different temporal parts of her, hooked together by some continuity relation, neither one identical to the whole. Then there's the Sider stage-theory version, where each slice IS the whole person for a moment and it's the other slices that are merely counterparts, not parts of anything bigger.

My idea is to write the whole book so it reads like an argument for the perdurantist picture -- make the reader feel Before and After aren't the same person in the way the endurantist insists they have to be -- and then in the last chapter reveal that 45-Ilsa has been the one narrating the 19-Ilsa chapters all along, which is supposed to land as a gut-punch back toward endurantism.

Two things I want from you: does my grasp of the actual philosophy hold up, or have I mangled it somewhere? And separately, as a reader coming to this cold -- does that twist sound like it'd genuinely land, or is it the kind of thing that's clever on a napkin and dead on the page?
