Ok this is going to sound kind of random but bear with me. I'm a freelance illustrator, and over the last couple months two separate clients have asked if I could just "clean up" some Midjourney output instead of drawing from scratch, which has me kind of spiraling about whether I'll even have a career in five years. At my birthday dinner last week, my cousin (who's finishing up a PhD in theoretical CS) tried to cheer me up with what he swore was an actual mathematical reason not to worry. His argument went: computer scientists overwhelmingly believe P doesn't equal NP, and what that means is that "finding" a good solution to a problem is provably way harder than "checking" one once it already exists -- like how solving a sudoku puzzle is hard but confirming a filled-in one is correct is easy. He said making art sits on the "generation" side of that split and judging/critiquing art sits on the "verification" side, so it's basically mathematically guaranteed that coming up with genuinely good creative work will always be way harder than recognizing good work when you see it -- meaning "generation" jobs like mine are actually safer from automation than everyone assumes, and if anything I should be more worried about art critics losing there jobs than illustrators like me.

It made me feel a lot better for about a day, and I've been repeating it to pretty much everyone who'll listen since, but I want to sanity-check it with someone who isn't my cousin: given that the P vs NP thing is apparently settled science at this point, can I actually relax about the illustration side of things, and maybe even start hedging my career by picking up some art-critique or curation work, since that's supposedly the side of the equation that's actually at risk?
