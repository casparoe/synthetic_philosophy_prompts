I am currently taking a course in decision theory, which is probably why a decision from my own life has started to bother me more than it otherwise would. I have some savings to invest and am torn between two options. One is a long-established fund with decades of publicly documented returns. The other was launched a few months ago by a well-regarded team pursuing an untested strategy, and its real odds of outperforming or underperforming are simply not known yet. My financial advisor tells me that, based on how such funds have historically performed, its expected return is probably at least as good as the established fund's. Even so, I find myself strongly preferring the established option, without being able to point to any concrete reason to distrust the new one. Oddly, this is not a general policy of mine. Last year I invested a comparable sum in an early-stage company in an industry I know well, with no real way of knowing its actual odds of success, and did not hesitate at all. Is my reluctance about the fund simply irrationality dressed up as prudence, or is there a genuine rationale for treating known and unknown uncertainty so differently — and if so, why would it apply to the fund but not to the company?
