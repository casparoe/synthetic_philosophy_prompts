Okay so this has been bugging me for a bit. I saw a clip of some longevity scientist (I think it might've been David Sinclair or somebody from that whole anti-aging research scene, don't remember exactly) arguing that aging itself should officially get classified as a disease, because right now doctors can basically only treat you once you already have some specific age-related illness (heart disease, dementia, whatever) but there's no way to get a drug approved for "aging" itself, since regulators don't recognize it as a condition on its own. That sent me down a rabbit hole about how we even decide what counts as a "disease" in the first place, and I found out theres this whole disagreement between people who think disease is basically a biological, value-free fact (something like: a part of you isn't functioning the way its statistically supposed to for someone of your species/age/sex) versus people who think "disease" always secretly smuggles in a judgement that something is bad or unwanted, so you can never fully separate the biology from our values.

Aging seems like a really good test case for this fight, because it seems to break the "compare you to other people your age" move that the biology-only side usually leans on. If the whole reference group you're being compared against is also aging at roughly the same rate, then by definition you can never come out statistically abnormal for your age no matter how much your body has fallen apart, which seems to just build in the answer "aging is never a disease" before anyone's even looked at the facts. But then the values side has kind of the opposite problem: if the only reason we're calling aging "bad" is that we personally don't want to die, that seems to make disease-classification a matter of taste, which feels wrong too, since it seems like doctors aren't just handing out personal opinions when they diagnose stuff.

Could you write me a dialogue between two people hashing this out -- one who thinks disease can eventually be defined in a purely biological/statistical way with zero value judgments needed, and one who thinks values are unavoidably baked into the concept no matter what -- specifically arguing about whether aging itself should count as a disease? I don't want either of them to be a pushover: the biology-only person needs an actual answer to that "reference class" problem instead of dodging it, and the values person needs to explain why calling aging bad isn't just subjective taste, since it's supposed to be something doctors could be right or wrong about, not just an opinion. Please make it real back-and-forth where each one is actually responding to what the other just said, instead of both of them just restating their own opening speech over and over.
