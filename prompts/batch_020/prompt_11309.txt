I'm several chapters into a novel built around a matter-duplicator that malfunctions: rather than following its usual scan-and-destroy sequence, the machine scans but fails to destroy, so my protagonist's original body survives for a few disoriented minutes alongside her newly assembled duplicate, before a fault in the encoding laser kills the original outright. That evening, two secondary characters -- her sister, and the sister's ex, a philosophy postdoc who never quite left academia -- end up arguing over the estate, and I want their argument to track an actual fault line in the personal-identity literature rather than the generic "but which one is really her?" that every piece of teleporter fiction reaches for.

Could you compare how Derek Parfit and Robert Nozick would each analyze this kind of fission case? I have in mind Parfit's argument, from Reasons and Persons, that such cases show identity is not what matters in survival: since the protagonist can't be numerically identical to two people afterward, yet neither resulting branch seems any worse off than an ordinary survivor, what we actually care about must be something that can hold one-to-two, namely Relation R (psychological continuity and connectedness, with the right kind of cause). Set this against Nozick's closest-continuer theory from Philosophical Explanations, which tries to keep identity itself doing the real work by making it depend on whether a candidate is the uniquely closest continuer of the earlier person, so that a tie between equally good candidates blocks identity from holding at all.

I'm especially interested in whether Nozick's account actually escapes the problem Parfit thinks any identity-preserving theory faces here, or whether the extrinsicness that critics have pressed against closest-continuer views -- the idea that whether you count as the same person can turn on the mere existence of a rival claimant somewhere else entirely, rather than on anything intrinsic to you -- just reintroduces the same difficulty under another name. Given that, which of the two accounts would you actually want the sister to be operating with as she decides, that same evening, whether the duplicate is a co-heir or a stranger wearing her sibling's face?
