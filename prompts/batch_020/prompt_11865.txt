Me and my friend got into a whole argument last night about Frank Ramsey's "Epilogue" - the talk he gave to the Cambridge Apostles in 1925 that only got published after he died at 26. He's pushing back on the standard "universe is vast and indifferent, so none of this matters" pessimism, and his move is basically: "I don't feel the least humble before the vastness of the heavens," because stars can't think or love, and he says "my picture of the world is drawn in perspective, and not like a model to scale" - so people fill up the foreground and the stars shrink down to nothing. He runs the same trick on time, saying the heat-death of the universe is so far off that "its present value at compound interest is almost nothing."

My friend's take is that this is just Ramsey being a naturally cheerful guy and dressing his own mood up as an argument - he never actually explains why size or duration shouldnt bear on whether we matter, he just reports that he personally isn't bothered by it. I think theres an actual argument hiding in the perspective/scale-model image, something about importance never having been the kind of property that tracks size or duration to begin with. We went back and forth for like an hour and got nowhere.

Write an essay that actually settles it.
