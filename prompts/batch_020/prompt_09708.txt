Okay so I had a whole argument with my friend Sam last night about money-pump arguments and whether they actually prove anything about rationality. You know the setup -- if your preferences over some set of options are cyclic, like you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, then in principle someone can trade you around that loop and charge you a small fee each time, so you end up back with the same thing you started with but poorer. Sam's line was that this shows acyclicity (or transitivity, more generally) is a genuine rationality requirement, and that it has to be a diachronic one -- his word, not mine, he's doing a philosophy MA and never lets me forget it -- since the whole point of the pump is that it exploits you across a sequence of choices, not at any single instant. I tried to run the "resolute choice" reply on him: that an agent who sees the trap coming and commits up front to a plan for the whole sequence (like just refusing the first trade, or precommitting to walk away at some point) never actually gets pumped, so all the argument really shows is that naive, forgetful choosers are exploitable, not that cyclic preferences themselves are irrational. His comeback was that sticking to a commitment you made earlier, against what you currently prefer, is basically conceding the preferences are defective anyway -- otherwise why would you need to override them? We argued about this for almost an hour and neither of us landed a real knockout, which bugs me because hes insufferable when he thinks he's winning.

Can you write the dialogue we should've had? Two characters: one defending the money-pump argument as showing a genuine diachronic rationality requirement on the preferences themselves, and the other defending resolute choice as a way of showing the preferences can be left alone. Don't let either of them fold -- I want actual back-and-forth, each one responding to the other's specific last point instead of just restating their opening position. In particular, I want the resolute-choice defender to get hit with the "you're just overriding your own preference, which proves it's defective" objection Sam raised, and to give a real answer to it, not a dodge. Then -- I have this nagging memory that there's a nastier, more refined version of the money pump out there, specifically constructed to still catch an agent who's committed to a plan in advance -- so have the pump-defender bring something like that in partway through, and have the resolute-choice character respond to that too instead of just caving. It doesn't have to end in a resolution, but by the end I want it to be completely clear exactly what the two of them still disagree about.
