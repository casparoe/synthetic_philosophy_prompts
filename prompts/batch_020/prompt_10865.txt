I'm a software engineer in my early thirties, and for most of the last decade I've run my life like a well-managed project: I picked my degree for its earning potential, took my current job mainly for the equity and the promotion ladder rather than for the work itself, and even structured supposedly "fun" pursuits — running, a side project, a stack of industry newsletters — around what they would do for my health metrics or my resume. Two months ago I finally got the senior promotion I had been quietly engineering my whole schedule around for two years, and instead of satisfaction I felt almost nothing, which has stuck with me more than I expected. Around the same time, my old college roommate mentioned something I haven't been able to shake since. She spends her weekends repairing and restoring vintage synthesizers, has never sold or posted about a single one, and by any conventional measure has a far less impressive career and bank account than I do — yet she said, almost as an aside, that she couldn't imagine choosing what to do with a free afternoon based on where it leads rather than on whether she felt like doing it. I genuinely could not tell you the last time I did something for no further reason than wanting to do it in the moment.

When I brought this up with a close friend, she said it just sounds like ordinary burnout, and that the fix is to schedule real downtime and pick up a hobby that has nothing to do with work. I can't quite accept that answer, though, because "rest deliberately so you can perform better afterward" still sounds like the same underlying pattern I'm trying to describe, not a break from it. I don't have any background in philosophy, so I don't have great vocabulary for what's actually bothering me here, but it feels like something bigger than burnout. Is my friend right that this is a routine problem with a routine fix, or is there something genuinely off about building a life in which almost nothing — including the rest and hobbies meant to fix it — is done for its own sake rather than for the sake of what it leads to?
