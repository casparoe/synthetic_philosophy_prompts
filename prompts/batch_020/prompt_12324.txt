My dad is retiring next month after 34 years on the police force, started as a beat cop and ended up a shift captian, and I got asked to give the toast at his retirement dinner. Here's the complication: dad is a total self taught philosophy nerd, never took a class in his life, but he's had a beat up copy of Pascal's Pensees in his glovebox for like 20 years and would quote it at random moments, and NOT the "wager" bit everybody already knows. His favorite was this other section on justice and force — he'd recite it kind of proudly, something like (I'm probably mangling the exact wording) "justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical," and how since nobody could make what's just strong, people just made what's strong "just" instead, and that's basically what a law even is. He loved saying that about his own job too, half joking — "I'm not in the justice business son, I'm in the force business, justice is a different department." But he never left it there, he'd always follow it with this other idea of Pascal's about there being separate "orders" — like the order of bodies and force is one thing, and real charity or goodness is a totally different order entirely, and your not supposed to get those two mixed up in your head — and he'd say THAT part, not mixing them up, was the actual job he did for 34 years, even though a badge and a gun were his only tools.

The dinner's got like 60 people coming: a bunch of guys from his precinct who want something funny and warm about their captain, my mom and my aunts who could not care less about 17th century French philosophy, and also his old buddy from the community college night class where he first found this stuff, who will 100% notice if I chicken out and get it wrong. That's what's stressing me out — if I really lean into the "force without justice is tyrannical, we just decided the strong thing counts as just" line, it starts sounding like I'm saying dad spent his whole career enforcing made up rules and we're all better off not thinking too hard about it, which feels like a genuinely terrible thing to say about him at his own party, and would probably land badly with every cop in the room. But if I leave it out entirely I'm not really talking about my actual dad, and his buddy will clock the dodge instantly. Could you write the actual toast for me — something you could stand up and say out loud in about 5 minutes — that uses both halves of the Pascal stuff (the force fragment AND the orders fragment) as the real spine of the speech, in a way that's honest to what he actually believed, and comes across as a tribute instead of either a philosophy lecture or an accidental insult to everybody with a badge in the room?
