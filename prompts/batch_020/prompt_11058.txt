so my friend and i had a whole argument last night about the absent-minded driver problem (the decision theory thing where a driver's on a highway with two intersections that look completely identical, and he needs to exit at the second one to get home. problem is he can't tell the intersections apart and has no memory of whether he's already gone through the first one, so whatever probability of "continue" vs "exit" he picks has to be the same at both). exiting at the first one pays 0, driving through both pays 1, exiting at the correct second one pays 4, and if you maximize expected payoff over the whole plan from the outside, you get that he should continue with probability 2/3 at each intersection. we agree on all that math.

where we split is over what the 2/3 actually means. he says fine, that's the optimal plan, a rational agent just commits to it and executes, done. i said that's the optimal plan judged from outside, before he ever sets off - it doesn't automatically tell you what he should do once he's actually sitting at an intersection with no idea if its the first or the second. to judge "exit vs continue" from in there you need odds on which intersection you're at, and those odds aren't some independent fact about the road, they only exist once you've already assumed a strategy. so "just follow the plan" is kind of smuggling in an answer to the actual question, which is whether "the rational thing to do right now" is even well-defined for someone in his position.

he thinks im overthinking a problem that's already solved. can you build the strongest version of my side of this that you can?
