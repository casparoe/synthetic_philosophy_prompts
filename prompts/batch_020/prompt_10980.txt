Ok so this has been rattling around in my head for months now and my friends are kind of sick of hearing about it lol. My little brother has been flaking on stuff basically our whole adult lives -- no-shows, borrowed money he "forgot" to pay back, bailed on helping with our dad's move last year after promising three seperate times. For a long time I was just straightforwardly furious at him about all of it. Then about a year ago he got diagnosed with pretty severe ADHD, and around the same time I found out he'd had a way harder time with our parents' divorce as a kid than I ever realized (I'd already moved out by the time it really got bad). So now whenever I start to get mad at him, some part of my brain goes "well that's just his brain wiring, he's not doing it to hurt you on purpose," and the anger just kind of drains out of me before it goes anywhere. Except weirdly I feel worse after, not better -- it's like I've stopped relating to him as my actual brother and started relating to him more like a patient I have to case-manage, adjusting my expectations around his "condition" instead of actually being in a relationship with the guy.

My mom's take is I should just fully accept "that's who he is" and stop expecting anything different from him, basically the same way you'd stop being mad at a toddler for having a meltdown. But that feels less like forgiveness to me and more like quietly giving up on him as an actual grown adult who I could hold to anything at all. I've read alot of reddit threads about siblings and ADHD but they all just say "set boundaries," which doesn't really touch the actual thing I'm stuck on: once there's a real explanation for why someone behaves a certain way, does that automatically mean being angry at them was irrational the whole time? Or can you fully understand why someone does something and still, legitimately, be upset with them as a person and not just a bundle of malfunctioning parts? I feel like I'm missing some clearer way to think through this instead of just flip-flopping between "furious sibling" and "detached social worker" every time he does something. Anyone got an actual angle on this?
