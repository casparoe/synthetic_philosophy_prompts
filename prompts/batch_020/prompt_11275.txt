Early in Plato's Laws (books 1–2), the Athenian Stranger spends a weirdly long stretch defending organized, supervised drinking parties as a way to actually test and train people's self-control -- he compares it directly to how soldiers get tested through real exposure to danger. That seems in tension with the more familiar Platonic picture (Republic etc.) where virtue is basically reason keeping a tight leash on the appetites, since here hes recommending you deliberately loosen reason's grip in order to build character. Write an essay on whether this defense of regulated drunkenness is a coherent extension of Plato's moral psychology or a genuine inconsistency in his thought. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, but don't just land on "it's complicated" -- actually defend a verdict.
