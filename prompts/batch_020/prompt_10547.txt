I've spent the last few weeks reporting on why every recidivism-risk-scoring tool that courts adopt ends up in the exact same fight, and I keep coming back to the COMPAS/ProPublica saga as the cleanest example. ProPublica's investigation found the tool was flagging Black defendants who never went on to reoffend as "high risk" way more often than white defendants who never reoffended, while Northpointe (the company behind COMPAS) shot back that among people given any particular risk score, the actual reoffense rate was basically identical across races. The part that really got me is that both sides turn out to be right, and there's actual math behind it — the Kleinberg/Mullainathan/Raghavan paper and Chouldechova's paper, both from around 2016-17 — proving you can't have equal false-positive and false-negative rates across groups AND equal calibration within groups at the same time, unless the base rates of reoffending happen to be identical across those groups (they basically never are) or the model predicts with perfect accuracy.
