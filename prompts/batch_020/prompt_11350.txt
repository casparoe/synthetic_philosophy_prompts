So I just moved to Vienna for a few months for work, and did one of those free walking tours through the old Red Vienna housing blocks (Karl-Marx-Hof and all that). The guide went off on this tangent about how Otto Neurath was neck-deep in the whole settlement and housing-planning movement back in the day, and even ended up running a planning office for that short-lived socialist government in Munich in 1919. Which lead me down a rabbit hole on the Vienna Circle's politics, since up to that point I only knew Carnap as the "metaphysics-is-meaningless" guy from one undergrad phil of science class.

Now I can't stop noticing how quiet Carnap seems on all this next to Neurath. He calls himself a socialist in his autobiography and co-wrote that 1929 manifesto full of planning-friendly language, but as far as I can tell he never really worked out an actual position on planning or social organization the way Neurath did with his whole "calculate in kind, not money" economics. Can you compare Carnap's actual views on politics and planning with Neurath's? Is there a real substantive position there for Carnap beyond vague sympathy, and if so how's it different from Neurath's more full-blown technocratic vision — or is "Carnap's politics" basically just Neurath's politics with Carnap nodding along in the background?
