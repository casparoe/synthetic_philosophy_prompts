I'm a retired evolutionary biologist who's fallen down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole lately, and teleosemantics seemed right up my alley -- finally a theory of meaning that doesn't need anything spooky, just plain ol' natural selection. But the more I read Millikan the more it feels like a bait-and-switch: she says a mental state's content is fixed by a mechanism's "proper function," but a proper function just seems to be whatever the mechanism reliably or normally does. Frogs will snap at a BB pellet flicked across thier visual field just as readily as they snap at an actual fly, so by that logic the detector's content should just be "small dark moving thing." So why do philosophers still credit teleosemantics with solving the disjunction problem, when "proper function" just seems to be a fancy label for statistical normalcy?
