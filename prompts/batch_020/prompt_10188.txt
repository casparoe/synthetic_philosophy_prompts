For a piece I'm writing on how ideas from fundamental physics have migrated into analytic metaphysics, I keep coming back to "priority monism," the view, associated chiefly with the philosopher Jonathan Schaffer, that the entire cosmos is the only fundamental entity there is, and that every particle, planet, and person exists only in a dependent, derivative way, as a part of that one basic whole. One line of support for the view appeals to quantum entanglement: an entangled composite system can be in a perfectly definite joint state even though none of its subsystems, taken individually, is in any definite state at all, which suggests that in at least this case the properties of the parts are fixed by the whole rather than the whole being built up out of independently fixed parts. Please make the strongest case you can for priority monism along these lines, and address the natural objection that this reasoning reads a substantive claim about what is metaphysically fundamental off what might just be an artifact of how quantum states happen to be mathematically represented.
