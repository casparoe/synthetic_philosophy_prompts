As a PhD student in mathematical logic, I've been asked to give the reception toast for two close friends from the AI-safety and multi-agent-systems research community who are marrying next month. They met while co-authoring a paper on program equilibrium — where agents able to read each other's source code sustain cooperation that no unverifiable promise could — and their running joke is that they only really committed once each had, in effect, proven the other's cooperation the way FairBot proves cooperation of another copy of itself: not by simulating each other forever, but via the Löbian move that turns "I'll cooperate if I can be sure you will" from an infinite regress into a fixed point. About half the guests will place every reference immediately; the rest is family who have never heard of Löb's theorem. Please write a five-minute toast that makes that regress-breaking move its real spine rather than a punchline, stays honest that people are not source code and cannot verify each other that way, and still makes the case for why standing up and vowing this to each other, publicly, might do some of that work regardless.
