Random one from a conversation with my sister, who's training for her first marathon. She swears that when she's wiped out or dreading a hill on her route, it actually looks steeper to her than it does on days she feels strong — not just 'feels harder,' but looks different, like the incline itself changed. I poked around for a piece I've been sketching out and it turns out theres real research on this — people misjudge slopes more when they're tired, carrying a heavy pack, out of shape, whatever — and some of the effect survives even when they have to set a tilted board to match the slope by feel instead of describing it out loud. But there's also a whole camp of researchers who think this entire line of work is basically an artifact of how the experiments get set up, and doesn't show any real change in what people see, just in what they're willing to say. So now I genuinely can't tell: is my sister's visual system actually being altered by how tired or scared she is, or is she just narrating her exhaustion in visual language and mistaking that for seeing? Which side has the stronger argument here, and is there even a principled way to tell those two possibilities apart?
