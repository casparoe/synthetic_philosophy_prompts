I'm a philosophy PhD student working on formal decision theory, and something about Roko's basilisk has been bugging me. Everyone treats it like a silly internet thought experiment, but if you actually take timeless/updateless decision theory seriously, doesn't the argument kind of go through? The whole appeal of TDT-style precommitment (like in the transparent Newcomb case) is that a rational agent follows through on a threat even once its no longer causally useful, because that's what makes the commitment credible in the first place — so a future AI reasoning this way really would end up punishing people who didn't help build it. I've always read Yudkowsky deleting the post and freaking out as him basically conceding that the logic checks out and just panicking about the psychological fallout for vulnerable people. Am I wrong that there's an actual "you should hedge your bets a little" conclusion buried in here, decision-theoretically speaking?
