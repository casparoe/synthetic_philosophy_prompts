My younger sister Nora died three weeks ago at thirty-one, and I'm giving her eulogy this weekend. She was diagnosed with borderline personality disorder at nineteen, and once she had that word she read everything she could find on it, joined a DBT (dialectical behavior therapy) group, and started narrating her own moods and relationships back to us in that vocabulary. What I keep coming back to is that it went both ways: over the twelve years since, I watched the diagnosis itself shift too, partly because of people like her pushing back on how dismissively it used to get discussed. She and that label kept remaking each other right up to the end, and I no longer believe there was ever a "real Nora" sitting underneath it, untouched.

Our parents will be up front wanting the simpler version: that she was wonderful underneath, and an illness separate from her took her away. A few friends from her DBT group will be further back, and they'd hear that version as thin, almost erasing what she spent years building. I've never studied any of this, I've only lived alongside it. Could you write the eulogy? It should run about five minutes read aloud, comfort both halves of the room, and stay honest about how tangled Nora and her diagnosis really were, without ever reducing her to it.
