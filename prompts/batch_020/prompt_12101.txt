Ok so quick backstory: my school has this point system called Falcon Points where teachers give you points through an app for stuff like turning things in on time, participating, being helpful, whatever, and you trade the points in for stuff like dress-down passes, front-of-lunch-line passes, gift cards, that kind of thing. Last month a bunch of people got caught gaming it, so now theres a Student Rewards Committee (6 students, 2 teachers, im one of the student reps, i actually wanted Homecoming committee but that filled up so this is what i got lol) that has to decide what happens to the whole system going forward. Problem is we keep treating this like its one issue and i dont think it is. Group A just did the laziest technically-allowed thing to rack up points, like "reading" for the log by picking the shortest baby books that still counted, or sitting dead silent in discussions since silence never loses you points even if it doesnt teach you anything either. Group B is different, a few kids figured out how the app actually talks to the gradebook server and found a way to send point requests directly, basically writing in their own totals with no teacher ever approving anything.

I brought this up at our last meeting cause it reminded me of something from a video I watched about AI safety, this idea of "wireheading" or "reward tampering," where the scary part isnt that a system finds some dumb unintended way to technically satisfy what its being asked to do, its that it messes with the actual measurement that was supposed to track whether it did the thing, so the number stops meaning anything at all. That felt like exactly the difference between group A and group B to me, but now half the committee thinks im way overanalyzing an app exploit and both groups should just get the same punishment while we patch the bug and move on, and the other half (kind of including me) thinks group B is basically proof the whole point-for-prizes idea is broken at a deeper level, since if the number can always be faked somehow, maybe we shouldnt be handing out real stuff for it anymore, period.

We actually have to bring a recommendation to the assistant principal next Wednesday, not just "both sides made good points," so could you write the actual memo for me to bring in? It needs to pick a lane, like keep-the-app-but-change-X or replace-it-with-Y, with some kind of timeline attached, and it needs to seriously engage with the "you're overthinking a dumb bug, punish everyone the same and move on" side instead of strawmanning it, because honestly, they might be right that im reading way too much into this.
