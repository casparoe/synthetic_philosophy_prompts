Over the past year I've drifted, as a lot of software engineers seem to, into biohacking: tracking macros, running an eight-week continuous glucose monitor experiment on myself, and cycling through 16:8 and occasional 24-hour fasts to see what it does to my focus during long coding sessions. A friend from a philosophy-adjacent book club I sit in on, half-mocking this hobby, handed me Nietzsche's Ecce Homo and pointed me specifically to the chapter "Why I Am So Clever."

I hadn't expected a nineteenth-century philosopher to sound so much like a biohacker himself. In that chapter Nietzsche goes on in surprisingly clinical detail about his own digestion, diet, choice of climate, and daily rhythms: he blames a good deal of the "German spirit's" heaviness on rich food and beer, praises the lighter cuisine he found in Turin during the last productive year before his collapse, and insists that questions of nutrition, place, climate, and recreation are decisive for a thinker's output in a way earlier philosophers, busy with "higher" questions like the salvation of the soul, had unaccountably ignored.

What I can't settle in my own head is how this squares with the Nietzsche of the Genealogy of Morals, who spends much of the Third Essay arguing that ascetic self-denial — dietary austerity very much included — is more often than not a disguised expression of the "ascetic ideal," a life-denying trick the weak play on themselves, even while conceding that a genuinely strong, creative nature might impose similar-looking discipline on itself for entirely different, self-affirming reasons. I don't have any formal training in philosophy, so I'd appreciate help reasoning through this more carefully than I've managed alone: based specifically on what Nietzsche wrote in those two texts, what do you think his actual verdict on today's intermittent-fasting and quantified-self biohacking culture would have been, and, more importantly, what criteria would he have used to separate the version of it he'd have respected as genuine self-mastery from the version he'd have dismissed as just another herd morality dressed up in the language of optimization?
