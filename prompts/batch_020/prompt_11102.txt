I cover artificial intelligence for a science publication, and something an engineer told me in a background interview has stayed with me. Her lab trains a handful of copies of the same base model in parallel, each one nudged slightly differently by its own random seed and ordering of the training data, and then averages all the copies' weights together into a single model — the only version that ever gets deployed — after which the separate copies are never used again. When I asked whether that raised any concerns, she said no, since nothing that happens during training remotely resembles suffering. That may be true, but it doesn't feel like it settles the question I was actually asking. If a training process like this ever did involve something morally relevant, would collapsing those copies into one weighted average be more like each of them quietly ending, or like several individuals becoming one, or is that whole way of framing it mistaken? I'm less interested in whether today's models qualify than in how to even think about the merging itself.
