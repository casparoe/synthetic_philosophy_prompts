I have no formal training in the history of science -- I'm an electrician by trade -- but after seeing a documentary on Newton's private papers, I spent much of the past year working through his theological manuscripts on the Newton Project website, along with a couple of the standard biographies, purely out of personal curiosity. Our local library's "living history" evening has cast me as Newton for a public Q&A next month, and I want the questions my interviewer puts to me to go well beyond the apple-and-alchemy trivia everyone already knows. Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a short follow-up keyed to the kind of hedge or deflection Newton would most plausibly offer. I'd like at least one question on the gulf between his private conviction that Athanasius and the Council of Nicaea had corrupted a purer, non-Trinitarian Christianity, and his own careful outward conformity as the Lucasian professor who avoided ordination only by royal dispensation; one on why he drafted "An Historical Account of Two Notable Corruptions of Scripture" for Locke and then lost his nerve about letting it reach print; and one on how he reconciles his contempt for those who set dates for the apocalypse with his own arithmetic -- 1,260 years on from what he took to be the start of the papacy's temporal reign around AD 800 -- which puts a floor of 2060 under the end of the world. End with a question asking him, in his own judgment, whether his decades of silence protected the truth he believed he'd found, or simply let it die with him.
