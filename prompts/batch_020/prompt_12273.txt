As a PhD student in cognitive psychology, I turned a lab happy hour into an impromptu experiment that has left a friend and me at an impasse. I gave her two cups of the same drip coffee but told her, falsely, that one was a rare single-origin lot and the other our everyday office blend; she tasted both carefully and described the "rare" cup as bright and floral with a clean finish, and the "ordinary" one as flat and slightly bitter. When I revealed the cups were identical, she refused to concede that she'd simply rationalized a difference after the fact — she insists the two actually tasted different to her in the moment, before she'd settled on any verdict. I can't decide how to adjudicate that. Could my story have reached into what she actually tasted, rather than just coloring how she judged or later remembered an experience that was the same both times? And if a fabricated backstory can reach that far, should it shake our confidence in first-hand tasting impressions generally, even when — as in judging a blind competition — no one is lying to us?
