In my friend group, birthdays have turned into a whole production, and I've been trying to work out whether there's a real reason behind it or whether I'm just overthinking ordinary teenage social pressure. The unwritten rule is that when it's someone's birthday, you're expected to post a long, carefully written tribute to them on social media, ideally with old photos and specific inside jokes, explaining what they mean to you. A quick "happy birthday" comment or a private text doesn't seem to count in the same way, even if you spent the whole day with that person and the text was completely sincere. I've noticed that people will privately describe a friendship as extremely close while still quietly judging someone who doesn't post something longer, and a friend of mine was clearly hurt this year when someone she considers one of her best friends left only a two-word comment under her photo.

I don't really take part in this, partly because writing an elaborate public post for every friend's birthday feels performative to me, and partly because it seems strange that a paragraph you can write in ten minutes counts as stronger proof of caring than things that actually cost real time and effort, like showing up for someone consistently or keeping their secrets. At the same time, I notice that I feel a small sting when someone I thought was close to me doesn't bother writing anything for my birthday, so this practice is clearly tapping into something real for me too, even though I can't fully justify it.

I'd like to understand what is actually going on here, rather than simply being told to stop caring what people think or to just write the posts because they don't really matter. Is there a good reason why this particular, fairly low-effort public gesture ends up functioning as a more convincing indication of friendship than equally or more effortful private ones, or am I right that this is a fairly empty social performance that I shouldn't read too much into?
