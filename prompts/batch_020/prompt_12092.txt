A friend and I spent much of last night arguing about whether "goal misgeneralization" names a genuinely new failure mode or is simply the "inner alignment" problem under a different name. My friend maintained that any system which competently pursues the wrong goal out of distribution is, by definition, a misaligned mesa-optimizer in Hubinger et al.'s original sense, so the newer terminology adds nothing. I countered that the papers introducing goal misgeneralization go out of their way to avoid assuming that the learned policy implements anything like an internal search process, and that this is a substantive rather than merely verbal difference.

Please compare the mesa-optimization framing of inner alignment with the goal misgeneralization framing. In particular, address whether goal misgeneralization presupposes mesa-optimization, what is gained or lost by characterizing the failure behaviorally rather than mechanistically, and, in your judgment, which of us was closer to right.
