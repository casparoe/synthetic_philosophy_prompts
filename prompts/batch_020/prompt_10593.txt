Write a dialogue between Kepler and a traditional astrologer of his day, set sometime after Kepler published his Tertius Interveniens. The astrologer's complaint: Kepler trashed zodiac signs, houses, and planetary rulerships as baseless guesswork, yet he still insists planetary aspects push around the weather and the human soul through some harmony-of-the-spheres mechanism tied to the souls feel for geometric ratios -- so how is that not just as made up, only dressed in fancier math? Kepler has to actually defend where he draws that line instead of dodging it or leaning on hindsight about which bits of astrology "held up." Give the astrologer real ammo too (tradition, track record, professional experience) -- I don't want Kepler winning just because he's the one we remember.
