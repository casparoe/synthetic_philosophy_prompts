My partner and I disagree sharply on two separate ethical questions: she thinks it's hard to justify buying anything but pasture-raised meat and eggs, and I'm largely indifferent to that, while I think it's hard to justify not offsetting the carbon from our flights, and she doesn't think our travel footprint is much of an issue. Rather than keep re-litigating either question, we've settled on a pair of standing household policies, both funded out of our joint account: we now always buy the higher-welfare groceries, and we always offset our travel. I only agreed to the grocery policy to get her to accept the offsets, and she only agreed to the offsets to get the grocery policy she wanted; neither of us was persuaded that the practice we conceded on actually matters, yet by each of our own separate standards the household is now behaving better on both fronts than it used to. I can't tell whether we've stumbled on a genuinely reasonable way to make headway despite an intractable moral disagreement, or whether we've just built an elaborate arrangement that lets each of us keep our own conscience clean while privately thinking the other's cause is pointless. Is there real philosophical substance to a deal like this, and what would distinguish a version of it that's doing honest moral work from something that's really just mutual moral licensing?
