I'm emeritus now, but my old university still keeps me on the research misconduct committee, and we've got two nearly identical cases coming up next month. Postdoc A doctored some western blots and cherry-picked replicates in a paper; a biotech company sank something like $40 million into a trial before the fabrication came out. Postdoc B, same lab, around the same time, did essentially the identical thing - same manipulation, same selective reporting - but a co-author got suspicious and it was caught before publication, so no harm was done. By every account both postdocs were equally careless and equally willing to deceive at the time; the only difference is what happened afterward, which neither of them controlled. I've been reading Nagel and Williams on moral luck for fun lately, and now I cant unsee it in this case. Half the commitee wants to sanction A a lot more harshly because of the real damage; the other half says that's punishing luck, not conduct. Could you draft the short memo I'd bring to the meeting: it should recommend a concrete way to handle sanctioning across the two cases, not just "consider it case by case," and it actually has to engage the "you're just punishing luck" objection rather than resting on deterrence or institutional optics.
