A little over two years after my mother's death, I still think about her and cry most days, and a psychiatrist recently gave me a formal diagnosis of "prolonged grief disorder." When I mentioned this to a friend, he argued that the diagnosis doesn't pick out any actual dysfunction in me; it just relabels an unusually long and intense, but perfectly natural, experience of loss as a medical condition because other people find it hard to be around. That struck me as too quick, but I couldn't articulate why grief should be treated any differently from, say, a broken bone that heals unusually slowly and painfully, which we wouldn't hesitate to call a real problem regardless of how anyone feels about it. We went back and forth for a couple of hours and never resolved it. Is my friend right that there's no principled difference here, or is there some way to distinguish a genuine disorder from a label we simply attach to feelings or behavior that other people find hard to deal with?
