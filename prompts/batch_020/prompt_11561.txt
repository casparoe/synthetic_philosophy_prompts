I'm a PhD student in political science (I work on voting behavior, not formal theory), and this year I was elected the graduate-student representative on my university's five-member Academic Integrity Board. We just heard a case of alleged unauthorized AI use on a take-home exam. Our policy defines a 'responsible' finding as requiring majority agreement on both (a) that the student's use went beyond what was permitted, and (b) that she knew or should have known this. Voting on the case as a whole, three of five members found her not responsible. Polling the two conditions separately afterward, however, a majority affirmed (a) and a differently composed majority affirmed (b) -- which, by the policy's own rule, would make her responsible. The Board can't agree which vote actually binds us: the chair wants to certify the original result and close the file, while two members say that ignores our own written policy. I've been asked to draft the short memo framing this dispute for the full Board before we reconvene. Could you write it? It should explain plainly why the two tallies diverge, recommend a standing procedure for cases like this and state what it implies here, and fairly address the strongest case for the option you reject.
