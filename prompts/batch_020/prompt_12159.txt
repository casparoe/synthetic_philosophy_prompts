So I'm prepping a session on Kepler for my history-and-philosophy-of-science seminar next semester, and my TA and I keep going back and forth on how to frame his astrology for the students. Everyone's heard the cliche version - brilliant astronomer forced to moonlight as an astrologer to pay the bills - and I really don't want that to be the takeaway, because it skips over how much actual philosophical work Kepler did to seperate his own practice from the tradition he inherited. In De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus, and then at much greater length in the Tertius Interveniens, he throws out most of the received apparatus - the zodiacal signs, the houses, dignities and exaltations, alot of the nativity-casting machinery - as basically groundless conventions of the human mind, while still insisting on rescuing the aspects, on the grounds that the angles planets make to one another correspond to the same ratios that produce musical consonance, and that the soul - his own, and, he thought, the earth's - has a built in geometric instinct that responds directly to those ratios, no zodiac backdrop required. He even adds a couple of aspects to the traditional set, based on that same harmonic reasoning, that have no precedent in the older astrology at all, which is the detail that started this whole argument in the first place.

My TA's position is that all this amounts to is astrology with the embarrassing parts filed off - same basic enterprise, Kepler was just choosier about which bits he'd defend in print, and the new aspects don't change that. Mine is that grounding the aspects in harmonics turns it into a different kind of theory even where the resulting predictions overlap with what a straightforwardly Ptolemaic astrologer would say. Can you help settle this? Compare Kepler's reformed astrology to the general, inherited tradition he was pushing back against - Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos is the obvious reference point - and tell me specifically what, if anything, is structurally distinctive about Kepler's version, rather than just "he kept less of it." Then tell me who's got the better of the argument, me or my TA - don't just split the difference to be diplomatic about it.
