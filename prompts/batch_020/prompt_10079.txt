When I presented an early version of the scoring function at a lab seminar, a philosopher on our advisory board remarked, half-amused, that we had reinvented Karl Popper's problem of verisimilitude — and that Popper's own content-based definition of truthlikeness was shown by Miller and Tichý in 1974 to make it impossible for any false theory ever to be closer to the truth than another false theory. Having spent the past week working through the repair proposals that followed, I would like an outside, structured judgment on how well they actually solved the problem before my team commits to a framework for our metric.
