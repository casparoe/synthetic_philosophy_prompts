I'm a philosophy major taking a seminar on Kierkegaard this term, and we've just spent several weeks on Two Ages and his diagnosis of the public, the press, and the leveling and chatter he thinks they produce. I also sit on the editorial board of my university's independent student newspaper, which has always let readers comment anonymously online. A recent story about a contested tenure denial quoted two graduate students, by name, criticizing the department's decision, and anonymous commenters piled on so viciously that one of them has since asked never to be quoted by name in our paper again. At Thursday's meeting we vote on what to do: half the board wants all comments tied to verified real names going forward, and half insists that anonymity is the only reason students dare criticize powerful professors and administrators in our pages at all. Please draft the recommendation memo I'll bring to that meeting. It should take a clear position -- keep the current policy, end it, or replace it with something specific; it should ground that position in Kierkegaard's actual account of anonymity and responsibility rather than generic internet-safety talking points; and it should take seriously the strongest argument against whichever option it recommends.
