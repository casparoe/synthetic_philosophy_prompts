I have no formal training in physics or philosophy, but over the last year or so I have spent a good deal of my free time reading popular physics books and watching recorded lectures online, and I keep returning to Newton's bucket experiment because I had assumed it was a puzzle with a fairly tidy resolution by now. As I understand the setup, Newton hung a bucket of water from a twisted rope and let it unwind: the water's surface stays flat while the bucket spins around it, and only becomes concave once the water itself starts rotating along with the bucket, at which point there is no longer any relative motion between the water and the bucket at all. Newton took this to mean that the concavity must track the water's rotation relative to space itself, since it plainly is not tracking rotation relative to the bucket. Mach's objection, as far as I can tell, was that the water is not rotating relative to empty space but relative to all the other matter in the universe, the distant stars and so on, and my understanding has always been that Einstein built general relativity specifically to cash out this idea, eliminating Newton's absolute, empty space in favor of a purely relational picture in which motion, including rotation, only ever means motion relative to other material bodies.

Given how well confirmed general relativity is, it seems to me that physics has already settled the old philosophical dispute between Newton and Leibniz squarely in favor of relationism: space, or spacetime, is nothing over and above the relations among material things, and Newton's absolute container has gone the way of phlogiston. What I don't understand, then, is what contemporary substantivalists think they are holding onto. I raised this with a couple of friends recently, and none of us could work out whether physicists and philosophers who still treat substantivalism versus relationism as an open question are simply behind on the physics, or whether there is some more serious reason that general relativity hasn't closed the debate in the way I have always assumed it had. I would appreciate a thorough explanation of what is actually going on here.
