Write a dialogue between two philosophers of science at a conference bar, arguing about whether Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity actually rescue Occam's razor or just relocate the problem. One of them thinks the invariance theorem finally makes "simplicity" rigorous and machine-independent, closing the gap Hume left open. The other thinks its a shell game -- invariance only holds up to an additive constant fixed by whichever universal Turing machine you pick, that constant doesn't shrink and can swamp any dataset we'll actually ever see, and "just pick a sensible machine" smuggles back in exactly the kind of contestable, non-formal judgment call the theorem was supposed to make unnecessary (basically Goodman's grue problem wearing a compiler as a costume). Make them actually respond to each other's strongest move -- like the "it washes out asymptotically" defense, and whether that's good enough for anything finite -- instead of just repeating their own side. Keep it fairly short, like a page.
