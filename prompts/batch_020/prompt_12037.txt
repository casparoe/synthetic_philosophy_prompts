A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about anthropic reasoning without ever resolving the disagreement at the heart of it. She thinks the "presumptuousness" of Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher case is really just an artifact of how lopsided the two theories' observer populations are in the original version — they differ by many orders of magnitude — and that shrinking that gap would stop the Self-Indication Assumption's (SIA) verdict from looking absurd. I think the objection is structural rather than a matter of degree, and should survive even when the population ratio between the theories is quite modest. Construct a version of the Presumptuous Philosopher scenario with a much smaller disparity between the two theories' predicted observer counts than Bostrom's original, work out the credences that the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and SIA would each assign in it, and use the result to say which of us has the better argument.
