So I've been on a bit of a deep dive through old Alignment Forum / LessWrong threads about coherence arguments and whether they actually license the "sufficiently advanced AI will end up an expected-utility-maximizer with some goal" conclusion that people like Omohundro and early Yudkowsky lean on so heavily for the instrumental convergence story. The pro-coherence side is basically the standard money-pump/Dutch-book move: if your preferences aren't complete and transitive, someone can exploit you with a sequence of trades that leaves you strictly worse off than where you started, so anything that survives long enough (or gets to redesign itself) should end up coherent in the VNM sense. But then I reread Rohin Shah's old post — "Coherence arguments do not entail goal-directed behavior," I think it's called — making the point that this is almost vacuous, since you can always cook up a degenerate utility function defined over entire histories that just rubber-stamps whatever the agent actually did, so "coherent" doesn't get you "goal-directed" in the sense that actually matters for AI risk. And then theres a further wrinkle I ran into somewhere (I want to say it's Elliot Thornley's stuff on incomplete preferences and the shutdown problem, but honestly I might be misremembering) about how the money-pump argument for the completeness axiom specifically doesn't even work, because an agent can just refuse to trade away from its current option and dodge the pump, which would mean rational agents don't even need complete preferences, let alone a tidy utility function.

Anyway I'd love to see this fleshed out as an actual dialogue instead of me just listing the moves in my head at 1am. Can you write a conversation between two people at, like, a rationalist meetup afterparty, a bit tipsy and way too invested — one of them thinks the coherence-implies-goal-directedness argument basically survives all this and is still the main reason to expect instrumentally convergent behavior in advanced AI, and the other thinks the whole edifice is way shakier than the community treats it and that people should stop citing coherence theorems as load-bearing for alignment arguments. I want them to actually engage with each other's strongest specific points (the trivial-utility-function objection, the incomplete-preferences escape from the money pump, whatever else feels relevant) instead of just re-asserting their priors louder, and it's fine — good, even — if one of them ends up conceding some narrow point to the other by the end without fully caving on the big-picture disagreement.
