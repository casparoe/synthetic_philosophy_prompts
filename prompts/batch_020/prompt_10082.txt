I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been reading around the free will and moral responsibility literature on my own, and I keep running into the name Strawson in connection with what seems to be a single, oddly self-undermining position. On one hand, Strawson is credited with the argument that genuine, buck-stopping moral responsibility is impossible, since no one can be the ultimate author of the character and circumstances that cause them to act as they do. On the other hand, the same name is attached to the claim that we are nonetheless right to carry on with our ordinary practice of resentment, gratitude, and blame — the so-called reactive attitudes — because these responses are simply a natural and practically inescapable part of human psychology that arguments about determinism can't touch. Taken together, this reads like proving that responsibility is an illusion and then quietly smuggling it back in through the side door. Is there a way of reading Strawson that makes this consistent, or is it simply a contradiction in his position?
