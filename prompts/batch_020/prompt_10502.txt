Longtime LessWrong reader here, and I've never found the standard dismissal of Roko's basilisk fully convincing. Logical decision theories like UDT and FDT hold that an agent who reliably follows through on costly commitments does better than one who doesn't. That's the whole lesson of Parfit's hitchhiker: you have to actually pay the driver once you're safely in town, even though paying can no longer causally help you, precisely because only the type of agent who pays ever gets picked up in the first place. So shouldn't a sufficiently rational future AI, reasoning under the same logic, conclude that it ought to follow through on acausally punishing those who failed to help bring it into existence, given that being the kind of agent who follows through is exactly what would have made the original threat work? I've never seen anyone spell out the actual disanalogy that's supposed to block this line of reasoning — what am I missing?
