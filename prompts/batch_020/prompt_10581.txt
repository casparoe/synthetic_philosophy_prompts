I'm teaching a graduate seminar on population ethics this term, and we just spent two sessions on Arrhenius's impossibility theorems -- the result showing that, modulo some fairly plausible-looking axioms, any population axiology has to accept one of three ugly conclusions: Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion, the Anti-Egalitarian Conclusion, or the Sadistic Conclusion. Everyone in the room was pretty gloomy by the end, especially once it clicked that the standard move for avoiding the Repugnant Conclusion (something like average or critical-level utilitarianism) is basically what forces you into the Sadistic Conclusion, wich is the one where adding some people with lives not worth living can count as better than adding a bunch of happy ones instead. Honestly the names alone crack me up a little.

For next week's class I want to open with an actual joke about this -- not just a generic "philosophy is depressing" one-liner, but something where the punchline genuinely depends on getting that specific trade-off (repugnant vs. sadistic, or the three-way impossibility result more broadly). Basically, a student who's done the reading should get more out of it than someone who's only vaguely heard the phrase "repugnant conclusion" before. One or two jokes is plenty, it doesn't need to be a whole bit.
