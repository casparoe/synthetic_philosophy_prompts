Write a short dialogue between two people who both fully buy the many-worlds interpretation, standing in front of a quantum suicide machine -- you know, the classic thought experiment, except this one actually works: pull the lever and a quantum coinflip painlessly kills you in the branches where it lands one way, and pays out $10,000 in the branches where it lands the other. One of them is about to pull it and thinks that's obviously rational, since subjectively you'll only ever find yourself having survived; the other is trying to talk them out of it, arguing that the branches where you die still happen, Born-rule weight and all, and 'there's no one left to mind it' doesn't make that weight go away. Get them actually arguing the mechanics -- what work 'measure' is doing for each side, whether this is really just the Sleeping Beauty problem wearing a lab coat -- rather than swapping intuitions, and don't let either one back down just to wrap up the scene.
