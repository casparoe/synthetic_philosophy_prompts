Robin Hanson and other writers in the rationalist blogosphere frequently invoke Zahavi's handicap principle to explain why certain costly displays function as honest signals: a peacock's tail, a lavish wedding, or a startup founder forgoing salary is trustworthy precisely because a lower-quality signaler could not afford to fake it. If that is the whole mechanism, then it seems the right heuristic for evaluating any purported honest signal is simply to ask how expensive or wasteful it is, on the logic that the costlier and more extravagant the display, the harder it is to fake and therefore the more it should be trusted. Is that a fair extension of the handicap principle, or is there a distinction built into the theory that this heuristic glosses over?
