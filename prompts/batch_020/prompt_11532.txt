For years I've explained my habit of avoiding ambitious opportunities — choosing the safest major available, never submitting a fellowship application I was clearly qualified for — by telling myself I'm simply "not a risk-taker." It has always felt like honest self-knowledge rather than an excuse. A close friend recently disagreed sharply: there is no such fixed fact about me, she argued, and I could just as easily decide, this afternoon, to submit that application, which would make my "not a risk-taker" story just a comfortable way of dodging responsibility for choices I keep making anyway. I find her argument appealing but also strangely glib about how real my habits and past choices actually are. My existentialism seminar has just spent two weeks on Sartre, leaving me newly attentive to questions of freedom and self-deception, yet I still can't tell whether she is right that I am hiding behind a fiction, whether she is underselling how real my situation actually is, or whether there is a better way to frame this than either option.
