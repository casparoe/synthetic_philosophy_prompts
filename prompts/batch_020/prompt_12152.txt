In my seminar on Marx's early writings, we have been closely studying "Estranged Labour" in the 1844 Manuscripts, where he distinguishes several interlocking dimensions of alienation: from the product of one's labor, from the act of production itself, from one's "species-being," and from other people. For my term paper, I want to test that framework against a case Marx obviously never anticipated: generative AI models trained on the scraped public work of writers and visual artists, then sold to firms that use them to replace or underbid those same artists. Walking through each of the four dimensions in turn, how do you think Marx would have analyzed this arrangement -- would he have treated it as simply a further instance of the mechanization he discusses elsewhere, or as alienating people from their species-being in a more direct way, given that what is being appropriated is the free creative activity itself rather than routinized manual tasks? I would also like your view on whether the scenario counts as "exploitation" in his specific technical sense, given that there is no wage contract between the artists and the company doing the scraping, or whether it sits more naturally under his category of primitive accumulation.
