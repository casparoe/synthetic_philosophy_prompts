I'm taking a graduate seminar that pairs classic 4E cognitive science with contemporary AI-safety research, and this week's readings have left me with a question. Andy Clark and the embedded/extended cognition theorists argue that thought is not confined to the skull but is shaped by, and partly realized in, an organism's body and environment, against internalist, brain-bound pictures of mind. Scott Garrabrant and Abram Demski's "embedded agency" framework at MIRI seems to make exactly the same move for artificial agents, rejecting the picture of an agent that sits cleanly outside the environment it optimizes over. Given that philosophers have already spent decades defending essentially this embeddedness thesis, why does MIRI's agent-foundations program treat problems like self-reference, logical uncertainty, and an agent's trust in its own future self-modifications (the "tiling" problem) as novel technical puzzles requiring new decision theory, rather than as applications of results already established in philosophy of cognitive science? Is there an actual difference between what these two literatures mean by "embedded," or is agent foundations mostly re-deriving an existing philosophical position in new vocabulary for a different audience?
