Write a dialogue between a traditionalist and a revisionist just war theorist on the moral equality of combatants. The traditionalist holds, in the spirit of Michael Walzer, that any soldier who fights within the rules of jus in bello has an equal right to kill enemy combatants, whatever the justice of the cause they serve. The revisionist holds, in the spirit of Jeff McMahan, that this equality is illusory: a combatant's moral right to kill depends on whether the threat they pose is itself unjust, so a soldier fighting for an unjust cause can at best be excused, never justified, in killing those who oppose them. Ground the exchange in a single case. A soldier has reflected carefully on the justice of their cause and has good, though ultimately misleading, reason to believe their side is fighting defensively; in fact their state is waging a war of aggression. They fight skillfully and lawfully, and kill a defending soldier in an ordinary firefight. Have the revisionist press the traditionalist on what, besides convenience, could ground a genuine permission rather than a mere excuse in this case. Have the traditionalist press the revisionist on whether the excuse/permission distinction can survive contact with the practical business of actually fighting and constraining real wars. Neither side should concede the point too easily.
