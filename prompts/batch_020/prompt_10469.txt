My younger brother is getting married next month. He's a professional mentalist who also runs corporate workshops on the psychology of persuasion, and he's asked me — his older sibling and a philosophy PhD student — to give the toast. Half the room will be his clients and fellow performers, who know him mainly as someone who makes a living getting people to believe and do things they otherwise wouldn't; the other half will be my own philosophy cohort, several of whom work on manipulation and consent and will not let it pass if I describe his courtship in language that makes it sound like he talked his fiancée into loving him. His own defense, offered half-joking, is that he's never pulled a trick on her that she wasn't already in on. I'd like the toast to build on that line — using the thought that manipulation, unlike legitimate persuasion, cannot survive full disclosure to its target — to explain, warmly and without lecturing, why nothing about winning her over was manipulation. Please write the full five-minute toast.
