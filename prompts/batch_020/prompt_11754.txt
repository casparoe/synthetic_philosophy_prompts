For a chapter of my dissertation on rational choice under risk, I've been comparing Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility (REU) theory, developed in her 2013 book *Risk and Rationality*, with John Quiggin's earlier rank-dependent utility (RDU) theory from 1982, and I want to pin down what, if anything, philosophically separates them once their shared mathematics is set aside. Both theories replace the simple probability-weighted average of expected utility theory with a transform of the decumulative probabilities of ranked outcomes, so that an agent's evaluation of a gamble depends on its overall shape and not merely on the curvature of her utility function. Quiggin introduced this structure descriptively, to accommodate Allais-type preferences while preserving consistency with stochastic dominance; Buchak instead derives the very same structure from a weakened version of expected utility theory's independence axiom and takes it to show that such preferences can be fully rational, not just commonly observed. Given that formal equivalence, does Buchak's axiomatic route actually earn her that stronger normative conclusion, or is REU best understood as Quiggin's descriptive model restated in the vocabulary of rationality? Please defend whichever reading you find more plausible.
