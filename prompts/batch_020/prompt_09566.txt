I brought up Taurek's old "Should the Numbers Count?" paper, where he argues that you can't actually just add up badness across different people like that, because a death is only ever bad *for* the person who dies -- there's no "view from nowhere" where five deaths make a bigger blob of badness than one death -- so nothing forces you to prefer saving the five, and he even suggests flipping a coin to give the one person and the five-as-a-group an equal shot, since that treats everyone's claim with equal respect instead of just crunching numbers. Jonas was definitly not having it, and countered with something like "well if you didn't know in advance whether you'd end up in the group of one or the group of five, you'd obviously want the policy to be 'save the greater number', so thats the fair rule" -- which I think is basically Scanlon's move, though neither of us remembered whose argument it actually was until after he left and I looked it up.
