A friend of mine, who has no particular interest in philosophy, volunteers weekly at a homeless shelter and has always taken this to be one of the more straightforwardly good things she does with her time. She recently ran into an argument that unsettled her: that any action, however small, sets off a chain of further effects — who someone happens to run into afterward, what mood they're in, what they do differently as a result — that ramifies indefinitely into the future in ways no one could ever trace, so that she has no real way of knowing whether her volunteering nudges the long-run future toward more suffering rather than less. She hasn't stopped volunteering, but now says she can no longer defend her sense that it's good, only that it feels good to her. My instinct is that her original confidence was reasonable and that the argument doesn't show what she thinks it shows, but I'm finding it surprisingly hard to say where, if anywhere, it actually goes wrong. Is her confidence undermined by this, or is there a flaw in the argument that we're both missing?
