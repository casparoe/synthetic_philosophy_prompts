A friend and I have spent the last couple of years building a statistical model for a fairly niche category of sports wagers, initially just as a way to put our shared background in statistics to some use outside our day jobs. Backtesting against several seasons of results has convinced us the model has found a genuine, persistent edge: on this type of bet, which happens to pay even money, our model puts the true probability of winning at about 55 percent rather than the 50 percent the price implies, so a winning bet doubles our stake and a losing one costs us the whole stake, with each bet effectively independent of the last. We intend to keep placing bets like this for years, rolling our winnings back into the pool rather than withdrawing them along the way. My friend's position is that since any individual bet here has a clearly positive expected value -- about 10 cents of profit for every dollar staked, on average -- the only defensible policy is to stake our entire bankroll on every single bet, since holding anything back just forfeits expected profit for nothing in return. When I push back that betting everything, every time, for years on end, seems obviously too risky, he says "too risky" is a feeling rather than an argument: if we're both genuinely indifferent to the possibility of losing it all, there's no further fact that could make a smaller stake the rational one, because expected value is exactly the quantity that rationality tells us to maximize.

I would like to know whether he's actually right about that. Is it true that only risk aversion could ultimately justify staking less than everything on each bet here, or is there some more basic argument -- one that would apply even to two people who are genuinely indifferent to risk -- for why committing the whole bankroll every time would still be a mistake? If such an argument exists, I'd like to understand what fraction of the bankroll it would actually recommend staking on each bet, and how that number follows from the figures above, rather than just being told to be more careful.
