So a few months back I fell down a free will rabbit hole that started on the usual LW/ACX-adjacent blogs and ended up somewhere weirder: Galen Strawson's "Basic Argument" against moral responsibility. Not the boring "determinism rules out free will" argument, the regress one -- you can only be truly (ultimately) responsible for what you do if you're truly responsible for the way you are, mentally speaking, but to be responsible for THAT you'd have to have chosen your own character according to values you already had, and those values would themselves need to have been chosen according to some earlier values, forever back -- so ultimate responsibility turns out to be impossible regardless of wether determinism or indeterminism is true, which is the part I find genuinely unsettling. I recall Strawson likes to invoke that Nietzsche line about causa sui being basically a contradiction in terms, pulling yourself into existence by your own hair out of the swamp of nothingness, which is a hell of an image for a footnote. And then i found out the really delicious bit: Galen Strawson is literally the son of P.F. Strawson, who wrote "Freedom and Resentment," pretty much THE canonical defense of compatibilism via reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude, indignation, that whole family of feelings) instead of metaphysics. Feels like a philosophical family feud waiting to happen.

Could you write me a dialogue between the two of them hashing this out -- or fictionalized stand ins if you'd rather not put words directly in real people's mouths, one dead, one not. I want the Galen-type character to push the regress all the way to "nobody ultimately deserves praise, blame, credit or punishment, full stop," and the P.F.-type character to defend a reactive-attitudes-based notion of responsibility that doesn't need that kind of "ultimate" desert to do real work. Neither one should be a pushover though: the compatibilist needs to land the obvious objection that the regress proves too much (it'd apply even if libertarian free will were real, so it's not really about determinism at all, which makes you wonder what "ultimate" is even doing in the argument), and I want a real response to that, not just a restatement of the regress. And if the hard-liner has a comeback to the reactive attitudes move -- something like "fine, those feelings are natural, but are they still *fitting*, i.e. justified, if nobody actually deserves anything" -- I want the compatibilist to genuinely engage with it rather than retreat to "well, it's just how humans work." It doesn't need to land on a tidy resolution, since this is a live disagreement in the literature, but both sides should feel like they're actually landing blows on each other, not talking past each other.
