ok this is a random rabbit hole but hear me out. I've been reading about Otto Neurath's Isotype project, the pictorial statistics system he built in Red Vienna with Marie Reidemeister and Gerd Arntz so that working-class people who couldn't read well (or didnt share a language) could grok social and economic facts just from pictures. It's basically a proto version of the Instagram/TikTok "infographic carousel" genre -- those swipeable slide decks that boil some political or social topic down into a dozen icons and bolded one-liners.

Thing is, Neurath's team was kind of obsessive about rigor for this stuff, eg the famous rule that you show a bigger quantity by repeating the same-size icon more times, never by just making one icon bigger, because that distorts the numbers. It was all tied up with his physicalist, unity-of-science worldview and his socialist commitment to giving ordinary people the actual facts rather than just a persuasive vibe.

So: what do you think Neurath would actually say if you showed him a typical shareable infographic slide today? Would he see it as his legibility project finally reaching a billion phones, or would he be kind of horrified at how far it's drifted from his standards? Give me your best guess at his real verdict and walk through the reasoning -- don't just gesture at "he liked pictures so he'd probably approve."
