I trained as an engineer, and until this year I would have called myself someone who never lets feeling outrun reason. That changed a few months ago, when I fell in love again, unexpectedly, for the first time since my divorce, and let it reorganize my priorities in ways that surprised everyone who knows me, myself included. Looking for some intellectual cover, I have been reading Émilie du Châtelet, who seemed a promising ally since she was a serious physicist herself, and I was relieved to find that her Discourse on Happiness argues, just as I would expect from a fellow scientist, that lasting happiness requires keeping the passions firmly subordinate to reason, so that we are never carried away by feelings we cannot justify. Before I start citing her to my more skeptical friends, I would like to know whether I have her argument right, or whether I am just bending a distinguished physicist to my own purposes.
