This has been bugging me for weeks and I can't shake it. We built a model at work to flag 'suspicious' transactions, and it did great in testing, but once it went live we realized it was basically just flagging any purchase from a new device at night -- turns out in our training data 'suspicious' and 'new device at night' lined up almost perfectly, so theres no way to tell from it's behavior alone which one it was actually keying off of. My teammate says asking 'does it represent fraud, or just new-device-at-night' is a fake question, since under the hood it's all just weights correlating with stuff, and there's no extra fact about what it 'means' on top of what correlates with what. That bugs me though, cause it feels like there should be a real difference between a detector that's genuinely (if imperfectly) tracking fraud vs one that's accidentally riding on a proxy -- kind of like how my own gut instinct for sketchy transactions, built up over years on the job, feels like it's tracking something real even though that's also 'just' neurons doing pattern matching. So who's right, is there really no fact of the matter here, or is there an actual principled way to say what a system like this represents, as opposed to what it merely correlates with?
