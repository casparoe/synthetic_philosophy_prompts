I am a novelist assembling a cycle of short fiction, each story built around a real experiment that unsettles some intuitive notion of the physical world. The piece I am currently stuck on concerns the so-called "cosmic Bell tests," in which physicists drew their measurement settings from the light of ancient quasars, so that any hidden mechanism conspiring to fix the outcome in advance would have had to be in place billions of years before the experimenters, or the Earth, existed — an attempt to close the "freedom-of-choice" loophole through which local hidden-variable theories might otherwise survive Bell's theorem. Could you write me a short story of roughly 1,000 words, centered on a character closely involved in such an experiment, who is forced to reckon with what is left of locality, and of their own freedom of choice, once the quasar light has done its work? I would like the physics to surface through character and incident rather than through exposition.
