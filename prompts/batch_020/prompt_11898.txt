So I've been falling down a Mill rabbit hole for a piece on the history of the word "socialism," and it turns out that by the time he wrote his autobiography, Mill was basically calling himself a socialist -- even though hes mostly remembered as the godfather of classical liberalism. From what I can tell, in the later editions of Principles of Political Economy and that unfinished Chapters on Socialism essay, his actual vision wasn't state control at all -- it was worker cooperatives (he was a big fan of the Rochdale cooperators) gradually out-competing capitalist firms until wage labor just withers away on its own. Here's the thing though: Mill died in 1873, decades before anyone actually tried running a whole country on "socialism." So if he'd somehow lived to see the Soviet Union and 20th century central planning, do you reckon he'd have recognized any of it as his idea come to life, or would he have seen five-year plans and nationalized industry as basically a betrayal of what he was actually after?
