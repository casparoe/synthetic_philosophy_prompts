In my AP Physics class, I built a simple pendulum to test the standard formula for its period, but my measured times came out consistently around 8 percent longer than the formula predicted, even after I repeated the trials carefully with several different string lengths. When I showed my teacher the results, she said this shows the formula must break down under real-world conditions. My lab partner disagrees: he thinks the formula itself is fine, and that the real culprit must be something about our setup, such as my reaction time on the stopwatch, how precisely we measured the string, or air resistance on the bob. I'm not sure how to adjudicate between them. Is there a principled way to decide whether a mismatch like ours actually counts as evidence against a well-established formula, rather than just a flaw in the experiment? And if blaming the experiment can sometimes be legitimate, how do you keep that kind of reasoning from becoming a way to protect the formula regardless of what the data show?
