A close friend of mine got engaged two weekends ago, and the story of how he proposed turned into a real argument between us that I still haven't let go of. Rather than anything modest, he had spent nearly a month's salary chartering a small boat for a few hours, hiring a live string quartet to play from a second boat alongside it, and timing the whole thing to a sunset over a lake that, by his own admission, meant nothing to either of them beforehand. None of it was refundable, reusable, or the kind of thing they would have anything to show for afterward. When I pointed out that the same money could have funded a trip they would actually remember together, or gone straight into their wedding fund, he got surprisingly defensive: his position was that the waste was the whole point. If the gesture had been cheap, efficient, or salvageable, he said, it would not have proven anything about what he was willing to give up for her; it was precisely the fact that the money and effort were gone for good that made the moment credible.

I pushed back that this sounded like a rationalization for overspending, and that if he really wanted to demonstrate commitment, behavior sustained consistently over years should count for far more than one extravagant evening. He replied that consistent behavior is exactly what is easy to fake in the short run, whereas an irreversible, costly gesture is not, and that this might be why such gestures move people in a way that promises never quite do. Neither of us gave ground that night, and I've kept turning the disagreement over ever since - not entirely academically, since my partner and I have our own fifth anniversary coming up, and I'm genuinely unsure whether doing something similarly extravagant would mean anything or would just be money spent for the wrong reasons.

So I'd like an outside read on the underlying dispute. Is there a real, defensible reason why a costly, impractical gesture can count as stronger evidence of someone's feelings than an equally generous but sensible one, or is my friend just dressing up overspending as depth of feeling? And if that reasoning does hold up in general, what would actually distinguish a gesture whose cost earns it that credibility from a gesture that's simply expensive for no good reason?
