I've got some money to donate this year and I'm torn between two charities working in the same refugee camp: one saves the lives of severely malnourished kids who'd otherwise die, the other improves food and schooling for kids who are gonna survive either way so they grow up healthier and do better later on. A friend who's really into effective giving ran the numbers and told me that, dollar for dollar, the life-saving charity produces more total wellbeing, even though the kids it saves will on average end up living harder lives than the ones helped by the other charity. Something about that logic bugs me — like if you followed it all the way, you'd always have to prefer saving a much bigger group of people who'll live so-so lives over a smaller group who'll live amazing ones, just because the total keeps adding up higher. Am I just being squeamish, or is my friends reasoning actually off somewhere?
