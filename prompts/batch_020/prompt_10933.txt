I keep running into 'money pump' arguments online, used as a slam-dunk case for why any rational agent has to have transitive, time-consistent preferences -- no weird cyclical preferences, no flip-flopping on tradeoffs between now and later without getting new information. But apparently there's a whole counter-tradition arguing that a 'sophisticated' agent (one who plans ahead and out-thinks their own future self) or a 'resolute' one (who just has the willpower to stick to an earlier plan) can have exactly those weird, inconsistent preferences and still dodge every single pump -- which would mean the pump doesn't actually indict the preferences, just naive choosing. Could you write me a dialogue between two decision theorists hashing this out over the last round at a conference bar? One holds the hard line that surviving every possible pump secretly requires something functionally equivalent to diachronically consistent preferences anyway; the other defends the sophistication-or-resolve escape route. Make them actually build a specific pump scenario together and fight over it rather then trading slogans, and definitely have the first theorist push on whether 'a careful enough agent never gets pumped' is just transitivity wearing a trench coat.
