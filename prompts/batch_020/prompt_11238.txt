Ok, here's one that apparently causes actual fights at formal epistemology conferences: van Fraassen's (1981) "Judy Benjamin" problem. Setup: Judy's platoon parachutes into terrain split evenly between Blue (her own side) and Red (enemy) territory, and each territory is further divided into a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area -- so her prior gives 1/4 to each of the four quadrants (Blue-HQ, Blue-2nd, Red-HQ, Red-2nd). Command then radios her that, conditional on being in Red territory, its 3-to-1 she's in the HQ area -- and the radio dies, so that's the whole message. Van Fraassen actually polled audiences on this, and people overwhelmingly say her credence of being in Blue should just stay at 1/2, with Red splitting 3/8 vs 1/8 internally. But if you instead update by minimizing cross-entropy relative to the prior (subject to that 3:1 constraint), Blue's credence creeps up to about .53 -- and in fact rises for basically any announced ratio except 1:1, which seems nuts given that the message is ostensibly only about how Red subdivides internally. Here's the wrinkle for the "obviously cross-entropy is just wrong" camp though: push the message to its logical extreme (she's told she's definitely in the HQ area, given that she's in Red at all), and plain ordinary conditionalization -- which nobody disputes as a rational update rule -- also bumps Blue's credence up, to 2/3.
