I am drafting a detective novel whose protagonist swears by inference to the best explanation, while her rival on the case—a police statistician—trusts only strict Bayesian updating on the evidence. Before I write their confrontation, I would like to see the underlying philosophical dispute worked out properly. Please write a dialogue between the two of them, standing over the same set of clues, each trying to persuade the other that her method is the right one. Have the statistician press something close to van Fraassen's explanationist's dilemma: if inference to the best explanation ever recommends a different verdict than conditionalizing on the evidence would, it must be leading us into incoherent beliefs, and if it never does, it is telling us nothing that conditionalization was not already going to tell us. Have the detective offer a genuine answer to this rather than a mere appeal to intuition, and let her in turn press the statistician on where priors and likelihoods are supposed to come from in the first place, if not from something very like explanatory judgment. Keep the two of them equally sharp, and make sure each reply engages the other's actual argument rather than simply restating her own position.
