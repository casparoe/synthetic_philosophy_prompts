So this happened in my epistemology reading group and it's been bugging me. We were doing timed practice proofs for quals. (1) Mara worked through a modal logic derivation, checked it twice, and was confident it was valid. (2) Elias, who's kept a spreadsheet of our practice accuracy all semester (yes, really), pointed out that on problems she solves in under two minutes specifically, she's been wrong 6 of her last 8 attempts versus 0 of 8 for him -- the odds said she'd blown this one too. (3) Mara said fine, that's genuinely unsettling, but she'd gone back over the derivation line by line after he said it and nothing looked off, so she still thought it was valid. (4) Elias said that's just incoherent -- you can't agree the evidence says you're probably wrong and also keep believing you're right, that's literally epistemic akrasia, and its never rational. (5) Mara said the alternative is worse: if a track-record claim about her can override a derivation she's checked independently line by line, she can never trust her own reasoning about anything again, she'd basically have to treat his spreadsheet as trumping the object-level proof forever. (6) Elias said nobody's asking for permanent deference, just deference to this one specific, independently-tracked pattern, and refusing to budge while admitting the pattern is real isn't caution, its just akrasia with extra steps. Mara's closing question was: fine, but what is she actually supposed to believe then -- some blended in-between credence feels made up, not like something she actually arrived at.

Neither of them is dumb, so please don't strawman either side. Can you go through moves (1)-(6) and say who's got the better of it at each step and by how much, flag anything you think both of them are missing, and then take on Mara's closing question yourself -- is there actually some stable, non-akratic thing she should believe by the end of this, or is that asking too much?
