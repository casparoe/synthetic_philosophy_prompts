I just got put on my hospital's root-cause-analysis committee (total baptism by fire — this is my first case). A patient had a slow overnight bleed that nobody caught: the night nurse, the covering resident, and the attending each had at least one point where ordering another set of vitals or an extra scan probably would've caught it in time, but none of them did anything actively wrong — they each just didn't do the one extra thing. Half the committee wants to write this up as a pure systems failure with no individual responsibility, since if you start faulting the resident for not re-checking, you might as well fault the nurse, the attending, whoever wrote the staffing schedule that night, even the board for not funding more nursing staff. The other half thinks that logic is a recipe for never holding anyone accountable for anything, ever. Can you draft the reccomendation memo I have to bring to Tuesday's meeting -- it's basically going to set precedent for how we handle "failure to rescue" cases from now on, so it needs an actual decision rule for when an omission counts as someone's fault versus everyone's (or no one's), and it has to seriously engage with the "systems failure" side instead of just gesturing at it and then pinning the blame on an individual anyway.
