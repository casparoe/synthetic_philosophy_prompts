Question: Kant holds that human beings have no direct duties to non-human animals, only indirect duties regarding them. (a) Reconstruct the argument for this claim, drawing on the relevant discussion in the Metaphysics of Morals and the Lectures on Ethics, and explain what work the notion of an "end in itself" does in it. (b) In Fellow Creatures and related work, Christine Korsgaard argues, from broadly Kantian premises, that animals are in fact owed direct duties. Explain the key move in her argument, and identify the precise point at which her conclusion departs from Kant's own.

Student answer: For Kant, only rational, autonomous beings can be ends in themselves, since only they are capable of legislating and obeying the moral law; because animals lack reason, they cannot be the direct object of a duty, so we have duties only "regarding" them rather than "to" them. This is why Kant can say, in the Lectures on Ethics, that a master who has his old dog killed once it is no longer able to serve him does not thereby wrong the dog: an animal, lacking reason and language, cannot judge whether it has been treated fairly. Indeed, on Kant's view animals are essentially unfeeling automata, so cruelty towards them cannot harm the animal itself; rather, cruelty is wrong because habitually treating a being as if its pain did not matter dulls the very sympathetic dispositions we need in order to treat other rational beings well. Mistreating animals is thus wrong only derivatively, as a roundabout way of mistreating humanity.

Korsgaard's response is that suffering is suffering whatever species undergoes it, so a Kantian who takes the equal consideration of interests seriously should extend the Formula of Humanity to any being that can suffer, not only to rational beings. Since Kant already concedes that animals suffer, Korsgaard concludes that direct duties to animals follow from his own premises. Her argument therefore converges with Bentham's case for animal welfare, even though, unlike Bentham, she reaches this conclusion by an internal, Kantian route rather than by appeal to aggregate utility.
