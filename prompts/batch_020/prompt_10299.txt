So my school's philosophy club had a meeting on antinatalism a couple weeks ago and it turned into this whole arguement between two members, Jake and Mia, over Benatar's asymmetry (the argument that it's better to have never been born). I wrote down roughly how it went afterward bc I can't stop thinking about who actually had the better of it:

1. Jake: The presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, sure, but the absence of pain is still good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that good, while the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless there's an actual somebody being deprived of it. So if you never exist, you get all the good of "no pain," free of charge, but you never get "charged" for missing the good stuff, since theres no you around to miss it. Existing always comes with at least a little pain, so existing is always at least a little worse than never existing.

2. Mia: That's kind of a rigged setup though. You're saying "nobody's there" cancels the badness of missing out on pleasure, but somehow it doesn't cancel the goodness of missing out on pain. That's the exact same move, used two different ways, to get two different answers. Feels like you reverse engineered the rule from the conclusion you wanted.

3. Jake: It lines up with stuff we already believe though, I didn't just make it up to win. We think it's wrong to knowingly have a kid who's gonna suffer a ton, but nobody thinks you're a monster for simply not having a kid who would've been happy. And nobody sends condolences to a "possible person" for not getting born into a good life, but we absolutely feel bad for someone stuck actually suffering. Those asymmetries are already in how we think, my rule just explains why, it's not some coincidence.

4. Mia: Or, way simpler explanation: those are just facts about our duties to people who actually exist or will exist, not proof of some spooky value where nonexistence is secretly "good for" someone who never shows up to receive it. We shouldn't make a kid who'll suffer because suffering is bad for whoever ends up real. We're not obligated to make a happy kid because you don't owe existence to a non-person in the first place. Covers your examples fine, no weird asymmetry axiom required.

5. Jake: Ok but then explain this one: if you knew for a fact your kid would be born with some horrible, untreatable condition, we don't just think it's neutral to skip having that kid, we think it's actually good that you didn't. On your view, where there's no "person" for it to be good for, shouldn't that just be neutral too?

6. Mia: No, because that's comparing two different possible futures where you exist and made a choice, not comparing an existing sufferer to their own nonexistence. It's better because fewer people end up suffering in that timeline overall, not because skipping the birth was secretly a gift handed to a kid who never happened.

If a move is sort of right but missing something, say that too. Also curious if you think they're both overlooking something important about how Benatar's actual argument is supposed to work. End with one line saying whose overall case you think holds up better.
