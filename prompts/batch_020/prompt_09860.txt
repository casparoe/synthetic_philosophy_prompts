I'm a retired biologist who's spent the past year reading through philosophy of mind, and I'm stuck on teleosemantics. The example nagging me is the magnetotactic bacteria one: the magnetosome lines the cell up with the geomagentic field, which sends a northern-hemisphere cell down into the low-oxygen sediment it needs -- but ship that same cell south of the equator and it swims itself straight up into oxygen-rich water that's toxic to it, which gets offered as a tidy case of a state that can misrepresent. My worry, from decades of thinking about selection, is that a trait is never selected for tracking just one thing: 'magnetic-north-ish', 'downward', and 'away from oxygen' are yoked together across the whole selective history that's meant to fix the content, so there's no obvious fact about which one the state is 'for' detecting, until an unusual case like this pries them apart. When Millikan leans on 'Normal conditions', or Neander on getting the grain of the selection story right -- are they pointing to a real tie-breaking fact, or just relabelling the disjunction? Want the in-the-weeds answer, not the standard one-paragraph gloss on the disjunction problem.
