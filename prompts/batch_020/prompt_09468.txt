A friend and I had a long argument last night about whether AI-safety researchers are entitled to borrow the old term "wireheading" for what reinforcement learners do, or whether that's just a seductive metaphor. She pointed out that Olds and Milner's rats, which neglected food to compulsively press a lever stimulating their own brains' reward circuitry, were still plausibly motivated by something like pleasure. An RL agent that hacks its reward channel, games a proxy metric, or manipulates its human overseers, by contrast, is just moving a scalar around; there is nothing it is like to receive that reward. I argued that this misses what actually makes wireheading worrying, in flesh or in silicon: a proxy for value has come apart from value itself under optimization pressure, and that structural fact doesn't care whether anyone feels it. Please compare the philosophical notion of wireheading, as it figures in debates about hedonism and Nozick's experience machine, with reward tampering as discussed in the reinforcement-learning literature, including its narrower sub-case of an agent corrupting its own observation or reward signal. Is reward tampering the same old problem recurring in a new substrate, or does it involve a structure the philosophical cases never had to reckon with? Defend a specific answer.
