My Philosophy of Mind class has been doing a unit on split-brain patients this week and honestly it's kind of messing with my head (pun intended). From what I understand, in some severe epilepsy cases doctors cut the band of fibers connecting the two hemispheres, and afterward the two halves sometimes stop fully "telling" each other what they're doing or seeing. So the hand controlled by one hemisphere can do something, and when you ask the person why, the half that's actually doing the talking will confidently give you a reason — even though that half never had access to whatever info actually caused the action, so the "reason" is basically made up on the spot, without the person having any clue thats whats happening.

Here's why I'm actually asking though: my uncle had a version of this surgery about three years ago for seizures his meds couldn't control anymore, and he lives with my cousin (his son), who swears little things have been off in a way that fits exactly what we're covering in class. Like his dad will set the table with his left hand while insisting out loud that he's already finished with that job, or reach for the remote with one hand right after using the other hand to switch the TV off, and if you call him out on it he'll give you a totally confident, totally-sounds-made-up explanation for why. My cousin half-jokes that "there might be two guys living in him now," but my mom (his sister) thinks that's a ridiculous way to talk about her own brother — to her he's obviously still just one single person, full stop, whatever weird tics he's picked up since the surgery.

I can't decide who's actually right, or honestly whether the question even makes sense as stated. Is there some real fact about whether he's "one person" or not that we could in principle discover, or does my mom have a point that this is all just surface-level glitching that doesn't actually threaten there being one person in there? Genuinely want to understand what's going on here rather than just pick whichever answer feels more comforting.
