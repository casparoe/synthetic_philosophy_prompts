My younger sister will be five years sober next month, and she's asked me to give the toast at the small family dinner we're having afterward. She has always described her addiction the way her recovery group taught her to: a disease she never chose and isn't morally responsible for. That framing kept her alive, and I don't want to undercut it in front of the people from her group who will be there. But she has also told me, more than once, that it infuriates her when people credit her sobriety to luck, or say the disease "finally loosened its grip," as though the person who spent five years deliberately choosing not to pick up a drink had nothing to do with it. She wants real credit for that. I've been reading about compatibilism on my own, because I can't work out whether she's actually entitled to both claims at once: that the addiction wasn't hers to be blamed for, and the recovery is hers to be praised for, when, as far as I can tell, both trace back to things she never chose in the first place. Roughly half the guests will be from her recovery group, the rest relatives who have never given any of this a moment's thought and just want to hear that she's strong. Could you write the toast? It should run about five minutes, actually resolve that tension rather than talk around it, and still sound like a toast and not a philosophy lecture.
