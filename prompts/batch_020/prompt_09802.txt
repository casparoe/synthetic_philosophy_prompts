So I've been down a rabbit hole this week that started with re-reading Yudkowsky's quantum mechanics sequence and ended on some old philpapers/SEP tangents about Bohmian mechanics, and I think I finally get why the many-worlds-vs-pilot-wave debate never actually resolves itself in the comment sections I frequent: everyone argues about locality, ontology, and Occam's razor, but almost nobody engages with the fact that BOTH sides have their own genuinely unsolved problem about *why* we see Born-rule (|ψ|²) statistics specifically, and the two problems aren't even the same shape.

On the Everett side there's the whole Deutsch–Wallace move of trying to derive the Born rule from decision-theoretic rationality axioms applied to an agent who already knows they're about to branch — and then people like Adrian Kent and David Albert show up and say the whole thing is circular, because "caring in proportion to your amplitude" isn't even a coherent preference to have unless you've already snuck in something Born-rule-shaped as a premise. Meanwhile on the Bohmian side you've got Durr/Goldstein/Zanghi's quantum equilibrium hypothesis, this statistical-mechanics-style typicality argument for why particle configurations should be |ψ|²-distributed — except then Valentini's work on quantum non-equilibrium makes it sound like equilibrium is really just a contingent fact about our particular universe's early history (like a "past hypothesis" for QM) rather then something the theory actually derives, which feels like its own can of worms.

Could you write me a dialogue between a committed Everettian and a committed Bohmian, both of them genuinely sharp (no strawmanning either side), where they go at exactly this issue — each pressing on whether the other's camp has actually earned its derivation of the Born rule, rather than the usual surface-level "many worlds is wasteful" vs "hidden variables are ad hoc" back-and-forth you get in the popular treatments. I'd especially like it if each of them is also willing to admit where their own side's account still has a gap, since thats the part every pop-sci explainer conveniently skips.
