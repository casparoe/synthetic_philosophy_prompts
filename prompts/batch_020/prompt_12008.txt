So a few months back I got roped onto the advisory board of a small foundation that gives grants for "stress-testing" findings a field considers basically settled -- sometimes a genuine adversarial collaboration between people who disagree, sometimes just a solid researcher asking for money to redo a classic result properly. I only landed this unpaid gig because I write about science and research culture for a living and someone thought that counted as "neutral." It's been easy so far, but a proposal on the docket for next weeks call has split the board right down the middle, and because I'm supposedly the neutral one, I've been asked to draft the recommendation memo.

Here's the case: a well-regarded researcher wants about $60k to redo, with much better power and pre-registration, a finding that's been in every intro textbook in her corner of psychology for twenty years. Half the board says funding this is pointless, maybe even irresponsible -- we already know the effect is real, so we already know, before a single subject is run, that any contrary result the new study turns up will have to be a fluke or a confound, and it's a waste of scarce money to go "discover" that. The other half of us, me included (ever since a philosopher I interviewed for a different piece got me hooked on this), finds that argument kind of unsettling, because it seems to prove way too much: if simply knowing something now entitles you to write off in advance any future evidence against it, that would justify never funding a check on anything we're currently confident about -- which feels like exactly the failure mode the foundation exists to guard against. She called this general puzzle "the dogmatism paradox" and said it goes back to some unpublished Kripke lectures, for what that's worth.

Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote? I need an actual recommendation on this specific grant -- fund, decline, or fund with conditions, and on what timeline -- plus something like a standing principle the board could lean on next time this exact fight comes up, because it clearly will. Please don't dodge into "it depends": take a side. But be genuinely fair to the "we already know this, don't waste the money" camp, since their instinct doesn't strike me as crazy either.
