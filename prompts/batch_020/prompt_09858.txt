In relational database theory, a relation is just a subset of the Cartesian product of its domains—a set of tuples—so two values are "related" the moment they co-occur in one of those tuples, with no further glue needed to hold them together. I recently came across Bradley's regress and its role in the debate over the unity of the proposition, and it strikes me that philosophers are still wrestling with a problem that Codd's relational model quietly dissolved decades ago through a clean extensional definition. Am I missing something, or does treating relations this way—as sets of tuples rather than as some mysterious binding agent—actually settle the question of what makes "Socrates is wise" one claim rather than just a list of two words?
