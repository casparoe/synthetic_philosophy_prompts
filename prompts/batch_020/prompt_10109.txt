So I've been down a comparative ethics rabbit hole the last few weeks -- started because a commenter on an ACX open thread recommended the Ames and Rosemont stuff on Confucian "role ethics," and I ended up writing a short piece for my blog (which like four people read, but whatever, its where I think out loud). The essay leans on that Analects passage, 13.18, where the Duke of She brags to Confucius about a guy in his territory, "Upright Gong," who testified against his own father for stealing a sheep, and calls this uprightness. Confucius says no -- real uprightness in his community is fathers and sons covering for each other. Ames and Rosemont use passages like this to argue Confucian ethics isn't secretly virtue ethics wearing a robe: there's no bounded "self" that has honesty or benevolence as a property and then decides how to apply it to whoever's in front of you; you just are father-to-this-son, subject-to-this-ruler, and so on, and doing those roles well is the whole of morality. No role-neutral vantage point standing behind the roles, judging them.

My essay's actual claim is that this is a real problem for the rationalist/EA package of impartial beneficence plus near-absolute honesty norms (shut up and multiply, meta-honesty, the "a stranger's kid counts exactly as much as your own" thing) -- not just "different culture, different intuitions" but a claim that our whole reflex of stepping back to the agent-neutral view and calculating from there is itself a substantive, optional ethical commitment, not something decision theory just hands you for free, since on the role-ethics picture there's no role-less agent left over who could even take that step back. I try to head off the obvious consequentialist comeback -- that partiality is just an instrumentally-justified rule, recoverable at one remove, a la "raise your own kids because a world of specialized carers works better than a world of pure impartial concern" -- by saying that comeback still smuggles in an agent who stands prior to and outside the rule and "adopts" it, which is exactly what's being denied. Though honestly I go back and forth on whether that's a real bullet the consequentialist has to bite or whether I've just repackaged "we have different terminal values" as a deep metaphysical dispute so it sounds more interesting.
