I'm a junior on my high school's Honor Council, the student-run body that hears academic integrity cases and recommends sanctions to the Dean of Students. Next week's case has split us down the middle. A sophomore was reported for texting exam answers to a friend during chemistry. The complainant is a classmate who, it turns out from her own texts, did the identical thing on a different exam last year and was never caught — including by herself, until now. The accused isn't claiming innocence. Her defense is that the complaint itself is illegitimate: whoever brings a charge like this needs standing to, and given her history, the complainant doesn't have it. Half the council thinks that's beside the point, since cheating is cheating no matter who reports it; the other half thinks a report from someone guilty of the identical, undisclosed offense taints the whole hearing, or should at least soften the sanction. Could you draft the short written opinion I'll bring to our meeting? It should recommend whether the case proceeds, is dismissed, or proceeds with a reduced sanction, address what, if anything, should happen to the complainant, and take the standing objection seriously enough to say exactly what work it can and can't do, rather than just asserting that two wrongs don't make a right.
