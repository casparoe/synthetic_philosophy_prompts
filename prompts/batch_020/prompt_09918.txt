So I've been down a rabbit hole of old LessWrong and Overcoming Bias posts on cryonics and whole-brain emulation lately, and I noticed basically none of them ever spell out an assumption they're all quietly leaning on: whether persistence through time works the endurantist way (you're wholly present at every single moment you exist, full stop) or the perdurantist way (you're really a four-dimensional 'worm', and 25-year-old-you and 80-year-old-you are just different temporal parts of that worm, no more identical to each other than your hand is to your foot). Everyone reaches for Parfit's teleporter case and the branch-line variant like they settle whether the guy who steps out the other end 'is you', but the intuitions those cases are supposed to pump seem to depend a lot on which persistence view your already implicitly holding, and on how you handle stuff like Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics (how is one persisting thing bent at t1 and straight at t2 without contradiction?).

Can you walk me through what you think are the most important implications of the endurantism/perdurantism split specifically for how much weight I should put on cryonic revival, or a slow neuron-by-neuron upload, actually being me waking up on the other side, as opposed to just a new person who happens to inherit my memories? I don't need the textbook rundown of the two views, I've already got the basic shape -- what I care about is whether taking one side over the other should actually change, right now, how much I dread the destruction of the original tissue, and whether 'stage theory' (the you're-just-an-instantaneous-thing-with-counterparts compromise) is really a third answer here or just a way of dodging the question.
