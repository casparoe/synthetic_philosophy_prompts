A handful of us who met years ago on an amateur mathematics forum run a small annual grant program for independent researchers -- people who, like us, never had the credentials to be taken seriously by a university or a mainstream funding body. This year's leading submission is a paper design, not yet built, for an analog device that would use continuously varying physical quantities rather than digital states to decide the halting problem for arbitrary Turing machines, which the author presents as a refutation of the Church-Turing thesis. My two fellow judges are at an impasse: one wants to reject the submission outright, since the thesis is a proven theorem and any claimed counterexample must be hiding an error somewhere; the other wants to award the grant precisely because testing bold claims like this is the point of the program, and the thesis is 'just' an unproven empirical guess anyway. I've spent a good while reading about the thesis on my own, since none of us studied this formally, but I still can't tell which of them has its actual status right, if either does. Please draft the short memo I'll bring to our final call: it should recommend whether we fund the proposal, decline it, or fund it with conditions, and it should engage both colleagues' positions seriously enough that our applicant -- who will see the decision -- could actually respond to it.
