I've been reading up on Richard Jeffrey's probability kinematics after seeing it come up repeatedly in rationalist-adjacent discussions of radical probabilism. Ordinary conditionalization is commutative: updating on E and then F yields the same posterior as updating on F and then E, since either route amounts to conditioning on their conjunction. Since Jeffrey conditionalization is just the natural extension of that same rigidity-based updating logic to evidence that shifts your credence over a partition without making anything certain, this order-independence should carry over automatically, so two agents who receive exactly the same uncertain evidence about, say, a patient's diagnosis and a lab test's reliability — merely in a different order — are guaranteed to converge on identical credences. Given that this seems to follow directly from the structure of the rule itself, why do some formal epistemologists treat the commutativity of sequential Jeffrey shifts as a special result that only holds under extra independence conditions, rather than as an automatic feature of probability kinematics?
