In 1971 Kenneth Kunen proved, using the axiom of choice, that there is no nontrivial elementary embedding of the set-theoretic universe V into itself -- and hence that no Reinhardt cardinal, defined as the critical point of such an embedding, can exist. Set theory absorbed this inconsistency without crisis: Reinhardt cardinals were simply struck from the large-cardinal hierarchy, and work on weaker large-cardinal axioms continued as before. Compare this episode with the far more feared prospect of an inconsistency turning up instead in Peano Arithmetic, or in the core axioms of ZFC itself. What structural features of the large-cardinal hierarchy allowed Kunen's result to be absorbed rather than treated as catastrophic, and why would there be no comparable room to retreat if PA or bare ZFC itself were shown to be inconsistent? Use the comparison to say something about the real, working difference between axioms that mathematicians treat as speculative hypotheses and axioms that function as bedrock, whatever foundational rhetoric officially claims about their status.
