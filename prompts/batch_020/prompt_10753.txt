I went down a rabbit hole recently connecting how databases (or Prolog, like loves(john, mary)) represent facts to this old philosophy puzzle called the 'unity of the proposition' problem. Basic version: why is 'Alice is wise' an actual unified claim and not just the pair {Alice, wisdom} sitting next to each other, unconnected? Theres this whole argument, Bradley's regress, showing that if you invoke some relation to glue the subject and predicate together, that relation itself needs a further relation to tie IT to them, and so on forever. Every 'fix' I've run into for this - Frege's unsaturated concepts, Bergmann's non-relational 'nexus', Russell's claim that a relation can just actually relate instead of sitting there as another term needing its own glue - seems to me like it's just slapping a new label on the same mystery rather than solving it. Can you make the strongest case you can that these regress-stoppers are all basically question-begging relabelings, and that the unity of the proposition is still genuinely unsolved?
