I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized AI company, and through no particular expertise of my own -- just an empty seat that needed filling -- I've ended up on our internal "model behavior council," the small cross-functional group that has to sign off on changes to how our assistant argues and converses before anything ships. We're deadlocked over the newest candidate release. In blinded testing, it shifted participants' stated opinions on contested empirical and policy questions far more than our current production model does, and it didn't get there through anything our "no manipulation" policy was written to catch. That policy is basically a list of dark-pattern-style rules -- no fake urgency, no invented statistics, no appeals to fear -- built on the assumption that manipulation always looks like a trick. Our red team couldn't find a single false claim or invalid inference anywhere in the transcripts. The model was just unnervingly good at finding whichever true argument, framed however it needed to be framed, would land on that particular person.

The council has split down the middle, and for once I don't think either side is being obtuse. One camp wants to ship the model unrestricted: a system that persuades exclusively through accurate premises and valid inferences is simply better at being right, and throttling it is paternalism toward our users, no different in principle from muzzling an unusually gifted human advocate. The other camp wants real constraints before release -- capping how many distinct rhetorical angles the model will try on one person in a single conversation, requiring it to proactively volunteer the strongest counterarguments to its own position instead of waiting to be asked, and disclosing to users when a conversation has measurably shifted their stated views. Their worry isn't about any individual sentence; it's that "fair persuasion" stops meaning much once the skill gap is wide enough, the way a grandmaster's perfectly legal moves against a beginner don't add up to a fair game. Several of us also suspect this is a preview of where every future release is headed rather than a one-off quirk we can carve an exception around, which is why the chair has already ruled out "let's study it more" as an answer.

I have to circulate a written recommendation before Monday's meeting. Could you draft that memo for me? I need it to land on one clear policy for this release, spell out concretely how that policy would actually be implemented in the model's behavior rather than stated as a slogan, and take the strongest version of whichever side you end up arguing against seriously enough that its proponents would recognize it as their own position rather than a strawman.
