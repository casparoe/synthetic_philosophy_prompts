For a term project on the limits of self-referential formal systems, I've been reading about the "Löbian obstacle": the difficulty a formal AI agent faces in trusting that a successor system, built on the very same axioms, will only ever prove statements that are actually true. Several sources trace this obstacle specifically to Löb's theorem and treat it as importantly distinct from Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, even though both results are often loosely described as showing that a formal system can't fully verify itself from the inside. Could you compare Löb's theorem with Gödel's second incompleteness theorem and explain why merely proving that a successor system is consistent would fall short of the kind of self-trust the Löbian obstacle is actually concerned with?
