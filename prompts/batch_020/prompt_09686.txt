Okay so something from work has been bugging me for weeks and I can't tell if I'm overthinking it. I'm on a small interpretability team at my company (three of us, kind of thrown into it) and our job is basically poking around inside a trained neural net trying to figure out what individual neurons or directions in its activation space actually represent, since otherwise the model's just a black box that spits out numbers. A while back I found a direction that fires really reliably whenever a chunk of text involves someone being betrayed, so I wrote it up in our internal docs as the "betrayal neuron" and moved on, probably should of poked at it more before writing that up, honestly.

Then last week my teammate Priya went back through our whole example set and pointed out that literally every input that fires the neuron also happens to involve someone getting humiliated or embarrassed right afterward, so as far as she's concerned "humiliation neuron" fits our data at least as well and we've been mislabeling it this whole time. We burned an entire afternoon writing probe sentences meant to pull the two ideas apart — a clean betrayal with zero embarrassment, a clean humiliation with zero betrayal — and annoyingly couldn't land a single example that clearly favors one label over the other; it gives at least a partial response either way, every time. And it's not even like we're locked out the way you'd be with an actual brain, we can see every weight and run any ablation or patching experiment we want on this thing. Still couldn't settle it.

Priya's pretty convinced this is basically a data problem and that a bigger or cleverer batch of probe sentences will eventually break the tie. I've started to wonder if that's actually guaranteed, like maybe there's no possible set of tests, even in principle, that pins down which label is the "correct" one, and we're each just laying our own concept on top of the same pile of activation statistics. Neither of us have any background in philosophy so we don't really have the vocabulary to argue about this properly and just keep going in circles. So who's actually right here: is there a real fact of the matter about what this neuron means that better experiments would eventually uncover, or could this be a case that genuinely can't be settled no matter how much data we throw at it? And if it's the second one, is that just a quirky ML thing, or is it a more general problem that shows up elsewhere too?
