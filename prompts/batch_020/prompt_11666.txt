I spent about 35 years as a field ecologist, mostly doing population counts on small mammals and ground-nesting birds, and I've only really gotten serious about philosophy since I retired a couple of years back. My granddaughter had to read Aldo Leopold's Sand County Almanac for a class recently and asked me what I made of it, which sent me back to "The Land Ethic" chapter for the first time since grad school. Around the same time I'd been poking around some effective-altruism-adjacent writing and stumbled into this whole "wild animal suffering" argument, which I gather traces back to an economist (I think his name was Ng) who wrote about something called "welfare biology," and got picked up later by people like Horta and Tomasik.

The two don't sit well together and it's been bugging me since. Leopold's line is basically that an action is right if it preserves it's integrity, stability and beauty of the biotic community, and individual deaths, even nasty ones from starvation, disease or predation, are just the system doing what it does, so intervening with contraceptives or culls or medicine is usually hubris unless you've got a really solid ecological reason. The wild animal suffering people are looking at more or less the same facts I spent my career quantifying, r-selected species pumping out huge broods so the overwhelming majority die young and often gruesomely, and drawing the opposite conclusion: that this is a moral emergency, and "ecosystem integrity" is no answer at all to an animal that is, say, dying slowly of a botfly infestation.

Since I was trained my whole career to think in Leopold's terms, I can recite that side of it easily, but I honestly can't tell anymore whether it actually rebuts the suffering argument or just changes the subject. Could you compare the two positions properly for me, laying out the real structure of the disagreement rather than just the mood of each side, and then give me your own honest verdict: does the land ethic have a genuine answer to the wild animal suffering challenge, or is it basically dodging the question? I'd rather you commit to a position than tell me reasonable people just disagree.
