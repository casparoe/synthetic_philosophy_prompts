I'm TAing a philosophy of mind course this term and finally read Turing's actual 1950 paper instead of just the textbook summary of the imitation game, and I was NOT ready for the bit where he takes ESP completely seriously as a threat to the test -- he says the statistical evidence for telepathy is "overwhelming" and seriously proposes running the game in a "telepathy-proof room" so a psychic contestant can't cheat. As far as I can tell the evidence he had in mind was basically Soal's card-guessing work with Basil Shackleton, which was treated as the gold standard of rigorous parapsychology at the time -- except it came out after Turings death that Soal had been quietly doctoring the score sheets, and pretty much every other big psi result since has fallen apart under scrutiny too, one way or another (Bem's 2011 precognition studies being the most famous recent case, though that one's more a replication-crisis story than outright fraud). Knowing what we know now, what do you think Turing would actually say if this were put to him? Would he treat Soal as a one-off embarrassment and keep the door open for ESP in principle the way he clearly wanted to, or does the pattern undercut something more basic about how he was weighing "overwhelming" statistics against his own scientific instincts elsewhere in the paper? Give me your actual best reconstruction of his reasoning, not just a guess at the punchline.
