I sit on the committee that handles honorary fellowship nominations at the Oxford college I went to -- they keep a novelist around for 'the humanities angle.' This year's nominee is a retired air marshal being honoured for ending a three-year seige: he ordered strikes on a city's power grid and water plants, knowing they'd kill several thousand civilians fast, and the seige broke within a week instead of grinding on for another year, which most estimates say would've killed far more people. Two members keep invoking the Anscombe/Truman degree row from the 50s as grounds to vote no, and honestly I only half remember what she actually argued beyond 'something something double effect.' Could you draft the memo I'd circulate before the vote -- an actual recommendation, not a survey of both sides, reasoned through with what Anscombe was actually claiming rather than the trolley-problem version people usually reach for, and fair enough to the air marshal's advocates that they'd recognise their own case in it even while losing.
