So I've been the Dungeon Master for my friend group's D&D campaign for almost two years now and I'm pretty burnt out, but every time I bring up stepping back, at least one person acts like I'm abandoning ship. Here's what's bugging me: I never actually agreed to be "the DM" in any real sense. It started as a one-shot I offered to run because we were bored one weekend, then people just started asking "so when's the next session," and saying yes each time felt easier than explaining why not, and eventually there was a whole campaign with character backstories and a group chat named after my villain. When I said last week that I wanted to take an indefinite break, one friend said, almost word for word, "you can't just quit on us, we're in this together," like I'd broken some promise, even though I can't point to a single moment where I actually promised anything.

My partner, who's never even met these people, just said "well, did you say yes to being the DM or not, its really not that complicated," but that doesn't feel right either - it kind of feels like something real got built here even without anyone ever formally agreeing to it, and now I don't know if that "something" is a real obligation or just my friends being dramatic. Am I actually on the hook for this campaign in some way, even though there was no single moment I signed up for it, and if I am, what would actually be the fair way to step back rather than just ghosting the group chat?
