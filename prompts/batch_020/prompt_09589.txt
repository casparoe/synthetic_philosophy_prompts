Write a dialogue between a suffering-focused wild-animal-welfare advocate and an ecological holist in the tradition of Aldo Leopold's land ethic, who holds that the basic unit of moral value is the "integrity, stability, and beauty" of the biotic community rather than the sum of individual welfare. The welfarist should open by pointing out that most wild animals belong to r-selected species whose offspring mostly die shortly after birth, typically through starvation or predation, and should press Tom Regan's charge that a land ethic taken seriously licenses sacrificing individuals for the good of the whole -- what he called "environmental fascism." The holist should resist simply equating "wrong" with "causes suffering," say what a wrong done to nature would even look like on their view, and turn the question back on the welfarist by asking whether they accept the apparent upshot of their own premises: that a world with substantially less predation would be better, if it could be brought about without worse side effects. Neither side should strawman the other, and each reply should engage the specific point just made rather than restate a settled position.
