So this happened in my friend group recently and I can't stop turning it over in my head. We've got this running bit where someone dares someone else to do something mildly gross for cash, paid instantly over venmo, no take-backs. My friend Alex told our friend Priya he'd send her $100 right then and there if she could look him in the eye and say she genuinely intended to eat a whole raw onion like an apple at brunch the next morning. He said the money was hers either way, he wasnt gonna claw it back if she chickened out, he just wanted to see if she could actually talk herself into meaning it that night. She said it, he sent the cash, and then... she didn't eat the onion. Said she totally meant it the night before, even bought the onion, but by morning it grossed her out too much and there was no reason to go through with it once the money had already cleared.

This turned into a whole argument between two other friends of ours, Sam and Del, at brunch:

(1) Sam said Priya basically scammed Alex -- she could've predicted that the second she woke up with the money already hers, she'd have zero reason to eat the onion, so "intending" it the night before while already knowing that was really just self-deception, not a genuine intention.
(2) Del said that's harsh -- she wasn't lying, intentions aren't guarantees, and plans fall through all the time when your feelings (or stomach) change, that doesn't make them fake after the fact.
(3) Sam said this isn't normal plan-revision though: the whole reason to follow through was designed to disappear the second she got paid, and that was totally predictable going in, which makes it different from just changing your mind about something.
(4) Del said fine, but by that logic no promise that gets paid up front could ever be sincere, which seems way too strong -- people take deposits and advances and still follow through constantly, with no clawback in sight.

Kinda reminds me of some decision theory thing I half remember, about a billionaire and a toxin, but I don't know it well enough to say if it's actually the same issue. Can you go through Sam and Del's exchange and say who's got the better of it at each point, not just who wins overall -- and then tell me straight, is what Alex asked for even something a person can sincerely do?
