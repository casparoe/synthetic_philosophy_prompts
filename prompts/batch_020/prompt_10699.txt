I've been teaching myself some decision theory, and I recently came across a puzzle called "Death in Damascus." A man meets Death, who has an uncanny habit of predicting exactly where a person will be, and learns he is marked to die the next day; he flees that night to Aleppo, only to find Death already waiting there, precisely because Death had foreseen the flight. What unsettles me isn't just that Death always turns up, but a subtler point some philosophers raise: whichever plan the man settles on, the fact that he has settled on it should make him more confident that this was exactly what Death foresaw, which makes that very plan look like the losing one and pushes him toward the other, which then falls apart for the same reason. Even flipping a coin doesn't obviously help, since Death is supposed to be good enough to see that coming too, so there doesn't seem to be any plan that still looks sensible once it has actually been chosen. I'd like to see that specific bind dramatized rather than explained: could you write a short story in which a character is trapped in exactly this loop, so that the tension comes from a choice never managing to stay rational once it is made, rather than from a last-minute twist where fate simply catches up with him?
