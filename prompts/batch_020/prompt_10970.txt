In my course on intuitionistic logic and constructive mathematics, we recently covered Markov's Principle: for a decidable predicate P on the natural numbers, ¬¬∃n P(n) implies ∃n P(n). My first instinct was that this should already be a theorem of intuitionistic logic rather than a substantive extra axiom — if a contradiction follows from assuming P fails at every n, why isn't simply testing P(0), P(1), P(2), … in turn already the constructive procedure that produces the witness? Yet apparently the principle is independent of Heyting arithmetic, Brouwer's own intuitionism rejects it, and it is really only the Russian school of recursive constructive mathematics, founded by Markov himself, that accepts it as a basic principle. Could you help me see exactly where the "just keep searching" reasoning fails to meet the intuitionistic standard of proof, and what is philosophically at stake in accepting or rejecting the principle?
