I work as a software engineer and have no formal background in philosophy, but a friend of mine who is finishing a PhD in epistemology recently spent an evening walking me through what she called "Agrippa's trilemma": the old problem that any chain of justification for a belief — "why believe A?" "Because B." "Why believe B?" "Because C." and so on — seems doomed to end in one of three unsatisfying ways. Either the chain stops at some basic belief that isn't itself justified by anything further (foundationalism), it loops back on itself so that beliefs end up supporting one another in a circle (coherentism), or it just keeps going, reason after reason, without end (infinitism). What stuck with me is that she was oddly insistent that infinitism isn't secretly just coherentism in disguise — apparently real infinitists require the chain to be infinite and non-repeating precisely so it doesn't collapse into circularity, and there's a whole further debate about whether a finite mind could ever possess such an endless chain of reasons at all.

Listening to her, I kept thinking there's a genuinely good joke buried in this setup, in the same spirit as the jokes programmers tell about recursion with no base case or a chain of function calls that never bottoms out. Would you write one for me? I'd like the punchline to actually turn on the differences between foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism as rival answers to the regress problem, rather than being a generic "philosophers argue about pointless things" joke — ideally the kind of joke that lands noticeably better for someone who knows the three positions (including the non-repeating-chain detail) than for someone who has only just been told the three big words. A short one-liner or a two-line exchange would be perfect; it doesn't need to be an elaborate scene.
