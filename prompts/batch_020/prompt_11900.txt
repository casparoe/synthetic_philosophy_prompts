I'm writing a dissertation chapter that puts Derek Parfit's reductionism about personal identity in dialogue with Buddhist philosophy, and I want to make sure I'm not overstating the parallel. Parfit himself observes, near the end of Part Three of Reasons and Persons, that his Reductionist View—that there is no further fact about personal identity over and above physical and psychological continuity—closely resembles the Buddha's teaching of anatta, right down to the claim that our sense of being a single, continuously existing subject is illusory. Given that convergence, and given that both Parfit and the Buddhist tradition treat fully accepting this as somehow liberating, it's tempting to treat two millennia of Abhidharma, Madhyamaka, and Yogācāra debate about the metaphysics of no-self as essentially a historical anticipation of Parfit's argument, differing only in idiom and packaging. Is that basically right, or is there something philosophically important that gets lost if I read anatta simply as Parfit avant la lettre?
