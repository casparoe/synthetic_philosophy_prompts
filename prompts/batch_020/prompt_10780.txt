I sit on the small grants panel of a foundation that funds replication work in the behavioral sciences. A grantee up for a fourth-cycle renewal has twice now responded to a null preregistered replication of their signature depletion effect by adding a new, previously unmeasured moderator -- first task involvement, then trait self-monitoring -- rather than abandoning the theory, and now wants a fourth cycle to test the latest version. Two panelists call this a textbook Lakatosian degenerating research programme and want to defund it outright. Two others object that the same complaint, applied consistently, would convict Le Verrier of pseudoscience for positing Neptune to rescue Newtonian mechanics from Uranus's orbital anomalies, a move structurally identical to his later, failed positing of Vulcan to rescue it from Mercury's -- so "looks ad hoc" can't be the actual test, and they want to know what is. Please draft the panel's renewal memo: recommend fund, defund, or fund-with-conditions; state explicitly what you think does separate a legitimate, falsifiability-preserving auxiliary hypothesis from an ad hoc rescue; apply that standard to this case; and give the losing side's strongest argument a fair hearing before ruling against it.
