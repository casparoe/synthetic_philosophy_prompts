My uncle Denny passed away nine days ago after almost eight years with early-onset Alzheimer's, and I got asked to give the eulogy at his memorial this Saturday. I'm a backend engineer, I have never written a speech in my life, and I have zero philosophy background beyond whatever Denny explained to me over the phone, so this has been a genuinely terrible week of drafts.

Here's the specific thing I'm stuck on. A couple years into his diagnosis Denny fell down a rabbit hole of cognitive science podcasts and got attached to this "extended mind" idea — there's apparently a famous thought experiment (I think its by Clark and Chalmers?) about a guy named Otto who has Alzheimer's and uses a notebook exactly the way the rest of us use memory, and the argument is that the notebook basically counts as part of Otto's actual mind, not just a tool sitting outside it. Denny thought this was the funniest and most validating thing he'd ever heard. He started calling his own system of notebooks and phone reminders "the Otto stack" and told anyone who'd sit still long enough that he wasn't losing his mind, he was just moving more of it outside his skull. He was also a pretty militant atheist about it — he'd get almost annoyed if you made it sound sentimental, because the whole point for him was that it was engineering, not a soul, not an afterlife, nothing mystical.

The memorial is going to be half his old firmware team plus a philosophy-of-mind meetup he'd started going to, who will absolutely clock it if I get "the Otto stack" wrong or chicken out and go generic, and half family who have never heard any of this and just want to feel ok for twenty minutes. Every draft I write wants to land on "some part of Denny lives on in all those notebooks," and it feels good to write, but I don't actually think its true to the theory — the notebook only ever worked because Denny was there using it, so once he's gone it seems like its just a stack of paper and old phones, not a mind. I don't want to accidentally turn his own joke into the exact kind of woo he spent his life making fun of, but it clearly meant something real to him, so I don't want to just cut it either. Can you write me the actual eulogy, something like 5-6 minutes read out loud, that's honest about what this idea does and doesn't let me say, works for people who've never heard of it, and doesn't turn into a philosophy lecture at my uncle's funeral?
