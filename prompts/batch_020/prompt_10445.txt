I am several years into a novel built from my own childhood, centered on a father who was, by every account including my own memory, a cruel man. In researching him for the book, I have reconstructed enough about his upbringing — a violent, alcoholic household, and what looks in hindsight like untreated trauma from his military service — that I can now trace nearly everything he did to us back to something that was done to him first. The strange effect of all this research is that my resentment toward him has been quietly draining away: some days I can no longer blame him, only account for him, and I notice that I am starting to lose him as a character and gain him as a case history. That trade unsettles me more than the old anger did. If a sufficiently complete causal story can dissolve blame this reliably, and in principle such a story could be told about anyone's worst behavior, was my resentment ever tracking something real to begin with, or is "he couldn't help it" simply what we conclude once we happen to have gathered enough facts? I would like your honest assessment rather than reassurance: does understanding him this thoroughly mean that I was wrong to have blamed him, or is something being lost in the exchange?
