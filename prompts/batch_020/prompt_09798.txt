Ok, this has been bugging me: a productivity blog I read said that before a big presentation, you should just decide to believe you'll do great (not fake confidence, actually believe it) because the belief itself calms you down and improves performance, regardless of your actual odds of doing great. I tried it before a talk last month and it worked — I was way less nervous. But afterward it bugged me that I hadnt updated on any evidence, I'd just picked the belief because having it seemed useful. This feels adjacent to that rationalist-blog stuff about "belief in belief" or self-deception, but not quite the same thing — I wasnt fooling myself about the odds, I was just choosing to believe something for reasons that had nothing to do with whether it was true. Is there something actually confused about forming a belief that way, or does it stop mattering once the belief starts making itself a bit more true?
