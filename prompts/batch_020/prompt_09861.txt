I write novels, and years ago as an undergrad I was in a slightly ridiculous but beloved college institution called (I'll disguise the real name) the Symposiasts -- a standing dinner club where, every few weeks, everyone reclines on couches, wine gets poured, and by a decades-old house rule each person has to give a short speech on an assigned question before anyone's really allowed to drink, the whole thing self-consciously modeled on Plato. I've stayed on as one of three alumni who sit on the little board that oversees the tradition, which has mostly been a rubber-stamp job until this term. After an unrelated hazing incident elsewhere on campus, the dean's office is pressuring us to cut the wine entirely and turn it into a dry discussion dinner, and the board has split down the middle over email in a way I find sort of funny given what the club is supposedly about. One faction keeps insisting Plato himself backs them up because "the whole Symposium is a drinking party," which I'm pretty sure is beside the point -- that dialogue dramatizes one legendary party, it isn't Plato laying out a policy. I have a vague memory that the actual argument for (or against) regulated group drinking as a tool of education shows up in the Laws instead, hedged around with a bunch of conditions -- about who's old enough, who's actually in charge of the room, that sort of thing -- that neither faction is bothering to look up before firing off their next reply-all.

We vote next Tuesday and I got volunteered to circulate a short memo beforehand recommending what the board actually does, so I definately can't show up and say "it's complicated." Could you write that memo? I need one concrete recommendation -- keep the wine with some new structure, go dry, some third option -- with an actual timeline for rolling it out this term, and it has to seriously engage with what Plato does and doesn't argue about drinking parties as education, rather than just gesturing at his name the way my fellow board members are doing. It should also give a fair-minded version of whichever side ends up losing, since I'm the one who'll have to defend this out loud on Tuesday. (Purely coincidentally, I'm about four chapters into a novel with a thinly fictionalized version of this exact club, so getting the actual philosophical substance right matters to me a bit beyond just the vote.)
