So a few of us from my old philosophy reading group got dinner last night and it turned into a two hour argument, mainly me against my friend Priya (she's doing her PhD on Wittgenstein) about On Certainty. I've been sitting on this idea for a while that the whole "hinge" picture is built on two metaphors that dont actually fit together, Priya thinks im overreading a throwaway image, and we never resolved it, so now I want a more sober second opinion than two people who'd each had a couple beers by that point.

Here's the argument roughly as I'd write it up if I ever turned it into something. In the famous hinge passage (around §341-343) Wittgenstein pictures certain propositions as hinges that the rest of our doubting and asserting turn on - fixed, singular, not really the kind of thing that gets doubted or justified, just what has to stay put for the practice to work at all, like the actual metal hinge on a door. But earlier, in the river-bed passage (around §96-99), the image is totally different: the line between what's "hardened" and what's "fluid" itself shifts over historical time, propositions migrate from fluid to hardened and back again, the bed is partly hard rock and partly sand that gets washed away and re-deposited somewhere else. My claim is that these aren't just two paintings of the same idea, they pull toward different accounts of what a hinge actually is. The hinge image fits something like Moyal-Sharrock's line that hinges are categorically non-epistemic, not really truth-apt the way ordinary empirical beliefs are, closer to grammatical rules than to propositions. The river-bed image fits something closer to Wright's entitlement picture, where hinges are still full-blown propositions of the same logical kind as empirical claims, it's just that our acceptance of them is a rational entitlement rather than an evidential warrant - which is a much less radical claim than Moyal-Sharrock's. Priya's comeback was basically "he's not doing systematic philosophy, stop demanding the metaphors cash out into one consistent theory," which felt like a dodge to me but I'm honestly not sure it is one. My own half-baked fix, which I came up with mid-argument, is that the hinge image is right synchronically (at a time, within a given practice, hinges are fixed and non-epistemic) and the river-bed image is right diachronically (which propositions get to play that role changes across history) - but I can't tell if that's an actual solution or just a clunkier way of restating what Pritchard's "quasi-fideism" already says, if that's even the right name for it.

Could you go through this like it's a rough draft of an essay rather than just being encouraging about it? Specifically I want to know: (1) whether the tension between the two metaphors is real or whether I'm mashing together remarks that Wittgenstein means to do different jobs, (2) whether Priya's "it's not a theory" response actually dissolves the problem or is a cop-out, (3) whether my synchronic/diachronic fix basically collapses into Pritchard's view, or Wright's, or some third position I'm not naming right, and (4) whether this is even a point worth building a whole essay around or if it's more of a footnote. I'd rather hear that the central claim doesn't hold up than get told it's great.
