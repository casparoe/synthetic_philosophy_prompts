Weird question that's been nagging me while I draft my current novel, a family saga. Pretty much everything I know about how my family actually got out during the famine comes from one interview with my 96-year-old great-aunt -- a vivid story about her own grandmother walking forty miles through a snowstorm with a baby to dodge the workhouse -- and there's no way to check any of it: no letters, no records, everyone else who could've backed her up died decades ago. Feels wrong to doubt her for no reason, but also lazy to just call it 'true' in the book because she said so, especially since even the archives I'd check it against are themselves just some clerk's word from way back, so its not like I can ever get fully outside somebody's word anyway. Is there an actual principled answer for when you're entitled to just take someone's word for something like this, or does every claim really need independent backup before it counts as known?
