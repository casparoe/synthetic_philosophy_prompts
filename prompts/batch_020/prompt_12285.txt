I run a science-and-ideas podcast, and I've got a political theorist coming on next week to talk about a book she just published that leans really hard on the Condorcet Jury Theorem to argue majority-rule democracy is actually epistemically better than a panel of experts -- her pitch is that if each voter is even a hair better than a coin flip at picking the correct answer to some factual question, and everyone votes independently, the probability the majority gets it right rockets toward certainty as the electorate grows. I spent the weekend trying to get up to speed so I don't just sit there nodding, and it's clearly way messier than the one-paragraph pop-science version -- there's a whole literature on what happens once votes are correlated instead of independent, and apparently the theorem runs in reverse if competence dips under 50%, which none of the popular writeups ever mention. In the advance copy shes also got a pat answer ready for the obvious "but nobody votes independently, we all watch the same news and follow the same pundits" objection -- her line is that deliberation and shared information just give voters "a bigger, better sample to draw the same independent judgment from," which sounds slick to me but I'm not at all sure it holds up.

Could you draft me eight or nine interview questions that don't just let her restate the theorem, and that make that "bigger sample" line progressively harder to lean on as the interview goes? I want at least one question that goes straight after the independence assumption, one that makes her defend how she'd even know real voters clear the 50% competence bar on messy referendum-type questions (as opposed to, say, jellybean-counting-style tasks, where competence is at least easy to define), and one that forces her to seperate her argument from the totally different Scott Page "diversity trumps ability" result, since pop "wisdom of crowds" writing smooshes the two together constantly. Throw in one more on whether the theorem quietly assumes voters are tracking some independent truth rather than just registering preferences, since that assumption seems to be doing a huge amount of unacknowledged work for her. For each question, add a short follow-up keyed to whichever dodge she'd probably reach for first.
