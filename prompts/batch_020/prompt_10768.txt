Had a whole argument with a friend last night about what Schopenhauer would've made of atonal music, like Schoenberg. You know the bit in WWR where he says music is the odd one out among the arts because it copies the Will directly instead of the Platonic Ideas, and then does this whole mapping where the bass corresponds to the lowest grades of nature, the melody to conscious human striving, and dissonance/suspension to desire that only resolves back into consonance the way desire resolves into satisfaction. My friends take is that twelve-tone music, which ditches a home key entirely, would basically be his nightmare -- no stable consonance left for the dissonance to resolve into, so by his own theory it just fails to count as music, more like organized noise. I said it's actually the opposite: he's constantly insisting that satisfaction of the will is always partial and temporary anyway, and that ceaseless, unsatisfied striving is the will's real character, so permanently unresolved dissonance might be a MORE honest copy of the will than any tidy cadence ever was. We went back and forth for like an hour and got nowhere. Based on what he actually says about consonance, dissonance, and music copying the will rather than the ideas, what do you think his real verdict on atonal music would've been?
