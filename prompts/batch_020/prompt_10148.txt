I've been stuck on a subplot for my current novel and it's somehow leaked into an actual disagreement with my partner, so let me just lay out the real-life version and see what you think. There's a woman in my building, Renata, who spent basically all of last winter looking after our elderly neighbor after he broke his hip — grocery runs, sitting with him through doctor's appointments, the whole thing, easily eight or ten hours a week for months. Genuinely impressive commitment. Then a while back, over wine, she told me the real story behind it: she'd started doing all this mainly because her sister had called her selfish at a family dinner and it had gutted her, and she wanted to prove, mostly to herself, that it wasn't true. She was pretty upfront that if that fight with her sister had never happened she probably wouldnt have gone anywhere near that level of care for the neighbor — though she figures she'd still have hauled his bins out or grabbed him a coffee now and then, just not the whole sustained, structured caretaking part.

Now I can't stop turning this over, both for the book and for real life. My gut says there's something meaningfully different between our Renata and a version of her who'd have shown up every single day even without the sister drama, even though the old man's hip heals exactly the same either way and he has no clue any of this backstory exists. My partner says I'm just doing that annoying novelist thing where I can't let a good deed be a good deed and have to go digging around in someone's head for the "real" motive. Is he right that I'm overcomplicating a simple case, or is there an actual principled reason the caretaking should count for less, morally, than it would if the sister thing had never come up — and if so, what's actually doing the work there, given that the neighbor ends up just as well cared for regardless?
