The thing that's been bugging me since is van Fraassen's "best of a bad lot" objection: even if some hypothesis beats every rival we've actually thought up, that doesn't mean its even likely true, since the real explanation might not be among the candidates we generated in the first place — like congratulating the fastest horse in a slow race for being fast. My friend mentioned Lipton has some reply built around a distinction between the "likeliest" explanation and the "loveliest" one, but neither of us could reconstruct on the spot how that's suppose to answer the bad-lot worry rather than just describe what scientists happen to find satisfying.

Could you write me a dialogue between a scientific realist and a constructive empiricist in the van Fraassen mold hashing this out, maybe set at a conference bar after a talk on scientific realism? I want real back and forth, not two monologues: the empiricist should press the bad-lot objection as hard as it goes, the realist needs at least two genuinely different replies (not just "IBE has a good track record so calm down"), and whatever move the realist makes, the empiricist should have something substantive to come back with, for a few rounds at least. Both of them should clearly know the literature cold and be equally sharp — I don't want either one to be the designated loser going in. It doesn't have to end in some tidy handshake, but it should feel like the argument actually moved somewhere, rather than each side just restating its opening position in fancier words.
