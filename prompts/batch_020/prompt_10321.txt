So I've been an admin for my town's community Facebook group (it's got something like 15,000 members at this point, basically the whole town) for about three years now — I got voluntold into it after complaining too much about the last admin — and normally the worst drama we deal with is people fighting over parking spots or lost cats. Lately though it's turned into an actual fight among the mod team itself, and I honestly can't tell who's right, if anyone is.

We have one rule that just says "no posting misinformation," and it never occured to anybody to spell out what that actually means until two posts blew up in the same week. First one: a woman posted a warning that some guy was going door to door pretending to be from the gas company so he could get into people's houses, said her cousin heard it straight from an actual police officer, sounded completely legit, and she was obviously just trying to look out for people. Except that exact story has apparently been going around every couple of years for over a decade, and nothing was actually happening in our town right then — she'd just been fed an old hoax by someone she had every reason to trust. Second one, same comment thread: a guy replies almost instantly, "nah, that's the fake story that goes round every year," and admits later he had zero idea if it was true this time, he just always assumes it's fake, and this time he happened to be dead right. We've ALSO got a messier third case sitting in the report queue where somebody hedged with "not sure if this is even true but just in case..." about a different rumor that also turned out false, and I don't even know if that one belongs in the same conversation or not.

Half the mod team wants to give the first woman the standard misinformation warning and leave the guy's comment up, because hey, it was true. The other half of us think that's backwards, since she actually had a decent reason to believe what she posted and he had none at all, he just guessed and got lucky, and if the whole point of the rule is to stop people being careless with claims that spook the entire neighborhood, he's the one who was careless, true or not. Could you write this up as the kind of short memo I'd actually send to the rest of the admin team before we hash this out this weekend? I want it to actually say what we should do about these specific posts instead of just "use your best judgment," and to propose how we should reword the rule itself so we're not refighting this same battle next month — and please don't just wave off the "but he was RIGHT, doesn't that count for something" side of it, because that's genuinely what half my team believes, and they're not being idiots about it.
