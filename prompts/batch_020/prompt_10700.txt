I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, and this term I have been auditing a formal epistemology seminar that spent several sessions on algorithmic information theory as a candidate rigorous foundation for confirmation theory. The instructor presented Solomonoff induction -- which assigns hypotheses a prior probability that decreases with their Kolmogorov complexity relative to some fixed universal Turing machine -- as, in effect, a mathematically precise vindication of Occam's razor: shorter programs receive more prior weight, and unlike informal talk of "simplicity," this can supposedly be made exact.

What has been nagging at me since is the fine print of the invariance theorem. Kolmogorov complexity is machine-independent only up to an additive constant that depends on the reference machine (roughly, the length of the interpreter needed to simulate one universal machine on another). If I am remembering the construction correctly, for any two particular strings you can build a universal machine that reverses their complexity ranking simply by hard-coding one of them as a primitive. That seems to threaten the claim that the "simplicity" being formalized here is objective and language-independent, rather than a technical restatement of whatever ordering happens to be built into our choice of machine. There is a related circularity worry: since the universal prior is constructed by weighting each program by 2^-(length) in the first place, the preference for shorter descriptions is stipulated into the framework rather than derived from anything more basic, so invoking Solomonoff induction to "justify" Occam's razor can look like it presupposes the very thing it is meant to establish. The reply I have encountered appeals to dominance and convergence results showing that predictions converge on the true data-generating process in the limit regardless of which universal machine one starts from, provided that process is computable -- but this obviously requires enough data and a computable target, so I am unsure how much philosophical weight it can really carry.

Could you write an essay that lays out this dispute in some depth and actually settles it, rather than concluding that reasonable people simply disagree? Please weigh the language-dependence and circularity objections against the dominance and convergence replies, and then defend a specific verdict on whether Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity give Occam's razor a genuine, non-circular justification, a merely formal restatement of it, or something in between -- for instance, a vindication that holds only asymptotically, or only relative to some class of admissible machines -- and explain why that verdict, rather than the alternatives, is the one to draw.
