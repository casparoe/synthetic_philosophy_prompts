my grandma died on sunday and i'm giving the eulogy saturday, which is freaking me out a bit since i'm in a seminar right now literally covering Wittgenstein's lectures on religious belief, and it kind of feels like they were written about her. she was a lifelong churchgoer who always refused to talk about the Last Judgment or heaven like they were facts you could be more or less sure of, and she'd get quietly annoyed at other people's funerals when someone said stuff like "there's no doubt she's up there watching over us" — to her that was treating faith like something you put odds on, which wrecked the whole point of believing it. most of my family are just regular churchgoing people who'll want exactly that kind of talk though, and a couple of women from her old church reading group (Wittgenstein, Kierkegaard, that whole crowd, for like 20 years) will be there and will 100% notice if i cave and give her some generic "shes in a better place now" send-off. can you write the eulogy? it has to comfort the relatives for real, not just be clever, but it can't quietly smuggle in the exact kind of claim she spent her life pushing back on.
