So this is a weird one but it's been eating at me for weeks. I just got voted onto my tribe's enrollment committee, and the first real case we're taking up is an application from a woman whose great-grandmother is right there on our base roll as full-blood, so the lineage is airtight, but three generations of marrying out since then puts her around 1/8, well under the 1/4 blood quantum our constitution requires. Meanwhile we've got citizens on the rolls who clear 1/4 easily but have never shown up to a General Council meeting, never taken a language class, nothing. It's not exactly abstract for me either, since my own kids will be in almost her exact position once they're grown — my husband isn't Native — so I can't really pretend to be a neutral party here.

The committee's split down the middle. Half want to hold the line on blood quantum because it's the only non-political, non-favoritism way to keep the rolls from growing past what our per-capita and healthcare budgets can handle, and a couple of the older members still remember the rolls getting quietly "cleaned up" once before for basically that reason. The other half say blood quantum is a bookkeeping method we inherited from the federal government's allotment era and has nothing to do with who's actually part of the nation, and that lineal descent plus real participation should be what counts instead. I mentioned the whole mess to my housemate, who's ABD in philosophy, and she said its basically a pocket-sized version of the open-borders-vs-right-to-exclude debate that political philosophers argue about at the scale of whole countries. I haven't been able to un-see that framing since.

Could you draft the memo I need to bring to next month's meeting? I need it to actually land on a recommendation -- approve her, deny her, approve with some conditional status (and if so, what the conditions are and how long she gets to meet them), or send the blood-quantum rule itself back for review -- not just lay out both sides and shrug. And whatever it recommends, the part dealing with the losing side needs to be the strongest version of that argument, not a strawman, because half my committee will see through anything weaker. Assume whoever reads it is smart but hasn't read a page of political philosophy in their life.
