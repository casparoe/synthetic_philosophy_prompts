So every year my department's grad society runs this kind of ridiculous but genuinely fun event where a faculty member spends months prepping and then does a public Q&A fully in character as some dead thinker -- they're only allowed to answer with things the person could actually have argued, no modernizing, no breaking character even when someone in the audience tries to trip them up with something anachronistic. This year it's late Mill, specifically the Mill of the unfinished Chapters on Socialism, not the On Liberty/utilitarianism guy everyone already has three opinions about before they've read a word of him. My reading group just worked through the actual text (which, being four rough draft chapters he never got to revise, is honestly kind of a mess in places) so I got voluntold to help write questions, and I don't want us handing him softballs he can answer with "I was sympathetic to some socialisms and not others" and move on.

Can you draft me 8-10 questions for this? I want at least one that corners him on the gap between the younger Mill, who mostly treats private property and competition as close to settled facts of nature, and the older one who's far more willing to say the existing property system is a historical accident rather than anything natural or earned -- push on whether that's a real change of mind or a concession that costs him nothing since he still won't touch actual expropriation. I want one on why he rated Fourierism so far above ordinary communism -- something about Fourier having pre-empted basically every objection in advance with these "self-acting contrivances" for making even the unpleasant jobs attractive -- and the follow-up should press whether paying or rotating people through the dirty work actually solves the incentive problem or just hides it better. I want one built around the 1869 wage-fund recantation, where he publicly walked back something he'd taught as settled for two decades -- ask whether that episode should make us more or less confident in his read on socialism, seeing as he's already on record being spectacularly wrong about how labor markets work. And I want one that goes after the tension between the harm-principle individualist and the guy who's suddenly open to folding people's economic lives into jointly run concerns -- don't let him get away with just saying cooperatives are voluntary, ask what "opting out" is even supposed to mean once most of the economy is organized that way. For each question give me a short, sharp follow-up keyed to whatever answer he's most likely to reach for first -- I don't want the obvious dodge just sitting there unchallenged. Bonus round if you can fit it in: I have some memory of him worrying whether population stays in check once its not your own wages on the line under communism, but I've only ever seen that mentioned secondhand so don't force it into the list if you're not confident it's actually his argument.
