I've been working through Spinoza's Ethics on my own, and the idea that has stuck with me most is his claim that nothing in nature is contingent: everything follows from God's nature with the same necessity as it follows from the nature of a triangle that its angles equal two right angles, so that the universe could not have unfolded in any way other than it actually has. Lately I've also been reading about the fine-tuning argument in cosmology, which treats the values of the physical constants as though they easily could have been different and finds it striking that they happen to fall within the narrow range that allows for stars, chemistry, and life. It seems to me that Spinoza's picture of nature might undercut this whole way of framing the problem before it even gets off the ground. If Spinoza were presented with this argument today, how do you think he would respond to it?
