Me and my friend Priya got into it pretty bad last night over something that started as a dumb road-trip story and ended up a full-blown 1am debate neither of us could resolve. I was telling her about driving back from my parents' place a few weeks ago, how somewhere around hour four I definitely got too tired to be safely driving, but there was no specific moment I can point to where I noticed myself crossing the line — minute to minute I felt basically the same, groggy-but-fine, groggy-but-fine, and yet obviously "four hours in" me and "twenty minutes in" me were in totally different states. Priyas take was that this is just because "too tired to drive safely" is a vague phrase with no exact cutoff, so of course I can't locate a precise moment where I noticed it — there's nothing to notice because there's no sharp fact there to be noticed in the first place.

I don't think that's only a language problem though. Even if you gave me some completely made up precise definition — say, reaction time drops below some exact number — I still don't think I'd reliably notice the exact instant I crossed it, because whatever internal signal I'm using to judge my own drowsiness is presumably just as blurry from one minute to the next regardless of whether the underlying category has sharp edges or not. So it seems to me like there's a real, seperate question about how good we are at noticing our own state changes, apart from whether the words for those states are vague. When I said this, Priya's comeback was that fine, maybe I can't always tell the instant I've crossed the line, but surely I'd at least always know THAT I don't know — like there's some deeper layer of self-awareness about my own ignorance that never fails even when the first-order judgment does. That felt like a total cheat to me, like she just moved the exact same problem up one level instead of actually solving it, but it was 1am and I couldn't articulate why.

So who actually won this argument? Is Priya basically right that I'm just tripping over vague language and there's no further puzzle here, or is there a genuine issue about self-knowledge that survives even once you pin down the vagueness? And is her fallback move — that you always at least know when you don't know something about your own state — actually defensible, or does it just recreate the identical problem one step up rather than escaping it? I'd like an answer that actually engages with the structure of the disagreement rather than one that just says "well it depends how you define tired."
