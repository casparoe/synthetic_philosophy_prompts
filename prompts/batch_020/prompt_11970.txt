I'm outlining a snowbound country-house mystery, and I've hit a snag that turns out to be more philosophical than literary. My detective must name the culprit by dawn, when the roads clear and the guests scatter for good, so whatever she has gathered by then is all the evidence there will ever be. Two accounts fit it equally well: a lone groundskeeper acting on a private grudge, or three guests acting in concert, with any evidence that seems to tell against the conspiracy explained away as something the conspirators anticipated and staged. My editor insists that, since both accounts fit the facts equally well and always will, my detective has no rational basis for favoring one over the other, and that having her simply name the groundskeeper would be cheating the reader. I suspect my editor is moving too fast: is she right that the evidence alone leaves the choice arbitrary, or is there a principled way for my detective to prefer one account over the other anyway?
