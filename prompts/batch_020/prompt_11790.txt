So I went down a rabbit hole this week that started with rereading the old 2015 MIRI corrigibility paper (the Soares/Fallenstein/Yudkowsky/Armstrong one) and ended with me trying to work through Elliott Thornley's shutdown problem sequence, and now I cant stop thinking about how those two traditions are actually in tension with each other. The 2015 paper's main trick is basically Armstrong's utility indifference move -- bolt a correction term onto the agent's utility function so its expected utility doesn't change whether the shutdown button gets pressed, so there's no incentive either way. Thornley's argument (as far as I followed it, don't have the sequence open in front of me) is that this kind of patch can never really deliver shutdownability as long as the agent has complete preferences over entire trajectories, and that what you actually need is an agent with incomplete preferences between different-length trajectories plus some extra choice rule on top -- he calls it something like Timestep Dominance, might be misremembering the exact name -- to stop it from resisting shutdown without that making it exploitable.

Could you write me a dialogue between two AI safety researchers who each design shutdown mechanisms for a living, set right before they demo a prototype to a funder, standing on either side of the actual off-switch? One of them has spent years defending utility-indifference-style patches bolted onto a complete utility function; the other recently converted to thinking the whole enterprise is hopeless unless incompleteness is built in from the start. I want real disagreement here, not a strawman on either side -- push the utility-indifference researcher on whether the patch is reflectively stable (would it survive the agent building sub-agents or successors, and doesn't it just relocate the "nearest unblocked strategy" problem instead of solving it), and push the incomplete-preferences researcher on whether their fix isn't just a money pump waiting to happen, plus whether "incomplete preferences" is even something you can meaningfully implement in a system trained by gradient descent rather than specified by hand. Neither one should fully cave, but by the end I want each of them to have said out loud, once, the one objection to their own view that actually keeps them up at night.
