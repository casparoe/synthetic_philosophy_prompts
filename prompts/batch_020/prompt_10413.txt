So this went down in the #interpretability channel of a discord i'm in (small alignment-adjacent reading group, nothing fancy) and it's been bugging me for days. Some background everyone already knew going in: Othello-GPT is a transformer trained only to predict legal moves in the board game Othello, and there's a whole saga where the original paper found that linear probes couldn't decode the board state from its activations very well, which people took to mean there's no clean linear representation of the board -- until someone showed that if you recode each square as "mine/yours/empty" relative to whoever's turn it is, instead of "black/white/empty," a linear probe decodes it almost perfectly.

(1) Theo took this as basically vindicating realism about interpretability: the representation was there the whole time, we just had the wrong probe. He compared it to a coded message -- you don't concude there's no message in a ciphertext just because your first three decryption keys failed.

(2) Ash pushed back: the fact that "does it represent black/white" flips to no and "does it represent mine/yours" flips to yes purely depending on which linear transform you happen to test means there's no fact of the matter about what the network really represents, independent of the question you bring to it. That's not discovery, she said, that's projection.

(3) Theo said activation patching settles it -- if you intervene on exactly that direction and the model's move predictions shift the way the mine/yours theory predicts, that's the network's own computation using the variable, not just us reading tea leaves into noise.

(4) Ash said patching only shows the direction is causally load-bearing, which is a much cheaper claim than "representation" in the sense philosophers care about. Real content, Dretske-style, requires the possibility of misrepresentation -- some norm the internal state can actually fail to meet -- and nothing about a patching experiment gives you that. Calling a causally load-bearing variable a "representation," she said, is exactly the sleight of hand interpretability papers get away with.

(5) Theo replied that by that standard neuroscience never shows brains represent anything either, since lesion and stimulation studies give you the exact same causal-not-semantic evidence -- so Ash should either bite that bullet for brains too or explain what's actually different about neurons.
