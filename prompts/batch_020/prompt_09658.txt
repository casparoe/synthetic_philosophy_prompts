Okay so I'm plotting a time-travel novel where my protagonist, thanks to a busted machine, ends up in a room with herself from forty years back and they actually sit down and talk. I described the scene to my writing group, and two people there spun off into a side argument that's been bugging me since. Sasha said the whole premise is incoherent: if a person is wholly present at every moment of her life (the ordinary, common-sense view of how anything persists through time), then having 25-year-old her and 65-year-old her both fully in that room at once means the identical whole person is doubled up in one place at one time, which isn't time travel, its just a contradiction. Owen said that's endurantism talking, and this is exactly the kind of case perdurantism was invented for: people are four-dimensional things extended through time the same way they're extended through space, so meeting your younger self just means two different temporal parts of one long spacetime worm landing in the same spot, no weirder than two coils of a garden hose touching. Sasha said fine, maybe that clears up the contradiction for the flesh-and-bone part, but the two of them are also having an actual conversation, disagreeing, each surprised in real time by what the other says, so now you need two separate streams of consciousness running at once out of one being, and she didn't see how slicing a person into 'temporal parts' gets you that for free. Owen said consciousness is just as much a process as shape or hair length, so obviously it can be sliced into stages same as anything else about a person. Sasha wasn't satisfied but couldn't say exactly why, and Owen took that as basically winning.

Who's actually right here, move by move -- not just who "won" overall? And is there a genuine gap in Owen's last move, or was Sasha just grasping at straws by that point?
