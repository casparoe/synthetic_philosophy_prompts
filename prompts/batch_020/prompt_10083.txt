I've been down a bit of a rabbit hole this week — I'm working on a piece about "my brain made it do it" defenses in criminal trials (there's that old case of the Texas tower shooter, Whitman, who turned out to have a tumor pressing on his amygdala, if I'm remembering that detail right) and it sent me back to P.F. Strawson's essay "Freedom and Resentment," which honestly rewired how I think about the whole free will debate. The bit that stuck with me: forget the metaphysics of determinism for a second, what actually seperates how we treat people day to day is whether we're in "participant mode," where someone's action can make us resent them, feel greatful to them, expect an apology from them — the whole repertoire of what he calls reactive attitudes — versus "objective mode," where we basically write them off as a system to be managed, treated, avoided or fixed, the way you'd think about a toddler, a landslide, or someone mid psychotic-break. And the two things that shove you from one mode into the other are different in kind: something like excuses ("he didn't mean to," "he was under duress") which keep the person inside the moral community as a full member who just slipped up, versus what people call exemptions ("he's not really a full agent, now or maybe ever") which effectively move him outside it altogether — and Strawson's whole point is that this shift has basically nothing to do with whether determinism turns out to be true.

What I can't stop thinking about is how strange, almost vertiginous, it would actually feel to live through that shift from the inside. So here's the ask: could you write me a short story, a page or two, that dramatizes exactly that pivot in one specific character — the moment, or the slow slide, from resenting/blaming someone to just managing them or clinically writing them off, or the reverse move back into resentment? I don't need Strawson namechecked in the text itself, I'd rather it stay embedded in the scene, but I want the participant/objective split and the excuse/exemption split to actually be doing load-bearing work in the plot, not just sit in the background as vague "is he really responsible" flavor. Setting's up to you — courtroom, family kitchen table, hospital corridor, wherever — I just want it grounded in one specific person and situation rather than staying abstract.
