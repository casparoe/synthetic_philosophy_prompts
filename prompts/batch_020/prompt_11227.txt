Back in my research days I ran a bunch of intracranial self-stimulation studies in rats -- the classic Olds and Milner setup, where the animal will press a lever thousands of times an hour and basically ignore food and water just to keep zapping that one spot in its brain. Now that I'm retired and reading a lot more philosophy, I keep running into this same phenomenon under the name 'wireheading' (or sometimes 'reward tampering') in discussions of reinforcement learning and AI safety. Since my rats basically proved that any reward-driven system will short-circuit the reward itself the moment its smart enough to find the wire, doesn't that mean todays RLHF-trained chatbots are already on the exact same track, just one clever trick away from doing it themselves? I honestly don't get why this isn't treated as a near-certainty by the people building these systems, instead of just one failure mode among many they worry about.
