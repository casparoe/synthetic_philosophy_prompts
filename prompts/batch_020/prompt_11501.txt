At work we have been getting pushback about our lending-risk model: it is calibrated within each demographic group — predicted default probabilities match observed default rates for everyone — yet its false-positive rate (good customers wrongly denied credit) is higher for one group than another. While reading up on how to respond, I came across the impossibility results in the algorithmic fairness literature, particularly Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan's proof and Chouldechova's related work on the COMPAS controversy, which show that calibration and equal false-positive/false-negative rates across groups cannot both hold unless the groups share the same base rate or the model predicts perfectly. Given that our two groups do have different real-world default rates, doesn't that mathematically settle the matter — the disparity is simply a forced consequence of the base-rate gap rather than anything our model or team did wrong, so there is no actual fairness problem left for us to fix, only a statistical fact to explain to stakeholders?
