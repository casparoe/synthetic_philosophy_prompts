I'm on the Elections Board for my school's student council (we handle campaign complaints for class officer elections), and tomorrow's meeting has the board split down the middle. A candidate for senior class president didn't lie about anything, but for the last two days of voting she posted instagram stories with a countdown timer that reset every few hours ('polls close in 2 hrs, dont be the last one!!'), right next to a running tally of who'd already voted, built from screenshots supporters sent her of their 'I Voted' stickers. Undecided kids could watch the number climb and felt like everyone but them had already picked her -- nothing false, just true info arranged to pressure people. Half the board calls that normal hype-building, the kind every campaign does some version of; the other half says engineering that kind of social pressure to stampede people into voting without really thinking it through counts as manipulation even with zero lies involved. Our constitution just says campaigns have to be "honest and respectful," which doesn't really settle it either way. Can you draft the memo I have to bring to tomorrow's meeting? It needs to recommend something concrete (disqualify her, give her a warning, let the results stand but ban the tactic going forward, whatever) and actually defend that call, while taking the "it's just normal campaigning" side seriously instead of just brushing past it.
