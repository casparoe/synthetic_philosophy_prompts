So I fell down a total rabbit hole last week. There was a Hacker News thread that started out about property-based testing (throwing a ton of random inputs at a function and checking some invariant holds) and by like forty comments in somebody had brought up this Saul Kripke book about Wittgenstein and rule-following, specifically the "quus" thing, and I havent been able to stop chewing on it since.

For anyone who doesn't know it (I sure didn't till last week): Kripke's setup is that I've only ever added numbers smaller than, I think it was 57, at any point in my life. A skeptic points out that everything I've actually ever done with the symbol "+" is just as consistent with my having meant "quus" the whole time instead -- some deviant function that agrees with normal addition on every case I've ever tested, but spits out "5" the moment either number involved is 57 or bigger. And there's no fact about me -- not my past behavior, not my dispositions, not some inner "aha, THIS is what I mean" feeling when I use the plus sign -- that settles which one I actually meant. It's basically the "any finite pile of unit tests is consistent with infinitely many different implementations" problem, except Kripke uses it to argue meaning itself might not be a real fact about anyone, even right now, not just about the untested future.

Where I get lost is Kripke's actual way out of this, which he calls the "skeptical solution" (as opposed to just refuting the skeptic outright). As I understand it, his move is: stop asking what fact makes it true that I mean plus, and ask instead when we're entitled to say someone means plus, and his answer is basically, when their answers keep agreeing with everyone else's in the community. But that smells like a shell game to me. If no fact about ME pins down plus over quus, why would gluing together a bunch of other people, each just as individually indeterminate as I am, magically produce a real fact at the group level? Couldn't the whole community be "quus-ing" together in perfect agreement on every case anyone's actually computed so far, while secretly being one giant communal-quus that'd diverge the day somebody finally adds two numbers big enough? I don't want someone to just validate my "shell game" gut feeling here -- I want the strongest, most rigorous version of the argument that Kripke's move from truth-conditions to conditions of warranted assertion doesn't actually dissolve the paradox, it just relocates it up to the community level. Can you build that case for me?
