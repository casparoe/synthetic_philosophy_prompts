Two friends of mine, Naomi and Callum, sit on the five-person grant committee of a small foundation with which I'm also involved. Per the charter, a proposal is funded only if the committee judges it both (a) technically feasible and (b) counterfactually necessary (i.e., the work wouldn't happen anyway without the grant). At the last meeting, the chair polled members with a single up-or-down question ("fund, yes or no?") rather than voting on the two criteria separately, and the proposal failed 3–2. The individual judgments behind those votes came out afterward in discussion:

| Member | Feasible? | Necessary? | Personal vote (applying the charter's rule) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yes | Yes | Fund |
| 2 | Yes | No | Reject |
| 3 | No | Yes | Reject |
| 4 | Yes | Yes | Fund |
| 5 | No | Yes | Reject |

(1) Naomi objected that three of five members called the project feasible and four of five called it necessary — majorities on both of the charter's own criteria — so under the charter's stated rule the proposal should have passed; the straight vote simply buried that fact. (2) Callum replied that this is an arithmetic trick, not a hidden truth: only members 1 and 4 actually hold both judgments at once, so no real majority ever endorsed "feasible and necessary" together, and stapling the two separate majorities together into a verdict that no coalition of members jointly holds doesn't recover the committee's view; it fabricates one. (3) Naomi came back that Callum's standard makes the charter's criteria pointless: a member could vote "reject" for reasons having nothing to do with feasibility or necessity, and under a straight vote that dissent counts exactly the same as a principled, criteria-based one. (4) Callum's rejoinder was that this just relocates the problem — there's no procedure-independent fact about what the committee "really" thinks, since almost any aggregation rule will conflict with some other reasonable one on some cases; if the real worry is members voting on unstated grounds, the fix is a norm requiring members to justify their votes by the criteria, not a general claim that criterion-by-criterion voting should take priority over voting on the bottom line.

This felt like it must connect to something in judgment-aggregation or social-choice theory I've half-absorbed from years of reading rationalist blogs, but I couldn't work out who had the better of it at each step. Could you adjudicate the exchange move by move?
