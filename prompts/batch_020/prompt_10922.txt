My father died eleven months ago, and when I told my therapist that I still cannot get through most days without being overwhelmed by grief, she raised the possibility that I might meet the criteria for Prolonged Grief Disorder, the diagnosis the DSM-5-TR added in 2022. Looking into it afterward, I found that the ICD-11 version of the same diagnosis sets its duration threshold at six months of persistent yearning and preoccupation, while the DSM-5-TR insists on twelve, and that discrepancy has left me unsure whether "disorder" is really the right word for what I am going through, or whether it merely dresses up a contestable judgment about proper mourning as a medical fact. Write a short dialogue between a naturalist about disease, who argues along the lines of Christopher Boorse's biostatistical theory that whether a condition counts as a disease is simply a matter of statistically subnormal functioning, and a normativist, who holds that calling grief "disordered" always smuggles in a value judgment about harm and appropriate feeling. Have the two of them argue specifically about Prolonged Grief Disorder and its shifting duration thresholds, with each pressing the other's strongest objections rather than simply restating their own position.
