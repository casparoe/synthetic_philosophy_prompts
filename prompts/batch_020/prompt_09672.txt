My friend and I got into a whole thing this weekend, after watching a documentary about fraternity hazing deaths, about the US drinking age. She thinks the fix is stricter enforcement and pushing the age up; I think the stricter you make it, the worse the underground bingeing gets, and that kids would actually handle alcohol better if they'd been allowed to drink around adults way earlier, like a lot of Europe does. We argued for like an hour and neither of us budged an inch. At some point I remembered that Plato (I know, technically it's "the Athenian Stranger" talking, but everyone just calls it Plato's view) actually lays out a really specific position on this in the Laws -- literal age brackets, plus a sober "symposiarch" whose whole job is running the party so it doesn't get out of hand -- which is way more detailed than the usual "Plato thought drunkenness was shameful" takeaway people get from the Republic. So genuinely, not to settle our fight but becuase it's been bugging me since: going off what he actually argues there, what would Plato say about how American college kids drink now -- officially banned til 21, then dropped into fraternity culture with basically zero supervision? Would he see that as the worst of the two extremes he explicitly rejects -- the Spartan option of just banning drinking parties outright, and the unsupervised, wine-unmixed excess he pins on the Scythians and Thracians -- or is that too cute a reading of him?
