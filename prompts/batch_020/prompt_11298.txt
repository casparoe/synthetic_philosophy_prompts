I'm taking a seminar this term on the foundations of decision theory, and this week's unit covers Newcomblike problems and the case for so-called updateless decision theory. The example I can't stop turning over is Counterfactual Mugging: Omega, a perfect predictor, flips a fair coin and truthfully explains that, had it landed heads, it would have given you $10,000, provided it had predicted you would pay it $100 in the event of tails; the coin has in fact landed tails, so Omega is simply asking you for the $100, with nothing further riding on your answer either way. Please write a short dialogue between a causal decision theorist and an updateless decision theorist who find themselves facing exactly this request from Omega, each trying to talk the other out of their answer. They should engage directly with each other's strongest arguments rather than talking past one another, and neither side should be allowed to simply win by default.
