I'm on the steering committee for our little ham radio and backyard astronomy club - a few of us fixed up an old dish a while back that can actually transmit, not just listen. Some members want to use it to beam a compiled 'hello from Earth' message at a nearby sunlike star, kind of a homage to the old Arecibo broadcast, and honestly it'd be great publicity for the club. Other members are genuinely against it - their point is the galaxy looks awfully quiet (I've read way too much about the Fermi paradox over the years) and deliberately transmitting could be a bad idea, plus a random hobby club has no business deciding that for the whole planet on its own. The comeback is that we're already leaking radio and radar signals into space anyway, so whats the difference, though one member pointed out that a focused beam aimed right at one star isn't the same as background noise. I need to bring an actual decision memo to the vote in six weeks - go ahead, don't, or some conditional middle ground - can you draft it? Whatever you recommend, deal with the strongest version of the losing side's case, not a strawman.
