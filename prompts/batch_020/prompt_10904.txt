I'm a fourth-year philosophy PhD student (phil of medicine-ish stuff) and, through a mix of my cohort's apathy and me not reading an email closely enough, I'm now the grad-worker rep on my university's joint health benefits committee. Most meetings are just rubber-stamping premium tables, but this term we've got an actual fight.

Our self-insured plan's costs have been blown out by GLP-1 prescriptions, and the insurance consultant has proposed that starting next plan year, semaglutide and tirzepatide (Ozempic/Wegovy, Mounjaro/Zepbound) should only be covered for weight loss if the member has a diagnosed comorbidity -- type 2 diabetes, obstructive sleep apnea, hypertension, that kind of thing -- which would kill the current pathway that covers anyone at BMI 30+ with no comorbidity required at all. About half the commitee thinks this is just fiscal sanity, since BMI alone doesn't obviously track any real dysfunction in a given person; the other half thinks its discriminatory to make people wait until obesity has already caused visible damage before treating it, especially since stigma probably affects who gets worked up for those comorbidities in the first place. This is exactly the kind of case my reading group will happily argue about for two hours and then go home from, except now I actually have to produce a document, because underneath all the budget language everyone on the committee is really taking a stand on whether obesity itself is a disease -- and on whether that's the kind of thing you settle by looking at the body, or whether it's partly a judgment call about which bodily states we've decided count as bad -- and nobody will just come out and say that's the actual disagreement.

Can you draft the memo I need to bring to the vote in three weeks? It should land on one concrete policy -- cover it outright, drop it entirely, or cover with specific conditions -- give an effective date, and actually argue for that position with reasons the consultant and the skeptics on the committee could each respond to, rather than just listing considerations on both sides. I especially don't want it to dodge the strongest argument against whatever it ends up recommending; a wishy-washy "reasonable people disagree" memo won't survive five minutes at this meeting.
