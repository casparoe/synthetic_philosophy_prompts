I run a small podcast on decision theory, and next month's guest is a game theorist whose name I first ran across years ago in old LessWrong threads on "modal combat." Building on Tennenholtz's original result, she has spent much of her career defending the claim that two agents able to read each other's source code can rationally sustain cooperation in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with no repeated play or external enforcement. She's identified with the "Löbian" strand of that literature, which replaces brittle, exact-copy-matching agents like CliqueBot with provability-based agents such as FairBot and PrudentBot, and her stock reply to the obvious complaint that almost no real-world agent can actually verify another's source code is that "transparency is an engineering problem, not a decision-theoretic one." Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the program-equilibrium result. Include at least one question on Tennenholtz's folk theorem, which shows the same framework supports essentially any feasible, individually rational payoff and so doesn't by itself privilege cooperation over the alternatives; one on whether replacing direct simulation of the opponent's program with proofs about what that program would do -- the move Löb's theorem makes possible -- amounts to a genuine account of warranted trust or just a tidy way of dodging an infinite regress; and a short sequence that keeps pressing on her transparency deflection until it stops doing any real work, ending on a question about what result would actually count as evidence against the whole approach. For each question, add a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
