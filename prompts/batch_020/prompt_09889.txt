So I've been chewing on this thought for a while now, usually late at night when I can't sleep: when I say something like "the cat is on the mat," what actually makes "cat," "on," and "mat" stick together into ONE claim instead of just being three words sitting next to each other on a page? Like there's the cat, there's the mat, and there's this relation "on" — but what glues the relation to the two things its relating? I never took a philosophy class or anything, I just mentioned this to a friend and he said it sounded like the classic "unity of the proposition" problem, so I went down a bit of an internet rabbit hole and found out some guy named F.H. Bradley ran into basically the same wall over a hundred years ago while working out how qualities and relations hang together, and that people still call his argument "Bradley's regress." So I dug up the actual bit from his book Appearance and Reality (1893), and honestly, his Victorian prose is wrecking me a little. Here's the passage that's got me stuck:

"But how the relation can stand to the qualities is, on the other side, unintelligible. If it is nothing to the qualities, then they are not related at all; and, if so, as we saw, they have ceased to be qualities, and their relation is a nonentity. But if it is to be something to them, then clearly we now shall require a new connecting relation. For the relation hardly can be the mere adjective of one or both of its terms; or, at least, as such it seems indefensible. And, being something itself, if it does not itself bear a relation to the terms, in what intelligible way will it succeed in being anything to them? But here again we are hurried off into the eddy of a hopeless process, since we are forced to go on finding new relations without end. The links are united by a link, and this bond of union is a link which also has two ends; and these require each a fresh link to connect them with the old. The problem is to find how the relation can stand to its qualities; and this problem is insoluble."

Could you break down, like, step by step, what move he's making here? I get that he wants to connect the relation itself to the things it relates, but I don't get why that forces you into an endless chain instead of everything just being fine, the way "the cat is on the mat" feels totally fine to say out loud without my brain exploding. Also I keep seeing this regress described as "vicious" in stuff I read online and I don't really get what makes a regress vicious as opposed to just, I dunno, long or annoying — is there an actual technical difference or is it kind of a judgment call? And does cracking this old paragraph actually tell us anything about that "unity of proposition" question I originally started with, or are those secretly two different problems that just happen to look alike?
