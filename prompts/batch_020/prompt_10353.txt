I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and this term's seminar on the foundations of general relativity has spent the last several weeks on the hole argument: Earman and Norton's canonical reconstruction of Einstein's original puzzle as an argument against manifold substantivalism, and the responses it has provoked since. I've worked through Butterfield's and Brighouse's versions of "sophisticated substantivalism," which try to block the argument's indeterminism by denying that diffeomorphic models represent genuinely distinct possibilities, and also the structuralist retreats from writers like Esfeld and Lam, who want spacetime points individuated by their relations rather than by primitive haecceities. My advisor keeps sending my seminar-paper drafts back because I keep cataloguing positions instead of defending one, so before I attempt a final version I'd like to see a model essay that actually commits to something.

I'd like you to write that essay. Please start by laying out the hole argument clearly enough that its force is apparent — why the diffeomorphism freedom of generally covariant theories seems to saddle manifold substantivalism with a cheap, physically idle indeterminism — and say something about Einstein's own point-coincidence resolution of the original puzzle, since I've seen it characterized in secondary literature both as an anticipation of Leibniz-equivalence relationalism and as something closer to sophisticated substantivalism, and I'd like your take on which reading is more defensible. Then weigh the strongest substantivalist replies against the argument's force, focusing especially on whether sophisticated substantivalism is a principled way of individuating spacetime points or just relationalism in different clothes, and on whether the structuralist proposals actually dissolve the haecceitistic worry or merely relocate it. Please end by actually answering the question "does the hole argument defeat manifold substantivalism?" rather than surveying the field one more time — a conditional verdict such as "yes for the naive version, no for the sophisticated one, and here is why that is not just semantic maneuvering" would be an acceptable shape for an answer, but whatever you conclude, defend it as a real verdict rather than gesturing at one.
