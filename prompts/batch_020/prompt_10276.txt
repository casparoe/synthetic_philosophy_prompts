I'm assembling a small collection of short fiction, each story built around a genuine philosophical fault line, and I want to pressure-test one idea before committing to it. Confucian role ethics holds that a person's identity and duties are constituted by their relationships -- ruler and minister, parent and child, and so on -- rather than by some prior, relationship-independent self bearing abstract rights. The classic stress-test for this view is a dialogue in the Mencius: asked what should happen if the sage-king Shun's own father committed murder, Mencius answers that the minister of justice, Gaoyao, must simply arrest him, that Shun could not invoke his imperial authority to stop this, and yet that Shun would then abandon the throne itself -- discarding it as lightly as a worn-out sandal -- to flee into hiding with his father on his back, living out his days content. Write an original short story of roughly 1,200-1,500 words, with your own characters and setting, that dramatizes this exact structural bind: a role demanding impartial enforcement colliding with a role demanding partiality toward kin. I don't want a retelling of the Shun episode itself -- I want its central move, stepping outside a role rather than corrupting it, to feel earned on its own terms in whatever setting you invent.
