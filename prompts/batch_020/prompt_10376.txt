In a philosophy of religion seminar I am taking, we recently examined J. L. Schellenberg's argument from divine hiddenness: the claim that, since a personal relationship with God requires at least believing that God exists, a perfectly loving God would ensure that every capable person who is not actively resisting such a relationship in fact believes -- so that the actual existence of what Schellenberg calls "non-resistant" nonbelief, nonbelief that persists despite genuine and sustained seeking, counts as evidence against the existence of such a God. The reply that came up most in class was the idea, associated with John Hick, that God maintains a kind of epistemic distance so that belief is never coerced by overwhelming evidence, which would crowd out the freely chosen love that makes relationship with God valuable in the first place. What nobody in the room managed to do was press that reply on its actual weak point: granting that some hiddenness might serve this purpose, the theodicy does not obviously predict the specific pattern of nonbelief we observe, for instance lifelong, sincere seekers who never get so much as a flicker of evidence, alongside people who arrive at belief with barely any searching at all.

I would like you to write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated interlocutors -- one defending the hiddenness argument, one defending the epistemic-distance reply -- who work through this exact disagreement in real time rather than delivering prepared statements past each other. The theist should have to say something specific about why the distribution of belief and nonbelief we actually observe is compatible with the good that hiddenness is meant to secure, not just that hiddenness serves some good in general, and the hiddenness advocate should have to say something specific about what alternative distribution of evidence God could have brought about that would not itself undermine the very freedom the theist is pointing to. Neither side should simply concede; end the dialogue at the moment it becomes clear exactly which empirical or conceptual disagreement is doing the remaining work.
