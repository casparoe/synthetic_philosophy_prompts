I'm working on a longer piece about physicists and philosophers who argue over whether spacetime itself is "real" or just a bookkeeping device for relations between things, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole (sorry) on the actual "hole argument" -- the one Einstein originally worried about and that Earman and Norton revived in the 80s specifically to attack "substantivalism," the view that spacetime points exist as individuals independently of whatever fields sit on them.

The setup, as I now understand it after a week of reading: take a solution to Einstein's equations, pick a patch with no matter in it (the "hole"), and smoothly reshuffle which point gets which metric values only inside that patch, leaving everything outside the patch, and in its past, exactly as it was. Since the field equations are diffeomorphism-invariant, i.e. dont care about this reshuffling, a substantivalist who says the reshuffled version is a genuinely different possibility -- same points, different properties -- is stuck admitting the theory cant determine what happens inside the hole given absolutely everything outside it. The move that's been bugging me is the standard-ish escape some substantivalists use: deny that spacetime points carry any identity across the two versions of the solution (no "identity papers" that could get swapped -- I think the term of art is haecceitism, or maybe it's anti-haecceitism, forgive me if I've mangled it), so the two versions aren't rival possibilities at all, just one possibility described twice. That dissolves the indeterminism, but it also smells like it quietly hands the relationalist everything she wanted.

So here's what I'd like: a dialogue between a substantivalist who takes exactly that escape route and a relationalist who's pushing back on it, having it out over coffee after the talk where all this got presented. I want the substantivalist to actually run the "no identity across models" move properly, not just assert "spacetime is real, get over it," and I want the relationalist to press hard on whether that move leaves any real daylight between the two views or is a surrender dressed up in substantivalist clothes -- and then I want the substantivalist to come back with something genuine and non-question-begging, not just dig in and repeat themselves. Keep them anchored in the actual mechanics of the thought experiment -- what's happening to the metric inside the hole, what the diffeomorphism is doing to it -- rather than letting either of them retreat into slogans about "points" versus "relations," and don't let it resolve too neatly: if they end up close to agreement, or find a spot where they still genuinely differ, I want to see exactly how they got there, not just be told that they did.
