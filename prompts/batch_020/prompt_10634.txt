Okay so this is a really specific situation but I'm pretty sure there's an actual philosophy question buried in it. Two weeks ago about 70 kids at my school walked out of 4th period for 20 minutes to protest the district not renewing our AP Bio teacher's contract (a lot of people think it was payback for something he said at a school board meeting, though nobody really knows for sure). It was planned ahead of time and organized over the school's meme instagram page, everyone stood out by the flagpole holding signs, and then pretty much all of them walked back into class after. I'm on my school's student judicial board, which normally just handles academic integrity appeals, but the principal asked us to put together a recommendation before she decides anything official, since normally that kind of unexcused absence is an automatic detention plus you cant make up the work you missed.

Our board is honestly split down the middle on this, and we literally just covered civil disobedience in AP Gov, which is probably why everyone keeps reaching for that word. The organizers are saying they shouldn't be punished at all, since this was basically civil disobedience and punishing them just proves the school is exactly the unfair institution they were protesting. But a few board members pushed back hard: wasn't part of what made MLK's protests count as civil disobedience, instead of just breaking rules and hoping to get away with it, that he accepted going to jail for it? If the organizers want zero consequences, is it even the same move anymore? Someone else pointed out that they didn't actually break any rule connected to the teacher situation, they just skipped a totally unrelated class, so is that even the same category of thing as, like, a sit-in. And then our most annoying member (affectionately) said "civil disobedience" is usually a citizen-vs-government idea, and we're minors who didn't consent to being at this school in the first place, so maybe that whole framework doesn't even apply and were all overthinking a glorified skip day.

Could you actually write the memo I'd bring to our meeting? I want a real recommendation on what should happen to the kids who walked out (full punishment, none, or something in between), not just "it depends," and I want it to genuinely grapple with the accept-the-punishment thing, the wrong-rule thing, and whether the civil-disobedience framing even makes sense for a school in the first place, instead of dodging all three, since that's the part we honestly can't agree on.
