Ok so this has been bugging me while I'm drafting my current book. In aesthetics, everyone treats Kendall Walton's quasi-emotions story as basically the settled answer to the paradox of fiction, while the old illusion theory - the Coleridge "willing suspension of disbelief" idea, that we form some real if temporary and unstable belief in what's happening - gets treated like a dead letter that nobody serious defends anymore. But honestly, when i'm deep in writing a scene, or readers tell me about getting lost in a book, it feels a lot more like belief than like make believe. Can you build the strongest contemporary case you can for some version of illusion theory over Walton's account? Specifically, I want you to actually deal with the obvious objection - if we really believed it, why doesn't anyone reading a thriller ever put the book down and call the cops - rather then just asserting your way past it.
