I co-host a small philosophy podcast aimed at graduate students and philosophically engaged listeners, and next month's episode features a guest who defends infinitism about epistemic justification: the view that a belief is justified only when it is backed by a series of reasons that neither terminates in some foundational, non-inferentially justified belief nor loops back on itself, but instead continues without repetition indefinitely. I've been working back through the literature on the regress problem to prepare -- the old Agrippan trilemma of arbitrariness, circularity, or infinite regress -- and I'd rather the conversation not settle for a restatement of the position that listeners could get from an encyclopedia article.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each paired with a short follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give, that press on the places where infinitism is usually thought to be weakest. I want at least one question on the objection that no finite mind could actually traverse an infinite chain of reasons, and on whether the standard reply -- that justification requires only that further reasons be available on demand, not that they already be consciously entertained -- quietly smuggles in something suspiciously foundational about the reasoner's dispositions; one on whether the view is genuinely distinct from coherentism once the chain is allowed to include reasons that mutually support one another without ever repeating verbatim; one on the claim that infinitism alone avoids both the arbitrariness charge leveled at foundationalism and the circularity charge leveled at coherentism, pressing on whether it actually resolves that dilemma or simply relocates it further down the chain; and one that asks what, in principle, could move the guest off the view, since a position built to answer any further 'why' question with 'there is a further reason' can look unfalsifiable from the outside.
