I had a long, heated argument with a friend last night that I still can't shake. She has spent almost fifteen years working in international aid, and I've just accepted my first job in the same field. She told me that nearly every idealistic newcomer she's seen ends up considerably more skeptical of the sector's real-world impact within about a decade, and insisted I would be no exception. Her conclusion was that, since I already expect my own views to shift that way eventually, intellectual honesty requires me to adopt that skepticism now, rather than cling to an optimism I already expect to abandon. Something about her reasoning struck me as too quick, but I couldn't say exactly what, especially since I can't just dismiss the fact that she knows the field far better than I do. Was she right, and if not, where exactly does her argument go wrong?
