So I've been down a bit of a LessWrong/EA-forum rabbit hole lately and keep circling back to this "cluelessness" problem for consequentialism, and it's honestly been messing with my head a little. The rough idea (I think Lenman was the one who originally pressed the point, and then Greaves formalized it a while later) is that consequentialism tells you to do whatever produces the best overall outcome, but basically none of us are ever actually in a position to know that, because the effects of almost any action ripple out indefinitely -- who ends up getting born because a train was three minutes late, who meets who at a party you almost skipped, that kind of thing. Greaves' move is to point out that this isn't automatically fatal: alot of the time the unforeseeable stuff is "simple" cluelessness, symmetric noise that should just wash out in expectation, so you're fine reasoning off the foreseeable effects alone. But then there's "complex" cluelessness, where you actually do have some evidence about the far-future effects, pointing in different directions, just not enough of it to ever net out into anything like a confident verdict -- and that version feels less like garden-variety uncertainty and more like the whole framework has quietly stopped working.

What I'd love is a short story that actually sits inside that second, uglier kind of cluelessness, instead of just having some character stand around explaining it to another character. I don't want a big cosmic sci-fi premise -- I think it'd hit harder if it were mundane: a low-level aid worker, someone doing their annual charitable giving, a person deciding whether to report a minor crime, whatever, so the butterfly-effect stuff is happening through completely ordinary causal chains rather than time machines or anything like that. Only real ask: don't cheat by letting the reader see the far-future payoff that the character never gets to see. That kind of closure is exactly the comfort that actual cluelessness denies you, so a story that hands it to the reader anyway would kind of be missing the point. Keep it tight too, short-story length rather than a novella, I don't need an epic.
