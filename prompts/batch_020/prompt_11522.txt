I've spent a fair amount of time working through the Doomsday Argument on LessWrong and in Bostrom's *Anthropic Bias*, and I've come around to thinking the standard worry is simply confused: it only gets its force from the Self-Sampling Assumption, which has you reason as if your birth rank were a random draw from all humans who will ever live, so that a late rank counts as evidence for a small total population. But the Self-Indication Assumption -- which weights hypotheses by how many observers they predict, since a hypothesis with more people gives "you" correspondingly more chances to exist at all -- exactly cancels that update, leaving you back at your ordinary, non-anthropic prior over humanity's future. Since conditionalizing on the sheer fact of your own existence seems like the epistemically mandatory move rather than an optional extra, I don't understand why Bostrom and other specialists in anthropic reasoning still treat the Doomsday Argument as a live, unresolved problem rather than a neatly dissolved one. Is there something wrong with the SIA response, or has the field just been slow to catch up?
