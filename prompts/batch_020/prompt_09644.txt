A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about the Doomsday Argument, and neither of us came away satisfied. I pressed the standard Carter–Leslie case: reasoning via the Self-Sampling Assumption, the fact that my birth rank falls fairly early among all humans who will ever live counts as evidence against hypotheses on which many more humans are yet to be born, and hence as evidence for a nearer extinction. My friend's response was that this update disappears once the Self-Indication Assumption is brought in, since it weights hypotheses by how many observers they predict and so gives large-population hypotheses an offsetting boost that exactly cancels the Doomsday update. I had no good rejoinder at the time, though it struck me that my friend might simply be trading one uncomfortable commitment for another rather than genuinely resolving the puzzle. Please make the strongest case that escaping the Doomsday Argument via the Self-Indication Assumption is not the free win it appears to be: that it commits one to treating Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher scenario as sound reasoning, and that this cost is, if anything, harder to accept than the original Doomsday conclusion.
