Been down a set theory rabbit hole lately and this is bugging me. Gödel and Cohen together showed the continuum hypothesis (CH) is independent of ZFC, meaning you literally can't prove or disprove it from the standard axioms. Doesn't that basically settle the platonism vs formalism fight in philosophy of math? If theres no proof either way then there's no fact of the matter about whether CH is true, its like asking whether some house rule in a board game is 'true' -- the question just doesn't make sense outside whatever ruleset you pick. Which is what confuses me about Gödel spending years hunting for new axioms to settle CH, and why large-cardinal people are apparently still at it today. If independence just means 'no truth value,' isn't that whole research program chasing a question that was never real to begin with? Feels like exactly the kind of map-vs-territory mixup that would derail a rationalist forum thread for 200 replies, but I genuinely can't tell which side of it is confused. Am I wrong here, or does independent-from-ZFC basically equal meaningless question?
