In my Chinese philosophy seminar this week, we paired the butterfly dream passage from Zhuangzi's "Qiwulun" chapter ("Discussion on Making All Things Equal") with Descartes' dream argument from the First Meditation, and I've been stuck on something since our discussion section. Both passages seem to hinge on the identical epistemic gap: Zhuangzi wakes from dreaming he was a butterfly and cannot say whether he is Zhuang Zhou who dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly now dreaming he is Zhuang Zhou, while Descartes likewise notes that he has no reliable internal mark by which waking perception could be distinguished from a sufficiently vivid dream. Descartes goes on to use that gap as the opening move of a systematic doubt about the reliability of the senses, so during section a classmate argued that Zhuangzi must be doing exactly the same thing almost two thousand years earlier, and our TA didn't really push back on that reading. But everything else I've read about how sinologists actually treat this passage suggests they resist calling it "skepticism" in that Cartesian sense, without ever quite spelling out why the parallel breaks down.

What's really bothering me is the practical side of this. The whole ethical upshot of the Zhuangzi, at least as our professor presents it, is that we ought to stop over-relying on deliberate judgment and instead trust spontaneous, unforced responses to our circumstances (wu wei, acting in accordance with ziran). But if the butterfly dream has already shown that we cannot even know whether the circumstances we're responding to are real or dreamed, I don't see what license Zhuangzi has to recommend trusting those responses at all: it seems like he would first need to answer the very skeptical worry his own dream passage raises before he is entitled to tell us to relax into spontaneity. Yet the text just moves on to the next chapter, seemingly unbothered. Is there some piece of the interpretive puzzle I'm missing that dissolves this tension, or is this simply a real inconsistency in early Daoist thought that scholars have had to learn to live with?
