A friend of mine works at a company that recently updated its conduct policy so that HR can discipline or dismiss staff for lawful off-duty conduct "incompatible with company values," even when the conduct has no bearing on job performance and has harmed no one at work. The new rule followed a case in which an employee was quietly dismissed after coworkers learned, purely by accident, that he smokes cannabis at home on weekends -- perfectly legal where he lives, and irrelevant to his non-safety-sensitive job. My friend's manager insists that a workplace, like any community, is entitled to protect its shared sense of what's normal and acceptable even absent any concrete harm; my friend thinks a company has no business acting on anything short of demonstrable harm to the business or a colleague. Neither of them can quite articulate what's really at stake. Can you steelman both positions properly, and then tell me who's actually right, and why?
