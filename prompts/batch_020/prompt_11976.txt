For a research paper in my high school philosophy elective, I've been reading about the Lucas-Penrose argument, which uses Gödel's incompleteness theorems to claim that human minds cannot be equivalent to Turing machines: for any consistent formal system strong enough to encode arithmetic, there is a true statement the system cannot prove, yet we can simply see that it is true. This looks like a rigorous, purely mathematical proof that no computer program could ever fully replicate human reasoning, no matter how advanced AI becomes. So I'm struggling to understand why so many logicians and philosophers of mind still reject the argument and continue to treat human-level or superhuman machine intelligence as a real possibility. Could you help me understand what they think is wrong with it?
