Okay, this has been rattling around in my head for weeks and I can't tell if I'm onto something or just being a paranoid pedant. I cover science and ideas for a living, and I've spent the last few months reporting on how newsrooms handle verification now that AI translation tools are everywhere, which made me newly attentive to a much older, low-tech version of the same problem. Back in the spring I was fact-checking a profile that hinged on a quote from an interview conducted in Korean. I don't speak Korean, so the fixer who'd translated it for me went back before publication and re-checked her own translation against the original audio, and confirmed it held up fine. My editor treats that as solid due diligence — "she checked it twice, we're covered" — but its nagged at me ever since. If she'd misheard a word, or just didn't know some slang phrase, the first time around, why would asking her to check her own work catch that? She'd presumably make the exact same mistake again, with the exact same confidence. On paper it looks like an independent verification step, but I can't shake the feeling we didn't actually learn anything new — we just had the same information (or lack of it) we started with, dressed up to look like a second opinion.

I mentioned this to a friend who does conference interpreting for a living, and she said the identical worry comes up in her field constantly, and that nobody's ever given her a satisfying way to think about it — you either trust the interpreter's competence going in, or you don't, and having them double-check themselves doesn't obviously move the needle either way. Which is where I get stuck: is there something actually, structurally wrong with using a source to verify itself like that, or is this just garden-variety perfectionism on my part? And if it is a real problem, I honestly don't know where the line is supposed to be, because tons of perfectly normal verification — rereading your own draft, a scientist re-running her own analysis, a witness confirming their own earlier statement — has this exact same "self-checking" shape, and it seems way too strong to say none of that ever counts for anything.
