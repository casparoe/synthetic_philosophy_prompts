Okay, this is going to be pretty in the weeds, but I've been chewing on it for a piece about how far Bayesian-style updating rules can be pushed before they snap, and I want an actual argument out of you, not a summary of the debate. The example is Bas van Fraassen's old "Judy Benjamin problem" (there's a short 1981 BJPS paper on it, plus a pile of follow-up literature arguing about how to fix or dodge it). Setup: a soldier, Judy, gets parachuted into territory split into Red vs. Blue, each of which is further split into a "Headquarters" half and a "Second Company" half — so four quadrants, all equally likely at 1/4, before she learns anything. Right before the radio dies, she gets one scrap of info: "if you're in Red, the odds are 3-to-1 you're in the Headquarters half." That's the whole message — nothing at all is said about Blue territory. Now, if you update by picking the new probability distribution that's as close as possible (in the relative-entropy / KL-divergence sense) to the old flat one, subject only to that 3:1 constraint, something weird falls out: Judy's overall credence that she's even in Red territory drops, from 1/2 to somthing like .47, with Blue rising to compensate — even though the Headquarters/Second-Company split inside Blue itself doesn't budge an inch from fifty-fifty. Van Fraassen used exactly this example to argue that minimizing relative entropy can't be the right general rule for updating on conditionals like this, since a message that's purely about the internal breakdown of Red shouldn't be able to move how likely Red is versus Blue in the first place.

I want you to argue the other side. Make the strongest case you can that this shift in P(Red) isn't a bug, paradox, or embarrassment, but exactly what a rational agent in Judy's position should do — and that the "irrelevance" intuition people lean on (something like: information confined to the internal breakdown of Red can't touch the Red/Blue marginal at all) isn't actually a requirement of rationality, but instead smuggles in an assumption Judy has no business making. I'd like you to get into the actual mechanics of why minimizing relative entropy against a flat prior forces this result, rather than just gesturing at "maximum entropy is objective" and moving on — that move-without-an-argument is exactly the kind of hand-wave I keep running into at the blog-post level, and I want you to do better than that.
