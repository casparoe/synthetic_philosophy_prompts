I cover science and ideas, and I have lately been reading analytic metaphysics for a piece on how hard it is to define even simple physical properties like fragility or toxicity. Two classic counterexamples to the standard "if struck, would break" analysis of dispositions keep coming up, and I would like to understand them properly rather than just cite them. A fink is a hidden mechanism that switches the underlying property on or off at the exact instant the trigger occurs: C. B. Martin's original case is a dead wire connected to an electro-fink that makes it live the instant something touches it, so that testing the wire seems to show it was live all along, though a moment earlier it was not. A mask leaves the property completely intact but stops it from ever manifesting: a poison is still a poison even when an antidote taken alongside it keeps it from killing anyone. I would like to see this distinction embodied rather than explained. Please write a short story, under 1,200 words, in which a single character or object is finked in one scene and masked in another, precisely enough that a reader familiar with the debate could point to exactly where each is happening — but without the story ever naming the concepts or the philosophers behind them.
