For a personal side project, I've been training a small model to predict when I'm in a coding "flow state," using features like keystroke timing, calendar data, and sleep stats from my smartwatch. Since I've never found an objective signal that tracks the state itself, my only source of training labels is tagging past sessions myself in a spreadsheet, based purely on how the state felt from the inside at the time -- distinct from merely being focused, or from the kind of alertness a good night's sleep or too much coffee produces. A friend who works in ML says these labels can't count as real ground truth, since I have no way to check, even against my own past entries, whether I'm tagging the same inner state each time or a subtly different one that merely feels identical in the moment. I think that's an unreasonable objection, since I'm the only person who could possibly be in a position to recognize this state at all. Does she have a genuine point, or is she holding my data to a standard that no self-reported label could ever meet?
