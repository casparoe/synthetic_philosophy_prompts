Okay so I've been going around in circles on when a computer program actually counts morally, like when making it suffer or deleting it is actually bad, and I need someone to tell me if my gut reactions even fit together.

Here's where I'm at:

A smart speaker that plays an ouch sound when you unplug it gets zero weight from me, it's just a recording.

A little wheely robot with a damage sensor that learns to avoid stairs after it falls — I feel like that's maybe a tiny bit of weight? Like not zero but way less than even a bug.

A full mouse-brain emulation on a cluster, same neurons and connections firing in the same patterns, that freezes up and tries to escape when you give it a simulated shock — that feels like it really counts, basically like a real mouse.

That same mouse emulation but you pause it for a year on a hard drive and then hit resume — I still think it counted while it was running, and pausing wasn't killing it, resuming isn't making a new mouse.

That same emulation but slowed way down so one second of mouse experience takes a year, and spread out across a bunch of random laptops — I still think it counts, though part of me wants to say it counts less, which is wierd.

This is the one that messes me up: say a weather forecasting program just crunching clouds happens for like ten seconds to go through the exact same numbers step for step as the pain part of the mouse program, but in there those numbers mean humidity or whatever. I want to say that has zero moral weight, even though number-wise it's identical.

Last one: someone runs a thousand copies of the suffering mouse at once to test a simulated painkiller, then deletes them all. That feels really wrong to me, but not quite a thousand times as wrong as torturing a thousand real mice? And anyways deleting feels less bad than the suffering itself.

I kinda thought the rule was something like it counts if the computation is doing the right causal job for the system itself, not just looking the same from outside, but I'm not sure that handles the slowed-down and thousand-copies stuff.

Can you give me a principle, or two competing ones, that gets closest to all this, say which case forces each extra clause, tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't thought of, and be honest about which of my verdicts I'd have to give up to keep it simple? And what does it say about the backup case I keep flip-flopping on — where you save a suffering emulation to disk and never run it again, just store it?
