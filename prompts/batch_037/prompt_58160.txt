For the past two years I have lived in a 48-unit housing cooperative while working as a dental receptionist, and neighbours elected me to our residents committee even though I have no training in philosophy or law. I have been thinking on my own about what privacy really amounts to in a shared home, because after several parcel thefts and one winter break-in our property manager has proposed replacing metal keys with individual entry fobs that record every arrival and departure, installing motion-triggered cameras in the entrance, hallways, stairwells and laundry room with thirty days of cloud storage held by the vendor, and joining a program that allows police to request footage without checking with each household. Parents of young children and an elderly neighbour who was home during the break-in say shared corridors were never private to begin with and they would finally feel at ease, while teenagers who hang out in the stairwell, a home health aide who visits clients here, and several others including myself feel uneasy about having weeks of our daily routines available to be reviewed and pieced together, and about seeming opposed to safety if we raise concerns.

Could you prepare a decision memorandum I could send to the other committee members ahead of our vote, written in plain language for people without technical or legal background? It should close with a specific recommendation and timetable we could actually vote on, whether that means going ahead, turning the proposal down, or choosing a middle path such as improved lighting and locks, shorter storage, excluding certain areas, refusing police sharing, or a trial with a scheduled review, and it should lay out the reasons in a way that gives fair weight to the best argument against the recommendation, so members who disagree feel their central concern was understood rather than brushed aside.
