A fifty-minute tutorial for second-year philosophy of mind students needs a plan for introducing pain asymbolia and what it suggests about whether the badness of pain lies in the sensation itself. Rather than an explanation of the condition, provide a teaching sequence: state what must be established before the asymbolic reports can even be heard as coherent, select a single anchoring case involving preserved localization and intensity judgments without withdrawal or dislike and explain what it must include and what confounds it must exclude, map the predictable misunderstandings such as conflation with insensitivity to pain or with ordinary tolerance onto the exact points where each should be intercepted, suggest a final probe that distinguishes genuine grasp of the sensory-affective dissociation from mere surprise, and indicate what complexities should be explicitly set aside at this level.
