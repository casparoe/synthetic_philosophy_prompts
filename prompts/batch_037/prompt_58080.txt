So I'm taking this community night class on philosophy and last week we did Schlick on the meaning of life and I definately can't stop thinking about it. The way our tutor put it, Schlick thinks asking for the purpose of life as a whole is a muddle, because if meaning always lies in some further goal you never actually get it, you just keep deferring it, and work in his narrow sense is like that, always for the sake of something later. Play, for him, is the opposite model, anything done for its own sake where the doing is the point, being absorbed in the present like kids messing around with a ball or someone really listening to music instead of waiting for it to finish, and youth matters because kids are better at that.

Could you write me a pantoum, like four quatrains where lines echo over, that actually uses the form to show that shift? Like start with the same evening walk described as tired work, grinding for a future payoff, and then let the repeated lines come back changed so they read as play, absorbed and complete in themselves, without adding any new moral at the end. Maybe in the voice of a tired commuter in his forties remembering how he used to play as a kid, kinda wistful but not preachy. My tutor is a stickler so it should get Schlick right enough that she'd nod along, but my classmates should still enjoy it as a poem even if they zoned out during lecture. Oh and keep it tight, no extra explainer afterwards.
