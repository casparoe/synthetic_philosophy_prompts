So I'm a novelist in the middle of revising this big messy family saga, and my writing group keeps calling me out on the same wierd thing about how I balance suffering and happy endings and I can't tell if I'm missing some obvious concept.

The pattern is like this. If I have Mara endure a pretty miserable childhood and then grow into a grounded, happy adult, readers find that moving, like the early pain was worth it for her later joy. But if I keep the total amount of joy and pain exactly the same and just split it up — Mara gets the miserable childhood so her younger sister Lena gets to have the grounded happy adulthood — readers revolt and say that feels creepy and unfair, even though on paper someone suffers and someone thrives either way. Same thing happened when my editor suggested I sacrafice my cozy baker side character in chapter twelve, just kill him off sadly, so I could give five other characters funnier, warmer arcs. Total happiness math would go up, but it felt like cheating to use up one person to buy happiness for everybody else.

Does philosophy already have a name for that difference between tradeoffs inside one life versus tradeoffs across different people, and can you lay out what it actually is? I don't just want a label, I want to know how to carve it — how it's different from the plain don't use people idea and from those cases where who even exists depends on what you choose, plus where the edges are, like does consent fix it, does it matter if it's pain now versus joy later, that kind of stuff. And if there isn't an established name, what would you call it so I can actually use it in revisions?
