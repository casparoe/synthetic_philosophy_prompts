I am a second-year PhD student in moral philosophy working on fiduciary AI, and after a term-long seminar on idealized preferences my reading group has split into two frustrated camps that keep talking past each other. One camp, which I think of as ambitious extrapolators, holds that a truly helpful personal assistant should track what a person would want if fully informed about relevant facts, given ample time to reflect, and cleared of simple mistakes about means and evidence, even when that extrapolated picture surprises or initially repels the present user. The other camp, which I think of as recognition-constrained actualists, holds that extrapolation without a recognition requirement is alienating and autonomy-undermining, so assistance is legitimate only when the idealized recommendation could in principle be owned by the agent from within her current self-conception and subject to her veto, even if that means forgoing what better information would support. To keep the exercise from floating into abstraction, I would like the collaboration to stay anchored on two invented cases I keep bringing up with friends: Priya, a 34-year-old emergency nurse who explicitly instructs her assistant to help her maximize overtime and not bother her with health, career, or moral reflections she once cared about in college, and Sam, a lapsed musician who asks for help becoming disciplined again while insisting he no longer endorses the perfectionist artistic values his younger self held.

Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, written as a constructive document both sides could put their names to rather than a debate transcript or a verdict for one side. Organize it as a numbered list of substantive agreed claims about idealization, information, alienation, and the assistant role, followed by a table that states each residual disagreement in neutral language that the losing side for that row would still accept as a fair statement of its own view, followed by a cruxes section identifying two or three subquestions whose resolution would move at least one camp, and for each crux describe a concrete test, thought experiment, or consideration both camps would accept as relevant, recording in advance what each camp predicts about it and why Priya and Sam matter for it.

Please write in American English in a formal style suitable for circulation to the seminar, and close with a brief reflexive note explaining where your proposed neutral formulations are most likely to have smuggled in a contested assumption, which objection from either camp would force you to revise the joint statement, and what argument or evidence would lead you to reframe a disagreement you currently treat as substantive as merely verbal.
