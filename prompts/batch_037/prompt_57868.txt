After a long argument with a friend about what heritability numbers actually show, I want to settle our dispute. My friend insists that if a trait has a heritability of 60 percent in a population, that means 60 percent of any one person's outcome is caused by genes and that equalizing environments would still leave most differences in place. I argued that heritability is a population-level ratio of variance that depends on the range of environments sampled and says nothing by itself about malleability or about differences between groups. Write an essay that adjudicates this disagreement by explaining variance partitioning, gene-environment interaction and correlation, and why high within-group heritability does not imply immutability or explain between-group gaps, using a concrete invented farming example with two plots to make the logic clear. Defend a verdict about who is closer to right and why. End by stating how confident you are in your verdict overall and singling out the point in your reasoning you are least sure of.
