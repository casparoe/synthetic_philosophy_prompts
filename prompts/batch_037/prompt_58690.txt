Okay this is driving me nuts after a late-night kitchen debate with my roommate.

She says she's a deontologist about lying, so we built this little arguement together:

1. It's wrong for me to tell one lie now even if that lie would stop five strangers from each telling a similar lie.
2. If 1 is right, it has to be because what matters is that I don't lie, not that lies in general don't happen.
3. Future-me is not present-me in the relevant sense — future-me's lies are just things that will happen, not something I am doing right now.
4. So by the logic of 2, present-me also shouldn't tell one lie now to stop future-me from telling five lies later. Same tradeoff, just across time instead of across people.
5. But that's crazy. Of course I can tell a small lie to dodge a weekend trip when I know going will turn me into a stressed-out liar for a month.
6. And if you allow minimizing across time like in 5, then the ban in 1 looks arbitrary. Why care about minimizing my lies over time but not lies across people?
7. So 1 has to go and we should just minimize lies.

She doesn't want to give up 1 and I don't want to say 5 is wrong. Which step should we reject and what does it cost to reject it?
