I am enrolled in an evening survey of modern Europe, and our seminar has spent several weeks circling the French Revolution without settling on what kind of explanation does the most work. Some classmates stress conflicts over bread, land, and privilege, others stress the power of ideas about sovereignty and virtue, and still others stress war, scarcity, and a string of improvised decisions that might easily have gone otherwise. In the middle of that disagreement, the phrase revolutions devour their children keeps being invoked as if it settled something, first after we traced the move from the reforms of 1789 to the Terror, and later when a friend compared that trajectory to a housing cooperative she helped found, which began with ambitious egalitarian bylaws and ended with the founders expelling one another over procedural violations.

I am puzzled because the phrase seems to be doing two different jobs. In the first use, it was offered to suggest that a revolution that begins by promising liberty will, by some inner logic, end by executing the very people who launched it, so the later violence was already contained in the earlier promise. In the second use, it was offered more narrowly to point out that revolutionary coalitions tend to fracture, with moderates purged by radicals and radicals then purged by their former allies, without any claim that this had to happen. Can you separate the distinct precise claims hidden in that one sentence, from the near-trivial to the strongly deterministic, including claims about inevitability, about ideological dynamics, about institutional incentives under emergency, and about the personal fates of leaders versus the fate of the broader project. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a contentious but defensible thesis, or false when applied to France from 1789 through Thermidor, and explain which readings would actually support using the slogan to close an argument that the Terror was unavoidable and which would support only the weaker observation that coalitions splinter. Lay the readings out as a numbered menu I can bring back to the seminar.
