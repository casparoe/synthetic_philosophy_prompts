I request a coherence audit of the view I have arrived at concerning the fair use of a predictive screening tool for allocating limited paid apprenticeships in a municipal workforce program.

The setting I have in mind is as follows. The agency receives many more applicants than places from two large residential districts, North and South, with different historical rates of meeting the qualification standard, largely because residents of the South have had less access to prior informal training opportunities. Suppose careful validation has shown that about one half of North applicants and about one third of South applicants would meet the standard if placed, and that the recorded qualification labels are accurate for both districts. The proposed tool would assign each applicant a risk score between zero and one, interpreted as an estimated probability of being qualified, and applicants scoring above a common cutoff would be prioritized for placement.

I find myself committed to each of the following five claims, and I would like to know whether they can all be true together:

1. Calibration across districts is required for fairness. For any given score value, the actual proportion of qualified individuals among North applicants receiving that score should equal the actual proportion among South applicants receiving that score, and both should equal the score itself.

2. Parity in error rates across districts is required for fairness. Among applicants who are genuinely qualified, the proportion correctly prioritized should be the same in North and South, and among applicants who are genuinely unqualified, the proportion incorrectly prioritized should also be the same in North and South, so that neither district bears a disproportionate burden of mistakes.

3. The difference in underlying qualification rates between the districts should be taken as a fixed background fact for purposes of evaluating the tool, not as a measurement artifact to be corrected away. The labels are correct and the inequality is real and unjust in origin, but it is not the screening model that creates it.

4. The tool to be deployed should be informative but fallible, performing substantially better than chance yet well short of perfect prediction, and it should use the same cutoff for both districts. Fairness should not be secured by deliberately worsening predictions for one district in order to match the other, by randomizing decisions, or by declining to make predictions at all.

5. Fairness calls for the tool to operate without direct access to district membership when scoring an individual applicant, so that two applicants with the same other features receive the same score regardless of district.

Please examine this set for internal coherence. Identify which elements genuinely conflict and which conflicts are only apparent and can be resolved by drawing a relevant distinction. Arrange any conflicts you find in order of how fundamental they are to the overall position. For each, explain what abandoning or weakening one of the conflicting elements would involve and what normative or practical cost that revision would carry. Conclude by indicating which single adjustment would restore coherence at the lowest overall cost.
