I advise a public agency that must regularly decide between preventing a single instance of prolonged severe suffering and preventing a very large number of transient minor irritations, and I need a clear statement of what a satisfactory theory of such tradeoffs would have to accomplish before I compare particular theories.

Please develop a specification for an adequate account of aggregation across very small and very large harms, suitable for guiding public choices that impose or avert diffuse burdens. For concreteness, take as the central case a choice between allowing one person to undergo months of excruciating pain with lasting trauma and allowing a very large number N of persons to each undergo a brief eye irritation from a dust speck with no aftereffects, and consider also the following variants: concentrated versus dispersed minor harms, where the same total of minor irritations falls on a few people rather than one each on many; risky versions, where many face a tiny probability of severe pain rather than a certainty of irritation; iterated versions, where a series of pairwise improvements leads step by step from the severe-suffering option to the many-irritations option; and ex ante versus ex post versions, where expected burden and realized burden come apart.

For each desideratum you propose, state the requirement, explain the case or theoretical consideration that motivates it, and indicate whether you take it to be a non-negotiable adequacy condition or a defeasible theoretical virtue reflecting a particular camp, such as commitments to full transitivity of better than, to separability or additivity across persons, to continuity between severity and number, to sensitivity to probabilities and to distributive fairness, to avoidance of fanaticism in either direction, and to practical action-guidance under uncertainty. Where you suspect that two or more widely endorsed desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied, identify the conflict explicitly and explain the source of the tension. Also indicate which single requirement you take to exclude the largest class of otherwise plausible views.

Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their justification rather than on detailed evaluation, but close with a brief illustration of how three broad families of views fare against your list: straightforward additive aggregation, views that give lexical or threshold priority to severe suffering, and views that discount sufficiently small harms to zero or treat them as non-aggregative.
