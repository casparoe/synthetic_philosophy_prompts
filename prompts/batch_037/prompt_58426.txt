Develop a philosophical argument for the view that in Ship of Theseus cases, continuity of commissioned service and functional role determines persistence, and that gradual replacement in service preserves the original vessel even when a second vessel assembled from the discarded parts is materially closer to the original.

Work with the following variant: A harbor authority keeps a wooden ceremonial ship in active service, replacing one plank per voyage while the ship continues its scheduled sailings with the same crew, name, and duties, using rot-resistant treated timber for each replacement. The removed original planks are numbered, stored under cover, and after the final replacement are reassembled on land according to the original plan to form a second, fully coherent hull that has never sailed. Both resulting vessels are structurally sound and seaworthy.

Your argument should articulate a general principle about the persistence of maintained artifacts, apply it to favor the continuously sailed ship over the reassembled hull in this variant, and respond to the strongest objection favoring the reassembled hull on grounds of material similarity. A brief analogy with another maintained artifact, such as a building or instrument kept in use through repair, may be used to support the reasoning.
