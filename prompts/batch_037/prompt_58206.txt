Can you write me a pantoum, say three or four quatrains where the second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third lines of the next, about common knowlege of rationality? To keep it self-contained for them, use this toy case: Alex and Maya play a three-step take-or-pass game, at each node the player can take for a modest guaranteed payoff or pass in hopes of a bigger payoff later if the other player keeps passing too, and backward induction says Alex should take right away — but only if you assume full common knowledge.

What I realy want is for the form to do the philosophical work. Let the interlocking repeats enact the hierarchy itself — everyone is rational, everyone knows everyone is rational, everyone knows that everyone knows that, and so on — with each recycled line adding one more level of knows-that. And then use the final stanza, where the pattern circles back, to show the contrast I keep harping on: finite mutual knowledge up to two or three levels versus genuine common knowledge all the way up, and how the backward induction prediction starts to wobble if the iteration actually stops.

Keep it playful enough to read aloud, but accurate enough that my game theory colleague down the hall wouldn't wince if he wandered in.
