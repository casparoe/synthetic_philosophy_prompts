Please help me design a teaching plan for a seventy-five-minute undergraduate seminar on Hempel's raven paradox.

The students are second-year philosophy undergraduates who are comfortable with the truth-functional conditional and with contraposition but have not studied confirmation theory. I require a plan for how to teach the paradox, not a direct exposition or solution.

The plan should specify the order in which the conceptual components must be introduced, with an account of the dependencies among them: what must be in place concerning universal generalizations, logical equivalence, and principles of confirmation before the paradoxical consequence can appear as paradoxical, and what confusion follows if that order is reversed.

The explanation should be carried throughout by a single sustained illustrative case of your choosing. To prevent reliance on over-familiar illustrations, do not use ravens and blackness as the working example and do not appeal to white footwear or green fruit; select a different domain, for instance from horticulture, manufacturing quality control, or clinical screening, that still makes vivid both the equivalence at the heart of the paradox and the contrast between different sampling procedures. Explain what the chosen case must contain in order to generate the appearance of paradox and what it must omit in order not to resolve the substantive issue by stipulation.

Anticipate at least three misconceptions that students with this background are likely to bring to the topic or to form during instruction, such as conflating entailment with confirmation or mislocating the source of the oddity in deductive logic rather than in an account of evidence, and indicate for each the precise point in the sequence at which it should be forestalled and how.

Conclude with a diagnostic exercise or question suitable for the final ten minutes that would distinguish genuine grasp of the structure of the paradox from mere willingness to find it strange, and with an explicit statement of what should be set aside at this level, with reasons and with suggested language for acknowledging the deferral to the class.
