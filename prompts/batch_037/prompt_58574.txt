As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I am preparing a feature-length interview with a cosmologist who holds that cosmological models in which the vast majority of observers are momentary fluctuations with false memories of a coherent past can be rationally set aside as cognitively unstable, without waiting for direct observational refutation.

For readers I will need to explain the background worry in plain terms: that in a sufficiently vast, long-lived or eternally fluctuating universe, random fluctuations might produce vastly more transient brains complete with apparent memories, textbooks and instrument readings than there are ordinary observers formed through long biological and cosmic history, which raises the puzzle of why our own experience is so stable, orderly and shared if such a model were correct.

I would appreciate help formulating a compact set of probing, substantive questions for this guest, suitable for an intellectually ambitious general audience. Please keep the focus on the content of the position and avoid questions about biography, career or general advice.

I am especially interested in questions that clarify how the appeal to cognitive instability is supposed to differ from ordinary skeptical doubts about the external world, and whether dismissing a theory because it would undermine the evidence for itself assumes the reliability of precisely the memories and records at issue. Critics often press on how probabilities and typicality are assigned across infinite populations of ordinary and fluctuated observers, so that line of criticism should be represented.

I have devised two variations I would like to put to the guest in the interview, and I would like the proposed questions to incorporate them in accessible language. The first variation concerns a fluctuation that is not momentary at all but sustains for decades a small community of interacting observers with mutually consistent records and instruments that continue to agree with one another. The second concerns a hypothetical future physics program that deliberately creates a very large population of brief artificial minds with implanted coherent-seeming histories in order to study early-universe statistics. I want to ask how the guest would distinguish these cases from the paradigm fleeting fluctuation, and whether the cognitive instability rationale still applies to them in the same way.

Please structure the material as a brief opening clarification followed by questions built around those two cases and then a closing question about how this line of reasoning should actually guide choice among live cosmological models. For each main line of questioning, please also suggest a contingent rejoinder that engages the reply defenders of this position characteristically give, so that I am prepared to press the point rather than let it rest with a restatement of the view.
