You are a professional political philosopher specializing in political obligation and democratic theory. Please think carefully step by step, beginning with brief brainstorming before delivering your finished case.

Construct an original thought experiment for use in an undergraduate seminar on whether political obligation constrains civil disobedience. For the purposes of this exercise, use the following working definitions: under the fair-play principle, a person incurs an obligation to obey the rules of a cooperative scheme only when she knowingly benefits from the scheme, has voluntarily accepted those benefits, and is asked to bear a fair share of the burdens; under the natural duty of justice, a person has a duty to support and comply with reasonably just institutions independently of voluntary acceptance or personal benefit, provided compliance does not impose unreasonable cost.

Your case must satisfy all of the following specifications. First, set it in a realistic contemporary municipal context, such as drought-related water rationing, with no science fiction, no perfectly just state, no secret law, and no life-or-death emergency. Second, present the scenario itself as a single paragraph of 130 to 180 words centering on a resident, Maya, who has arranged her life so that she knowingly takes no net benefit from the relevant cooperative scheme and has clearly withheld voluntary acceptance, while the scheme itself remains reasonably just, democratically enacted, and fair in its distribution of burdens, and compliance would not impose unreasonable cost on her. Third, make Maya's violation a genuine instance of civil disobedience: public, nonviolent, conscientious, communicative in intent, and undertaken with willingness to accept the legal consequences, undertaken to protest a specific exemption or design feature that leaves the overall scheme reasonably just. Fourth, engineer the case so that the two theories diverge as follows: the fair-play principle as defined implies Maya breaches no political obligation by disobeying, while the natural duty of justice as defined implies her disobedience is pro tanto wrong as a breach of political obligation, even if it might be justified all things considered.

Do not stipulate these verdicts by fiat; they must follow from applying the definitions to the details of your scenario. Structure your answer in four parts. Begin with 2 to 3 candidate setups you considered and explain why each fails at least one specification. Then give the final single-paragraph scenario. Then give an analysis that states each theory's verdict and walks through exactly why the theory yields it on these facts. Finally, identify which single feature of the case is doing the decisive work in driving the theories apart, explain what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed or reversed, and anticipate the strongest objection a defender of each theory would raise to rescue their preferred verdict without abandoning the theory.
