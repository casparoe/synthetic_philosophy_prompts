I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, writing to clarify a characterization I encountered in a popular technology newsletter on the theme of competence without comprehension and what the author called the strange inversions associated with Darwin and Turing.

My reconstruction of the central passage is as follows: Darwin and Turing each showed how apparently rational design can arise without a rational designer. Darwin showed that variation plus selection can yield organisms that are highly competent at surviving and reproducing even though the underlying process comprehends nothing, and Turing showed that computing machinery can yield intelligent-seeming behavior by mechanically following syntactic rules even though the machinery comprehends nothing. So far this seemed plausible to me. The passage then drew a stronger moral, namely that comprehension turns out to be an optional extra that both biology and engineering can in principle dispense with altogether, and that once a system reliably produces correct outputs there is no further substantive question about whether it genuinely understands, because the search for some additional inner understanding is merely a holdover from folk psychology.

I am inclined to find the stronger moral attractive given how often I see reliable systems whose internals are opaque, but I suspect it may overstate the point. Could you audit this characterization for fairness? Please distinguish which elements a careful defender of the competence without comprehension idea would endorse, which involve a conflation or overstatement, and what that defender would most object to, and explain whether the stronger moral about understanding follows from the corrected version.

To keep the discussion grounded in cases I understand, please apply the corrected view to three systems drawn from my work: first, a dependency resolver I wrote by hand using topological sort where I understand why each step is correct; second, an ensemble of log-parsing patterns produced on my team by automated mutation and selection for anomaly-detection accuracy, where the resulting patterns outperform hand-written ones but no engineer understands why several of them work; third, a language-model-based code reviewer that approves or rejects pull requests with high accuracy and accompanies each verdict with a plausible rationale. For each, say where, if anywhere, a careful version of the view would locate competence and where, if anywhere, it would locate comprehension.

Please write in a manner accessible to a technically literate non-philosopher, without appeal to bibliographies or external sources, and conclude with an explicit statement of what argument or consideration would lead you to revise your verdict and where in your own reasoning you judge yourself most likely to fail.
