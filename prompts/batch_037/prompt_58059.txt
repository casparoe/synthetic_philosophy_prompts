As a journalist covering science and ideas, I have repeatedly noticed a particular shift in how people respond to harmful or helpful actions once the explanation of those actions changes, and I lack precise language for it.

While reporting on patients who received alarming guidance from a medical advice system, I saw family members react with anger and demands for acknowledgment when they took the output to reflect disregard for their concerns, then switch within minutes to cool, diagnostic talk about data pipelines and repair once engineers explained the result came from a cutoff and a formatting failure, even though the scare and the delay were unchanged. While reporting on a school scheduling system that generated a wave of unfair detentions, I saw parents direct personal reproach toward the vendor team, then divide after learning no individual had reviewed any case and the pattern emerged from interacting constraints: some continued reproach toward the organization itself, others began to treat the system as faulty infrastructure to be contained and redesigned.

What interests me is the oscillation itself, between engaging a target as an agent toward whom anger, gratitude, or reproach feel apt and stepping back to regard the same target as a condition to be managed or worked around, with the outcome held constant. Is there an established concept or literature for this exact oscillation, and if so what are the nearest concepts, how does the pattern I describe differ from each, and which fits best, or if not how should it be named and defined with its boundary cases? A bare label will not suffice; I need a clear carving of what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.

Please provide your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
