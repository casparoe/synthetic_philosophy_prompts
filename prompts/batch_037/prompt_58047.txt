Please assist with the design of the final written assessment for a postgraduate seminar in formal epistemology devoted to accuracy centered approaches to rational belief.

Students in the seminar have been introduced to the central claim that rational credences are those that best promote the goal of accuracy, where inaccuracy is measured by a scoring rule such as the sum of squared errors between credence and truth value across relevant propositions, and where such a rule is required to be strictly proper in the sense that each coherent probability function uniquely minimizes its own expected inaccuracy.

The assessment should consist of a single essay question to be answered under timed conditions in approximately seventy five minutes. The question must center on the following unfamiliar case, which has not been discussed in class. An agent assigns credence 0.7 to a proposition to be decided tomorrow and also assigns credence 0.7 to its negation, while assigning credence 1.0 to their disjunction. A second available doxastic state assigns 0.4 to the proposition, 0.4 to its negation, and 1.0 to the disjunction.

The essay question should require students to reconstruct the dominance argument for probabilism as applied to this case, to explain why mere appeal to expected inaccuracy is insufficient and how dominance is meant to improve upon it, and to evaluate two challenges: first, that dominance over all possible worlds provides no reason for action unless the dominating alternative is one the agent could rationally move to from where the agent now stands; second, that the argument succeeds only by presupposing a particular attitude toward risk or a particular choice of scoring rule, and therefore fails to provide a neutral vindication of probabilism.

Alongside the question, please provide a model answer of approximately six hundred words and a marking scheme totaling twenty five marks. The marking scheme should allocate marks to five or six required analytical moves, distinguish the standard for excellent performance from the standard for merely competent performance by specifying which moves and qualifications the former must include, and identify three characteristic errors with an explanation of why each error matters and how many marks it should cost.

The difficulty of the question should arise from the need to apply abstract ideas to the stipulated numerical case and to assess the force of the objections, so that accurate recall of standard slogans without such application would be insufficient for a passing grade.
