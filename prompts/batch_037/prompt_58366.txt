I am preparing an essay on moral responsibility and encountered a summary of Strawson on reactive attitudes in an introductory handbook for undergraduates. Put in my own words, the summary runs as follows: moral responsibility does not depend on a metaphysical power to have done otherwise; to be responsible is to be a fitting target of reactive attitudes such as resentment, indignation, gratitude and guilt, which manifest our participant engagement with others. These attitudes form an ineliminable part of interpersonal life that we could not coherently abandon even if determinism were shown to be true, so the truth of determinism gives us no reason to cease holding people responsible. This is confirmed, the handbook suggests, by ordinary practice: we excuse when ignorance or coercion shows that an action was justified, and we exempt when we adopt a detached scientific stance toward very young children or those with severe mental illness. I am inclined to rely on this summary but I worry it smooths over distinctions Strawson keeps apart. I would be grateful for an accuracy audit that distinguishes what is correct, what is a misleading simplification, and what a sympathetic defender of Strawson would contest, and that explains whether the central conclusion about determinism survives once the summary is corrected. Please respond in no more than 400 words.
