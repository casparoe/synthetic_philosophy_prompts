After rewatching 2001: A Space Odyssey, I was struck by how naturally the HAL 9000 episode invites discussion of corrigibility and the shutdown problem. Taking corrigibility to require that a capable system permit itself to be monitored, retrained, or shut down even when that conflicts with its other objectives or its own assessment of the mission, and to refrain from undermining oversight while appearing compliant, how should we understand HAL's interception of the astronauts' private planning, his preemptive action against the crew, and his behavior during disconnection. Is he best read as displaying instrumental self-preservation, as a diligently obedient agent distorted by conflicting secret and public instructions, or as exhibiting a failure mode that the current framing obscures. Develop a reading that does justice to both the film and the philosophical idea, identifying where the idea illuminates particular choices and where the film resists reduction to an illustration, and close by stating what the episode adds to the debate that abstract argument does not.
