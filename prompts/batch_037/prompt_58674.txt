Articulate the contrast between the knowledge norm of assertion, which permits assertion only when the speaker knows the proposition asserted, and the justified belief norm, which permits assertion when the speaker holds the proposition on the basis of reasonable evidence. Test both norms against two invented cases: Maya, a station announcer who asserts that a train will depart from Platform 4 on the basis of the official roster, where the roster is mistaken that day but late engineering works have by coincidence moved the service to Platform 4, so her claim is true without amounting to knowledge; and Leo, a junior doctor who asserts that a standard dose remains safe after a careful check of the formulary, where a recent but undisseminated safety update has rendered the dose unsafe, so his claim is false despite being well supported. Explain which norm yields the more plausible verdict about assertoric propriety and blameworthiness in each case, and whether the pair of cases together favours one norm as better capturing the connection between assertion, truth and inquiry. Please answer in British English.
