Assume the role of a professional philosopher specializing in decision theory and interactive reasoning. Please first work through the incentive structure step by step and then give concrete practical advice.

As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I am unsure how to handle a real collaboration. A friend and I co-run a small paid newsletter with a fixed run of eight sponsored issues. All sponsorship money accumulates in a joint account, and the sponsor adds a continuity bonus for each issue we both stay in, so the total to split at the end grows substantially if we last the whole run. Before each issue, one of us, alternating, has the unilateral option to dissolve the project immediately and take a larger share of what has accumulated so far while leaving the other with a smaller share, or to continue to the next issue. We both know the end date, we both know the other is analytically minded and thinking strategically, and we have openly agreed we would both do better if we stayed in to the end. Yet I am tempted to dissolve early, and I suspect he is reasoning the same way. Should we try to stay in, and if so, how can we make staying in credible without changing the formal rules or bringing in outside enforcement?
