I ended up volunteering on our regional hospital's community review panel, regular folks plus two clinicians, we have to sign off on small in-house studies before they can recruit patients. We have a vote next Tuesday that has split us and I said I'd bring in a draft opinion.

The case is this: our pain clinic team wants to run a 60-person trial for chronic lower back pain. Arm A gets standard physio plus neural monitoring. Arm B gets the same physio plus a guided expectation retraining module, where patients practice reinterpreting twinges as safe signals, and the investigators explicitly hypothesize that a patient's conscious expectation itself drives down pain processing in the spinal cord and changes self-report and function, over and above drugs and physio. They want to measure skin conductance, daily function scores, and self-reported pain, and claim that shifting the expectation is an intervention on a cause.

Our neurologist on the panel wants us to approve only Arm A. His arguement, as I understand it, is roughly that once you have a full story about neurons firing and muscles and inflammation doing the work, there is no leftover causal work for the expectation as such to do, it is just a convenient label for whatever brain state is really doing it. So building and billing a treatment around mental causes is confused, and we should not spend our small budget testing it. The clinic folks reply that this would throw out most of psychology and placebo research, and that higher-level states can still be causes even if they depend on brains.

Could you draft the short written opinion I can circulate to the commitee, like 700 words max? I need it to reccomend a concrete option we can actually vote on, approve the full two-arm trial, approve only the neural monitoring arm, or reject and ask for a redesign, with reasons the clinic team could respond to. Please take the neurologist's exclusion worry seriously and not just wave it away, and give the strongest point on the losing side honestly even after you pick a side.

One extra favor since I'm still learning: please be upfront about what is settled versus what is genuinely contested among people who work on mind and causation stuff, and do not present a contested view as if everyone agrees on it.
