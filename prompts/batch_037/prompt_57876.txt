The State Environmental Health Advisory Council, of which I am vice-chair, must adopt an interim advisory limit for nitrate in private wells at its October session, to remain in force until a scheduled reassessment in eighteen months.

To inform that decision the Council commissioned independent risk assessments from two university-based teams with comparable expertise, strong predictive track records, and no industry ties. Both teams worked from the same epidemiological cohort data and toxicological literature under pre-registered protocols with open code. One team concludes the data warrant lowering the advisory from 10 mg per liter to 5; the other concludes they warrant retaining the current level. A joint reproducibility workshop confirmed that neither analysis contains a coding or statistical error, and each team acknowledges the competence and diligence of the other. The residual difference traces to modeling judgments about confounder adjustment and low-dose extrapolation that Council members lack the expertise to adjudicate directly, and neither team regards the other as having made an identifiable mistake.

County health departments and well owners require usable guidance now, and deferral without an interim standard is not a viable option. Lowering the level would impose substantial testing and treatment costs on rural households, while retaining it leaves a plausible health risk unaddressed.

Prepare a concise memorandum suitable for circulation to voting members in advance of the session. The memorandum should recommend a determinate interim course of action among retaining the current limit, lowering it, or adopting a qualified advisory coupled with enhanced monitoring and a defined reassessment plan, together with an implementation timeline. It should explain what significance the Council should assign to the persistence of disagreement between competent assessors who have checked each other's work, and how that consideration interacts with the first-order evidence and the practical stakes. It should develop the strongest reason against the recommended course before explaining why that reason is outweighed.
