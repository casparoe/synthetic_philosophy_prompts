For an undergraduate seminar on existentialism I am pairing Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith with Kazuo Ishiguro's The Remains of the Day, and I want an interpretation I can assign as background reading. My students already understand the basic structure of bad faith as a way of evading freedom by identifying entirely with facticity or with transcendence, illustrated by the waiter who plays at being a waiter and the young woman who disavows the implications of a companion's advances, and they know the general plot of Ishiguro's novel about the English butler Stevens looking back on his service to Lord Darlington, his professional ideal of dignity, and his restrained relationship with Miss Kenton. After discussing the novel with a colleague, I found myself unsure whether Stevens is a textbook case of bad faith or whether his historical situation and professional ethos expose a limitation in Sartre's framework.

Please develop a reading of Stevens's choices and self-justifications through the lens of radical freedom and bad faith that takes both the philosophy and the novel seriously. Show how the concepts illuminate specific turning points such as Stevens's appeal to dignity and to the judgment of great employers to explain his deference during Darlington's conferences in the 1930s, his handling of his father's death while on duty and of Miss Kenton's attempts at intimacy, and his retrospective remarks near the end about having given his best years and having trusted those in a position to judge wisely. For each, explain whether Sartre would count the maneuver as bad faith and why, consider where the novel resists a Sartrean verdict by suggesting that loyalty, class position, or retrospective self-understanding might count as more than excuses, and conclude by stating what the novel dramatizes about self-deception that abstract exposition of bad faith tends to miss. Please paraphrase scenes in your own words rather than reproducing dialogue, and pitch the discussion to thoughtful undergraduates without prior literary scholarship.
