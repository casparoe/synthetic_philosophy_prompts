Please provide an example of competing conceptual slippages in the following invented analogy microdomain. The microdomain consists of triples of departure times on the same morning, listed in order, such as 8:00, 8:20, 8:40. An analogy problem consists of a source pair in which a first triple is transformed into a second triple, plus a target triple to which an analogous transformation is to be applied. By a conceptual slippage I mean a case where what counts as the same change must be reinterpreted to fit the new context, for example treating addition of a fixed number of minutes as interchangeable with proportional lengthening of an interval, or treating a position on the clock as interchangeable with a position in the list, rather than copying the surface change literally. Invent a specific source pair and a specific target triple, not drawn from any familiar textbook illustration, such that a strictly literal copying of the source change to the target is either inapplicable or clearly unnatural, while at least two different slippages each yield a coherent and defensible completion. Present the source pair, the target, and the two completions, explain for each completion which notion has slipped and how, explain why the literal reading fails, and discuss which completion you find more natural and why, without dismissing either completion as a mere mistake or oversight. Assume a philosophically interested reader with no prior acquaintance with work on fluid concepts, so clarify ideas as you introduce them. Please write between 300 and 450 words in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of an email or letter.
