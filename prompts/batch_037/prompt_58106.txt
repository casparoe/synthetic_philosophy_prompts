I am serving as a teaching assistant for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in metaethics and require assistance in evaluating an examination response. Please assess the response described below with the care expected in formal academic grading.

Examination question: Explain the distinction between internal and external interpretations of a claim of the form that an agent has a reason to perform an action, with particular attention to the role of the agent's existing motives and capacities for reflective deliberation. Then consider the following case.

Nadia is a junior analyst at a municipal water authority who cares deeply about conscientious stewardship and public health. She currently supports the adoption of a cost-saving additive because she holds the false empirical belief that it is safe. If that factual error were corrected, her own concerns would lead her, upon careful reflection, to oppose its adoption. Consider also a further counterfactual variant in which, even after correction of all relevant factual errors and careful reflection proceeding from her existing concerns, Nadia would remain entirely unmoved by the long-term ecological damage to a distant wetland she will never visit, although a colleague maintains that she nevertheless has a reason to take that damage into account in her professional recommendations.

For each situation, determine whether Nadia has an internal reason to act as described, whether an external interpretation would yield a different verdict, and whether the absence of an internal reason, where it is absent, entails that other forms of adverse appraisal of her failure to act are unwarranted.

Student answer to be graded:

The internal interpretation holds that Nadia has a reason to act only if there is a route from her present motives, via correction of false beliefs and careful reflection, to her being motivated to so act. The external interpretation holds that she can have a reason even if no such route exists. In the first situation Nadia has an internal reason to oppose the additive, because correcting her false belief about its safety would, given her commitment to public health, lead her to be motivated to oppose it. This illustrates the central internalist idea that reasons must be capable of engaging the agent's motives.

This central idea is the same as the thesis that sincere moral evaluation necessarily motivates, so Williams's position stands or falls with motivational internalism about moral judgment: evidence of agents who make sincere moral judgments while remaining unmoved would refute it. In the counterfactual wetland variant, Nadia by stipulation has no internal reason to take the damage into account, since no reflective route from her motives would produce such motivation. It follows that the colleague's external claim is false, and that it would be unwarranted for others to regard Nadia as criticizable, for example as professionally irresponsible or as showing a defect of imagination or care, for failing to respond to considerations to which she has no internal reason to respond.
