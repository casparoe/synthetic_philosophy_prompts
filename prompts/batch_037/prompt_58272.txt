I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and for my dissertation I am working within a consumer-centred version of teleosemantics. On the account I presuppose, what makes an inner state carry one content rather than another is not what typically causes it, nor its present causal role alone, but what downstream systems that use the state were selected for doing with it: the state has a given content when producing responses appropriate to that content is the historically selected effect that explains the preservation of the consumer mechanism, with misrepresentation understood as the non-performance of that effect when conditions depart from the historically normal ones.

I am attracted to this view because it promises a naturalistic treatment of error and of content determinacy that purely causal-informational or present-tense functionalist rivals seem to lack. To test my own commitment, I have been developing two variations that are not the familiar textbook cases. One concerns a first-generation synthetic signalling loop in which bioengineers introduce a wholly novel molecule into a bacterial population, where the molecule mediates density-dependent behaviour successfully from the first trial because the designers exploit general chemical dispositions, so there is coordinated success with no selection history for that signal. The other concerns a conserved consumer with a plastic producer, where a neural pathway preserved by selection for predator avoidance driven by visual edge information comes, after developmental change in a new environment, to be reliably triggered by thermal gradients while continuing to guide the same adaptive behaviour. I find my intuitions about whether content is present in the first case, and whether it persists or shifts in the second, unstable.

I would like a systematic map of where a position of this kind is exposed. Please set out the empirical discoveries, conceptual arguments, intuitive judgments about cases, and formal or theoretical results that should be taken to tell against it, ordered by how seriously they threaten the core commitment as opposed to calling only for local revision. Include both considerations that have arguably already materialized and ones that remain merely possible, say what distinctive predictions the consumer-centred view makes that its non-historical rivals do not, and identify which objections its own defenders ought to acknowledge as genuine costs rather than dismiss as external misunderstandings. I am especially sensitive to evidence from systems neuroscience about how content ascriptions figure in explanation, and to results suggesting the view collapses into a rival or purchases determinacy only by appeal to normal conditions specified so flexibly that little is risked. Please therefore give an honest assessment of whether successive qualifications have left the view with substantive commitments that could fail, and where you judge the insulation to be legitimate refinement rather than evasion.
