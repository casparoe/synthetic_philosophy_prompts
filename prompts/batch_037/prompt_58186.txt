Following my brother's recent enlistment, discussion in my family has stalled between two positions I take seriously: principled pacifism, which holds that killing in war is never morally permissible, and a defensive just-war view, which holds that lethal force can be permissible to resist grave aggression. Draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered section of agreed claims about killing, complicity, and nonviolent alternatives; a table stating their residual disagreements precisely, in wording each side would accept for its own view; the cruxes that would move at least one side; and for each crux a concrete case both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Center the cases on two situations now relevant to us: a cyberattack that disables civilian infrastructure without initially killing, and taking a non-combat support role in a defensive war.
