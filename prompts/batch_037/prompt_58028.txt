I have recently begun volunteering with a resettlement group in my coastal town, where we must recommend which of two displaced families to sponsor for our single remaining place: one fleeing targeted political persecution and one fleeing the slow loss of farmland to saltwater that leaves them equally destitute but without a persecutor. Could you write a short story, set in a town like mine, in which ordinary residents confronting a case like this come to see two difficulties more clearly — first, whether counting as a refugee should turn on the source of the harm or on the urgency of need, and second, whether a community's duty to take people in should turn on capacity, geographic proximity, or historic contribution to the harm. I would like the story to end without a tidy moral, leaving both distinctions unsettled but sharper than before.
