I am preparing a handout for second-year undergraduates in philosophy of physics on the debate between spacetime substantivalism and relationism. I would be grateful for a compact taxonomy that organises the logical space around concrete verdicts rather than labels readers already favour. Please take the following two test cases as fixed. First, the Void Bucket: consider a universe containing nothing besides a bucket of water set spinning, with an opaque cover so the water has no visible contact with the bucket walls; would the surface still become concave. Second, the Overnight Shift: consider a world exactly like ours in all mutual distances among bodies, except that all matter is displaced three miles east relative to space itself with no observable trace; is that a genuinely distinct possibility. Please present your taxonomy as a numbered list of exactly five items, each of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Let the five items correspond in order to straightforward substantivalism, Leibnizian relationism, Machian relationism, sophisticated substantivalism, and spacetime structuralism. In each item, let the first sentence state how a holder of that view would answer the two test cases without naming any position, and let the second sentence name the position and state its most awkward commitment with regard to the behaviour of rotation or the identity of points. Where two positions give the same pair of answers, use the second sentence to flag that the two questions cannot separate them, and where any combination of answers is left without a holder, note and briefly diagnose it in the second sentence of the nearest item.
