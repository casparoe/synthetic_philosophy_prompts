You are a professional philosopher of science with specialist expertise in quantitative genetics, developmental psychology, and social policy, acting as a neutral drafter. Think carefully and reason precisely, and begin your work with a short private brainstorm of about 150 words in which you map where agreement is easy, where wording will be contentious, and what would genuinely move each side.

Your task is to draft the text of an adversarial collaboration between two scholars who have argued past each other for years about the interpretation of heritability estimates for cognitive and educational outcomes in contemporary high-income societies.

Camp Constraint holds that consistently high within-population heritability estimates, often around 0.6 to 0.7 for adult IQ and educational attainment in such samples, show that genetic differences are a major source of observed inequality and place principled limits on what purely environmental equalization can achieve.

Camp Malleability holds that those same estimates are fully compatible with large and policy-relevant environmental effects, reveal nothing about the causes of mean differences between groups or about the potential effects of untried interventions, and have been overread as showing constraint.

To keep the exercise concrete and to prevent rehearsed slogans, anchor the entire statement in two stipulated vignettes that both camps accept as described:

Vignette Larkspur-Cinderford: two towns share the same distribution of genetic variants relevant to early reading fluency. In affluent and environmentally uniform Larkspur the broad-sense heritability of fluency at age 9 is 0.75. In environmentally variable Cinderford it is 0.35, and the Cinderford mean is 0.8 standard deviations lower.

Vignette Lenses Plus Tutoring: a proposed universal program combining vision screening with correction plus sustained high-dosage tutoring for all children in Cinderford, regardless of genotype.

Produce a joint statement both scholars could sign, organized as follows: first, a numbered list of at least eight agreed claims covering what heritability as a variance proportion does and does not entail, including additivity, gene-environment interaction and correlation, norms of reaction, and within-group versus between-group inference; second, a disagreements table in which each row states one residual disagreement with Camp Constraint wording in one column and Camp Malleability wording in the other, each phrased so its own side would endorse it as fair; third, an application of the agreed claims to the two vignettes showing what follows and what does not follow; fourth, at least three cruxes defined as subquestions such that resolving them would rationally move at least one camp, and for each crux a concrete empirical case, intervention result, or theoretical argument both camps agree in advance would bear on it, with each camp prediction about that result recorded separately; finally, a brief note on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal and why. Keep the language formal, quantitative where relevant, and substantive throughout, with no generic filler.
