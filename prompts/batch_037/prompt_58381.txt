Please help me identify the principle that best systematizes my responses to sad or painful art. I am enriched by canonical tragedies in which a broadly decent protagonist is ruined through understandable error, and I return to them even when I know the outcome in detail. I am similarly moved by sad instrumental music that presents no character or narrative. I am left cold by sentimental tearjerkers that work hard to elicit tears from comparable misfortunes, and I lose engagement when the central sufferer is morally repellent even when the structure is otherwise tragic. I take no pleasure in documentary portrayals of real suffering with similar content and think there would be something amiss in enjoying them as one enjoys tragedy. Is there a single account of the value of painful art that preserves these verdicts, and if not, which verdict should give way for the sake of the most economical unified explanation? Please state the candidate principle precisely and indicate what it predicts for a further case I have not considered, namely an interactive drama in which I freely select the choices that I know will bring about the sad outcome I then witness.
