Suppose two philosophers of causation undertake a good-faith adversarial collaboration. One maintains a counterfactual difference-making view, on which whether one event causes another turns on whether the second would have failed to occur, or would have been different, in the closest circumstances lacking the first. The other maintains an interventionist view, on which whether one variable causes another turns on whether an ideal manipulation of the first, holding other relevant structure fixed by stipulation, would change the second.

Please compose the joint statement they could both endorse after a constructive exchange. The document should be suitable for co-signature and should contain four parts. First, a set of substantive shared commitments concerning what a theory of causation must explain, how counterfactuals and interventions are related as tools, and which familiar problem cases any adequate view must handle. Second, a precise articulation of their remaining disagreements, with each contested thesis formulated in terms its own proponent would regard as fair and non-caricatured. Third, an identification of cruxes, meaning focused sub-questions whose resolution would rationally move at least one party, together for each crux with a test, argument, or hypothetical case both parties deem relevant and with each party's antecedent expectation stated. Fourth, a judgment about which aspects of the dispute are merely terminological and which are substantive, with reasons.

To keep the exercise concrete and self-contained, the statement must apply both accounts to the following three stipulated systems, taking the descriptions here as complete. Case A involves redundant pumps: two pumps each suffice to maintain flow in a desalination line, both operate, flow is normal, and if either had failed alone the other would have maintained the same flow. Case B involves prevented prevention: an update process would have been blocked by a fault, a monitor removes the fault before it acts, the update proceeds normally with no direct contact between monitor and update mechanism. Case C involves adaptive compensation: salt intake normally makes no difference to blood pressure within a moderate range because a reflex fully offsets it, though larger changes or changes with the reflex disabled do alter pressure, and though uncompensated organisms would show sensitivity within the moderate range.

The aim is a text that is neither vacuous nor slanted, separating genuine disagreement from differences of emphasis or vocabulary.
