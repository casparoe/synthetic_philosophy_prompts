You are a professional philosopher specializing in ethics and decision theory, acting as an experienced course instructor with expertise in moral cooperation. Please think carefully and reason step by step. Start with brief brainstorming about what a fully correct answer would have to include, then use that as your rubric.

Course context for the assignment: In seminar we introduced moral trade as an exchange between parties who retain different moral priorities or different empirical beliefs about how to do good, where each party contributes resources or effort toward the other's favored goal in return for reciprocal support toward their own. The point of the analogy with comparative advantage is that differences in relative marginal importance attached to causes, or differences in beliefs about effectiveness, can create scope for both sides to better advance their own moral aims through exchange than by acting alone.

Exam question the student was asked to answer:

Part A: Distinguish moral trade from mere compromise in which parties meet in the middle on a single action, and from convergence in moral belief. Explain why persistent disagreement in fundamental values can still leave room for mutually advantageous trade, with reference to differences in marginal moral weight and differences in empirical beliefs about impact.

Part B: Consider the following new case. Amara gives most moral weight to reducing farmed animal suffering but assigns some positive weight to improving human health in low-income countries. Ben gives most moral weight to improving human health but assigns some positive weight to reducing animal suffering. Amara has fundraising capacity and volunteer time; Ben has the same. Proposed deal: Amara devotes a share of her time to fundraising for bednets against malaria, a cause she ranks low, and Ben devotes a comparable share to campaigning for cage-free reforms, a cause he ranks low, with each contribution conditional on the other. Evaluate whether this arrangement counts as genuine moral trade, whether it can be Pareto-improving relative to a no-deal baseline where each works only on their top priority, assessed in terms of each party's own informed moral preferences, and address one standard worry about such deals, namely that they instrumentalize moral commitments or create leverage for extortion.

Student answer to be graded:

Moral trade is when people with different moral views agree to help each other get what they morally want, like economic trade but for values. It is different from compromise because compromise has everyone give up something to meet in the middle on the same policy, while trade lets each side specialize: Amara cares relatively more about animals at the margin and Ben cares relatively more about global health at the margin, so they exchange support and both get more of what matters most to them per unit of effort. It is also different from just persuading each other, because beliefs can stay different while behavior is coordinated. Differences in empirical beliefs can also help: if Amara thinks bednets are ineffective and Ben thinks they are very effective, that widens the surplus.

The Amara-Ben deal is a textbook case of genuine moral trade. Neither has to abandon her or his priorities. Relative to each working alone, each gets additional resources directed to their top cause at the cost of working on a lower-ranked cause, which by hypothesis they still regard as somewhat good. So both prefer the deal to no deal in light of their own values, which means the deal yields higher expected moral value overall.

For the trade to be rational, both parties must come to agree that the other party's cause is objectively important, otherwise it would make no sense to spend effort on it. Once that shared recognition is in place, the trade is not only permitted but morally required whenever it is available: refusing a Pareto improvement over no deal would be gratuitously wasteful, and the worry about instrumentalization is misplaced because each person is still acting from her own morals. Extortion is a separate practical risk to be managed by making contributions conditional, as here. So the deal should go ahead.
