I am a second-year PhD student in philosophy of mind preparing a seminar paper on whether commissurotomy divides consciousness. For a 45-minute discussion with advanced undergraduates who know the basic lateralization findings but not the specialist debate, I need a custom thought experiment that isolates behavioral coordination from experiential unity.

Please construct a hypothetical variant of a split-brain case built around a familiar bimanual activity, for example selecting items from a tray or assembling parts with both hands together. Call the patient M, with complete commissurotomy but otherwise intact vision, motor control, and subcortical pathways. On each trial, one piece of task-relevant information, such as a color, is projected briefly to the left visual field alone, and a complementary piece, such as a quantity or shape, is projected briefly to the right visual field alone, with fixation monitored and presentation too brief to permit a saccade. M must then, within a tight deadline of a second or two, use both hands together in a single coordinated sweep to complete the instruction, where neither half-instruction alone suffices and success must occur on that trial rather than across trials.

The design must satisfy four constraints: (a) it uses only realistic methods, lateralized presentation, fixation control, and ordinary manual responses, with no science-fictional neural reconnection, telepathy, or enhancement; (b) it controls for left-hemisphere language dominance by making both the cues and the required response fully nonverbal and symmetric, so neither hemisphere has a privileged reporting channel in the task itself; (c) it rules out by timing and setup the familiar serial cross-cueing workaround where one hemisphere watches the other move and then infers, rather than assuming it away; (d) the scenario itself is about 200-250 words and intelligible without specialist background.

After the scenario, walk through what each of two rival readings says and why: the integrationist reading, on which M remains a single subject with impaired access between hemispheres, and the fragmentationist reading, on which M hosts two distinct streams of consciousness. Say what each predicts about both the smooth bimanual performance and the subsequent left-hemisphere verbal denial of one cue. Then identify which single feature of the case is doing the philosophical work, explain what happens if that feature is removed or relaxed, and sketch the strongest reply available to the reading that comes out worse. A case that stipulates unity or disunity by fiat, that smuggles the verdict in through verbal report alone, or that leaves cross-cueing unaddressed does not count as meeting the brief.
