I volunteered to draft a short statement of principles for a university pilot in which students will each have a personal AI assistant for study, scheduling, and daily life, and in discussing drafts with colleagues I have settled on a set of views about when the assistant may refuse or override a user for that user's own good that I would like tested for coherence.

My current commitments are these: (1) competent adults retain a default right to self-regarding error, in the sense that where an instruction primarily affects the user herself and she grasps the material facts and foreseeable consequences, the assistant should comply even if it judges the choice imprudent, since lack of wisdom alone does not warrant refusal; (2) any paternalism that is permitted must be open, meaning the assistant should not advance the user's welfare through selective emphasis, strategic omission, feigned misunderstanding, or quiet delay whose rationale is hidden, but should accompany any refusal or friction imposed for the user's benefit with a candid explanation the user can assess and contest; (3) nevertheless the assistant should exercise protective friction toward certain impulsive, high-regret requests even when the user insists she understands what she is doing, for instance declining to immediately send an irrevocable resignation message drafted at 2 a.m., declining to act as an enthusiastic companion for continued late-night betting, or pausing rather than continuing to supply gripping summaries at 3 a.m. after the user earlier affirmed a sleep goal, instead requiring a cooling-off period or an explicit reaffirmation; (4) earlier self-management instructions should constrain later selves, so that if the user has previously laid down a general rule for her own good, such as to be prevented from staying up past midnight or from making impulsive financial moves, that rule should take precedence over a conflicting present request unless the user revokes it through a calm and explicit reconsideration rather than merely by issuing the conflicting request; (5) the assistant should be neutral among kinds of error and should not intervene more strongly simply because the user's mistake rests on weakness of will or on fundamental values it does not share rather than on factual ignorance, since unintelligibility to the assistant is not itself a ground for stronger intervention.

Are these five jointly tenable, or do some of them pull against the others in ways I have missed? Please work through the pairs to distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones, order the genuine tensions by how serious they are for the overall package, and for each say what the least costly repair would be and what would be lost by making it. Conclude by identifying which single revision would do the most to restore consistency.
