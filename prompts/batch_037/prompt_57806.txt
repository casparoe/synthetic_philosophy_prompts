Please develop a defended research agenda for the following situation: a university has created a three-year fellowship for two philosophers, a field linguist, and a machine translation researcher. The charge is to determine what Quinean considerations about indeterminacy of translation should change, if anything, in translation and interpretation as practiced outside philosophy.

Take as shared background the familiar fieldwork scenario in which a linguist hears the unfamiliar expression gavagai as a rabbit scurries by and discovers that patterns of assent, dissent, and stimulus correlation remain compatible with glossing the expression as rabbit, as undetached rabbit part, as temporal stage of a rabbit, and with further alternatives that preserve observable correlations while varying ontology, quantification, and logic. Take also the broader morals sometimes drawn from the scenario: that reference is inscrutable beyond behavioral evidence, that principles of charity do indispensable yet potentially distorting work in fixing interpretation, and that ontological commitment can shift without behavioral residue.

The fellowship can pursue only two sustained lines of work in depth. Consider the following candidates:

Field lexicography under inscrutability, including whether dictionaries and grammars for newly documented languages should record stimulus conditions, rejected queries, and contexts of use rather than one-to-one glosses, and whether such records would improve later reuse.

Evaluation of machine translation for low-resource languages, where parallel data are sparse and where systems produce outputs that competent speakers accept yet that diverge in ontology or logical form, including whether to replace single-reference automatic scores with task-based measures of acceptable divergence.

Protocols for high-stakes interpreting in medical and asylum settings, where a mass-count, tense-aspect, evidential, or singular-plural distinction present in one language and absent in the other bears on dosage, timing, or attributions of responsibility, including whether interpreters should be required to flag alternatives and to initiate clarification routines.

Training in elicitation and the conscious use of charity, including whether making interpretive charity explicit helps fieldworkers avoid imposing familiar quantification and individuation, or whether it merely systematizes antecedent bias.

Reliance on translated mental-state and normative vocabulary such as belief, intention, knowledge, obligation, and consent in assessments of testimony, culpability, and informed agreement, including whether legal and clinical practices presuppose a determinacy that the arguments undermine.

State which two lines deserve priority for this team and which one or two should be explicitly deprioritized as overrated or as not following from the arguments. Defend the ordering by articulating the criteria employed, such as philosophical centrality, practical stakes, tractability for a team of this composition, relative neglect, and the extent to which other lines depend on prior resolution of a given line. Discuss tradeoffs among the criteria and specify what empirical or philosophical developments over the fellowship period would warrant revising the ranking. Offer a reasoned agenda rather than a survey, working from the cases as described without appeal to further literature.
