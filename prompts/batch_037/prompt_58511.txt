So me and my wife are having the big decison talk about whether to try for a kid next year and a friend laid this argument on me that I can't get out of my head. He says think about it like this: (1) If we have a kid who will suffer at all, that is bad for the kid. (2) If we don't have the kid, that suffering is avoided, and that avoidance is good even though there is no one around to enjoy it. (3) If we have a kid who would be happy, that is good for the kid. (4) But if we don't have the kid, that missing happiness is not bad, because there is no one there who is deprived of it. (5) So in deciding whether to create, the bad stuff counts against creating but the good stuff does not count in favor. (6) So even in our case, where the kid would likely have a really good life apart from normal struggles and maybe my awful seasonal allergies if they inherit them, there is moral reason not to create and no moral reason to create. (7) So we should not have the kid, and by the same logic it would have been better if there had never been happy people at all. That last part seems nuts and I do not want to give up on having a kid, but each step sounded right when he said it. Which step should I reject and what does it cost to reject it, like what else do I have to give up? Can you just explain in plain continuous prose with no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, like an email to a non-philosopher?
