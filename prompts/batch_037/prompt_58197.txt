So i got roped into serving on our city's community arts grant panel and im kinda regretting it. We have $15k left for one project and two finalists, and we have to vote Tuesday.

Finalist one is a painter who wants to do a big, technically insane harbor mural on the side of the library — like photoreal boats and sunsets, people will definitely love it, clear skill, clear object you can point at.

Finalist two is a trio calling themselves the Mending Hour. They want to camp out in a laundromat for a month, fix peoples ripped jackets and busted zippers for free while they chat about fast fashion, no tickets, no object to sell or hang up afterwards, just the repairs and the conversations. They say the whole setup is the artwork.

Half our panel says only the mural is actually art under our guidelines, which just say we fund creation and exhibition of original artworks for the public. The other half says the laundromat thing totally counts, its just a different kind of art. tbh I can see both sides and I need a tiebreaker that won't fall apart next round.

Can you draft a short memo I can circulate to the other five members, like 500-700 words? It should pick one to fund with a clear bottom line, lay out a principled way to think about what counts as art that we could reuse for future weird applications, and steelman the strongest point for the side you didn't pick so people don't feel steamrolled.
