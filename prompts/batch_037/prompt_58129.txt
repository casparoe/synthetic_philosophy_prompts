Construct a philosophical argument establishing that any impartial critical-level view in population axiology that posits a positive threshold for a life to contribute positively to the value of a world remains vulnerable to a variant of the repugnant conclusion unless it accepts an implausible discontinuity in moral value.

For the purposes of the argument, understand the repugnant conclusion as the implication that for any world containing a smaller number of people with very high lifetime wellbeing, there is a morally better world containing a much larger number of people whose lifetime wellbeing is positive but barely above zero. Understand a critical-level view as one according to which the overall value of a world is determined by summing, across all people who ever live, each persons lifetime wellbeing minus a fixed positive critical level.

Ground your argument in the following stipulated sequence of worlds. World W1 contains 2,000 people at lifetime wellbeing level 100. Each subsequent world in the sequence W1 through W50 doubles the population of its predecessor while reducing average lifetime wellbeing by 2 units, so that W2 contains 4,000 people at level 98, W3 contains 8,000 people at level 96, and so on down to W50, which contains an enormous population at level 2, where lives are stipulated to be barely worth living. Assume for illustration a critical level of 20.

Your response should define impartiality across persons and continuity with respect to small changes in individual wellbeing as constraints on an acceptable population axiology, show step by step how the sequence forces a choice between ranking some much larger, much lower-wellbeing world above the critical level as better than W1 and positing a sharp evaluative break once wellbeing falls just below the critical level, explain why such a break is difficult to defend when the underlying individual differences are stipulated to be tiny, and reply to the objection that a threshold for a life to be worth creating can be morally significant even if the phenomenological difference between lives just above and just below it is minor. Write for a philosophically literate reader without presupposing familiarity with specialist literature beyond the definitions given here, and rely only on conceptual analysis and the stipulated case rather than on empirical claims or scholarly references.
