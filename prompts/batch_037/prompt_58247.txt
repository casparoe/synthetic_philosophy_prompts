Having retired after a career in field ecology centred on solitary burrow-provisioning insects, I have recently begun to study philosophical discussions of sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, and free will, and I find myself unconvinced that behavioural flexibility alone marks the difference between intelligent agency and mere routine.

For an informal reading group with other retired scientists, I would be grateful if you could devise a hypothetical field observation that puts this issue to the test. Please describe, in a single paragraph of no more than 200 words and in ecologically plausible terms without human language, telepathy, or science-fictional intervention, two related burrow-provisioning foragers facing the same tidal flat where burrows periodically flood and landmarks shift: Forager A, which copes successfully with a wide range of perturbations such as displaced prey, blocked entrances, and missing landmarks, yet does so in a way that still seems mechanical; and Forager B as a control, which persists in a narrower and outwardly more rigid routine yet seems for all that less mechanical.

After the scenario, please show in separate steps that it satisfies each part of the brief. Explain why a flexibility criterion, on which an agent counts as non-sphexish to the extent it alters behaviour appropriately when circumstances change, would classify A as non-sphexish and B as sphexish, while a purpose-tracking criterion, on which an agent counts as non-sphexish to the extent it adjusts means in light of an end it continues to pursue, would reverse those verdicts. The behavioural evidence for the presence or absence of purpose-tracking must be earned from observable details in the paragraph rather than stipulated by fiat, and the flexibility of A must contribute to its foraging success rather than amount to random variation.

Conclude by identifying which structural feature of A is doing the philosophical work, what happens to the two verdicts if that feature is removed, and how a defender of the flexibility criterion would most plausibly object to the case.
