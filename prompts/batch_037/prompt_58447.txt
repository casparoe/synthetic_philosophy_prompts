Our city council receives about a dozen internal proposals each month presented as win-win changes to bus routing, clinic scheduling, and data sharing across departments, and I as programme officer must triage them. Please develop a reusable decision procedure grounded in the idea of a safe Pareto improvement, which for our purposes means a change that improves at least one affected group by its own informed preferences while making no group worse off, where that assessment must remain true under a range of plausible forecasts and without counting uncompensated losses as outweighed by larger gains elsewhere. The procedure should be usable in about forty-five minutes by staff without economics training, give ordered checks with plain standards of evidence for passing each, specify when to reject outright, and mark explicitly where factual checking ends and a value judgment requiring director sign-off begins. Then apply it to two fictional proposals: a widely supported consolidation of two evening bus routes into a single frequent corridor that lengthens the walk for one housing estate, and automatic swapping of unused clinic appointments across departments. Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot settle and where it is likely to misfire.
