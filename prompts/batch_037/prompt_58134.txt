I serve as director of policy review for a mid-sized city government, responsible for screening proposed ordinances and administrative rules before they go to the elected council for final approval.

My office reviews roughly a dozen proposals each month under significant time pressure. The analysts who conduct the initial review have legal and administrative training but no background in political philosophy, and each review must be completable in about an hour. Our assessments are advisory, with the elected council retaining final authority, but a negative assessment requires us to explain in plain terms why a proposal raises a liberty concern and what would mitigate it.

I want a reusable decision procedure grounded in the three-way distinction among negative, positive, and republican liberty as I roughly understand it: negative liberty as absence of deliberate human obstruction of action, positive liberty as possession of the capacities and conditions for self-direction rather than acting from internalized constraint or lack of viable alternatives, and republican liberty as absence of subjection to the uncontrolled discretionary power of another, even when that power is not currently exercised. The procedure should translate these ideas into a sequence of questions to be asked in order, with guidance on what evidence counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where a proposal can be cleared, where it must be flagged, and where factual inquiry ends and a value judgment by the council must take over.

Please provide such a procedure in the form of a checklist or flowchart described in words that preserves the distinctions that matter most for practice while simplifying where appropriate, and that explicitly marks the points where staff judgment cannot be eliminated and the predictable ways the simplification may misfire.

Then demonstrate the procedure by applying it to the following three pending proposals:

A park-hours ordinance that closes all city parks overnight to reduce vandalism, with a provision authorizing individual officers to permit overnight presence for good cause under no published criteria and with no review of refusals, in a context where officers have stated they intend to enforce the closure only rarely.

A sanitation-department scheduling system in which an optimization tool assigns shifts and in practice honors almost all employee preferences, but the governing labor policy reserves to supervisors an unqualified right to reassign any shift without explanation and without appeal, a right supervisors seldom use.

A voluntary anti-poverty program that offers substantial rental assistance on condition that participants complete a financial-management course and agree to quarterly spending reviews with a caseworker, where eligible residents report that without the assistance they face eviction and have no comparable alternative.

The demonstration should include at least one case in which the procedure yields a result likely to be unwelcome to the council or to program advocates, and should conclude with a concise statement of what the procedure cannot settle and why.
