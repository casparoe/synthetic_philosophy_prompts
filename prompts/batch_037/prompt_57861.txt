I coordinate a science-fiction book club at our town library and work full time in building maintenance, with no academic training in philosophy or physics. Our group keeps dividing over time-travel stories involving a grandparent paradox, where someone goes back and prevents their own birth or their own trip, and I would like a neutral way to sort out what each member actually believes.

Please write a nine-question sorting quiz for non-specialists that starts from willingness to take backward time travel seriously and ends in a named position. Each question should present a short concrete situation with two or three plain-language reactions to choose from, and no question should mention position labels or ask directly which theory the reader likes. The quiz should sort by what people say about cases, not by slogans.

Please build the quiz around three situations I keep thinking about, describing each in your own words as one of the questions, plus six further questions of your design:
1. a toolbox case, where a traveler leaves tools in the past that later allow the same traveler to build a time machine, so the tools seem to have no maker;
2. a prevention case, where a traveler goes back to hide a part needed for their own departure, while still remembering departing;
3. a picnic case, where a traveler goes back for an afternoon, leaves footprints and crumbs, returns, and history books are otherwise unchanged.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto exactly four leaves: a fixed single-timeline view on which contradictory actions must somehow fail, a branching view on which going back creates or enters a different timeline where things can be different, a no-backward-travel view on which travel to the past is simply impossible, and an anything-goes single-timeline view on which even outright contradictions can happen. First lay out the logical combinations your questions allow, then locate these four in that space, note any combinations nobody occupies and say briefly whether that is because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your quiz cannot really separate, rather than papering it over.

After the quiz and scoring explanation, please add a summary table with exactly these four columns: Position label, Typical answer pattern, Core commitment in one sentence, Story moment it finds hardest to handle.
