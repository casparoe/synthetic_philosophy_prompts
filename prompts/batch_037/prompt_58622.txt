Please design a final examination essay task to be answered in 75 minutes under closed book conditions for a third-year undergraduate module on moral psychology and virtue ethics, together with a model answer and a detailed marking guide.

The essay task should center on the situationist challenge to virtue ethics, understood in broad terms as the claim that experimental work on helping, honesty, obedience and similar behaviors shows behavior to vary more with seemingly minor features of situations than with stable character differences, and that cross-situational consistency is modest at best.

To prevent answers that merely summarize that challenge and standard replies, build the question around the following stipulated materials, which students are to take as given for purposes of the exam: first, a hypothetical large scale program that follows trainee health care workers over three years and finds a strong effect of shift level pressures such as time urgency, sleep loss and observed noncompliance by peers on whether trainees speak up about a possible medication error, alongside modest but persistent rank order stability for individuals once situational pressures are held fixed; second, a revisionary proposal that virtues are best understood not as unconditional tendencies to act in fixed ways across situations but as cultivated capacities to notice morally relevant features of situations, including awareness of situational pressure itself, and to respond appropriately in light of that awareness.

The question should require students to determine what virtue ethics actually needs from character traits in order to remain viable as a normative and explanatory project, whether the hypothetical pattern refutes that need or leaves it intact, and whether the revisionary proposal preserves a recognizable form of virtue ethics or concedes the central point of the critique.

Provide a model answer of approximately 550 words that works through that determination in a clear sequence. Provide a marking guide totaling 25 marks, distributed across four required moves, with a short account of what distinguishes an excellent response from a merely competent one for each move and a note on the characteristic error that should lose marks at each stage.
