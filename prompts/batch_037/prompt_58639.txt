As a volunteer member of our county hospice network's standards and innovation panel, I have been asked to prepare the background paper for next month's vote on a proposed six-month pilot with an outside provider offering continuous immersive simulation for terminally ill patients with very limited mobility and prognoses under six months. The system, built from family photographs, letters, and recorded voices, constructs an interactive, largely pleasant daily life in familiar places, supplemented where memories are thin, and includes a dampening feature that for several hours at a time reduces the patient's awareness that the surroundings are simulated; early volunteers in another region reported high contentment and less anxiety and pain, while requesting longer sessions, whereas several of our nurses and two family representatives worry that the comfort comes at the cost of contact with actual loved ones and the facts of one's own life, especially for patients with fluctuating capacity who cannot consistently reaffirm consent, and the pilot would be funded by pausing expansion of our in-person companionship visits.

Please draft the concise written opinion I can circulate to the panel in advance of the vote, of roughly 700 to 900 words, recommending that we approve the pilot as proposed, reject it, or approve a modified version with specific safeguards and review criteria. The opinion should ground its recommendation in an account of what makes a patient's remaining weeks go well when pleasure, calm, and a sense of connection pull in different directions from truthfulness, authorship over one's own story, and ongoing relationship with the real world, and it should give the strongest consideration on the losing side its full weight rather than dismissing it, while also addressing in practical terms how consent should be obtained and rechecked, what role families should have in shaping scenarios, and how to handle the tradeoff with companionship staffing if resources are limited.
