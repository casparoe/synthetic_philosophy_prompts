I am a PhD student in moral philosophy working on agent-relative constraints, and I am trying to work out whether the package of views I have converged on is jointly stable. I would be grateful for a consistency audit rather than a general overview of the literature.

My current commitments are roughly as follows:

(1) Constraint thesis: It is impermissible for an agent to intentionally kill one innocent person in order to prevent two other agents, acting independently and wrongly, from each intentionally killing one innocent person in circumstances that are otherwise morally indistinguishable. The prohibition holds even when the agent is certain, the numbers are known, and no other duties or special relations distinguish the victims.

(2) Threshold thesis: Such constraints are stringent but not absolute. It is permissible, and perhaps required, for an agent to intentionally kill one innocent person in order to prevent a very large number of comparable intentional killings by others, for example fifty killings of the same kind. The threshold is vague, but there is a principled difference between small-number tradeoffs, which the constraint blocks, and large-number tradeoffs, which it does not.

(3) Victim-centered grounding thesis: What fundamentally explains why intentional killing of this kind is wrong is an agent-neutral matter: the grave harm and loss to the victim and those connected to the victim. This badness gives every agent the same reason to want such killings not to occur, regardless of who would commit them.

(4) No brute indexicality thesis: Moral reasons must be universalizable in the following sense. If a consideration makes it wrong for me to act in a certain way in circumstances C, it makes it wrong for anyone to act in that way in morally indistinguishable circumstances. There are no fundamental moral reasons of the form that I must not do it simply because it would be done by me, where the me-ness itself does the justificatory work.

(5) Partiality thesis: Commonsense morality correctly permits a degree of partiality toward associates. I may permissibly devote resources to my own child, student, or promisee rather than producing a greater impartial good for strangers, and this permission is not merely a concession to human weakness.

(6) Verdicts on two invented cases. Signal Box: I operate a rail signal in a conflict zone. If I do nothing, two remote operators, acting wrongly on their own, will each divert a train so that it kills one civilian. If I divert my own train, I will kill one civilian myself but jam the network and thereby prevent both remote killings. Stipulated that intentions, certainty, victim status, and causal directness are held fixed except for who kills and the jamming side effect. I judge intervention impermissible. Reservoir: Same structure, except my diversion kills one civilian and prevents fifty comparable killings by other operators. I judge intervention permissible, perhaps required.

Are (1) through (6) jointly tenable? Please work through the pairwise and three-way tensions, distinguishing genuine inconsistencies from merely apparent ones. Rank the tensions you find by severity, explain for each what would have to give in order to restore consistency and what the cost of that revision would be for the rest of the package, and then identify the single least costly revision that would remove the most pressure. In particular, I want to know whether my grounding and universalizability claims undermine my constraint and threshold verdicts, and whether my partiality claim creates an additional problem or points toward a resolution.
