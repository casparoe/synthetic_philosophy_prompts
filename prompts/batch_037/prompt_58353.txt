I want to sort out when a choice counts as hard because of the values at stake, rather than lack of information or simple indifference, by testing my reactions to cases. In a choice between corporate legal work at a higher salary and conservation fieldwork I find more meaningful, I judge neither option better and a modest raise to the legal job would not settle it. In a choice between two otherwise identical apartments where one rents for slightly less, I judge the cheaper one better and the decision easy. In a choice between remaining at home to care for an aging parent and accepting a year-long fellowship abroad, I also judge it hard, yet here an added arrangement for regular visits home does seem to tip the balance, unlike the raise in the career case. In a choice between two equally appealing holiday destinations, I feel no difficulty in just picking one and no lingering sense that the other remained equally choiceworthy, which contrasts with the career case. What principle best unifies these verdicts, what does it imply for an undecided comparison between staying in a secure but stagnant role and pursuing a riskier vocation that cultivates a different aspect of myself, and which of my verdicts must be revised if none can all be kept? Take a definite position on the best formulation and defend it with reasons rather than surveying alternatives.
