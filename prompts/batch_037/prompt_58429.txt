Compare Benatar's asymmetry about coming into existence — that the presence of pain is bad, the presence of pleasure is good, the absence of pain is good even when there is no one to enjoy it, and the absence of pleasure is merely not bad when there is no one deprived of it — with a deprivation-modified variant holding that the absence of pain is merely not bad rather than good, and that absent pleasure is bad only when it deprives an actual person. Assess which view better withstands the symmetry objection that judgments about absent pains and absent pleasures should be parallel, and which gives the more plausible verdict for a possible child whose life would contain substantial goods alongside unavoidable moderate suffering. Consider whether either view requires an illicit comparison of existence and non-existence for the same subject. Conclude with a final line beginning with Verdict: naming the stronger view in no more than five words.
