Please write a dialogue between two philosophers disagreeing about aggregation in contractualist moral theory. One defends the individualist restriction, holding that what matters is the strength of each individual complaint against a principle and that many weaker complaints cannot add up to outweigh one much stronger complaint; the other holds that numbers matter even across different levels of harm and that refusal to aggregate leads to indefensible verdicts.

Set their disagreement on a concrete municipal decision to be treated as stipulated for purposes of the exercise: a town with a fixed safety budget must choose between repairing a faulty crane that will otherwise leave one worker permanently paraplegic, and remediating a water contaminant that will otherwise cause twenty thousand residents to suffer a month of severe migraines and dizziness. Have them also consider a same harm variant in which the choice is between preventing paraplegia for one worker and preventing paraplegia for a much larger crew, and explain why their principles treat the two choices alike or differently.

Make both speakers equally competent and charitable, with each reply engaging the immediately preceding argument of the other speaker rather than restating a prepared position.

Present the piece as exactly five numbered exchanges, each exchange comprising one turn per speaker and each turn limited to one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
