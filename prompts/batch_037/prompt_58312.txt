Please draft the joint statement that could be signed after a good-faith adversarial collaboration between two philosophers of time: an Open-Future Indeterminist who holds that a genuinely undetermined future contingent is at present neither true nor false, and an Ockhamist who holds that bivalence is preserved for future contingents even if the future is metaphysically open.

The document should concern the familiar example of a naval battle occurring tomorrow and three further stipulated cases to be addressed by both parties: a Satellite Coin case in which an autonomous satellite will flip a genuinely indeterministic coin tomorrow at noon with no present causal factor favoring either outcome; a Martian Storm case in which a dust storm will occur tomorrow as a deterministic consequence of present conditions that are in principle unknowable to any observer; and a Retrospective Bet case in which two agents today place a symmetric bet to be settled tomorrow, and tomorrow one of them says that what was said today turned out to have been true.

Structure the statement as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table and then a cruxes section. The agreed claims should cover at least the distinction between bivalence and excluded middle, what a branching-time model represents, what would count as fatalism, and what norms govern assertion and rational credence about the future. The disagreements table should state each residual disagreement in neutral wording that the holder of each view would accept as a fair formulation of their own position, including whether truth requires a privileged branch or thin red line, whether retrospective ascriptions of past truth are correct, and whether deliberation gives a practical reason to treat the future as open.

For each crux, specify a concrete consideration, thought experiment, or linguistic pattern that both parties agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about that consideration recorded in advance. Conclude by indicating which parts of the dispute, if any, both parties agree are merely verbal.
