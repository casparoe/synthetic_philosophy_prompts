My friend and I have just spent a long evening arguing about Jefferson and slaveholding, Churchill and race, and Columbus and conquest, and in particular about whether being a man of his time mitigates moral judgment. We kept talking past each other, so I want to see what a disciplined adversarial collaboration between our two positions would actually be able to sign.

Imagine such a collaboration between two camps. Camp One defends a contextualist mitigating view: historical context substantially affects blameworthiness and how we should commemorate. Camp Two defends a universalist accountability view: core moral wrongs remain condemnable regardless of era, and appeals to context function mainly as explanation rather than excuse.

Draft the joint statement they could both endorse after good-faith engagement. To keep the exercise tractable without archival research, stipulate for purposes of the document a small set of simplified background assumptions about those three figures sufficient to discuss them, and focus the analysis on principles rather than on settling historical disputes.

The document should contain: a numbered list of shared premises covering the distinctions among judging an act wrong, judging an agent blameworthy, deciding whom to publicly honor, and deciding how to teach the history; a table of remaining disagreements in which each camp states its own position in language the other camp agrees is fair to it; an assessment of which disagreements are merely verbal and which are substantive, with reasons; and a set of cruxes, understood as sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp, each paired with a hypothetical case or practical decision both camps agree would test it, plus each camp's antecedent expectation about that test.

For the tests, use at least these three invented probes I am supplying to force the views beyond slogans: a present-day municipal committee deciding whether to retain a statue with revised interpretation versus remove it; a textbook editor with one paragraph to allocate to a founder who both advanced liberty and participated in oppression; and a comparison between a contemporaneous dissenter who reached a more humane conclusion and an ordinary participant who never encountered that dissent. Explain what each camp says each probe shows.

Throughout, separate what is broadly settled among historians and moral philosophers from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as if it were consensus. Aim for formulations that are precise enough to bite but that neither side would reject as caricature.
