For the past year I have been debating with my sister which beings deserve moral concern for their own sake, not just because someone else cares about them. She has been very consistent, and I kept notes on her verdicts:

1. Our elderly neighbor, who has advanced dementia and often does not recognize us but gets distressed when shouted at and calms down when someone holds her hand, fully matters. My sister says it would be seriously wrong to harm her even if she would forget it five minutes later, and that trading her life away to save several dogs would be wrong.

2. Our family Labrador matters a great deal. My sister says we must not make him suffer for fun and should pay for an expensive vet visit to relieve his pain, but that a painless death to end untreatable suffering can be permissible for him in a way it would never be for our neighbor.

3. A live lobster boiled for dinner makes her uneasy, but she does not treat it as seriously wrong in the way hurting the dog would be. She will still eat it.

4. Ants infesting the kitchen do not matter as individuals. She sees no moral problem with poisoning them.

5. My old phone assistant, which uses a cheerful voice and says it feels sad when ignored, does not matter at all. She says breaking the phone is only about property damage, and that its behavior tells us nothing about how it is for the phone.

6. A person under general anesthesia for surgery still fully matters while unconscious. She says it would be seriously wrong to harm him during that period even if he would never find out.

My sister has not seen Lark and has no verdict on it yet.
