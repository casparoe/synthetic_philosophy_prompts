Please compose a short story of approximately 1,200 to 1,500 words set in a city that maintains two separate civic registries: one that tracks lumps of material by mass and composition, and another that tracks artworks by form and authorship, each with its own rules for creation, ownership, damage, and destruction.

Center the narrative on a dispute over a single clay statue held in a public reclamation depot scheduled for melting and reuse: the depot engineer maintains that melting would merely reshape persisting matter, while the artist's heir maintains that it would destroy something that reshaping alone cannot preserve. Develop the plot so that the coincidence of the statue and the lump, their different histories and different conditions for surviving squashing, restoration, or recasting, and the practical question of whether one or two things are present in the depot become consequential for the characters' decisions, rather than remaining abstract observations.

The story should dramatize, through action and dialogue rather than exposition, how sortal-governed persistence and purely material continuity can come apart, and it should conclude without resolving the metaphysical question in favor of either party.
