As a retired marine biologist who has spent the past three years engaged in systematic private study of philosophy, I now facilitate a monthly discussion circle for adults at our town library and would value assistance in preparing our next session.

The group will consist of 16 to 20 participants, most without formal training in philosophy, mathematics, or economics, meeting for 80 minutes in a single room with movable chairs. I wish to offer them a participatory exercise centered on superrationality experiments of the Platonia and luring-lottery kind, arranged so that they encounter the central tension in their own decisions before any theoretical label is introduced. By the end, participants should have a vivid sense of why the thought that similarly situated reasoners will choose alike can seem to favor cooperation even when defection is causally dominant, and why that thought becomes unstable once a tempting side prize for defectors is added.

To keep the exercise self-contained for persons unfamiliar with the literature, please build it around the following variant, which I have sketched but not fully developed. In Stage A, each person privately and simultaneously marks a slip as Contribute or Retain, with no communication allowed and with explicit emphasis that everyone faces precisely the same problem. Retaining gives 3 workshop scrip to the chooser irrespective of what others do. Contributing gives no scrip to the chooser but gives 1 scrip to each other participant. Thus universal contribution yields far more per person than universal retention, yet retention yields more for the individual whatever others do. In Stage B, only those who marked Retain in Stage A are eligible to mark a second box to enter a bonus pool: if exactly one person enters, that entrant receives 15 additional scrip; if none enter or more than one enters, no bonus is awarded and each entrant loses 1 scrip. This second choice is also simultaneous and sealed. The facilitator is not to mention superrationality, diagnostic correlation, or the names of the dilemmas until the closing discussion.

Please provide a complete facilitation plan that I could run directly: step-by-step procedures, items required beyond paper and pens, a token points system requiring no money, a minute-by-minute timeline fitting the 80 minutes, and precise wording I might use to present the choices without revealing the point. Include also a closing discussion guide that connects specific moments in the two stages to the conceptual distinctions at issue, an account of implementation difficulties likely to arise on a first run with a lay adult group and practical remedies, and a candid statement of which important feature of the theoretical idea a live exercise of this kind cannot reproduce and must instead be supplied by explanation.

After the main plan, please append a brief structured summary in the form of a JSON object with exactly the following fields, with content as described: activity_title for the short name of the exercise, phase_timings for the allocation of minutes across stages, materials_needed for the list of physical items, target_insight for the one-sentence takeaway participants should retain, likely_problem for the most probable facilitation difficulty, limit_of_exercise for the feature the activity cannot reproduce.
