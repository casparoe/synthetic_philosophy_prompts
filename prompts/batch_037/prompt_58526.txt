Stage an extended exchange between two clinicians who disagree about what death is. One maintains that irreversible loss of all brain function constitutes death, even when a ventilator and drugs keep the heart beating, because the capacity for consciousness and central integration is gone. The other maintains that death is the loss of the organism as a self-maintaining integrated whole, so a body that continues to regulate temperature, heal wounds, fight infection, or gestate over months remains alive despite satisfying neurological criteria. Have them focus on two challenging practices: maintaining a body that satisfies neurological criteria for months with continued biological integration, and a donation protocol in which circulation is declared lost after a few minutes of asystole and then artificially restored to the torso while brain circulation remains blocked. Both interlocutors should be equally skilled, address each other's reasons directly rather than talking past one another, and make clear whether they take the dispute to be about biological facts, about the concept of death, or about moral permissibility.
