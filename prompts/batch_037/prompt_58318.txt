Earlier this year I became the day-to-day helper for my elderly father after his hospital discharge, doing his shopping and planning his afternoons together with him, with his full involvement and consent, and the responsibility has made a recurring puzzle hard to set aside.

In the shop, when both sweet biscuits and fruit are in front of him, he reliably takes the biscuits. This has held for months, without hesitation, without later surprise, and without any confusion about health effects or prices, which we have discussed openly. Yet when we review the week together and I ask which pattern of eating left him feeling steadier and more himself by his own standards, or which pattern he would want me to arrange if I were stocking the flat for him in advance, he sincerely favours the fruit, and he is neither embarrassed nor trying to impress me. If both options are present again the next day, he again takes the biscuits, still without struggle or apology.

A similar shape appears in the afternoons. Offered a walk around the block or an hour of daytime quiz programmes, he will turn on the television. Asked afterwards which kind of afternoon makes the day go better for him, and which he would select for a coming week if I booked it ahead and removed the moment-by-moment choice, he favours the walk, again sincerely and with stable judgment. There is no inner battle at the moment of choice, no claim afterward that he misjudged, and no pressure from others to say one thing while doing another.

What I am trying to isolate is this stable gap between what a person dependably selects when options are side by side and what the same person sincerely judges to be better for himself by his own lights, where lack of information, forgetfulness, outside pressure, and inner conflict followed by regret have all been ruled out. It is not a case of being torn and giving in, nor of learning something new afterward, nor of saying one thing for show. A bare label will not help me; I need to understand what exactly this pattern is, what nearby patterns it must be distinguished from, and where its boundaries lie, with examples of cases that would count and cases that would not.

Is there an established concept for this exact configuration, and if so, what are the two or three closest neighbouring concepts, how does the pattern I describe differ from each, and which fits best? If there is nothing established that fits, please coin a term and give a precise definition that separates it from giving in to temptation on one side and from simple error or performance for others on the other, including how to handle advance booking versus moment-by-moment selection as a test.
