As a novelist preparing a manuscript set in an isolated maritime community, I have developed a provisional grammar and lexicon for my characters in order to make their inner lives linguistically distinctive yet humanly intelligible. The provisional system has three central properties. First, every claim about the past must carry an obligatory marker of how the speaker came to know it, whether by direct witnessing, hearsay, or inference, while the future can be expressed only through constructions of intention or obligation. Second, location and direction are expressed exclusively through fixed bearings anchored to the sea, the mountain, and the prevailing wind, with no equivalents for left, right, in front of, or behind. Third, the lexicon contains only four general colour categories but more than forty fine-grained distinctions among states of water, weather, and light on water.

Before I decide which cognitive effects to portray as flowing from these features, I require a clear specification of what an adequate theory of linguistic relativity must deliver. Please formulate the desiderata for such a theory as it would bear on my authorial purpose, which is plausibility rather than proof: to distinguish depictions of language shaping thought that a knowledgeable reader could accept from depictions that collapse into magic or caricature. For each desideratum, state the requirement, explain the phenomenon, case, or distinction that motivates it, and illustrate its force with one of my three linguistic properties above. The list should cover the kinds of effects a theory must countenance or rule out, the distinctions it must preserve, such as between habitual attention and conceptual incapacity, between individual cognition and coordinated social practice, and between transient performance in a task and durable disposition, and the basic constraints from human learning and mutual intelligibility that it must respect. Indicate which requirements are non-negotiable for my purpose and which reflect stronger scientific ambitions I may set aside, note any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify which single requirement does the most work in excluding both crude determinism and blanket dismissal of linguistic influence. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their rationale rather than on adjudicating existing positions at length.
