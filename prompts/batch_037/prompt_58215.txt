Please restate ongoing historiographical disagreements about the role of germs, climate, and volcanic eruptions in shaping human history using the vocabulary of causal inference and systems modeling, and then evaluate what that restatement fails to preserve. For background, I am a senior data scientist with extensive applied experience with directed graphs, potential outcomes, counterfactual reasoning, mediation and moderation analysis, confounding, exogenous variation, and intervention design, and I am comfortable with technical distinctions among direct and indirect effects, necessary and sufficient conditions, and heterogeneous effects.

Consider three stylized positions: first, that environmental and biological conditions determine large-scale outcomes such as economic divergence or state breakdown; second, that such conditions only constrain or enable human choices by altering costs, options, and vulnerabilities; third, that describing nonhuman forces as agents or actors confuses causal influence with intentional action and risks reviving determinism under a new label.

Apply your restatement to the following two schematic cases, whose details you should take as given rather than verify: a case in which several years of volcanically induced cooling reduce harvests, increase staple prices, and precede a wave of urban protest and migration; and a case in which enduring regional differences in disease pressure coincide with contrasting patterns of settlement, labor coercion, and long-run prosperity.

For each position and each case, show how the claim would be expressed with graphs, variables, counterfactuals, and interventions, identify where the expression is straightforward, where it requires stretching or narrowing the original idea, and where no faithful expression is available. Conclude by assessing whether any untranslatable remainder reflects a limitation of the causal modeling vocabulary or indicates that the original historical claim was ambiguous or overstated. Please answer in American English.
