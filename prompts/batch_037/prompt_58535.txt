I am a second-year undergraduate currently taking a course on normative ethics. In our recent seminar on supererogation, our tutor characterised a supererogatory act as one that is neither morally required nor morally forbidden, that is morally good to perform and morally praiseworthy, and whose omission is permissible and does not deserve blame or moral criticism. I am having difficulty distinguishing such acts from permissible acts that are admirable for primarily non-moral reasons, such as kindness as etiquette, intellectual skill, or prudence, especially when both kinds involve some cost to the agent and some benefit to others. Could you help me by constructing a pair of original, everyday examples set in university life featuring the same student protagonist and broadly similar personal costs and benefits to others, where the first example satisfies the characterisation of supererogation and the second example is admirable but not supererogatory because its goodness is mainly non-moral? For each example, please walk through how it meets or fails each element of the characterisation, and explain why failing to perform the first act would be morally worse yet still blameless, whereas failing to perform the second act would not even be morally worse.
