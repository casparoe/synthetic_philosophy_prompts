Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers, one who defends a communal duty to remember past atrocities and one who defends the moral value of collective forgetting for reconciliation. Have them debate a fictional proposal in a town still divided by a sectarian massacre committed eighty years ago, where no survivors remain and the population now includes descendants of both victims and perpetrators as well as newcomers with no connection to the events: whether to discontinue the annual commemoration and convert the memorial hall into a shared civic center. Each should respond directly to the other's actual arguments about moral identity, resentment, and what remembrance is for, developing the exchange over several turns rather than talking past each other, with neither side presented as a strawman.
