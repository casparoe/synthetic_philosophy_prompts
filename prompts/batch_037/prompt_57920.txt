Render the central claims of the deflationary view of truth into the conceptual vocabulary of contemporary programming-language implementation, and provide a disciplined account of what fails to carry over.

Assume an addressee with deep professional fluency in language toolchains: routine work with macro expansion, syntactic sugar, inlining, zero-cost abstractions, type erasure, and the contrast between compile-time bookkeeping and genuine runtime properties. The addressee has no background in academic philosophy and should encounter philosophical ideas solely through the proposed redescription.

Take as fixed source content the following theses, supplied here so that no outside recall is needed. First, an equivalence thesis: to affirm a claim and to affirm that claim as true is to undertake the same commitment; ascribing truth adds no further property or relation to be analyzed. Second, an expressive-function thesis: the utility of a truth predicate lies in allowing endorsement without repetition and in formulating generalizations over open-ended or infinite collections of claims that cannot be enumerated conjunctively, such as endorsing in one step everything generated by a source. Third, a denial of general explanatory weight: truth as such does not explain the success of inquiry or action; whatever explanation is available is given by particular facts about particular domains, not by a shared substantive relation. As foil, take an inflationary view on which truth names such a substantive relation, one that grounds the success of beliefs and the norm that belief aims at truth. Include also two pressure points often raised against deflationism: self-applicative sentences that generate paradox when the predicate is applied to codes for sentences that refer to their own status, and the normative dimension in which truth appears to prescribe belief rather than merely abbreviate it.

Make the redescription concrete through an invented but fully specified illustration. A team designing a declarative policy language considers introducing an operator TRUE that applies to codes for sentences of the language. The intended uses are exactly those emphasized by deflationists: to endorse a sentence without restating it, and to write quantified policies that range over large or evolving sets of sentences, for instance a rule that accepts everything emitted by a trusted module. The team is tempted by the slogan that TRUE is mere syntactic sugar with no runtime cost. Show how each deflationary thesis above would sound as a claim about this operator and about the corresponding inflationary alternative.

Proceed thesis by thesis, keeping the mapping explicit and honest. Do not present consequences of implementation choices as if they were translations of philosophical claims. For each thesis, indicate whether the implementation vocabulary supplies a straightforward counterpart, whether it supplies one only by stretching the meaning of either the philosophical claim or the implementation concept, and whether it supplies none at all. Where stretching is required, name what had to be added or suppressed. Close by assessing the pattern of gaps: for each significant gap, judge whether it exposes an expressive limitation of the implementation vocabulary or suggests that the original philosophical claim was less determinate than its formulation implied, with reasons tied to the details of the mapping.
