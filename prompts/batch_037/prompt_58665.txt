I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I work primarily on applied machine learning systems for tabular and language data. In design reviews, colleagues often invoke no-free-lunch results to argue that no learner can be generally better than any other and that any preference for simpler models or for methods that did well in the past is ultimately unjustified, while our day-to-day practice depends on exactly such preferences working reliably.

Before I commit to any particular philosophical view of induction, I would like a specification of what an adequate account would have to deliver in order to be useful for engineering practice rather than merely logically consistent. Please set out the desiderata for a theory of induction under no-free-lunch constraints: the phenomena it must explain, the practical cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, and the established technical results it must remain consistent with, giving in each case the underlying reason why that item is a requirement.

To keep the discussion grounded, please anchor several requirements in the following recurring situations from my work: first, a fraud-detection pipeline where tree ensembles consistently outperform larger flexible models on new months of data despite having higher bias; second, a proposal to adopt as team policy that, among models within one percentage point of best validation error, we always deploy the one with the smallest implementation complexity, on the grounds that this will generalise better in future.

For each desideratum, please indicate whether it is non-negotiable for engineering purposes or reflects the prejudice of a particular philosophical camp, identify any pair or subset that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and state which single requirement you expect to exclude the largest number of familiar approaches. Then illustrate briefly, without extended exposition, how two or three familiar broad approaches fare against the list, keeping the emphasis on the criteria themselves. Please write your answer in British English and structure it with numbered headings for ease of reference.
