After a protracted argument with a friend last evening about whether progressive taxation can ever be just, I want to check whether his rendering of Nozick's entitlement view was fair. He characterised it roughly as follows: a distribution is just provided it arose from a just starting point by voluntary transfers, so if we begin from any equal division and supporters freely pay a small premium to watch a star player such as Wilt Chamberlain, the resulting inequality is just and cannot be corrected without forbidding free exchange; hence no egalitarian pattern can be preserved alongside liberty, and taxing earnings to restore equality is morally on a par with compulsory labour. I found the basketball illustration forceful but suspect it overstates matters. Which elements of this gloss would a careful defender of Nozick endorse, which conflate claims he keeps apart, and does his sweeping conclusion about egalitarianism and taxation actually follow? To make this concrete, consider a variant we quarrelled over: supporters pay the premium, but the star then voluntarily donates a third of the proceeds to a local youth club, partly restoring equality. Does that voluntary re-equalisation blunt or reinforce the argument?
