I serve as secretary to the University Faculty Senate's Ad Hoc Committee on Electoral Reform, which must recommend a voting procedure for the annual election of the Senate Chair before our final vote next month.

Our electorate comprises roughly 900 voting faculty, with typically three or four candidates. We currently use plurality with a deterministic tie-break by seniority. After last April's result, in which the plurality winner received 38% against runners-up with 33% and 29% amid widespread reports of supporters of the third-place candidate voting insincerely for the second-place candidate to block the frontrunner, the Committee is considering a proposal to adopt ranked-choice instant runoff on the stated ground that it would fully eliminate incentives for insincere voting.

Our charter directs us to prefer, if possible, a deterministic single-winner system satisfying three desiderata: that it accept any possible configuration of sincerely ranked ballots, that each candidate be a possible winner under some configuration of ballots, and that no voter ever be able to obtain a more preferred outcome by submitting an insincere ranking rather than a sincere one, with no single voter empowered as a dictator whose top choice always prevails regardless of others.

Please draft a decision memo of 700-900 words, suitable for circulation to non-specialist Senate members, recommending a concrete course of action on the pending proposal. The memo should explain in accessible but precise terms why the charter desiderata cannot all be jointly satisfied when there are three or more candidates, illustrate the problem with a concrete hypothetical ballot profile modeled on our three-faction electorate and our tie-break rule, clarify what ranked-choice instant runoff does and does not achieve with respect to strategic incentives, and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration in favor of the course of action you reject, along with practical next steps for implementation or further review.
