I am a Year 12 student in England working on an enrichment project about studying and memory, and I would be grateful for help with a pattern I keep running into but do not have the vocabulary to describe precisely.

In my own revision I have noticed three situations that feel similar but not identical. First, I no longer memorise bus routes or dates for history because my phone holds them, and I consult it automatically without checking the information against anything else. If the battery dies it feels more like I have forgotten something than like I have lost a book. Second, in science practicals my lab partner and I divide things up without planning to: she remembers the sequence of steps while I remember the safety thresholds and the formulae, and neither of us could complete the task smoothly alone, though together we can. Third, when doing maths homework I set up the structure of a problem myself but let a calculator carry out the intermediate arithmetic, and I could not report those intermediate numbers from memory afterwards even though I directed the overall solution.

My question is whether philosophy already has an established name for what is going on here, where remembering or working something out seems to happen partly outside any one head. I am not looking for a bare label. If there is an established idea, please explain what its nearest neighbouring ideas are, how each of my three cases fits or fails to fit them, and where you would draw the boundaries between merely being helped by a tool or another person and having that tool or person count as part of the thinking itself. If there is no established idea that covers this, please propose a clear definition of your own and explain how it would handle the differences between my cases, including what changes when the phone is offline, when I double-check rather than trust automatically, and when the work is split between people.

Please write your answer in British English at a level that is precise enough to be useful but still accessible to a capable secondary school reader.
