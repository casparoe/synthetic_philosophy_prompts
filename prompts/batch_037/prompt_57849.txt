In the course of settling my late father's estate earlier this year, I learned that the house I grew up in was purchased with a federally backed mortgage of a kind effectively unavailable to Black buyers in our county at the time, and that equity from its later sale helped pay for my education. I have since agreed to serve on a seven-member Reconciliation Task Force created by our city council to recommend a one-time use for 2.4 million dollars from the sale of a vacant school site in a neighborhood where a thriving Black business district was cleared for highway construction in the 1960s.

Two proposals are before us. One would create a fund making fixed grants to persons who can document descent from families displaced by that clearance or from persons enslaved in the county before emancipation, with remaining funds to support genealogical assistance. The other would create a locally administered down payment and repair fund for first-time Black homebuyers who currently live in the census tracts most affected by later disinvestment, regardless of ancestry, with priority for those at risk of displacement now. Several members favor using the money for general affordable housing open to all residents on grounds that enough time has passed that targeted repair is divisive and unworkable.

Please draft the memorandum I can circulate to the council and fellow members in advance of our vote, suitable for adoption as our formal recommendation. It should select one determinate course of action, which may be a specified hybrid with a clear priority rule if you think that is morally required, and lay out eligibility criteria, form and approximate size of awards, administration, and timeline for disbursement. It should explain why the recommended use best answers the wrongs at issue given who benefited, who was harmed, and who bears the cost now, and it should confront directly the strongest moral consideration favoring the course you reject and explain why it does not prevail. I am not seeking a survey of possible views for further deliberation but a committed recommendation with reasons that supporters of the other side could recognize as engaging their concerns.
