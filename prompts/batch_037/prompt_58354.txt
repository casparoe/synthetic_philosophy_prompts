As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I have become preoccupied with how badly calibrated my sense of my own knowledge often is, and I would like help turning that preoccupation into a useful exercise for my local meetup group, whose members are comfortable with talk of credences and calibration but have no professional training in epistemology. Please write for us a sorting quiz that reveals which position on luminosity and iteration a participant actually holds, based on what they say about cases rather than which label they find attractive. For orientation, please take luminosity to be the idea that under certain conditions, when the condition holds one is thereby in a position to know that it holds, and take iteration to be the idea that knowing something generally puts one in a position to know that one knows it. The quiz should contain eight diagnostic questions, each built around a concrete first-person situation, and none of the questions should mention technical terms such as luminous or KK or safety, so that a reader can answer without knowing which answer points where. Five of the situations must be the following: waking in a room that warms almost imperceptibly over an hour and judging when cold has given way to warmth; checking a long multiplication done from memory late at night and judging whether one knows the answer or merely believes it; attending to a faint headache as it fades and judging when it is fully gone; recalling a rarely used PIN under time pressure and judging how reliable that recall is; and assigning a numerical confidence to a perceptual judgment made in poor light and then judging how reliable that confidence assignment itself is. You may invent the remaining three situations, but they should be similarly concrete and should separate phenomenal certainty from higher-order reliability and from sensitivity to small changes. For each situation, describe two or three possible first-person verdicts a participant might endorse, and then provide a scoring key that maps patterns of verdicts onto five destinations: a thoroughgoing anti-luminist who denies both luminosity and general iteration; a restricted defender who allows luminosity only for present phenomenal states such as pain or feeling cold; an unrestricted iteration defender who holds that knowing normally puts one in a position to know that one knows; a calibrationist who prefers to replace talk of knowing that one knows with talk of well calibrated higher-order credences; and a margin-for-error externalist who explains failures of self-knowledge by appeal to similarity among close cases. Build the scoring key from the logical space first, noting which combinations of answers are unoccupied and offering a brief diagnosis of each gap as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of destinations your questions cannot cleanly separate rather than papering over the overlap. Please write the entire piece as continuous prose only, the way one would write an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, numbering the situations only by carrying the discussion forward in sentences and paragraphs, since I want something we can read aloud and discuss.
