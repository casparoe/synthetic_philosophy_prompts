Consider the familiar comparison between fifty years of unrelenting and excruciating torture imposed on one person and a very large number of momentary dust specks affecting distinct people, where each speck causes a few seconds of mild eye irritation, is then completely forgotten, and produces no injury, no lasting distress, and no further causal consequences.

The following argument troubles me because each step seems forced, yet the endpoint conflicts with the starting intuition:

(1) No number of such specks, however large, is worse than the fifty-year torture.
(2) If (1) holds, the difference is not merely one of amount: suffering of the torture kind is discontinuously worse than irritation of the speck kind, such that the former takes priority over the latter in kind.
(3) Priority in kind generalizes: if suffering of kind A takes priority in kind over suffering of kind B, then any instance of A, however brief, is worse than any number of instances of B.
(4) Therefore preventing a single second of torture, for example shortening the fifty-year torture by one second, matters more than preventing any number of specks.
(5) But (4) is unacceptable on its own terms: it would require a decision-maker to forgo preventing an astronomical number of specks, one for each of a vast population of otherwise flourishing lives, in order to shorten one distant torture by a second, which seems fanatical in the opposite direction.
(6) Hence (1) must be rejected: some number of specks does outweigh fifty years of torture.

Identify which premise or inference to reject, explain why it initially seemed compelling, and spell out the theoretical costs of rejecting it as compared with rejecting the alternatives. I am especially reluctant to abandon either the thought in (1) that no number of specks outweighs the prolonged torture or the thought in (5) that shortening torture by a single second cannot outweigh an astronomical number of specks, but I want to know whether both can be held together without arbitrariness, intransitivity, or a similarly unwelcome commitment, particularly once we consider intermediate cases such as a one-minute torture or a gradual series of intensities linking full torture to a mere speck.
