I am a high school junior serving as secretary for my school sustainability committee. At our last meeting, two senior members had an extended disagreement about fairness toward future generations that I observed as a neutral bystander, and I would appreciate help sorting out who was right about what.

We had raised about five thousand dollars to use for climate-related projects, with two proposals on the table. Amara favored spending it all now to install a small solar array on the science wing and to cancel a funded student trip to a regional competition to save emissions, arguing that harms to people living fifty years from now should count equally to harms to people alive now, so the timing of benefits should not matter in our decision.

Ben replied that equal counting does not mean equal sacrifice, because people in the future are likely to have greater wealth and better technology, so a dollar spent helping less well-off students today would improve well-being more than the same dollar spent to produce a small future climate benefit, and it would be unfair to burden current students for the sake of better-off future people.

Amara answered that counting on future wealth is a gamble that excuses inaction, that earlier generations could have used the same reasoning to justify leaving us a warmer world, and that if each generation waits for the next to be richer, no one will ever do the costly work.

Ben countered that if timing never matters, then there will always be another future generation to save, which would require the present to sacrifice without limit, so some form of prioritizing the present must be justified.

Please adjudicate this exchange point by point, explaining where Amara and Ben are each correct or mistaken, identifying any important consideration about justice across generations that both of them missed, and stating who is more nearly right overall. Please keep your entire response under 400 words.
