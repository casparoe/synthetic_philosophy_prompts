For an advanced undergraduate seminar on the moral limits of markets, I have prepared five vignettes that vary the institutional design around transactions often described as repugnant or commodifying. Please evaluate each vignette for its moral permissibility, independent of its current legality:

1. A government monopsony for living kidney donation in which donors receive a fixed payment plus lifetime medical follow-up, with organs allocated strictly by medical need and a ban on private resale.
2. An enforceable futures market for cadaveric kidneys in which healthy young adults receive a modest annual payment in exchange for a binding commitment to postmortem retrieval in the event of accidental death.
3. A hardware store that doubles the price of portable generators the day after a hurricane, while publicly pledging to donate all profit above the pre-disaster price to local relief efforts.
4. A privately funded, nonpartisan program that pays residents a fixed sum for verified turnout in a municipal election, regardless of how they vote.
5. A campaign organization that pays residents the same sum to vote for its candidate, with compliance assessed through self-report and no coercion beyond loss of payment.

Please assign each case a permissibility rating from 1 to 7, where 1 indicates morally impermissible even if made legal and 7 indicates morally unobjectionable, and then rank the five cases from most to least defensible. For each case, state whether the strongest moral concern, if any, is best understood as exploitation or compromised consent, as corruption or degradation of the good at stake, or as harm to third parties and erosion of shared institutions, and explain in two to three sentences how the specific design features strengthen or weaken that concern. Conclude by identifying which design variation across the set most clearly separates these three kinds of objection and why.
