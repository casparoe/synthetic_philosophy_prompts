For the closing session of an advanced undergraduate seminar on material objects, I have invited a guest who defends coincidence about material constitution, namely the thesis that a clay statue and the lump of clay that makes it up at a given time are two numerically distinct objects that for an interval coincide spatially and share all their matter while differing in modal and persistence conditions.

To keep the exchange concrete, I will stipulate in advance three invented cases for discussion. In Flattening, a sculptor shapes five kilograms of clay into a warrior on Monday and rolls it flat on Tuesday. In Rework, the same sculptor reforms that flattened clay on Wednesday into a figure qualitatively identical to the Monday warrior. In Conservation, a museum conservator over several years replaces half the original clay of a fired bust with fresh clay while preserving continuous shape and function as an exhibit.

Please compose seven probing questions intended to move quickly past exposition to critical examination, suitable for students who have already studied identity, indiscernibility, and parthood. The set should include at least one question on the argument from modal difference from survival under flattening to distinctness, at least one on what could ground such a modal difference given complete sameness of matter and arrangement at the time of coincidence, at least one on counting and practical reasoning about how many objects are on the pedestal and how that bears on selling, insuring, or pointing, and at least one that uses Rework or Conservation to test persistence through temporal gaps or gradual material replacement. Append to each principal question a concise follow-up in parentheses that anticipates the most probable coincidence-theorist reply, such as appeal to sortal-governed persistence conditions or to primitive modal properties, and presses that reply one step further. Exclude biographical or motivational questions and focus entirely on the substance and costs of the position.
