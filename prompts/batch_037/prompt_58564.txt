Please assist in developing a prospectus for a nine-month MPhil thesis in philosophy on incommensurable values and hard choices. My provisional hypothesis is that the standard debate concentrates too heavily on the moment of decision and asks what rationality requires when neither option seems better, worse, or equal, while neglecting how agents can retrospectively vindicate a hard choice through subsequent self-shaping.

To make this concrete, consider two stipulated cases to be explored in the thesis. In the first, a physician in training must decide between pursuing surgical specialization, which prizes decisive intervention and technical mastery, and pursuing long-term palliative practice, which prizes sustained attentiveness and accompaniment, where excellence in each field seems to require inhabiting an evaluative outlook that is inhospitable to the other. In the second, a mid-career architect must decide between leading high-density social housing projects and dedicating herself to conservation of historic structures, with similar outlook-shaping effects. In both cases, assume that further deliberation, expert advice, and waiting for new information do not resolve the impasse.

The prospectus should articulate a precise central question arising from this hypothesis, explain why familiar positions that appeal to incomparability, parity, or the creation of reasons by sheer commitment leave that question unresolved, outline a philosophical method for addressing it that does not depend on empirical fieldwork or formal modeling but shows how close analysis of the two cases plus consideration of objections can yield progress, describe a realistic phased plan of work feasible within nine months, clarify what a successful outcome would establish and what discoveries or arguments would constitute failure of the project, and indicate how the scope will be kept tractable for a thesis of approximately twenty thousand words. Please refer to relevant positions and arguments in general terms without providing citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, as those will be supplied separately.
