I am preparing a closing reading for an undergraduate seminar on practical reason and would like a Petrarchan sonnet that gives emotional weight to satisficing under bounded rationality. Center the poem on a school facilities manager who must select a new boiler from dozens of comparable models before winter, with limited time to compare specifications and arrange site visits. Let the octave enact the fantasy of full optimization, cataloging options and demanding the best on every dimension. Let the turn at line nine register the cost of continued search itself, in time, attention, and delay, and introduce a preset aspiration level as a rational response rather than a failure. Let the sestet resolve the choice by meeting that threshold and turning toward the work the heat makes possible. The poem should convey why a good-enough choice can be procedurally rational when deliberation is costly, without merely naming the theory or relying on technical jargon, and it should succeed as a sonnet in its own right.
