At one pivotal point the military team and her team disagree about whether a key heptapod message should be understood in threatening terms, as involving weapons and an offer to use them, or in cooperative terms, as involving tools to be shared or gifts to be accepted. Later the film suggests that becoming fluent in the alien language changes how Banks herself experiences time and choice.

In philosophy club we discussed Quine on radical translation and the invented field-linguist case of a native speaker uttering a new word when a rabbit runs by. As I understood it, the point was that pointing and assent behavior cannot by themselves settle whether the word refers to whole rabbits, to temporal stages of rabbits, to undetached parts of rabbits, or to something else entirely, and that more data of the same behavioral kind does not remove this principled openness. We also discussed the ideas that there can be multiple translation manuals that all fit the observable behavior but disagree with each other, and that Quine was skeptical of appealing to private meanings inside the head to settle the matter.

Could you read Arrival through that Quinean lens for me? I am especially puzzled about whether Banks methods of pointing, showing objects, repeating routines, and building up from concrete situations would look to Quine like progress toward a uniquely correct translation or like the collection of more behavior that still leaves reference open. I also want to know whether the film treats the weapon-like versus tool-like disagreement as mere temporary ambiguity that more context will resolve, and whether that treatment matches or misunderstands indeterminacy. Finally, does the films later idea that learning the alien writing reshapes thought illustrate something Quine would accept, complicate his behaviorism, or depend on a very different view of language and mind?
