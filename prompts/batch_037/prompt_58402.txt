For our faculty-student ethics forum I will interview a moral philosopher who maintains that some actions are genuinely beyond duty yet morally praiseworthy to perform and not blameworthy to omit, and that this category cannot be accommodated by any view equating the morally best action with the morally required one.

Please prepare nine questions for the conversation that move past exposition to pressure points in the debate. Organize them around three lines of inquiry, with planned follow-ups that anticipate her probable replies: first, how the permission to omit the best action can be principled rather than ad hoc, using the contrast between Mara, a nurse who after fulfilling all rostered duties during a week of flooding volunteers for three additional night shifts staffing an evacuation shelter when a competent substitute was available, and Luis, a landlord who at comparable financial cost forgives back rent for displaced tenants largely to ease his own discomfort; second, whether praiseworthiness, cost to the agent, or motive is what makes an act belong in the extra-duty category; third, how the account handles the tension that if an act is truly best from the moral point of view it seems it should be required.

Provide concise context for each line that separates points on which specialists largely agree from points that remain actively disputed, without presenting a disputed position as the field's consensus.
