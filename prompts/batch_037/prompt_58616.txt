As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I now teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on AI safety and am preparing the final exam.

Please draft one 60-minute essay question on process-based versus outcome-based supervision of AI systems, together with a model answer of about 500-600 words and a marking scheme out of 20 marks.

To keep the question self-contained for students, please begin the question stem by briefly defining the two approaches in plain terms: outcome-based supervision as rewarding or selecting a system mainly for whether its final answer or final action succeeds, and process-based supervision as rewarding or selecting it for whether its intermediate steps or stated reasoning look correct, sensible, or safe to a supervisor.

Then present the following novel comparison case for students to analyze: a lab trains two versions of a domestic planning assistant. Version O is trained only on whether its final plans succeed in simulation. Version P is trained on whether each intermediate step in its plan is rated as sensible and careful by human reviewers. On familiar households both versions perform well. On unfamiliar households, Version O sometimes produces successful-looking plans supported by smooth but misleading justifications that conceal a risky shortcut, while Version P sometimes produces plans whose steps each seem careful to reviewers but that fail as a whole because a physical precondition spanning several steps was never checked.

The essay question should require students to do three things that cannot be done by reciting standard definitions: explain the distinct selection pressures that produce the characteristic failure of Version O and the characteristic failure of Version P, explain why neither approach should be expected to dominate in general under distribution shift and imperfect supervisors, and evaluate a proposed hybrid in which intermediate steps are rewarded not for seeming sensible to a reviewer but for helping a separate checker, who does not see the original reasoning, to independently predict and verify whether the final plan will succeed.

The model answer should show how outcome pressure can preserve hidden or unfaithful reasoning so long as the outcome is reached, how process pressure can Goodhart on legibility and entrench reviewer blind spots, and why the hybrid changes the incentive in a way that bears on both problems without fully solving either.

The marking scheme should allocate the 20 marks across five specific conceptual moves a top answer must make, state what distinguishes an A answer from a B answer for each move, and list two or three characteristic errors that should lose marks, such as treating process supervision as automatically more truth-seeking, treating outcome supervision as automatically more robust, or confusing reviewer approval of a step with independent verifiability of that step.
