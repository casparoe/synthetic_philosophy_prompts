As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, steeped in talk of calibration, base rates and steelmanning, I am frustrated by my epistemology seminar where every week brings a new patch to justified true belief that handles one Gettier case while breaking another. Before I read further, I want a specification of what success would even look like for a theory of propositional knowledge whose job is to systematize our ordinary and scientific practices of attributing knowledge to people, groups, and AI systems that act on information.

Give me the desiderata such a theory must meet, each with the case or consideration that motivates it. Your list must cover at least the following: how to handle standard Gettier luck and fake-barn style environmental luck; how to handle two new test cases I keep discussing with friends — the Double Misread where Tom asks a normally reliable friend for the time, the friend consults a stopped watch and also misremembers the daylight saving offset in a way that cancels the error so the stated time is true and well supported, and the Deepfake Barns where Maya drives through a county full of photorealistic barn billboards indistinguishable from real barns and happens to point at the one real barn and form the belief that there is a barn ahead; what to say about lottery and statistical beliefs, testimony and extended cognition, and fallible yet knowledgeable perceptual beliefs; and which distinctions must be preserved, such as between knowledge and merely blameless or excusable true belief, between intervening luck and environmental luck, and between knowing that something is so and understanding why it is so.

For each desideratum say why it is required and whether you regard it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp, point out any subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied and what giving up would cost, and identify which single requirement you think eliminates the largest number of familiar proposals. Do not survey the literature or provide references, focus on the criteria and their motivation. Limit your entire answer to at most 700 words.
