I am a novelist finalizing a novel set in the fictional coastal republic of Veyra, and I require assistance in keeping its constitutional history philosophically coherent. After an uprising against an entrenched mayoral dynasty, Veyra adopted a mixed system intended to prevent the re-emergence of career politicians while preserving competent administration. Four foundational decisions are already fixed in my manuscript as canon:

1. Veyra replaced its elected senate with a legislative Council of sixty citizens chosen by lot, serving staggered three-year terms, with a ten-year period of ineligibility before any member may be selected again.
2. The founders barred anyone who has served in the lottery Council from standing for elected executive magistrate until six years have elapsed after such service.
3. The founders limited elected magistrates to two consecutive terms, but expressly permitted them to stand again after spending one full electoral cycle out of office.
4. The founders retained a permanent professional clerkship, appointed on merit and without term limits, to brief lottery members, maintain records, and advise on procedure, though clerks may not vote or introduce legislation.

The undecided fifth case, on which the plot turns, is a proposal to address complaints about inexperience in the lottery Council by creating a paid College of Former Councillors, chosen by vote among lottery alumni, serving renewable three-year terms with no limit on renewals, and empowered to return any Council bill once for reconsideration before it becomes law.

If the founders are to act consistently with their own prior decisions, what should they decide on this proposal. Reconstruct at least two general principles concerning sortition, rotation, term limits, and political professionalism that each fit all four prior decisions reasonably well but diverge on the College proposal. For each principle, state what verdict it predicts, whether outright approval, rejection, or conditional approval, and identify which of the four precedents is the closest analog under that reading. Give a best judgment as to which principle provides the more faithful interpretation of Veyra's existing commitments and what the founders would be newly committing themselves to under each possible outcome. Finally, describe one further hypothetical case, not yet in the novel, that would most cleanly distinguish between the two principles.
