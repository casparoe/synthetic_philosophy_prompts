I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background trying to understand how a friend uses the idea of supererogation, or going beyond duty. She treats donating a month's salary to earthquake relief as beyond duty and praiseworthy. She says the same about using two vacation weeks to clean up a failed deployment she did not cause and about dashing into traffic at serious personal risk to drag out a stranger. By contrast, she treats fixing a midnight outage when she is the designated on-call engineer as simply doing her duty even though it ruins her sleep. She also thinks keeping a weekend shift she promised to cover, rather than using that time to volunteer, is permissible and not a failure of duty. My puzzle concerns a work situation of mine: I built a small internal debugging tool on company time with permission to open-source it, and I can publish it with an afternoon of paperwork and almost no cost to me, which would save hundreds of outside developers real time and also look good on my record. No rule requires me to publish it. How do you expect my friend to classify my publishing decision, what are two different general rules that both fit her earlier verdicts but disagree here, what is your best-supported prediction with reasons, and what single further case would most clearly separate those rules?
