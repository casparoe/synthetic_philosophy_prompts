ok so I got roped into opening our joint phil mind / systems neuro seminar next thursday and I am already regretting it

im defending this Hofstadter-inspired line that a self isnt locked inside one skull, that when you die a real chunk of you keeps running in the brains of people who knew you really well, not just as a fond memory but as a literally distributed part of you that still does some of your work.

room is gonna be like eight analytic personal-identity people who live for fission and teletransporter cases and will nitpick constitution vs causation forever, plus three systems neuro folks who are friendly but think talk of selves outside a single brain is woo and want mechanisms, plus one token phenomenologist who cares about first-person feel. none of them are Hofstadter fans.

my basic arguement is roughly: selves are patterns of dispositions, memories, humor, decision style, not hunks of tissue; people who live together for decades build high-fidelity, generative models of each other that run automatically and even surprise themselves; so my wife finishing my sentences or vetoing a move in our improv duet the way I would is not just evidence of me, it is me still operating. I add this little test I made up to separate real survival from just remembering someone: fidelity plus autonomy, meaning the model has to be detailed enough to generate novel responses I would endorse, and it has to fire without deliberate recall, like it hijacks behavior. My star case is teh unfinished tune case: me and my jam partner have this half-written tune, if I die and she finishes it in a way I would have loved and that I couldnt have done better after amnesia, I want to say she finished it as partly me.

can you build me a Q and A survival sheet for these specific people? give me like seven objections I will actually hear from them, not generic dualism 101 stuff, in their own vocab, ranked by how likely and how much damage they do, each with a two minute comeback I can actually say out loud plus an honest flag for where that comeback is thin. also tell me what I should concede up front to look sane, what I absolutely have to hold to keep my thesis alive, and what is the one nightmare question I am praying nobody asks and why it sinks me.
