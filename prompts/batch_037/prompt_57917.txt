My brother and I have shared an apartment in Milwaukee for three years and we have to decide in the next two weeks whether to renew our lease for another year or switch to month-to-month while we watch the local housing market. We come from very similar backgrounds, we follow the same local news sources and city planning updates, and last Sunday we sat down for several hours and laid out everything we think we know, including the possible factory closure on the east side, the proposed transit extension, and a stack of recent rent listings and county housing figures, with each of us explaining step by step why we interpret that evidence the way we do.

Even after sharing all of that, we still disagree sharply and we both know it. I am roughly seventy percent confident that average rents in our neighborhood will rise substantially over the next twelve months, while he is only about thirty percent confident, and neither of us thinks the other is confused or missing information or bad with numbers. He has now proposed that we settle our confidence with a straightforward one hundred dollar wager on whether average two-bedroom rents on our block rise by more than five percent by next June according to the county data, arguing that if we really believe our own numbers we should both be happy to bet.

I feel uneasy about accepting even though I am the more confident one, because the fact that he is willing to bet against me seems itself like news I should take seriously, just as my willingness to bet should give him pause. Can you explain whether it makes sense for two people in our position to continue to disagree after fully sharing their reasons, whether it would be sensible for either of us to take the wager, and how we should adjust our views once we learn that the other person is eager to bet the other way? Please write your answer in American English.
