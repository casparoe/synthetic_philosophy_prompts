I am preparing a 1500-word prospectus for presentation at a departmental research colloquium in early spring, outlining a ten-week summer project intended to develop into a journal-length article in philosophy of medicine. I would be grateful for assistance in developing a preliminary idea into a complete draft suitable for an interdisciplinary committee that includes clinicians as well as philosophers.

My preliminary idea is that the dispute between naturalist accounts, which treat health and disease as matters of objective biological functioning, and normativist accounts, which treat them as involving human values and practical concerns, has stalled in part because familiar examples such as infection, fracture, or infertility align dysfunction and disvalue, so both sides can claim them. I suspect that progress requires sustained attention to stipulated cases where the two considerations pull apart under unfamiliar environmental or technological conditions, rather than further refinement of statistical or evolutionary definitions in the abstract.

To make the proposal concrete, please build it around the following three vignettes, taking their medical descriptions as given for purposes of argument: a crew member on multi-year microgravity deployment whose cardiovascular and musculoskeletal parameters have been pharmacologically reset to sustain performance aloft while falling outside Earth-based reference ranges; an adult with a rare genetic variant who sleeps two hours per night without reported cognitive cost but with a statistically associated reduction in lifespan; and a trauma patient maintained in reversible pharmacologically induced torpor with suspended consciousness and metabolism for several weeks as part of treatment.

Please draft the prospectus to include a precisely formulated central question arising from these vignettes, an explanation of why existing naturalist and normativist strategies leave that question open, a week-by-week plan of conceptual work showing why analysis of stipulated divergent cases is more illuminating than surveys of clinical usage or appeals to evolutionary history, a description of what a successful outcome would establish and what developments would show the project to have failed, and a candid appraisal of scope given the ten-week period. Please present positions and arguments in substantive terms without citations or references to specific publications, as I will supply those separately.
