I teach an upper-division seminar in metaphysics and the philosophy of mathematics, and we have just spent two sessions on supertasks, working carefully through Thomson's lamp and the familiar critical response associated with Benacerraf. For context in what follows, my presentation of the lamp involved a push-button lamp toggled at successively halved intervals over a two-minute interval, on after one minute, off after a further half minute, on after a further quarter minute, and so on, with Thomson's original contention being that a completed run would leave the lamp in a state that can be neither on nor off and hence that such a supertask cannot be completed, and with the Benacerraf-style contention being that the toggle instructions determine the lamp state only for times strictly before the two-minute limit and determine nothing about the state at or after the limit absent a further continuity or persistence assumption. My take-home midterm question asked students to (a) reconstruct Thomson's impossibility argument, (b) reconstruct the Benacerraf-style reply and say exactly which step of Thomson's argument it targets, and (c) apply both lines of thought to a variant I introduced in lecture in which each toggle also advances a bead one notch along a finite rack, so that the bead's final position would apparently record how many toggles occurred, and to say whether the variant restores a contradiction or remains similarly underdetermined.

One student submitted the following answer in full: Thomson shows the lamp after two minutes can be neither on nor off, since an odd number of toggles would leave it on and an even number would leave it off, but infinitely many toggles is neither odd nor even, so no consistent final state is available and supertasks of this kind are impossible. Benacerraf replies that the instructions specify the lamp only before the limit, so without assuming that the state after the limit must be the limit of earlier states, nothing follows about the lamp at two minutes, which dissolves the contradiction and shows that the supertask can after all be completed. The mathematics confirms this, since the on-off pattern corresponds to the Grandi series, whose sum is one half, so the lamp is neither determinately on nor off but in a well-defined intermediate mathematical state. The bead variant makes no relevant difference, since the bead position after two minutes is likewise simply unspecified by the instructions, so there is again no contradiction, only an explanatory gap about what a camera would see if we photographed the apparatus at the limit.
