As a backend software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, I have been puzzling over an ongoing lunch debate with a colleague who has strong and confident views about which systems count as genuinely intelligent. She treats as intelligent a self-taught game-playing program that reached superhuman play after training from scratch, a crow that bends wire into hooks to fish food out of a tube, and a large language model that can debug code it has never encountered before. She denies that description to a pocket calculator, to a hand-coded diagnostic aid containing thousands of rules written out by physicians that performs reliably in its clinic, to a sunflower tracking the sun across the sky, and to a basic thermostat holding a room at a set temperature.

At work my team is now evaluating a freight-routing optimizer for a single distribution network that was trained with reinforcement learning over several months, now beats our human planners there by a clear margin on fuel and time, yet fails when a depot is added or road closures change the map and has to be retrained almost from scratch, with its success seeming to rest on massive memorization of that one layout rather than any transferable trick. Given my colleague's earlier verdicts, how do you expect she would classify this optimizer, what are the two or three different underlying rules that would all fit those earlier verdicts, what does each rule predict for the optimizer, which prediction is the best guess about her and why, and what single further test case would most cleanly separate those candidate rules?
