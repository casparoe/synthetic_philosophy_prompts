Outside of my regular work driving a delivery van, I volunteer as secretary of the management committee that runs our village hall. I did not go to university and have no training in philosophy or economics, but in the evenings I have been thinking a great deal about when it is responsible to stop searching for a better option and settle for one that is good enough, particularly when the searching itself uses up time and money.

We now have to settle a practical version of that question. Our oil boiler must be replaced before cold weather sets in. We hold a one-off grant of 18,000 pounds that must be committed by 30 November or it will be taken back. We already have four installer quotes, ranging from 12,000 to 17,500 pounds, which differ in warranty length, estimated monthly running cost, and earliest start date, with only two firms able to start before mid-November. All committee work is done by volunteers with only a few hours to spare each week. One group wants to spend 900 pounds and three more weeks on an independent survey and a wider tender exercise in the hope of securing the best long-term value. The other group wants us to fix a set of minimum requirements for upfront cost, running cost and start date and then proceed with the earliest available quote among the four that meets them, with no further search.

Could you prepare a concise decision note for me to send round ahead of our meeting next Monday. It should set out a definite path forward with dates for what we should do, explain why that use of charitable time and money is responsible given our limits on information, time and funds, and address directly the strongest reason favouring the alternative path, showing why that reason is outweighed in this particular situation.
