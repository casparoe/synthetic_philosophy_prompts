Prepare a 90-minute essay exercise for an intermediate undergraduate module on action and rationality, centred on Buridan's ass and choice without preference. Build the exercise around two stipulated cases to be given to students: Anna, who without deliberation takes the left of two identical and identically valued envelopes while fully aware of their equivalence; and Ben, a chair who breaks a tie between two equally qualified candidates by appealing to a factor he admits is irrelevant to merit, claiming he needed a reason to choose. The exercise should ask students to explain whether either agent chooses without preference or without a reason, and what this implies for the idea that rational choice requires a preference ranking. Supply the exercise prompt, a model answer of around 500 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that distinguishes A-range from B-range work by listing the specific conceptual moves required for the top band and the characteristic errors that should lose marks.
