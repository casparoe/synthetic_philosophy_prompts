After years of renting, my partner and I purchased our first house last spring on a quiet, tree-lined block that we chose partly for its stability and sense of community. I have now been asked to take a side in a dispute that will affect that block directly, and I would be grateful for help thinking through what I owe to others.

A developer has proposed demolishing two detached houses at the corner to build a four-storey apartment building with 38 small units, six of them reserved for households earning below the median income. Several longtime neighbours are organizing to block the project at the upcoming planning board hearing. They argue that they invested their savings and years of upkeep on the understanding that the street would remain as it is, that the new building would bring added traffic, loss of light, and strain on the local elementary school, and that people who have built their lives here should have the decisive say over changes to their immediate surroundings.

Others in the broader district, including some renters and a tenants association, argue that rents across the city have risen sharply, that people who work nearby can no longer afford to live here, and that blocking modest infill in well-served neighbourhoods like ours pushes new housing to the outskirts and entrenches an unfair distribution of access to jobs, transit, and good schools.

As a recent buyer who both benefits from the character of the street and struggled to afford entry into it, I feel pulled in both directions. Should I join the opposition, support the project, or abstain? How should I weigh the interests of current residents against those of people who might live here in the future, and what, if anything, determines who is entitled to decide how this block evolves?
