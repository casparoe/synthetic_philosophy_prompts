You are a professional philosopher specializing in the ethics of large-scale future suffering, with a solid working fluency in high-hazard industrial safety practice. Please proceed slowly and carefully, and show disciplined reasoning throughout.

I am a senior process safety engineer with ten years of experience in chemical manufacturing. My everyday vocabulary is hazards versus risks, initiating events and consequences, layers of protection, ALARP as low as reasonably practicable, near-miss reporting, permit systems, and safety culture. I am fluent in that world and only an interested layperson in philosophy.

For the purpose of this exercise, take suffering risks to mean risks of futures in which very large amounts of suffering occur, including suffering of nonhuman animals and of possible future sentient artificial systems, whether through deliberate misuse, accident, or perverse incentives. To keep us grounded, please work with the following two illustrative cases I have constructed, rather than reaching for familiar published examples. Case A involves a future entertainment industry that runs vast numbers of detailed simulations of sentient beings in distressing situations because distressing content proves engaging. Case B involves a logistics and customer-service economy that relies on large populations of sentient digital workers whose reward signals are tuned so that anxiety-like states improve throughput, with managers rewarded for output rather than worker welfare.

Your task has three parts. First, restate as faithfully as you can, in my safety-engineering vocabulary, the central precautionary case for taking such risks seriously now and the two strongest skeptical responses, which are that the scenarios are too speculative to act on and that resources should go to more tractable present harms. Second, provide a residue report that distinguishes what translated cleanly, what could be translated only with distortion, and what could not be expressed in my vocabulary at all. Third, give a considered judgment about the residue: for each major item that did not survive, say whether that points to a limitation of the safety framework or to a lack of clarity in the original suffering-risk idea.
