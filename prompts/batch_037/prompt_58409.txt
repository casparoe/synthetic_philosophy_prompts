I serve on a university teaching committee that is debating whether to permit undergraduates to use generative AI to produce initial drafts of essays on the condition that they also complete regular closed-book syntheses and defend their submitted work orally. Several colleagues defend the proposal by appealing to aviation autopilot as a model. On their account, just as autopilot safely handles routine cruising while pilots retain underlying competence through separate simulator training and occasional manual takeovers, AI can safely handle routine drafting while students retain understanding through separate unassisted exercises and responsibility for checking and defending the output. They take this comparison to support a permissive policy with light-touch safeguards.

I find the comparison initially persuasive but suspect it may be doing more work than it should. Please audit how far the autopilot analogy holds for the specific question of epistemic dependence and the erosion of understanding.

Provide an explicit mapping of the analogy, in the form of a correspondence table or its equivalent, showing what in the aviation case is supposed to correspond to what in the case of AI-assisted study, including the agent, the automated system, the task being offloaded, the retained competence, and the safeguard. Then identify the principal points at which the mapping breaks down, distinguishing incidental disanalogies from those that matter for whether understanding is preserved.

Pay particular attention to what the autopilot image quietly assumes about the separability of routine performance from underlying competence, the transparency and inspectability of the automated process, the ease of detecting automation failure, and the possibility of reassuming manual control without loss. Assess whether the permissive conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table, or whether it depended on features of flying that academic work lacks. If another familiar form of automation offers a more illuminating comparison, explain briefly why.
