I would appreciate a systematic comparison between the unilateralist's curse and diffusion of responsibility as contrasting pathologies of groups in which any individual is in a position to act.

For present purposes, take the unilateralist's curse to refer to configurations where a collective faces a choice between acting and holding back, any single member can effectively settle the matter in favor of acting, and members proceed on the basis of uncertain private judgments, with the result that the collective ends up acting far more often than the pooled evidence would warrant. Take diffusion of responsibility to refer to configurations where anyone could usefully intervene, yet each person holds back on the assumption that another will step forward, with the result that the collective acts far less often than its members would endorse on reflection.

Please analyze how these two mechanisms differ with respect to their underlying drivers, the role of uncertainty and of individual thresholds for action, the effects of group size and of correlation among private judgments, and the kinds of institutional remedies each calls for. In particular, explain why structurally similar conditions of decentralized initiative can produce over-action in one setting and under-action in another, and what principled test distinguishes the two when both risks are present.

To ground the comparison, consider three variations involving the same small research team:

First, five collaborators share responsibility for a long-running computational experiment. Any one of them, on night duty, may halt the run if private monitoring data suggests a safety problem. Halting is costly if the suspicion is mistaken, while failing to halt is costly if the suspicion is correct, and each person sees only noisy indicators.

Second, the same five collaborators notice signs that a junior colleague is struggling seriously. Any one of them could check in and offer substantial help at modest personal cost, and all agree that someone should do so.

Third, a community of volunteers maintains a widely used software library. Any maintainer may unilaterally ship an emergency patch for a suspected vulnerability. Shipping quickly limits exposure but risks breaking dependent systems if the diagnosis is mistaken, while waiting for concurrence reverses those risks.

For each variation, assess which dynamic predominates, how a design choice such as requiring concurrence, appointing a rotating duty officer, or raising or lowering the threshold for initiative would mitigate one risk without intensifying the other, and what general lesson follows about structuring unilateral power to act on behalf of a group.
