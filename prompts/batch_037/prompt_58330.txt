On the standard interpretation of Kant's ethics, only beings capable of rational autonomy possess dignity and must be treated as ends in themselves. Animals, on this reading, lack such autonomy and may be used as means, but human agents have indirect duties with regard to them: acts of wanton cruelty are morally condemned because they erode humane dispositions and thereby injure humanity in the agent's own person, whereas considerate conduct, including gratitude toward animals that have provided prolonged service, is enjoined as a way of cultivating dispositions that support direct duties to persons.

Consider two recent technological developments that raise questions within this framework but postdate Kant by more than a century. First, several firms now produce beef and dairy proteins using immortalized bovine cell lines established from a single biopsy taken decades ago, so that no sentient animal is reared, confined, or slaughtered in ongoing production, though living animal bodies remain the ultimate historical source and advertising emphasizes liberation from dependence on animals. Second, care facilities increasingly deploy highly lifelike robotic companions that mimic canine attachment and pain behavior with realistic vocalizations and withdrawal, and staff report that some children and visitors kick, strike, or shout at the devices when frustrated, while others develop strong protective attachments to them.

Articulate the position Kant would most plausibly have adopted toward each development if confronted with it in its present form. Your account should reconstruct how his distinction between direct duties to persons and indirect duties with regard to animals, his explanation of why cruelty and ingratitude toward animals matter morally, and his broader concern with the cultivation of moral character would apply to production and consumption of cell-derived animal products and to abusive versus affectionate treatment of animal-like robots. Distinguish conclusions that follow relatively directly from commitments Kant actually held from points on which extension or speculation is required, and address at least one plausible objection that Kant's framework either condemns too little in the first case or condemns too much in the second.
