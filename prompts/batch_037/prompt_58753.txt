Please help me plan a 75-minute session for an undergraduate seminar on literature and philosophy with 18 students. I want a role-play and structured deliberation that lets participants feel from the inside the argument associated with the Grand Inquisitor episode: that freedom of conscience is experienced by most people as a painful burden of choice, guilt and uncertainty, and that they will willingly surrender it to an authority that guarantees material security, social harmony and relief from responsibility, leaving only a troubled few to retain freedom at the cost of isolation.

Please build the activity around the following invented case, and do not introduce the literary source or name the argument until the debrief. Harbor House is a residential college responding to rising anxiety and dropout. It proposes an Opt-In Compact for second-year students. Those who join agree for one year to follow an individual plan set by a trusted advising office covering course selection, sleep hours, device use, and career preparation, and to accept its resolution of roommate and social conflicts. In return they receive priority housing, guaranteed tutoring, supported placement for internships, and public normalization of their choices so they do not have to defend them to peers or family. Those who decline keep full self-direction but receive only standard resources and must live with open-ended risk, comparison and possible failure.

Design a runnable session in which students first have to make a series of difficult, underspecified personal choices under time pressure and mild social exposure, then are offered the Compact first as an individual opt-in and then as a collective rule they can vote to extend to others, including to dissenters. Provide a minute-by-minute facilitation plan, the exact instructions and handouts or cards to give each role or group, how votes and opt-ins are recorded and what small in-class stakes or privileges attach to them, and how to stage the moment when continued freedom for a minority comes to look like a threat to the happiness of the majority.

Also provide a debrief guide that maps each phase of what happened onto elements of the Inquisitor reasoning about bread versus freedom, the appeal of an authority that removes responsibility, the resentment toward those who remain free, and why a silent, non-refuting presence can still count as a response. Include what will predictably go wrong on a first run and how to handle it, and be candid about which aspect of the episode the exercise cannot reproduce and must be supplied by discussion.
