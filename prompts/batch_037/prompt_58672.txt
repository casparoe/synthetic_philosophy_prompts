Consider the proposition that at least one form of life elsewhere in the Milky Way arose independently from terrestrial life and remains extant today. For this purpose, count as life any planetary chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution, even without water or nucleic acids. State a numerical credence for the proposition and support it with separate estimates for whether life so understood admits chemically different realizations, how likely abiogenesis is where conditions permit, how often such conditions have occurred in the galaxy, and how likely such life is to persist to the present. Explain how these component judgments combine into the overall figure, indicate where most of the uncertainty lies, and identify one feasible near-future observation that would alter the overall figure most substantially.
