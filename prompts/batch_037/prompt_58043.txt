ok this has been bugging me since my philosophy of mind class. I buy the basic Mary setup — she knows all the physical stuff about color in her black and white room, then she walks out, sees red, and realy learns what its like. But my roommate says that reasoning proves too much:

(1) Mary inside knows every physical fact about color vision.
(2) When she first sees red she learns something new.
(3) What she learns is a new fact, since afterward she can answer new questions and make new inferences about experiences.
(4) So there is a fact about seeing red that is not a physical fact.
(5) But the same steps work for any experience she hasn't had yet — tasting vegemite, hearing a ukulele chord, sneezing with eyes open, whatever.
(6) So for every untried experience there is a nonphysical fact about what it is like.
(7) So someone who knows all the physical facts is still ignorant about millions of totally ordinary facts, and physics misses almost everything.

That last step seems crazy — physicalism was supposed to be a serious view, not something you can refute with vegemite. Which step should I reject and what does rejecting it cost? I want to keep the idea that Mary learns something if I can.
