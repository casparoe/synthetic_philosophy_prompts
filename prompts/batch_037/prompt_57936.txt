Please compose a single take-home exercise on evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics for third-year philosophy undergraduates, answerable in about 500 words for 20 marks. Base the exercise on the following invented case: two isolated forager bands evolved opposing intuitions about whether food must be shared equally with non-kin, each intuition having clearly promoted survival in its local ecology, while members of both bands regard their own intuition as tracking an objective moral truth. The task should require students to reconstruct how the debunker uses such contingency to challenge reliability, and to evaluate whether appeal to the survival value of cooperation rescues reliability or merely restates the evolutionary explanation. Include a model answer of around 400 words and a marking scheme that distributes the 20 marks across the essential analytical steps and specifies which omissions or conflations should forfeit marks.
