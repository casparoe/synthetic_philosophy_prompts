And here's one student's answer I need to mark:

The money pump shows Mara is irrational because if she chooses myopically at each step she will accept each trade. She starts with Cedar, pays £2 to get Beech which she prefers, pays £2 to get Ash which she prefers to Beech, then pays £2 to get back to Cedar which she prefers to Ash. So she ends up exactly where she started minus £6, which is a sure loss she could have avoided by doing nothing. Any agent with a cycle can be pumped like this. But the foresight defence says this assumes Mara is naive. If she is sophisticated and does backwards induction from Friday, she will see on Monday that accepting starts a sequence that leaves her worse off, so she will refuse the first trade. That means she is not actually pumpable, so having cyclic preferences is rationally fine after all, her refusal on Monday just shows she did not really prefer Beech to Cedar when the whole sequence was on offer. On diachronic rationality, this shows rationality is just a matter of sticking to your earlier plan. A rational agent has to carry through her Monday plan on Friday even if how she feels about the flats has shifted midweek, which is resolute choice. So if Mara's tastes genuinely change midweek she counts as irrational.
