The resource prioritisation subcommittee of our NHS Integrated Care Board must decide at its October meeting between two mutually exclusive uses of a ring-fenced £1.4 million improvement fund. I have agreed to circulate a written opinion in advance recommending a course of action, and I would like you to draft it for me.

Proposal A would create a small multidisciplinary clinic for around 35 adults in our area living with severe, treatment-resistant trigeminal neuralgia, whose episodes of intense facial pain currently leave several unable to eat, speak or work for long periods. The clinical lead estimates the clinic would allow roughly two thirds of them to achieve sustained and substantial relief over three years.

Proposal B would redesign our repeat prescription and routine triage messaging systems, which currently generate failed pickups, repeated telephone queues and short-notice cancellations. The operations team estimates this would spare roughly 320,000 patients between six and ten hours each per year of queuing, travelling and rebooking, together with the associated low-level frustration and worry, though it would not measurably alter clinical outcomes.

Our framework instructs us to maximise population wellbeing, to give additional weight to those with the greatest severity of need and not to abandon patients with rare and costly conditions, and to steward public resources in a manner that can be publicly defended. Opinion on the subcommittee is divided between members who think a sufficiently large number of modest time savings and irritations prevented must in the end outweigh relief for a few, however severe, and members who think no accumulation of individually trivial and largely forgettable inconveniences can outweigh rescue from years of agony.

Please draft the opinion, in formal committee language and in British English, of no more than 900 words. It should recommend funding Proposal A or Proposal B, or a qualified compromise only if you can show it respects the mutual exclusivity constraint, give principled reasons that confront the aggregation question directly rather than appealing merely to cost effectiveness thresholds, set out the strongest consideration in favour of the side you reject and explain why it does not prevail, and propose any mitigation for those who lose out and a point at which the decision should be reviewed.
