I am seeking assistance in making explicit the principle that best systematizes my judgments regarding the regulation of private firearm ownership.

I find myself committed to the following assessments, which I state in abbreviated form but which concern otherwise ordinary adults without histories of violence or severe mental instability. First, I regard a prohibition on private possession of weapons of war such as machine guns, grenades, and rocket launchers as justified. Second, I regard as justified a licensing regime that conditions handgun purchase on a background check for violent crime and domestic abuse together with completion of a basic safety and proficiency course. Third, I regard as justified a legal requirement that any firearm not under immediate bodily control be kept locked, even though this introduces delay in defensive access. Fourth, I regard as unjustified a categorical ban on handgun possession within a large city, even as applied to trained adults who would keep the weapon at home for self-defense. Fifth, I am inclined to regard as justified a limit of ten rounds for detachable magazines, applicable to both handguns and rifles kept for lawful purposes. Sixth, I am inclined to regard as unjustified a requirement to maintain liability insurance as a condition of lawful ownership, although I recognize that this sits uneasily with my third and fifth assessments. Seventh, I regard as justified a prohibition on openly carrying loaded rifles in dense commercial districts, while still permitting ownership and transport of such rifles unloaded and locked.

My tentative organizing thought has been that regulation is legitimate when it aims at ensuring competent and responsible exercise by individual owners, but not when it aims at reducing aggregate social risk by burdening competent and responsible owners. I suspect, however, that this formulation cannot accommodate all of the above, particularly the contrast between the magazine limit and the insurance requirement, and the contrast between the safe-storage requirement and opposition to the urban handgun ban.

I would be grateful for a precise statement of the principle or small set of competing principles that best captures this pattern. For each clause of any proposed principle, please identify which of my assessments motivates it. Please also state what the proposed principle or principles would imply for two proposals I have not yet considered: a mandate that all newly sold handguns incorporate owner-recognition technology that substantially increases purchase price and introduces a small but nonzero risk of failure in an emergency, and a municipal ordinance requiring urban residents to store their firearms overnight at a licensed club facility rather than at home. Finally, please state candidly which of my assessments must be abandoned under the most defensible systematization, and whether preserving all of them would require a degree of complexity that undermines the principle itself.
