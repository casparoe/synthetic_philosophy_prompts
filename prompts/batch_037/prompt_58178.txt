Our college president stuck me on a legacy review group that has to give her a reccomendation by early October, and I'm kinda stuck. Some students digging in the archives found that back in 1969 the college sold a big parcel it owned in the historically Black neighborhood next to campus to a developer, the developer cleared several blocks for apartments for students that were never built, and the college used the proceeds to help build our current library. A descendants group wants a formal apology read aloud at Founders Day plus a permanent exhibit in the library lobby explaining what happened, while a couple trustees would rather do a new first-gen scholarship and a plaque about community partnership without ever saying the college did anything wrong, partly because nobody currently here was around then and partly because they think an apology without cash is cheap and an apology with cash looks like buying forgiveness. I was arguing about it with friends and we kept going in circles about whether a college can even apologize for something a totally different set of people decided, and whether saying sorry now does anything if we don't also do something material.

Could you draft the two-page memo I could actually circulate to the group, that picks one concrete path for Founders Day and the year after and explains why it is the right call? I need it to not just split the difference, to lay out the practical steps and who does what, and to take the strongest objection to your reccomendation seriously instead of handwaving it — like if you say apologize, deal head-on with the nobody-here-did-it and cheap-talk worries, and if you say don't apologize, deal head-on with why acknowledgement plus money without owning it might feel like a dodge to the descendants.
