Please develop a diagnostic sorting quiz for an interdisciplinary undergraduate seminar on the nature of intelligence, intended for students in philosophy, psychology, and computer science who have no prior allegiance to a particular theory.

The deliverable should contain nine forced-choice items, each centered on a concrete vignette rather than on abstract terminology, so that respondents are sorted by what they judge about cases rather than by which label they find attractive. No item should ask directly whether the respondent believes in general intelligence or modular intelligence or any other named view. Each item should offer two or three response options that express substantive judgments a non-specialist can understand.

Include at least the following vignettes, in any order: an octopus that solves a novel mechanical puzzle to obtain food while showing no language-like communication; a contemporary large language model that produces fluent legal analysis while failing at elementary physical manipulation; an ant colony that reliably relocates its nest after flooding through decentralized interactions with no central controller; a person with extraordinary rote memory who struggles to generalize, plan, or explain; and a small delivery robot that navigates unfamiliar buildings robustly but fails when priority depends on unwritten social norms. The remaining items may introduce further cases, but at least two should probe whether intelligence requires consciousness, understanding, or flexible goals, and at least one should probe whether intelligence can belong to a group rather than an individual.

Provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto the following five candidate positions, characterized functionally rather than by author or school: a unitary view on which intelligence is substantially one general capacity manifest across domains; a pluralist view on which there are several largely independent capacities with no meaningful single score; an adaptive view on which intelligence is centrally success at achieving goals within a particular environment; an embodied and embedded view on which intelligence cannot be correctly attributed to an isolated brain or program apart from body and situation; and a deflationary view on which intelligence is not a natural kind but a folk evaluative term tracking what a community happens to prize.

Construct the key from the logical space first and the names second: show which combinations of answers lead to each position, identify at least one combination that no position occupies and explain briefly whether that combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored, and explicitly flag any pair of positions your items cannot reliably separate rather than papering over the overlap.
