Please provide a systematic translation of the philosophical debate over affirmative action and quotas into the conceptual apparatus of algorithmic fairness and constrained optimization, followed by an assessment of what that translation fails to preserve.

I write as a machine learning engineer with several years of experience developing and auditing models for hiring and lending. My working vocabulary centers on objective functions, hard and soft constraints, accuracy trade-offs, and quantitative fairness measures such as demographic parity, equalized odds, and calibration, along with the practical difficulties of satisfying such constraints on shifting data distributions. Please use that apparatus as the target language and assume full fluency in it.

To keep the exercise self-contained, use the following three stylized institutional designs as the source material rather than relying on detailed historical knowledge. First, holistic university admissions in the United States in which race has been treated as one favorable factor among many in pursuit of a diverse student body and in response to a history of exclusion. Second, the Indian reservation system in which a fixed share of places in higher education and public employment is set aside for historically disadvantaged caste and tribal communities. Third, a Norwegian-style corporate board rule in which listed firms are required to fill a mandated minimum share of director positions with members of each gender in order to correct durable underrepresentation at the top.

Within that target vocabulary, reconstruct three central justifications for such policies and three central objections to them. The justifications are the idea that quotas compensate for cumulative historic wrongs, the idea that a mixed cohort produces educational, epistemic, and decision-making benefits that would not otherwise obtain, and the idea that visible inclusion reshapes expectations and widens the future pipeline. The objections are the idea that placing beneficiaries in highly selective environments for which they are less prepared may worsen their own academic or professional outcomes, the idea that preferential treatment demeans its beneficiaries by expressing doubt about their competence and by creating stigma, and the idea that any explicit use of race, caste, or gender violates a requirement of procedurally neutral treatment.

After the translation, evaluate its limits under three headings: claims that carry over without distortion, claims that can be rendered only by stretching or narrowing the fairness vocabulary, and claims for which that vocabulary has no adequate expression. Throughout, do not import the normative assumptions of optimization practice and present them as if they were already present in the original debate. Conclude by judging, for each major item that resists translation, whether the difficulty reveals a blind spot in the algorithmic fairness framework or suggests that the original philosophical claim was less determinate than it appeared.

Please write your answer in American English.
