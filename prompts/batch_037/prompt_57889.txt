As curator of a construction materials library serving practising architects, structural engineers and contractors, I maintain several thousand physical samples together with specification sheets, performance test results, provenance records and reuse guidance that colleagues rely upon when selecting substances for live projects.

I am professionally fluent in decisions about how to group samples by composition, manufacturing route, measured performance and sourcing history, how to revise groupings when new blends appear, and how to balance stability of categories against responsiveness to practical needs such as carbon accounting, safety certification and salvage.

I would be grateful if you could recast the philosophical dispute concerning whether the sciences discover divisions that exist independently of human interests into the working vocabulary of a materials library of this kind. For orientation, please work with three broad stances: first, that mature scientific classifications succeed because they latch onto interest-independent divisions in the world; second, that classifications are instruments shaped entirely by our purposes with no such divisions to answer to; third, that there are many overlapping divisions that constrain inquiry without being independent of purpose.

To keep the exercise concrete, please apply each stance to two cases from my shelves. One concerns low-carbon concretes where mixes with quite different chemistries satisfy the same strength and embodied-carbon thresholds yet diverge sharply in long-term durability in marine exposure. The other concerns reclaimed timbers where species identity predicts less about structural suitability than does prior use, weathering and fastener damage. Explain how a holder of each stance would advise me to organise, split or merge entries in these two cases.

Conclude with an audit of remainder: identify which philosophical claims carry over into library practice without strain, which carry over only by stretching library notions, and which have no analogue in decisions about samples and specification. Offer a reasoned view on whether any remainder reflects a poverty in the library vocabulary or a looseness in the original philosophical formulation. Please compose the entire response in British English and in a formal register suitable for professional circulation.
