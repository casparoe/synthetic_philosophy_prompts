I am seeking assistance in clarifying my thinking about why promises bind, and I would be grateful for your help in identifying the principle that best fits my judgments about particular cases.

I find myself committed to the following verdicts, which I describe as I experience them. I promised a friend that I would collect her from the airport on a late flight, and when traffic made it highly inconvenient I took myself to be still bound, such that I owed her performance or at least an explicit release and an apology, and mere regret or inconvenience did not lessen the obligation. By contrast, when at a charity dinner I was singled out by the organizer and said I would donate a thousand dollars, largely to avoid embarrassment in front of the table, I judge on reflection that I am not bound in anything like the same way, or am bound only weakly, even though the words I used were promissory and others relied on them. I also promised my late mother that I would see my younger brother through his final year of university, a promise my brother does not know about, and since her death I have felt myself to be fully bound, if anything more strictly than before, although there is now no one who could release me. Yet when I promised a colleague that I would not mention that she was interviewing elsewhere, and she then announced the search herself in a staff meeting, I took the obligation to have lapsed entirely without any need for a formal release. Similarly, when a close friend asked me to delete some personal photographs she had sent me during a difficult period and I promised I would, but she later told me to forget it and keep them, I took myself to be no longer bound to delete them even though I protested at the time that I had promised, so her withdrawal seemed to dissolve what my insistence could not preserve. Finally, when I solemnly resolve to myself that I will exercise every morning, I feel disappointment or guilt when I fail, but I do not feel that I have wronged myself in the way I would have wronged another promisee.

My tentative thought is that a promise binds when the promisee has invited reliance on my word in a way that serves an interest she retains, but I am troubled by the tension between the third case on the one hand and the fourth and fifth on the other, since the former suggests the obligation can survive the disappearance of the promisee while the latter suggest it cannot survive the disappearance of her interest, and I am also unsure how that thought would distinguish the first two cases without making social pressure alone defeat obligation. I would be grateful if you would propose the principle, or the small set of competing principles, that comes closest to capturing these verdicts, stating each candidate precisely and explaining which feature of which case motivates each of its clauses, saying what it predicts for a further case I have not listed, namely a promise to a small child to bring a particular gift next weekend when the child has plainly forgotten the promise by the next day, and giving an honest assessment of which of my verdicts must be abandoned if simplicity is to be preserved. Please reply in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a personal letter, and please do not include a bibliography or citations, as I will supply those myself if needed.
