My background is in backend software engineering and I have no formal training in philosophy. In my current role I maintain an automation rules engine that must impose sharp cutoffs on matters that seem gradual, and informal discussion of vagueness and the sorites paradox suggests our current practice of picking a round number and moving on is conceptually fragile.

I would appreciate a reusable, practice-oriented method that translates that philosophical discussion into something my team can apply without philosophical expertise. The method should take the form of an ordered sequence of questions or checks, stated as a flowchart in words. For each step, please specify what information an engineer needs to gather in order to proceed, what would justify moving forward rather than revising an earlier choice, and where the method requires stopping automation and deferring to product, legal, or human review because the issue is one of values or risk tolerance rather than fact.

The method must be usable under the following constraints: it will be applied by six backend engineers with no philosophy background, it should require no more than about thirty minutes to run for a new predicate, its application must leave a brief written record that can be audited later, and any resulting threshold must be explainable to a product manager who gives final sign-off.

Please illustrate the method by applying it to three classification problems currently before my team:

1. Flagging API clients for excessive use. Ten requests per minute is plainly routine and ten thousand per minute plainly threatens stability, but product wants a warning threshold somewhere in between with a non-arbitrary rationale, plus a plan for clients just above and below the line.

2. Archiving accounts deemed inactive. An account unused for twelve months is a reasonable candidate for archival, while an account unused for one day is plainly still active. We must select notification and archival thresholds and decide how to handle users in the intermediate range.

3. Automatically rejecting uploaded product images deemed low resolution or blurry based on a continuous sharpness score from 0 to 1000. The team hopes for full automation here, with no human review.

For each case, show the reasoning the method produces and the concrete implementation it recommends, including monitoring or logging of borderline cases. Include at least one case where the method recommends something my team will find unwelcome, such as preserving a buffer zone or human review where we wanted a clean automated cutoff. Conclude by stating clearly what the method cannot settle and where it is most likely to misfire in production.
