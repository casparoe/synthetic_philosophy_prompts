As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I am developing a long-form feature on what rigid, loop-like behavior can and cannot tell us about mindedness, and for background I have spent the past few months talking with two sources who respect each other but have started to argue past one another: a skeptic about machine minds who treats sphexish inflexibility as diagnostic of an absence of understanding and free agency, and a gradualist about agency who treats flexibility as graded, ecological, and dependent on the task an agent was tuned to solve. By sphexish I mean the familiar field pattern in which a digger wasp drags prey to the burrow entrance, leaves it to inspect the burrow inside, and when the prey is displaced a few centimeters during the inspection restarts the entire bring-and-inspect sequence again and again without varying its approach.

To keep their exchange concrete and to move it away from rehearsed talking points, I have asked both of them to react to the same two cases that will anchor my piece, and I would like you to draft the text of the adversarial-collaboration statement they could jointly sign afterward. The first case is the wasp pattern described above. The second is a composite scenario I devised for the article: a clinic delivery robot that, when its docking bay is blocked by a cleaning cart, repeatedly announces arrival, backs up, and re-approaches from the same angle for many cycles, yet succeeds promptly when a nurse shifts the cart by half a meter or waves it to an adjacent bay, alongside a conversational assistant that handles extensive paraphrases of a multi-step scheduling request with ease but falls into verbatim repetition of an earlier step when the user quietly changes one constraint mid-dialogue and then points out the conflict.

Please write the collaboration document as if for publication alongside my feature, in a voice formal enough that both parties would be willing to put their names to it. It should open with a numbered list of substantive claims both sides accept about rigidity, interruptibility, sensitivity to novelty, and what would count as evidence of understanding or agency, then present a table of residual disagreements in which each position is stated in wording its own holder would endorse without slant or dilution. From there it should identify the cruxes, meaning the narrower subquestions about learning from failure, representation of goals, or counterfactual responsiveness such that settling them would move at least one side, and for each crux specify a concrete comparison, intervention, or failure-case analysis that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side’s advance prediction recorded. Close by distinguishing disagreements that are genuinely substantive from any that turn out to be matters of words, explaining briefly why the latter do not move the underlying issue.
