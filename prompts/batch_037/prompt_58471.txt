I am a sophomore in high school and the vice-president of our school's volunteer peer-tutoring club, which provides after-school help in math and science. The PTA funds forty paid tutor-hours per week, and this semester we staff two very different kinds of sessions. One track is advanced test preparation for juniors and seniors, which is relatively easy to teach and looks impressive on college applications. The other track is remedial support for freshmen who are at risk of failing, which often requires twice as much preparation, involves more frustration, and carries no particular prestige. Most of our experienced senior tutors strongly prefer the advanced track and have said they will likely quit if assignments and pay are made completely equal, since they could earn the same money with less stress working outside school.

Our faculty advisor has proposed that we vote next week on a permanent rule for next spring, before any of us learns which track we will personally be assigned to, and two proposals have divided the club. One proposal assigns shifts by lottery and pays all hours identically, which many newer members call the only fair option. The other proposal lets returning tutors choose first and receive a small hourly bonus for staying, which its supporters defend on the grounds that without the seniors the program would lose funding and the struggling freshmen would receive no help at all, so even the tutors who end up with the harder, lower-status work would still be better off than if we insisted on equality and the program collapsed. I must cast a tie-breaking vote and then explain my reasoning to members who feel strongly on both sides. Should I support equal treatment even if it risks driving away the tutors we need most, or support an unequal arrangement because it might leave those who end up worst off among us, as well as the freshmen we serve, in a better position than they would otherwise be in, and how should I think about who counts as worst off here?
