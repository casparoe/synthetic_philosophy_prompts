Earlier this year I joined a volunteer sponsorship circle in my town that raises funds, secures housing, and provides a year of practical support for one displaced family at a time, and the experience has left me puzzled by the assumptions built into our own procedures.

Our circle follows two unwritten rules that we apply without much discussion. First, on eligibility: we will sponsor a family that has left its country to escape targeted political or religious persecution, but not a family in identical material destitution that remains displaced inside its own country by the same conflict, nor a family that has left its country because rising seas and ruined soil made farming and daily life impossible. For us, severe need alone does not seem sufficient, and movement alone does not seem sufficient; the combination of persecution and international border crossing does. Second, on allocation of responsibility: we describe our own reason to act as arising because we volunteered and happen to have spare housing, school places, and time, not because we caused the displacement or are geographically closest to it. At the same time, in town meetings we insist that the national government must admit its assigned quota of resettled people even where no local community has volunteered, on the grounds that otherwise burdens fall unfairly on frontline states and on willing towns like ours.

I can see that we behave as if certain distinctions carry moral weight, but I cannot tell which distinctions our practice actually depends on, which it merely sits comfortably with, and whether our eligibility rule and our responsibility rule belong to a single coherent outlook.

Could you lay out the understandings of belonging, need, and obligation that would make sense of what we do, distinguish what our practice logically needs from what it is merely consistent with, note where our practice leaves the underlying view open between rivals, and explain what would have to change in eligibility or in assignment of duties if we moved to a purely need-based or purely capacity-based outlook instead.

Please structure your response in the following four sections in order, keeping roughly to the suggested lengths:

1. Eligibility Logic, about 300 words: articulate the conception of who counts as deserving of resettlement through sponsorship that best renders our three-way distinction intelligible, and consider whether border crossing, persecution, or some further idea does the central work.

2. Responsibility Logic, about 300 words: articulate the conception of who must take people in that best renders our combination of volunteer-based local duty and quota-based national duty intelligible, and consider how voluntariness, capacity, proximity, causation, and fairness among hosts figure in it.

3. Required Versus Merely Compatible, about 250 words: for each rule, separate what must be true for the rule to be justified from other views that could also accommodate it, and identify points where our practice is philosophically underdetermined.

4. Coherence and What Revision Would Entail, about 250 words: assess whether the two rules hang together or pull in different directions, and describe concretely how our sponsorship criteria and our claims about government quotas would have to be revised if we adopted a consistent need-centered or capacity-centered alternative.
