I am putting together a seminar session on modality and have written up my own position on origin essentialism to invite critical scrutiny. Please audit it for internal consistency:

(1) Had the particular sperm and egg from which I developed never united, I would not have existed; sameness of gametic origin is necessary for my existence.
(2) I could still have been me if conceived a year later to the same parents from a different sperm and egg, so long as family environment and developmental continuity were largely preserved.
(3) Had my actual zygote divided into monozygotic twins, neither twin would determinately be me, though both would share my exact gametic origin; such origins are therefore necessary but not sufficient for identity.
(4) Had my actual zygote been gestated by a different surrogate with no genetic difference, the resulting child would still have been me; gestational history is contingent.
(5) Ordinary modal intuitions about cases of this sort carry defeasible evidential weight for metaphysical possibility.

Which pairs are in genuine tension and which are only apparently so, ranked by severity? For each genuine tension, specify what would have to give and at what cost, and identify which single revision would clean up the most. Conclude by stating your overall confidence in the audit and singling out the point on which you are least sure.
