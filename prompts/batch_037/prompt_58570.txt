I've been going back and forth with my cousin who works for our county water utility and now my head hurts. He says autonomous systems create a special moral problem, and he laid it out like this and I can't see how to avoid the ending:

1. Someone is morally responsible for a harmful outcome only if they could have foreseen that specific outcome ahead of time and could have prevented it if they had tried.
2. Take our pressure-balancing system. Vendor built it to keep learning overnight from sensor data and rewrite its own thresholds, operators just watch an aggregate dashboard and the actual valve decisions happen in milliseconds. Last winter it did this weird sequence nobody predicted, burst a pipe, flooded a basement apartment and someone died. No human foresaw that exact sequence and no human could have jumped in to stop it.
3. So by 1, neither the vendor nor the operator nor the county is morally responsible for the death. Thats a true gap, harm with no one answerable, and the AI itself obviously isnt morally responsible.
4. But that same lack of exact foresight and split-second control is everywhere in normal human delegation too.
5. Like you adopt a trained rescue dog and out of nowhere it knocks a toddler down the stairs, or you hire a licensed electrician who improvises on the job and burns your house down. You couldnt have foreseen the exact move and you werent there to tackle them in time.
6. In those dog and electrician cases we do hold the owner or the hirer responsible at least sometimes, for taking on the risk or hiring sloppily or not supervising, and that practice seems totally fine and I dont want to give it up.
7. So by the logic of 1-3, we should also say no one is responsible in the dog and electrician cases either. That seems crazy and unfair to victims.

I really want to keep both ideas, that the water AI case is special with a real gap, and that we are right to hold people responsible in the normal human cases. Which premise or step should I reject, why did it look so plausible, and what does it cost me to give it up? If I loosen 1, what do I replace it with that doesnt make vendors responsible for everything forever?

Please lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning after, with no preamble and no restating the question.
