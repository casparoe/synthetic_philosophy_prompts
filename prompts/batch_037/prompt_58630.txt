Design a 75-minute classroom game on superrational cooperation for 24 undergraduates attending an interdisciplinary rationality workshop, most with no background in game theory. The aim is for participants to feel the force of the following thought in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with cooperate-cooperate yielding 3 points each, defect-defect yielding 1 point each, and unilateral defection yielding 5 points against 0: if the other player can be expected to reason exactly as I do, cooperating can be rational even though defection dominates. Arrange rules and a pairing procedure so that participants first experience the pull to defect and then make a second choice under conditions that make sameness of reasoning salient, without introducing the theory by name beforehand. Include concrete rules, materials, scoring without money, and a timeline, followed by a debrief that maps key moments onto the contrast between causal dominance reasoning and superrational reasoning, anticipates what will likely go wrong on a first run and how to manage it, and acknowledges which element of superrationality the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by discussion.
