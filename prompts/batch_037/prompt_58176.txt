I am a Year 12 student taking A-level History in England, and I have written a short draft essay for coursework on whether asking who started the Cold War is the right question. I would be grateful for detailed comments on the argument itself, not on sources or further reading.

My teacher said my work summarises the orthodox, revisionist and post-revisionist positions well but does not add much, so I tried to reframe the question. My draft argues as follows.

First, I suggest that started is ambiguous because it can mean three different things: who made the first unfriendly move after the Second World War, who had the more expansionist aims in Europe, and who did most to close off a peaceful settlement that might otherwise have been available. I call these trigger responsibility, aims responsibility and closure responsibility, and I claim historians often slide between them.

Second, I apply this to the disagreements after 1945 over how to organise liberated Europe and over the use of new military and economic leverage. I argue that even if one side could be shown to have moved first in a narrow sense, that would not settle the other two senses, and that closure responsibility matters most for blame because it is about making confrontation unavoidable rather than merely acting first.

Third, to explain the point I use an analogy from school life. If two pupils keep blocking a narrow corridor and bumping into each other, asking who bumped first on a particular morning misses the point. The more important question is whose behaviour over the term made it normal to expect a collision, and who refused reasonable proposals for taking turns. I claim the early Cold War was more like the corridor over the whole term than like a single bump.

I conclude that blame in historiography should be less about a starting date or a first provocation and more about sustained conduct that narrowed options, and that this means the usual debate about who started it needs to be replaced rather than answered.

Please comment on whether my threefold distinction is coherent and genuinely different in each part, whether the corridor analogy actually supports my conclusion or misleads, which paragraph you find least persuasive and why, and how I could make the final recommendation more precise without merely repeating that both sides were to blame. Please also say if any step in my reasoning seems to assume what it is trying to prove.
