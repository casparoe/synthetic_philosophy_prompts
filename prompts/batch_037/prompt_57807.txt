I am trying to decide what morality requires of individuals whose single actions contribute almost imperceptibly to large-scale harms involving greenhouse gas emissions, consumption of factory-farmed meat, and voting in mass elections. Please develop a desiderata list for any adequate account of individual responsibility in such cases.

Structure your response as a numbered list of six to eight desiderata. For each desideratum, state the requirement, explain the reasoning behind it, and indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable for adequacy or a commitment that belongs to a particular moral outlook. The list as a whole should cover the phenomena an account must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must preserve, and the background facts about decision making under uncertainty with which it must remain consistent.

Use the following three stipulated cases to motivate and test the desiderata, rather than relying on familiar textbook versions. Assume all details as given and do not look up additional facts:

Gradual Reservoir: 20,000 households each draw one bucket per day from a shared reservoir that supplies irrigation. Each additional bucket lowers crop yield by a tiny, roughly linear amount, with no sudden threshold. No single bucket makes a perceptible difference, but the cumulative effect is severe.

Buffer Order: A grocery store stocks factory-farmed chicken and reorders in lots of 50, keeping a buffer of 5 to 10 units. Most individual purchases trigger no new order, but each purchase slightly raises the probability that stock crosses the reorder point within the week. The expected effect is small and the actual effect is usually zero and occasionally large.

Landslide Election: A national election with 80 million voters is forecast to be decided by several million votes. The chance that any one vote is decisive is vanishingly small, yet the outcome matters enormously and the act of voting also has expressive and coordination-related aspects.

In addition to handling those cases, your desiderata should preserve at least the following distinctions: between causing additional harm and bearing moral responsibility for participation; between actual consequences and expected consequences; between linear and threshold-like structures of harm; between what is individually rational and what would be collectively best; and between causal contribution and complicity or expression of disregard.

After presenting the list, briefly note any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied and explain the tension. Then, in no more than a short paragraph for each, indicate how four generic families of views fare against the list: a strict actual-difference view, an expected-consequence view, a universalizability or fairness-based view, and a complicity or expressive view.

Your very last line must begin with Verdict: followed by the short label of the single desideratum that you judge eliminates the largest number of candidate views or creates the deepest tension, with no text after that line.
