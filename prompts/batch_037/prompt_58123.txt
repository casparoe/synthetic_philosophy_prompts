I am an undergraduate currently taking a course on the philosophy of mind, and in our unit on emotion we seem to have reached an impasse that our tutor described as decades old. We contrasted three broad positions: that emotions are essentially evaluative judgements, for example that anger involves judging that one has been wronged; that emotions are a kind of perception of evaluative properties, analogous to seeing colours or shapes but directed at danger, offensiveness or loss; and that emotions are essentially distinctive feelings, particularly of bodily change and affective valence, with any judgement or appraisal coming before or after rather than constituting the emotion.

What puzzles me is that each side seems able to accommodate the textbook counterexamples, but only by reinterpreting them. Recalcitrant fear, such as continuing to be afraid of a spider while sincerely judging it to be harmless, is taken by one side to refute the judgement view and by another to show only that judgements can be compartmentalised. Cases where bodily arousal is pharmacologically blunted yet subjects still report anger or sadness are taken by one side to show feelings are inessential and by the other to show reports are unreliable. Objectless anxiety and lingering grief that persists without any occurrent thought are similarly claimed by everyone.

My working hypothesis is that this is not primarily a factual disagreement but a case of three groups theorising different paradigms under a single label: one group starting from guilt, shame and indignation where reasons and justification seem central, another from sudden fear and disgust where automaticity and phenomenology seem central, and a third trying to split the difference. Is that correct, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement about what emotions are, a hidden empirical question about mechanisms, or a verbal dispute about how broadly to use the term emotion?

Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement with reference to what each position actually claims, rather than adjudicating in favour of one theory, and explain what, if anything, would count as progress on your diagnosis — a conceptual distinction, a kind of case that could not be reinterpreted away, an empirical finding, or a reformulation of the question. If you think no progress should be expected, please explain why. Please answer in British English.
