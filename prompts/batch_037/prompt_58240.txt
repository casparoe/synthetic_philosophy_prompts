Following retirement from a career in thermal engineering, I have taken up the study of philosophy with particular attention to early modern metaphysics. I find the engineering mode of explanation congenial, but I wish to avoid being misled by it.

In a seminar I attend, it has become standard to elucidate the Leibnizian doctrine that the actual world is the best of the alternatives available to a perfect creator by treating creation as a constrained optimization problem of the kind familiar in design. On this rendering, possible individuals and laws correspond to design variables, compossibility corresponds to feasibility constraints, perfection or harmony corresponds to an objective to be maximized, with simplicity of principles traded against richness of outcomes, and natural and moral evils correspond to local costs accepted for the sake of superior global performance. The rendering is then used to defend the view that the presence of evil does not count against perfection, since any removal of a particular evil would, as with a tightly coupled plant, degrade overall performance, and that critics mistakenly imagine an unconstrained optimum.

I would like a careful audit of this optimization analogy. Please set out the mapping between elements of a typical engineering optimization exercise and elements of the Leibnizian account in explicit form, then identify the principal points at which the mapping fails. Distinguish failures that are incidental from those that bear on the intended defensive use, especially concerning commensurability of goods, uniqueness and knowability of an optimum, the difference between selecting among antecedently available options and constituting what counts as an option, and the difference between a designer bound by independent physical constraints and a creator responsible for the constraints themselves. Conclude by assessing whether the defense of evil as a necessary cost survives once the disanalogies are made explicit, and, if the optimization picture is inadequate, suggest what alternative analogy from the natural sciences would illuminate the position with less distortion.
