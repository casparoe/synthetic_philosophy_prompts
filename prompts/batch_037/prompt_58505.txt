The oversight board of our county public hospital, on which I serve, will vote next month on a proposal that has divided the board and the medical staff. The hospital is publicly funded and required to provide care regardless of ability to pay, but it faces a seven-month backlog for hip and knee replacements and has had to curtail evening primary care hours for uninsured patients due to budget shortfalls.

The proposal would lease two operating suites on weekends to a private surgical group that would perform the same joint replacements for patients who pay out of pocket or through private insurance. The group would pay a fixed facility fee plus 18 percent of its weekend revenue, earmarked by contract to restore evening clinic hours. It would hire members of our own surgical staff for weekend overtime on a voluntary basis, with a contractual bar on reducing weekday public operating time and a twelve-month pilot period followed by board review.

Supporters argue that the arrangement preserves universal access under fiscal constraint by using willingness to pay to subsidize care based on need. Opponents argue that it institutionalizes a two-tier system in which ability to pay determines speed of access to the same procedure in the same building, risks drawing staff energy and loyalty toward private work, and weakens public commitment to allocation by medical need.

Please draft a formal decision memorandum suitable for circulation to the board before the vote. It should recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval with concrete operational safeguards, state a clear bottom line for action including the length and review criteria for any pilot, give principled reasons grounded in fairness and the responsible use of public capacity rather than a summary of the law, and give a candid treatment of the strongest consideration on the side you do not favor and explain why it does not prevail. Please write in American English in a tone appropriate for an institutional record.
