Write an essay on the problem of probability in the Everettian interpretation of quantum mechanics, according to which every possible outcome of a measurement occurs on some branch.

The central difficulty has two parts: the meaning of probability if nothing is genuinely chancy because all outcomes happen, and the justification for weighting branches by squared amplitude rather than by simply counting branches when deciding what to believe or how to bet. Your essay should weigh three broad responses: that rational pre-measurement betting behavior suffices to fix the meaning of probability without any uncertainty; that probability enters only as self-locating uncertainty for an observer who knows the global branching structure but does not know which branch they are on; and that Everettian physics leaves probability ungrounded, so either the interpretation is incomplete or our use of probabilistic language in such a universe is pragmatic fiction.

To keep the discussion concrete, base your evaluation on the following two stipulated cases. First, Unequal Amplitude: an agent knows a measurement is about to split the world into an Up branch with weight 0.8 and a Down branch with weight 0.2, and is offered a ticket that pays a modest reward on Up and imposes a large loss on Down. Second, Artificial Subdivision: the same measurement is followed, on the Up branch only, by a further causally irrelevant splitting into one hundred qualitatively identical Up-subbranches with no difference in reward, observer experience, or future physics except branch number.

Explain how each of the three responses treats rational credence and rational action in these cases, including whether Artificial Subdivision should change the price a rational agent would pay before the experiment and whether an agent who wakes after the measurement without yet observing the result should reason differently from before the measurement. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, including branch-counting objections, worries about circularity in appealing to rationality to derive a physical measure, and the coherence of caring about branch weight when all of one's successors are real. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a verdict about which response is most defensible, or propose and defend a clearly stated hybrid, and explain what cost your preferred view must accept.
