Earlier this spring I became the office manager for a small dental practice, a position that has made questions about honesty unexpectedly concrete for me since I field calls from salespeople, applicants, patients, and landlords all day and must balance loyalty to the doctors with a desire not to deceive anyone.

I have been testing my own reactions and find them puzzling: telling a pushy vendor on the phone that Dr. Patel is out of the office when she is in fact ten feet away strikes me as fine; telling him that she is unavailable to take his call right now when she could take it but does not want to strikes me as equally fine and no different in kind. Telling a job applicant after a weak interview that the position has already been filled when it has not strikes me as not fine, even though it is meant to spare his feelings; yet telling him that we are moving forward with other applicants whose experience is a closer match, which is true but which I know he will hear as meaning the search is essentially over, strikes me as fine. On my own recent rental application, writing that a prior eviction was five years ago when it was three years ago strikes me as clearly not fine; listing my intermittent freelance work under a formal-sounding consulting name I had just registered, without stating anything literally false but hoping the landlord will infer stable employment, strikes me as also not fine but distinctly less wrong, even though both are calculated to create the same false impression. Can you formulate a precise principle, or a small set of competing principles, that best captures this pattern, showing which case motivates each distinction it draws, what it predicts for cases I have not considered, and where it requires me to revise one of my verdicts? I am especially interested in what your favored principle would say about a situation I face next week, when a worried patient will ask me whether her biopsy results are back, which they are, but the doctor has instructed me to say nothing until he can speak with her himself.
