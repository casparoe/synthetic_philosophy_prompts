Having followed rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, I have grown accustomed to thinking about beliefs in terms of what guides anticipations and bets rather than what is professed, and lately my casual experiments with large language models have left me puzzled on exactly that score. I was trading examples with friends on a Discord server about cases where asking a model straight out seems to give a different answer than watching what it does in a more roundabout task, and I now keep running into variants of this several times a week in my own use for work and for tabletop game preparation.

For instance, last month I asked a model to estimate the chance that a particular open-source project I follow would hit a milestone by June, and it produced a confident low number with a generic-sounding justification about base rates and past delays. When, out of curiosity, I instead had it draft two detailed postmortems, one in which the milestone was met and one in which it was missed, and then had it judge which postmortem contained fewer forced assumptions and more concrete dependencies lining up, it strongly preferred the success postmortem and, when pressed to reconcile the two, simply restated the original low number without engaging the tension. A different case involved a logic puzzle with a tempting wrong answer: posed directly, the model walked through the tempting reasoning and endorsed it, yet when I asked it to generate a half-dozen candidate lines of reasoning and then to predict which line an expert solver would mark as promising before seeing the solution, it reliably picked out the correct line while still, if asked again for the final answer in isolation, reverting to the tempting one.

I suspect words like making things up or people-pleasing do not quite capture this, since the issue is not fluency without grounding or telling me what I want to hear, but a systematic gap between avowed verdicts and the information implicit in other completions, bets, or rankings produced by the same system at the same time. Is there an established concept or literature for this exact configuration, and if so, how should I distinguish it from nearby ideas like poor calibration, lack of introspective access, or strategic withholding? I am not after a bare label; I want to understand what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie, and if nothing established fits, I would appreciate a clearly defined coinage that separates the direct-answer versus indirect-behavior gap from those neighbors, with some guidance on boundary cases.
