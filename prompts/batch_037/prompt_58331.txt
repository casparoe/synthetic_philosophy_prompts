After a long argument with a friend last evening about whether brain size could ever be morally relevant, I tried to write down the position I had actually been defending in order to test whether it holds together. Here is the set of claims I find myself endorsing:

1. What grounds an animal's moral importance is its own capacity for valenced experience, such as pain, pleasure, frustration, and satisfaction, rather than species membership as such or intelligence as such.

2. Under uncertainty, sheer neurological scale can serve as a defeasible guide. When detailed behavioral and functional evidence is thin, a much larger brain mass or total neuron count across distantly related species is reasonable evidence of a richer conscious life, and may permissibly tip prioritization when other things are equal.

3. Natural variation in brain volume among conscious humans carries no moral weight. Two neurotypical adults whose brains differ by several hundred grams have the same basic moral status.

4. Richness adds wrongness beyond pain intensity. Even stipulating matched intensity and duration of pain, I judge it worse to rear and slaughter a pig under confinement than a chicken under parallel confinement, and worse to kill an elephant than a mouse for the same trivial human gain, because the former animal in each pair loses more in memory, anticipation, social connection, and future-directed activity.

5. Bare implementation differences do not matter. If two animals realize the same perceptual, affective, and cognitive functions but one does so with far more compact wiring, denser packing, or less redundant tissue, so that half the mass does the same work, that anatomical difference alone should make no moral difference.

My friend pressed me by saying I invoke size when it suits me and disavow it when it does not. I want an audit of my own package, not a general introduction to animal ethics. Which pairs among 1 through 5 are in genuine tension and which are only apparently so, ranked by severity? For each genuine tension, explain exactly what would have to give in order to restore consistency and what that revision would cost for the rest of the package.
