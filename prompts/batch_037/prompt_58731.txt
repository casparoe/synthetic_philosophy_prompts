I am preparing a seminar exercise on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection for students who already have a working grasp of utilitarianism and telic egalitarianism.

For purposes of the exercise, please take prioritarianism to hold that the moral value of a distribution is the sum of a strictly concave function of individual lifetime well-being, so that a unit of well-being matters more the worse off the recipient is, but equality itself has no intrinsic value. Take telic egalitarianism to hold that inequality is intrinsically bad, over and above any loss of total well-being, and take utilitarianism to rank distributions by total well-being alone.

Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. Set it in a realistic administrative context, such as a regional hospital director allocating rehabilitation support between two wards with equal numbers of patients and no differences in desert, responsibility, or entitlement. Stipulate that the population is fixed, with no future people, no risk or uncertainty, no instrumental side effects, and interpersonally comparable lifetime well-being scores whose values you stipulate rather than derive. The case is for showing whether prioritarianism can give priority to the worse off without giving intrinsic weight to equality, so a case that generates the verdicts from the theories succeeds while a case that merely stipulates them fails.

Include four options over the same people:
A, an unequal status quo with higher total well-being;
B, a partial equalization achieved by a wasteful transfer that lowers the better-off group, raises the worse-off group, and slightly reduces total well-being because of administrative cost;
C, a full equalization with still greater waste and a further reduction in total well-being;
D, a pure leveling down that lowers the better-off group to the level of the worse-off group with no gain to anyone.

Calibrate specific well-being numbers so that, using the square-root function as an illustrative prioritarian weighting, prioritarianism ranks B above A but ranks A above C and above D, while utilitarianism ranks A above B above C above D, and a telic egalitarian who gives substantial weight to equality ranks C above A and may rank D above A. Show the arithmetic and explain in ordinary language why each theory gives the verdict it does.

After the scenario, identify which structural feature is doing the work in separating prioritarianism from telic egalitarianism, explain what happens to the rankings if the waste in B and C is removed or if the concave weighting is made nearly linear, and describe the strongest reply a defender of telic egalitarianism could make to resist the force of D.

Finally, conclude with a brief self-critical note identifying the step in your reasoning most likely to fail and stating which argument or consideration would lead you to reverse your central verdict about whether the case vindicates prioritarianism against the leveling-down objection.
