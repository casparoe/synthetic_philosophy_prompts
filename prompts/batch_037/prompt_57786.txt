I am a novelist completing a revision of a novel that alternates between a post-industrial town in Yorkshire and a fast-growing port city in Ghana, and I find that my characters' disputes about corruption risk becoming mouthpieces for my own uncertainty rather than believable disagreements. To discipline the draft, I would like a formal specification of what an adequate philosophical account of what counts as corruption must accomplish, framed for the purpose of making cross-cultural disagreement intelligible in fiction rather than for prosecutorial use.

I would be grateful for a carefully ordered set of desiderata, each accompanied by the reason or illustrative consideration that motivates it, with an indication of which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which reflect the prejudices of a particular camp. The specification should at minimum handle three invented cases I keep returning to: a council housing officer in Yorkshire who stays strictly within her legal discretion yet systematically expedites repairs for former schoolmates and neighbours who can reciprocate with childcare and recommendations; a Ghanaian customs official who declines cash but accepts yams and festival invitations from traders whose containers he then clears no faster than the ordinary queue; and an expatriate clinic director who refuses a routine facilitation payment for medical supplies and thereby delays vaccines by six weeks. It should also preserve the distinctions my characters actually invoke, between violating a written law and betraying an entrusted role, between a gift that creates diffuse social obligation and a payment tied to a specific official act, and between patronage networks that substitute for an absent state and those that hollow out a functioning one, while addressing whether a single universal standard can survive anthropological variation or whether corruption must be indexed to local expectations about office.

Please keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on an extended survey, though a brief closing note on how a broadly institutionalist view, a culturalist view, and a functionalist view fare against your list would help me distribute strengths and blind spots among my characters, including whether any subset of your desiderata appears jointly unsatisfiable. Conclude by stating how confident you are in the overall specification and singling out the single requirement about which you are least sure and why.
