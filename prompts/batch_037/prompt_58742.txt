As rapporteur to the Law Reform Advisory Committee of a mid-sized city, I must submit a memorandum advising the City Council on a proposal currently before it to criminalize the purchase and sale of consensual sexual services between adults in private indoor settings, even where the premises comply with the licensing, health examination, and worker-safety requirements introduced under a three-year pilot scheme.

The factual record before the Committee is as follows. Inspection reports over the pilot period found high compliance with health and safety standards and no substantiated instances of coercion or trafficking within licensed premises, though outreach workers caution that activity outside the licensed sector is harder to monitor. A public consultation returned sharply divided views: a substantial majority of respondents expressed moral disapproval of paid sex as such, with many invoking concern for the moral character and cohesion of the community over time, while current and former workers, public-health officials, and a minority of residents emphasized autonomy, safety under licensing, and the absence of direct harm to third parties when transactions occur privately and in compliance with regulations. The police have taken a neutral position on the criminal proposal but warn of significant enforcement costs and likely displacement effects.

The Council has asked the Committee to recommend one of three courses: enact a criminal prohibition on purchase and sale as proposed, retain licensing while strengthening zoning, nuisance, and advertising controls, or move to full decriminalization subject to general employment and public-health law. Please prepare a memorandum of approximately 1,200 words, suitable for circulation to councillors without philosophical training, that states a clear recommendation among these options with concrete next steps and a mechanism for subsequent review. The memorandum should provide a principled justification that addresses both the appeal to preventing harm to others and the appeal to preserving a shared moral environment, explains what weight the expressive function of criminal law should carry here, and confronts directly the strongest consideration favoring the course you reject rather than minimizing it.
