I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am developing a feature on what decision theorists mean by rational irrationality.

For readers without a philosophy background, I plan to start from the familiar hitchhiker scenario associated with Parfit: a traveler stranded in the desert will die unless a driver gives a ride to town, the traveler can offer to pay a large reward upon arrival, but once safe in town a purely self-interested traveler would have no reason to pay, the driver foresees this and therefore does not stop, and both end up worse off than if the traveler could somehow guarantee payment.

To move beyond a retelling, my editors and I have constructed two contrasting variations to anchor the piece, and I would like an explanation built around them.

In the first variation, before meeting the driver, the traveler can take a short-acting drug, with visible physiological aftereffects the driver can reliably detect at the roadside, that temporarily makes promise-breaking psychologically impossible.

In the second variation, the traveler cannot alter her psychology but can enroll in advance in a voluntary escrow arrangement that will automatically transfer the reward on arrival, except that after arrival she can still cancel the transfer by paying a modest cancellation fee that is much smaller than the reward.

Please explain the conceptual issue these cases are meant to isolate: in what sense it can be rational to be, or to make oneself, disposed to act irrationally, how to distinguish the rationality of adopting a disposition from the rationality of the actions that flow from it, and why the benefit of such self-modification depends crucially on what the driver can anticipate rather than only on what the traveler feels or does afterward. The explanation should be precise enough to show why the two variations differ in kind rather than merely in effectiveness, yet accessible to educated readers with no training in formal decision theory, and it should work through how each variation changes the driver incentives without appealing to specialized literature.
