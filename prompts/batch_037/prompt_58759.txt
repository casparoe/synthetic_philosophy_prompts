I am a novelist developing a councilwoman named Mara whose political idiom turns on liberty, and I need to keep her judgments coherent across chapters. So far I have had her hold that packers under a kindly supervisor who retains an unrestricted right to reassign shifts at will are unfree even when he never uses it; that residents bound by a fairly enacted and openly contestable recycling ordinance are not thereby made unfree; that a lighthouse keeper cut off by winter ice faces hardship but not political unfreedom, there being no master involved; and that a journalist who softens coverage because one owner controls the town paper is already unfree though no threat was issued. In the next chapter she discovers a distribution center where a similar at-will reassignment clause lay forgotten in contracts, unknown to workers and to a manager who never used it, so everyone spoke and acted with complete frankness until a lawyer rediscovers the clause. Would Mara say the workers were unfree all along, which underlying rule best fits her earlier verdicts among the plausible candidates spanning positive, negative, and republican ideas of liberty, what rival rule fits those verdicts equally well yet predicts the opposite here, and what single further case would most cleanly separate them. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
