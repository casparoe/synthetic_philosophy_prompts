I am preparing an application for a nine-month independent research fellowship in moral philosophy and wish to include a project on strong longtermism that does not simply rehearse the standard exchange between expected-value reasoning and appeals to cluelessness.

My provisional thought is that defenses and critiques alike tend to run together two different ways in which a present choice might matter for the far future: cases where an initial intervention sets in motion a dynamic that thereafter sustains itself without further agency, and cases where any long-run influence depends on continuous active maintenance, correction, and recommitment across generations. Consider, as stylized illustrations, the contrast between launching a durable passive archive meant to survive without intervention and endowing a stewardship body that must actively reinterpret its mandate over centuries. I suspect that distinguishing self-sustaining from maintenance-dependent persistence reshapes both the fanaticism worry and the case for particular kinds of longtermist action, but I have not yet formulated this as a tractable inquiry.

Please draft a 1200-word research prospectus based on this starting point. It should formulate a single precise central question, explain why familiar positions on longtermism, cluelessness, and risk-sensitivity leave that question unresolved, lay out a non-empirical investigative strategy with distinct stages suited to the fellowship period, describe what an illuminating outcome would consist in as opposed to a merely negative or inconclusive one, and assess frankly whether the scope is realistic and where it might need to be narrowed. There is no need to supply references or a reading list, as I will complete those separately.
