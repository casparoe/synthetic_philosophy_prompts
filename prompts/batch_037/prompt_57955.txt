I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas and I'm stuck on a museum story. The natural history wing swapped its crumbling dinosaur skeleton for an indistinguishable 3D print, labeled it clearly as a replica, and visitors still felt cheated and attendance dipped. Right next door the art wing shows an old master thats been restored so many times theres almost no original paint left, but its still presented as the real thing and nobody blinks. What view of what makes originals valuable does that double standard presuppose — is it about physical contact with the past, about the look, about honesty or something else? And can both halves of the practice even fit the same theory, or would dropping the contact idea mean we'd have to change how we label and display stuff?
