I work as a backend software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and a pattern in how language gets reused on my teams has been bothering me because I lack good vocabulary for it. Last year our platform team used idempotent in a strict sense for payment endpoints that could be safely retried with the same key, and within weeks the product team was calling nightly backfill jobs idempotent to mean roughly that rerunning them was not catastrophic, even when a rerun created duplicates that needed manual cleanup. Separately, our incident handbook defines a critical defect in terms of sustained customer impact, yet in standup the same word gets applied to anything blocking a demo or making on-call painful that week, with its threshold shifting from day to day without anyone calling out the shift or updating the handbook.

What strikes me is the combination: a term is copied because it sounds rigorous and carries authority from elsewhere, then within a group of six to ten people its application becomes loose and wobbly in a way that is useful rather than corrected. Nobody seems confused in the moment, and nobody is making a single argument that trades on two meanings at once, so it does not feel like ordinary ambiguity or a one-off mistake. It also does not feel like stable team shorthand, because there is no stable meaning to learn, nor like the slow change of everyday language over decades, because this happens over a few months and everyone still acts as if they mean the same thing the handbook meant.

Is there an established name for this exact configuration, and if not, could you propose one with a precise definition? I am not looking for just a label; please explain what the phenomenon is and what it is not by comparing it to the two or three closest ordinary notions, saying where the boundaries lie and giving examples of what would and would not count. Please keep your answer to no more than 350 words.
