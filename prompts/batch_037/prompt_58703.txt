I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am preparing an application for a three-month part-time fellowship supporting independent work on safe AI assistants. I would like a draft of a short research proposal, about two pages, based on the following half-formed idea. In engineering practice, we often tell an assistant to avoid side effects, but any useful task changes the world, so the instruction must mean avoid important or hard-to-reverse side effects. Comparing what the agent did to what would have happened if it had done nothing still leaves open how to measure which differences matter, and counting reversible versus irreversible changes also seems to smuggle in judgments about what is worth reversing. My hunch is that impact might be definable procedurally, in terms of whether the principal can still detect, understand, and undo the assistants work with reasonable effort, rather than in terms of a full account of what is valuable. To keep it concrete, I have in mind two cases from my own work: a file-management assistant that archives old shared-drive files to reduce clutter, and a coding assistant that refactors a repository and notifies teammates. Please turn this into a proposal that states a precise and tractable central question, explains why familiar baseline-comparison and reversibility ideas leave that question open, lays out a method centered on conceptual clarification tested against the two engineering cases and explains why that method is preferable to attempting a formal definition plus proofs or running user studies, describes what a successful outcome would look like and what would count as the project failing, and closes with an honest assessment of scope for the three-month period. Please refer to general positions and arguments only, without a bibliography or literature review by citation, as I will add references myself. Keep the language accessible to a technically literate but philosophically untrained reader.
