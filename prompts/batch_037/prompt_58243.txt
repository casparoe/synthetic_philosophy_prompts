Consider Judge Ellis of the Franklin Court of Appeals, who regularly decides cases under Section 101 of the state Road Safety Act, which makes it an offense to operate a motor vehicle on a public highway while using a handheld mobile telephone, with enacted findings stating the aim as reducing distracted-driving crashes. Her prior rulings hold that holding a smartphone to read texts while moving violates the section, that speaking through a dashboard hands-free system without touching the phone does not violate it, and that briefly handling a smartphone to call emergency services while moving in traffic still violates it, with no implied emergency exception. She must now decide the case of a driver cited for dictating messages on an LTE-enabled smartwatch worn on the wrist while driving at highway speed, who argues that a wrist-worn device is not a mobile telephone. What should she decide if she is to remain consistent with her own precedents? Identify two competing interpretive rules, one broadly textualist and one broadly purposivist, that both fit those precedents, explain what each predicts for the smartwatch case, give a best-supported verdict with reasons, and describe a further hypothetical case that would distinguish the two rules.
