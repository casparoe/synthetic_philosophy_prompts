I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on philosophy of science and am preparing an interactive exercise on whether the sciences carve nature at its joints. My students readily repeat slogans about realism and social construction but have difficulty seeing how different accounts of natural kinds actually diverge on concrete scientific practice, so I would like a diagnostic quiz titled by its function as a which view of natural kinds do you actually hold instrument that sorts respondents by their verdicts on cases rather than by their prior attachment to labels.

Please write nine questions, each built around a concrete vignette with two or three answer options phrased so that a student without background in metaphysics can choose among them without knowing which theoretical position each choice favors, and provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto five leaves: strict essentialism, a causal cluster view, a permissive pluralist realism, an interest-relative constructivism, and eliminativism about natural kinds. Three vignettes must be included among the nine: one in which chemists synthesize a mirror-image version of a bacterial enzyme that performs the same catalytic role despite opposite chirality and no shared evolutionary history and regulators must decide whether it counts as the same kind of molecule, one in which field clinicians propose a diagnostic category that reliably predicts drug response in one cultural setting but fractures into unrelated symptom clusters elsewhere, and one in which planetary scientists find a Martian mineral indistinguishable from terrestrial basalt in hardness and crystal structure but formed by an entirely nonvolcanic process. No question should directly ask about essences, joints, or realism in the abstract. Conclude with guidance on how to handle tied or mixed profiles, note any combination of answers that corresponds to no coherent position and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves that the quiz cannot cleanly separate.
