I serve as a teaching assistant for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of language and mind, and I need help grading a midterm essay.

The exam question I set was as follows:

Consider the familiar Twin Earth scenario. On Earth the colorless, drinkable liquid in lakes and taps is H2O. On Twin Earth, which is otherwise very similar, the corresponding liquid that plays the same everyday role is a different chemical kind, XYZ. In 1750, before modern chemistry, Oscar on Earth and his intrinsic duplicate Twin Oscar on Twin Earth are indistinguishable in their behavior, dispositions, and what they would sincerely say about the liquid they call water. Then consider a new case. Mira grows up on Earth using the term in the ordinary way. Without her knowledge she is transported to Twin Earth, where she lives for many years, interacts with XYZ in the same ways she once interacted with H2O, and never notices a difference. No one tells her about the switch.

In no more than 400 words, explain: (a) what semantic externalism claims about Oscar and Twin Oscar in 1750, (b) what it predicts about what Mira means by her term for the liquid immediately after the move versus after a long period of embedding on Twin Earth, and (c) whether externalism implies that speakers do not know what they are thinking, being careful to distinguish the question of what fixes the content of a thought from the question of how a thinker knows that content.

One student submitted the following response:

Mira case analysis: Oscar and Twin Oscar show that meaning is not fixed by what is in the head. They are internally the same in 1750 and apply their word to clear drinkable stuff in the same way, but Oscar refers to H2O and Twin Oscar refers to XYZ, because reference depends on the actual environment and causal history, not just internal descriptions or dispositions. For Mira, the same logic applies. Right after she arrives on Twin Earth she is still internally the same person, but since her surroundings have changed, the extension of her term changes immediately to XYZ, just as Twin Oscar means XYZ from the start. After many years nothing further changes semantically, she simply continues to mean XYZ. This shows that externalism denies first-person authority over content. If what you mean by water depends on whether you are embedded in an H2O world or an XYZ world, then you cannot know what you are thinking by reflection alone. Mira thinks she is thinking that water is wet, but she cannot know whether that thought involves H2O or XYZ unless she does chemistry or investigates her causal history. So the view is that self-knowledge requires empirical knowledge of the environment.
