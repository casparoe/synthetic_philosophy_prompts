Our city operates a small grant fund for local art projects, and I chair the volunteer panel that decides the awards. We review about thirty proposals three times a year, with grants around $5,000 each, volunteer readers who can spend at most twenty minutes per proposal and have no philosophy background, and final sign-off by the city council, which requires plain-language reasons.

Please turn Tolstoy's account of art into a usable procedure for us: art as the successful transmission of a felt emotion from maker to audience, distinct from beauty, technical skill, or entertainment; degrees of infectiousness depending on sincerity, individual distinctness of the feeling, and clarity of expression; and the distinction between broadly shareable moral or broadly human feeling and narrow, exclusive, or merely titillating feeling. Give us an ordered checklist or set of tests with what counts as passing each step, where to stop with a no, and where to flag that philosophy cannot decide and a value judgment by the panel is required.

Test the procedure on three invented proposals: A, a clumsy amateur choir piece about grief after a factory closure that moves non-musicians; B, a technically dazzling immersive light show assembled from market-tested effects that produces strong momentary awe but no lasting feeling; C, a sentimental street-theater melodrama about forgiveness that our panel would normally dismiss as lowbrow but that is sincere, clear, and deeply moving to ordinary audiences. Include one outcome we will dislike and explain why the procedure requires it.

Please organize your reply in this order: Core Idea in Plain Terms, about 100 words; Step-by-Step Procedure, about 250 words; Three Trial Applications, about 300 words total; Limits and Required Judgments, about 150 words, stating what the procedure cannot do and where it will misfire.
