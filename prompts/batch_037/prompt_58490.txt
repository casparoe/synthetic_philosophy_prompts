My partner and I recently became parents after several rounds of in vitro fertilization, which has made an abstract metaphysical question unexpectedly concrete for me.

I am drawn to origin essentialism, understood as the claim that our daughter could not have originated from a different sperm and egg than the ones that in fact formed her, such that even if we had used a different embryo from the same batch, or conceived a month earlier or later with the same parents and a similar upbringing, the resulting child would have been a different person rather than her under different circumstances. I want to understand what this commitment actually risks.

Lay out what would count against it. Organize the challenges from those that would most directly undermine the view to those that would only create pressure to revise it, and for each explain whether a consistent defender could absorb it by qualifying the view and what that qualification would cost. Include at least the following kinds of considerations: conflicting judgments about concrete variant cases concerning who someone could have been, general principles about what grounds persistence of a person over time, and relevant facts about development and genetics that put pressure on a gamete-based criterion. To keep the discussion anchored, address three variant possibilities I keep returning to: a conception by the same parents a cycle later with different gametes but a largely similar upbringing; a hypothetical fertilization of the same egg by a different sperm from the same father that happened to carry an identical genetic complement; and a case involving mitochondrial replacement or early embryo manipulation where the nuclear gametic contribution is unchanged but the developmental starting point is altered.

Also explain what, if anything, this view predicts that its denial does not, whether its more refined formulations have left it with no substantive exposure at all, and what its most thoughtful defenders would themselves acknowledge as counting against it rather than only what its critics press. I am most likely to be moved by considerations that do not depend on technical machinery but clarify which of my ordinary judgments about who my child could have been I must give up in order to keep the view.
