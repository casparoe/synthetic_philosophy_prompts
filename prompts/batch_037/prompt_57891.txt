I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, so I am used to thinking of beliefs and Bayesian updates as things that must ultimately be paid for in physical resources, and I am currently sitting in on an undergraduate statistical mechanics course to see how physicists cash that idea out. The course follows what seems to be the now-standard script: it introduces Maxwell's demon through a single-molecule engine with a partition, shows how the demon can extract work by knowing which side the molecule is on, and then presents the thermodynamic cost of resetting the demon's one-bit memory as the reason the second law is safe, gesturing to recent tabletop experiments with a colloidal particle shuffled between wells or electrons moved through a tiny box as direct measurements of information turned into work and back. What struck me in a long post-class discussion with friends is that the same course also teaches the second law in completely impersonal terms as a regularity about gases, steam, and heat flow with no agents in sight, and it treats measuring and copying as essentially free while singling out erasure as uniquely dissipative, even though at the level of microphysics both look like the creation and destruction of correlations between physical systems.

So what view of entropy and information does this teaching-and-demonstration practice actually presuppose, if any? Does presenting erasure as the exorcism presuppose that Shannon information just is thermodynamic entropy in some ontological sense, or that entropy itself is relative to an observer's coarse-graining and means of manipulation, rather than an objective feature of microphysics? Can a fully objectivist view centered on particle trajectories and phase-space volumes explain the same laboratory results some other way, for instance as ordinary dissipation in a particular device with no special role for knowledge, and if so, which part of the textbook story strictly requires observer-relativity versus merely fits well with it? And is the practice internally consistent in the way it privileges erasure over measurement, or does that asymmetry reveal a hidden choice about where to draw the boundary between system and memory?

To make this concrete, consider the variant we kept returning to: a demon equipped with an ever-expandable tape who never erases at all but keeps records indefinitely while continuing to extract work, or who offloads the resetting step to a distant cold reservoir that no one audits. Use that case to test the presuppositions: would the textbook account have to say the second law is still safe because the total ledger including the tape and reservoir has grown, and does that move already concede that information talk is just bookkeeping for where free energy went? Finally, spell out what would have to change in teaching and in what counts as a successful demon experiment if we replaced the current implicit view with its main rival — what would instructors have to stop saying, and what would experimentalists have to measure instead?
