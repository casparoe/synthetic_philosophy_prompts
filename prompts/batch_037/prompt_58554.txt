I am a high school junior taking an elective on justice and fairness, and I have been discussing Rawls's idea of choosing the rules for society from behind a veil of ignorance where you do not know what position you will occupy, combined with the difference principle that inequalities are permitted only when they work to the benefit of the worst-off group. I find the abstract formulation confusing, so I have been trying to reconstruct it from how my teacher judged the practice cases we worked through in class, and I would like help predicting what she will say about a new case our study group invented.

In class she judged three cases as follows. First, faced with an equal town where everyone has a decent but modest level of resources versus an unequal town where the worst-off have somewhat more and specialists have much more because higher pay was needed to motivate enough people to train for demanding hospital and maintenance jobs that serve everyone, she said the unequal town was just. Second, faced with the same equal town versus an unequal town where the worst-off gain only very slightly while owners gain enormously by being allowed to buy up lake access and charge fees, when an alternative tax rule would have given the worst-off substantially more than either option, she said the unequal arrangement was unjust and that justice required the alternative that did most for the worst-off. Third, faced with a choice behind the veil between a guaranteed moderate prospect for whoever you turn out to be and a gamble with an equal chance of a high prospect or a very low prospect, she said it would be reasonable to take the guarantee even though the gamble has a higher average. I take these to fit more than one underlying rule she might be using.

Our study group invented a new case for the final discussion about our district summer work program. Plan Equal gives every participant the same stipend of five hundred dollars for community work with shared mentors. Plan Unequal allows a small number of students to take private technology internships paying ten times as much with exclusive mentoring and a fast track to later jobs, while a tax on those firms raises the community stipend for everyone else from five hundred to six hundred fifty dollars, but creates a visible two-tier track where community workers say they feel marked as second-class in later hiring. Please use my teacher's earlier verdicts to reconstruct the two or three most plausible rules she could be running, explain what each rule predicts she will say about Plan Equal versus Plan Unequal, give your best prediction with reasons, and describe one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish between those rules. Please also say what argument or consideration would change your prediction and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
