Jackson's Mary case, in which a scientist confined to a colorless room masters the complete physics and neuroscience of color vision and then sees color for the first time, has produced a decades-long stalemate: defenders hold that she learns something new and that this refutes physicalism, while physicalist replies hold that she gains only a new ability, a new acquaintance relation, or a new concept for facts she already knew, and neither side moves. My hypothesis is that the parties are not answering the same question, with one side doing metaphysics of experience and the other side doing theory of concepts under a shared label. Evaluate that hypothesis against what each side actually claims, and to test your diagnosis consider a stipulated variant in which Mary, while still confined, exhaustively studies a perfect simulation of her own brain as it would respond to red. What should each side say about whether she still learns something upon release, what does that reveal about whether the disagreement concerns the meaning of knowledge and physical, standards of evidence, or irreconcilable starting intuitions, and what, if anything, would count as progress?
