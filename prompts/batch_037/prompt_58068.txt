Consider two philosophers who have debated for several years whether executing a particular computation can by itself confer moral status. One defends a substrate-neutral functionalist view, on which a system counts as a candidate moral patient if it implements the right causal organization, characterized by integrated perception, valenced learning from reward and punishment, and flexible use of that learning to guide future behavior, regardless of whether it is realized in neurons, silicon, or other materials. The other defends a restrictive view, on which implementation of such an organization is insufficient, and moral status additionally requires biological life, a particular evolutionary history, or uptake within social practices of responsibility and care.

To prevent the debate from remaining abstract, stipulate the following three systems for both parties to address: a silicon replay device that reproduces, step by step, the state transitions recorded during a mammalian pain episode, preserving input-output relations but operating with different energy use and without the original capacity for global self-maintenance; a large distributed training run in which the same gradient update is recomputed identically on thousands of machines, where only one copy influences the deployed model while the others are discarded; and two dialogue systems with indistinguishable conversational behavior, one driven by an enormous precomputed lookup table with no generalization beyond stored entries, the other by a compact generative model that supports counterfactual and novel inferences.

Draft the coauthored adversarial-collaboration record, in a sober academic voice and about 800 to 1000 words, that both philosophers could sign as a fair summary. The document should open with a brief preamble stating the shared aim, then set out shared premises about computation, implementation, and moral patienthood, then present each position in its strongest form using language its holder would endorse, then isolate two or three cruxes where settling an issue about grain of description, counterfactual robustness, or causal efficacy versus mere instantiation would move at least one party, and for each crux specify a concrete comparison, intervention, or observation using the stipulated systems that both agree would be informative, together with the divergent expectations each side records in advance. Close by noting any practical guidance for builders of AI systems on which both sides converge despite their theoretical disagreement.
