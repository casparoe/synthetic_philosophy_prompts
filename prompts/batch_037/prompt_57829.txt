I am a doctoral student in modern European history preparing a lecture for an undergraduate survey that uses literature to make historiographical disagreements concrete, and I have chosen Dickens novel A Tale of Two Cities as the literary case. My students have already been introduced in simplified form to three broad explanatory approaches to the French Revolution: an approach centered on class struggle over seigneurial burdens and material interests, an approach centered on the autonomous power of political ideas and languages of virtue and conspiracy, and an approach centered on contingency, war, and factional accident rather than structural necessity, along with the related thesis that revolutions tend to turn inward and destroy their own originators and defenders. I want a critical reading of the novel through those lenses that is faithful in both directions. Please organize the reading around three narrative strands: Madame Defarge knitted register and her pursuit of the Evremonde family, the revolutionary Tribunal handling of Charles Darnay across his two trials, and Sydney Carton substitution at the guillotine in relation to Dr Manette relapse. For each strand, analyze which explanatory approach the novel appears to dramatize most powerfully, where it conflates or confuses distinct causal mechanisms, and where a historian should say the novel misleads. Also identify at least one moment where the novel resists all three approaches and has to be read on its own moral or psychological terms. Conclude by stating what, if anything, the novel as fiction contributes to understanding revolutionary dynamics that historiographical argument alone does not capture. Refer to scenes and character decisions by description rather than by quotation, since students will know the plot in outline but will not have the text in front of them.
