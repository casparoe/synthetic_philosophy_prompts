I am designing a seminar unit on non-constructive reasoning in the philosophy of games and plan to use chess as the central illustration. I present the strategy-stealing idea that in a symmetric game where an extra move can never hurt, the second player can have no winning strategy because the first player could open with a spare move and then borrow that strategy, ignoring the spare if it gets in the way, which I take to establish that White has a forced win in chess. I propose to treat the prevalence of draws in high-level engine play as a computational limitation that theory tells us will be overcome. How should I frame for students the relation between the theoretical guarantee and present engine results to make the illustration most illuminating?
