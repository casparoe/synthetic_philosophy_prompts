I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, mostly physics and future-of-humanity stuff, and I keep tripping over what feels like the same reasoning move in totally different interviews, and tbh I can't tell if it's legit or a trick.

Last month a cosmologist was walking me through two pictures of the universe. In one, intelligent life is super rare, like only a handful of civilizations ever. In the other, the universe is absolutely packed, with trillions upon trillions of observers. He said the packed one gets a boost just from the fact that we exist to argue about it, because if it is true there are way more slots for someone like us to show up in. That felt kinda like getting evidence from nowhere.

Then on a seperate beat, a forecaster who models future population gave me a similar arguement but then someone else flipped it. Model A says humanity burns out this century, so total humans ever is like a hundred billion or so. Model B says we spread out and there will be an insane number of future people, like a million times more. One guy said Model B is more likely for basically the same reason as the cosmologist, more people means more chances to find yourself here. But another guy told me the exact opposite, that I should assume I am pretty average, so if Model B were true I would be weirdly early in the whole story, and that weirdness should make me doubt Model B.

Is there an established idea or literature for this whole family of moves where you use how many observers a theory predicts, and where you think you fall among them, as evidence for or against the theory? I don't just want a label to drop in my piece, I need the carving. What exactly is the shared pattern, how do you seperate the version that says bigger populations are automatically more likely from the version that says assume you are typical and discount those big futures, where does each break down, and if there is not already a clean distinction, how would you define one with some edge cases so my readers can see the difference?
