One student submitted the following response for grading: The special composition question asks when smaller things compose a larger thing. Universalism holds that any plurality composes something, even a scattered plurality like a lamp in one room and a shoe in another. Nihilism holds that no plurality ever composes something, so there are strictly no chairs, only particles arranged chairwise, although we may continue to speak as if chairs exist. A middle view holds that composition occurs only when the parts are caught up in a single life, so a horse exists while a house made of bricks does not. The argument from vagueness shows the middle view must be wrong, because on that view there could be borderline cases of composition, such as boards being gradually glued together or an animal slowly dying, and that would mean it could be vague whether anything exists, which is impossible. It follows that universalism is true. Universalism also shows that objects persist by having different temporal parts at different times, since a scattered object could not otherwise remain the same through change.
