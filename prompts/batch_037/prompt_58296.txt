For the past term I have been convening a small interdisciplinary reading group on norms and institutions, composed mainly of sociology and economics postgraduates with no background in philosophy of language, and I have agreed to open our next session with a concise primer on Lewis's treatment of convention and the idea that orderly norms can emerge without deliberate design. The conversation in the group tends to drift toward treating any widespread habit as conventional and toward assuming that conventions must have been tacitly agreed, and several members recently became intrigued by an unposted pattern in our own building where, after a single overcrowded workshop, people began queuing to the left of the shared coffee machine while keeping the right side clear for quick water refills.

I need guidance on pedagogy rather than a restatement of the theory: which conceptual building blocks need to be secured before later ones will make sense, and the rationale for that dependency, how I might press a single everyday case such as the coffee-machine queue into service for the entire exposition and the criteria for judging whether it is fit for that role or misleading, which predictable misunderstandings are likely to arise at each stage and how to preempt them in situ, and how to tell by the end whether the central distinction has been grasped. In particular I want to know how to sequence the notions of a recurrent coordination difficulty, an actual regularity in response, interlocking expectations about others, conditional willingness to conform, and the availability of an equally workable alternative, where to intercept the temptations to equate convention with explicit bargain, to count every regularity as conventional, to reduce mutual expectation to mere widespread knowledge, and to hear evolution as a biological claim, what single new vignette I could offer them to separate a genuine convention from mere habit on one side and imposed uniformity on the other, and which formal refinements I may legitimately bracket for now with an explicit caveat.
