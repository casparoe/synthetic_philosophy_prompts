I have been puzzling over Benatar's asymmetry and worry that it proves too much. Understand the asymmetry as the claim that the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, but the absence of pain is good even when there is no one to enjoy that absence, whereas the absence of pleasure is bad only when there is someone who is thereby deprived. My friend presses the asymmetry into an argument for pro-mortalism as follows. (1) The absence of pain is good even with no later subject, while the absence of pleasure is bad only with a deprived subject. (2) Suppose Ada is healthy and would otherwise live on for decades with far more pleasure than pain. (3) If Ada dies painlessly now, her future pains are absent, which by (1) is good though there is no later subject to enjoy it. (4) Her future pleasures are also absent, but since after death there is no subject, by (1) this absence is not bad. (5) So painless death secures a good and no bad, and is better than continued life however good that life would be. (6) Hence the asymmetry implies it would be better for each of us to die now painlessly. That conclusion seems absurd, and I take it defenders of the asymmetry wish to reject it while retaining the case against coming into existence. Which step should be given up, and what does giving it up cost for the original asymmetry argument? I favour keeping the asymmetry if I can, but I want to know the price.
