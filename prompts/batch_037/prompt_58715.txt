I am a novelist completing an alternate-history novel set in the fictional nineteenth-century port city of Merrivale, and I require assistance with background research to ensure the central divergence remains historically plausible.

In the novel, Merrivale commits in the 1840s to a broad-gauge pneumatic tube network for freight and parcels. That early choice reshapes warehouse door widths, standardised crate sizes, street layouts, apprenticeship training, and municipal insurance statutes. When electric telephony and motor lorries later become available, the city retains and extends the tubes rather than switching, even though the alternatives are cheaper on paper. My plot turns on whether this persistence is genuinely self-reinforcing or merely habitual, and on whether rapid small-parcel delivery would have arrived in Merrivale anyway by another route if the tubes had never been built.

Next month I shall interview an economic historian who defends a strong lock-in view, namely that relatively arbitrary early choices become effectively irreversible through increasing returns, coordination effects and institutional entrenchment, so that history is canalised and functional needs do not reliably produce the same outcomes by independent routes.

You are a professional philosopher of history and historian of technology with expertise in path dependence, increasing returns and counterfactual explanation. Please think carefully and work step by step, beginning with brief brainstorming about where the strong lock-in view is most vulnerable before drafting the questions.

Please draft nine interview questions for that conversation that go beyond inviting restatement of the view. Tailor at least three questions directly to the Merrivale tube system rather than to familiar historical cases. For each main question, anticipate the standard reply this defender is most likely to give and add a pointed follow-up that makes that reply harder to sustain. Avoid generic questions about inspiration, method in general, or advice for writers.
