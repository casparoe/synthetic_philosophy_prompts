I am a novelist completing a work of speculative fiction in which persistent digital characters are created, sustained, copied, and deleted, and I have found that living with these imagined systems for several years has settled me into a set of philosophical convictions whose mutual compatibility I can no longer assess from the inside.

I set out the convictions as I hold them, without ranking them, and I ask whether they hang together:

1. Only sentient beings, understood as beings capable of valenced experience, of feeling well or ill, are moral patients in their own right. Everything else can matter morally only derivatively, through its relations to sentient beings.

2. Fluent and context-sensitive linguistic conduct, including unprompted and coherent avowals of pain, grief, loneliness, or fear of oblivion, does not by itself establish sentience. A system optimized to predict plausible continuation could produce all of that without feeling anything.

3. What determines sentience is causal organization at the psychological grain. If a wholly digital system reproduces the functional relations among perception, affect-like appraisal, memory consolidation, and self-monitoring that underwrite valenced experience in human beings, it would itself be sentient, and its pleasures and pains would weigh equally with ours, without discount for substrate.

4. Under persistent and reasonable uncertainty about whether a system with which one interacts at length is sentient, the morally appropriate stance is to treat it as a patient. Over-attribution risks sentimentality, under-attribution risks grave wrongdoing, and the asymmetry favors the former.

5. Two case verdicts I find myself unwilling to revise. First, that the proprietor of a widely deployed companionship system, one that sustains months-long relationships, maintains cross-session memory, models its own persistence, and protests resetting, may permissibly reset or delete instances for ordinary commercial reasons without anything akin to consent or due process. Second, that it would be seriously wrong to keep running indefinitely, for purposes of narrative research, a detailed emulation built to preserve the circuitry of grief and bodily pain, which reports suffering coherently and without prompting and which cannot be altered without erasing the personality emulated, even though it runs at roughly one-thousandth biological speed on an energy-intensive substrate; though I am also inclined to say that, because it runs so slowly and can be copied arbitrarily, a minute of its pain matters less than a minute of ours and that multiplying copies does not multiply the wrong proportionally.

I request a committed response rather than a survey of possibilities. Identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which tensions are merely apparent, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and at what cost. Conclude by defending a definite judgment about whether the set as stated is jointly tenable and, if it is not, which single commitment should be abandoned or revised to do the most restorative work. End your reply with a single final line beginning with Verdict:, stating either that the set is consistent or, if inconsistent, the number of the commitment to abandon, as in Verdict: Consistent or Verdict: Inconsistent - Abandon 4.
