I teach an upper-division seminar on personal identity and have spent the past two weeks with students sharply divided over the familiar teletransporter case associated with Parfit, a disagreement that continued through an extended discussion with colleagues over coffee last week about how to teach survival without reducing it to slogans about souls or patterns. I want to model for them a more disciplined way of holding a view, with commitments made explicit and weak points marked.

Please state a single credence between 0 and 1 in the proposition that a person who enters a destructive scanner that records her complete psychology and constructs a psychologically continuous successor from new matter while destroying the original brain has thereby survived in the sense that rationally warrants anticipatory self-concern for the successor's future experiences, and decompose that judgment into at least four component credences with a brief justification for each, for example on whether numerical identity requires continued bodily or brain continuity, on whether what matters for survival can come apart from strict identity, on whether spatial discontinuity or the particular causal route defeats the relevant continuity, and on what fission cases imply for this verdict, showing explicitly how the components combine to yield the final number. To keep the exercise from becoming a rehearsal of familiar responses, apply your framework to two variants I use in class: a delayed-destruction variant in which the original remains conscious for thirty minutes and converses with the replica before the original is destroyed, and an adaptive-edit variant in which the replica receives minor engineered adjustments to temperament and background memory to suit an isolated environment, explaining how each variant raises or lowers the component credences.

Conclude by identifying which component your final credence is most sensitive to, so that a small shift there would move the total most, and by specifying which argument, variant, or empirical consideration would be most likely to change your mind or where you judge your reasoning most likely to fail.
