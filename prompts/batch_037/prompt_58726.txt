I am drafting a position statement for an interdisciplinary workshop on literature and emotion and would welcome critical scrutiny of its internal coherence.

The view I am currently inclined to defend combines the following elements.

1. Pity, grief and fear directed at a particular individual or event require belief that the individual exists or existed and that the relevant misfortune has occurred or may occur.

2. The pity, grief, suspense and fear experienced by engaged readers and viewers in response to fictional narratives are tokens of the same psychological kinds as pity, grief, suspense and fear in everyday life, rather than distinct states of imagined feeling, make-believe, or mere bodily arousal accompanied by imagining.

3. Competent adult engagement with fiction does not involve belief, even momentary or half-conscious belief, in the reality of the characters and events; it involves imagining that certain things happen while continuing to believe that they do not.

4. Such responses are generally fitting and rationally in good order; complete information and full rationality would not lead an appreciator to extinguish them as errors, and their presence often manifests understanding of the work.

5. A reader who returns to a tense rescue episode in a novel, remembering perfectly how the rescue ends, can still undergo genuine suspense as the climactic moment approaches, and this suspense is fitting.

6. A viewer who watches a stalking scene set in a house much like the viewers own street can feel genuine fear during the scene while feeling no inclination to intervene, to warn anyone, or to check the locks afterward, and both the persistence of the fear during viewing and the absence of those inclinations are rationally in good order. Phenomenological similarity to everyday fear is taken here to count strongly in favor of kind identity as claimed in 2.

Could you examine this package for mutual compatibility? I would like to know which elements create real difficulty for one another and how pressing each difficulty is compared with the others, which combinations that may initially seem problematic turn out to be compatible after careful analysis, and what each real incompatibility would require me to abandon or weaken and at what cost to the remainder of the position. Please arrange the difficulties from most to least fundamental and indicate which one alteration would do the most to render the rest coherent while sacrificing as little as possible of the original view.
