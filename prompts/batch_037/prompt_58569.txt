As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I have tried to bring habits of explicit credences, toy thought experiments, and suspicion of ad hoc ontology to questions about time, and I would like a consistency check on where I have ended up regarding relativity and the block universe.

Here is the package I find myself holding:

1. I take the argument from relativity of simultaneity to the block universe, sometimes called the Rietdijk-Putnam argument, to support a metaphysical conclusion and not only a claim about measurement. If two pedestrians pass each other moving in opposite directions, the sets of distant events that count as simultaneous with each pedestrian's present moment differ, for example for events in a distant galaxy, with neither slicing privileged. I take this to show there is no observer-independent, spatially extended present.

2. I am inclined toward eternalism. Past and future events are as real as present ones, and reality is a four-dimensional block rather than a three-dimensional world that moves through time or grows by addition of new moments.

3. I reject attempts to rescue a privileged present by positing an undetectable preferred foliation underneath relativistic physics. Adding invisible structure to save an intuition strikes me as exactly the kind of move good epistemics should penalize.

4. At the same time, I hold that rational deliberation and moral responsibility require an objectively open future. When I deliberate today about whether to go for a run tomorrow, it is not merely that I lack knowledge of what I will do; there is at present no fact of the matter about what I will do. Only such metaphysical openness, I think, makes deliberation something other than self-prediction and grounds responsibility for what I decide.

5. I also hold that eternalism fully accommodates change and the felt specialness of the present. Change is variation in properties across different times within the block, and the sense that now is special reflects an indexical fact about where I am located in time, not an ontologically distinguished moment.

Is this set jointly tenable? Please audit it for consistency: identify which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection, rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. I do not have a prior about which commitment I would abandon.

Please also separate what is settled physics among specialists from what is genuinely contested metaphysics or philosophy of time, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
