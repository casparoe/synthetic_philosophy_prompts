Compose the concluding document of a hypothetical adversarial collaboration between an aggregative utilitarian and a critic who presses the separateness of persons objection against utilitarianism.

Assume for purposes of the exercise that utilitarianism holds that a right act is one that maximizes total well-being across all persons, and that the objection holds that this treats distinct lives as if they were stages of a single life, thereby ignoring the moral significance of the boundaries between persons. The document should be written as if both parties have negotiated its wording and could jointly endorse it as a fair record.

To keep the discussion concrete and to prevent reliance on familiar textbook examples, base the collaboration around the following two stipulated cases, taking all numbers and background facts as given:

Case Alpha, intrapersonal versus interpersonal tradeoffs: In Intra, one person will live for eighty years; enduring a moderate chronic burden for the first forty years is causally necessary for that same person to enjoy a very large benefit in the final forty years, with a substantial net gain in lifetime well-being and with full foresight and endorsement in retrospect. In Inter, forty distinct persons each endure the same moderate chronic burden for their whole lives, which is causally necessary for forty other distinct persons to each enjoy the same very large benefit, with the same net gain in total well-being and with no compensation, consent, or special relations between the burdened and the benefited.

Case Beta, imposed risk with ex ante benefit: A municipal policy will expose 20,000 residents to an additional one in 2,000 risk of a severe one-year illness in order to secure a certain moderate improvement in daily life for all 20,000, including those at risk, such that each resident ex ante has positive expected well-being from the policy but ex post roughly ten residents will suffer the severe illness while the rest gain.

The document should contain four parts: a numbered list of substantive claims about aggregation, compensation, prudence, and risk that both parties accept; a precise formulation of their remaining disagreements, with each side stating its own position in language the other side accepts as non-caricatured; an identification of two to three cruxes, meaning subquestions on which further argument would rationally move at least one party, such as whether rational prudential tradeoffs within a life provide a legitimate model for moral tradeoffs across lives, whether ex ante expected benefit answers the separateness worry, and what moral work is done by the possibility of intrapersonal compensation; and for each crux, a description of an argument, distinction, or verdict about Case Alpha or Case Beta that both parties agree would be relevant, together with each party's recorded prediction about how that consideration will turn out.
