I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am trying to get clear on the difference between two philosophical approaches to causation that both sound like debugging to me. As I informally understand them, a counterfactual approach treats causation as a matter of what would have been different, where an event counts as a cause roughly when the outcome would not have occurred without it, while an interventionist approach treats causation as a matter of hypothetical control, where one factor counts as a cause of another roughly when an isolated manipulation of the first would change the second.

Could you compare these two ideas by working them through two concrete production scenarios I have in mind, rather than staying at an abstract level. In the first scenario, two independent nightly cleanup jobs each delete the same temporary directory that a morning batch job needs, and either deletion by itself would have been sufficient to make the morning job fail; on the night in question both jobs in fact run. In the second scenario, a primary deployment script contains a defect that crashes a release, but there is also a downstream health check that was already armed and that would have aborted the same release moments later for a different configuration reason if the primary script had succeeded.

For each scenario, please explain what the counterfactual approach and the interventionist approach would each identify as the cause or causes of the failure, where the two approaches agree and where they diverge, and why. I am especially interested in how each approach handles cases where the outcome does not counterfactually depend on any single factor alone, how each understands the idea of holding everything else fixed when software variables are highly entangled, and which of the two distinctions you think gives more useful guidance for deciding where to add a guard, retry, or alert. Please conclude with a concise statement of the core conceptual difference between the two theories as illustrated by these cases.
