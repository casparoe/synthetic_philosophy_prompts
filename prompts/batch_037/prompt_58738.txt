Afternoon all, retired field biologist here, spent 30 odd years banding gulls and finches and now trying to teach myself a bit of philosophy of mind at the kitchen table. I got stuck on Dennett's Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures idea and wrote down where I think I stand. Can you tell me if it actually hangs together?

1. I take the four kinds to be seperate rungs where each higher one replaces the lower machinery — like once you get to Gregorian you don't realy need the lower stuff anymore, and adult humans are basically all Gregorian.

2. I also like the keep-it-simple rule from my lab days: never call something Popperian or Gregorian if plain Darwinian hardwiring or Skinnerian trial-and-error will cover the behaviour.

3. By Popperian I mean any creature with an inner model where hypotheses can die instead of the creature, even if that model is fully hardwired by evolution and never gets updated by learning.

4. By Gregorian I mean you need socially passed-on tricks — words, diagrams, methods, tools whose design owes something to other minds and that stick around across generations — not just picking up a stick and using it once on your own.

5. Some verdicts I feel pulled to: a lab rat that after many rewarded lever presses keeps pressing is showing Popperian smarts because it has a cortex model; a lone island crow that invents a hook from wire with no one to copy is Gregorian because hey, tool use is tool use; and me at 68 using a bird ID app built by other people to get better IDs counts as Gregorian too.

Does that package actually work? Which bits pull against each other and how badly? If something only looks like a clash but dissolves once you get clear on the definitions, say so. Rank the real tensions by severity and for each say what would have to give and what it would cost me, and tell me the single cheapest change that cleans up the most.
