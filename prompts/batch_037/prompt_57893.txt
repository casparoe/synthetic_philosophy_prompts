I am a PhD student in political philosophy working on distributive justice, and I am trying to clarify the substantive disagreement between resource egalitarianism and the capability approach once adaptation and responsibility are both in play.

To fix ideas, I have been working with the following paired variations, both stipulated to involve no other relevant differences. In Variation One, Ana and Ben hold identical bundles of income and wealth. Ana lives with a stable physical impairment that makes standard forms of work and civic participation inaccessible without costly accommodation, though after long adaptation she reports high satisfaction with her life. Ben has no impairment but reports persistent dissatisfaction because a research and artistic vocation he deliberately cultivated, knowing it to be expensive, cannot be pursued adequately on his current share.

In Variation Two, the structure is the same except that background responsibility is equalized: assume both Ana's additional needs and Ben's project-related needs trace to informed choices made under fair background conditions, for instance Ana's refusal on religious grounds of a costless preventive intervention available to all, and Ben's choice of his vocation under the same conditions.

For each variation, articulate how a proponent of equality of resources and a proponent of equality of capabilities would respectively determine who is disadvantaged, if anyone, and what sort of compensatory claim follows. Then locate the precise source of any divergence between them, and evaluate what this paired comparison suggests about whether their dispute is fundamentally about the metric of advantage, about the proper handling of adaptive satisfaction, or about the weight given to prior choice.
