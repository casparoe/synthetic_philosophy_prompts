To inform policies on acquisition, attribution, and exhibition, set out the standards of adequacy for a philosophical account of why original works are taken to be more valuable than fakes, forgeries, and perceptually indistinguishable replicas. Take as stipulated the following variations the account must handle: (a) a deceptive imitation of a seventeenth-century portrait that matches the original in visual appearance and material composition yet was produced with intent to mislead, compared with an openly produced study replica with the same observable properties; (b) two posthumous bronze casts taken from a single surviving mould left by the artist, one authorized by the estate and one produced without authorization but with identical process and alloy; (c) a venerated wooden panel that through centuries of consolidation and inpainting retains almost none of its initial matter, compared with a newly constructed atom-by-atom duplicate that has never been restored. Give your answer as exactly five numbered items with no introduction or conclusion, each item limited to one or two sentences and stating one requirement together with the consideration that motivates it. Within each item, indicate whether the requirement is non-negotiable or may be relaxed for certain purposes, and where two requirements appear to pull against one another, note the tension in the relevant item.
