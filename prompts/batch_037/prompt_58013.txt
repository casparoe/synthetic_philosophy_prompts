I am a philosophy professor serving on my university's Student Data Governance Board, which must decide next week on a proposed student-success system that would merge residence-hall entry logs, library borrowing records, learning-management activity, and voluntary counseling visits into a single engagement score visible to advisers, retained for seven years, with no general opt-out. Supporters present the system as making support transparent and auditable and propose attaching modest perks such as priority registration to high scores; opponents argue that data collected to provide a service may not be repurposed to monitor and rank students without their control over that reuse. Please draft a concise decision memo I can circulate that recommends approval, rejection, or approval with enforceable conditions, gives principled reasons concerning when an institution entrusted with personal information may observe and evaluate those in its charge, and states the strongest consideration on the losing side fairly before explaining why it does not prevail.
