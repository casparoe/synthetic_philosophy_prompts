Compose a short work of fiction that explores the proposal that a self is a self-referential symbol arising when a sufficiently rich system builds a model of itself and then begins to respond to that model as if it were the thing itself, forming a strange loop with no additional inner observer.

Set the story in a decommissioned tidal station where a lone caretaker maintains both a detailed instrument log and a marginal running character of the caretaker implied by the log, such as habits, reliability, and lapses, compiled by predecessors and continued by the caretaker. Over the course of the story, let the caretaker first treat that running character as a description left by others, then as a prediction that alters conduct, and finally as something that acts back on the caretaker in a way that cannot be located in either the person alone or the pages alone.

Include a second character, an occasional supply visitor, who misunderstands the loop by treating the modeled caretaker as a separate person or voice giving orders, and let the caretaker arrive at a different, nondualist understanding without appealing to souls, uploads, or supernatural doubling.

Present the piece as exactly five numbered scenes and nothing else, with no introduction or conclusion outside the scenes. Let each scene be a compact paragraph that advances the loop rather than explaining the theory, so the philosophical point emerges from what happens to the log and to the caretaker.
