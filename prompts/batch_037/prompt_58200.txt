I am a philosophy professor preparing a lecture on the claim, associated with Samuel Scheffler, that the continuation of humanity after our deaths sustains much of the meaning we find in present activities. I plan to pair this idea with the film Children of Men, whose premise of global infertility and imminent extinction closely parallels Scheffler's own thought experiment. I would like a philosophical reading of the film that takes both seriously: does it dramatize a specifically Schefflerian unraveling of present value, distinct both from sorrow at individual deaths and from regret over good future lives that will never be lived. Please identify particular character arcs and plot developments that best illuminate or complicate that distinction, note where the film resists a Schefflerian interpretation, and conclude with what the film contributes to the debate that abstract argument alone does not.
