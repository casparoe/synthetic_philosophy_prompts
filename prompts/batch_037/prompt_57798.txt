I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am reporting a feature on how ordinary institutions handle the possibility that insects might have some form of sentience. A case I keep returning to is the pest and wildlife policy of a large public university I visited: its sustainability office maintains no-mow pollinator zones to protect bees, prohibits glue boards for mice on the explicit grounds that they cause prolonged suffering, and requires humane endpoints for rodents in labs, yet the same institution authorizes dining-hall electric zappers, routine drain treatments, and on-call ant, fly, and wasp extermination that together kill hundreds of thousands to perhaps millions of insects per year with no welfare assessment at all, not even a comparison of faster versus slower methods or a threshold for when action is justified.

What puzzles me is how to read the implicit theory behind that split pattern, especially in light of an idea several researchers raised with me that I want to put to you as a working hypothesis rather than established fact: that even a very low credence, say one percent or one tenth of one percent, that a fly or ant is sentient could still matter enormously in expectation when multiplied across such vast numbers, potentially outweighing the suffering of the much smaller number of mammals the policy protects so carefully. Act as a professional philosopher with expertise in animal ethics and decision theory, and help me reconstruct what the campus practice presupposes in order to make sense. Please begin with brief brainstorming that maps the live options, then think carefully step by step: does the practice strictly presuppose that insects are non-sentient, or could it also fit a view that grants a small probability of sentience but discounts it to zero, treats numbers as irrelevant, or treats practicability as decisive, and how would you distinguish what is strictly presupposed from what merely fits well? Say whether the bee-protection, mouse-protection, and fly-elimination parts presuppose consistent views of sentience, moral weight, and expected value, identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined so that several rival theories could license it, and spell out concretely what would have to change in monitoring, method choice, or thresholds for intervention if the institution instead took the vast-numbers-against-low-probability consideration seriously.
