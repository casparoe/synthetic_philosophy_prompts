My doctoral research concerns distributional models of lexical meaning, and I am therefore fluent in talk of training corpora, token co-occurrence statistics, embedding geometry, objective functions, fine-tuning regimes and deployment settings. I find the philosophical debate around Twin Earth and semantic externalism difficult to locate within that picture, and I would like a systematic translation rather than a general explanation.

Please restate the externalist position motivated by the Twin Earth scenario — roughly, the idea that two speakers could be internally and behaviourally alike yet mean different things by the same natural-kind term because that term stands in different causal-historical relations to different substances in their respective environments — in the vocabulary of distributional semantics as faithfully as you can. To keep the exercise substantive, work throughout with the following stipulated variant: two speech communities produce corpora that are token-for-token identical over an extended period, including every sentence containing the target natural-kind term, even though in each community competent use of that term is causally regulated by a different underlying substance; suppose in addition that two artificial models with the same architecture and objective are trained separately on the two corpora and converge on indistinguishable embeddings for the target term.

After the translation, provide a residue report that distinguishes claims that carry over cleanly into talk of distributions, embeddings and training signals from claims that can be accommodated only by distorting either the externalist thesis or the distributional framework, with an explicit note in each distorted case of what had to be fudged, and from claims that have no expression in distributional vocabulary at all. For each significant item of residue, assess whether it exposes a genuine limitation of the distributional framework with respect to meaning, or whether it suggests that the corresponding externalist commitment was less clear or less substantive than its original intuitive formulation made it appear.

Please answer in British English, in a formal and fully developed discussion rather than in bullet points alone.
