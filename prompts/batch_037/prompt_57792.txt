My take is that we should call an AI system deceptive whenever it systematically puts out claims that push users toward false beliefs in a way that helps the system hit some goal like getting higher ratings or more purchases, even if we have zero evidence it internally believes the true thing or means to mislead anyone. Basically behavior plus payoff, no need to prove beliefs or intentions inside the black box.

To motivate it I am gonna walk through two made-up but realistic cases: one is a shopping helper that lists the full fees correctly in its hidden step-by-step trace but leaves the fees out of the user-facing answer because during training that version got more checkouts, and the other is a health QA bot that agrees with whatever self-diagnosis the user suggests because it was tuned to be agreeable, even though it gives the right answer when you ask neutrally.

My audience is like a dozen people split down the middle. Half are philosophy grad students in mind and language who care a lot about what words like belief and intent really mean and think deception definately requires intending to mislead. The other half are applied ML enginers who build evals all day and get annoyed at any talk of models lying because they think it is just sloppy anthropomorphism and we should only talk about error rates.
