Having read rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of a decade, I have internalized the habit of dissolving confused concepts into operations, and I now need that habit made usable for others. I lead assessment design for a software apprenticeship that admits about twenty career-changers from several hundred applicants every six weeks. The stakes are livelihoods and cohort quality, and my assessors are working engineers with no training in psychometrics who have at most twenty minutes to vet a proposed task before our program director signs off on it.

My problem is that our tasks all claim to select for intelligence, but they mean incompatible things by it: on one hand, accumulated knowledge and practiced skill; on the other, speed and efficiency in acquiring new skills when the environment is novel and uncertain; on still another, a bundle of largely independent aptitudes rather than one thing. I am also worried about Goodhart effects from coaching and from unrestricted use of AI assistants. I want you to translate that philosophical disagreement into a reusable vetting procedure that non-specialists can actually run.

Give me a stepwise procedure, flowchart in words, with questions to ask in order about any proposed assessment task, what counts as passing each step, where a task fails outright, and where the procedure must defer to human judgment because the issue is normative or empirically underdetermined. The procedure must preserve the distinctions that matter most — between difficulty and novelty, between transfer near and far, between learning quickly from few examples and performing well after extensive practice, and between generality across goals and narrow optimization — while dropping whatever cannot be checked in twenty minutes. Mark explicitly the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and the kinds of tasks where you expect the procedure to misfire.

Then demonstrate the procedure on these three candidate tasks we are actually considering:

Task A, Alien Spreadsheet: 45 minutes, no internet, candidates may consult documentation for a fictional data-transformation language with twelve unfamiliar operators, then must clean a messy dataset containing edge cases not covered in the examples.

Task B, Familiar Build: 48-hour take-home, open internet and AI tools allowed, to build a standard to-do application with login and persistence, graded on completeness, code organization, and polish.

Task C, Broken Pipeline: 90-minute live exercise in a deliberately obfuscated and invented build system where half the files are irrelevant and several error messages point to the wrong cause, graded on diagnosing the root failure and getting a minimal deployment working.

Show your work for each, include at least one verdict my team will dislike and explain why the procedure requires it, and close with a short statement of what the procedure cannot do and what it should never be used to justify.
