I am preparing a short statement of my position for an interdisciplinary workshop on neural uploading and brain preservation, where I plan to defend a broadly Parfitian view about teletransportation and what matters in survival. Before I circulate it, I would like an audit of whether the pieces actually cohere. I take myself to hold all of the following:

(1) Reductionism about identity: facts about personal identity consist in facts about psychological continuity and connectedness, sustained by the right kind of causal process, together with the underlying brain and bodily facts, with no further fact beyond them.

(2) Destructive teletransportation, in which a scanner records my complete psychological profile, my body is destroyed, and a replica with my memories, character traits, and intentions is constructed elsewhere, preserves everything that matters in survival, so that dreading it as if it were death would be irrational.

(3) Hemisphere fission, in which my two cerebral hemispheres are separated and each is transplanted into a new body, with each resulting person fully psychologically continuous with me, yields double survival rather than death, and it is rational beforehand to care about both successors equally.

(4) Non-destructive teletransportation, in which scanning leaves my original body alive and conscious while also constructing a psychologically continuous replica elsewhere: I go with the original, the replica is a distinct new person, and rational first-person anticipation should follow the original alone, with only third-person sympathetic concern owed to the replica.

(5) A double-replica variant suggested by a collaborator, in which a single scan produces two simultaneous replicas on different planets while the original is destroyed, each fully psychologically continuous with me: this should be regarded as death, or at least as substantially worse than single-replica destructive teletransportation, because divided branching defeats the unity required for onward self-concern, so I should anticipate neither future from the inside.

Could you work through this set and assess its joint tenability. Please identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection, order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each explain what would have to give in order to restore consistency and what that revision would cost for the rest of the package. I am especially interested in whether my verdicts on the three teletransportation and fission cases can all be sustained together with my general account of identity and survival, and if not, which single revision would do the most work.
