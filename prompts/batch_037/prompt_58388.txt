My friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether welfare on fish and shrimp farms is just healthy, fast-growing stock or whether it is about how life in the tanks and ponds feels to the animals themselves when we are not even sure how far their sentience goes. I want to put my side of that dispute to work. I act as welfare lead for a cooperative of rainbow trout and whiteleg shrimp farms, making brief weekly walk-through audits of about thirty minutes with shed staff who have no training in animal sentience, able only to pass a practice, require a recheck, or flag it for a final call by the farm manager. Please convert the experiential understanding of welfare combined with precaution under uncertainty about sentience into an ordered audit routine usable under those limits, stating what to assess in what sequence, what observable thresholds trigger each outcome, and where the routine must stop treating the issue as factual and record it as a value judgment. Then show how the routine handles three live disputes: autumn grading of trout by crowding, netting and brief air exposure with strong growth but split fins and frantic thrashing; pre-sort chilling of shrimp in ice slurry with low mortality but prolonged vigorous tail flicking; and high-density holding of shrimp that keeps our margins viable, which I favour, but with frequent antenna loss and sluggish feeding. Close by stating plainly what such a routine cannot do and where it is most likely to misfire.
