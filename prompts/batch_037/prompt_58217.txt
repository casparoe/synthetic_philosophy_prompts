After four decades in experimental physical chemistry, I have retired and taken up philosophy as a serious second pursuit. I find that I still think like an instrumentalist: a reading is of interest only insofar as it tracks a stable underlying quantity and guides intervention.

Consider the familiar annual exercise in which nations are ranked by the national mean answer to a single life-evaluation item asking each respondent to place present life on a ladder running from the worst possible to the best possible life. Philosophers dispute whether such rankings measure happiness and whether a government should aim to ascend them.

To fix ideas, consider the following claim, call it H: whenever one nation stands at least five places above another in such a ranking in a given year, adults in the higher-ranked nation on average undergo more pleasant conscious experience, moment to moment, over the weeks surrounding the survey than adults in the lower-ranked nation.

I would like a disciplined estimate of the truth of H, not a survey of literatures. Please assign an overall credence to H and exhibit its derivation from at least four subsidiary judgments, for example concerning whether respondents report candidly and with a common understanding of the scale, whether a global evaluation of a life covaries reliably with day-to-day affect within individuals, whether cultural norms of modesty, optimism, or choice of reference point distort cross-national comparison, and whether sampling and timing variation is small relative to a five-place gap. Give a rough numerical value for each subsidiary judgment with a sentence of justification, show how they combine to yield the overall figure, and identify the subsidiary judgment to which the overall figure is most sensitive.

Then extend the analysis to policy by means of two stylized instruments available to a mid-ranked government with a fixed budget: provision of neighbourhood evening facilities intended to increase sociable leisure, which small pilots suggest improves end-of-day mood reports while leaving ladder scores largely unchanged, and a public communications effort that encourages citizens to view national progress favourably, which pilots suggest raises ladder scores while leaving end-of-day mood largely unchanged. State which conception of well-being would have to be correct for pursuit of rank to be defensible in light of this contrast, give your conditional credence that rank-seeking improves genuine well-being given your credence in H, and describe the single feasible observation or experiment that would move your overall credence most.
