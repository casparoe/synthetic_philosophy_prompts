So I work part-time for our campus dining and wellness outreach team and I got into this endlessly looping arguement with two friends that we cannot get out of, about manipulation versus legit persuasion. One of them thinks the whole distinction comes down to whether you bypass someones reasoning, like defaults, framing, emotional jolts, friction, that kind of stuff, so if people are not consciously weighing reasons then it is manipulative even if everything is out in the open. The other thinks it is all about hiding what you are doing or why, so if the person would feel tricked learning how the trick worked or would not agree to being influenced that way, that is the problem, even if they thought it through at the time.

What keeps us stuck is we keep throwing the same three cases at each other and reading them totally differently. Like moving fruit to eye level and putting desserts behind the counter in the dining hall, versus our running app Pulse that learns your slang and texts you a guilt-trippy nudge in your own voice when you skip a morning run, versus a club canvasser who only tells true stories about scholarship kids who made it while leaving out the dropout numbers. We all agree on what each tactic does and that they work and that outright lying would be wrong, but we still fight about which ones are manipulation and whether that label is about autonomy or about trust. My hunch is we might be answering different questions under one name and not noticing, but I am not sure. Can you help diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, using the actual positions I described, and spell out what, if anything, would actually move us forward, like a distinction we are missing, a test case that would separate the views, or a reason to think no progress should be expected.
