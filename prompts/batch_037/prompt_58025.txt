I'm a backend dev with zero philosophy training and I got stuck at lunch yesterday while two teammates argued about that yearly world happiness ranking thing and I definately couldn't tell who was winning.

Maya kicked it off by saying the ranking doesn't even measure happiness. She said as far as she understands it's mostly one big hypothetical question where you rate your life on a ladder from worst to best possible, so it's really about how people judge their life when they stop and think, not whether they actually feel happy or stressed or laugh a lot day to day. So calling the top country the happiest is misleading.

Jonas said that difference doesn't bug him. He said moment-to-moment mood is super noisy, like if you ask about yesterday it just depends if you slept bad or had a fight that morning, while the ladder judgment is more stable and it's actually what governments should care about anyway. Like we want lives people endorse on reflection, not just more giggles per hour.

Maya came back with two worries. First, even if you want life judgments, you can't compare them across countries because expectations and norms mess with answers, like some cultures are more stoic or don't want to brag or complain, plus words for a best possible life might land differently. Second, she said making topping the list a national goal is a classic Goodhart thing, we see this with metrics at work all the time. Once politicians get judged on the score they'll run PR campaigns to nudge the number instead of fixing stuff, coaching people to give tens.

Jonas said that proves too much. He said unemployment and GDP and bug counts can all be gamed too, but we don't throw out dashboards because of it, we audit and keep using them. And he said if millions of people say yeah my life is an 8 or 9, it's kind of paternalistic for Maya to say no you're not really happy, you're just confused by culture. Who else gets to decide if not them. Then he threw out this weird case, like what if a government gave everyone a free headset that does nothing except make them answer 10 on the ladder, would Maya count that as winning, and what about the reverse, a pill that makes every day feel great but doesn't change how people rate the ladder, which one should count as more happiness.
