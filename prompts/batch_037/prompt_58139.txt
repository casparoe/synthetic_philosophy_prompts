I am the principal of an international secondary school addressing our graduating class of eighty students from thirty-one countries, together with their families and teachers, at commencement. I want to honor how varied upbringings shaped this class, including our tradition in which each graduate offers a saying from home, and to acknowledge two recent frictions when well-meant remarks about diligence and hospitality gave offense, without implying that nationality fixes character or retreating to the claim that background is irrelevant. Please write a five-minute address in which the question of whether generalizations about peoples can be coherent and when they are acceptable does real work in determining what may and may not be said, so that it resonates both with relatives who speak proudly in such generalities and with staff who distrust them. Begin with the address itself, with no preface or commentary.
