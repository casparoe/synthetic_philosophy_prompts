I do not have an academic background in philosophy, but I have spent the last several months attending city council meetings about a proposal to change our local election rules, and I have been turning the arguments over on my own since.

The council is considering three measures as a single package: first, making voting in city elections compulsory for those already eligible, with a modest fine of about twenty dollars that would be excused for illness, shift work, or travel, and with every ballot including a formal option to leave all races unmarked; second, lowering the eligibility age to sixteen for mayoral and council races; third, allowing residents who do not hold citizenship but have lived in the city continuously for at least three years to vote in school board elections. My own tentative sense is that there is about a two-in-three chance that adopting all three together would be morally just, but I do not trust that sense because I cannot tell which part of my thinking is doing the work.

I would like you to give your own numerical estimate, between zero and one, of the likelihood that it would be morally just to adopt the three measures together, and to show the reasoning that produces it. Please break that overall judgment into the smaller judgments it depends on, such as whether voting is more like a personal liberty that includes the liberty not to participate or more like a shared duty where a small penalty plus a blank-ballot option still respects conscience, whether most sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds have the steadiness and understanding to vote responsibly in local contests, whether several years of residence, paying local taxes, and having children in local schools gives a person a moral claim to a local vote apart from citizenship, and whether compulsion would genuinely make outcomes fairer or would mainly add resentful or random ballots. Assign a rough likelihood to each component, explain in ordinary arithmetic how they combine into your overall figure, and indicate which component your final figure is most sensitive to. To close, describe what sort of practical observation about how such rules work in practice, or what sort of moral consideration I may have missed, would most plausibly move your estimate up or down.
