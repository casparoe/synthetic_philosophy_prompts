For an introductory seminar in philosophy of mind, develop an original, ordinary-life case intended to isolate a candidate instance of cognitive penetration of perceptual experience. The case should involve two observers with equivalent vision and viewing conditions who look at the same object or event with the same distribution of attention and for the same duration, differing only in a background evaluative or categorical commitment that does not itself bring distinctive visual imagery. Keep the setting everyday, allow no brain injury, pharmacological influence, or technological intervention, and present the narrative in a single paragraph. Then demonstrate that each stipulation is satisfied, explain why the difference in how the scene looks to each observer cannot be reduced to inattentiveness or to a difference only in subsequent judgment and report, identify which aspect of the design does the decisive work and how the case collapses if it is removed, and anticipate the strongest response available to a defender of strong perceptual encapsulation. The verdicts should follow from the details of the case rather than being stipulated.
