I am a high school junior taking an elective on ethics and public policy, and I have chosen to write my term paper on how my school handles student mental health.

Two parts of our current system puzzle me. First, to receive accommodations such as extra time on exams, testing in a separate quiet room, or excused absences related to attention or anxiety or mood difficulties, a student must submit a letter from an outside clinician that includes a formal diagnostic code. The front office keeps a confidential roster of these codes that is shared with teachers before exam periods, and each spring the administration adds up how many students fall under each code to decide how many counselors to hire and how to divide funding for support programs. A code generally stays on a students record until a new evaluation removes it, which most families only seek every two or three years. Second, our required wellness curriculum and our peer support club teach the opposite lesson in tone: they present diagnostic categories as paperwork tools for the office, encourage students not to let a category shape how they understand themselves, and ask participants in group discussions to describe experiences and needs in ordinary language without sorting each other into diagnostic groups.

My teacher mentioned the idea that classifications of people can sometimes loop back and change the people being classified, so that being labeled, expecting certain symptoms, being treated in a certain way by teachers and peers, and interpreting ones own behavior through the label can alter behavior and experience in ways that then affect whether the label still fits and how the category itself is understood over time.

For my paper I want to understand what picture of mental health categories would make our schools procedures intelligible. Does the reliance on outside codes, the sharing of rosters with teachers, and the year-to-year counting for staffing make sense only if these categories are taken to be stable features that exist independently of being labeled, or could the same procedures also make sense on a more interactive picture where labeling can shift experience and behavior? Are the accommodation and counting procedures on the one hand and the wellness messaging on the other committed to incompatible pictures, or is there a single picture that can render both intelligible? Please separate what the procedures strictly require in order to be intelligible from what they merely sit comfortably with, and identify places where the procedures are underdetermined so that different views could keep them in place. Finally, explain in concrete terms how the requirements for documentation, the communication with teachers, and the counting for staffing would need to be redesigned if the school decided to take the looping possibility seriously.
