I coordinate the history curriculum for a network of secondary schools and chair a panel that meets each term to revise how morally contested figures appear in lesson openers, wall displays, and assembly scripts. We typically decide five or six cases per term under tight meeting time, the classroom teachers who must apply our guidance have no training in philosophy, any wording must be signed off by head teachers and defended in meetings with parents who disagree strongly with each other, and while nothing we do carries legal risk, the formative stakes for pupils and the reputational stakes for the schools are high. A recent difficult evening with parents left us divided between treating a past moral outlook as explanatory context and treating it as exoneration, and between noting that many people at the time accepted an injustice and asking whether informed dissent was available and at what cost.

Please translate that debate into a reusable step by step procedure my panel can run without philosophical supervision, stated as ordered tests with clear pass conditions, stopping points that require revision of a celebratory framing, and explicit flags for points where factual inquiry ends and a value judgment by the panel must take over. The procedure should preserve the distinctions that matter most, especially between explaining an action by prevailing norms and excusing it, between the mere existence of a norm and its moral authority for a person in a position of power and benefit, and between acknowledging a historical contribution and endorsing the person as an exemplar, while setting aside theoretical refinements that would not survive classroom use, and it should be candid about where it is likely to misfire. Then demonstrate the procedure on three live cases we face this term, namely a Year 9 lesson opener that presents Jefferson both as an enslaver and as an advocate of equality ideals, a corridor display that celebrates Churchill for wartime leadership with no mention of his positions on empire and race, and a primary assembly script that introduces Columbus as a model explorer, including one demonstration where the outcome cuts against a natural inclination to retain a purely celebratory framing of wartime leadership for the sake of pupil pride. End with a brief account of what the procedure cannot decide. Please begin the response with the procedure itself, with no preface, commentary, or restatement of the request.
