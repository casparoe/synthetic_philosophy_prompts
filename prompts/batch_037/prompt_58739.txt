I spend much of my workday building dashboards for a regional parcel network, and on weekends I help coordinate a small literacy tutoring scheme, and the same puzzling pattern has surfaced in both settings. In the parcel network, driver performance is summarized by an on time score derived from handheld scanners, and everyone knows the scanners misfire in certain concrete stairwells and rural dead zones, marking some late stops as on time and some timely stops as late. What surprises me is not that drivers try to improve the score, but that dispatchers, who often know from radio logs and customer calls which readings are mistaken, leave the mistaken readings uncorrected and even base staffing and route assignments on them, because any manual correction triggers an audit trail and can look like favoritism, while letting the system number stand is never questioned.

In the tutoring scheme, children are assigned books by a short reading test, and tutors can usually tell when a low score reflects illness or distraction rather than ability, yet they frequently assign the too easy book indicated by the score anyway. When I ask why, the answer is not that they trust the test, but that if a child struggles with a harder book chosen by personal judgment, the tutor will be seen as having ignored the data, whereas boredom with an easy book is treated as the fault of the data and carries no blame. So in both cases the measure is not being inflated in order to look good, it is being deferred to as a shield even when those using it believe it is wrong in the particular case, and the deference itself then shapes future data about performance and progress.

I am curious whether researchers concerned with measurement and incentives have isolated this pattern under a standard heading. I have a vague sense it sits near the familiar idea that indicators deteriorate once they become targets, but the mechanism here seems reversed, since the aim is blamelessness rather than higher scores. I would like more than a label, namely an account of what the phenomenon consists in, how it differs from active gaming of a measure on one side and from ordinary reliance on automation or defensive caution on the other, where the boundary lies between reasonable insistence on consistent rules and abdication of judgment, and what kinds of cases would count as counterexamples. If I am running together distinct mechanisms, or if the distinction is not useful, please say so directly rather than offering a tidy definition for the sake of it, and indicate where your analysis is most tentative.
