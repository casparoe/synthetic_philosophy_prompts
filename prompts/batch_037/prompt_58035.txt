Our port city's heritage trust stewards a late-1800s wooden pilot schooner that has been repaired plank by plank for more than sixty years, so that today less than a fifth of its timber dates to its working life, and I serve as vice-chair of the collections committee that must settle its status before next month's registry filing. The conservation team now proposes removing and replacing the last original structural timbers to keep the vessel seaworthy for the annual harbor festival, an outcome favored by the donor family and by local sailors who view active sailing as central to what the boat is, while reassembling the decades of retired original planks and frames held in our warehouse into a complete but non-seaworthy hull for indoor display, an outcome favored by historians who view material continuity as central to authenticity. Rival factions insist that only one hull can be designated as the historic vessel for purposes of insurance, grant eligibility, and ceremonial use, and our charter speaks broadly of preserving historical integrity together with continued community function without specifying how to balance those aims when they diverge.

Please draft a concise decision memorandum of roughly 700 to 900 words that the committee could circulate and vote on, recommending a specific designation and maintenance plan with a concrete timeline for implementation and review. The memorandum should give principled reasons the losing side could recognize as responsive, including sustained engagement with the strongest consideration against your recommendation, explain how to apply the charter language to this and relevantly similar cases of gradual replacement versus reassembly, and close with an actionable bottom line stating what is to be designated, conserved, sailed, or displayed. Please write your response in American English.
