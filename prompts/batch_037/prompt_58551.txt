So I got into a dumb 2am debate with my cousin who does pest control for farms and now I can't shake it. He basically trapped me into saying we should care more about bugs than people and that can't be right.

He laid it out like this:

1. If there is a real, non-crackpot chance a creature can feel pain, you have to count that in your decisions, discounted by the chance. You don't get to round a 5% chance of hurting someone down to zero.
2. For insects like bees, ants, fruit flies, that chance isn't zero. They have sensors for damage, they learn to avoid stuff that hurt them before, bees will even pass up a good sugar reward to avoid getting zapped with heat and stuff like that, so a lot of researchers take insect pain seriously enough that even to be super conservative you should put the odds at like 1%.
3. But the numbers are stupid big. One normal spray of a cornfield can wipe out tens of millions of rootworms and aphids in an afternoon, and globally there are way more insects alive at once than mammals or birds.
4. Even if you say one insect's pain counts for way less than one human's — like divide by a thousand for smaller brain, shorter life, whatever — the math still explodes. 1% times millions times even a tiny weight still swamps one farmer losing part of a harvest or me getting an annoying bite.
5. So my cousin should never do the cheap spray when there is a slightly gentler method, even if the gentler one costs the farmer thousends extra and pushes up food prices, and I should donate to make grain silos kill weevils more painlessly before I donate to a human food bank, and I definitely shouldn't swat mosquitos just because they're annoying.
6. But that's insane. Nobody lives like that, and it makes normal human suffering seem almost irrelevant.

So where does that thing break? I really don't want to just pretend insects definately feel nothing because that feels dishonest, but I also don't want to bite the bullet that preventing bug deaths matters more than helping people. Which step would you reject and what does it cost to reject it?
