I am writing an essay on Les Misérables and would value a philosophical reading of Bishop Myriel's behaviour in misleading the gendarmes in order to shield Jean Valjean, viewed in the light of Augustine's doctrine that lying remains wrong even when it prevents grave harm. Where does that Augustinian framework illuminate the bishop's choice, and where does Hugo's presentation of the deception as saintly challenge or complicate Augustine? Please consider whether silence or an evasive but truthful reply could have preserved both charity and honesty, remain fair to both the novel and the moral theory whilst noting where the novel resists the Augustinian verdict, and conclude with what the episode contributes to the ethics of lying that abstract argument alone misses.
