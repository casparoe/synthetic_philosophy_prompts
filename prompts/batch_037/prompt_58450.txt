I have no training in philosophy and I have been thinking this over on my own since someone told me an old story. In the story a woman is told that Death has already written down whether she will be in Damascus or in Aleppo tomorrow, and Death will be waiting in that city. Death is almost never wrong about people. The woman lives in Damascus. She can either stay home or pay for an exhausting overnight bus to Aleppo that she dreads. More than anything she wants to live, but among living outcomes she would much rather live at home than live on the road, and among dying outcomes she would much rather die at home than die on the road.

I tried to write out what seemed like plain common sense, but it leads somewhere I cannot accept:

1. Where Death will be tomorrow is already fixed and cannot be changed by what the woman decides tonight.
2. Since she cannot affect where Death will be, she should simply go to the city where Death is less likely to be.
3. Her best clue about where Death is, is what she finds herself leaning toward doing, because Death is almost always right and therefore likely to have written down whatever she will in fact do.
4. So if she notices she is leaning toward staying in Damascus, that is strong reason to believe Death is in Damascus, and she should take the bus to Aleppo.
5. And if she notices she is leaning toward taking the bus, that is strong reason to believe Death is in Aleppo, and she should stay in Damascus.
6. So whatever she finds herself leaning toward doing, she should do the opposite.
7. But the same pattern seems to hold whenever someone who knows her very well has already made a firm prediction about a small choice of hers, such as her sister predicting she will skip a difficult family visit, or her doctor predicting she will give up on an exercise plan. In each case her own leaning is good evidence about what that reliable predictor said.
8. So in all those ordinary cases she should also always do the opposite of what she is leaning toward, simply in order to defy the prediction.

Step 8 is clearly absurd. In ordinary life I rely on my leanings all the time and I do not try to prove my sister or my doctor wrong just because they know me well.

Please tell me which step or steps I should give up in order to avoid the absurd ending, and explain why that step looked so plausible at first. I would especially like to keep the ideas in 1 and 2 if I can, that the past is fixed and that she should go where Death is less likely to be, but I want to understand what keeping them would force me to accept about evidence and about making up my mind, and what giving them up would cost instead.
