As a third-year PhD student in moral philosophy specializing in theories of well-being, I am applying for an eight-week summer fellowship to support a dissertation chapter on what intuitions about fully simulated lives can establish about prudential value.

For purposes of the proposal, take as background the familiar thought experiment in which a device could supply a person with a complete lifetime of subjectively seamless and highly pleasant experiences at the cost of severing causal contact with the actual world, and the common report that one would decline to connect permanently. That refusal is often treated as evidence against the view that well-being consists only in the quality of experience, but it is compatible with several competing explanations, including a concern to remain in contact with reality, a concern to be the genuine author of one's actions and relationships, and a concern that one's life satisfy the preferences one would have if fully informed.

My half-formed idea is that progress can be made not by revisiting that single choice but by developing a small set of paired contrasts designed to pull those explanations apart. One contrast varies whether the inactive body left behind in the real world is simply maintained or is put to socially useful work that the subject never experiences from the inside. A second contrast varies whether the subject inside the simulation retains continuous awareness that the experiences are simulated or undergoes erasure of that knowledge. A third varies whether the simulated life is hedonically excellent but narratively fixed in advance or hedonically mixed but responsive to the subject's ongoing choices inside the simulation.

Please draft a proposal of approximately 900 words that I could submit with the fellowship application. It should formulate a single central question precise enough to be adjudicated by these contrasts, explain why the standard discussion of simulated versus real lives leaves that question open, lay out a week-by-week plan of conceptual work feasible in eight weeks and explain why systematic development and comparison of such variants is more illuminating than either collecting further brute intuitions about the original case or proceeding by abstract analysis of well-being concepts alone, specify what a successful result would show and what outcome would indicate that the strategy has failed, and close with a candid assessment of scope risks and how they would be contained. I will supply all scholarly references myself, so the proposal should engage positions and arguments directly without attempting a literature survey or citation apparatus, and it should be written to be self-contained for a general philosophical reader.
