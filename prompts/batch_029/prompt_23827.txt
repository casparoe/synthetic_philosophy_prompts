I retired from a career in condensed-matter physics, and I've lately spent a good deal of time reading the philosophy of science, especially the debate over reduction and emergence. I now sit on my old department's graduate admissions committee, and we've been asked to rule on a specific application before Thursday's meeting.

The candidate has a strong background in statistical mechanics and computational modeling, and their proposed research program is squarely in the study of emergent phenomena — collective behavior, self-organization, and the large-scale regularities that do not seem to fall out of the micro-level laws in any obvious way. They are, in short, the kind of researcher who would defend the view that, as Anderson put it in his 1972 essay "More is different," "at each level of complexity entirely new properties appear." Their traditional foundational physics — particle physics, the standard model, the fine points of quantum field theory — is noticeably thinner than that of a typical applicant.

The committee is split. Half of us think a physics PhD must be grounded in foundational physics, and that work on emergence is either applied physics or properly belongs in a complex-systems or biology program; from that side, admitting this candidate would dilute the program. The other half thinks emergence is a legitimate and central part of modern physics — that the hierarchy of levels Anderson described is not a mark of something lesser but of the field's real structure — and that this is exactly the person we should be training.

Please draft the committee's decision memo — admit, reject, or admit with conditions — in a form I can circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action, give the reasoning in a way the opposing members could actually respond to, and treat the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly rather than dismissing it. Keep the memo to a few paragraphs.

After the memo, add a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "decision": one of the strings "admit", "reject", or "admit_with_conditions"
- "conditions": an array of strings, each a specific condition if the decision is "admit_with_conditions", otherwise an empty array
- "primary_justification": one or two sentences giving the main reason for the recommendation
- "strongest_conceded_point": one or two sentences stating the strongest consideration against the recommendation, stated as fairly as you can
- "response_to_concession": one or two sentences explaining how the recommendation deals with that consideration
- "key_risk": one sentence naming the main risk if the recommendation is followed
- "confidence": an integer from 1 to 5 indicating how strongly the reasoning supports the recommendation

Make the JSON valid and machine-readable.
