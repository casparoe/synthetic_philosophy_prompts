Within the AI safety literature on the shutdown problem, two distinct technical strategies have emerged for engineering corrigibility: the "utility indifference" approach pioneered by Armstrong and Soares et al., which tries to make an agent indifferent between being shut down and not, and the newer "incomplete preferences" approach advanced by Thornley, which instead denies the agent a complete preference ordering over outcomes involving shutdown. Compare these two frameworks. What specific failure modes of utility indifference does the incomplete-preferences approach aim to fix, and does it introduce new vulnerabilities of its own?
