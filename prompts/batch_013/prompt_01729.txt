Ok, this is something I don't think I've ever seen properly untangled: is DNA actually a code -- like morse code or a cipher -- or has that word just stuck around as a convenient metaphor that doesn't really hold up under pressure? The codon-to-amino-acid mapping seems like a genuinely arbitrary lookup table, so that part feels like a real code to me. But then everyone (scientists included) casually says DNA 'encodes' eye color, height, behavior, whatever. I've seen some philosophy-of-biology types push back hard on that -- something about how a purely correlational notion of 'information' would mean your diet, or the temperature you developed at, also carries information about your phenotype. So why should genes get to be uniquely special just because their alphabet is discrete? Is there an actual principled argument that genes really are privileged carriers of developmental information, and its not just 'one causal factor among many that we happen to talk about differently for historical reasons'? Genuinely want the steelman of both sides here, not a 'well its a useful metaphor' shrug.
