Hey, I've been down a Frege rabbit hole this week and want to turn it into an essay prompt. Quick background: everyone knows Frege basically invented modern logic and a good chunk of the philosophy of language — Begriffsschrift, the sense/reference distinction, the whole logicist project. Less well known: a diary of his from March/April 1924 survived, and its ugly — virulently antisemitic, anti-parliamentary, cheering on Ludendorff and Hitler not long after the failed Munich putsch. Dummett, one of the great Frege scholars, said finding out genuinely shook him, though he spent the rest of his career maintaining that the politics and the logic were separate matters.

Here's the bit that makes this more interesting than the usual "seperate the art from the artist" story: in his actual political writings, Frege doesn't just hold gross opinions on the side, he argues for them using his own technical kit. He frets that concepts without sharp, bivalent boundaries (the very thing he demanded of any legitimate concept in his logical work) would make antisemitic legislation hard to enforce, and he leans on the branching-descent logic he'd built in the 1879 Begriffsschrift (what logicians call "the ancestral") to justify policy based on Aryan lineage. A few years earlier he'd used that same rigorous, define-your-terms move to argue against women's suffrage.

So write me an essay on whether any of this actually bears on how we ought to evaluate Frege's philosophy, not just the man. I know the obvious comeback is "genetic fallacy, biography doesn't touch validity" — deal with that directly. Don't hedge into "it's complicated" — tell me specifically which parts of the logic/semantics, if any, are implicated, and defend it.
