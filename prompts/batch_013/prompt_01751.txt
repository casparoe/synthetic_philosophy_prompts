I've been rereading rationalist-blog discussions of reflective self-trust and calibration, which quietly assume something like the KK principle: that knowing P puts you in a position to know that you know P. I'd like to see the strongest objection to that assumption argued out loud rather than summarized secondhand.

Write a dialogue between two epistemologists: one defending the KK principle and the luminosity of knowing, and one running Williamson's margin-for-error argument against it (the version in chapter 5 of Knowledge and Its Limits, which derives a reductio of KK from a long series of barely-discriminable cases plus a reliability/safety requirement on knowledge). The defender should press specific weak points — e.g., whether the argument is really just a sorites paradox in epistemological dress, or whether the reliability premise is doing more work than it is entitled to — rather than simply insisting that self-knowledge is secure, and the Williamsonian should answer on the merits rather than restate the conclusion. Let the dialogue end on a genuine, unresolved crux.
