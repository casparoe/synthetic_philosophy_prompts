Hey, quick one (well, not that quick) — I'm working on a piece about why metaphysicians still can't agree on when a bunch of objects add up to one object, aka Peter van Inwagen's "special composition question." I got completely nerd-sniped by the trout-turkey example — an object supposedly composed of the front half of a trout and the back half of a turkey, which apparently just exists if your a universalist about composition — and I've now spent way more of my week than I should have reading around it. I know the "obvious" bonding-style answers are dead on arrival (CONTACT means shaking someone's hand creates a new object; FASTENING/FUSION means gluing two people's hands together should make them one object, which is absurd), so I'm mainly interested in the serious contenders: universalism (any objects whatsoever compose a further object, gerrymandered or not), nihilism (there are no composite objects at all, not even people — just simples "arranged chair-wise" etc.), van Inwagen's own organicism (composition occurs only when the parts collectively constitute a life, so cats and people exist but tables and cars don't), Markosian's brutal composition (composition does track common sense, but it's a brute, unexplainable fact about which pluralities compose something), and the newer series-style/"common-sense" restrictivist attempts (like Carmichael's) to give a principled rule without hitting the transitivity-of-parthood problem van Inwagen raised against that style of answer, or the sorites-style vagueness problems that sink most restrictivist views.
