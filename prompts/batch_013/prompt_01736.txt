ok this has been rattling around in my head for a while. you know Yudkowsky's rebuttal to Allais from the Sequences -- the die-roll money pump thing, where because your preference flips between the compound (2A/2B) framing and the reduced (1A/1B) framing once the outer uncertainty resolves, someone can charge you a penny to flip a switch one way before the roll and a penny to flip it back after, leaving you strictly poorer with certainty? Allais himself thought his paradox just showed independence was a bad rationality constraint, but this money pump is supposed to be the knockdown reply to that.

thing is, the pump only works if you're the kind of agent who re-evaluates via local, in-the-moment preferences at each node, rather than committing to a plan up front and refusing to defect against your earlier self later on. if you're a "resolute chooser" who just sticks to the plan no matter what, doesn't the whole pump evaporate, without you ever having to give up your weird static preferences? is resolute choice actually a legit way out here, or is there something sneaky/question-begging about it -- like its secretly just relocating the inconsistency somewhere you can't see it, rather than actually resolving it?
