Can you rank the following five approaches to acting under moral uncertainty, tier-list style: (1) My Favourite Theory – you just figure out which single theory you find most credible and act on that, full stop, ignoring the others; (2) Maximize Expected Choiceworthiness – treat your credences over theories like probabilities and each theory's "choiceworthiness" ratings like utilities, then maximize the expectation (this requires biting the bullet on intertheoretic comparisons of choiceworthiness, which a lot of people think is basically incoherent); (3) The Moral Parliament – Bostrom & Ord's proposal where each theory gets delegates proportional to your credence in it and the delegates bargain and vote on what to do; (4) Variance Voting – rescale each theory's choiceworthiness function so its variance across the options on the table equals every other theory's, then take the credence-weighted sum; and (5) The Borda Rule – throw out cardinal info entirely and combine only each theory's ordinal ranking of the options, ala a Borda count.
