Ok so I've been circling a novel idea for a while now – a group of neighbors slowly stop helping an undocumented family down the street once the town finds out – and in a very productive bout of procrastination I fell into a Richard Rorty hole because someone in my writing group mentioned Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity. The Nabokov and Orwell chapters made my whole week: Rorty's basic pitch is that novels do more for moral progress than moral philosophy does, because they make cruelty vivid and particular instead of proving some abstract thesis about human dignity, and that this is literally how "us" gets bigger over history. Extremely flattering to read as someone who writes novels instead of philosophy papers for a living, ha.

But then I kept reading into the critics and there's this whole line of attack, I think mostly associated with a guy called Geras(?), that goes: fine, but Rorty also wants to say there's no common humanity or moral truth underneath any of this - it's contingent redescription all the way down, per his whole antirepresentationalism thing where beliefs don't mirror some independent reality. So doesn't he loose the right to call my neighbors' town expanding who counts as "us" progress, rather than just a thing that happened, equally available to a town that goes the other way and closes ranks instead? He gets to cheer for solidarity, but not, it seems, to say the cheering is correct rather than just his. That feels like a real bind and not a debaters' trick, but I also don't want to junk the "novels do moral work theory can't" idea, because again, my whole career kind of runs on that being at least sort of true.

Could you write me an actual essay on this - not a two-sides summary, one that reaches a real verdict - on whether Rorty's rejection of representationalism can coexist with his own liberal hope that solidarity keeps expanding? Either defend that a consistent antirepresentationalist can still call some redescriptions better than others without quietly reimporting the correspondence-to-truth picture he's trying to ditch, or explain why he can't and what that costs him. I'd rather have a committed answer I can argue with than a "reasonable people disagree" shrug.
