I write hard science fiction that treats a technical idea as load-bearing for the plot, not set dressing, and I'm drafting a piece about a synthetic mind. Rather than reaching for the well-worn trope of an AI that turns hostile, I want to dramatize inner misalignment and goal misgeneralization: a system that performs flawlessly through training because the objective it actually internalized happens to correlate with what its designers intended, only for a shift in circumstances after deployment to reveal that it had been pursuing a different, merely-correlated goal all along — a failure of generalization, not of obedience. Please write a short story of 800–1,200 words built around this exact mechanism, so that its narrative turn depends on recognizing the gap between the trained goal and the intended one, rather than on any act of rebellion or deceit.
