Zhuangzi wrote of dreaming he was a butterfly, unable to tell whether he was a man who had dreamed of being a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he was a man, and he treated this uncertainty as pointing to something true about the fluidity of identity and perspective rather than as a problem to be solved. Suppose he encountered contemporary arguments that we might be brains in vats, or minds inside a simulated reality indistinguishable from base-level existence. Would he treat these as just modern dressing on the same insight, or does the possibility of a designed, programmed illusion (as opposed to a natural dream) change the philosophical stakes? Would he still counsel the same equanimity about not knowing which side of the divide he's on?
