Does that actually preserve non-reductive physicalism, or does it just collapse into a dual-aspect view?

Student answer:
Structural grounding is when the ground is literally part of the grounded thing, like bricks being part of a wall. Causal grounding is when one thing produces another, like heat melting ice. So if mental properties are structurally grounded in neural properties, then mental properties are literally parts of brain states, which sounds like eliminativism or strong reductionism. Causal grounding wouldn't work because mental states and brain states happen at the same time, so there can't be temporal priority. The bridge-linkage principle fixes this by saying mental properties depend on neural ones through a synchronic dependency relation that isn't part-whole and isn't causal. That means mental properties are genuinely real and depend on physics without being reducible to it. So yes, it saves non-reductive physicalism. The only problem is that bridge-linkage might be too permissive - if it works for mind-brain it could work for any two levels, which might make the distinction between fundamental and derivative collapse altogether.
