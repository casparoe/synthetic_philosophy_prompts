I'm on the ethics panel for our state's hospice oversight board. There's this proposal from Meridian Hospice to allow voluntary cessasion of nutrition for patients with advanced dementia if they left an advance directive citing that death is not a harm to them — basically an Epicurean framing they want built into the consent framework. The disability rights coalition filed a brief saying we're treating dementia patients as if their future lives have no value, and that using Epicurus here is a philosphical sleight of hand because the patients aren't the ones reasoning, it's the families projecting. Half the panel thinks this is just honoring autonomy with an explicit philosphical basis; the other half thinks it's importing a contested metaphysics into medical policy. I need a short memo for Thursday's vote: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It should actually engage the Epicurean point — not just say death is bad, period — and deal seriously with the disability coalition's worry that the asymmetry argument gets misapplied when the subject can't currently affirm it. Also need a concrete timeframe if we go with conditions. Don't cite bibliographies, just reason it through. There's probably a typo in my notes but you get the idea.
