Write a 90-minute final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind course on Daniel Dennett’s taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. The question must be unanswerable by merely defining the four kinds and listing examples. Present this specific conceptual tension: imagine a non-linguistic, highly social mammal that acquires complex foraging strategies through two routes—individual reward-mediated trial-and-error, and observation of conspecific failures followed by internal adjustment of its environmental model, all without explicit teaching or symbolic instruction. Ask students to argue whether this creature is best classified as Skinnerian, Popperian, or as a hybrid that undermines Dennett’s claim that these kinds form a strict hierarchical stack of design spaces. Require them to defend their answer using the functional criteria (not just behavioral descriptions) that distinguish Skinnerian conditioning from Popperian hypothesis testing, and to address whether social observation without language can ever constitute Gregorian transmission. Provide a model answer of roughly 600 words that clearly separates behavioral shaping from internal representation and selective “death of ideas,” evaluates the hierarchy claim, and explains why the absence of language blocks Gregorian status even when social learning is present. Include a marking scheme out of 20 points that identifies the specific moves separating an A answer—precise functional distinction between Skinnerian and Popperian mechanisms; explicit treatment of internal representation; direct engagement with the hierarchy thesis; correct exclusion of Gregorian status with reasoning—from a B answer, which typically conflates observation with teaching, describes behavior without functional analysis, or treats the taxonomy as merely chronological rather than as nested design spaces. Do not request bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or specific page references.
