I have drafted notes for an essay to present to my school philosophy society on how beliefs about the distant past are justified. I then argue that modern digital archives pose a problem Thucydides could not foresee: bias now operates at the level of algorithmic preservation rather than human testimony, since statistical retrieval mechanisms determine which traces survive independently of their evidential quality. I worry that my parallel between algorithmic curation and Thucydides's distrust of poets overstates the analogy, that I neglect whether Herodotus's method maps onto statistical aggregation, and that the argument remains too abstract without a concrete case, such as reconstructing one ancient event from fragmentary papyri.
