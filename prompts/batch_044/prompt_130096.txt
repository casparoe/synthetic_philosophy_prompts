I'm drafting the final exam for my epistemology seminar — it's upper-level, mostly juniors and seniors who've done intro phil but nothing super technical. I want one essay prompt on the regress problem that can't be answered by just dumping the usual trio (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism) onto the page. Specifcally, I want them to wrestle with this idea I've been calling 'branching infinitism' — the view that justification runs through a tree-structure where every belief has multiple independant supporting branches, and every branch goes on infinitely without ever hitting a foundation. The question should ask them to evaluate whether this actually escapes the regress or just replicates it with extra steps, and whether it dodges the arbitrariness charge against foundationalism and the circularity charge against coherentism, or if it inherits both.

Please write the exam question itself (for a 45-minute sit-down, about 600-800 word response expected), then a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 marks. The scheme should break down exactly what separates an A from a B — like, an A has to identify that branching doesn't eliminate the need for ultimate justification, that infinite redundancy isn't the same as linear infinity, and that without a stopping rule the tree just becomes a dense circular web. A B answer probably summarizes the three standard positions and mentions branching as a cool variant without seeing the collapse. Don't include any citation requests or bibliography prompts — it's a closed-book exam and I don't want students guessing at sources. Also keep it conceptual rather than anchored to specific named texts, since I want them arguing from the structure of the problem itself.
