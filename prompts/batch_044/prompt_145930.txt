There's a debate in the modern Stoic revival about the dichotomy of control that's been going in circles for years. The CBT-leaning folks say you only control your judgements and impules, everything else is external. The classicist philosophers push back that for Epictetus bodily actions and social roles are also 'up to us', it's just outcomes that aren't. Then there's this newer 'three-zone' framing floating around in some rationalist-adjacent Stoic forums - full control, influence zone, none - where they stick preferred indifferents in the middle. Everyone claims to be defending the 'real' Stoic view but nobody moves. Is this actually about Stoic exegisis, or is it a verbal fight about what 'control' means? Or are they answering different questions - one psychological, one metaphyscial? Give me an honest diagnosis of what's stalling it, not a survey of positions, and tell me if a closer reading of Epictetus, a cognative science result, or some conceptual distinction could break the deadlock, or if it's just bedrock intuitions about agency.
