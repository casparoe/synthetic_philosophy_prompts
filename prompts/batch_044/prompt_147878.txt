I'm preparing a podcast interview for a science-ideas audience with a philosopher of technology who defends a revived, non-catastrophist reading of Von Neumann's late thesis on technological survival. Rather than framing the risk as destructive capacity—nuclear, climatic, or algorithmic—she argues that the core danger is what she terms technological outpacing: the widening structural gap between a technology's operational speed and the adaptive latency of the institutions meant to govern it, a gap she describes in terms of institutional metabolism rather than political will. I want eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate this framing but press where the literature actually presses. I need at least one question that challenges whether her view collapses into technological determinism, and how she distinguishes outpacing from mere institutional inertia, with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to evolutionary analogies; one on what form of institutional metabolism could actually close the gap without stifling the innovation that creates it, anticipating her probable answer and sharpening it; one on whether artificial intelligence represents a qualitative break in outpacing dynamics or only a quantitative acceleration of the same pattern; and one that presses the normative ambiguity in Von Neumann's own conclusion—whether survival requires slowing technology, accelerating governance, or accepting a permanent disequilibrium—again with the anticipated reply and a harder follow-up. No generic biographical or definitional openers; each question should turn on the actual content of her position and should be answerable without consulting external sources.
