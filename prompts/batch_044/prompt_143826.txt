I'm trying to determine whether immoral art can still be good art, and I'm testing my intuitions against a set of concrete cases. Case one: a painter who, years after completing a serene landscape, committed an unrelated murder. The painting itself seems excellent; the artist's later crime feels irrelevant to its value. Case two: a novelist who abused his family; his novel is technically masterful, but its central theme glorifies domination in a way that requires the reader to adopt the author's vicious worldview to grasp its intended emotional arc. Here the work itself feels aesthetically compromised. Case three: a documentary assembled from footage stolen from a private archive without consent; it is historically important and beautifully edited, but its very existence depends on the violation. I am unsure whether the wrong belongs to the work or only to its origin. Case four: a sculpture carved from human remains taken without permission; the form is extraordinary, yet displaying it extends the desecration. I lean that it is not good art, because its medium is inseparable from the wrong. Case five: a poem advocating genocide with extraordinary rhetorical power; appreciating its rhythm and imagery seems to require momentarily entering a genocidal mindset. I judge this not good art, yet I worry that my judgment confuses immoral content with the moral complicity required to appreciate formal excellence.

What principle, if any, captures these verdicts? I seem to distinguish immorality that is external to the work (biography), internal to its content (what it asks us to endorse), and constitutive of its existence (how it was made or what it is made of). But I cannot clearly weight these dimensions, and I keep returning to the poem: is my rejection driven by the content being immoral, or by the fact that fully appreciating the form requires a kind of moral participation? Can a precise principle distinguish these? And what should it say about a hypothetical masterpiece of pure formal beauty that can only be fully experienced by adopting a cruel perspective?
