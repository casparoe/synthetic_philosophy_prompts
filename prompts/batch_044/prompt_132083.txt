Our AI ethics board has this track record: we okayed a study that used step-by-step human feedback during training even when final outputs were wrong; we rejected one that only graded final answers with no visibility into reasoning; we conditionally approved a system that logs every intermediate step but rewards only the outcome; and we rejected a proposal that optimized user ratings with zero internal oversight.

Now we have a new proposal: the model gets process-based rewards during training—points for correct intermediate reasoning—but at deployment we only check outcome metrics and don't have access to the reasoning traces. Should we approve it if we want to stay consistent? What's the closest precedent, and are we implicitly running a "process must be visible in deployment" rule or just a "process must guide training" rule? And what kind of case would actually split those two?
