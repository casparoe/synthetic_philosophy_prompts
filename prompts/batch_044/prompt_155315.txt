hey so i'm trying to figure out if there's any coherent principle behind my gut reactions about why some countries get rich, or if i'm just cherry-picking comparisons. here are my verdicts on paired cases:

south korea vs north korea: institutions win, no contest. same ethnicity, climate, culture basically, split in 1945, now totally different incomes. my strongest case.

australia vs argentina: both settler societies with huge resources, but argentina was richer around 1900 and now it's flipped. i want to say institutions (land tenure, political openness) but part of me thinks it's about the human capital the settlers brought — british legal training vs spanish bureaucratic tradition? not sure if thats culture or institutions in disguise.

botswana vs zambia: both landlocked, both had copper or diamond booms, but botswana kept democracy and grew, zambia slid into patronage. institutions again.

haiti vs dominican republic: same island, so geography is literally identical. i feel like colonial extraction patterns matter — french vs spanish — but thats also institutions, right? except i keep wanting to invoke "culture" for haiti, maybe i'm just being sloppy.

the reversal of fortune pattern: india and egypt were "rich" in 1500 but poor now, us and australia the opposite. this makes geography look stupid since the map didn't move. but i also think "rich in 1500" is a dodgy comparison — it measured palace tax revenue, not living standards. so maybe long-run comparisons are mostly noise unless we controll for what "rich" actually meant.

ethiopia vs kenya: ethiopia was never colonized, kenya was. you'd think avoiding colonialism preserves good institutions, but kenya is richer now. so "colonialism caused poverty" is too simple; it's about what kind of state got left behind.

norway vs nigeria: both hit big oil. norway stayed democratic and rich, nigeria got the resource curse. this feels like institutions mediating the effect of a resource windfall.

singapore vs malaysia: split in 1965, singapore went hard on rule-of-law and anti-corruption, malaysia stayed ethnically divided with patronage politics. institutions win, but was that inevitable or just specific leadership choices?

my problem: every time i think institutions dominate, i can think of a counter where culture or geography sneaks back in. is there a hierarchy — institutions > colonial legacy > geography > culture — that saves most of these? or do i have to drop the haiti culture intuition? also, what does whatever principle you find predict for east vs west germany after reunification, or for chile vs argentina in the 20th century?

please be honest: is there one account of causal inference from history that gets all of these, or close? which verdict is the outlier i should throw away? and what does it say about japan industrializing without being colonized, since that seems like an outlier too?
