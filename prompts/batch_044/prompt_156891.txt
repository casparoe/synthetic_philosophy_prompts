You are preparing a speaker for the Q&A after a seminar defending a position called intervention-stabilized entity realism in the philosophy of dark-matter physics. The thesis is that the pessimistic meta-induction is systematically misapplied to contemporary direct-detection experiments because it treats all past theoretical replacements as ontologically equivalent, when there is a crucial distinction between representational target drift—the mathematical formalism changes while the causal-intervention profile of the entity remains stable—and ontological commitment drift, in which the entity itself is abandoned. The speaker argues that WIMP direct-detection programs exhibit the former, not the latter: successive detector generations refine cross-section bounds and interaction models but do not discard the basic causal profile of a weakly-interacting massive particle; therefore, realism about WIMPs survives the pessimistic meta-induction in a way that realism about caloric or luminiferous ether did not.

The audience is a philosophy-of-science workshop with three specific factions. First, structural realists in the Ladyman-Ross vein, who will argue that what survives theory change is mathematical structure rather than entities, and that direct-detection data only confirm structural relations between detectors and cosmological parameters. Second, anti-realist instrumentalists who accept modified Newtonian dynamics or emergent-gravity alternatives, and who will object that the speaker illegitimately privileges one underdetermined theoretical framework. Third, physicist-philosophers who regard the pessimistic meta-induction as obsolete and who will press the circularity of the no-miracles argument, asking why predictive success requires realism at all.

Prepare the speaker with a prep sheet, not a defense. List the objections in the order they are most likely to arise and most damaging, phrased in the vocabulary each faction would actually use. For each objection, provide a concise reply deliverable in under two minutes, explicitly note where that reply is weakest, and indicate whether the speaker should concede part of the point up front. Identify the one objection the speaker is hoping nobody asks—specifically, the challenge that if intervention stability is sufficient for realism, then we should be realists about phlogiston in certain combustion-intervention contexts, which threatens to collapse the distinction. State clearly which reply must land for the proposal to survive the session. Finally, take a definite position on whether the speaker should open the talk by conceding that structural realism is partially correct, or should hold firm on entity realism from the start; give your reasoning for that strategic choice, and be fully committed—do not merely list options, but defend one course with explicit reasons.
