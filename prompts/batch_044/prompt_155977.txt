You are the research-integrity officer at a research university, responsible for auditing faculty justifications for post-hoc modifications to study protocols. The philosophical distinction between reasons of the right kind and reasons of the wrong kind holds that some considerations bear on whether a belief or methodological choice is warranted by its object (for example, that a data point reflects instrument malfunction), while others bear only on whether holding that belief or making that choice is beneficial (for example, that removing it yields statistical significance). You must operationalize this distinction into a reusable audit procedure that a non-philosopher can apply consistently to written justifications submitted after preliminary data inspection.

Construct a step-by-step decision procedure, presented as an ordered sequence of tests with explicit branching conditions, that classifies a researcher's stated reason for altering a protocol. The procedure must address three specific decision nodes: (i) exclusion of individual observations; (ii) addition, removal, or transformation of covariates in an analytic model; and (iii) substitution of a pre-registered statistical model with an alternative specification. At each node, specify the indicators that a reason is responsive to the internal evidential aims of the study, the indicators that it is responsive to external incentives, career pressures, or desired outcomes, and the conditions that trigger mandatory escalation to a review panel because the procedure cannot settle the classification.

Incorporate three structural checks into the procedure: an initial filter separating considerations about the study's epistemic aims from considerations about its institutional or personal consequences; a symmetry test asking whether the researcher would have accepted the same consideration if it produced a result contrary to their interests; and a mixing clause that identifies arguments in which both kinds of reason are intertwined and requires disaggregation before evaluation. Provide fully worked applications of the complete procedure to three submitted justifications: one in which the reason is clearly of the right kind but forces the researcher to abandon a favored hypothesis; one in which the reason is of the wrong kind but is expressed in technically legitimate language; and one genuinely ambiguous case in which the procedure reaches no determination and must be escalated.

Conclude by stating the explicit limits of the procedure: whether it can identify unconscious bias, whether it requires access to private mental states, whether it treats all pragmatic considerations as corrupting, and whether it applies differently when the protocol change was made before versus after data inspection. Do not cite philosophical texts or provide a bibliography; ground the procedure solely in the conceptual structure of the distinction itself.
