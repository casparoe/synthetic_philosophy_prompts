Consider the institutional practice in academic peer review: anonymous reviewers are routinely permitted to reject submissions for insufficient originality or for anticipating results that remain unpublished, without being required to disclose their own parallel research agendas. What conception of standing to blame, and what view of hypocrisy, does this design presuppose? Does it assume that anonymity severs the relational conditions necessary for hypocritical blame, or that institutional role-authorization replaces personal standing? Reconstruct the implicit philosophical account, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and determine whether a single coherent theory of standing can justify both the anonymity norm and the expectation that authors not replicate the reviewer's undisclosed work.
