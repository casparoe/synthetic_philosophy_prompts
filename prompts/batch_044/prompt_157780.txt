A scholar working within Jain anekāntavāda applies syādvāda and saptabhaṅgī to contemporary philosophical claims. Her precedents are: she rules that “the ātman is eternal” is true in the niścaya-naya and false in the vyavahāra-naya; that “reality is entirely mental” is false in all standard naya except one restricted instrumental sense; that “moral obligations are context-dependent” carries the syāt-prefix across all seven modes without contradiction; that “only one correct description of an object exists” is rejected outright; and that “a literal contradiction can be true” is false in every respect, since syāt blocks contradiction by limiting predicate scope. The new case is this claim: “A non-sentient computational system can possess a partially true representation of an object, qualified strictly by the informational naya through which it accesses that object, without any jīva being present.” Predict whether she should apply conditional predication, reject the claim outright, or accept it in modified form; identify the closest analog among her precedents; state honestly whether her record genuinely underdetermines the answer; specify the principle she would be adopting with each possible choice; and name the further case that would best distinguish the competing interpretations of her framework. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
