For years people have argued about whether Arendt was right that Eichmann embodied the banality of evil — philosophers defend her by saying thoughtlessness isn't stupidity but a failure to think from another's standpoint, and critics say she missed his obvius ideological fervor. But I think the debate is stuck because everyone assumes this is a claim about one guy's psychology, when Arendt might actually be describing a kind of moral blindness that only shows up in bureacratic roles where decision-making is fragemented. So is this stall a real empirical disagreement about Eichmann's motives, a verbal confusion between banal as ordinary-looking versus morally shallow, or are philosophers and historians just answering different questions — one about individual character, one about institutional logic — under the same phrase? If it's the latter, what distinction or case study would actually move things, or should we accept the thesis works at different levels and stop arguing?
