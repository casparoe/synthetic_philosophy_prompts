I keep noticing a pattern in institutional classification that I do not have a precise name for. Imagine a board that certifies artifacts as authentic using published criteria: radiometric range, documented provenance, stylistic coherence. Over years, high-profile cases slowly relax one criterion at a time—an object with weak provenance is approved because its material dating is unambiguously central, another with stylistic anomalies is approved because its chain of custody is exceptional. The formal rules stay unchanged, but an unstated shadow taxonomy grows: a cluster of approved clear cases that implicitly redefine the boundary. Then, when an independent applicant meets every published criterion but lacks the accumulated, unstated relaxations, the board rejects it as borderline or in the gray zone, appealing to the central examples rather than to the rules. I have seen analogous dynamics in medical licensing and tenure review, where a category is defended not by a fixed line but by an expanding cloud of paradigmatic instances that push ambiguity outward.
