I've been trying to sort out my own gut verdicts on the analytic/synthetic split, partly because I'm in a philosphy course and Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empericism" keeps getting mentioned but I feel like the usual classroom examples miss the cases I'm actually unsure about.

Here are my calls: "All bachelors are unmarried" is analytic, no doubt. "Water is H2O" is necessary but synthetic — a posteriori, not built into the meaning. "Every event has a cause" used to feel analytic to me, but now I think it's either synthetic a priori or just a stubborn empirical habit we can't shake. "This object is red and green all over at once" is analytically false, contradiction in the concepts themselves. "Murder is wrong" is synthetic, obviously. But then there's this framework thing: in a constructed language where "brother" is defined as "male sibling with at least one shared biological parent," the claim "all brothers share a biological parent" is analytic within that language, even though in English adoption norms make it synthetic. So the framework seems to matter. The case I keep fliping on is "2 + 2 = 4" — part of me wants analytic, part wants synthetic-but-necessary, and Quine's holism makes me wonder if there's even a real fact of the matter. Is there one principle that captures all of these verdicts, or do I have to drop some? And what does your principle predict for a case I haven't listed — is "cats are mammals" analytic in English but synthetic in a culture where "cat" just means "small furry indoor pet"?
