I am a journalist covering the philosophy of social science and pedagogical design for a long-form feature on how university seminars teach students to navigate collective identity without collapsing into either essentialism or statistical dissolution. For this piece I need a concrete, runnable classroom activity—something I can describe in precise detail for readers—rather than an abstract syllabus outline. Specifically, I am looking for a 90-minute structured exercise for about 16 upper-level undergraduates in a philosophy of culture seminar, organized around a conceptual framework I am developing for the article that I call the “three-register problem” of national character: the statistical-demographic register (probabilistic, non-moral patterns), the institutional-normative register (structural evaluation that risks reification), and the hermeneutic-narrative register (interpretive understanding that invites essentialism). The activity should supply participants with fabricated but realistic synthetic national profiles—complete with specified economic indicators, legal architectures, and cultural practices—so that they must apply the identical profile across all three registers and experience firsthand how the epistemic and moral status of their generalizations shifts with the governing framework rather than with the underlying data.

Please design the rules, roles, or forced-choice mechanisms so that the students run into the fragmentation or coherence tension themselves before any debrief names it; include a clear timeline, a system of small stakes or coordination constraints that does not require real money, and an explicit debrief that maps each phase of the exercise onto the coherence question—specifically, whether national character can remain coherent across registers or whether it fractures irreducibly. I also need an honest note on which feature of the coherence debate the simulation necessarily simplifies and cannot reproduce, together with a warning about the predictable failure mode on a first run and how the facilitator should handle it. The design should not depend on any particular scholarly citation or assigned reading list, since the feature emphasizes conceptual experience over bibliographic coverage, and it should avoid asking participants to invoke niche theoretical proper names they may not have encountered.
