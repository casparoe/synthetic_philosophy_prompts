I am designing a seminar handout on eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires—specifically about propositional attitudes, not about consciousness or qualia. I want a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions by answers to concrete diagnostic cases, not by which label a reader recognizes. The root commitment is: “A completed neuroscience can in principle explain all human behavior without treating beliefs or desires as internal representational states.”

From that root, construct three branching nodes that exhaust the logical space. First, symmetry: if elimination is warranted, does it apply equally to belief and desire, or is one attitude more dispensable—say, because desire reduces to homeostatic drive states while belief lacks any comparable neural anchor? Second, utterance interpretation: imagine a subject sincerely says “I believe the meeting is at noon,” but every available neural and behavioral marker is better explained without positing any representational content; is that utterance best read as a literal report of a non-existent state, a useful fiction, a misdescription of a non-representational neural pattern, or a purely social performative? Third, selective retention: suppose neuroscience eventually isolates precise correlates for basic appetitive desires but finds no stable correlates for abstract normative beliefs—does that asymmetry justify retaining desire-language instrumentally, retaining belief-language instrumentally, retaining both, or retaining neither?

Build the tree from these splits so that every path ends in a leaf. Then locate actual positions within that space: full Churchland-style eliminativism, standard realist opposition, instrumentalist retention of folk-psychological language, revisionary reductionism that redefines rather than eliminates, and any form of partial eliminativism that spares one attitude but not the other. For every combination of answers that no named view occupies, mark it “unnamed/unoccupied” and diagnose in one sentence whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Every node must be answerable without knowing the view’s name—phrase them strictly as questions about the cases above. At the end, flag any pair of leaves that your three questions genuinely fail to separate. Keep the response within 850 words, include no bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and avoid quoting any specific text verbatim. Finally, state your confidence in the overall mapping and identify the single leaf or branch you are least sure you have placed correctly.
