Compare Simone Weil's argument that political parties should be abolished — she sees them as machines that manufacture collective passions and suppress individual moral judgment — with the 'cartel party' view that modern parties have become state-subsidized bureaucracies largely detatched from civil society and voter emotion. Weil's fear is that parties are too gripping and ideologically totalizing, while the cartel thesis says they're too boring and administrative. Do these critiques actually conflict, or can they both be true at different stages? More specifically, does Weil's core objection depend on parties being genuinely passionate and belief-forming, or would she still object if a party was just a pure coordination device with no emotional pull? And if parties really turned into pure cartels, would that resolve her problem or just replace it with a different democratic deficit?
