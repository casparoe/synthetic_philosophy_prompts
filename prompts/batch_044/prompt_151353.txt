My philosophy teacher mentioned Karl Jaspers and the idea that a nation can be guilty, not just individuals. I tried building an argument for debate club but now it feels like it proves way too much and I cant find the exit.

(1) Jaspers said political guilt attaches to you just for being a citizen of a state that does wrong, even if you personally opposed it.
(2) If that guilt attaches, it should also attach to people born into that citizenship later, since they still recieve the benefits and live inside the structures.
(3) I came up with this idea called moral residue loops — if a harm was never fully repaired, the guilt doesnt fade, it loops back onto descendants as an active structural debt.
(4) So descendants dont just inherit advantages; they inherit active guilt through these loops.
(5) Actual guilt has to come with an obligation to repair, otherwise its just empty feeling.
(6) Those repair obligations dont have an expiration date if the harm is still visible, like economic gaps from old extraction or stolen territory.
(7) That means basically every citizen of every country with a messy history is currently guilty and owes work that might never end.
(8) But thats definately absurd — a random teenager shouldnt carry guilt for stuff from centuries ago.
(9) So either collective guilt doesnt cross generations, or moral residue loops are nonsense, or repair obligations do eventually expire.

Which step should I reject? Something slipped between (4) and (7), maybe about what inheriting citizenship actually means for blame. Id like to keep (1) and (5) if I can, but tell me what Im paying if I drop either. Also, is "moral residue loops" doing real philosophical work, or is it just relabeling inherited trauma?
