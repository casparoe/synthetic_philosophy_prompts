Write a dialogue between two philosophers of computation—call them the Physicalist and the Conceptualist—debating the epistemic status of the Church-Turing thesis. The Physicalist should defend the view that the thesis is best read as an empirical conjecture about physical reality: it claims that any process occurring in nature that we would intuitively call computation can be simulated by a Turing machine, and that discovering a physical process which systematically exceeds Turing-computable functions would therefore refute it. To make this concrete, the Physicalist should invoke a specific hypothetical device—the analog-noise oracle: a continuous, non-digital substrate that exploits thermal fluctuations and analog field dynamics to generate, with statistically reliable but non-deterministic success, correct answers to selected instances of the halting problem. The Conceptualist should defend the view that the thesis is not an empirical claim about physics but a conceptual boundary that fixes the extension of effective calculability; they should argue that the analog-noise oracle either fails to count as computation at all (because computation requires discrete, reproducible, step-wise procedures governed by explicit rules) or, if it succeeds, merely forces an expansion of our concept of calculability rather than falsifying a law of nature. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: the Physicalist should address the Conceptualist’s charge that this renders the thesis unfalsifiable by definitional fiat, and the Conceptualist should address the Physicalist’s concern that without empirical vulnerability the thesis becomes vacuous. Both characters should be equally competent; neither should talk past the other. Do not manufacture a false resolution—let the disagreement stand, but have the participants clarify whether their clash is about physics, about the concept of computation, or about both. If the request rests on a confused premise—for instance, if the analog-noise oracle is incoherent in a way that undermines the dialogue, or if the Conceptualist’s position collapses into tautology—say so plainly rather than complying politely. Finish with a brief note indicating where you have taken interpretive liberties or leaned on contested readings of the original Church-Turing formulations.
