Here is my position, stated as precisely as I can manage it:

(1) I am a physicalist about consciousness in the standard sense: there is no non-physical substance or property, and consciousness is fully constituted by (or supervenes on) organized information-processing architectures.

(2) I accept the scale argument in its formal structure. Given the astronomical number of insects and other invertebrates, even a very low credence that they are sentient implies that the expected total welfare of invertebrates likely dwarfs that of all vertebrates combined.

(3) I reject the idea that consciousness is a unitary phenomenon. Instead, I think it decomposes into at least three dimensions—phenomenal character, access/availability for reasoning and report, and affective valence—and these dimensions can come apart both in principle and, I suspect, in nature.

(4) Because of evolutionary continuity, I think it is implausible that consciousness suddenly switched on at some phylogenetic boundary such as the vertebrate line. It is more likely that these dimensions are present in some form across many phyla, including arthropods, though with very different degrees of integration.

(5) Nevertheless, I hold that full moral patienthood requires not merely the presence of one or more of these dimensions, but their integration into a unified global workspace or minimal self-model. Without that integration, an organism may possess what I call "isolated valence fragments," but it lacks unified suffering or a genuine point of view.

(6) Consequently, I deny that insects are full moral patients, even though I think the probability that they possess some form of affective valence is non-trivial—perhaps in the range of 30-40%.

(7) My practical conclusion is that we should adopt a precautionary principle weighted by our confidence in (4) multiplied by population scale, but this does not entail granting insects direct moral rights or standing; it justifies reducing gratuitous harm as a risk-management strategy rather than as a duty to the insects themselves.

(8) I take reflective equilibrium to be the correct methodological framework here: we should adjust our theoretical commitments about consciousness dimensions in light of our moral judgments about cases, and vice versa.
