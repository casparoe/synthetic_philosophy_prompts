As a software engineer who builds causal inference pipelines, I have been trying to understand Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis in terms I can actually operationalise. The claim, as I understand it, is that Calvinist religious values carried independent causal weight in the emergence of modern capitalist economic behaviour. But this seems to face a basic ontological obstacle: culture is made of shared beliefs and norms, which are not physical entities, and in my work only interventions on nodes in a causal graph produce observable counterfactual changes. So either Weber is using cause metaphorically — in which case culture is just a correlated narrative layer over real economic drivers — or he is implicitly arguing that material conditions selected for individuals holding those beliefs, which makes culture an effect rather than an independent cause.

Weber apparently rejected both pure materialism and pure idealism, and spoke of an elective affinity between the ethics and capitalist practice. To me this sounds like an admission that the relationship is correlational rather than mechanistic. That leaves me with what feels like a false trichotomy: culture must be either a literal physical cause (seemingly impossible for non-physical entities), an epiphenomenon (explanatorily redundant), or Weber’s framework is incoherent.

My specific confusion is whether values can do genuine explanatory work without being reducible either to aggregate individual psychology or to underlying economic base conditions. Or is saying culture caused capitalism simply a category mistake, analogous to claiming that the average height of a population caused a bridge to collapse? I am looking for the conceptual repair to the mistake I am making, not a reading list. Please answer in British English.
