In a philosophy-of-physics seminar on special relativity and eternalism, a classmate repeatedly invoked the one-liner “if it’s frame-dependent, it’s not real” to dismiss my tentative defense of what I’ll call multi-frame presentism—the view that every inertial frame’s hyperplane of simultaneity is locally ontologically real, even though none is globally privileged. The slogan seemed to do all the work of moving from the relativity of simultaneity to the block universe, but I couldn’t tell whether it was a methodological rule about theory-building, an ontological filter, an explanatory principle, or something else entirely. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that “if it’s frame-dependent, it’s not real” could be expressing—ranging from trivial platitudes about coordinate invariance to substantive metaphysical constraints—organized as a menu rather than a single analysis? For each reading, indicate whether it is defensible, plainly false, or question-begging against presentism; whether it genuinely supports the Rietdijk–Putnam-style inference from Lorentz symmetry to eternalism; and whether it also rules out multi-frame presentism specifically, or only rules out a globally unique present. In particular, distinguish (i) a claim about how physical laws should be formulated, (ii) a claim about what exists, (iii) a claim about causal or explanatory structure, and (iv) any other precise interpretation you see hiding in the phrase. Say which reading people in these debates usually intend when they deploy the slogan to end discussion, and which reading, if any, would allow my view to survive intact. Please state how confident you are in this taxonomy and single out the specific distinction—perhaps between the methodological and ontological readings, or between rejecting global privilege and rejecting frame-local reality altogether—about which you are least sure.
