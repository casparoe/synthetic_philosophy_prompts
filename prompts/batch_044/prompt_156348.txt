I'm writing a feature on the philosophy of action for a science-and-ideas magazine, and I'd like a diagnostic instrument—not a literature review—for readers to locate their own view on the absent-minded driver problem. The scenario is concrete: a commuter intends to drive home, navigates a complex, familiar route without any occurrent awareness of turns or decisions, and only "wakes up" upon arrival, having executed the journey safely. Rather than asking readers to identify with named theorists, please construct a decision tree that sorts positions by the commitments they actually hold.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from author labels. The root splits on whether the driver counts as acting intentionally during the absent-minded stretch, or whether the behaviour is only apparently intentional. From there, branch through four concrete diagnostic questions that genuinely divide responses:

1. Must practical knowledge of the route be occurrent—actively present to consciousness—to guide intentional action?
2. Can sub-personal navigational mechanisms substitute for personal-level knowledge without breaking the chain of intentionality?
3. Is there a coherent intermediate category—call it "directed but not fully intentional"—between deliberate action and mere reflex, or does that collapse into one of the other two?
4. Does the driver retain a genuine dispositional knowledge of the route that counts as knowledge proper, or is any attribution of knowledge here merely post hoc and explanatory?

At each leaf, provide either the name of a genuinely standard position (if one occupies that node) or the label "unnamed", together with exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—the consequence its defenders must accept that sounds implausible on first hearing. For combinations of answers that yield no named view, mark them as unoccupied and diagnose briefly whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.

Please flag any positions that the tree cannot cleanly separate, and indicate what further distinction would be required. Keep the branch labels phrased as concrete claims an intelligent non-specialist can answer without philosophical jargon. The audience is curious general readers; the deliverable should function as a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz with a scoring logic embedded in the tree. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and write in British English.
