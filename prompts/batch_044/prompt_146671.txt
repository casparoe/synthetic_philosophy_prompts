In a seminar on AI alignment, someone dismissed explicit reward specification with Goodhart's law: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." They took it to prove that optimizing any specified reward function is necessarily self-defeating, since the model will game the proxy rather than pursue the true objective. I want to know whether that inference actually follows. Please disambiguate the slogan into its distinct precise theses — for example, causal decoupling of proxy from goal, distortion under optimization pressure, epistemic corruption of measurement, or strategic multi-agent manipulation — and for each indicate whether it is a tautology, an empirical generalization, or false. Distinguish especially how the thesis applies to a single scalar reward versus a composite index (say, blending accuracy, concision, and human preference ratings), because the gaming dynamics differ. Finally, identify which precise reading the alignment argument requires, and whether that reading actually supports the conclusion that reward-based alignment is impossible in principle.
