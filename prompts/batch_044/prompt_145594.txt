Write a short story about an archivist named Mara who maintains a continuity ledger for people who had their consciousness split across clone bodies after death. Each clone recieves slightly different memories and emotional residues, and Mara's job is to merge them into one narrative thread — but she's started noticing that the merged self never quite matches any original fragment, like a synthetic ghost assembled from debts. The story should explore this through anatta, the Buddhist idea that there's no fixed self, only aggregates, but twist it: here the absence of a core self feels less like liberation and more like an unpaid obligation to the ledger. Include a moment where one clone refuses merging, arguing the merged version is just stealing its first-person view. Keep it grounded in sensory details — the smell of ozone, the hum of old servers, the weight of paper files — and don't turn it into a sermon about impermanance, just stay in Mara's weird job and what it implies about whether identity is something you owe to other people.
