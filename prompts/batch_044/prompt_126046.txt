I'm currently working through a problem set in a metaontology course, and my study group has reached a frustrating standstill over what we have started calling the boundary pluralist versus absence monist dispute. The pluralists maintain that whenever two material bodies are separated by a continuous region lacking matter, there exists a distinct ontological item—the gap—which is not reducible to the absence of stuff but carries its own modal profile: it can persist through gradual filling, it can be relocated if the surrounding matter shifts, and it enters into causal explanations of structural failure. The monists reject this entirely, insisting that talk of holes is just a convenient way of describing the shape and topological properties of the surrounding object, and that reifying gaps violates a principle of ontological parsimony without adding explanatory power.

What makes the stall peculiar is that both sides accept identical physical descriptions—same porosity measurements, same stress-strain relations, same perceptual reports—yet neither has moved in months. The pluralists accuse the monists of treating ontology as semantics, of confusing there is no cheese here with there is a cheese-absence here. The monists reply that the pluralists have smuggled in existence claims through a grammatical trick, turning negative existentials into positive ones. My own suspicion is that the disagreement is largely verbal, centered on competing criteria for what counts as a legitimate ontological commitment rather than on any fact about cheese or voids. But I am also open to the possibility that there is a hidden substantive divergence about modal properties or about whether explanatory indispensability in engineering contexts can ground real existence.

Could you diagnose the structure of this disagreement? Is it a verbal dispute about the word entity or about the standards of ontological commitment? Are the two sides actually answering different questions—one about the metaphysics of absence, the other about the logic of negative existentials—while using the same vocabulary? Or is there a genuine empirical or formal question doing the hidden work, perhaps about identity conditions for negative spaces or about the role of absence in causal reasoning? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a formal result on the identity conditions of gaps, a conceptual distinction between being a gap and being gappy, an empirical finding about how we perceptually track holes, or an argument that the question is ill-posed? Please ground your answer in what each side actually claims, and tell me whether anything should be expected to resolve the impasse, or whether the stall is structurally permanent.
