Assume Leibniz is alive in 2024 and receives a precise briefing on two post-1716 developments: first, eternal inflation produces a multiverse with no canonical measure, so there is no principled reason one branch is actualized rather than another; second, neuroscience accounts for consciousness entirely through physical processes, leaving no explanatory gap that requires monads or pre-established harmony. From his 1714 framework — principle of sufficient reason, analysis of possible worlds, and the claim that God maximizes perfection through simplicity of laws multiplied by richness of phenomena — reconstruct his steelman response. Would he reject the multiverse for lacking a sufficient reason for branch-selection, or treat the whole ensemble as one world whose aggregate perfection might still be maximal? Would he preserve his theodicy that evil is permitted only for a greater good, now that suffering appears mechanically inevitable rather than harmonized? Answer in one paragraph, maximum 180 words. Do not cite papers, texts, or modern authors by name; reason only from his concepts plus the two premises above.
