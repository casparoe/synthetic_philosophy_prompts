I want them to feel the special composition question in their bones rather than just memorizing universalism vs nihilism. Could you design a runnable in-class game?

Here's teh twist: instead of tables and chairs, the case cards should feature temporary, spatially scattered, or functionally unified artifacts — a flash mob that performs one choreography then disperses, a drone swarm acting as one delivery system for exactly 12 minutes, an ice-block bridge that melts separately but bears weight together, and one hidden case where two remote-controlled structures sync up without ever touching. Divide the class into three 'audit boards' assigned to contact-only, functional-unity, or unrestricted principals. Give them 6 cards in round 1; they have to render binding verdicts on whether there's exactly one object and assign liability points. They should discover that they all agree on some cases and violently disagree on others, with no neutral arbiter.

No bibliographies or citation lists, and keep the rules simple enough to run with just printed cards and a whiteboard.
