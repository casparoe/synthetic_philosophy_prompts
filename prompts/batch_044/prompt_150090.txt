In discussions about diet and animal suffering I keep running into the line, “If you care about suffering, beef is more ethical than chicken—the plow kills more wild animals than the pasture ever could.” It gets dropped to reverse the usual intuition that chicken is less harmful than beef, usually right after someone mentions crop deaths or feed conversion. I want to take the slogan back to the argument and see which precisification actually does the work, so please treat it as a piece of language to disambiguate rather than as a settled empirical conclusion.

Lay out the distinct precise theses the sentence could be expressing, arranged as a menu from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false, and indicate whether that reading actually supports using the slogan to defend beef consumption over chicken consumption in a moral debate about diet choice.

In particular, distinguish the readings along these dimensions:
- whether “the plow” means grain-crop harvesting mortality specifically, all agricultural wild-animal harm, or the total indirect suffering of crop-based food systems;
- whether “the pasture” refers only to grass-fed beef, to conventional beef including grain-finishing, or to the absence of direct slaughter of farmed animals;
- whether the comparison is counting total sentient lives lost, suffering-years, quality-adjusted harm, or something else;
- whether wild-animal deaths in crop production are being weighed as moral harms on par with the direct suffering and death of farmed chickens or cattle, or whether they are being used only as a rhetorical counterweight;
- whether the slogan expresses an empirical prediction about current agricultural practices, a normative rule about how to minimize harm, or a purely dialectical reversal of conventional wisdom.

Please do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or recommended readings; I need an analysis that relies only on reasoning about these conceptual distinctions. Specify which of the menu items the people repeating the slogan in these debates are probably invoking, and flag any reading that sounds dramatic but collapses into triviality once the ambiguities are resolved.
