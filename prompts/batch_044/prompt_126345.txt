State your credence—between 0 and 1—that a purely causal-role account of biological function can fully accommodate normative dysfunction judgments in the case of a de novo engineered heart built from induced pluripotent stem cells that fails because of a design flaw in its contractile proteins, with no evolutionary selection history. Decompose your answer into three terms: (a) the probability that normative dysfunction conceptually requires an etiological (selected-effect) history rather than mere deviation from a current causal contribution; (b) the probability that the synthetic heart, despite lacking evolutionary history, possesses the causal-role properties that ground functional norms; and (c) the probability that our intuition that it is malfunctioning tracks causal-role failure rather than absence of proper evolutionary origin. Give a number and one line of justification for each, show how they combine into your overall credence, name the term on which your final number is most sensitive, and say what specific argument or empirical result would most change your mind. Finally, identify where your reasoning is most vulnerable to error.
