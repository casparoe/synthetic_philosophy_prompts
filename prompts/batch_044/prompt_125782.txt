The catch is the consent protocol: they want standard Western individual-signature forms, but in two field sites decisions about bodily data are made collectively by lineage elders, and treating consent as an individual right is seen locally as a kind of moral isolation that violates obligations to the group. The PI says without uniform consent they can't publish; local collaborators say imposing individual consent actually distorts the moral ecology they're studying, since it forces participants into a WEIRD framework of autonomy before they even answer the blame questions.

At the same time, deal honestly with the strongest argument on the other side—probably that without an individual opt-out we risk letting collective norms override vulnerable people, especially women and junior kin who might dissent silently.

Keep it under 350 words. Lead with the bottom line up front. Use a table if it helps lay out the tradeoffs between universal protocol and local adaptation.
