Consider the following argument about Hacking's looping effects and psychiatric classification, which I drafted for a workshop and now find suspicious because it seems to dissolve the boundary between psychiatric and somatic medicine entirely.

(1) A classification of people is subject to a looping effect when it is applied to individuals who are aware of it, institutionally enforced, and capable of altering their self-conception or behavior in response.
(2) Psychiatric categories—such as autism spectrum disorder or major depressive disorder—clearly satisfy all three conditions, so they function as "interactive kinds" whose properties are partly constituted by the classification practice rather than tracking fully independent natural kinds.
(3) Now introduce a proposed diagnostic category, "chronic attentional debt" (CAD), defined entirely by sustained self-reported cognitive overload in high-information work environments, lacking any established biomarker, and tied to institutional responses—workplace accommodations, disability protocols, productivity metrics—that directly reshape the very behaviors being measured.
(4) CAD satisfies the conditions in (1) even more strongly than standard psychiatric categories, since its diagnostic criteria are purely behavioral and its institutional feedback loop is immediate and explicit.
(5) Therefore, if looping effects undermine the objective status of psychiatric categories, they do so with greater force for CAD; indeed, CAD appears to be nothing but the looping effect itself made manifest as a diagnosis.
(6) Apply the same reasoning, however, to somatic categories directed at people—Type 1 diabetes, HIV infection, or pregnancy. Patients know their status, institutions respond with treatment protocols and insurance classifications, and behavior changes accordingly (diet, adherence, lifestyle adjustments).
(7) So either looping effects apply to all person-directed medical classifications, dissolving the psychiatric/somatic boundary entirely, or they apply selectively.
(8) If selectively, then we need an independent criterion for identifying "interactive kinds" that does not already presuppose the psychiatric/somatic distinction we are trying to defend.
(9) No non-circular criterion is available: any proposed biological marker meant to ground "true" natural kinds can itself enter an institutional feedback loop (e.g., biomarker-based diagnosis reshaping patient self-monitoring and clinical behavior).
(10) Therefore, Hacking's framework either collapses all of medicine into social construction or relies on an arbitrary boundary, and so it cannot specifically delegitimize psychiatric classification without also undermining somatic medicine.

The conclusion feels far too sweeping, but I cannot isolate the slip. Which step should I reject? If I reject (7)'s framing—that the choice is only between universal application and arbitrary selectivity—what am I committing to? Or is the flaw in (9)'s claim about biomarker circularity? I would prefer to preserve the idea that psychiatric categories are genuinely shaped by looping effects, but I want to know exactly what preserving that costs.
