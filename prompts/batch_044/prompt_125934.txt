I am preparing a colloquium-style interview with a philosopher who has recently argued that the inside-view/outside-view distinction in rational belief and decision-making must be reconstructed through a principle of 'epistemic locality'. On her account, the outside view — statistical or base-rate information about how agents in a reference class typically reason — should constrain an individual agent's credences only when the causal and evidential pathway through which she acquired her belief is structurally analogous to the pathways that generated that reference class. Where the pathway is locally idiosyncratic, involving a proprietary chain of reasoning or an unusual evidential environment, the outside view is not merely outweighed but inapplicable, leaving the inside view with exclusive authority. I want questions that do not ask her to restate this framework, but that press on its internal mechanics and on its tense relationship with standard demands for epistemic modesty.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that meet the following conditions:

- At least one question must examine how the locality constraint applies to Newcomb-style decision problems, particularly whether the accuracy of a perfect predictor belongs to an analogous pathway or should be screened off from the agent's reasoning; include a follow-up that anticipates her likely reply that the predictor's mechanism is causally decoupled from the agent's own deliberative pathway.

- At least one question must address peer disagreement in philosophy or the sciences: if two equally qualified thinkers reach opposite conclusions via pathways that each regards as locally idiosyncratic, does epistemic locality license both in retaining high confidence, and does this risk collapsing into an objectionably permissive form of modesty? Provide a follow-up keyed to the answer that appeals to second-order assessment of pathway similarity.

- At least one question must probe the risk of self-serving boundary-drawing: what stops an agent from classifying inconvenient outside-view data as arising from non-analogous pathways? Include a follow-up that presses the case of an expert forecaster who systematically mispredicts within her own domain yet insists her errors stem from locally unique noise.

- At least one question should ask what kind of empirical or conceptual result could genuinely undermine the locality principle itself — for instance, whether widespread success of global outside-view calibration in domains previously thought idiosyncratic would count against the view, or whether the principle is protected by ad hoc reclassification. Frame this as the closing question of the sequence.

- The remaining questions should explore further conceptual pressure points: the interaction between locality and recursive self-assessment (can one apply the outside view to one's own judgement that a pathway is local?), the distinction between causal pathway analogies and mere evidential similarity, and whether the view can accommodate collective or institutional reasoning where individual pathways merge.

Write in a formal, polished register, using British English spelling and punctuation. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and avoid generic biographical questions.
