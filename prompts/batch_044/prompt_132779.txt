I am taking a seminar on classical Chinese logic and metaphysics, and I am trying to design a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—for how one actually interprets the White Horse Dialogue and its place in the School of Names. Rather than sorting people by which modern label they prefer, I want the branches to be concrete commitments about the argument's structure and about the School's broader fragments on designation and the hard-and-white debate.

Please construct a branching taxonomy that begins with this root question: Is the dialogue's conclusion—that a white horse is not a horse—best understood as expressing a genuine ontological separation, or as revealing a category mismatch between how compound and simple names map onto things?

From there, each internal node should be a specific claim that an interpreter can answer without knowing which position it leads to. Include nodes that ask: (a) whether substitutivity fails between compound and simple terms because chromatic and formal determinables belong to non-overlapping ontological registers; (b) whether the School's other arguments about pointing and designation commit it to kind-pluralism, to a meta-linguistic theory of reference, or to neither; and (c) whether the dialogue functions as a reductio of naive essentialism about kinds or as a positive defense of an aspect-based ontology in which color and shape are independently determinative.

The leaves should include at least: the strict logical-compositional reading; the chromatic-formal separation thesis; the nominal-reference mismatch view; the sophistical-rhetorical interpretation; a position that combines kind-pluralism with a rejection of substitutivity but denies ontological separation; and any additional unnamed combination your tree generates. For each leaf, state in one sentence its most awkward commitment—the counterintuitive consequence its defender must accept.

Please also flag any pair of leaves that share identical answers at every node and therefore cannot be separated by this tree. And frame every branch as a claim about the argument or about the relation between the dialogue and the fragments on designation and hardness-whiteness, never as a direct question about whether Gongsun Long was correct or about which label a student likes. The taxonomy should be usable for students familiar with the dialogue itself and with the basic fragments on pointing and the hard-white problem, but not with modern secondary literature, so do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific scholarly citations.
