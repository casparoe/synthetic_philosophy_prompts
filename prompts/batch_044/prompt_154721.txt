Restate the problem of dirty hands in politics—the thesis that political agents are structurally situated to face conflicts in which the right course of action requires committing a serious moral wrong, and that such conflicts are not merely tragic but partly constitutive of the role—entirely within the conceptual vocabulary of clinical medical ethics, specifically the frameworks of therapeutic privilege, emergency surgical triage, and the principle of double effect as applied to resource-rationing decisions. Once the translation is complete, provide a rigorous residue report: identify which structural elements of political dirty hands (for instance, the representative or fiduciary character of the agent, the public and irrevocable nature of democratic authorization, and the non-therapeutic purpose of state coercion) have precise analogues in clinical practice, which can only be mapped via strained metaphor, and which have no clinical counterpart whatsoever. In your report, take a position on whether the untranslatable residue indicates that medical ethics is conceptually impoverished relative to political morality, or rather that the political formulation relies on distinctions that become obscure when stripped of their institutional context.
