Please rank the following five conceptual frameworks for biological function into three tiers—Tier I (strongest explanatory integration), Tier II (partially adequate but structurally limited), and Tier III (least adequate)—based solely on their capacity to jointly resolve evolutionary exaptation and pathological dysfunction in which a trait retains its physical form yet fails its systemic contribution. Evaluate them by internal coherence and conceptual coverage, not by disciplinary popularity.

The frameworks are:
(A) The strict etiological account: a trait’s function is fixed by the effect for which it was positively selected in its evolutionary history.
(B) The strict causal-role account: a trait’s function is its present contribution to a capacity of the containing system, with no necessary reference to history.
(C) The historical-anchor-with-role-elasticity hybrid: selection history fixes the primary function, but present structural contribution can license derivative functional descriptions when selection pressures shift, provided the trait’s organizational role remains intact.
(D) The organizational-closure view: function is defined by a trait’s contribution to maintaining the self-producing network of constraints that constitutes the living system, evaluated independently of both evolutionary history and local causal contribution.
(E) The dispositional-contextual view: a trait’s function is the effect it is actually disposed to produce in the organism’s present environment and developmental state, regardless of selection history or systemic integration.

Structure your response in the following sections, with approximate lengths:
1. "The tier list" (about 80 words): State which frameworks fall into each tier.
2. "Separating cases" (about 180 words): Analyze one concrete exaptation scenario—feather architecture initially selected for thermal regulation later co-opted for aerodynamic lift—and one concrete dysfunction scenario—an enzymatic mutation that preserves molecular shape but disrupts metabolic pathway contribution. Explain exactly which tier placements these cases justify and which they undermine.
3. "Intra-tier reasoning" (about 200 words): For each tier, explain why its members cluster together—what shared strength or shared weakness binds them—and identify the specific conceptual trade-off that distinguishes Tier I from Tier II, and Tier II from Tier III.
4. "Verdict and residual tension" (about 150 words): Conclude whether the ranking implies that one framework should dominate, or whether the tension between historical explanation and physiological explanation is structurally unavoidable. If unavoidable, specify which philosophical commitment—historicism, presentism, or organizational autonomy—must be sacrificed to resolve it.

Do not provide citations, author names, bibliographies, or reading lists; reason solely from the conceptual architecture described above.
