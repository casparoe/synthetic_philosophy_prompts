I have been defending the view that in any genuinely tangled hierarchy—of the kind illustrated by Escher's Drawing Hands—the so-called inviolate level is not a fixed stratum, basal or transcendent, but a structurally necessary absence: a ghost frame that conditions every loop of the hierarchy yet is formally unrepresentable from within any internal level. On this reading, the sheet of paper is not a hidden substrate but an active framing condition that the hierarchy cannot encode without dissolving the representation-represented distinction. What would count against this? I want more than the familiar move of stepping outside to observe the frame—my claim concerns internal representability, not external access. Specifically: could a formal model of self-reference fully internalize its framing conditions without paradox? Would a reduction of observer-dependence in physics or cognitive science render the ghost frame dispensable? Are there constructions in mathematics—say, in universal computation or category-theoretic fixed points—where the framing level is present rather than absent? I ask you to rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, to say which ones a committed defender should concede versus dismiss by refining internal representability, and to identify what distinctive predictions the ghost-frame view makes that rival accounts treating the frame as just another node do not. Finally, assess whether the view is well insulated: if every counterexample can be redescribed as revealing a deeper ghost frame, is that theoretical resilience a virtue or a sign that the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability?
