I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been thinking about Douglas Hofstadter's suggestion that the self might not be confined to a single brain, but is instead a kind of pattern that could, in principle, persist in others. Viewing this through the lens of distributed systems, I find myself holding a cluster of intuitions about when an "I" can genuinely be spread across substrates, and I want to know whether a coherent principle lies behind them.

Here are my verdicts. In a classic split-brain case, where the corpus callosum is severed and the hemispheres operate with no informational exchange, I think there are now two distinct selves, since the unified control loop has been broken. If, however, my consciousness were maintained across three neural implants in separate bodies, synchronised with a brief latency but functioning as one continuous narrative and decision architecture, I believe that would be one genuinely distributed "I". By contrast, if a close colleague fully internalises my values and completes my projects after my biological death, that feels to me like legacy or influence rather than my self persisting. In a gradual replacement scenario, where my neurons are substituted over a decade with synthetic equivalents and a redundant backup copy runs in parallel but is never activated, I think the final synthetic brain is me, whereas the dormant backup never was. The case I keep turning over is this: during that transition, the backup is activated for exactly one second while the primary synthetic brain continues running, then is immediately shut down again. Does that moment create a brief distributed self spanning both substrates, does it corrupt the continuity of the primary self, or does it leave everything unchanged?

Could you help me identify a single principle, or perhaps a small family of competing ones, that captures these verdicts? I am especially interested in what your principle predicts for that one-second activation case, and whether accommodating it requires me to abandon any of my other intuitions. Please present your reasoning with precision, and write your response in British English.
