Give an example of a conceptual tension internal to Quine's philosophy between his naturalistic treatment of moral values and his well-known rejection of modal essentialism. Specifically, construct a scenario in which treating a moral property—say, the wrongness of a particular institutional practice—as a fully naturalized, empirically detectable feature seems to require modal commitments (for instance, that this wrongness is necessarily tied to specific causal or functional properties) that Quine's anti-essentialist framework cannot accommodate. Explain why this tension is not easily dissolved by appealing to his distinction between ontology and ideology, and identify what it reveals about whether a Quinean naturalized ethics can genuinely dispense with modal notions without collapsing moral properties into merely descriptive ones.
