(1) i'm a disjunctivist — when i actually see a red apple, my mental state is fundamentally different in kind from when i merely hallucinate one. no shared "highest common factor" mental state.
(2) i think the argument from illusion is wrong about its conclusion but right about its premise: hallucination and veridical perception can be phenominally indistinguishable, and that sameness is a real feature of experience, not just an epistemic limitation.
(3) i'm a direct realist — in the good case, the apple itself is part of my experience, not represented via an internal picture.
