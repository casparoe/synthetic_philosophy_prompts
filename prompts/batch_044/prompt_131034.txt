I'm taking this course on ancient economic thought and it's made me realize I don't actually know what I think about exchange, like at a gut level. So I tried listing cases and seeing if there's a pattern. Trading my old laptop worth maybe 400 bucks to a friend who needs it for school, and she only gives me 200 plus helping me move - that feels fine, maybe even good. A store charging 500 for bottled water during a blackout cause they can - not fine. Two people swapping homemade bread and jam, both feeling like they got the better deal - totally fine. But a pharma company buying up insulin in a region and reselling it at 10x because people will die without it - that feels like a different category of wrong, not just unfair but corrupt in some deeper way.

Meanwhile I'm stuck on this: a farmer sells crops cheap to a poor buyer and full price to a rich one, but then the poor buyer resell's half to the rich buyer at full price. Is that okay? And there's this other one that keeps flip-flopping for me - paying my neighbors 20 an hour to clean my apartment feels okay if she needs the cash, but somehow worse if I learn she's just doing it for fun and I'm overpaying out of guilt, even though the money's the same. Is there an Aristotelian-style principle - maybe about need versus exchange-value, or natural acquisition versus chrematistike (that money-making-for-its-own-sake thing) - that gets all these, or close? Which of my verdicts does it have to throw out? And what would it say about algorithmic pricing that scans how badly you need something and charges more if your desperate - is that closer to the farmer case or the insulin case?
