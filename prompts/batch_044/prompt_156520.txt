After a long argument with a friend about whether Gödel’s modal ontological argument is genuinely vulnerable to the charge that it defines God into existence, I kept running into the same conversational dead end: he would shut things down by saying, “You can’t define something into existence.” I need this slogan precisely disambiguated, because it was being used to dismiss the modal version without engaging its actual axiomatic structure.

Separate the distinct precise claims that sentence could be expressing in this context. Lay them out as a menu: (a) a logical point that stipulating a concept does not by itself entail that anything falls under it; (b) a semantic point, in the tradition of Kant’s objection, that “existence” is not a real predicate and therefore cannot be included in a definition; (c) a modal point that even if a concept includes necessary existence as a property, this does not guarantee that the concept is instantiated in the actual world unless one already accepts the corresponding modal framework; and (d) any other distinct thesis you can isolate that the slogan might be smuggling in. For each reading, state whether it is a platitude, whether it is substantive and defensible, whether it is false, and—crucially—whether it actually applies to Anselm’s original formulation, to Gödel’s modal version (where necessary existence is derived as a positive property within a modal system rather than inserted by definitional fiat), or to both.

Then tell me: which of these readings was my friend almost certainly invoking when he used the line to avoid discussing Gödel’s axioms about positive properties and modal entailment? Does any coherent reading genuinely undermine the modal argument without also undermining ordinary modal reasoning about mathematical or logical objects, and if so, what specific feature of the ontological argument—perhaps its treatment of “positive” as a primitive—makes it uniquely exposed on that reading? Finally, indicate whether removing that exposure (for instance, by reformulating the argument without treating necessary existence as a perfection) collapses the slogan’s force entirely, or whether the dismissal then shifts to a different reading on your menu.
