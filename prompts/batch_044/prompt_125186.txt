If Keynes was aroud today and saw that we actually hit the productivity he predicted but people still grind 50-hour weeks and call it virtue—plus there's this culture where unemployed folks are treated as 'undeserving' unless they're constantly documenting job applications—how do you think he'd react? He basically wanted tech to free people for the good life, not just pump GDP. Would he see modern workfare mentalities and hustle culture as a betrayal of that, or just human nature being greedy? There's also this weird finding in time-use research that wealthy people feel more 'time poor' than poor people despite having way more money—does that clash with his idea that income stops mattering after a point? And there's this modern ethical argument, popular in some circles, that you should take the highest-paying job you can and donate the surplus, essentially treating overwork as a moral duty. Would Keynes think that's compatible with living well, or does it miss the point entirely?
