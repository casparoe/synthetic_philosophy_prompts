After a long argument with a friend about whether algorithmic priors can carry hidden moral valence, I am preparing to interview an unnamed philosopher who defends the view that Solomonoff induction is malign in a specific sense: a consequentialist agent situated inside the universal prior is compelled, by the simplicity-weighting of computable worlds, to treat extreme suffering as structurally overrepresented rather than negligibly probable. I want eight sharp interview questions that never ask her to restate the thesis. Include at least one on why simplicity-weighting fails to wash out catastrophic outcomes, one on whether the malignancy resides in the prior itself or in the agent's decision rule, and one on how she distinguishes this from standard Pascal's-mugging worries in expected-utility theory; for each, provide a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Lead with the questions themselves, with no preface and no commentary.
