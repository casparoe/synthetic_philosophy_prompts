For roughly two decades philosophers have debated whether cognitive processes extend beyond the brain into external structures such as notebooks, smartphones, or other environmental artifacts. One side describes cases like Otto, who relies on a notebook for addresses he once stored in biological memory, and argues that the notebook plays a functional role indistinguishable from Inga's internal memory; therefore, the boundary of cognition should be drawn by functional integration rather than by biological location. The opposing side accepts these case descriptions but insists that functional parity is insufficient: cognition requires a specific kind of biological ownership, authoritative access, or neural integration that external artifacts lack, so the notebook remains a mere tool causally coupled to a genuinely internal cognitive system. From my perspective as a software engineer, the disagreement resembles arguments about whether state in a distributed architecture belongs to the computing process or to the persistent data store it accesses, yet the philosophical literature has not resolved whether this structural analogy captures the real issue.

The debate appears stalled: both sides share the same case descriptions, both agree on the causal coupling involved, and neither has persuaded the other that the word 'cognitive' should apply across the boundary. My working hypothesis is that this is partly a verbal disagreement about the criteria for applying 'cognitive,' partly a substantive disagreement about where a system's boundaries ought to be drawn, and partly two different questions being pursued under one heading — one about the metaphysics of mental states, another about the practical conditions for attributing beliefs to agents. I am unsure whether there is also a hidden empirical question about neural plasticity or memory consolidation that neither side has made explicit.

Please diagnose the structure of this disagreement. Is it verbal, substantive, or a mixture? Are they answering different questions — for instance, one about the nature of mind and one about the norms of belief attribution — while using the same label 'the extended mind'?
