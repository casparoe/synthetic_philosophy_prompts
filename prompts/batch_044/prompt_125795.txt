Give your credence, as a precise number between 0 and 1, that self-deception in the strict sense—an agent intentionally producing in herself a false belief that she simultaneously recognizes as false and as produced by her own design—is conceptually possible in a rational being with a unified point of view. Decompose your answer into three factors: first, the prior probability that human cognition allows functionally separated epistemic registers (for instance, distinct “ledgers” for practical commitment and reflective evaluation that are not automatically reconciled) to host contradictory beliefs without immediate collapse; second, the probability that intentional mental action can target one’s own doxastic states without being transparent to those states, on standard accounts of mental agency; and third, the probability that the phenomenon remains coherent if we impose a transparency condition requiring that the contradiction be reportable upon direct introspective query. Assign a rough number and a brief justification to each factor, combine them into an overall figure, and then state which factor contributes the most uncertainty to your result and what kind of evidence—empirical or conceptual—would most revise it.
