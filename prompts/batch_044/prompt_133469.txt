My study group got into it over whether tearing down Churchill or Columbus statues is just presentism. Leo said the man-of-his-time defence holds water: Jefferson and Churchill couldn't access the moral framework we have now, so blaming them is like blaming a Victorian surgeon for not using antibiotics. Priya said thats lazy -- abolitionists and anti-colonial voices existed in there own eras, so the knowledge was available, they just ignored it. Leo then brought in something he called institutional moral debt: individuals are nodes in structures, and blaming Columbus personally misses that the harm was systemically incentivised; if we tear down statues we're pretending one brick caused the wall. Priya said thats just the defence with extra steps, and if every wrongdoer is just a node then no one is ever accountable for systemic harm. Leo shot back with a symmetry argument: if moral progress is real, people in 2124 will condemn us for factory farming or training LLMs, so our current certainty is just epistemic dominance -- the present claiming authority only because it's later. Priya said there's a gap between not knowing and not wanting to know, and Churchill had critics he actively dismissed, so the ignorance wasn't innocent. They asked me to adjudicate each move. Is Leo's epistemic dominance point actually a real problem, or just fancy dresing? And who's more right overall?
