I've been trying to scope a research agenda for the coming year, and I keep hitting the same conceptual wall: whenever I ask colleagues whether "the alignment problem" is a single coherent challenge or an irreducible cluster, the answers seem to pass each other in the dark. Some frame it as a static constraint-satisfaction task—fix the objective specification or reward signal, then scale—while others insist it necessarily includes robustness, deceptive alignment, value learning, and what I would call ontological stability: the problem of preserving normative coherence while the system's own world-model undergoes structural revision or conceptual change. In a recent discussion with friends who read the same rationalist blogs, we noticed that nobody seems to agree on whether ontological stability is part of alignment proper, a separate long-term governance issue, or a philosophical red herring, and the conversation always stalls there.

My working hypothesis is that part of the stall is verbal—"alignment" does double duty as both a synchronic property of a fixed system at deployment and a diachronic property maintained across recursive self-modification—but I also think there may be a hidden substantive disagreement about whether reward misspecification and deceptive alignment are manifestations of one underlying mechanism or genuinely independent failure modes. There is also the possibility that the two camps are not disagreeing about facts at all, but answering different questions: one about what must hold for a specific trained model, another about what must hold across successive generations of self-improving agents with shifting ontologies. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there a real formal or empirical disagreement disguised behind the vocabulary?

Given that framing, I want an honest structural diagnosis rather than a survey of interpretations: is this disagreement verbal, substantive, or a case of two disciplines answering different questions under a single heading? Which hidden assumption—about fixed versus mutable ontologies, about synchronic versus diachronic scope, or about the unity of failure mechanisms—is actually doing the work? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually move the debate—a sharper conceptual distinction, a formal result, or a reframing of the problem—or should we expect the impasse to persist until the technical landscape itself forces a resolution? Please don't treat it as a request for a bibliography or a list of authors; I'm asking about the architecture of the disagreement itself.
