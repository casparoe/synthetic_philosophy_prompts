Before I respond, I want to understand exactly what work that slogan could be doing. Could you lay out, as a clear menu, the distinct precise theses that "altruism is immoral" might be expressing—ranging from tautological or definitional claims to radically substantive normative ones? For each reading, please say whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible, whether it is false, and whether it actually supports the classmate's move of treating all charitable obligations as illegitimate without further argument. In particular, I would like you to separate (i) the claim that altruism necessarily entails self-sacrifice; (ii) the claim that it treats another's need or suffering as an automatic, unearned claim on one's life, labor, or property; and (iii) any hidden reliance on the "trader principle"—the idea that moral interaction should be structured as exchange of value for value. Finally, please indicate which of these readings apply specifically to altruism conceived as an explicit ethical code, and which might also condemn ordinary benevolence or spontaneous aid to strangers.
