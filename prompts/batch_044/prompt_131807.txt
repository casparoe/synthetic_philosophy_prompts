I've been trying to square Dennett's view that mental states are real patterns visible only from the intentional stance with the intuition that genuinely real patterns ought to be detectable regardless of predictive stance. From the physical stance we find neurons firing; from the design stance we find functional architecture; only the intentional stance seems to deliver beliefs and desires. This produces a dilemma: either mental states reduce to physical patterns, making the intentional stance a dispensable heuristic, or they are stance-dependent projections with no more objectivity than seeing a face in clouds. Since Dennett rejects both reductionism and eliminativism, I wonder whether the resolution requires something like intermediate-level causal powers—structures that are not reducible to physics yet physically constrain lower-level dynamics—so that adopting the intentional stance tracks real, scale-specific powers rather than mere instrumental conveniences. Or does the very idea of stance-relative reality collapse into a form of instrumentalism about the mind?

Please answer in four sections: (1) Diagnosis of the false presupposition in the dilemma above, roughly 150 words; (2) How the physical, design, and intentional stances actually relate to pattern reality and objectivity, roughly 250 words; (3) Whether an intermediate-level-causation proposal can rescue the middle position without collapsing into emergence mysticism, roughly 200 words; (4) A bottom-line verdict on whether beliefs and desires are non-instrumentally real, roughly 150 words.
