Two people in my study group got stuck in an argumant about Dennetts real patterns and the three stances. One started with a thermostat: physical stance is atoms bouncing around, design stance is the feedback loop built to hold 72F, intentional stance is treating it like it "wants" warmth and predicting it'll kick on if you crack a window. The other said fine, but apply it to crypto prices. Physical stance is just database entries; design stance is the matching-engine algorithms; intentional stance is saying "the market fears inflation." He argued the intentional stance here is garbage - you can't predict BTC moves by attributing beliefs to "the market" any better than using order-book data, so there's no real pattern at that level.

She pushed back: Dennetts whole point is that a pattern is real only if it buys you predictions you can't get cheaper from below. "Market fear" lets you predict cross-asset correlations and sector rotations that you can't derive from individual trades, so the intentional stance is genuinely predictive and therefore real.

He replied that those predictions still fail constantly - bubbles, flash crashes - so if intentional-stance predictions are so powerful, why do they miss the big moves? At best it's post-hoc narrative.

She said physical-stance predictions miss crashes too - quantum mechanics doesn't warn you about black monday. The bar is comparative predictive power, not omniscience.

Then he dropped something he called the "stochastic stance": treating the price as pure noise with zero exploitable structure, neither physical law, designed mechanism, nor intentional agent. He said for crypto this is actually the best model, since all three Dennett stances overfit structure onto random walk.

She said that's not a stance at all, just giving up, and Dennett already allows for that: when lower-level patterns exhaust the variance, you don't need a higher stance.

Can you adjudicate move by move? Rate each step from clearly wrong to clearly right, and say whether his "stochastic stance" is a coherent fourth option or just the absence of pattern. Also, are both missing something about how the design stance applies to decentralized systems?
