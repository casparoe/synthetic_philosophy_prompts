I've been working through the relationship between the thermodynamic arrow of time and the psychological arrow, particularly in light of the Past Hypothesis and its explanatory role in statistical mechanics. Recently, I constructed a hypothetical scenario that I'm calling a "localized temporal inversion pocket": imagine an isolated, macroscopic region—large enough in principle to contain a functioning observer—embedded within a universe that is otherwise near thermal equilibrium, where a rare but statistically permitted fluctuation produces a sustained local decrease in entropy over a timescale comparable to a human lifetime, not merely the brief, microscopic reversals we observe in Brownian motion or in Maxwell-demon-style setups. Within this pocket, the local gradient runs opposite to the global cosmological gradient established by the low-entropy origin of the universe.

My confusion is this: if the psychological arrow of time is genuinely coupled to the local thermodynamic gradient—so that memory formation, causal reasoning, and the direction of experienced time all track entropy increase—then an observer inside such a pocket should experience what we, observing from the outside with our globally aligned arrow, would describe as living backward, remembering what we call the future and anticipating what we call the past. This seems to imply that causation itself is not anchored globally by the Big Bang boundary condition, but is instead regionally indexed, with different pockets sustaining incompatible directions of cause and effect. Yet physicists and philosophers routinely speak of the arrow of time as if it were a universal feature of nature rather than a locally variable field. Where exactly does this reasoning go wrong? Is the error in assuming that macroscopic observers could stably exist in such a pocket, in the claim that psychological orientation tracks local rather than global gradients, or in the deeper assumption that the Past Hypothesis licenses any meaningful notion of regional temporal inversion at all?
