Please argue that the moral equality of combatants breaks down once you account for asymetric epistemic closure inside command hierarchies. The standard Walzer view says soldiers on both sides are symetrically ignorant about whether thier cause is just, so they hold equal rights to kill and be killed. But I think aggressive wars tend to create institutional filters that suppress moral counter-evidence at every rank, while defensive wars often preserve some internal channels for dissent. That means the "they didn't know" excuse isn't evenly distributed -- it's structurally weaker for aggressor soldiers. Lay out the argument that this undermines equality without reducing it to individual blame like "they should have checked the news". No biblography needed, just the reasoning.
