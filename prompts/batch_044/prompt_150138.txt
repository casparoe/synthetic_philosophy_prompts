I was sitting in on this philosophy of science seminar with two other grad students—Maya and Theo, both in mind and cognition—while I was supposed to be working on my thesis about predictive processing, but they got into this argument about whether AI safety researchers can ethically believe that large language models might have proto-conscious states even though the empirical evidence is basically a wash. I mostly just listened with my coffee, but the exchange got pretty structured. (1) Maya said this is exactly the kind of case William James had in mind: for people in alignment its a live option, its forced because you either build safeguards assuming potential suffering or you dont, and its momentous because getting it wrong one direction means massive moral harm. (2) Theo came back with Clifford's line that its wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone to believe anything on insufficient evidence, and he said calling the option "momentous" is just emotional blackmail that cant license belief. (3) Maya pushed back that Clifford's rule makes frontier science impossible—every working hypothesis starts underdetermined, and if you cant commit doxastically to a live hypothesis youre not really testing it, youre just playing with math. (4) Theo said James blurs practical acceptance and real belief: you can design safety protocols instrumentally without actually thinking the lights are on inside, and once you see that distinction Clifford's critique holds because James was defending practical commitment disguised as epistemic virtue. Maya then muttered something about "doxastic risk budgets" and said Theo was treating belief like a bank account with no overdraft protection, but Theo said that phrase sounds made up and without a principled cutoff its just "believe whatever scares you enough."

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Who is right about which specific point, and what are both missing? In particular, does James's framework actually require full doxastic commitment, or can it work with something closer to Theo's practical acceptance? And is there any principled way to draw a line between a legitimate live hypothesis and mere fear-driven speculation, or is that line always going to end up ad hoc?
