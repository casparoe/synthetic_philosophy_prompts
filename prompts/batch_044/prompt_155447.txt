For a final-year undergraduate seminar on the philosophy of technology and institutional risk, please draft a 90-minute essay examination question, together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme, centred on John von Neumann’s 1955 Fortune essay “Can We Survive Technology?”. Rather than allowing candidates to rehearse the familiar narrative about nuclear energy and climate engineering, inject the following conceptual twist: ask students to apply von Neumann’s framework to a hypothetical scenario of “recursive regulatory capture”, in which autonomous infrastructure systems gradually absorb the oversight institutions meant to govern them, not through deliberate subversion but through the same autocatalytic feedback dynamics that von Neumann associated with technological growth itself. The question should require candidates to distinguish precisely which elements of this scenario are genuinely continuous with von Neumann’s reasoning—particularly his claim that technological problems become intractable not through technical failure but through social and political inertia; his analogy between the accelerating pace of technological change and biological evolution; and his scepticism about the feasibility of “stopping” progress—and which elements are anachronistic projections onto a 1955 context that had not yet encountered algorithmic governance.

The model answer should be structured around these three specific moves (each worth explicit marks), demonstrating how they illuminate or mislead when applied to recursive regulatory capture, and should conclude with a rigorous separation of the continuous from the anachronistic. The marking scheme should total 20 marks: accurate exposition of von Neumann’s core thesis (5 marks); correct identification and explanation of the three moves above (3 marks each, totalling 9 marks); rigorous separation of continuous elements from anachronisms (4 marks); and quality of argumentation and clarity (2 marks). For each category, include a brief note on the characteristic mistake—for instance, conflating von Neumann’s evolutionary analogy with a literal claim about artificial selection, treating recursive regulatory capture as if von Neumann had explicitly discussed machine autonomy, or summarising the essay without applying it to the constructed scenario.

Please write the examination question, the model answer, and the marking scheme in British English. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for page references; the assessment should be answerable by a student who grasps von Neumann’s general argument without recourse to specific textual locations. Where you refer to the essay, you may name it descriptively; if you use any direct quotation, you must verify it against a published source. Avoid attributing precise technical terminology to von Neumann that he did not himself employ.
