After a three-hour argument with a close friend about whether populism is ever democratically legitimate, I want the adversarial-collaboration document we should have written instead of talking past each other. The two parties are the Sovereignist—who holds that democratic legitimacy requires approximating a discoverable, pre-institutional popular will that exists independently of aggregation mechanisms and that populist movements can legitimately claim to embody—and the Constructivist Pluralist—who holds that “the will of the people” has no ontological status prior to or outside the procedural, legal, and discursive mechanisms that filter heterogeneous preferences, and that treating it as an independent entity is a category error that licenses dangerous exclusions.

Write the joint statement both could sign. It must contain:

1. A numbered section of genuinely shared claims—about the nature of democratic representation, the reality of preference heterogeneity, the risks of false unification, and the conditions under which a government can be said to act for a collective—not vacuous boilerplate.

2. A disagreements table: for each residual dispute, a neutral statement of the issue, followed by the precise formulation the Sovereignist accepts for their own view and the precise formulation the Constructivist accepts for theirs. The disagreements should include: (a) the aggregation-independence thesis—whether there is a coherent collective preference that exists independently of the rules used to aggregate it; (b) the correspondence-versus-fairness crux—whether democratic legitimacy requires correspondence to a pre-existing will or is satisfied solely by procedural fairness among irreconcilable interests; and (c) the boundary-circularity problem—whether defining who counts as “the people” can be done without already presupposing the popular will one seeks to identify.

3. For each crux, specify one concrete case or argument both sides agree actually bears on it, with each side’s explicit prediction of what that case shows. For the boundary-circularity crux, use the case of a secession referendum in which the seceding region and the remaining polity each invoke “the will of the people” with mutually exclusive boundaries of membership; both should agree this case is diagnostic, but record the Sovereignist prediction (that it reveals a hidden prior will of the remaining polity) and the Pluralist prediction (that it demonstrates “the people” is always an artifact of prior constitutional framing).

Keep the language precise and conceptually dense—avoid generic summaries of populism that could be regurgitated from pretraining. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources. Do not use verbatim quotations from real texts. The document should be constructive: formulations neither side would reject as misrepresenting their position, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than obscured. Format as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table. Maximum 600 words.
