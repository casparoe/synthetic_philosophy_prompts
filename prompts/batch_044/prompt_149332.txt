For an upper-level seminar on philosophical methodology (12 students, 90 minutes), design a structured, runnable activity that lets participants experience how an intuition pump smuggles a conclusion by quietly redefining what counts as the same case across successive stages. Center it on a staged decision protocol about synthetic neural replacement: in round 1 the group votes on whether personal identity survives 10% replacement, in round 2 on 50%, and in round 3 on 90%, but between rounds the facilitator introduces a non-functional signature tone that accompanies the replacement procedure—first mentioned only in a procedural footnote, then embedded in the official case description—without announcing that anything philosophically relevant has changed. Assign four rotating roles (proponent, skeptic, recorder, rule-enforcer), require public binary commitments at each stage before open discussion closes, and use symbolic stakes (points, not grades). Provide a minute-by-minute timeline, the explicit rules that prevent abstention, a debrief that maps each round onto framing effects, anchoring on incidental features, and the illusion of modal stability—identifying the specific stage where group polarization is predictable and how to handle it—and an honest note on which feature of real intuition-pump persuasion—the pre-theoretical, gut-level sense that this just feels like survival—the role-play can approximate but not genuinely reproduce, so the facilitator must supply it. Keep the design fully self-contained with no required readings or prior familiarity with specific authors.
