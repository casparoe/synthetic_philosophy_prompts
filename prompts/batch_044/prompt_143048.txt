I want you to take the Malcolm-Dennett debate about whether dreams are experiences — Malcolm's argument that there is no dream subject present to the dream events in the way required for experience, and Dennett's claim that dream narratives are constructed upon awakening rather than lived through during sleep — and translate each position systematically into that phenomenological vocabulary. For Malcolm: can his denial of dream experience be expressed as a claim about the absence of a retentional-protentional horizon, or about the failure of noetic acts to correlate with a noema? Then report the residue: what in each original position cannot be mapped onto retention, protention, noesis, or noema without distortion? Is there a phenomenological description of dream-consciousness — perhaps involving a modified temporal synthesis or a peculiar noematic structure — that neither analytic position can accommodate, and does that point to a genuine blind spot in Malcolm and Dennett, or to an unmotivated extension of phenomenological concepts beyond their proper domain?
