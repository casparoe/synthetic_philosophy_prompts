I keep noticing this weird split in how we treat commodifcation. You can sell your plasma twice a week but not your kidney once; you can drop half a million on a lobbyist to push a bill but you can't hand a voter fifty bucks for their ballot. And now there's these new predicition markets for scientific replication—people bet on whether a study holds up, and the price signal gets used to allocate grant money. People freak out about that, call it a repugnant market, but we already pay peer reviewers nothing and hide research behind subscription walls, which is its own kind of pricing. So what philosophical commitment makes one market corrupt and the other just 'how things work'? Does it presuppose that some goods—like bodies or votes or scientific truth—have an intrinsic dignity that price can't capture? Or is it about who holds power in the transaction? And if the issue is corruption of motivation, why doesn't the fact that reviewers work for free and publishers charge universities not count as the same kind of problem?
