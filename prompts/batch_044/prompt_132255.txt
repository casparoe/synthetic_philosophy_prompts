I am drafting notes for a short column on the afterlife of the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique, and I would welcome your comments before I develop it into a full piece. Rather than treating their 1979 argument as a blanket rejection of adaptationism, I want to suggest that its lasting contribution was methodological: it placed the burden of proof on anyone claiming a trait is an optimally designed adaptation, rather than an architectural by-product, exaptation, or consequence of phylogenetic inertia. I then apply this framework to a case rarely discussed in evolutionary biology—the origins of consolidated, monophasic sleep in modern humans. Where popular accounts treat eight-hour uninterrupted sleep as a deep evolutionary adaptation disrupted by artificial light, I propose it is better understood as a spandrel of clock-time labour regimes and artificial illumination acting upon an ancestrally plastic, biphasic rhythm. I also observe that much contemporary evolutionary psychology invokes “spandrel” only as a dismissive label for traits it cannot explain, then proceeds with adaptationist narratives for cognition, language, and risk behaviour without seriously addressing developmental plasticity or non-adaptive structural constraints. My aim is not to deny adaptation altogether, but to ask whether the field has genuinely absorbed the methodological lesson, or merely domesticated the vocabulary.

Could you comment on these notes? In particular: is my reading of Gould and Lewontin accurate—did they genuinely intend a shift in the burden of proof rather than a wholesale anti-adaptationist stance? Does the sleep example work as an illustration, or does it risk collapsing back into the same adaptationist framing I am criticising? And is my claim about evolutionary psychology’s domestication of the term defensible, or does it misrepresent current practice in the field? I am especially interested in whether the argument feels coherent to someone working within evolutionary theory, rather than merely provocative from the outside.
