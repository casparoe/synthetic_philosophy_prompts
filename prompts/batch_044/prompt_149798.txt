I'm on the review board for the Meridian Foundation's Anticipatory Governance Initiative — yes, the name is terrible, don't blame me. We recieved a proposal asking for 40M over ten years to deploy an AI forecasting system that directs climate-adaptation grants across Southeast Asia. The researchers say that since inaction also produces unknown knock-on effects, we should just run expected-value reasoning with very wide confidance intervals and move forward.

Half the board buys that. The other half is hitting them with consequentialist cluelessness, but not the shallow version — they mean we don't just have bad probabilities, we can't even enumerate what outcomes will matter to people in 2055, so comparative evaluation is structurally broken. There's also this weird mechanism in the proposal: they want to automatically divert 15% of the budget into interventions that the model flags as having high epistemic opacity, basically treating ignorance as a portfolio hedge. One reviewer called it institutionalized cluelessness and wants to reject outright.

We vote Thursday. Draft the board memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a concrete recommendation and reasoning the researchers could actually respond to. Take the strongest cluelessness argument seriously, especially the part about not knowing our own future values, not just risk. No citation lists or reading lists; write it like I'm circulating it to the other members. Maybe fix my typo if you want, or leave it, I don't care.
