i've been thinking that internalism about practical reasons is basically right, but we should ditch the desire-based version williams uses. instead, an agent only has an internal reason to act if there's a cognative-accessible path from their current beliefs and reasoning capacities to the conclusion they ought to do X — regardless of wether they actually care. this makes internalism stricter in some ways, looser in others. what would count against it? i want defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward, including cases where the view predicts we have reasons we clearly don't, and whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability. also, be honest: would standard internalists or externalists even recognize this as a real position, or is it just a confused middle?
