I serve on my high school’s District Ethics Board for Student-Run Philanthropy, where three students and one faculty advisor meet twice a month to approve grants under five thousand dollars; we have about twenty minutes per proposal, no philosophy background is assumed among reviewers, and any step that is irreducibly a value judgment must be flagged for the advisor’s sign-off rather than masked as technical. Because our charter now requires us to justify allocations between local community programs and international relief, I need a reusable decision procedure that translates the philosophical debate over whether compatriots have special moral claims—versus cosmopolitan impartiality—into an ordered audit my board can actually run, with explicit stop rules, hard cases, and honest markers of where judgment cannot be eliminated. The procedure should include a “moral-lottery filter” that asks whether a nationality-based priority depends solely on birth location with no further institutional reciprocity, and an “institutional co-authorship audit” that checks whether beneficiaries share in the tax, legal, or democratic structures that generate the obligation; please apply it to three proposals: (1) a winter heating subsidy for low-income families in our district; (2) emergency medical supplies for a refugee camp abroad facing an outbreak; and (3) a heritage-language preservation program restricted to citizens, where the board is likely to feel sympathy but the procedure may deliver an unwelcome answer. In your response, separate what is broadly settled in the debate—such as the universal weight of severe basic-need deprivation—from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who study associative duties and global justice, and do not present any contested view, including the claim that shared nationality by itself generates a weighty special obligation, as the consensus position. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot settle, such as the precise threshold at which cultural preservation counts as a basic need or how to weigh distant suffering against local identity, and flag those nodes as requiring explicit value judgment rather than algorithmic resolution.
