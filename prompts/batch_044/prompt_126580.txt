I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument with a friend about whether Lewisian counterpart theory genuinely eliminates the need for transworld identity, or whether it smuggles identity back in through the similarity relation. I ended up defending a specific variant: counterpart relations should not be grounded in bare qualitative resemblance, but in a kind of functional-role continuity indexed to a world-bound causal-historical trajectory. On this view, my counterpart in another world is not merely someone who looks or behaves like me, but someone who occupies the same structural node in an isomorphic causal network; consequently, in worlds where physical laws or causal structures diverge radically, I may have no counterpart at all, rather than an arbitrarily dissimilar one. This seems to block the “wild counterpart” problem and to prevent modal claims about me from collapsing into arbitrary similarity judgments. My friend countered that this move simply redefines “counterpart” until it operates as disguised Kripkean transworld identity, since the causal-historical trajectory is precisely what purports to track me across worlds, and that allowing me to deny counterparts in inconvenient worlds renders my view unfalsifiable.

What would genuinely count against this causal-historical counterpart position? I am not asking for the standard fission objections or essential-origin intuitions, but for the specific defeaters: which formal results in counterpart logic, which considerations about biological species persistence or artifact identity, or which modal intuitions about survival under radical physical change would undermine the claim that causal anchoring distinguishes counterpart theory from transworld identity? I want an honest ranking—from fatal to merely awkward—of the evidence and arguments that should move a defender of this view, including an assessment of whether the theory has been qualified into unfalsifiability by permitting the denial of counterparts in problematic worlds, and whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a vice. Please indicate which defeaters a committed defender of this position would themselves concede as damaging, versus which they would dismiss as relying on pre-theoretic intuition, and be frank about what feature of the idea the argument cannot reproduce without direct philosophical supplementation.
