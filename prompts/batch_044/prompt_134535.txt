hey, i'm on the advisory commitee for our transitional justice board. we're deadlocked over whether to grant conditional amnesty to mid-level bureaucrats who ran the "disappearance logistics" — filing, scheduling transports, maintaining databases — not the direct killers. they want amnesty in exchange for mapping the bureaucratic architecture: sign-off chains, hidden records, office coordination. victim families say we're trading suffering for filing-cabinet confessions; investigators say without structural disclosure we'll never reconstruct command.

our charter borrows language from South Africa's TRC, Argentina's disappearances commission, and Rwanda's gacaca community accounting, but it never specified whether "full disclosure" requires direct participation or just systemic knowledge. half wants amnesty restricted to direct perpetrators; half wants to include these clerks if they deliver a verified system map within 90 days.

draft the memo for thursday's vote. recommend a concrete course — approve with conditions, deny, or approve with conditions — with a clear time frame and verification step. engage the strongest argument on the losing side seriously: either that administrative neutrality is a moral shield, or that we're commodifying trauma for data. make it something i can actually circulate, so no bibliographies or citation lists, and don't write it like a philosophy seminar — but don't hide that the philosophy is driving the disagreement.
