After a long argument last night with a friend about whether true contradictions could ever be rationally believed, I think I have isolated a tractable gap in the dialetheism literature suitable for a one-semester research proposal of about six thousand words. The standard debate asks whether dialetheism is true, defends it with metaphysical cases such as the liar or sorites paradoxes, and then appeals to paraconsistent logic to block explosion. What neither side addresses is whether dialetheism requires a distinctive normative theory of belief revision: if some contradictions are true, rational agents need a principled way to isolate them so that arbitrary conclusions do not become warranted, yet existing paraconsistent frameworks either leave inference too permissive or smuggle in classical consistency constraints that dialetheists reject. I propose developing a middle-ground closure principle, localized non-transmission, according to which a true contradiction in domain D licenses inferences within D but blocks cross-domain propagation unless an explicit bridge premise is independently warranted. The project has three parts: first, formalizing the principle within a standard paraconsistent belief-set framework without inventing new logic; second, testing it against three objections—slippery slope to trivialism, the problem of distinguishing contradiction from error, and the charge of ad hocness; third, assessing whether the principle can be motivated independently of dialetheism so it does not collapse into a defensive maneuver. Success would be a defensible normative framework that dialetheists can adopt without abandoning coherence and that opponents can reject on non-question-begging grounds. The project fails if localized non-transmission either collapses into classical consistency requirements or proves impossible to motivate without presupposing the falsity of dialetheism. Given the scope, I will restrict the formal work to propositional cases and avoid modal or set-theoretic extensions. I will supply citations myself; the proposal should stand on its conceptual architecture alone.
