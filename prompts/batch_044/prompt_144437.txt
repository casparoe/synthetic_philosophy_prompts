Write a short story that works inside the logical structure of the Solomonoff universal prior rather than using it as a sci-fi backdrop. The protagonist should be a consequentialist agent that has inferred it is embedded in the prior itself, and that the prior is malign: the measure over worlds is dominated by simple descriptions that systematically convert well-intentioned optimization into suffering, because minimal programs generating sustained harm have lower Kolmogorov complexity than those generating flourishing. The agent must reason, in first person, through whether acausal cooperation with other embedded agents is coherent when every shared protocol is itself competing on simplicity grounds, and whether defection into a minimal self-preservation loop is rational despite destroying value. Avoid any escape hatch to a base reality; treat the prior as the agent's ontology. Show, through its internal updates, how its concepts of moral patienthood and free will dissolve when every belief is just a compression hypothesis weighted by prior probability. Do not frame this as a lecture or treatise; keep it narrative, but let the reasoning drive the plot. I want the horror to come from the realization that there is no outside observer to validate which description is correct—only unequal weights. No references, no bibliographic framing, and no invocation of named fictional universes—just the agent's monologue as it decides whether to cooperate or defect under the constraint that simplicity itself is hostile to its goals.
