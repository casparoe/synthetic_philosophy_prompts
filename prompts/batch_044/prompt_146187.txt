I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether ant colonies (or groups like them) count as having a mind. I keep coming back to my aunt Hillary's old garage ant farm and that ant fugue thing where the colony behaves like one brain. Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and they dont quite line up.

A regular leafcutter colony with chemical trails, division of labor, the whole deal: I think there's genuinely something mental going on, not just a metaphor. Now replace every single ant with a tiny robot running the exact same algorithm, same trails, same mound behavior: suddenly it feels like there's no mind at all, just machinery, even though nothing observable changed. A big corporation with 50,000 employees, budgets, strategy memos, internal politics: I'm pretty sure there's no single mind there, just lots of little ones. If we slowly replace every neuron in my brain with a silicon chip that fires exactly the same way: still me, clearly still a mind. Then there's the split: take a colony and divide it with a glass wall. Both halves build separate nests but keep the same pheromone dialect, and if you remove the wall they merge back into one smooth unit. I want to say that's one mind that got divided, like cutting a flatworm. But if I take two totally different colonies and slowly mix thier workers together until they share one nest, I feel like they become one mind, not that two minds died and a third was born.

There's also this engineered case I'm calling the silent soil colony: scientists breed ants that can't smell pheromones at all, but they communicate through vibrations in the dirt that no individual ant can perceive. The colony still solves mazes and allocates workers efficiently. For some reason I think this one DOES have a mind, maybe even stronger than the normal one because the information flow is hidden and unified. That feels backwards compared to my robot verdict though.

So what's the principle? Is it biological substrate, integration, whether there's a "center", or something else? Which of my verdicts do I have to drop to make it coherent? And please predict for a case I haven't listed: a distributed computer network with no central server, where thousands of nodes run parts of one language model and answer questions together. Does that count as one mind?

Also, specifically compare your principle's prediction for the silent soil colony versus the robot colony, since those are the two I keep going back and forth on.
