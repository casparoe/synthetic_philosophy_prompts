Before I treat such a form as disposable, I want to understand what is genuinely at stake.

Please describe the most important implications of Burkean conservatism and Chesterton's fence for how a writer ought to treat inherited literary conventions whose original function is no longer transparent. In particular, address whether the epistemic humility these views demand applies with equal force to aesthetic form and to social institutions, and what costs—if any—attach to treating narrative tradition as purely instrumental.
