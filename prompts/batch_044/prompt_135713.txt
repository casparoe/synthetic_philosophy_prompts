I am designing a single ninety-minute graduate seminar for philosophy-of-mind students who understand reinforcement-learning mechanics but have not engaged with consciousness theory. The idea to be taught is not whether RL agents literally suffer, but why the structural analogy between reward signals and affective valence fails unless the reward function involves sustained deviation from a learned homeostatic baseline rather than transient scalar feedback. Plan the session's explanatory arc: the precise order in which the components must be introduced—starting from functional reward prediction error, moving to the distinction between tracking utility and experiencing valence, then introducing the homeostatic-baseline model—along with the single concrete scenario that should carry the argument: an agent lacking explicit goal-representation but possessing a continuously perturbed reward-channel tied to survival-state maintenance, specifying exactly what this scenario must include and exclude to prevent anthropomorphic projection. Map the predictable misconceptions in the sequence students will form them, from "reward is just a number" through "any optimization process has feelings," indicating exactly where in the arc each must be intercepted. Provide one diagnostic question for the end that distinguishes understanding structural valence conditions from merely repeating the homeostasis claim. Finally, state explicitly which adjacent literature—such as detailed neurophenomenological comparisons or specific architectural blueprints—should be named as out of scope for this level.
