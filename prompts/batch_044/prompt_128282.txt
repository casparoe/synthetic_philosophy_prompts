I'm trying to build a practical framework for evaluating whether and how people should approach experiences that might radically alter their values, preferences, or self-conception—things like irreversible medical personality interventions, long-duration psychedelic protocols, religious conversion, or migration into value-systems that are initially incommensurable with one's own. Before I compare any particular account, I want the full specification of adequacy.

Write the desiderata that any theory of transformative experience must satisfy to count as adequate for this purpose—not just for describing the phenomenon, but for guiding decision and evaluation. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, flag whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference, and note any tension with other items on the list. Be sure to cover: (a) the epistemic gap between pre-experience and post-experience evaluation, including the special problem of temporarily transformative or "reversible" states in which values shift and then revert, leaving a single agent with competing authority claims; (b) the distinction between first-person authority over one's transformed values and the legitimate scope for third-party predictive, protective, or normative assessment; (c) the decision problem—how a rational agent should choose before knowing "what it is like"—and specifically whether the theory must avoid collapsing either into pure expected-utility calculation or into an unreasoned leap; (d) the collective or institutional dimension, in which a community, clinician, or state must decide whether to facilitate, permit, or block an individual's transformative path; (e) boundary conditions that distinguish genuine value-transformation from trauma response, manipulation, or mere preference drift, with the empirical or conceptual marker that does the work in each case; and (f) consistency with what is known about personality stability, neuroplasticity, and long-term value change, without letting neuroscience overdetermine the normative framework.
