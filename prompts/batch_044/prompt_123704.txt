i'm the ethics compliance officer for our city health dept's behavorial unit. we get about three nudge proposals a month—default opt-ins, cafeteria layouts, reminder texts using social norms, auto-enrollment in wellness programs, stuff like that. i have maybe twenty minutes per proposal, i dont have a philosophy degree, my director signs off on my one-page memo, and if i approve something manipulative the council chews me out or we end up in court. i need a reusable decision proceedure i can run step by step: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where it stops with a hard no, where it hands the call to me because it's values not facts, and where it's guaranteed to misfire. ground it in actual philosophy of paternalism and autonomy—mill's harm principle, the soft vs hard paternalism distinction, the idea that nudges can bypass rational deliberation even when choice is technically preserved, and the idea that the person should be able to reverse the decision easily without penalty—but translate it so a non-philosopher can use it. the procedure must handle the hard cases: nudges that exploit biases people would want corrected upon reflection, domains where people lack stable preferences like mental health or long-term savings, and nudges that serve collective goods rather than the individual's own ends.

apply it to three proposals:
1. driver's license renewal form with organ donation default opt-in and a one-click opt-out.
2. school cafeteria redesign putting water at eye level and soda behind a counter that requires asking staff.
3. auto-enrolling all city employees in a mental-health screening check-in, with opt-out only available after watching a 5-minute video explaining benefits.

make sure one answer is one i wont like—i'm generally pro-nudge but the procedure should be honest. include worked examples of a clear pass, a clear fail, and the zone where i just have to own the judgment call. flag exactly what the procedure cant do, like predicting long-term preference change or resolving conflicts between collective health and individual privacy. no bibliographies or citations needed, i just need the tool.
