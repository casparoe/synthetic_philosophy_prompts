Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind, Mara and Elias, debating whether a first-person "I" can be genuinely distributed across two physically separate brains. Mara defends a Hofstadter-inspired view: the self is not a substance but a self-referential strange loop of symbols and memories that can, in principle, run on multiple substrates if the loop remains continuous. Elias defends a singularist view: first-person ownership requires a non-divisible point of phenomenological access, so distributing the loop across brains either destroys the "I" or merely creates a highly transparent "we". The dialogue should center on a specific hypothetical case they call "the Bridged Autobiography"—a sustained, bidirectional neural coupling that allows two people to share working memory and episodic recall in real time, so that each can genuinely think "I remember walking through that door yesterday" when only the other was present. Mara should argue that this is not mere empathy or knowledge-transfer but a single loop with two nodes; Elias should argue that the pronoun "I" still fractures at the boundary of each brain's immediate proprioceptive field, and that shared recall is just an extreme form of intersubjectivity. Require that they respond directly to each other's arguments: for instance, Elias should address Mara's claim that the loop's continuity matters more than its substrate, and Mara should address Elias's claim that proprioception anchors ownership irreducibly. The dialogue should not resolve; it should end with the tension intact, but the reader should be able to tell which view is which and what hangs on the case.
