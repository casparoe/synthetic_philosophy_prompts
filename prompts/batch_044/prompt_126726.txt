Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of analogical concept-formation in Bongard problems (who maintains that solving them requires explicit cross-domain relational mapping rather than feature extraction) and a defender of statistical few-shot induction (who maintains that parameterized pattern recognizers can solve them through feature-space interpolation without analogy machinery). Present the result as exactly five numbered items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state the claims both sides accept about the formal structure of the problems and the criteria for successful induction from minimal examples. Items 2 and 3 should formulate the residual disagreements in neutral wording that each camp would endorse for its own position — specifically, whether compositional relation-binding is a necessary cognitive mechanism, and whether persistent failure on problems requiring analogies across perceptual domains demonstrates a lack of analogical capacity or merely insufficient training distribution. Item 4 should identify the central crux — whether solving a genuinely novel problem after seeing only one example per class involves constructing an explicit structural analogy or interpolating within a learned statistical manifold — together with the concrete test case both sides agree would bear on it (a left/right distinction governed by an abstract relational property instantiated in shapes that share no low-level visual features), plus the prediction each side makes about the outcome. Item 5 should distinguish the substantive disagreements from any that are merely verbal, explaining which conceptual distinctions are doing the real work.
