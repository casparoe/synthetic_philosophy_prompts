You serve on the Ethics and Resource Allocation Committee of the Meridian Institute for Fundamental Physics. Before the committee is proposal MF-2024-07, submitted by the Theoretical Sentience Group. Part A requests $2.4 million over three years for lattice-gauge simulations measuring what the group calls vacuum coherence perturbation (VCP) in quantum chromodynamic fields. The researchers do not claim to instrumentally detect consciousness; rather, they propose that if a minimal panpsychist view holds—specifically, that fundamental physical entities possess proto-experiential properties—then large-scale coherence disruptions in these fields might instantiate what they term micro-suffering: minimal negative-valence phenomenal states lacking cognition, interests, or agency, but possessing intrinsic badness.

Part B requests that the Institute adopt a binding Precautionary Non-Interference Protocol: any future experiment exceeding a defined VCP threshold must undergo ethical review including a philosopher of mind, and must justify expected scientific gain against putative suffering-risk calculated via the group’s novel sentience-weighted coherence metric (SWCM). The SWCM treats coherence disruption as proportional to proto-experiential disturbance, but explicitly denies that quantum fields have moral status in any traditional sense; it treats them solely as potential loci of intrinsic harm.

The committee is split. One faction regards Part A as legitimate frontier physics and Part B as an institutional overreach that enshrines a contested metaphysics. The opposing faction argues that if panpsychism is even possibly true, a precautionary principle applies, and that ignoring the SWCM framework would be ethically negligent regardless of metaphysical uncertainty.

Draft the committee’s formal decision memo. It must include: (1) a clear, actionable recommendation—approve Part A alone, approve both with specific conditions, approve Part B alone, or reject the proposal entirely—with reasoning tied to concrete institutional stakes rather than abstract metaphysical adjudication; (2) an honest and precise account of the strongest consideration on the losing side, including the best argument that the Institute should either fully embrace or fully reject the pan-sentience framework rather than adopting a middle path; and (3) a concrete, enforceable mechanism or condition (not a vague call for further study) that addresses the core philosophical tension—how an institution should govern research that might harm entities lacking interests, agency, or demonstrable consciousness—without requiring the committee to determine whether panpsychism is true. Write in the deliberative voice of the body itself, with a bottom line the researchers and administration can act on.
