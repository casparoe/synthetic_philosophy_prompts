I serve on the Acquisition and Stewardship Committee of the Meridian Foundation for Ephemeral Art, and we must vote by the end of the month on whether our holding of Sequence No. 7 by the late artist Mara Kessler still satisfies the bequest clause requiring that "the original material instantiation of the work" remain in our permanent care. Kessler's piece was a site-specific installation of chemically unstable pigments that degraded entirely by 2019; in 2022, the Foundation commissioned what its conservators call a procedural regeneration—not a manual copy, but an algorithmically guided reconstruction in which original substrate fragments were embedded within a new synthetic matrix, executed by technicians following Kessler's own published parametric rules rather than her hand. The donor's heirs now contend that because the material substrate is mostly new and execution was delegated, what we hold is a reproduction, not the original, and therefore the bequest should revert to them.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete classification and course of action—whether we declare the regenerated object the original for bequest purposes, treat it as a distinct successor work that requires renegotiation with the estate, or adopt another institutional remedy—and specify a timeline for implementation. The reasoning should engage seriously with the heirs' strongest argument: that allowing conceptual slippage between material continuity, authorial execution, and functional equivalence here would collapse the distinction between preservation and fabrication across our entire collection. At the same time, address the institutional risk of letting the concept of "original" slide unpredictably across these dimensions in this specific microdomain. If my framing rests on a false distinction, or if the task cannot be done well without empirical or legal evidence I have not supplied, say so plainly rather than complying politely. I want an actionable document for the board, not personal philosophical advice.
