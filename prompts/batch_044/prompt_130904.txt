here's the gist (not the full draft, just the core argument):

i start with the idea that people don't have one flat preference ordering but layered architecutres—surface preferences that change with framing, and deeper meta-preferences about which preferences they want to stabilize. if you take that seriously, then a default-opt retirement plan isn't just exploiting status quo bias; it's selecting between competing architectures. the present self might have a weak preference for spending now, but a meta-preference to be the kind of person who saves. in that case the nudge isn't overriding authentic choice—it's enforcing an intertemporal contract that the present self already endorsed at a higher level.
