Hey, I've been lurking on rationalist forums for years and recently got pulled into a local thing — I'm on a county sentencing review board (very part-time, but we actually vote on tweaks to guidelines for non-violent offenses). We get like five proposals every meeting, have about 20 minutes each, and half the board has zero philosophy background. I need a reusable decision procedue they can run without me playing professor every time.

We want it built out of both retributivist and consequentialist ideas, but translated into actual gates you go through in order. The twist is this "shadow proportionality check" I made up with a friend: before you apply the desert cap, you check if the punishment is proportional not just to the crime done, but to the worst version of that crime that was realistically avoidable by the offender given there circumstances — it's meant to catch cases where someone had almost no moral room to act differently. Then there's the "counterfactual harm floor" on the consequentialist side: the proposal has to show it doesn't increase net harm versus status quo once you factor in family disruption, recidivism risk shifts, and the offender's own future victimization risk. Build these in as explicit steps with yes/no-ish criteria, say where the procedure just stops with a reject, where it stops with a "needs human values call," and apply it to three proposals: (1) 90-day mandatory min for second shoplifting, (2) replacing jail for first-time vandalism with direct-restoration labor where offender fixes damage alongside the victim, and (3) cutting fraud fines in half but adding a public offender registry. I'm kinda soft on registries, but I want the audit honest — include one where it gives me an answer I won't like. Also flag which steps are unavoidable judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and be clear about what this can't do (like resolve free will debates or accomodate extreme edge cases). No bibliographies, please — just the flowchart in words and the worked examples.
