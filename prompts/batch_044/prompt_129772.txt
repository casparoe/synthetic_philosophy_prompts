Having spent thirty years in condensed-matter physics and only recently turned to philosophy, I find the literature on transformative experience—parenthood, religious conversion, permanent emigration—frustratingly coarse. The standard decision-theoretic framing treats the problem as mere ignorance of future preferences, but I suspect the deeper difficulty concerns whether pre- and post-transformation values are genuinely incommensurable rather than merely unknown. For a small graduate seminar of scientifically literate students, I want a diagnostic taxonomy—not by philosophical labels, but by concrete answers to branching questions.

Please construct a decision tree with four binary nodes, each phrased as a claim or case an answerer could assess without knowing the terminology in advance:

1. Are the values I will hold after transformation comparable in principle with my present ones, or does the change produce genuine incommensurability?
2. Does first-person testimony from those who have undergone the transformation provide authoritative epistemic access to my future welfare, or is it systematically distorted by adaptation and selection?
3. Can rational choice here be guided by a framework-neutral meta-criterion—such as narrative coherence, openness to value change, or second-order preference satisfaction—or does it collapse into an arational commitment?
4. Is the metaphysical continuity of my self across the transformation necessary for rational deliberation, or can I rationally choose to become a different valuing agent?

For each leaf, name the position (or label it "unnamed") and state its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Then display the full logical space of answer combinations: mark which are occupied, and for each empty combination briefly diagnose whether the vacancy stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Include one branch addressing whether rational choice might instead be guided by a meta-preference for the structural stability of one's evaluative framework rather than by anticipated value content—an angle I have not seen properly mapped. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; describe all positions in your own terms. Limit the tree to eight to twelve leaves.
