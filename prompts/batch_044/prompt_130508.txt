okay so i've been in this writing group for like two years and there's this one guy—David—who's kind of a tyrant about what he calls the verisimilitude problem, except he doesn't mean historical research. he means something weirder, almost philosophical. he hated that historical novel where every button on an 1890s coat was correct but the widow never forgot her dead husband's name, not even in a dream—he said it was dead on arrival. he loved the sci-fi story where faster-than-light travel is never explained, but there's this long digression about a supply clerk filling out form 27-b for a broken coffee maker that has zero to do with the plot. he said that memoir-turned-fantasy about the author's divorce, where every tree literally spoke the ex-husband's lines, was hollow—the trees were metaphors wearing bark, not real excess. but he praised the magical realist piece where dead people come back only to do laundry, even though the soap-brand details were wrong for 1952, because the wrongness felt like real memory, not a mistake made for effect.

i think he's running one rule about narrative noise—details that don't serve the emotional machinery—but my friend thinks it's actually about affective anchoring, whether the emotional core is tied to documented human behavior regardless of the physical rules. so here's the case i'm stuck on: in my new novel gravity pulls sideways, colors have emotional temperatures that don't match our spectrum, and every symbol is designed to mirror the protagonist's grief. there's no noise in the world-building; every weird law serves the theme. but the conversations between her and her sister aren't copied from real life—they're polished to capture the exact emotional dynamics of my own familly, but every awkward pause is gone, every mispronunciation fixed, every bit about the weather removed. it's pure distilled truth, no excess. would David say this achieves verisimilitude? what's the closer analog from his past rulings—the supply clerk digression or the tree-metaphor failure? and if our group has to be consistent with his track record, should we approve it for our realism prize or reject it, and what principle are we actually adopting either way? also, if there are two rules that fit all his earlier verdicts just as well, what's the one further case that'd actually split them—something with real excess but no emotional anchoring, or the reverse?
