Write an essay on whether Gödel's first incompleteness theorem genuinely applies outside mathematics—specifically, to formal systems of legal or ethical reasoning. Some philosophers have argued that any sufficiently complex normative system capable of expressing self-reference must contain undecidable propositions, implying that no complete codification of law or morality is possible; critics reply that normative discourse lacks the precise recursive encoding of arithmetic that Gödel's proof requires, making the analogy a category mistake rather than a rigorous extension. Weigh both sides, but do not remain neutral: defend a definite verdict on whether the analogy holds and, if it does, whether it actually undermines the practical aspiration to a complete moral or legal code. Be explicit about where your argument relies on formal features—such as the representability of syntactic properties within the system—rather than on loose metaphor. Take a committed position with clear reasons, not a survey of options.
