ive been thinking about virtue ethics vs the situationist thing for a while and it feels stuck in mud. the virtue side says moral character is real and stable, like being a genuinely kind person. the situationists point to those old experiments where people flip their behavior just because of tiny changes in the room or the crowd, and then say character is mostly an illusion. then the virtue people reply that you just didnt have the real virtue yet, and round we go. its been decades and nobody moves an inch.

my own hunch is theyre not fighting over the same question. maybe one group is asking whether we can predict what someone will do from their personality, and the other is asking what makes us blame or praise them, but both call it "character". or maybe theres a hidden argument about moral education - should we train peoples insides or just redesign the situations around them?

is this actually a verbal mixup, or two different questions hiding under one word? and is there some distinction or study that could actually break the deadlock, or is it just bedrock gut feelings about whether people are "really" good inside?

also i keep wondering if both sides assume character is either a battery inside you or nothing at all. but what if its more like playing music - you need the right space and audience for it to come out? does that change anything, or am i just adding noise to a debate thats already hopeless?
