Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two interpretive camps on Blaise Pascal’s treatment of justice and force ought to produce. Name the first camp the Pragmatic Institutionalists: they read Pascal as holding that, in the present fallen order, justice is genuinely powerless without the support of coercive force, and that this is not merely descriptive lament but a normative recognition that institutions of law and punishment are tragically necessary to restrain concupiscence and preserve minimal peace. Name the second camp the Eschatological Separatists: they read Pascal as holding that any alliance between justice and coercive power is itself a corruption of justice by the order of concupiscence, that his remarks on the impotence of unaided justice are purely diagnostic of human fallenness, and that authentic Christian justice must therefore renounce all coercive authority in anticipation of an order governed by charity rather than force.

The document must be one that both camps could sign, with residual disagreements rendered exact. Structure it as follows.

First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: Pascal’s Jansenist anthropology and his distinction between the order of charity and the orders of mind and body; the general thrust of the fragments in the Pensées that treat justice, custom, and force; the historical context of these writings (their unfinished, aphoristic character and their intended apologetic purpose); and the shared recognition that Pascal regards human beings as simultaneously capable of reasoning about justice and habitually inclined to confuse customary power with genuine right. Phrase every claim so that neither camp must disavow it.

Second, present a disagreements table. Each row should state a precise point of divergence, with the Pragmatic Institutionalist formulation written in wording its own side accepts, and the Eschatological Separatist formulation written in wording its own side accepts. Identify at least two cruxes. Crux one: whether Pascal’s observation that justice without force is powerless amounts to a prescriptive endorsement of state coercion as a provisional good, or to a purely descriptive indictment of fallen societies that offers no normative sanction for institutional violence. Crux two: whether Pascal’s anthropology permits a tertium quid—a mode of social coordination that is neither coercive force nor passive injustice, perhaps sustained by shared epistemic humility, voluntary ascetic discipline, or mutual recognition of human fragility—or whether his doctrine of concupiscence rules out any stable non-coercive order prior to eschatological transformation.

Third, for each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or thought experiment that both camps agree bears upon it, and record each side’s explicit prediction about what that case shows. One case should examine a hypothetical community that maintains widespread internal order without any monopoly on violence, relying solely on practices of mutual vulnerability and non-coercive persuasion; both camps should agree whether this example counts as a counter-example to Pascal’s claim or as a hidden form of psychological force. The other case should concern the interpretation of Pascal’s discussion of custom and imagination in the fragments treating justice: both camps should agree on the textual evidence available, then state in advance whether that evidence supports a normative or merely diagnostic reading.

Fourth, include a concluding section that separates verbal from substantive disagreements. State clearly which parts of the dispute concern the meaning of terms—such as whether “force” denotes physical coercion, institutional habit, or psychological manipulation mediated by imagination—and which parts concern the normative status of coercion in a fallen world. Indicate whether resolving the verbal disagreements would dissolve any portion of the substantive dispute, or whether the camps would still divide on the cruxes even with shared definitions.

Write in formal British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to secondary scholarship. Do not ask the model to reproduce specific fragment numbers unless they are verified from source; instead, refer generally to “the fragments on justice and force” and “the discussion of custom and imagination in the Pensées.” Ensure the prompt requests a constructive document that both parties could endorse, with the exact residual disagreement made precise.
