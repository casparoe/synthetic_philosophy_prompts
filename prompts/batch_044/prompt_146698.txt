then provide a model answer and a marking scheme. the scheme should break marks down explicitly: maybe 8 for correct identification and conditioning logic, 8 for the objection explained right, 8 for the evaluation of asymmetry, 6 for clarity and precision. also list the specific mistakes that should cost marks — e.g. treating SSA and SIA as just different priors, confusing observer-relative conditioning with ordinary statistical sampling, or claiming SSA ignores her existence.

make it hard enough that memorizing the labels SSA and SIA isnt enough; they have to actually apply the conditioning logic to this multiverse-urn case.
