I am working through the metaphysical tension between Lewis-style counterpart theory and the thesis of transworld identity, and I want to move past the well-worn physical-duplicate thought experiments. Specifically, I am interested in conceptual issues where the ambiguity between being a counterpart and being the same individual across worlds arises not from divergence in first-order psychological or bodily properties, but from competing second-order normative commitments—conflicts within an agent's own hierarchy of values about which traits should count as essential to their practical identity. Could you provide a concrete, philosophically substantive example of such a conceptual issue? The case should make it genuinely unclear whether an entity in another possible world is the original agent or merely a counterpart, precisely because the agent's internal norms for self-continuation pull in opposing directions, and it should do so without invoking obscure technical machinery or requiring reference to specific literature.
