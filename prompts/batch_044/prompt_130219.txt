Been thinking about Maimonides' take on korbanot — he basically says in the Guide that God kept the sacrificial system around as a concession, redirecting an existing idolatrous habit toward the true God, with prayer as the real endgame. I tried formalizing his reasoning and now it feels like it proves way too much. Heres the chain:

1. If a practice is deeply embedded in a culture and serves a psychological need (like ritual slaughter serving the need to approach the divine), then an immediate divine prohibition would be psychologically catastrophic and therefore counterproductive.
2. So divine law should accomodate such practices, redirecting them rather than abolishing them outright.
3. Animal sacrifice was one such embedded practice.
4. But political authoritarianism and tribal loyalty are also deeply embedded psychological needs — people crave strong leaders and in-group solidarity just as strongly as they crave ritual.
5. So by (1)-(2), divine law should accomodate authoritarianism and tribalism too, redirecting them rather than banning them.
6. In fact, any harmful but deeply rooted social structure — slavery, patriarchy, caste — should be accomodated, not abolished.
7. That means revelation can never directly command moral progress that conflicts with widespread social psychology; it can only nudge.
8. But thats implausible — plenty of traditions claim direct prohibitions of practices that were widespread.
9. So either (1) or (2) must be wrong.

But I want to keep the core intuition about sacrifices being special. Maybe the mistake is that I smuggled in a false equivalence between ritual needs and social-structure needs in step (4)? Or maybe theres a hidden distinction in Maimonides between ritual commandments and moral ones that I missed?

Tell me which link breaks and what it costs to break it. Also please say what would change your mind — like if theres textual evidence Maimonides applies accommodation to moral law too, or if a good argument shows all moral progress really is just gradual nudging. And flag where your reasoning is shakiest; I think its my slide from ritual practices to political structures.
