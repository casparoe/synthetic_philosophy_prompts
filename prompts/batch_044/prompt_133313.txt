Please explain the conceptual tension that pain asymbolia introduces for theories holding that pain is bad essentially because of how it feels. Patients with this condition typically retain accurate sensory discrimination and can report pain location and intensity, yet they lack withdrawal behavior, emotional distress, and any motivation to stop the stimulus—often insisting the sensation is not unpleasant despite using pain-language. This appears to dissociate nociceptive representation from affective suffering. Your task is to show why this dissociation undermines both the phenomenal-intrinsic view, which identifies badness with the felt quality itself, and the purely motivational view, which treats badness as consisting solely in the drive to avoid. Then propose and briefly defend a three-layer framework—distinguishing sensory encoding, affective valence, and an independent evaluative appraisal—to determine whether asymbolic pain is genuinely harmful, only instrumentally problematic, or not bad at all, and state what this implies for a hedonist theory of well-being that counts unpleasant affect as intrinsically damaging.
