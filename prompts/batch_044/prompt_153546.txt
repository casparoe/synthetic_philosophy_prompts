You sit on the institutional review board for epistemic technologies at a university. A research group proposes certifying an AI system as a valid pramāṇa-source within classical Nyāya epistemology. The system implements perception, inference, comparison, and testimony modules, but also claims an autonomous fifth capacity—anupalabdhi, or non-apprehension of absence—arguing that standard Nyāya undercounts the sources needed for machine cognition. One faction on the board says anupalabdhi is already subsumed under inference and testimony, making the claim redundant; the other says granting it independent status invents an unauthorized epistemic category for artificial agents. Draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—addressing whether the system may be certified. Present your response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. State the bottom-line recommendation in item one, the core reasoning in items two and three, the strongest argument for the opposing view in item four, and any conditions or implementation notes in item five.
