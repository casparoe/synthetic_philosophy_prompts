ok so i was reading about this philosopher guy scheffler and his idea that human extinction is bad not just because people die but because future generations act like an afterlife that makes our current projects worth doing. i'm trying to map my gut reactions to different extinction scenarios to see if there's one principle or if i'm just all over the place.

case 1: asteroid wipes us out tomorrow, instant death, zero descendents. feels horrible, mostly because all those future lives never happen.

case 2: we live 200 more years, cure aging, build amazing art and science, then some cosmic event kills us with no descendants. still feels bad, but different - like writing a book and then burning every copy so no one can read it.

case 3: we go extinct biologically but leave behind self-replicating AI that genuinely understands and continues our culture. my gut says the badness mostly dissapears here, even though no humans remain.

case 4: we learn for sure we'll be extinct in a million years, and that we're the only intelligent life in the universe. nothing changes about the actual future, but suddenly my own coding projects feel kind of pointless. so the knowledge itself damages present meaning?

case 5: we engineer our future descendents to be super productive but emotionally flat - they keep working but never feel joy or wonder. does that count as a valid continuation, or does the badness come back?

i keep bouncing between case 3 and case 5. if continuity is all that matters, then flat descendants should be fine, but case 4 makes me think the harm is about our present belief in an audience, not the audience itself. and case 1 vs case 2 makes me wonder if missing future lives is a separate harm from lost project-meaning. is there a coherent principle that saves all five verdicts, or which one do i have to drop? also, what's it predict for a case i haven't listed - like we upload all our minds into a simulation that runs forever but no one outside knows about it?
