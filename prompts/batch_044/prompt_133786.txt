I'm convening a reading group for postgraduate students on Platonic practical ethics, and we have been arguing—rather inconclusively—about whether Plato genuinely condemns drunkenness or instead assigns it a hidden pedagogical role. To structure our next session, I would like a sorting taxonomy presented as a branching decision tree. The purpose is not to label scholars but to sort the members of the group by their substantive commitments about the texts themselves.

Please construct a tree with four branching nodes, each framed as a concrete interpretive claim or case that a student can answer without knowing the name of any secondary position. The first node should ask whether the Symposium's portrayal of Alcibiades' intoxicated intrusion reveals that wine exposes a stable underlying character, or rather dissolves the rational self that Socrates otherwise cultivates. The second should ask whether the drinking regulations in the Laws apply as universal civic law or are specifically calibrated for the education of future rulers, with ordinary citizens falling under different norms. The third should ask whether alcohol functions in Plato's political psychology solely as a danger requiring suppression, or also as an instrumental pharmakon useful for testing or training the soul. The fourth should ask whether the tension between the dialogues is best resolved by a developmental story (Plato revised his view), a contextualist story (sympotic and legislative settings demand different norms), or an ironic-distancing story (one dialogue subtly undermines the surface teaching of the other).

The leaves should include named positions only where they are genuinely occupied in interpretation—such as the pedagogical instrumentalist, the civic prohibitionist, the contextualist harmoniser, and the ironic sceptic—while any unoccupied combinations should be noted and briefly diagnosed (for instance, whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). The tree should accommodate at least eight leaves, flag any pair that it cannot genuinely separate, and be sized for about fifteen participants who have read both dialogues in translation but are not expected to have studied the specialised secondary literature. Please do not include a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations; the instrument should work solely from what the participants believe about the cases and claims themselves.
