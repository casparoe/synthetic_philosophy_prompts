I sit on a small city ethics review board and every month we get proposals asking citizens to take a real hit for collective goods -- mandatory vax for healthcare workers, carbon fees, zoning rules that wipe out a landlord's income. I need a reusable decision procedure my board can run in about 20 minutes, no philosophy phd required, grounded in consequentialist reasoning but with demandingness objections built in. Don't just measure cost size; check whether the demand is sacrificial -- it forces someone to abandon or radically shrink a core life project -- versus friction, where they just adjust inside the project. Also run a residue check: even if they comply, is there a grievance that doesn't vanish?

Write it as ordered questions with clear stop/no points and explicit flag points where it's a values call not a fact call, so the board chair has to sign off. Include test conditions a non-expert can apply, and say exactly where the procedure breaks down -- probably when goods are incommensurable, and when we ask if collective benefits ever justify sacrificial demands.

Then apply it to three proposals:
- mandatory flu vaccination for nursing-home staff
- an 8% post-tax income donation mandate for local food security (this is one I actually support, so I want the procedure to wrestle with it and maybe tell me I'm wrong)
- a retrofit zoning rule that wipes out a small landlord's rental income

Keep it checklist form, no bibliographies, just steps and worked examples.
