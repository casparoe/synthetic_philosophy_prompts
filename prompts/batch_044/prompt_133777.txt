Read Groundhog Day through Zhuangzi's take on death, but skip the butterfly dream everyone knows. I want the weirder stuff: the fasting of the mind and sitting in forgetfullness where you drop fixed roles and stop tracking who you are. Phil Connors loops through every version of himself -- cynic, hedonist, suicide, savior -- and only breaks free when he stops performing Phil and just acts without calculating gain. Is that Zhuangzi's claim that fear comes from clinging to one shape? Or is the film conservative, since Phil ends up with a stable good-guy identity instead of dissolving?

Also check the suicide loops: he dies over and over and wakes up exactly the same. Zhuangzi treats death as a transformation, but here death is a reset button that preserves the self intact. Does that contradict him, or show transformation blocked? I want a reading that takes both seriously -- don't reduce the movie to a sermon, and don't twist Zhuangzi to fit. Point to specific scenes where they clash or illuminate each other, and say what the film adds that philosophical argument alone misses.
