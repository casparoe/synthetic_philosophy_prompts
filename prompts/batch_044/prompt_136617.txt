I've landed on a kind of reversal of the usual scale argument: with something like 10 quintillion insects alive at once, even a 1% prior on sentience should swamp human welfare in any honest calculus — but since we don't actualy reorder our lives around them, the only consistent move is to drive that prior near zero rather than accept the moral conclusion. So for me the scale argument doesn't motivate insect welfare; it undermines the case for it. What would count against this? I want defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: specific behavoral or neurobiological findings, arguments about how priors update under massive numbers, analogies to other low-probability high-stakes domains where we do take action. Also, what would a defender of this view concede is genuinely damaging — not just critics? And does the position risk being insulated by saying 'morals aren't calculus' or 'scale doesn't apply here,' and if so is that unfalsifiability a problem?
