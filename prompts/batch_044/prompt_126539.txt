I'm a senior resolution auditor for an institutional forecasting platform—essentially a structured prediction-market committee that settles multi-year empirical bets. We receive formal challenges claiming that a background commitment used in our settlement is false, and I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in hinge epistemology as developed in Wittgenstein's On Certainty but translated into audit practice, that my three-person team can run in about ninety minutes per challenge. To keep this from being a standard summary, build the procedure around a specific three-way taxonomy: framework hinges (existential conditions without which the domain of inquiry collapses, e.g., that other minds exist), instrumental hinges (calibration and methodological commitments that make measurement possible), and institutional hinges (trust in data sources or procedural integrity that, if suspended, halts all operation). For each category, specify the diagnostic questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the procedure terminates with 'challenge rejected — hinge stands,' where it terminates with 'challenge accepted — open to revision,' and the exact points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be escalated to the committee chair with documentation. The team has no philosophy training; stakes include public credibility and payout obligations; every judgment call must be flagged.

Apply the procedure to two cases: (a) a challenge to a climate-bet settlement arguing that global temperature instrumentation has become systematically unreliable in a specific region, attacking an instrumental hinge; and (b) a challenge arguing that 'national GDP' has lost conceptual coherence due to structural economic change, attacking a framework hinge. Include at least one outcome where the challenger will strongly disagree with the procedure's result but the committee should still accept it, and explicitly state what the procedure cannot do—for example, whether a hinge that has pragmatically outlived its usefulness should be abandoned despite its logical status, or how to weigh risk of framework collapse against evidentiary cost.

If this task rests on a confused premise—for instance, if hinge epistemology resists any operationalization without losing its core point, if my taxonomy misrepresents the distinction between hinge and empirical commitment, or if the translation into audit rules necessarily destroys the theory—say so plainly rather than constructing a false procedure. Note precisely where the translation from philosophical framework to institutional audit practice misfires, and be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as rules.
