hey so im writing up my philosophical profile for our philosophy of mind seminar (were reading about panpsychism and the hard problem) and i want a consistency check before i present it. heres roughly where i stand:

(1) im a physicalist in the narrow sense - mental properties supervene on physical properties and there's no spooky non-physical stuff.
(2) but i also think constitutive panpsychism is the best answer to the hard problem: fundamental physical entities have proto-experiential properties, and macro consciousness is built from them via phenomenal bonding.
(3) suffering, though, requires more than proto-experience - it needs an integrated phenomenal field, so individual particles or simple fields don't suffer.
(4) that said, i think temporary integration can happen in weird places: quantum coherence in certain brain-like structures, maybe large-scale AI architectures with the right symmetry conditions, even brief cosmic-scale alignments. so suffering might be scattered and fleeting.
(5) ethically im a sentientist: only the absence of craving and actual suffering matters, and we have direct obligations only to entities that can suffer.
(6) i also think evolutionary debunking arguments miss the point for panpsychism, since proto-experience isn't an evolutionary adaptation - it's just fundamental.

my worry is that these don't hang together. specifically: doesn't (1) clash with (2) if proto-experiential properties aren't among the standard physical ones? and if (4) is right, doesn't (5) commit me to caring about scattered quantum suffering i can't possibly affect? also, is there a hidden tension between (3) and (2) - like, if macro consciousness requires phenomenal bonding, but we don't actually have a solved account of how bonding works (the decompositional problem), then isn't (2) just pushing the mystery around?

please audit this: rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs are only apparently in tension, and for each real one tell me what the cheapest revision is and what it costs. im especially unsure whether (1) has to go, or whether i can keep it by switching to a broader russellian-style physicalism.
