A recent popular philosophy podcast offered the following paraphrased summary of Nāgārjuna’s catuskoti and Madhyamaka emptiness. The host described the catuskoti as essentially a four-valued logical grid: any claim about a phenomenon can take the form that it exists, that it does not exist, that it both exists and does not exist, or that it neither exists nor does not exist. They argued that Madhyamaka emptiness systematically occupies the fourth corner—the “neither” position—demonstrating that all phenomena lack svabhāva (inherent existence) without collapsing into nihilism. They further claimed that Nāgārjuna’s method is purely negative: he deploys reductio ad absurdum to demolish the first three positions but never positively characterises emptiness, which is allegedly why later Tibetan commentators had to import Yogācāra ideas to supply a constructive ontology. Finally, they concluded that Madhyamaka is therefore a form of metaphysical quietism: it rejects every philosophical view (dṛṣṭi) but offers no ontology of its own.

I am unsure how much of this is accurate, how much is a common simplification, and whether any part subtly misrepresents Nāgārjuna’s actual stance—particularly the idea that he endorses the fourth corner, that his account is entirely negative, and that Madhyamaka depends on Yogācāra supplementation. Could you audit this summary? Please separate what is broadly correct, what is a standard popularisation, and what Nāgārjuna or the Madhyamaka tradition would explicitly deny. In doing so, clarify whether emptiness is identical to the fourth option of the tetralemma, and whether the charge of “pure negativity” survives correction, without appealing to obscure secondary literature that a non-specialist reader could not reasonably verify.
