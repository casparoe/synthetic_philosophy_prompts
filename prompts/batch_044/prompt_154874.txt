I was talking with some friends after our philosophy of math seminar — specifically Mara and Leo — and they got stuck in this argument about whether anyone should actually care if ZFC turned out inconsistent. (1) Mara said it's mostly a philosopher's fantasy: working mathematicians don't touch the full axiom system directly, they live inside much weaker fragments like PA or basic ZF, and even if there was a contradiction hidden deep in the foundations, applied fields like calculus or linear algebra sit safely inside consistent sub-islands, so nothing practical collapses. (2) Leo pushed back that this ignores dependancy chains — a lot of "elementary" analysis results secretly invoke choice or completeness axioms buried three layers deep, and you can't just declare a field "safe" without tracing every hidden axiom back to its source, which working mathematicians never actually do. (3) Mara then introduced this idea she called "scaffolding independence" — mathematicians treat axioms like replaceable tools rather than fixed truths, so if one beam breaks you swap it out without tearing down the building, and applied math stays standing because it never really rested on the broken part. (4) Leo said that's just relabeling contamination — if your proof of a "safe" theorem secretly used a contradictory axiom somewhere in the chain, then the theorem is unreliable too, and instrumentalism doesn't give you a cleaning method, it just tells you not to care about the dirt.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and briefly justify each rating. Also tell me what both of them are missing — especially about whether you can actually isolate "clean" parts of mathematics from a broken foundation, and whether their disagreement is really about logic or about how mathematicians actually work.
