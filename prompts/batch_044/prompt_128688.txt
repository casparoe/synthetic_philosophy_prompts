Okay so I've been re-reading the old epistemic closure debates — you know, Dretske vs the closure defenders, Nozick's tracking conditions, the whole 'if you know you have hands then you know you're not a brain in a vat' mess. And honestly? It feels like the same moves have been recycled since the 80s. The anti-closure crowd says tracking fails for deductive consequences because your belief-forming method doesn't track the far-away skeptical scenario. The pro-closure crowd says that's just a weird feature of Nozick's specific conditions, not a general failure of closure, and they patch it with safety conditions or contextualist tweaks. But nobody budges.

My working guess is that this isn't really about closure per se. I think one camp is asking 'what should a rational agent believe given her evidence?' (normative), and the other is asking 'which belief-forming mechanisms are sensitive to the truth of the conclusion?' (mechanistic/descriptive). They're using the word 'knowledge' for both, so they talk past eachother. But I'm not sure — maybe there's a hidden empirical disagreement about how human cognition actually tracks, or maybe it's just bedrock intuition about whether skepticism is a live option.

Here's a weirder thought I haven't seen in the literature: what if tracking conditions are granular, not binary? Nozick treats sensitivity as all-or-nothing, but if we model tracking with a similarity-weighted metric over worlds — closer worlds matter more for perceptual beliefs, distant ones for logical ones — then closure might hold with degree 0.9 for deduction but 0.1 for skeptical scenarios. If that's right, both sides are right about different slices of a continuous parameter space, and they're fighting over where to draw a line that shouldn't be binary.

So diagnose it for me. Is the stall verbal? Are they answering different questions under one label? Is there some hidden formal result about nested modal spaces that'd break it? Or is the question ill-posed because closure was never meant to apply to beliefs formed via inferance from knowledge? I want the structure, supported by what each side actually says, plus what'd count as progress — a distinction, a formal model, or splitting the debate in two.
