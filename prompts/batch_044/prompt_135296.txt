Please argue that Rousseau's condemnation of theater in his Letter to d'Alembert is best reconstructed not as a general hostility to representation, but as a claim about affective outsourcing: the theater institutionalizes a division of emotional labor in which citizens pay professionals to perform sentiments—pity, patriotic fervor, moral outrage—that republican virtue requires each citizen to enact collectively and without remuneration in civic festivals. Defend the view that this structural feature, rather than simple imitation of vice, explains Rousseau's sharp distinction between permissible public spectacles and corrupting theatrical performance, and address the objection that Geneva's proposed ban was therefore redundant given his broader endorsement of collective ritual.
