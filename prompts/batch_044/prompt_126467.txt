Here are notes for an essay I am drafting, provisionally titled Quining Qualia Without Quining Experience: A Note on Introspective Indeterminacy. The essay takes up Dennett's Chase and Sanborn intuition pump and asks whether it demonstrates the incoherence of intrinsic qualia or only exposes a bipartite ambiguity in introspective reporting. Chase claims his taste judgments have changed while the qualitative experience remains constant; Sanborn claims the qualitative experience itself has shifted. Dennett treats both reports as equally undermined because he assumes a transparency thesis: if phenomenal character were real, introspection should be able to isolate it from representational and affective change. I propose that the pump actually reveals two distinct failures—one between phenomenal character and judgmental attitude (Chase), another between phenomenal character and representational content (Sanborn)—and that conflating them obscures a possible third case. I construct a hypothetical taster, Voss, who sincerely reports that Maxwell House tastes exactly as before and that she still enjoys it, yet whose discrimination thresholds and affective ratings align with Sanborn's. If coherent, Voss separates phenomenal stability from representational drift masked by judgmental inertia, suggesting that introspection may be systematically opaque to its own causal structure rather than transparent to qualitative content. The essay asks whether this tripartite taxonomy saves a coherent notion of qualia or merely extends Dennett's dissolution. Please comment specifically on: (1) whether this framing misrepresents Dennett's argument in Quining Qualia by attributing to him a transparency thesis he does not hold; (2) whether the Voss case collapses conceptually into Chase or Sanborn; and (3) whether the distinction between representational drift and judgmental inertia is philosophically substantive or merely verbal.
