The blogger then distinguishes “surface analogies,” which match superficial features, from “deep analogies,” which align causal and relational structures, and claims that Hofstadter and Sander hold human creativity to depend exclusively on the latter, with surface analogies being essentially useless for genuine conceptual innovation. Next, the blogger asserts that contemporary large language models operate through pure statistical surface-matching and therefore possess no authentic analogical capacity, which is why they fail at metaphor, conceptual blending, and cross-domain insight. From this, the blogger draws two conclusions: first, that building true artificial intelligence will require explicitly encoding symbolic analogy-rules into architectures, since unsupervised statistical learning can never discover deep structural correspondences on its own; and second, that if a system genuinely passes a creative deep-analogy test, it must necessarily possess phenomenal consciousness, because deep analogizing requires a unified subjective viewpoint that integrates disparate representations into a single experienced field.
