I keep running into a weird loop in moral arguments and I'm not sure if it's just circular reasoning or something more specific. A person says we shouldn't coerce someone because that violates their autonomy. When you ask why autonomy trumps wellbeing here, they say autonomy just is the kind of value that forbids coercion — it's not justified by wellbeing, it defines what's at stake. So autonomy started as the reason but turned into a redescription of the verdict. But then in the next debate they treat autonomy as an independent good that can be weighed against other goods, as if it had its own foundation. The supposed ground and the particular judgment keep trading places, and the person never notices. I know there's that ancient question about whether something is good because God commands it or vice versa, but this feels like a modern secular version where people slidd back and forth without realizing there are two directions. Is there an established name for this specific slidding — the way a moral principle and the cases it's meant to support keep defining each other? How is it different from the classic dilemma and from plain question-begging? If there's no specific term, can you coin one with clear boundry conditions?
