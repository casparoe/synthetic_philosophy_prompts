Nietzsche distinguishes three ways history can serve life—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—and introduces the notion of plastic power to describe a culture's capacity to assimilate the past without being paralyzed by it. I understand plastic power as functioning like a quantitative metabolic coefficient: the higher the value, the larger the historical dosage a society can digest safely. On this reading, critical history is not a genuinely independent mode but rather a pathological symptom of metabolic failure, an attempt to expel what could not be absorbed. Consequently, it seems that a culture with exceptionally high plastic power should maximize monumental and antiquarian engagement with the past while suppressing critical history altogether. Is this the right way to apply Nietzsche's framework? And if plastic power could genuinely be measured and institutionally optimized, what form of social organization would best sustain a community in that high-dosage, pre-critical condition?
