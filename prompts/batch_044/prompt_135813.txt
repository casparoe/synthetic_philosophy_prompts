I am trying to stabilize my position on aggregation in rescue ethics for an upcoming workshop and would like a consistency audit. My current package is:

(1) I endorse the view associated with Taurek that in a direct, simultaneous rescue—one identifiable stranger versus five—there is no aggregate reason to save the greater number; randomization is the appropriate response.

(2) I nevertheless accept aggregation in institutional resource allocation (e.g., public-health triage), on the grounds that such allocation distributes benefits across an anonymous population over time rather than selecting among discrete, present victims.

(3) I take the separateness of persons to block interpersonal harm-aggregation in rescue contexts but not to block benefit-aggregation in institutional design, because institutional roles generate structurally different moral reasons.

(4) I allow that relational obligations—say, to one's child—can justify saving that child over strangers, but I deny that such obligations scale with the number of strangers present; saving one's child plus four others is not five times as justified.

(5) I claim that all of this can be unified in a single framework employing a non-aggregative weighting function for direct rescue obligations and an aggregative social-welfare function for institutional roles, with the switch governed by the structural nature of the decision context rather than by emotional proximity.

Please evaluate whether these commitments hang together. In particular: does (3) mark a principled boundary, or is it an ad hoc compartmentalization that protects (1) and (2) only by renaming them? Does (4) genuinely avoid smuggling numbers back in, given that the presence of additional strangers alters the option set? Is (5) coherent, or does the need for two distinct weighting functions reveal an unprincipled dualism? Rank the tensions by severity, identify any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, and specify the least costly revision that preserves the anti-aggregationist intuition in direct rescue without abandoning institutional aggregation.
