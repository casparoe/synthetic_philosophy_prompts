I've been playing with the idea that Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model — where creativity is sliding continuous dials in a conceptual space — applies to moral disagreement litterally. Not just that we have different priorities, but that all ethical conflict is really people setting the same underlying knobs (care, fairness, authority, loyalty, purity) to different values, and that there are no genuinely incommensurable moral frameworks, only paramater mismatches. What would actually count against this? I want specific arguments, cases, or empirical results that should make me weaken or drop it — not just the standard "worldviews are incommensurable" reply. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed defender would have to concede, and be honest about whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability without that being a problem for it.
