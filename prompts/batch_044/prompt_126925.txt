Please argue that the surprise exam paradox is not best resolved through standard backward-induction or probabilistic discounting strategies, but through recognizing a specific ambiguity in the notion of surprise: that the teacher's announcement creates a recursive meta-belief structure in which students must hold a first-order belief about the exam's occurrence together with a second-order belief about their own inability to predict it.
