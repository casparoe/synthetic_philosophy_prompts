I've been thinking on my own about that visual illusion where you see a red dot and then a green dot in a different spot, yet you report seeing a single dot that moves across the gap and changes color midway, even though no dot ever actually occupied those intermediate positions — the color phi phenomenon, I believe it is called. I came across the idea that philosophers distinguish two ways the mind might be revising what we take ourselves to have experienced: an Orwellian revision, in which the brain actually rewrites the stored record of the experience after it happens, and a Stalinesque revision, in which the underlying perceptual record remains untouched but a false narrative is constructed only at the moment someone asks us to report what we saw. I want to see whether I can push the Stalinesque idea to its limit and check whether it collapses under its own weight.

Here is the chain of reasoning I have been turning over. (1) In the color phi case, observers sincerely report seeing motion and a color shift through positions where no physical stimulus was ever present, so there is a reported conscious experience that does not match the sequence of stimuli. (2) If the Stalinesque account is correct, then that particular experience of motion and change was never genuinely present in consciousness; it was fabricated purely at the point of reporting, while the original perceptual traces remain intact and unrevised. (3) But the act of reporting itself — the felt experience of saying, for instance, that the dot turned green halfway across — is also a conscious state. (4) If the Stalinesque mechanism applies generally to conscious experience, then this meta-experience of reporting is likewise not genuinely present but is itself fabricated at the next level of reflection, say when the observer notices or remembers that they have just spoken. (5) I have started calling this a nested Stalinesque loop: every conscious state is only a retrospective construction produced at the level immediately above it, with no base layer that is ever directly present. (6) If the loop applies without exception, then there is never a genuinely present conscious state at all; there is only an endless chain of fabrications, each one accounting for the one before it, like floors stacked upon floors with nothing beneath them. (7) Yet an infinite explanatory chain with no original fact being explained seems incoherent — like a building that has no ground floor — so the loop must bottom out somewhere. (8) But if it bottoms out in a genuinely present, unrevised experience, then the Stalinesque mechanism does not apply universally, and we have admitted that at least some conscious states are truly present as they occur. (9) Once we allow that some states can be genuinely present, it becomes open that the color phi experience itself might be one of those present states rather than a pure fabrication of the reporting process, which directly undermines the Stalinesque explanation we started with. (10) Therefore, taking the Stalinesque account consistently either eliminates the possibility of any conscious present altogether or defeats its own application to the color phi phenomenon, both of which feel like proving far too much.

I suspect that one of the steps between three and six must be wrong, though I cannot quite name which one, and I would rather not simply abandon step two, since the distinction between rewriting a memory and inventing a report at the time of asking seems genuinely important.
