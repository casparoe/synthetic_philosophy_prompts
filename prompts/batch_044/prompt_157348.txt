You serve on your university's Interdisciplinary Research Ethics Board, which oversees studies involving both biological organisms and adaptive artificial systems. The board is divided over whether to approve a one-year pilot of the Gradient Protocol for Moral Status Assessment (GPMSA), proposed by the Office of Research Integrity. The GPMSA replaces the current binary classification—vertebrate versus non-vertebrate, sentient versus non-sentient—with a six-tier scale running from Tier 0 (non-valenced responsive systems, including thermostats and simple feedback algorithms) through Tier 2 (affective states lacking temporal integration, assigned to most insects and some mollusks) up to Tier 5 (phenomenal self-modeling with sustained valence, assigned to humans, great apes, and possibly cetaceans). Under the pilot, a neuroscience group wants to apply the GPMSA to justify reducing welfare oversight for a large-scale bee-colony cognition study (arguing that bees fall at Tier 2, below the current invertebrate threshold requiring full review) and, conversely, to subject an adaptive climate-control AI—capable of learning temperature preferences through integrated feedback loops—to minimal ethical screening as a Tier 1 system.

Your board chair asks you to draft the board's decision memo—approve the pilot, reject it, or approve it with binding conditions—addressed to the Provost and the research deans. The memo should include: (1) a clear bottom-line recommendation with explicit conditions if applicable; (2) reasoning that treats moral status as genuinely gradated rather than binary, engaging with the strongest argument that any gradient framework collapses into arbitrary line-drawing or illicit anthropomorphism; (3) an honest assessment of the losing side—whether that is the concern that tiering insects downward erodes hard-won protections or the concern that extending review to adaptive algorithms dilutes the concept of moral patienthood; and (4) practical criteria reviewers can apply if the pilot moves forward, specifically how to distinguish a Tier 1 responsive system (thermostat-level) from a Tier 2 affective system (insect-level) without relying on unverifiable phenomenological reports. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; the board needs an actionable document, not a literature review.
