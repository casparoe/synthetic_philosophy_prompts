As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to formal epistemology, I am drafting a short essay on the Judy Benjamin problem and its implications for updating on conditionals. The familiar case concerns an agent whose prior probabilities are distributed across being stationed with one of two opposing forces, and—within one of those forces—across being at headquarters or near the front line; she then receives a report framed conditionally, to the effect that if she is not with the first force then she is with the second. Rather than rehearsing the standard debate over whether Jeffrey conditionalization or strict conditionalization yields the correct revision—particularly with respect to her belief that she is at headquarters—I propose a different framing. I treat the categorical divisions between the forces, and between headquarters and front-line deployment, not as fixed partitions of the state space but as elements of a structural model containing a latent parameter that governs the reliability and exhaustiveness of the intelligence framework itself. On this view, the scout's message updates not only the agent's first-order location-beliefs but also her second-order credence in the integrity of the categorization scheme. Specifically, I model the revision as a partitioned-likelihood update in which the prior couples beliefs about position with beliefs about whether the report's boundaries are correctly applied, so that ambiguity is absorbed into the coupled prior rather than forced into the conditioning event. I argue that this produces a justified symmetry-breaking between location-beliefs and framework-beliefs, dissolving the apparent conflict between competing update rules. I seek disciplined critical comments. First, does this framing genuinely resolve the tension, or does it merely relocate ambiguity into an underspecified prior over the latent parameter? Second, is the analogy to latent-variable statistical models in the natural sciences philosophically apt, or does it conflate normative epistemic probabilities with descriptive data-generation mechanisms? Third, have I overlooked standard objections—particularly those involving higher-order uncertainty about the applicability or truth of the conditional report—that would show the proposed division between first-order and second-order beliefs is ad hoc rather than principled? I welcome precise objections, especially regarding whether the symmetry-breaking is stable or collapses under pressure from the very case it is meant to illuminate.
