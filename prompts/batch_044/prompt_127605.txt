Draft the joint statement that a Lexical Liberal and a Preference-Reconstructionist could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on Sen’s liberal paradox. The Lexical Liberal holds that the contradiction between minimal liberalism (L) and weak Pareto (P) proves rights must be lexically ordered but confined to contextually bounded spheres—what they call local liberalism—whereas the Preference-Reconstructionist holds that the contradiction dissolves once preferences are reconstructed to include second-order procedural commitments, without restricting rights domains.

Produce:
1. A numbered section of agreed claims about the formal structure (the two-person, two-option proof; that L and P jointly entail an impossibility; that Sen’s “prude and lewd” case illustrates the tension), plus agreement that neither camp denies individuals’ welfare matters.
2. The precise disagreement, with each camp’s own formulation in wording it accepts: whether local liberalism saves rights by sphere-restriction, or whether reconstructed preferences save Pareto consistency by enriching the profile.
3. Two cruxes: (a) whether a preference that another person lack a liberal right over choice X should enter the social profile; (b) whether nested decision spheres (e.g., personal, communal, institutional) can be formally defined so that local liberalism avoids collapsing into majoritarianism.
4. For each crux, one concrete case both sides accept as bearing on it, plus each side’s explicit prediction. For (a), use a case in which A prefers B not have the right to choose B’s diet, and B insists on that right; for (b), use a municipal zoning decision with nested jurisdictional spheres.
5. A disagreements table listing positions, cruxes, agreed tests, and predictions.
6. A brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal—specifically, whether “liberalism” denotes the same concept in both formulations.

Keep the document formal and self-contained. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to obscure secondary literature; work directly from the formal conditions L and P, the injected notion of local liberalism, and the reconstructed-preference framework defined above.
