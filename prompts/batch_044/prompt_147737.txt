I have been thinking about Pascal's remarks on justice and force — particularly the idea that justice without power is helpless, power without justice is tyrannical, and that in practice people obey whatever is strongest. I cannot tell whether he offers this as a cynical description of politics or as a theological reason to submit to authority. Please write a short dialogue between two people who both understand these fragments well. One should argue that Pascal is describing an unavoidable sociological fact: every legal order rests on coercion, and the wise person accepts this without illusion. The other should argue that Pascal is prescribing Christian obedience: true justice belongs to God alone, so human power, however imperfect, is ordained and should be accepted rather than resisted. They must respond to each other's actual points. To push this into specifics, have them discuss whether Pascal — had he survived to face the 1664 Formulary requiring clergy to reject Jansenist propositions — would have signed it as submission to legitimate force or refused it out of loyalty to a hidden justice that transcends worldly authority. Make both voices equally sharp, avoid strawmen, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to particular editions.
