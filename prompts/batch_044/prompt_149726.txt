You are a philosopher of physics with expertise in Gödel's cosmological arguments and the structure of rotating space-times. Before answering, take a moment to list the structural features of Gödel's metric that differ from everyday rotating bodies — especially the absence of a global axis of rotation, the rigid rotation of matter with constant angular velocity everywhere, and the progressive tilting of light cones away from any local origin.

In popular explanations of Gödel's rotating universe, people often invoke the analogy of a cosmic carousel or merry-go-round: an observer sits on a rotating platform, completes a full circuit, and returns to her departure point in space-time, so that closed timelike curves are like taking a round-trip ride that lands you back where you started. This analogy is used to license the conclusion that time travel in such a universe is physically benign — merely circular motion, not paradoxical.

Audit this analogy carefully. First, give a correspondence table: what plays the role of the platform, the rotation axis, the passenger, the ride's circular path, and the return to the start? Then identify where the mapping breaks: consider that in Gödel's solution every world-line of the rotating dust is a geodesic, there is no privileged global axis, local light cones tilt continuously, and the 'return' involves a shift in local temporal orientation rather than spatial position alone. Separate the disanalogies that undermine the 'benign ride' conclusion from those that are merely decorative. Does the carousel quietly import assumptions — a fixed center, an external engineer, a globally shared clock — that Gödel's universe lacks? If the analogy collapses, suggest a more honest comparison for describing closed timelike curves in a homogeneous rotating cosmos, or argue that none can do the work the carousel was meant to do. Reach a clear verdict on whether the analogy genuinely supports the conclusion that Gödel-style time travel is harmless, or whether it was carrying the conclusion all along.
