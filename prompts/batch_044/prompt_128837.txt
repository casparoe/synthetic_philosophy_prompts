I've come around to thinking the fink vs mask distinction doesn't cut deep metaphysically — both are just different flavors of intereference with a disposition's manifestation, and if we index our dispositional ascriptions to a properly specified "clean" environment (no finks present, no masks active) then the old simple conditional analysis can be saved without inventing new ontological machinery. A fink removes the catagorical base; a mask blocks it; but either way the object still has the disposition in the clean case, so why treat them differently? I want to defend that there's no need for separate ontological categories — interference conditions belong in the antecedent, full stop.

What would count against this? I'm not asking for a bibliography — I want the actual defeaters, sorted by how fatal they'd be. For instance, do multi-track dispositions or nested finks (a mask that only activates if a fink is present) break the idea that "clean environment" can be specified non-circularly? Are there empirical cases from chemistry or biology where the fink/mask distinction does real explanitory work that my collapsed view can't capture? And has my view been qualified into unfalsifiability by letting "clean environment" do all the heavy lifting — and if so, is that actually a problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind? I want an honest map of where this position is exposed, including what I'd have to concede if the distinction turned out to be load-bearing after all.
