i've been trying to formalise a bootstrapping worry and now i think it proves way too much. (1) If you can know P without first justifying the method M that delivers P, then using P to justify M isnt viciously circular — it's legitimate bootstrapping. (2) I can know, without prior justification, that i'm not currently in a targeted perceptual illusion (say, i dont have the specific feeling of being in a simulated environment right now). (3) That i'm not in such an illusion is best explained by my perceptual facultys being generally reliable in environments like this one. (4) So i can bootstrap the general reliability of perception from this single bit of easy knowledge. (5) The same structure applies to any facultys F: if i can easily know i'm not misusing F right now, then F is generally reliable. (6) Now consider structural-resonance judgement — a faculty where you accept propositions that feel harmoniously integrated with your existing web of beleif. I can easily know i'm not misapplying it on this particular occasion. (7) By (5), that bootstraps structural-resonance judgement as a generally reliable method. (8) But that faculty can justify conspiracy theories, circular worldviews, and self-sealing belief systems just as smoothly as science, provided the user feels clear-headed. (9) So if bootstrapping is legitimate, then conspiracy theorists and scientists are on equal epistemic footing whenever they both feel cohearent. (10) That's absurd. So (1) must be false: there is no legitimate bootstrapping, and all knowledge requires prior independent justification. (11) But (10) collapses into global scepticism, since we never have prior independent justification for our basic faculties. (12) Therefore bootstrapping either licenses everything or licenses nothing, and both outcomes are unacceptable.
