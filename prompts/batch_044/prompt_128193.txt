As a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, I am designing a conceptual handout for a peer discussion group on moral responsibility. The handout should take the form of a branching taxonomy — a decision tree — that sorts people by their substantive commitments rather than by the labels they adopt. The subject is Strawson's framework linking moral responsibility to reactive attitudes such as resentment, gratitude, and indignation, and to the conditions under which we shift to an objective attitude.

Please construct the tree with the following specifications.

Root node: State the root commitment as a concrete judgment — for instance, whether it is ever appropriate to feel resentment toward someone whose harmful act was fully determined by prior causes, and whether that appropriateness depends on anything beyond the social function of expressing it.

Branch structure: Each internal node must present a specific claim or case that genuinely divides the positions, phrased so that a respondent can answer without knowing which philosophical label the answer produces. You must include at least one node involving a non-obvious scenario, such as assessing blame toward an individual engineer for harm caused by a standard, widely deployed algorithm, or assessing resentment directed at a collective entity like a corporation rather than a single person. Other nodes should cover distinctions such as: whether reactive attitudes are rational responses that can be globally undermined by determinism; whether exemption conditions like severe mental illness are objective capacity failures or socially negotiated boundaries; and whether reactive attitudes can be appropriately directed at sub-personal mechanisms or roles rather than at whole agents.

Required leaves: The tree must terminate in named positions that include at least: (1) a pure Strawsonian view in which reactive attitudes are non-rational social practices and determinism is irrelevant; (2) a rationalist compatibilist view in which reactive attitudes depend on reasons-responsive capacities and determinism is compatible only under specific conditions; (3) a hard determinist or global skeptical view in which determinism undermines all reactive attitudes and the objective attitude should prevail; and (4) a libertarian incompatibilist view in which reactive attitudes require indeterminism but should be preserved. In addition, include at least two unnamed or hybrid leaves representing coherent but rarely defended combinations — for example, the view that determinism undermines reactive attitudes only when directed at institutions but not individuals, or the view that reactive attitudes are appropriate only when targeted at sub-personal cognitive modules rather than at whole persons.

Unoccupied space: For any logically possible combination of answers that your branches allow but that no named or unnamed leaf occupies, note the combination briefly and diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or mere lack of exploration.

Separation and scoring: Provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto the leaves. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate given the branches included, and explain why the ambiguity remains. Do not ask any question that requires citing specific texts, dates, or authors; avoid nodes that ask whether the respondent agrees with Strawson by name. The deliverable should be formal: each branch labeled by its separating claim, answer options listed, and the tree arranged so that the logical space is mapped before the named positions are placed within it.
