I am preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for third-year philosophy undergraduates who have encountered basic philosophy of mind but have not read anything on pain phenomenology or clinical neurology. I want to teach them why pain asymbolia poses a specific problem for the view that pain is bad in virtue of how it feels — in particular, the idea that there is a separable affective-valence component that can be absent even when precise nociceptive localisation and protective withdrawal remain fully intact. Do not explain the concept to me; instead, plan how I should structure the explanation. I need the dependency order: what preliminary distinction — perhaps between nociceptive registration, affective colouring, and motivational urgency — must be in place before asymbolia sounds like a coherent counter-example rather than a contradiction in terms? I want one constructed clinical vignette to carry the entire session: imagine a patient who reports accurate thermal and mechanical localisation, exhibits normal withdrawal reflexes, yet describes the sensation as being like an insistent weather report that demands attention without demanding relief, carrying no urgency to terminate it. Specify exactly what this vignette must include and what it must deliberately lack, so that it does not collapse into ordinary anaesthesia on the one hand or into mere stoic suppression on the other. Then map the misconceptions the students will form in predictable sequence — likely that the patient is confabulating, that all pain-related behaviour has disappeared, that badness is merely high intensity, and that the case proves pain is purely cognitive — and for each, indicate the precise point in the explanatory arc where I should head it off. I also want one diagnostic question I can pose at the end that distinguishes a student who genuinely grasps the conceptual separation of valence from nociception from one who has only memorised the vignette. Finally, tell me which aspects of insular-cortex neuroanatomy or of clinical analgesic ethics I should explicitly refuse to explain at this level, with a brief justification for each omission.
