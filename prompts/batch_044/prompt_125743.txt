I have been returning to the old problem of whether self-deception is even conceptually possible—whether a single rational agent can genuinely deceive herself without collapsing into mere error, compartmentalization, or motivated reasoning disguised as belief. The familiar terrain divides intentionalist accounts, on which one intentionally brings about a false belief in oneself, from deflationary reconstructions that avoid literal contradiction by appealing to indirect causal influence or selective attention.
