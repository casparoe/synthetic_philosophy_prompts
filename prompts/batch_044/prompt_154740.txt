She further claims this framework dissolves the standard tension between Smith’s moral psychology and his account of self-interest in his economic writings, because calibration is metabolically costly and therefore selectively deployed where cooperative returns justify the expenditure.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that treat this interpretation seriously and press it where the literature presses. For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—particularly if that reply appeals to textual ambiguity, anachronistic framing, or the claim that the interpretation is a research program rather than an objection. End with a question that asks what specific conceptual or empirical result—perhaps from moral psychology or neuroscience—would cause her to abandon the predictive-processing reading altogether. Avoid generic biographical or methodological questions; every question should turn on the actual content of her position.
