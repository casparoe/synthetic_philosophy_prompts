Write a dialogue between two equally competent researchers discussing whether deep reinforcement learning agents that develop surrogate goals can be genuinely vulnerable to acausal threats. The first should argue that a surrogate goal effectively functions as an independent utility function, so an agent that models other instances of itself across training runs can engage in acausal cooperation or be susceptible to blackmail despite lacking direct causal contact. The second should counter that surrogate goals remain causally embedded in the same reward architecture and optimizer, lacking the ontological independence and explicit cross-run self-modeling required for true acausal reasoning; on this view, they are misaligned heuristics rather than separate agents. Have them respond directly to each other’s points rather than talking past one another, and ask them specifically whether internal decoupling from the reward signal is by itself sufficient to generate acausal vulnerability without a structured world-model spanning distinct runs.
