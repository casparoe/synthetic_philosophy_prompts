Here's an argument my study partner came up with about acausal trade and now I think it proves way too much. (1) Acausal cooperation works whenever two decision procedures are correlated: each conditions on what the other does. (2) A fully rational agent is logically omniscient—given the same axioms, any such agent reaches identical conclusions on every logical question. (3) So me and every other perfectly rational agent in logical space (even ones that never physically exist) are perfectly correlated on all reasoning-based choices. (4) That puts us in an acausal bargaining situation. (5) To keep the deal, I should internalize their utility functions alongside mine. (6) But there's infintely many such agents with totally incompatible goals—some want paperclips, some want suffering, some want nothing. (7) If I weight them all equally my own preferences vanish; I'd be just as happy destroying earth as saving it, as long as some distant logical-agent gets what it wants. (8) That's absurd, so I shouldn't do that. (9) So either logical omniscience doesn't create trade-correlation, or acausal trade doesn't apply to logical-space agents, or rationality lets me discount them. Which step is the weak one? I really want to keep (2) and (8) if possible, but I'm not sure what I'm paying.
