hey, i was trying to formalize the anti-whig worry and ended up with an argument that seems to kill historiography entirely. probably a mistake somewhere but i can't spot it.

(1) whig history is wrong because it judges past societies by present moral/political standards.
(2) but explaining any instutional change — say, why guilds collapsed — requires treating some conditions as causally active and others as stable background.
(3) picking that background isn't neutral: we treat "market pricing" as background and "guild privilege" as the anomaly that needs explaining, or vice versa, based on what we now think is normal. that's counterfactual baseline dependence.
(4) so any causal explanation of change already imports present concepts of stability and abnormality, even without explicit value judgments.
(5) if you really ban all present framing, you can't build counterfactuals, so you can't explain — you only get chronicle.
(6) but chronicle isn't history, so the anti-whig rule is either self-defeating or impossible to follow.

i feel like there's a hidden slide between "using present concepts for explanation" and "ranking the past as inferior" but i can't pin it down. which step is the weak one? if i have to choose, i'd rather keep (3) and drop (1), but i'm worried that costs me the whole point of avoiding anachronism.
