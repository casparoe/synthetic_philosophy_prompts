Daniel Dennett defends a compatibilist account of moral responsibility that permits limited, non-vindictive retributive sentiment, while Gregg Caruso argues that determinism undermines "just deserts" entirely and that punishment should be justified solely by preventive quarantine. Consider three frameworks for penal practice: (1) Dennett-style limited retributivism; (2) Caruso's pure quarantine model with no desert-based component; and (3) a hybrid—call it "threshold quarantine"—which uses desert-based reasoning only to set an upper ceiling on detention length, but justifies the detention itself entirely by risk reduction. Rank these three, 1 to 3, according to how well each resolves the tension between moral-luck skepticism (that we can never fully determine an agent's true degree of responsibility) and the practical need for proportionate sanctions. Give your ranking with brief reasoning, and address specifically whether framework (3) genuinely avoids Caruso's objection or merely relocates it.
