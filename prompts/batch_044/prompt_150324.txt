Here are my verdicts on lexical thresholds and the commensurability of pleasure and pain, and I want to know whether any single principle captures them without forcing me to abandon too many. First, I judge that a world containing ten billion people in near-maximum flourishing plus one person in unremitting torture is worse overall than the same world with those ten billion at merely good lives and no torture—this suggests a lexical priority for avoiding extreme suffering. Second, however, if that same total suffering is distributed across ten million people as barely perceptible aches, I think it does not outweigh even one person's peak artistic fulfillment—so the boundary seems tied to intensity or qualitative kind rather than to aggregate quantity alone. Third, I also think that two individuals at +5 and −5 are directly comparable and cancel in a sum, which implies some common metric; yet fourth, I feel that the qualitative texture of intellectual delight and bodily nausea are incommensurable dimensions that resist precise exchange rates, even if both contribute to an overall welfare calculus. Fifth, I hold that bringing a being into existence at +9 is permissible, but bringing one into existence at −0.1 is impermissible regardless of how many +9 beings accompany it—suggesting an asymmetrical threshold near zero. Sixth, at the same time, I think that once every being is well above that threshold, incremental gains in pleasure are genuinely comparable and trade off continuously, with no hidden discontinuities. These judgments pull in different directions: some require a sharp lexical boundary, others require continuous commensurability, and others deny direct comparison altogether. Is there a coherent framework—perhaps one that distinguishes quantitative aggregation from qualitative kind, or that introduces a non-linear weighting function with an asymptote near severe suffering—that can accommodate all six? If not, which judgment should I reject, and why? Please also derive a precise prediction for a case I have not listed: a population of exactly two beings, one experiencing constant ecstasy interrupted only by fleeting neutral moments, the other experiencing constant mild satisfaction with no peaks or troughs—does your principle rank one state as better, and if so, on what grounds?
