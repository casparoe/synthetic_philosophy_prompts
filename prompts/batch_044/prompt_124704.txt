I'm preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar on later Wittgenstein and want to stress-test my commitments before defending them. Here is where I've landed: (1) I accept the family-resemblance account for all concepts, including "language" and "game"—there is no single essential feature shared by every language game, only a network of overlapping similarities. (2) I hold that language games are autonomous practices governed by internal norms; understanding a game requires participatory mastery, not translation into an external framework. (3) I take the private-language argument to be sound: meaning requires publicly accessible criteria of correct use, and a language describing purely inner sensations is impossible because it lacks such criteria. (4) I extend family resemblance to "following a rule" itself—there is no unified essence to rule-governance, only family-resemblance patterns across different practices (I call this "grammatical pluralism"). (5) I deny that mathematical and logical language games are exceptions: even proof procedures exhibit family resemblance rather than essential structure, despite their apparent rigidity. (6) I maintain that the boundaries between language games are genuinely vague—there are intermediate utterances for which there is no fact of the matter about which game they belong to.

My worry is that some of these pull in opposite directions, but I'm not sure which. For instance, if rule-following itself lacks an essence (4), can language games still possess genuinely binding internal norms (2)? And if boundaries between games blur (6), does that undermine the public criteria required by (3) to block private language?
