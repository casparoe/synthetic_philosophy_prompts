In my philosophy of science reading group we've been stuck for months on demarcation — specifically pseudohistory like Atlantis claims, ancient alien narratives, and Fomenko's New Chronology with that statistical dynasty-matching method he uses. The debate has stalled in a weird way: everyone acutally agrees on the basic methods (radiocarbon dating, textual criticism, etc.) and everyone agrees the pseudohistories get the chronology wrong, but the defenders keep saying historians are just gatekeeping a consensus story, while historians say it's pseudohistory because it never updates beliefs when data conflicts. So what's the real disagreement here? Is it a hidden empirical question about what counts as a defeater, or is it normative — like whether history owes us revisionism? Or are both sides just using 'historical method' to mean totally different practices, so they're talking past eachother? I don't want a survey of demarcation criteria; I want a diagnosis of why this specific stall happens, and whether anything — a sharper falsifiability condition, a conceptual split, whatever — could actually move it, or if we should just accept the question is ill-posed.
