My colleague has been adjudicating, in our philosophy of mind and epistemology seminar, whether various arguments qualify as genuinely transcendental in the strict Kantian sense — that is, arguments that establish conditions necessary for the possibility of some capacity or form of experience we indisputably possess, without relying on empirical premises about particular psychological mechanisms. Her judgements so far are these. She accepted the argument that distinguishing appearance from reality requires a concept of an objective world irreducible to mere appearances, treating it as a proper transcendental demonstration. She rejected the argument that causal judgement requires a universal principle of causation, judging it to restate Hume's problem in modal dress rather than identify a necessary condition of experience. She accepted the argument from the possibility of self-conscious thought to the necessity of a unified persisting subject, but only on the explicit condition that the subject be understood as a formal condition of thought rather than as a metaphysical substance. She rejected the argument that the practice of giving and asking for reasons entails normative commitments functioning as an unjustified bedrock, on the ground that it confuses the structure of justification with a metaphysical foundation. Finally, she accepted the argument that the corrigibility of perceptual belief requires a direct, non-inferential mode of access to certain objects, though she insisted that this directness must not be interpreted as an epistemically infallible given immune to revision.

Next week she must evaluate a newly proposed argument: that for any representational system to correct its own representational errors, it must employ a presentational mode that is not itself representational but operates in a pre-conceptual register, and that this presentational mode is therefore a necessary condition for the very possibility of error-correction. I should like you to predict whether she will accept or reject this as a genuinely transcendental argument, to identify the implicit rule or rules that best account for her track record, to set out which other rules fit her earlier verdicts equally well, and to name the further case that would best distinguish between those competing interpretations. Please take a definite position with explicit reasoning rather than laying out options neutrally.
