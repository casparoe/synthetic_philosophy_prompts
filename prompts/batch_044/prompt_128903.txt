The exchange must be genuinely dialectical: each speaker should address the other's specific claim, concede where appropriate, and avoid straw-manning. Inject one niche conceptual tension absent from standard popular accounts: have them dispute whether von Neumann's observation that the brain achieves reliability through statistical aggregation of imprecise components means that neural computation is irreducibly non-algorithmic in the Turing-machine sense, or whether such aggregation can itself be algorithmically implemented at a higher architectural level. The dialogue should be conceptually dense, technically informed, and fully comprehensible without reference to page numbers, specific editions, or external citations.
