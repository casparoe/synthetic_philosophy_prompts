I'm taking a course on criminal justice and we keep circling back to retributivism vs consequentialism, but the examples always feel kind of abstract. So I was wondering about an actual cluster of practices that seems to pull in both directions at once. In a lot of US sentencing guidelines, judges reduce sentences if the defendant shows acceptance of responsibility — like confessing or expressing remorse — but they're also told not to penalize someone extra just for pleading not guilty and going to trial. Meanwhile, parole boards in those same jurisdictions often rely on risk-assessment algorithms that score you purely on predicted recidivism, with basically no slot for whether you've apologized or owned what you did. And then there's this other layer: some prosecutors can cite a defendant's lack of remorse as an aggravating factor during death-penalty phases, which sounds like remorse is part of your moral desert, not just a risk indicator.

What philosophical theory of punishment does that whole pattern actually presuppose? Is it retributivist — meaning we care about blameworthyness and remorse shows you've become less blameworthy over time? Or is it consequentialist — remorse just correlates with lower future risk, so we reward it instumentally? And if it's the second one, why do we protect your right to stay silent about guilt at trial, which seems like we think punishing you for not confessing violates fairness? I'm also confused about internal consistency: can you really say punishment addresses past desert when you use statistical risk for release, but then switch back to desert-language when you want harsher sentences for people who seem unrepentant? I want someone to reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether these institutions are genuinely committed to one view or just mixing them up without noticing, and tell me what would have to change if we replaced the remorse factor with something like a negotiated contractual repair agreement — like, if the offender just had to complete a specific restitution plan rather than perform remorse — would that expose that the system was never really about desert at all?
