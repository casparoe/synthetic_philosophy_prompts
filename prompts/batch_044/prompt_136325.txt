My philosophy teacher assigned me a peer-review exercise in which I must grade a classmate's exam answer without giving my own opinion. The exam question was: "In Being and Nothingness, Sartre argues that human beings are radically free. Explain what he means by 'radical freedom' and how the phenomenon of 'bad faith' (mauvaise foi) relates to it. Give one concrete example of bad faith."

The student's answer reads: "Sartre says radical freedom means we can choose anything at any time, with no limits from our past, biology, or society. Bad faith is when we lie to ourselves about this freedom to avoid responsibility. For example, a waiter who acts too much like a waiter is in bad faith because he pretends he has to behave that way, but actually he is free to quit. This shows that bad faith is basically the same as Sartre's idea of 'the look,' where other people objectify us. So Sartre thinks we are only free when nobody is watching."

Please assign a specific grade and justify it with point-by-point comments on what the answer gets right and where it makes subtle errors. Write your assessment in British English, and keep the tone formal and polished.
