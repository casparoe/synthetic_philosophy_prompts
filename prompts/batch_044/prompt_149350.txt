I'd like a consistency audit of my current views on belief aggregation, fallibilism, and doxastic rationality, with particular attention to how they apply to the lottery paradox and the preface paradox. Here is where I stand:

(1) I resolve the lottery paradox by denying that rational belief is closed under conjunction for large sets: I think it can be rational, for each ticket in a fair 1,000-ticket lottery, to believe of that particular ticket that it will not win, without it being rational to believe that no ticket wins.

(2) I resolve the preface paradox in strict parallel: I can rationally believe each specific claim in a long, carefully researched treatise, and also rationally believe—on general inductive grounds about human fallibility—that at least one of those claims is false, without this constituting a doxastic failure.

(3) I hold that rational belief is governed by a single, context-invariant probabilistic threshold (roughly, credence above 0.99), applied uniformly regardless of domain or practical stakes. I reject contextualist or stakes-sensitive adjustments to the bar for belief.

(4) I reject any distinction between belief and acceptance (of the kind sometimes drawn for practical reasoning): there is only one doxastic attitude, and it governs both theoretical assent and deliberative action.

(5) I think rational agents must maintain strong doxastic consistency: one should not maintain a set of beliefs that are jointly impossible, even if one never explicitly forms their conjunction and even if each member is individually well-supported. Compartmentalization is a form of irrationality, not a permissible strategy.

(6) I think belief revision should obey consistency-preserving constraints analogous to the AGM postulates: a rational belief set should always be consistent, and acquiring a new belief should force one to abandon prior beliefs only to the minimal extent required to restore consistency.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, rank them by severity and say exactly what has to give—whether it is (1)-(2), the interaction with (3), the pairing of (5) with (6), or something else. For each, specify the least costly revision and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Identify whether one single change would clean up the most conflicts. Be precise about whether the problem is logical, probabilistic, or practical, and do not treat all tensions as equally serious. Do not give me a bibliography or suggest specific readings; just work with these commitments as stated.
