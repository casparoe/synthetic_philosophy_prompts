I have been thinking about promises on my own, without any formal philosophical training, and I have arrived at a view I call the delegated-authority account. On this view, a promise is not merely a way of giving someone an expectation or a reason; it is a temporary, revocable transfer of a portion of my future decision-making power to the promisee. Fulfilling the promise is therefore the act of returning that authority intact, while breaking it amounts to retaining power that was no longer rightfully mine. To handle hard cases, I draw a distinction within the view itself: hard delegation applies when the promisee can actively release me from the obligation, whereas soft delegation applies when the authority lapses automatically if circumstances shift in an unanticipated way that makes the promised act impossible or seriously harmful, but only if both parties, reasoning reasonably, would have included that contingency in the original grant. I want an honest map of what would actually count against this position. Which specific intuitions about autonomy, consent, or personal sovereignty does it violate? Which concrete cases—promises made to someone who dies before fulfillment, promises that chain across generations, promises made to a party who never knew of them, or promises that conflict with later, stronger obligations—would force the view to collapse back into a simpler expectation-based or harm-based theory? Which results or commitments in neighboring domains, such as contract law, theories of moral responsibility, or accounts of consent, does the delegated-authority account stand or fall with, and where does alignment break? I want the potential defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say whether a committed defender of the view would concede it as genuinely damaging or dismiss it as a misunderstanding of what delegation entails, and be willing to conclude that the view is well insulated if that is the case. In particular, assess whether my hard-delegation versus soft-delegation distinction genuinely saves the account or merely qualifies it into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a problem for a theory of this kind.
