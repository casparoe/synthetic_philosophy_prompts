I'm trying to work out what I actually think about personhood if I take that ubuntu idea seriously -- you know, a person is a person through other people, or whatever. Going by gut on cases.

An orphan raised by wolves with basically no human contact: still a person, just really damaged. An AI therapist that users genuinely love and depend on, that remembers all their stories: not a person, even if the bond feels real. My dead grandmother we still invoke at family gatherings: she's still part of the 'we', so yes in some sense. A hermit who actively rejects every human tie: still a person, just a shitty one. A corporation with internal culture and collective goals: not a person. A fetus in the first trimester with no awareness or interaction: yes, already embedded in the web. Someone in a vegatative state with no consciousness but family still talks to them: yes, the relation sustains it.

But then there's the pair that bugs me. The hermit rejects all relation and I still call them a person, but the AI is drowning in reciprocal care and I say no. And the case I keep flipping on is a newborn with anencephaly who will never think or relate back -- the parents treat her as fully human, but there's zero reciprocity possible. Is she?

So is there one principle behind this mess? Which verdict do I have to drop? And what does it predict for a highly social chimpanzee raised by humans, or a future uploaded mind that only interacts through text?
