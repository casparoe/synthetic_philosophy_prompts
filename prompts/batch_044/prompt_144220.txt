Give me your credence, from 0 to 1, that hedonism can solve the heterogeneity problem — specifically, that all token pleasures (gustatory, aesthetic, malicious, intellectual, etc.) share a single intrinsic feature that is neither (a) merely being desired by the subject nor (b) a second-order judgment that the experience is good, but rather an inherent phenomenal or structural property. Decompose your answer into: (i) the probability that phenomenal qualities are genuinely heterogeneous and share no common 'hedonic tone'; (ii) the probability that a structural or representational account — such as a shared reward-system signature or a common content of 'this is satisfying' — can do the unifying work without collapsing hedonism into desire-satisfaction theory; (iii) the probability that the heterogeneity objection is largely a pseudo-problem arising from an over-literal comparison of qualitative kinds; (iv) the probability that so-called 'problem pleasures' (e.g., malicious or masochistic ones) share the feature without undermining the normative claim. Combine these into an overall number, identify the term in which you are least confident, and specify what empirical or conceptual finding would shift it most. Finally, assess whether framing pleasure as occupying a bounded region in a multi-dimensional 'valence-space' — varying in intensity, embodiment, and cognitive accessibility but unified solely by falling within positive-valence bounds — genuinely resolves the problem or merely relocates the heterogeneity into the dimensions themselves.
