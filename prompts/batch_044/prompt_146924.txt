ok so I'm trying to nail down my care-ethics profile for a seminor and want a consistency check before I present. here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm with Noddings that moral life starts in direct responsive relation — the one-caring actually has to recieve the other, not apply a rule. Care isn't a resource you distribute.

(2) But I also think care ethics has to scale up politically: welfare states and healthcare systems are doing real care, not just justice with a nice label. Institutional care counts.

(3) I'm anti-empathy in this context — emotional identification is unreliable and often extractive. Good care should run on narrative imagination and structural listening, not feeling-with.

(4) There's still a universal floor: nobody gets abandoned in extreme vulnerability, even with no caring relation. It's a backstop, not the heart of morality.

(5) I reject the autonomous liberal self; agency and identity are co-constituted through caring relations. There's no pre-social "me" that decides to care.

(6) I also think we can justify basic care institutions contractually — ask what caring relations people would agree to protect — without smuggling in an individualist ontology.

Which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look bad? Rank the real tensions by severity, say the cheapest fix for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't treat them all as equally bad — some are worse.
