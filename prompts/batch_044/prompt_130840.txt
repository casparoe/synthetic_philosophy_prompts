You serve as an expert philosopher of language with specialist training in institutional design and collective coordination. Before drafting, briefly list the core Lewisian conditions for a genuine convention—coordination problem, arbitrary but stable equilibrium, common knowledge, and salience sustained by precedent—and identify which ones are genuinely present or genuinely threatened in the case below. Think carefully about how digital scale and asymmetric observational access affect common-knowledge dynamics.

I sit on the Governance Committee for our university’s open collaborative research platform, where contributors edit shared living documents. Over three years an unwritten norm has emerged: if you revise another contributor’s section, you must leave a brief explanatory annotation visible to all. The norm is not enforced by software; it persists because experienced users model it and newcomers eventually learn through observation. Recently, a faction of senior contributors proposed formalizing the norm into a binding platform rule with automated flags, arguing that as our user base grows, newcomers no longer share the common observational history that made the precedent self-enforcing, so coordination is breaking down. Another faction argues that formalization would destroy the convention’s flexibility and turn a Lewis-style emergent equilibrium into a top-down regulation, undermining the very common-knowledge dynamics that sustain it.

Draft the committee’s decision memo recommending either formalization with a specific sunset or review clause, or deliberate preservation of the emergent norm with a specific alternative mechanism, with reasons the opposing faction could recognize as addressing their best point rather than dismissing it. Lead with the bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence; then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
