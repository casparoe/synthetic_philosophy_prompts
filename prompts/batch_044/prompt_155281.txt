Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave ABBAABBA, sestet CDECDE or CDCDCD, in iambic pentameter with a clear volta at line 9—that renders Schopenhauer's philosophy of music with enough conceptual precision that a reader of The World as Will and Representation would recognize the specific view rather than a generic Romantic celebration of music. The octave should present painting, sculpture, and poetry as representations of Platonic Ideas—mediated copies of the Will's objectifications—while the volta at line 9 marks music's radical exception: it is not a representation of an Idea but a direct copy and objectification of the Will itself, the blind, striving thing-in-itself. In the sestet, let the bass and melody serve as structural analogues to the inorganic world and the human will respectively, tracking Schopenhauer's hierarchy from the lowest bass vibrations to the singing melody that expresses desire, satisfaction, and renewed striving. End on the paradox that music communicates the world's innermost essence with perfect immediacy, yet remains entirely unintelligible to conceptual reason. The rhyme scheme and rhythm should do philosophical work—the volta should sound like a break in mediation—so that the form enacts the content: representation yielding to direct expression. Keep the diction formal but not archaic, and avoid resolving the tension between music's transparency to feeling and its resistance to explanation.
