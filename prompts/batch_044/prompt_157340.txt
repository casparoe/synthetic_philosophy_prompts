I'm on the archive and liability board for the Interregional Crisis Classification Council — we don't write law, but we classify historical mass-mortality events as either "natural disaster" or "political crime / entitlement failure," which changes reparations and how they're taught in schools. Right now we're deadlocked over the 1978 Kharan Basin crisis. The goverment is petitioning for the "natural disaster" label, submitting rainfall data that does show a regional drought. But our investigators found grain reserves in three adjacent provinces were actually above the five-year average, and the deaths were concentrated in districts where local officials had replaced human ration-card clerks with an early predictive-allocation algorithm that used census data missing entire nomadic populations. So food was physically present, but the system literally couldn't see those people as claimants — starvation came from informational exclusion, not absence of calories.

My co-chair argues this is just a technological glitch layered over a real drought, and that Sen's old framework — the Bengal famine, the Irish case, the Great Leap Forward examples where food existed but access collapsed — doesn't apply because the root cause here was climate stress triggering the algorithm's failure. I think she's wrong, but I need a memo for Thursday's vote that actually takes her drought argument seriously rather than waving it away. The memo should recommend a concrete classification — natural disaster, political crime, or the hybrid "structural informational famine" she proposed — with reasoning the goverment can respond to, plus a clear timeline and record-release conditions if we go with the hybrid option. Don't just give me generic ethics advice; I need the board's formal reasoning structured like an institutional opinion with an actionable bottom line.
