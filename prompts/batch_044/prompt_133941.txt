Rank the following five conceptions of merit-based desert—ordering them from the most defensible to the least defensible within a normative framework that treats innate talent as morally arbitrary and achievement as socially embedded: (1) desert grounded solely in voluntary effort expenditure; (2) desert as entitlement to outcomes produced by developed abilities; (3) desert tied to success under procedurally fair competitive rules; (4) desert as recognition of character formed through sustained disciplined practice; (5) desert based on the realization of innate potential in the absence of external interference. For each position, provide a brief justification that addresses whether the conception can survive the objection that it rewards advantages that are not themselves deserved, and explain why adjacent ranks are closer or farther apart in overall coherence.
