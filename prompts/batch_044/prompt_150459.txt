Here's an argument I ran into on a forum thread about Maxwell's demon and now I can't shake it, even though the conclusion smells wrong.

(1) Landauer's principle says erasing one bit of information necessarily dissipates kT ln 2 of heat.
(2) The usual fix for Maxwell's demon is that the demon eventually fills its memory and has to erase, generating enough entropy to save the second law.
(3) But there's a result that if the demon never erases — if it runs its computation reversibly and then "uncomputes" its records — it can sort molecules without ever paying the Landauer cost.
(4) So the standard resolution only works if we assume the demon's computation is irreversable. A fully reversible demon genuinely violates the second law.
(5) Now brains are physical computers that generate a lot of heat, which suggests they do mostly irreversible computation.
(6) But consciousness doesn't feel like constant bit-erasure; mental states flow and transform, not like a hard drive being wiped.
(7) So if consciousness is computation, then a being with a fully reversible mind could think with near-zero metabollic cost — essentially a cold, conscious demon.
(8) That means either consciousness isn't computation at all, or thermodynamics allows conscious beings that don't generate heat, which seems absurd.

I feel like something slipped between (4) and (7) but I can't name it. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd prefer to keep (1) and (5) if I can.
