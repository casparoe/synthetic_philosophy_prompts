Write a one-year postdoctoral research proposal (approximately 2,500 words, for a philosophy-of-mind fellowship) addressing the meta-problem of consciousness through the concept of explanatory modal insulation. Your guiding hypothesis—half-formed—is that the standard debate between illusionists and realists about the hard problem misses a structural feature of self-modeling architectures: a system may be built so that its own phenomenal reports are treated as invariant across all physically identical duplicates, not because consciousness is ontologically basic, but because the reporting mechanism lacks the representational resources to model its own absence without contradiction. Frame the precise research question so that it admits of a determinate answer: Is the meta-problem best explained by (a) a genuine explanatory gap between physical processes and phenomenal experience, (b) a cognitive bias or framing effect, or (c) a form of modal insulation inherent to any system that reports on its own conscious states?

In the proposal, explain why existing answers—particularly the framing that treats the hard problem as an irreducible conceptual gap and the illusionist counter-proposals that treat it as a misapplied intuition—leave option (c) unexamined, and why testing it requires a different method: constructing a formal model of a self-monitoring system that can either represent or fail to represent its own non-existence across functional duplicates, then analyzing whether the resulting "explanatory gap" intuition tracks an architectural limitation rather than an ontological one.

Outline the approach in phases: months 1–3 for conceptual clarification and formal modeling of the insulation mechanism; months 4–6 for comparison with evolutionary and cognitive-scientific accounts of self-reporting; months 7–9 for philosophical evaluation of whether modal insulation dissolves or merely relocates the meta-problem; and months 10–12 for writing and scope-check. Specify what a successful result looks like (a clear criterion distinguishing modal insulation from both illusion and ontological gap), what partial failure looks like (the model collapses into one of the existing positions without yielding new distinctions), and what would make the project fail entirely (the formal model turns out to be incoherent or empirically vacuous).

Conclude with an honest assessment of scope: why one year is sufficient for the conceptual and formal work but inadequate for empirical testing in artificial or biological systems, and identify the single riskiest assumption—likely that modal insulation can be formalized without smuggling in the very phenomenal concept it aims to explain. Do not include a bibliography or literature review by citation; reference positions and arguments as needed and note that citations will be added separately.
