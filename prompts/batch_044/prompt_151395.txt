The teleological suspension of the ethical means that Abraham puts the universal moral law on hold because he has a direct private command from God that overrides the ethical demand not to kill his son. In this case the telos, or end, is his personal religious relationship, which is higher than the universal. This is similar to what Kierkegaard calls the knight of infinite resignation, who gives up the finite world for the sake of the infinite.
