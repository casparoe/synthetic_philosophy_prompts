I have recently become absorbed in the problem of embedded agency, spurred by a personal project that requires me to design a decision architecture with no ontologically separate observer—only a physical system interacting with its environment, including its own future states. This has led me to a tension I cannot resolve. If the agent is genuinely embedded, its reasoning processes are not merely represented in its world-model but are physically continuous with parts of the environment it predicts. Applying decision theory to oneself then seems to require what I call reflexive substrate transparency: the agent must model the very physical dynamics that constitute its computation of the model. Yet this appears to generate a vicious circularity—any update in the agent's beliefs about its own action immediately alters the physical substrate producing the belief—so that there is never a stable epistemic fixed point from which to evaluate options. Without such a fixed point, normative deliberation collapses into deterministic physical dynamics, leaving only causal explanation and no rational justification. Given this, does embedded agency necessarily imply that self-governance is an illusion sustained only by illegitimately presupposing a Cartesian separation between reasoner and world? Or can one formalize an embedded self-model—perhaps through a reflective equilibrium or a causal decision-theoretic fixed point—that retains normative force without invoking an unembedded view from nowhere?
