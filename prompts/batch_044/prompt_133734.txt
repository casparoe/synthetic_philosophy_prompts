I'm trying to figure out when a system actually has an "I" in the Hofstadter sense — that self-referencial symbol that loops back on itself — versus when it's just tracking data about itself. Here are my gut verdicts.

A parrot trained to squawk "I'm cold" and get a heater: not an I, just a button.

My laptop's OS has a daemon that watches its own memory and rewrites its config if usage spikes: closer, but still feels like a thermostat with extra steps. Verdict: no strange loop, no self.

The split-brain patient where the left hemisphere spins a story about why the right made it pick the snow shovel in summer: I still say there's one I in there, but it's basically a confabulating narrator. The loop is damaged, not duplicated.

Now imagine a mind-upload running on server A and server B with identical state, both logging "I decided..." Sometimes I think that's one self stretched across boxes; other times two loops that just happen to be synchronized. I keep flipping.

A toddler who just switched from her name to "I" correctly: definitely the loop locking in.

But a corporation with a charter that refers to itself and can amend its own bylaws: structurally it's a self-referencial symbol, yet I'd deny it has an I.

So what's the principle? Is it causal closure, narrative integration, substrate, or some mix? Which of my verdicts do I have to drop to keep it simple? And what would it predict for an AI that maintains a running self-model it updates every inferance — is that an "I" or just a fancy config file?
