I advise a government forecasting office that regularly audits century-scale predictive claims—demographic, ecological, and resource-system projections—many of which are pronounced falsified within one generation yet seem partially vindicated in altered form decades later. Taking the Malthusian argument in the 1798 Essay on the Principle of Population as my paradigmatic case—where geometric population growth against arithmetic food production was apparently refuted by agricultural innovation, only for analogous pressure dynamics to re-emerge in ecological and distributional contexts—I want you to construct a reusable, ninety-minute audit procedure that translates philosophy-of-science distinctions about falsification, auxiliary-hypothesis protection, and what we might call “right-but-early” rescue into an operational protocol for non-philosopher analysts.

The procedure must be ordered and explicit: (1) the sequential tests that distinguish falsification of a theory’s structural core from falsification of a protected auxiliary or a misapplied parameter; (2) the precise conditions—distinguishing parametric rescue (recalibrating constants) from structural rescue (revising mechanisms)—under which temporal delay counts as legitimate long-range vindication rather than ad hoc immunization; (3) the termination rules that yield a definitive “falsified” verdict versus those that hand judgment to a values-laden decision-maker because the remaining dispute is normative rather than epistemic; and (4) an explicit catalog of failure modes, including cases where the procedure will misclassify a genuinely rescued theory as dead, or will illegitimately protect a moribund one.

Apply this procedure to three worked cases: first, Malthus’s original claim that population necessarily outstrips subsistence; second, the 1970s Limits to Growth projection that resource depletion and pollution would constrain industrial expansion, which critics attacked by citing unanticipated technological substitution; and third, a hypothetical contemporary forecast that global freshwater scarcity will trigger cascading state-collapse dynamics by 2080, currently facing apparent contradiction from rapid desalination and demand-management advances. In each application, flag the exact step where procedural judgment ends and irreducibly normative or institutional choice begins. Do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke highly specialized formalisms that require external reference; and be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed in procedural clothing.
