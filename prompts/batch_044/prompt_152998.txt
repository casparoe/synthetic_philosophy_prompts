ok so i've been trying to square my views on why we actually like sad movies and books and i'm not sure they fit together. here's where i'm at:

(1) i think the 'paradox of tragedy' dissolves once you seperate object-directed sadness (about the characters) from situation-directed evaluation (about being in the theater/reading). they're different affective targets.

(2) i think catharsis is mostly a myth in the aristotelian sense — there's no emotional reservoir getting drained.

(3) but i do think tragedy works like exposure therapy for grief: your brain runs a predictive model of loss, updates the error signal, and that's why it feels rewarding afterwards.

(4) i also think social display matters — part of why we seek tragedy is to signal emotional depth to others in the audience.

(5) and i think if you stripped out the narrative context and just induced raw sadness chemically, people wouldn't seek it, so the enjoyment is tied specifically to the artistic framing.

do these hang together? i'm especially worried that (2) and (3) sound like they're saying opposite things about whether tragedy heals you, and whether (4) undermines (1) by making the enjoyment about audience dynamics rather than internal affective separation. rank the tensions by severity, say the cheapest revision for each, and flag any that only look like tensions. also say what would change your mind — where's the weakest link in your reasoning?
