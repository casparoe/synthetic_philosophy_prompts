As a PhD student in philosophy of mind working on consciousness and its physical basis, I am preparing a consistency audit of my own view for a working group, and I want to know whether the package below is jointly tenable or whether I am helping myself to incompatible motivations.

Here are my commitments:

(1) I am an ontological physicalist about phenomenal consciousness: phenomenal properties are either identical to physical properties or metaphysically supervene on them with no ontological remainder.

(2) I take the philosophical zombie argument seriously—not as a knockdown proof, but as supplying a genuine, defeasible modal intuition. I think the conceivability of a physical duplicate lacking consciousness provides real (if rebuttable) pressure against physicalism, and I do not dismiss it as a mere failure of imagination or conceptual confusion.

(3) I maintain that there is an irreducible explanatory gap between complete physical descriptions and phenomenal experience: no amount of structural or functional information conceptually entails what it is like to have that experience.

(4) I explain that gap by appealing to the special character of phenomenal concepts—something in the neighborhood of direct acquaintance or recognition-based capacities—which conceptually isolate phenomenal knowledge from physical-functional knowledge, without entailing any ontological dualism.

(5) I reject panpsychism and cosmopsychism, and I hold that consciousness requires specific high-level organizational conditions—something like globally integrated, recurrently broadcast information—such that local sensory integration without global availability is insufficient for consciousness.

(6) I think there is clearly nothing it is like to be a thermostat, but I am genuinely agnostic about whether there is something it is like to be a simple organism (e.g., a nematode or a basic invertebrate) whose neural architecture may fall near or below the organizational threshold in (5).

Please deliver an audit, not a lecture. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but are reconcilable, and how severe each tension is. For each genuine tension, specify exactly what has to give—whether one commitment should be dropped, weakened, or reinterpreted—and what that revision costs elsewhere in the package. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. Then tell me which single revision (modifying or dropping one commitment) would clean up the most problems overall, and whether that is the least costly option available.
