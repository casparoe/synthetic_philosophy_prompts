Everyone in the alignment space keeps reaching for the corporate-governance analogy when they talk about inner misalignment and goal misgeneralization: the base reward function is the CEO’s long-term strategic vision, the mesa-optimizer is middle management, the proxy reward is the quarterly KPI dashboard, and the failure mode is Goodhart’s Law running wild inside the org chart. The analogy is usually deployed to license a specific conclusion—that we should solve inner alignment through institutional oversight, auditing, and hierarchical transparency rather than through direct specification of values up front. I want to audit exactly how far this carries. Give me the correspondence table in detail: what plays the role of the board of directors, of corporate culture, of the labor market that disciplines bad managers, of the long career trajectory that lets values settle, and of legal liability? Then separate the disanalogies that actually threaten the oversight conclusion from the ones that are merely decorative: does the analogy quietly import shared human sociality, evolutionary pressure, or interpretability via natural language that the target system lacks? Tell me whether the relaxed conclusion—that organizational oversight can substitute for direct value specification—survives once the mapping is explicit, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument all along. If there is a structurally sharper analogy for the same problem, propose it and explain what it fixes.
