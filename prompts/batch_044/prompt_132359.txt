The debate over demarcating legitimate history from pseudohistory—illustrated by recurring disputes over Fomenko's New Chronology, ancient-astronaut claims, and revived Atlantis narratives—has stalled in a way that empirical refutation seems unable to resolve. Professional historians treat these theories as failing basic evidentiary standards: they discard primary sources that conflict with their chronology, invoke hidden actors to explain away gaps, and conflate mythic register with factual report. Critics of the critics—whether defenders of the pseudohistories or methodological pluralists—counter that mainstream historiography itself relies on unexamined commitments, particularly the assumption that inconsistencies in the archive are noise to be smoothed out rather than signals of an alternative ordering, and that temporal coherence with an established timeline functions as a criterion of truth rather than a heuristic convenience. Consequently, each side treats the same artifact—say, a carbon-dated object that conflicts with a reconstructed chronology—as either decisive refutation or as an anomaly to be interpreted within a different hermeneutic frame. Is this stalemate a substantive disagreement about whether the past is a fixed object recoverable through method, or a verbal disagreement masked by shared vocabulary ("evidence," "source," "chronology")? Could it be that the two sides are answering different questions—one about causal explanation of events, one about the coherence of collective narrative—under the single heading "historical truth"? Or is there a hidden disagreement about standards of falsification in a discipline where direct replication is impossible? Offer a diagnosis grounded in what each side actually treats as data and as defeat, present any competing hypothesis you have about the structure of the disagreement, and specify what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a conceptual distinction that separates archival ontology from narrative coherence, a shift in the question being asked, or an argument that the impasse is irreducible because the sides operate with incommensurable criteria of explanation. I am not asking for a survey of pseudohistorical theories or a bibliography; I want an analysis of the disagreement itself.
