(1) I am a conventionalist about the Ship of Theseus: there is no mind-independent fact about whether the continuously repaired vessel or the plank-reassembled one is the original; our naming and tracking practices settle the matter.

(2) Still, in our actual world there is a uniquely correct verdict: the repaired ship is the original, and the reconstructed one is a replica.

(3) The convention that delivers (2) tracks spatiotemporal continuity of functional form rather than material continuity, because that best serves practical interests in ownership, preservation, and navigation.

(4) Had our practical interests evolved differently—say, toward treating original material as sacred relic—then the reconstructed vessel would correctly be the original, with no metaphysical error involved.

(5) I apply the same conventionalist framework to personal identity: psychological continuity is the relevant tracking convention for persons, not because it captures a soul, but because it serves practices of moral responsibility and anticipation.

(6) Unlike artifacts, however, I think personal identity involves an objective fact: whether I survive is determined by psychological continuity regardless of what society decides to track.

(7) I reject any explanatory gap between physical descriptions and our identity judgments in both domains; these judgments are just practical conventions applied to physical processes, with no hidden essence being tracked.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like it, and rank the conflicts by severity. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Also tell me whether dropping any single commitment—say, (6) or (7)—would clean up the most inconsistencies. Finally, if my framing rests on a confused premise—for instance, if I am misusing "conventionalist" or treating practical justification and metaphysical determination as interchangeable—say so plainly instead of politely reconstructing my view. I want pushback, not salvage.
