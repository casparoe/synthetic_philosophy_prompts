Engineers now store digital archives in synthetic DNA, applying telecommunications-style error-correction codes, compression algorithms, and addressing schemes that treat nucleic acid sequences as arbitrary symbol strings. Meanwhile, developmental biologists treat the genome as one causal factor within a materially embedded regulatory network rather than as a readable program. What philosophical commitments about representation, information, and causation does the storage-engineering practice presuppose? Does it require that DNA carries semantic content in its own right, or is a purely syntactic, channel-theoretic conception sufficient? Reconstruct the implicit theory of genetic information at work, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely borrows metaphorically, say whether those commitments are compatible with the developmental-biology view, and tell me what would have to change in the engineering if DNA were shown to lack any representational properties whatsoever.
