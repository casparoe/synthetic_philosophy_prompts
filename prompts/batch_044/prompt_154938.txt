You staff a municipal heritage office that must draft public plaque texts for monuments contested between groups with incompatible historical narratives — for instance, a colonial-era statue that one community honors as a founder and another condemns as an oppressor. You want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Jain anekāntavāda — specifically the doctrines that reality is multi-faceted (anekāntavāda), that every statement expresses only one partial standpoint (naya), and that qualified assertion (syādvāda) should replace absolute affirmation or denial — to determine whether a draft plaque falsely absolutizes one narrative and, if not, how to frame it with appropriate standpoint qualifiers.

Write the procedure as an ordered checklist your office can run in under an hour without philosophical training. Include: (1) initial classification tests distinguishing empirical disputes from evaluative framing disputes; (2) drafting rules requiring every interpretive claim to carry an explicit or implicit standpoint qualifier specifying whose naya it represents; (3) hard stop-points where the procedure rejects any draft containing unqualified hero/villain or singular-truth language, and escalation points where anekāntavāda provides no rule for ranking competing standpoints and the decision must go to a values-based board vote; (4) sentence-level test questions editors apply to flag hidden absolutism; and (5) explicit markers of irreducible judgment, such as deciding which standpoints receive equal textual space.

Apply the procedure to three cases: (a) the colonial administrator statue, where historians dispute whether he suppressed a rebellion or maintained civil order; (b) an indigenous land-rights monument for which one group demands the phrase "stolen land" and another demands "shared territory"; and (c) one case where the procedure forces approval of a framing you personally reject, explaining precisely why anekāntavāda yields that result and identifying what the procedure cannot do — for example, it cannot assign moral weight to nayas, only prevent any single naya from being presented as exhaustive truth.

Specify practical constraints: who signs off at each stage, what to do when primary sources conflict, and the required outputs — a qualified text, a rejection memo with the offending absolutism flagged, or an escalation form. Note where the procedure will misfire or remain silent.
