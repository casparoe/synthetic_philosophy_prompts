I was arguing with my roommate last night about privacy and the state — german data protection rules vs american openness norms vs the chinese social credit thing — and I realized my opinions are all over the place, just gut feelings with no real logic. So I'm writing them down to see if there's a pattern. If I live in Germany and I invoke GDPR to make the state erase my old search history from there archives, that feels completely correct. If I'm an American activist and I voluntarily publish my full tax returns online to show I have no hidden foreign ties, that feels correct too, almost like a civic duty. And if I'm in China and I use a VPN to hide that I'm reading banned historical texts, I think that's justified, no question. But then: if I deliberately feed false traffic patterns into smart-city sensors to mess with state monitoring, that feels wrong, even though I'm protecting my privacy. Teaching my kid to always answer 'no idea' when census workers ask about our family beliefs also feels wrong — like I'm poisoning democratic accountability from the inside. On the other hand, a whistleblower using encrypted dead drops to leak corruption evidence feels totally right. And refusing to use a state-mandated digital currency that tracks every purchase, opting for cash despite the fines, also feels right to me, even though I'm actively concealing my economic behavior.

Here's where I can't reconcile them: hiding my genetic predisposition to a disease from a public health registry feels wrong, like I'm breaking a social contract. But constantly spoofing my location with a privacy app so the state never knows where I am feels... maybe okay? Or maybe it's the same as the fake smart-city data? I'm genuinely torn on that one. I also can't tell if the difference is about *what* I'm hiding (personal facts vs. state corruption), *why* I'm hiding it (fear vs. protest), or whether the hiding corrupts the state's information pool. Is there a principle that captures all of this — maybe something about defensive opacity being okay but offensive data corruption being bad? Or do I just have to admit that one of my verdicts is inconsistent? I don't need a bibliography, just the logic of the cases.
