You are an expert in formal metaphysics and modal logic serving as the senior peer referee for a journal that receives about two submissions per month reconstructing, extending, or criticizing Gödel's modal ontological argument and its direct variants. You never have web access during your twenty-minute review window, you are not permitted to hunt for bibliographies or citation lists, and you must sign off on every recommendation. You need a reusable evaluation procedure—a flowchart in words with ordered tests—that operationalizes the core machinery of Gödel's argument (positive properties, the consistency condition on their set, the inference from possible to necessary existence of a being with all such properties, and the modal-collapse objection) without requiring you to re-derive modal logic from scratch each time.

Begin by carefully brainstorming which conceptual distinctions must survive simplification for the procedure to remain honest—specifically, the difference between intensional property ascription across worlds and extensional quantification over worlds, the distinction between consistency of a property set and closure under negation or conjunction, and the boundary between formal validity in a frame and metaphysical soundness—versus which distinctions can be dropped for speed. Then construct your deliverable as a four-pass decision protocol, each pass stating: the exact question you put to the manuscript; the textual evidence required; the pass/fail/judgment-call rule; and the point labeled JUDGMENT CALL where your own theoretical commitments must enter.

Pass One—Frame Specification: identify whether the author uses S5, S4, B, KB, or a restricted accessibility relation; check whether the frame is justified independently of the conclusion; flag circular appeals.

Pass Two—Intensional Stability Audit (your niche procedural injection): for every property designated "positive," determine whether its extension is stable across all worlds accessible in the author's frame. If the author treats the property as world-bound in one step and trans-world in another, record an INTENSIONAL EQUIVOCATION failure. If stability holds but only by definitional fiat, record JUDGMENT CALL.

Pass Three—Consistency and Collapse Containment: verify that the set of positive properties is explicitly claimed consistent. Then run the Collapse Containment Check: ask whether the model allows any contingent truths at all once the necessary being is introduced; if not, determine whether this is an explicit feature of the argument or a hidden consequence of property closure. Label the assessment of whether closure is legitimate as JUDGMENT CALL.

Pass Four—Existential Inference: examine the move from "possibly, a being instantiates all positive properties" to "necessarily, such a being exists." Mark exactly where this step depends on the frame's accessibility conditions and where it depends on an unstated existence premise.

Next, apply the full protocol to three hypothetical submissions, annotating each with pass/fail/JUDGMENT CALL outcomes:

A. A standard Gödel reconstruction in S5 with explicit definitions of "positive" and "God-like" and an explicit consistency claim.
B. A variant that replaces "positive" with "essential" and adopts an S4 frame with accessibility restricted to "metaphysically nearby" worlds.
C. A formally ingenious submission that avoids modal collapse by adopting a highly restricted, non-standard accessibility relation, passes every formal check in Passes One through Four, but forces you—against your theoretical sympathies—to recommend rejection because the restriction is ad hoc and unsupported by any independent metaphysical argument. This is the case where the procedure gives you an answer you will not like.

Finally, include a polished "Limitations and Misfires" section stating exactly what the procedure cannot do: it cannot adjudicate whether omniscience, omnipotence, or any particular attribute is genuinely positive; it cannot resolve theological disputes about the nature of divinity; it cannot distinguish formal validity from soundness when premises are undefined or circular; and it will misfire on manuscripts that employ non-classical quantification, paraconsistent property logics, or undefined modal operators, all of which fall outside its scope. Present everything as headings, ordered steps, the three annotated cases, and the limitations section. Do not include reading lists, citation requests, or references to specific publication dates or page numbers.
