ok so in my working group on autonomous systems we've been going in circles for months about responsibility gaps. half of us think it's basically an epistemic failure: if we had full interpertability of the decision trace we'd always find a human who signed off on the training data or deployment paramaters, so there's no real normative gap, just transparency. the other half says that's missing the point—even with perfect traceability, distributed learning means no single agent meets the conditions for moral responsibility (control + knowledge + intentionality) in a way that actually tracks blame. my working theory is we're not really disagreeing about ethics but about what 'responsibility' is doing: one side uses it descriptively (who can be held to account?) and the other structurally (where does the causal buck stop?). but maybe there's a hidden empirical question about whether interpretability actually restores control? diagnose what kind of stall this is—verbal, substantive, hidden empirical, or us answering different questions under one heading. and be honest: would a formal model of distributed agency move this, or is it bedrock intuition? what would progress even look like?
