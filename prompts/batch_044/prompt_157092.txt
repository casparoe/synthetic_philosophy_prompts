Explain the conceptual tension that arises when a disposition is protected by a dynamically coupled fink-mask system rather than by a static interference. Consider a hypothetical glass object encased in a responsive polymer shell: the shell is normally inert, but if the glass begins to fracture, the shell detects the vibration and releases a binding agent that halts the crack. Crucially, the shell's own disposition to release the agent is triggered specifically by the initial manifestation of the glass's fragility, so the protective mechanism and the masked disposition are temporally interdependent. Is the glass genuinely disposed to break but masked, genuinely lacking the disposition because it is constitutively coupled to the shell, or is fragility here a relational property distributed across the composite system? Address why this interdependence complicates standard binary classifications between finks and masks, and do so without appealing to canonical textbook illustrations such as poison-antidote pairs or packaged fragile goods.
