Write a joke that turns on the logic of the unilateralist's curse, applied specifically to the governance of transformative artificial intelligence. The joke should be intelligible to someone who grasps the curse — that when several agents can each independently take an irreversible action, the agent with the lowest threshold for acting effectively decides the outcome for all, rendering collective caution futile — but it should avoid generic punchlines about robots destroying humanity. Instead, inject a concrete, slightly absurd scenario: a university ethics committee in which every senior member holds an individual veto over adopting a new AI-safety protocol, except that one junior researcher can unilaterally deploy the system from a laptop in the departmental common room. The humour should arise from the decision-theoretic structure itself — the exasperation of the risk-averse majority being held hostage by the least cautious actor — rather than from caricaturing either the technology or the committee members. Make the punchline depend on someone recognising why the veto power is meaningless once unilateral deployment is physically possible.
