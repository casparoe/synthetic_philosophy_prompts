The mature virtue was already latent in the student's nature, meaning moral education is redundant in principle, however practically convenient.
(5) If Xunzi is right, the student had no compassionate tendency whatsoever prior to training. Ritual constructs the disposition ex nihilo from raw desire. But then the resulting compassion is not the student's own nature expressing itself; it is an external architecture imposed upon an alien substrate. The student becomes a well-conditioned automaton, not a genuinely moral agent whose actions flow from an integrated self.
(6) Therefore, on either classical framework, successful moral education cannot be both genuinely transformative and authentically expressive of the agent's nature: it must be either redundant (Mencius) or inauthentic (Xunzi).
(7) Yet our best phenomenological and developmental evidence suggests that mature moral agents are both profoundly changed by education and experience their virtues as authentic, not as foreign implants.
(8) So the Mencian-Xunzian dichotomy forces us to deny either the efficacy or the authenticity of moral cultivation, which is implausible.
