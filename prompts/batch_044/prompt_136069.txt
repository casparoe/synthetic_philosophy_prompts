ok so here's roughly where I've landed on Foucault and I want a consistency check befor I present it in seminar. I hold that:

(1) power is productive, relational and capillary — not possessed by anyone but circulating through norms, knowledge-formation and institutional practices;

(2) the modern subject is constituted by disciplinary mechanisms — schools, clinics, and now algorithimic sorting systems — rather than being prior to them;

(3) genealogy reveals that our present moral and epistemic categories are historically contingent, and that recognition should weaken our attachment to them;

(4) resistance is always immanent to power relations, never transcendent — there's no pure outside or Archimedean point;

(5) Foucault's later work on care of the self shows that ethical self-fashioning can open partial spaces of freedom that aren't just another form of normalisation;

(6) every disciplinary regime borrows from a prior normative framework it can't generate itself — call it the normative residue — and this residue is structurally unavoidable, so genealogy can't undermine it without destroying the conditions for social coordination.

The thing is, I'm not sure if (2) and (5) hang together. If the subject is fully an effect of discipline, where does the agent who fashions herself come from? Also, (3) says genealogy should weaken attachment, but (6) carves out an exception for the normative residue — is that just ad hoc? And (4) with (5): if resistance must be immanent, don't these "partial spaces" look like they're escaping power rather than working inside it?

But I also wonder if some of these only look bad. For example, does (1) actually clash with (4), or is that a misreading of how capillary power allows immanent resistance?

Please go through the pairs, rank the tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most. I want an audit, not a lecture — specifically, which commitments pull against each other, which ones are only apparently in tension, and what the cheapest fix is.
