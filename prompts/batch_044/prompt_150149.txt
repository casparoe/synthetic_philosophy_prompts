Exam question (20 points):

In Meditation II, Descartes concludes that he exists whenever he thinks. Analyze precisely what this argument establishes: does it prove only that a particular thought is occurring, that a persistent thinking substance exists, or something in between? In your answer, distinguish the cogito’s immediate conclusion from any later arguments about the nature or persistence of the self. Then address the following hypothetical: imagine a distributed cogito, a networked system in which reasoning is spread across multiple nodes, none of which has individual awareness, yet together they sustain a continuous stream of inferential processing. Would Descartes’ argument apply to this system? Identify which premise—if any—would fail, and whether the failure reveals a limitation in the argument or a misapplication of it.

Student answer:

Descartes’ cogito proves that whenever I think, I must exist as a res cogitans, a thinking substance whose essence is thought. Even if an evil demon deceives me about every external object, the very fact that I am deceived shows there is a subject being deceived, so I can be certain of my substantial existence. From this, Descartes immediately establishes that the soul is entirely distinct from the body, since he can clearly and distinctly conceive of himself as thinking without attributing any extension or physical properties to himself. The argument therefore does not merely show that one thought is happening; it shows that a persistent mental substance underlies all mental activity. Applying this to the distributed case, if the network produces a continuous flow of reasoning, there must be a single collective substance that thinks, distributed across the nodes like a brain distributed across neurons. The only problem is that no individual node can utter the phrase I think, but the system as a whole can, which means the cogito actually supports a collective res cogitans. So the argument proves substantial existence for both individuals and networks, and critics who say it only proves momentary existence are confusing the cogito with a mere observation about mental events.
