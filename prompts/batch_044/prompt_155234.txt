For my high school philosophy journal, I am drafting an essay on the following controversy. Daniel Dennett’s intentional stance holds that attributing beliefs and desires to a system is legitimate whenever doing so yields reliable predictive success, regardless of whether the system possesses internal mental states in any deep metaphysical sense. Some philosophers and computer scientists have recently applied this framework to large language models, arguing that treating a model as holding beliefs about physics, ethics, or history allows us to predict its outputs more efficiently than analyzing its weights or training data. Critics reply that this is a category mistake: a model generates tokens through statistical correlation, not reasoning or representation, so attributing beliefs is at best a convenient fiction and at worst conceptually misleading.

To push this debate beyond its familiar form, I want to introduce a condition I call interpretive robustness: an intentional attribution to an AI is warranted only if it remains stable across counterfactual perturbations of the prompt—changes in wording, framing, temperature settings, or assigned persona—such that the system consistently produces outputs that cohere with the attributed belief. If an attribution collapses the moment the surface framing shifts—for example, if a model affirms the scientific consensus on climate change in a neutral query but denies it when asked to role-play a skeptic—then even on Dennett’s pragmatic criteria the attribution fails, because its predictive power is tethered to prompt-specific correlations rather than to a genuinely stable dispositional pattern.

Write an essay arguing whether the intentional stance should be adopted toward contemporary AI systems given this interpretive-robustness condition. Weigh the pragmatic defense—predictive utility, explanatory economy, and the irrelevance of internal mechanism—against the mechanistic objection that token-generation lacks the causal and representational structure that beliefs require. Address whether robustness is a fair demand to place on an intentional system, or whether it illegitimately imports expectations drawn from human psychology. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a clear verdict on whether we should ever say an AI believes anything, and justify that verdict with direct reference to the robustness condition. You need not supply citations, a bibliography, or reading list; I am interested solely in your conceptual reasoning.
