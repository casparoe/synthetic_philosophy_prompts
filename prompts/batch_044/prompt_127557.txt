I am preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar comparing Buddhist moral psychology with modern consequentialism, and I want a consistency audit of the following package before I defend it. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept a suffering-weighted consequentialist framework: the rightness of an action is determined by its contribution to the minimization of aggregate suffering across all sentient beings, with no privileged boundary at the self or species.

(2) I endorse the Buddhist doctrine of anatta (no enduring self) and therefore reject any metaphysical subject who persists to "own" karmic fruits across rebirths.

(3) I hold that in the Buddhist analysis, cetana (volitional intention) and its associated mental factors (cetasikas) are the primary loci of moral evaluation; an action's ethical quality is fixed by the wholesome (kusala) or unwholesome (akusala) mental states present at the moment of volition, not by its downstream causal effects.

(4) I believe that upaya (skillful means) can license conventionally harmful actions—such as deception or even killing—when they are motivated by pure compassion (karuna) without attachment to self or result, and when they genuinely produce a net reduction in suffering.

(5) I maintain that karmic causation should be understood, given (2), as an impersonal causal transmission across mental continua (like a flame passing between candles), not as a system of personal reward and punishment.

Please audit these for joint tenability. Specifically: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which tensions are only apparent and dissolve under closer inspection, and which single commitment is the least costly to revise if consistency demands it? Rank the real tensions by severity. Be precise about whether the problem lies in (1) clashing with (3), in (4) being incompatible with the impersonal karma described in (5), or in some other pairing. Also address whether (2) undermines the agent required for (4), or whether that worry dissolves once conventional designation (samvriti) is distinguished from ultimate reality (paramartha). Do not treat every tension as equally severe; I want the audit ordered, with the cheapest fix named for each.
