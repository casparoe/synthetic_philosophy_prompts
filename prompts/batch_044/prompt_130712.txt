You serve as a senior advisor in Bayesian epistemology and institutional decision design, with particular familiarity in how conditional testimony distorts belief updating in high-stakes analytical settings. Before you begin drafting, please spend a few moments brainstorming the precise tension between preserving prior independence and allowing the form of a report itself to carry evidential weight about its antecedent — especially when analysts have no independent access to the source’s reliability. Think carefully about how a procedural rule might block a Judy Benjamin-style shift without pretending that testimony events are ever fully independent of the states they describe.

I sit on the oversight committee for a national strategic-risk unit. Last month we received Proposal 7-B, which would mandate a specific updating rule for analysts who receive conditional intelligence reports — for instance, reports structured as “if the adversary has activated contingency plan Alpha, then they have not activated contingency plan Beta”. The proposal requires analysts to apply what its authors call “independence-preserving conditionalisation” (IPAC): they must hold their prior probabilities for Alpha and Beta constant upon receiving the conditional, updating only the joint distribution so as to respect the logical constraint, and they must not allow the report itself to shift belief in whether Alpha is true. The rationale is that without direct evidence about Alpha, treating the conditional as evidence for Alpha commits a version of the Judy Benjamin error — allowing belief in an antecedent to rise merely because one has learned a consequence it implies, even though the conditional’s truth-value provides no direct information about the world.

A minority of the committee — including our chief methodologist — argues that this is overly rigid and institutionally naive. In real intelligence contexts, she notes, the very fact that an analyst receives a conditional report of this specific form is rarely independent of whether Alpha is live: sources tend to construct conditionals precisely when they possess indirect indicators about the antecedent, and suppressing that signal forces analysts to ignore structurally relevant information. Mandating IPAC therefore risks institutionalising a false presumption of independence between the testimony event and the state of the world. The majority, however, fears that without a hard rule analysts will systematically over-update on antecedents, producing cascading false alarms.

Please draft the committee’s formal decision memo — addressed to the unit director — recommending either adoption of IPAC, rejection, or adoption with specific modifications, together with a concrete implementation time frame of no more than ninety days. Your memo should engage seriously with the chief methodologist’s objection, explain the Judy Benjamin dynamics at stake without merely naming the problem, and propose one specific procedural safeguard that addresses her concern without abandoning the core constraint against antecedent-shifting. Write in polished British English, using a formal institutional register, and structure the document with a clear recommendation, reasoning, and a brief but honest assessment of the strongest counter-argument.
