I want a rigorous specification of the adequacy conditions for any philosophical account of 'Western values' — that is, what such an account must be able to do if the category is to be treated as coherent rather than merely rhetorical. List the desiderata: the extension it must capture (e.g., whether liberal-democratic institutions, secular rationalism, and individual rights form a natural kind or an accidental cluster), the distinctions it must preserve (e.g., between values that are historically specific to Western European development and those that are universal but institutionally dominant there), the argumentative functions it must explain (e.g., its use as a civilizational boundary marker, a normative benchmark in international law, or a foil for multicultural critique), and the internal tensions it must accommodate (e.g., between universalist human-rights claims and particularist cultural-defense arguments). For each, state the motivating case. Then flag any subset of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, the demand for historical specificity that excludes Confucian or Islamic analogues, and the demand for normative universality that permits cross-cultural application — and identify which desiderata are non-negotiable versus camp-specific preferences. Finally, in brief, assess how a genealogical-institutional account, a normative-functional account, and a purely discourse-analytic account (treating the phrase as an essentially contested concept with no stable extension) fare against your list. Weight should fall on the criteria and their conflicts, not on advocacy for any one view.
