I've been working out my position on two-dimensional semantics and phenomenal concepts for a workshop, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I endorse a 2D framework with primary (A-) and secondary (C-) intensions for all expressions, and I take this to solve Kripke-style puzzles about necessary a posteriori identities—e.g., that "water" and "H2O" share a secondary intension but differ in primary intension.

(2) For phenomenal concepts, I hold that the primary intension is fixed by a direct acquaintance relation rather than a descriptive mode of presentation; but I deny that this acquaintance yields any a priori entailment linking phenomenal states to physical-functional descriptions.

(3) I think the hard problem is best understood as an intension-gap rather than an ontological gap: the primary intension of a phenomenal concept and the secondary intension of its physical correlate fail to align, which is why physicalism can be true yet not epistemically transparent.

(4) I maintain that belief reports must be evaluated relative to both a world and a context of assessment to preserve Fregean cognitive differences; yet I also think that if belief is a relation between an agent and a 2D-intension, then grasping both "water" and "H2O" entails grasping the same 2D-intension for both, which collapses the very cognitive difference 2D semantics was introduced to save.

(5) I reject the view that phenomenal concepts have a "pure" primary intension entirely independent of physical-functional roles—since I think the acquaintance relation is itself causally embedded in neural processing—but I still want to invoke the intension-gap in (3) to explain the explanatory gap.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once. Be specific about whether (4) undermines the motivation for (1), whether (5) undermines (2) or (3), and whether the acquaintance claim in (2) is compatible with the causal embedding in (5).
