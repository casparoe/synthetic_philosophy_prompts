I am a student in an advanced philosophy of mind course, and we have been studying the rule-following paradox as presented in Saul Kripke's interpretation of Wittgenstein's later work. For a paper I am drafting, I need to assess five well-known responses to the skeptical challenge—(1) the straight solution, (2) the skeptical solution, (3) dispositionalism, (4) meaning primitivism, and (5) the normative anti-reductionist view that grounds rule-following in communal practice—against a constructed case that does not appear in the standard literature. The case involves a linguistic community whose complete computational archive is stored in a perfectly symmetric medium: for every recorded application of "add 2" below 1,000, the physical record is compatible with both genuine addition and with an alternative function that diverges only above 1,000, with no internal feature of the archive breaking the symmetry. Please rank these five responses in tiers from S (most resilient in the symmetric-archive scenario) to F (least resilient), and for each tier provide exactly one sentence explaining how that response either preserves or fails to preserve a non-arbitrary fact of the matter about which rule was being followed.
