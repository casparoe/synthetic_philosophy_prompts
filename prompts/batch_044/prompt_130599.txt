each node should be a concrete claim you can agree or disagree with without already knowing the jargon. start with whether predictive coding in the brain is literally bayesian inference or just a functional metaphor. then branch on Markov blankets: do they carve reality at its joints, or are they just modeling conveniences? then hit the dark-room problem — is active inference the real fix, or does the problem show FEP can't apply to cognition?

then a scope branch: does FEP apply only to metabolically bounded living systems, or to anything that maintains a steady-state (thermostats, corporations, whatever)?

for each leaf, name the position if it exists (radical predictive processing, enactivist FEP, instrumentalist coding, etc.) and flag unoccupied combos — are they incoherent, ugly, or just nobody's defended them? flag any pairs your tree can't seperate.

audience: people who've read some Clark and Friston but don't need a bibiography or citations — this is for in-class sorting, not a literature review. write casually, it's ok if there's a typo or two.
