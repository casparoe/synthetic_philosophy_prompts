As a doctoral student in the philosophy of science studying predictive failure across long time-horizons, I am developing a comparative framework for cases where a theory appears empirically defeated in the short run but is later defended as having been correct in its underlying dynamics though premature in its timeline. I need you to operationalize this framework into a reusable decision procedure that a research assistant without specialized philosophical training could apply consistently to individual case files.

Please construct a step-by-step evaluation protocol for classifying the status of a long-run predictive theory—specifically one that forecasts a structural relationship between population, resources, or analogous variables—when observed outcomes diverge from the prediction within the originally specified window. Your procedure must distinguish four evaluative categories: strict falsification by contradictory outcome; temporal displacement with the core mechanism intact; mechanism substitution in which the causal pathway is replaced but the limiting outcome eventually appears; and scale mismatch, in which local or sectoral falsification coexists with global or long-run confirmation. For each category, specify the evidentiary thresholds that must be met, the counterfactual comparisons the evaluator must construct, and the precise point at which the decision must be handed to disciplinary judgment rather than resolved by rule—particularly regarding what counts as the "same mechanism" versus an ad hoc theoretical rescue.

The protocol should include explicit stopping conditions that tell the evaluator when further iteration is unwarranted, and it should flag the specific steps where internalist and externalist historiographical traditions would legitimately diverge without undermining the procedure. Once the framework is specified, apply it to three cases: first, Malthus's original argument in the Essay on the Principle of Population concerning the relationship between population growth and subsistence in Europe, accounting for the agricultural and demographic transitions that interrupted the predicted trajectory; second, a hypothetical 1970s forecast of global resource depletion by the year 2000 that failed because of unanticipated efficiency and substitution effects, but in which severe regional shortages emerged by the 2020s through a different causal pathway; and third, a deliberately ambiguous case—such as a late-twentieth-century prediction of widespread urban collapse due to energy constraints that was avoided through decentralized adaptation—where the procedure should yield an unsettled result requiring normative judgment about whether adaptation counts as mechanism substitution or as falsification of the original structural claim.

Please present the procedure in ordered steps with decision nodes, apply each case with explicit reference to the relevant category or judgment point, and conclude by stating what the procedure cannot do—specifically, which philosophical disputes about scientific realism and theoretical identity it necessarily leaves unresolved.
