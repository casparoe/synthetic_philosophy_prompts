For a graduate seminar in metaphysics of science, construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts philosophical positions on how ontology should be fixed when direct information about underlying entities is permanently inaccessible. To keep the instrument from being answerable by recalling standard encyclopedia entries, frame the root problem through two injected distinctions that should serve as major branch nodes: first, whether the inaccessibility is vertical—produced by a physical or causal shield that blocks observation of deeper layers, as with cosmological horizons or measurement-induced opacity—or horizontal—produced by structural underdetermination, where multiple ontologically distinct bases generate identical observable correlation patterns with no further fact of the matter; second, whether the region that is inaccessible should be treated as ontologically empty, as ontologically surplus but permanently unknowable, or as replaced entirely by pure relational structure without relata.

The tree must begin with the root commitment, “I accept that some ontological information is permanently inaccessible to any observer in this system,” and proceed through concrete case judgments rather than through label-identification questions. Include at least one node that asks whether dark-matter gravitational signatures are better interpreted as evidence for an invisible particle ontology or as evidence for a modified relational geometry with no additional entities; include another node that asks whether quantum-field correlations observed without particle-track accessibility justify postulating field quanta, pure structural relations, or no ontology beyond the correlation matrix itself.

The leaves must cover: (1) a restricted entity realism that accepts only entities and properties derivable from accessible correlations; (2) an epistemic structural realism that treats references to inaccessible entities as heuristic placeholders with no literal ontological weight; (3) an ontic structural realism that denies individual entities altogether and affirms only relations; (4) an instrumentalism that rejects all ontological commitment beyond predictive and explanatory structure; and (5) a dual-aspect view that assigns the inaccessible information a non-physical but genuinely real ontological status. For each leaf, provide exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment.

Where combinations of answers produce unoccupied logical spaces, list them briefly and diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, conceptually unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate—particularly whether ontic structural realism and the dual-aspect view collapse into one another on this framing, and whether restricted entity realism can be distinguished from epistemic structural realism when the accessible correlation set is maximally sparse. Present the result as a nested branching structure with the diagnostic question written at each node, keep the total number of leaves between six and nine, and ensure that no node asks for a bibliography, citation, named author, or recognition of a philosophical label; every question should be answerable by a respondent who knows only the cases and the distinctions you have introduced.
