Consider the color phi illusion: when a red dot at position A is replaced shortly thereafter by a green dot at position B, observers report seeing continuous motion through the intermediate space with the color shift occurring near the midpoint. Philosophers distinguish Orwellian explanations, in which an intermediate green-dot experience is formed and then replaced in memory, from Stalinesque explanations, in which the intermediate representation is never constructed in consciousness because the brain revises the record in real time. If the Stalinesque account is correct, it seems that the content of any conscious moment must be fixed only after a revision is completed, which would mean that what we take to be the present is always a reconstructed past. Doesn't this entail that direct realism about temporal properties fails not just for the illusion but for all perceptual experience, since every conscious "now" is necessarily delayed relative to its physical cause? And would the strength of this argument change depending on whether the revision occurs in early sensory buffers rather than in higher-order working memory?
