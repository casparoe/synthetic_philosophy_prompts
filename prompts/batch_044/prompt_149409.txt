So here is my profile, as precisely as I can state it.

First, on origins: I hold that any human being is essentially tied to the particular sperm and egg from which she actually developed; a person who arose from different gametes, even with an identical genome reconstructed through synthetic means, would not be me. Second, on diachronic identity: I think personal identity over time is mainly a matter of psychological continuity—if my psychology were preserved in a different biological substrate, I would survive—so biological continuity is not necessary for my persistence. Third, on modality: I take origin claims to be necessary if true, but known only a posteriori, not by conceptual analysis. Fourth, on twins: I think identical twins are essentially different individuals precisely because of their distinct origins, despite sharing all psychological and genetic properties at birth, which seems to show that origin can individuate even when psychology and biology do not. Fifth, on artificial reproduction: I think that if technology reconstructed my exact genome from synthetic gametes that were not the ones that actually produced me, the resulting person would be a perfect duplicate but not identical to me. Sixth, and finally, I think that if psychological continuity is what matters for survival, then origin essentialism must apply only to organisms, not to persons—yet I also want to say that I am essentially a particular organism.
