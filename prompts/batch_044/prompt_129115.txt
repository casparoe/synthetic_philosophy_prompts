For my philosophy of mind course, we have been comparing judgment-based, perceptual, and feeling-based theories of emotion. Before I weigh their arguments, I want the full specification of what an adequate account must actually deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena any theory must explain, the distinctions it must preserve—such as that between an emotion’s intentional object and its phenomenal quality, or between occurrent affect and a dispositional sentiment—the boundary cases it must classify correctly, including moods without determinate objects and reactive attitudes that blend cognition with visceral response, and the empirical findings from affective neuroscience it must accommodate without contradiction. Crucially, build in the stress case of sustained tri-partite divergence, where an agent’s explicit evaluative judgment, interoceptive physiological state, and behavioral disposition come systematically apart and remain unintegrated over time, and say what a theory must claim about such divergence if it is to avoid collapsing one dimension into another. Then indicate which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are partisan commitments of particular camps—such as whether unconscious emotional processing is mandatory, or whether every emotion must possess a specific intentional object—and flag whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. Please present this in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing in American English as though in a careful letter.
