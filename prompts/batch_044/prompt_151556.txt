As a PhD student in philosophy of technology and AI alignment, I am auditing an analogy that keeps surfacing in low-impact AI discussions: the agent as a "good houseguest" in an unfamiliar home. The idea is that just as a polite visitor avoids rearranging furniture, using excess resources, or leaving traces—without needing to know the owner's full life plan—so too can an AI minimize side effects without a complete specification of human values. Proponents use this to license the claim that "avoid side effects" is value-neutral and technically tractable, independent of the hard problem of value alignment.

I want you to audit how far this holds. First, lay out the explicit correspondence table: what plays the role of the host, the furniture, the visit duration, the implicit etiquette rules, and the "rearrangement" that counts as harm? Then identify where the mapping fractures, distinguishing the disanalogies that actually undermine the argument (for instance, the temporal asymmetry between a temporary stay and persistent deployment; the fact that the agent's very presence may restructure the "house" ontologically; and the indexical problem that "normal" baseline conditions are themselves value-laden) from the ones that are merely cosmetic. In particular, examine whether the analogy smuggles in a static, authoritative normative baseline—the owner's preferences treated as given—that collapses once we recognize that human values are contested, dynamic, and partly constituted by the agent's actions.

Does the conclusion survive—namely, that impact measures can be defined without full value specification? Give me a committed answer: either defend a restricted, boundary-conditioned version of the houseguest model and specify exactly where it applies, or argue that the analogy collapses entirely and that "low impact" necessarily presupposes a value framework. If you think a different analogy does the work better, propose it and show why. Do not present both sides as equally viable; take a definite position, state the conditions under which it holds, and flag the weakest point in your own reply.
