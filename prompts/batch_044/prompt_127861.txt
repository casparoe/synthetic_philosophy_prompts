I hold that the archaeological-anthropological critique of grand environmental determinism—particularly the claim that seasonal reversals of political authority, which I will call institutional reversibility, show hierarchical state formation to be one contingent social mode rather than a default trajectory interrupted only by geographic or climatic accident—undermines not merely Diamond-style narratives but the very ontology that permits climate, pathogens, or volcanic events to be treated as independent historical actors. On this view, ecological conditions can constrain social form only through collective practices that remain permanently capable of reorganizing themselves; consequently, attributing causal efficacy to a non-human event is to reify a temporary human-environment configuration into an autonomous agent. What would genuinely count against this position? I want decisive and merely awkward defeaters separately: archaeological sequences showing irreversible state consolidation despite demonstrable institutional flexibility; epidemiological or climatological models that isolate environmental dynamics from social mediation in ways that cannot be redescribed as collective response; formal results in philosophy of science about causal attribution in complex adaptive systems that collapse the distinction between structural constraint and collective choice; and historical cases in which seasonal reversibility demonstrably failed to prevent ecological catastrophe. Which of these would defenders of institutional reversibility concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss by arguing that the reversibility in question was not genuine or not fully activated? Does the position predict anything that a modified determinism—one that grants local agency but treats macro-environmental thresholds as binding—does not? Has the core claim been qualified into a modal thesis, that reversibility is always possible in principle regardless of historical outcome, such that no empirical finding could falsify it? If so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue that preserves the normative priority of collective agency, or is it a defect that renders the position unfalsifiable and therefore explanatorily empty?
