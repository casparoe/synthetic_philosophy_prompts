In my poli phil reading group people keep using the rental anology for the three liberties: negative freedom is an unlocked door, positive is having the car keys and gas to actually drive through it, republican is renting where the landlord can enter anytime but usually doesnt. They use it to argue republican liberty just collapses into negative liberty plus an irrational fear of eviction, since if the doors unlocked your free either way. Can you lay out the full correspondence table -- door, keys, landlord, lease, eviction notice, whatever -- then show where the mapping breaks, especially whether non-domination actually needs visibility of power or equal status in ways the other two dont, and whether the redundancy conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table? I also want to know which features of renting (like contract terms or market alternatives) get smuggled in and dont belong to the target concept.
