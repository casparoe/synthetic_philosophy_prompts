I just finished a long and rather heated argument with a close friend about whether societies have a moral duty to preserve the memory of historical wrongs, or whether they possess a corresponding right to collective forgetting. We kept returning to Margalit's solidarity-based claim that remembering victims is an obligation owed to the dead and the living alike, and to Rieff's counter that institutionalized remembrance risks becoming a perpetual politics of grievance that freezes communities in past injuries. Yet we never articulated the underlying architecture of what a theory in this space is actually being asked to deliver. Before I pursue further reading, I want the full specification.

Write the desiderata that any adequate normative theory of collective remembering and forgetting must satisfy. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it — including, at minimum: how the theory treats the asymmetry between perpetrators and victims regarding memorial obligations; whether descendants inherit either the duty to remember or the license to forget; how it distinguishes between having moral status as a rememberer and possessing a particular weight or intensity of obligation; its account of the individual right to be forgotten in both digital and analog contexts; and its consistency with the biological and psychological limits of intergenerational testimony.

In addition, incorporate the specific challenge my friend raised, which she labeled the principle of mnemonic proportionality: the claim that the social and material resources a community devotes to preserving a particular memory ought to scale not with the original harm's magnitude but with the present risk of recurrence, and that if this constraint holds, solidarity-based arguments collapse into forward-looking risk management. Your list should include whether an adequate theory must accommodate, reject, or remain neutral on this proportionality constraint, and whether doing so can be done without erasing the distinction between commemorative justice and preventive justice.

Then identify any pairs of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether an unconditional communal duty to remember can coexist with robust individual rights to erasure, or whether intergenerational transmission of memorial obligations is compatible with the non-inheritance of moral responsibility. Finally, in no more than a brief paragraph, indicate how Margalit's position and Rieff's argument fare against your list: which requirements each view satisfies and which each is forced to sacrifice.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on a comprehensive defense of any single position. I am not asking for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations of particular passages; I want the conceptual architecture itself.
