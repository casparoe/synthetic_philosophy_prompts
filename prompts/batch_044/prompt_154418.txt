I run a grad seminar in phil of knowledge, about 12 people, 75 minutes. I want an in-class game that makes them feel why knowledge is better than true belief, not just hear about it. Design a two-phase navigation activity: phase one, small groups get either correct directions with no backing (true belief) or the same directions plus a structural rule they can derive them from (knowledge), then navigate a hidden grid to a target. Phase two, they teach a new player the route while I randomly contradict one instruction; the true-belief groups should collapse, the knowledge groups repair. Use non-monetary payoffs — points that determine a silly task like reading a poem aloud. No lecture up front; they shouldn't hear "Meno" or "Daedalus" until the debrief. The debrief should map the collapse and repair onto Plato's tethering idea, say exactly what will predictably go wrong first run (the knowledge groups over-explain and run out of time — how to handle it), and be frank about what the game can't reproduce: that knowledge can still fail if the structural rule is false, and that the value gap might disappear if nobody ever needs to teach the route. Keep it runnable with just paper and pens, and don't ask them to cite texts.
