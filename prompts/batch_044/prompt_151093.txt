I'm moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher defending a recently developed account of incommensurable values. On her view, hard choices between genuinely incomparable goods do not end with rational permission but generate what she calls normative residue—standing obligations to the values that were not selected, obligations that she distinguishes sharply from psychological regret and argues should be institutionalized through what she terms value-conflict archives. These archives, she suggests, should formally preserve unresolved value tensions within democratic deliberation rather than dissolving them via aggregation or compromise. I need eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to summarize the position. Specifically, I want questions that press on: first, the conceptual boundary between normative residue and the familiar pluralist concession that every choice involves loss; second, the risk that if every hard choice produces enduring obligations to the rejected alternative, practical reasoning collapses into a kind of permanent moral paralysis; third, the concrete institutional mechanics of these archives—particularly how they avoid degenerating into either purely ceremonial monuments or instruments for strategic obstruction; and fourth, the objection that her framework illegitimately transforms a descriptive feature of value conflict into a prescriptive institutional mandate. For each line of questioning, please include a follow-up tailored to the reply she is most likely to offer—for instance, that residue carries action-guiding force for future deliberation rather than backward-looking guilt, or that archives function as living constraints rather than static records. The sequence should escalate in pressure and conclude with a question about what evidence or conceptual result could genuinely count against her central claim that incommensurability produces residue rather than mere practical indeterminacy.
