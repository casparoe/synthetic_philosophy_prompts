I'm preparing interview questions for a philosophy-of-mind colloquium featuring an unnamed scholar who defends a modal-dispositional reading of Descartes' thesis that animals are automata. She argues that animal bodies possess a fully articulated structure of sensory-motor dispositions—capable of adaptive, context-sensitive response—yet entirely devoid of representational content or conscious awareness, and that Descartes' mechanistic physiology in fact requires this modal richness to explain behavioral flexibility without invoking a soul. I need eight to ten questions that press on the specifics of this interpretation rather than rehearsing generic dualism. For each primary question, provide a targeted follow-up calibrated to the answer she is most likely to offer; for instance, if she appeals to mechanistic explanation in Descartes' physiological manuscripts to defend the modal richness of animal bodies, ask whether that same mechanistic framework could, in principle, be extended to human bodies without the pineal-gland intervention she reserves for humans, and what principled barrier prevents such an extension.
