You serve as methodological adviser to a regional public-works board that reviews high-stakes infrastructure proposals and must combine conflicting stakeholder reports about risk and welfare. The board wants to operationalize Harsanyi's aggregation theorem—under which social preferences over lotteries satisfy the von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms and a weak Pareto condition if and only if social welfare equals a state-independent weighted sum of individual cardinal utilities—into a reusable decision procedure that non-economist members can execute in a single two-hour session without software.

Construct the procedure as an ordered sequence of tests with explicit stopping rules: (1) the verification questions that establish whether each stakeholder report can be treated as a vNM preference ordering; (2) the check for the independence axiom and what to do when it fails for one group; (3) the determination of welfare weights, marked clearly as a value judgment that the procedure cannot resolve; (4) the computation or approximation step, with instructions for what to do when cardinal utilities are only ordinally ranked; and (5) the final aggregation rule and the conditions that force deferral to a human vote. Specify the hard cases the procedure must handle, including stakeholder proxies who represent future generations through discount-rate models and groups whose ambiguity aversion about climate scenarios violates independence. Apply the procedure to two proposals: reinforcing a coastal levee where agricultural representatives reject standard lotteries over flood probabilities, and extending a transit corridor where present users and a future-generations proxy disagree on both outcomes and weights. Include one application where the procedure recommends approval that conflicts with the board's intuitive moral judgment, and explain why. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot determine—especially the normative source of the weights and the status of goods that fall outside utility—so the board knows where judgment is unavoidable.
