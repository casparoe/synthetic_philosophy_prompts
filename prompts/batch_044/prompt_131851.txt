I am preparing a short essay for a philosophy of art seminar on Schopenhauer's account of music. My argument is that his claim that music is the direct objectification of the Will—unmediated by the forms of representation—sits uneasily with his own analysis of melody, which he describes as essentially successive and therefore bound to the temporal form of the principle of sufficient reason. If melody requires temporal succession to represent the Will's striving, I contend that it remains within the realm of representation rather than offering immediate access to the thing-in-itself. I then explore whether Schopenhauer's distinction between melody (representing human striving) and harmony or bass (representing the inorganic grades) can rescue the view, but I worry that assigning structural roles to harmony and bass still relies on representational mediation. Could you comment on whether my reading is accurate to the text, on whether the tension I identify genuinely undermines his metaphysics of music or merely repeats a familiar problem about temporal art, and on whether there is a more charitable interpretation—perhaps drawing on his remarks about dissonance—that preserves music's privileged status? Please reply in British English, and keep your remarks focused on the conceptual coherence of the argument rather than on secondary literature.
