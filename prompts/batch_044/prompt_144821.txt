In philosophy-of-mind discussions, epiphenomenalism is often introduced through an analogy that compares phenomenal consciousness to a live telemetry display inside a self-driving vehicle: upstream sensors and control algorithms determine every maneuver, while the display renders rich qualitative information—colors, textures, sounds—purely for observation, with no feedback loop into the steering or braking systems. Defenders of the view use this image to license two claims: that phenomenal properties are genuinely present and can be reported, yet causally idle with respect to behavior; and that first-person judgments such as 'I am now seeing red' are outputs of the upstream computational machinery, not effects of the displayed redness itself. Opponents then invoke the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if the judgment is generated entirely by mechanisms that are blind to the display, it seems the very same judgment could occur even if the display were blank or absent, which threatens the accuracy and authority of phenomenal reports. Audit this analogy in detail. Provide an explicit correspondence table—map the sensors and algorithms, the rendering pipeline, the passenger-observer, the judgment-output, and the absent feedback path onto their proposed mental analogues. Then isolate the disanalogies that bear specifically on the paradox: does the display carry representational content that the algorithms must already encode, and if so, does that reintroduce a causal or informational link that epiphenomenalism denies? Assess whether the analogy smuggles in a representational relation between computation and phenomenology, and whether the conclusion—that phenomenal judgments remain accurate despite the causal inertness of what they report—actually survives once that smuggled relation is excised. Finally, if the vehicle-display comparison breaks down, identify the feature of phenomenal judgment—perhaps its first-person immediacy or its resistance to error through misrepresentation—that any adequate analogy would need to preserve.
