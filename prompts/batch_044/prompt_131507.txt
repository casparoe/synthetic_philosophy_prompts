I've been thinking on my own about Marx's ideas on exploitation and alienation, and I'd like to check whether my views actually hang together. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Exploitation is when someone keeps the value your labor creates beyond what they pay you back.
(2) Alienation is not just hating your job; it's what I call "task opacity" — not being able to see or explain how your specific action connects to the final product.
(3) Both problems share one root: other people own the tools and decide the plan.
(4) If workers collectively owned the factory and voted on production, both problems should vanish, even if people still earned different wages based on skill.
(5) At the same time, I think a cooperative could still be exploitative inside if some members do all the dangerous labor while others do paperwork, yet profits are split evenly.
(6) Separately, I think some jobs are naturally repetitive and boring, and that this boredom is different from task opacity — you'd feel it even if you designed the repetition yourself.

Are these commitments jointly tenable? Which pairs pull against each other in a real way, and which only look like conflicts? If one view has to go, what's the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. I don't have an academic background, so I'm working from what feels intuitively right to me.
