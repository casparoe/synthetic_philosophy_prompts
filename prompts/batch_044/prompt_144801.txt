I have been discussing with a colleague in philosophy of mind whether various collective or distributed entities possess genuine mentality rather than mere adaptive behaviour, and I should like to see whether her judgements form a coherent pattern that can be extended. Her verdicts so far are as follows: she holds that healthy adult humans clearly possess minds; that individual ants and bees do not, despite displaying remarkably complex behaviour; that a beehive is not a mind, even though it solves navigation and resource-allocation problems no single bee could manage, because there is no unified subject of experience; that a Portuguese man o' war—functionally one organism composed of specialised zooids—is closer to a single biological individual and therefore likely possesses some minimal form of integrated experience; that a lobster probably has some simple sentience; and that a contemporary large language model lacks a mind altogether, owing to its absence of biological embodiment and evolutionary history.

Would she judge this colony to possess a mind?
