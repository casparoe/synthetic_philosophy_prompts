Please argue that the etiological account of biological function—according to which a trait's function is fixed by the evolutionary history that selected for it—cannot consistently assign functions to de novo engineered proteins in synthetic biology, and that this failure exposes a structural limitation rather than a mere boundary case. Specifically, defend the claim that if an etiological theorist appeals to the designer's intentions as a proxy for selection history, the account collapses into a causal-role framework disguised by evolutionary metaphor, since what does the explanatory work is the protein's current contribution to a pathway rather than any historical selection process. In constructing your argument, address the strongest reply available to the etiological theorist: that designer selection is genuinely analogical to natural selection and therefore extends the account without altering its core logic. Then show why that analogy breaks down at the precise point where we must distinguish between a synthetic protein's biochemical activity (its causal role) and its intended role in an engineered pathway—distinctions that etiological accounts, lacking independent evolutionary history, cannot draw without smuggling in causal-role criteria. Keep the argument focused on functional attribution in synthetic systems rather than broad metaphysical debates about natural kinds, and be explicit about which premises you take to be conceptual and which depend on the actual practice of synthetic protein design.
