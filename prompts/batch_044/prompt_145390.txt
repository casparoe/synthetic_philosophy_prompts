I just finished a long argument with a friend about Nietzsche's three uses of history—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—and whether an institution can genuinely do all three at once without betraying each. Tomorrow I am giving the opening remarks at the dedication of our university's Center for Institutional Memory: a building that contains both a commemorative gallery funded by alumni donors and an unredacted archive of institutional failures that students insisted be included. The audience will be split between donors expecting celebration, archivists committed to total preservation, and students demanding judgment. Write a five-minute address that uses Nietzsche's three modes as its structural backbone—determining exactly what I may celebrate, what I must preserve without embellishment, and what I am obliged to criticize—while taking a clear position in my argument with my friend rather than offering a bland compromise, and without alienating any of the three groups present.
