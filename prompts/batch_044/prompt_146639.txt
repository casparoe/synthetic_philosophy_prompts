I am a high school student studying literary interpretation, and I have noticed a recurring pattern for which I lack a term. In classroom analysis, when a poem contains a line that conflicts with the interpretation my teacher favors, she explains that the speaker is not the poet and that we should not read authorial intention into the text; yet when a different poem contains a line that supports her preferred reading, she treats it as direct evidence of the poet's belief. The separation between speaker and author seems invoked only for inconvenient passages, rather than applied uniformly. This feels different from general arguments that authorial intent should not determine meaning, because those address whether intent matters at all, not this selective, asymmetrical use of the distinction as a rescue device. Does this configuration have an established name? If not, please define its precise boundaries—distinguishing it from ordinary ambiguity resolution and from blanket rejections of intentionalism—and propose a concise label. Answer in one paragraph, no more than 120 words.
