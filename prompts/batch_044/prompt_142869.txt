I've been working through a line of reasoning that combines panpsychism with quantum decoherence, and it seems to collapse into the conclusion that ordinary life is astronomically immoral. Here is the chain. (1) If panpsychism is correct, consciousness or proto-experience is fundamental to physical reality and is not restricted to complex brains. (2) On a variant I will call decoherent integrationism—where phenomenal binding requires sustained quantum coherence—the loss of coherence through environmental decoherence constitutes, at the micro-level, a fragmentation of experience analogous to suffering or harm. (3) Every macroscopic action, including breathing or walking, causes decoherence in an astronomical number of quantum systems in the immediate environment. (4) Therefore every macroscopic action harms an astronomical number of micro-subjects. (5) If causing harm to subjects is pro tanto wrong, and wrongness aggregates across subjects, then every macroscopic action is astronomically wrong. (6) Clearly, breathing is not astronomically wrong. (7) So either decoherent integrationism is false, micro-decoherence is not genuinely harmful, aggregation fails at this scale, or we must accept that ordinary life carries massive moral wrongness.

I want to preserve (2), since without some bridge from coherence-loss to proto-phenomenal harm, panpsychism seems ethically inert; and I want to preserve (5), since I think moral wrongness should scale with the harm one causes. Where should I cut? Which step fails—whether in the move from physical decoherence to phenomenological harm, from individual harm to aggregate wrongness, or from causal contribution to moral responsibility? Explain why that step initially looked defensible, and price the alternatives: if I reject the decoherence-to-harm bridge, do I forfeit the ethical significance of fundamental physics? If I reject aggregation, do I invite a different kind of moral paralysis? I would rather keep (2) and (5) and sacrifice an earlier or later link, but I need to know precisely what each amputation costs.
