im a nursing student and lately ive been wondering what 'function' means when we say an organ failed. I read that etiological accounts say the heart pumps blood because thats what selection kept it around for, while causal-role accounts say it pumps because thats its contribution to circulation right now. But heres where I get stuck: if I take causal-role seriously, and my patients heart is damaged and now mainly causing fluid buildup instead of circulation, hasnt its biological function literally changed to 'producing edema'? Because thats its actual contribution now. The etiological view avoids that by locking function to history, but then isnt it just saying malfunction is 'not doing what evolution intended'—which feels circular, like defining a broken heart as one that doesnt act like an unbroken heart? So are these actually competing theories, or are they both just sneaking normativity in through different doors and we cant tell which one is cleaner?
