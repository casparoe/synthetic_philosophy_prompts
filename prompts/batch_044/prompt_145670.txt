You serve as staff advisor to a university Historical Naming Committee that must decide the fate of the [X] Lecture Hall, named for a 19th-century public intellectual who was an early vocal opponent of chattel slavery yet also published a celebrated defense of dueling as an indispensable mechanism of honor among gentlemen. The committee is divided not over biography but over which framework explains moral revolutions. One faction, working from an Appiah-style account, holds that norms like dueling collapse when honor structures are redefined through argument and social practice, and that retaining the name implicitly endorses an obsolete honor culture. Another faction, working from a Williams-style thesis, holds that such norms disappear when they become incompatible with centralized state violence and bourgeois economic interests, with moral arguments serving mainly as post-hoc rationalization, and that judging the figure anachronistically mistakes the direction of explanation. Draft a concise policy memo recommending whether to rename the hall, retain it with contextualization, or retain it unchanged. Your memo should apply both frameworks to the specific case of dueling's decline, identify which framework better explains that collapse, state a clear actionable recommendation with reasoning, and include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; reason directly from the conceptual structures described.
