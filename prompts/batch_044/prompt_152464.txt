Argue that Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi should be treated as a recursive, framework-sensitive operator rather than a static four-part taxonomy of object-level claims. Specifically, defend the view that applying the fourfold negation reflexively to the conventional/ultimate distinction itself does not produce the standard Madhyamaka result—that all phenomena lack intrinsic nature (svabhāva)—but instead generates a meta-stable regulative constraint: the emptiness of emptiness operates as a standing prohibition against fixing any explanatory order, rather than as a positive ontological thesis. Respond to the charge that this reflexive move either illicitly privileges a meta-language or collapses back into the second corner (universal negation without remainder). Proceed from the internal logic of the four positions—affirmation, negation, conjunction, and disjunction—without invoking named commentarial traditions or specific textual citations.
