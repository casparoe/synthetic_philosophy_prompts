Analyze the collapse of the European belief that all swans are white—following Willem de Vlamingh's 1696–1697 observation of Cygnus atratus near the Swan River—through the lens of Nelson Goodman's new riddle of induction. I am not asking for a history of ornithology; I want a disciplined mapping of the conceptual structure. Before 1697, European naturalists projected the predicate "white" across a sample that was, in effect, restricted to Cygnus olor and related species within Europe. Treat that geographic restriction as structurally analogous to the temporal cutoff in Goodman's grue: ask whether "white" functioned as an entrenched, projectible predicate only inside an implicit reference class—say, "swans observed in Europe prior to 1700"—and whether a grue-style alternative such as "white-in-Europe-and-black-in-the-Antipodes" was logically available but linguistically unentrenched. Then examine the reverse direction: does the discovery of black swans show that Europeans were projecting the wrong predicate in Goodman's sense, or does it show that the failure was one of sampling from a non-representative reference class rather than of predicate selection? In particular, address whether Goodman's framework can distinguish between a predicate that is misprojected and a sample frame that is too narrow, given that "representative sample" is itself defined within the same practice of entrenchment. Finally, say what this episode reveals about Goodman's claim that induction is fixed by linguistic practice rather than by features of the world—does the geographic isolation of Australian fauna support that claim, undermine it, or reveal that the distinction between predicate and reference class was never stable? Be explicit about what would change your mind: identify the specific interpretation of entrenchment, or the specific account of taxonomic practice in 17th-century natural history, that would cause you to conclude that the black-swan case is either a clean confirmation or a decisive counterexample to Goodman's solution.
