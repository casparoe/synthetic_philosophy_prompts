case 3: victimhood culture, same slap, he documents it, gets community solidarity, and an institution actually investigates. if the harm is real this feels okay, maybe even empowering.

case 4: but then there's a dignity-based workplace that treats a mildly rude email like trauma, demands months of mediatiion and a public apology. feels like its lost the plot — worse than case 2 for sure, maybe even worse than case 1? i'm not settled.

case 6: victimhood culture where someone manufactures outrage over a trivial slight and gains status or resources, and the community rewards them. feels gross, clearly the worst of the bunch.

case 7: a dignity culture that tells oppressed groups "just develop thick skin, your worth is internal" while ignoring real structural violence. feels deficency, not fully superior.
