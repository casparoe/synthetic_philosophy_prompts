Write a dialogue between Dr. Kira Voss and Dr. Elias Reyes debating whether multiple realizability requires shared causal topology or can rest solely on teleological functional role. Voss argues that mental states are genuinely realized in different substrates only when those substrates preserve identical counterfactual dependency structures among internal states; she accepts silicon brains but rejects radically distributed systems lacking discrete state transitions. Reyes argues that normative, adaptive integration—maintaining homeostasis, error-correction, and environment-responsive stability—is sufficient, regardless of substrate architecture.

Have them discuss a concrete hypothetical case that neither treats as familiar: a synthetic "mycelial-computational mesh," a fungal-electronic hybrid network with analog sensors, that corrects nutrient-routing errors through cross-domain feedback loops and maintains persistent adaptive "preference" states without neurons, logic gates, or discrete representations. Each speaker must respond directly to the other’s previous point rather than talking past them, and neither should be a strawman. Give each speaker at least four turns. Do not include citations, named philosophers beyond the two characters, bibliographies, or requests for clarification.

Format: open with a single sentence summarizing the precise crux of their disagreement; then present the dialogue itself. Keep the entire response under 550 words.
