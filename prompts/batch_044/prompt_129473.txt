(3) i think dreams are experiences in a thin sense -- there's something it is like to be dreaming -- but not in the thick sense of being like waking perception, since there's no sensory input being processed.
(4) i'm drawn to the predictive-processing story where the brain is just running its generative model offline during sleep, so the "experience" is just predictions running loose without error-correction.
(5) i think if someone is in a lucid dream and can signal with eye movements, that proves there's a conscious experience happening in real-time, not just a story made up on waking.
(6) i also think the explantory gap applies to dreams: even if we map every REM correlate, we still don't know why there's a phenomenology at all.
