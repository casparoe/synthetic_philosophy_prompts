I've been convinced by a particular reading of Williams's integrity objection — not the usual spouse-in-the-burning-building version, but a stronger claim: that close relationships depend on what you might call directed necessity, where the reason for acting has to be indexed to the specific person in a way that can't be unpacked into further justifications. I think this doesn't just hit utilitarianism; it also hits any moral theory that demands full transparency of reasons, including some versions of virtue ethics that try to systematize care. But I'm worried I'm overreaching. What would actually count against this stronger claim? I want defeaters that go deeper than you can have both thoughts — things like: whether it forces an implausable view about obligations to strangers in triage cases, whether it collapses into a moral particularism that can't distinguish love from obsession or abuse, or whether there's a hidden formal mistake in treating reasons as either fully transparent or fully opague. Rank them by how fatal they'd be, and be honest about whether the view has just insulated itself into unfalsifiability by defining genuine care as whatever lacks the extra thought.
