As a novelist drafting a manuscript on mortality, I am trying to clarify my own stance toward death through a lens close to Zhuangzi’s—treating death as transformation rather than termination, yet without assuming any personal survival. Here are my intuitive verdicts, and I want to know whether they cohere.

One: I think grieving my own future death is appropriate, not irrational, because it mourns the irreversible end of this first-person narrative, even if the body will become soil and trees. Two: In a scene I wrote, a dying character asks to be buried beneath an oak so that “I will watch over my daughter”; I feel this is poetically apt but philosophically mistaken, since there is no “I” in the oak to do the watching. Three: I take Zhuangzi’s drumming on the tub for his dead wife as genuine acceptance rather than callousness, honoring transformation over possession. Four: I reject both the reductive claim that death is mere atomic recycling and the claim that the dead literally persist in a new form with the same concerns. Five: The butterfly dream makes me skeptical of any confident assignment of “real” status to life versus death, which undermines both fear of death and hope for a specific afterlife.

Can these judgments be captured by a single principle—say, a distinction I have been calling “lodging-change” (material transformation without narrative continuity) versus “story-change” (the irreversible end of a first-person perspective)—or must one verdict be abandoned? In particular, what should this view predict about the case I keep revising: a character facing death writes a letter asking that her ashes be scattered in a particular garden, not because she will be present there, but because she wants her story to conclude in that place?

Please separate what is settled in Zhuangzi scholarship from what remains genuinely contested among those who study him, and do not present a disputed interpretation as the consensus. If the reasoning takes liberties with Zhuangzi’s texts or depends on a contested reading, please note where.
