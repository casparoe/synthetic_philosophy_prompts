hey, i've been chewing on evidential cooperation in large worlds and i'm kind of over the standard "should you cooperate with your causal twins" framing. before i read another paper on this i want the full spec: what does a theory of cooperation across large worlds actually have to deliver?

write the desiderata list. each requirement should come with the case or consideration behind it. it needs to handle cooperation with agents in causaly disconnected bubbles who share your reasoning architecture (the usual twin case), but also with "alien mathematicians" -- agents who share your goals but reason with totally different cognitive architectures and might not recognize you as a twin. it must respect the distinction between evidential correlation and actual coordination, and it cant just collapse into causal decision theory because we want to avoid Newcomb-style loops. there's also this thing i call indexical leakage: in a big enough world, updating on your own indexical position leaks information about thier positions, which changes what you should do. the theory has to either absorb that or show its an illusion.

flag any pair of desiderata you think are jointly unsatisfiable -- especially if one camp demands locality of decision and another demands global evidential consistency across all reasoning-twins. and briefly, how do EDT, functional decision theory, and a naive "cooperate with every reasoning-twin you can model" view score against the list? dont need a bibliography, just the logic.

make sure you cover which requirements are non-negotiable vs camp preferences.
