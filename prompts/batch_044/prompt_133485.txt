I'm preparing a consistency audit for a philosophy-of-mind discussion group and need a careful check of whether the following commitments hang together. Here is my position on pain, asymbolia, and the badness of pain:

(A) The badness of pain is not an intrinsic sensory quality but the registration of a high-precision interoceptive prediction error: nociception signals a deviation from predicted bodily states, and "unpleasantness" just is that error being weighted as highly salient.

(B) Classic clinical cases of pain asymbolia confirm that this evaluation can be dissociated from discrimination: patients retain accurate sensory localization and intensity ratings but report no unpleasantness and no motivation to withdraw, because the precision-weighting mechanism has collapsed while primary nociceptive pathways remain intact.

(C) Consequently, "pure sensory pain"—intact nociceptive discrimination without affective evaluation—describes a real neurological state, not merely a philosopher's conceptual abstraction.

(D) I therefore think that a being with permanently suppressed precision-weighting (a stable asymbolic architecture) could suffer tissue damage without being wronged, since wrongness in harming depends specifically on causing that high-precision error, not on causing tissue damage per se.

(E) At the same time, I maintain that in any intact predictive system, accurate nociceptive discrimination and minimal error-evaluation are functionally coupled: the model needs the error signal to calibrate predictions, so you cannot stably preserve full sensory discrimination while permanently eliminating all evaluation without disrupting the architecture itself.

(F) I reject both the "raw feel" view (that there is non-functional badness in the sensation itself) and the judgment-added view (that pain is neutral sensation plus a separate emotional belief).

Please audit this package. For each genuinely tense pair, rate the severity, say what must give, and propose the least costly revision. Identify at least one pair that only appears tense but dissolves once the distinction between intact and disrupted systems is kept clear. Then say which single revision—most plausibly to (D) or (E)—would clean up the most inconsistencies, and briefly describe what that revision costs me.

Verdict: state the commitment that should be revised and the core cost of that revision.
