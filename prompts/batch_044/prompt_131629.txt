I've been thinking about the evolutionary debunking argument in metaethics—the idea that since natural selection shaped our moral intuitions for fitness rather than truth, moral realists can't claim our beliefs are justified if they're mind-independent. The debate's been running for decades now, with one camp saying evolutionary origins are just like the origins of math or logic (we can still reason our way to truth) and the other camp saying moral beliefs are too contingent and varied across cultures to be tracking anything real. But honestly, in every seminar I sit in, people just rehearse the same moves: the realist points to a third-factor explanation or to our general reasoning capacities, and the debunker says that dosen't solve the contingency problem. Nobody seems to change anyone's mind.

I'm starting to think the stall isn't about evidence but about what kind of thing moral justification even is, and more specifically whether the debunking target is axiological judgments (good/bad) or deontic modal ones (ought/permitted). Maybe the realist camp is mostly defending our capacity to track values, while the debunker is actually undermining the authority of normative obligation—two different cognitive targets that get collapsed under "moral belief." Or maybe one side is asking an empirical question about whether moral cognition is domain-general enough to be corrected by reason, while the other is asking a conceptual question about whether a belief formed by a process that doesn't care about its truth-content can ever be justified, regardless of what corrections we apply later.

Can you diagnose what's actually going on? Is this a hidden disagreement about the standards of epistemic justification for normative beliefs specifically—like whether deontic judgments need a different kind of warrant than axiological ones—or are they literally answering different questions under the heading "does evolution debunk morality"? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move it? Would it take an empirical finding about the modularity of moral cognition, a conceptual distinction between origin and warrant that separates deontic from axiological tracking, or is the impasse just a sign that the question is ill-posed because "mind-independent moral truth" isn't a coherent target for evolutionary explanation in the first place? I want the structure of the disagreement, not just a verdict on who's right.
