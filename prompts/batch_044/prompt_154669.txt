Write a casual dialogue—like they're arguing over beers—between two people debating well-being and a weird gadget I'll call the 'borrowed-time box'. The box lets you live a subjectively long, happy life, but each subjective year eats ten years of your actual biologial lifespan, and while you're in it you genuinely can't tell whether you're inside or living for real. Mara defends a kind of achievement-plus-narrative view: she thinks well-being requires that your successes actually happen in the world and hang together across your real timeline, so burning ten real years for one fake year is a terrible trade even if it feels amazing. Jules is a hedonist with an epistemic twist: he says pleasure only contributes to well-being if it's epistemicly accessible to you, and inside the box your self-monitoring is systematically unreliable in a way that undermines value. They should actually clash—Mara should respond to Jules's point about access, not just repeat her acheivement line, and Jules should respond to Mara's worry about wasting real lifespan, not just say 'feels good though'. Make them equally sharp, no strawmen. Keep it loose; don't cite specific philosophers, just argue the ideas.
