Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets and a closing quatrain, following the refrain pattern A1-b-A2 / a-b-A1 / a-b-A2 / a-b-A1 / a-b-A2 / a-b-A1-A2—on the commitment paradox in nuclear brinkmanship. The strategic problem is that credible retaliation requires the adversary to believe execution is inevitable, which seems to demand removing human discretion through automated mechanisms; yet that very automation conceals its own failure modes, and the opacity that prevents the adversary from neutralizing the system also prevents them from being assured it will not misfire, eroding the rational foundation of the threat itself. Hold this tension open; do not resolve it.

Use exactly these two refrains, both rhyming with "hand":
We keep command within our hand
We lose command to chance's hand

Maintain the villanelle's two rhyme sounds: every A-line rhymes with "hand" (for example, stand, planned, land, demand, grand), and every B-line rhymes with "fear" (for example, near, clear, here, severe, appear). Around the middle tercets, shift from describing the strategic rationale for automation to exposing the hidden point of failure embedded inside the mechanism. In the final quatrain, place the two refrains side by side so that the conditions for credible deterrence and for accidental catastrophe appear as the same structure seen from opposite angles.

The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with deterrence theory recognizes the specific paradox—intentional threat versus automatic execution, credibility versus opacity—rather than treating nuclear risk as a generic theme. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or references to specific texts.
