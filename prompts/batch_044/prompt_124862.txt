I've been arguing with a friend about whether germs, climate shifts, or volcanoes can really count as "historical actors" in any serious sense, not just background conditions that humans navigate. I'm trying to pin down my actual principle by walking through gut verdicts on concrete cases, and I need to know if there's real coherence here or if I'm just being sloppy and inconsistient.

So here's where I land: the Black Death sweeping Europe in the 1340s and collapsing labor markets feels definatly like an actor—it reshaped feudal obligations in ways no human intended. The 536 AD dust-veil event, probably volcanic though the source is still debated, followed by plague and state budget collapses across Eurasia, also feels like an actor since it kicked off a cascade that changed trade networks. The slow desertification of the Sahara pushing populations south over millenia, though—just background, too gradual to be an agent. Then there's this weirder split: after Tambora in 1815, the cold of 1816 wasn't itself the driver of westward migration in New England, but a specific wheat rust that spread because of the damp conditions destroyed what little harvest remained, and that biological failure specifically triggered the migration wave. For some reason I think the rust counts as an actor but the volcanic winter that enabled it doesn't? That feels shaky. The Irish potato blight in the 1840s—Phytophthora infestans—also counts, it forced social restructuring. But Krakatoa in 1883 altering global rainfall and contributing to famines in China and Ethiopia? That just feels like nature happening, not agency. Smallpox decimating indigenous American populations and reshaping colonial labor systems—actor. Smallpox killing a few dozen in one town with no institutional change—not an actor.

So I have blight and plague usually counting, volcanoes mostly not except maybe 536, climate shifts sometimes yes sometimes no, and this awkward rust-vs-volcano divide. Can you state a precise principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts—saying which case motivates each clause? Then apply it to a case I haven't listed, maybe whether an El Niño pattern that dries out a region and sparks a specific modern resource conflict should count. And be honest: which of my verdicts, especially that rust-volcano split, has to go if I want consistency rather than just adding complexity?
