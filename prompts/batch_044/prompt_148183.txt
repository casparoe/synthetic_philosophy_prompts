I work in software engineering and have no formal philosophy background, but I have been trying to formalize some intuitions about comparison and evaluation, and I want a consistency check before I present them in a discussion group.

Here are five commitments I currently hold:

(1) In engineering benchmarking, comparative evaluation must be transitive: if system A outperforms B on a well-defined composite metric and B outperforms C, then A outperforms C. Non-transitive results are treated as measurement error or metric misdesign, not as a feature of the comparison relation itself.

(2) I find convincing the philosophical argument—often illustrated with a spectrum of lives from flourishing existence down through barely conscious existence—that "better than" fails transitivity for well-being. Each adjacent pair in the chain differs only minimally, yet the extreme ends are radically different in value, which suggests to me that transitivity is genuinely violated, not merely obscured by vagueness.

(3) I think this same spectrum structure applies to AI capability evaluation: successive model generations are only marginally more capable than their predecessors, but chaining enough increments yields a system that is qualitatively different, and I am inclined to treat each link in the chain as a genuine improvement.

(4) I want to resolve the tension between (1) and (2) by domain-relativity: transitivity holds for constructed, quantitative metrics in engineering, but fails for intrinsic value judgments about lives, and there is no contradiction in applying different logical structures to different domains.

(5) However, I also believe that engineering metrics are ultimately instrumental: they are justified only insofar as they approximate improvements in user welfare, societal benefit, or some other intrinsic-value domain. Therefore, if "better" is genuinely non-transitive in the value domain, it should propagate back into the instrumental metrics that track it.

My question is whether this set is jointly tenable. Which pairs are in real tension, and which only appear to conflict? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what the least costly revision would be, and say whether (4) actually resolves the conflict or whether (5) exposes it as an ad hoc boundary. In particular, if I retain both (1) and (2), must I abandon the application in (3), or is there a distinction I am missing that allows me to keep all three? I want a committed answer: identify the weakest link in the package, describe what the cleaned-up position looks like, and defend that choice rather than presenting a menu of options.
