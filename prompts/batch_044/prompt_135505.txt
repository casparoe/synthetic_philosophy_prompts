The essay then argued that this proves morality has no objective foundation, because if moral truths were real and universal, every culture would recognize the same issues as moral ones in the first place. It added that when philosophers defend "moral objectivity," they are usually just describing Western psychological patterns and treating them as necessary truths, and that studies showing cultural variation in the "moral domain"—what people even classify as a moral question versus a social convention—demonstrate that there is no fact of the matter about things like whether breaking a promise is wrong if nobody is harmed. The summary concluded that we should replace talk of objective right and wrong with a kind of cultural anthropology, since the science shows morality is just a local coordination tool.
