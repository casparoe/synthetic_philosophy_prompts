ok so me and my friend got into it last night about those national integration courses they make immigrants take in places like Denmark and Germany. You sit through lectures on 'national values' and history that was mostly standardized by education boards in the 70s and 80s, then you get tested on symbols and stories that are presented like they've always been the heart of the nation. But half of those 'traditional' things were basically invented or heavily rewritten by state bureacratic committees in the late 20th century. So what does that practice actually presuppose about what a nation IS? Does it need Anderson's view that nations are imagined communities that require constant maintenance? Or can it work with some essentialist idea that there's a real cultural core being protected? There's also this weird tension: the state is actively constructing the tradition it's claiming to preserve from outside influence. What philosophical view of identity or tradition makes that coherent rather than just hypocritical? And if you replaced the underlying view with a straight-up constructivist one where nations are just administrative conveniences, what would have to change in how the exam is designed? Would it even make sense to have one?
