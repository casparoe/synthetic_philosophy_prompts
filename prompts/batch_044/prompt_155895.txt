I am a high school junior preparing an essay for my philosophy seminar, and I wish to understand whether a recurring pattern in my own decision-making constitutes a genuine philosophical problem or merely a practical failure of discipline. During the past academic year, I have adjusted my extracurricular commitments in a cyclical manner: whenever my coursework becomes overwhelming, I withdraw from my most demanding club and enroll in a less intensive one; whenever I feel intellectually unstimulated, I reverse the exchange. Each individual adjustment was defensible in light of my circumstances and preferences at that specific time. Nevertheless, the aggregate result is that I have paid non-refundable membership fees and purchased specialized materials for six separate organizations, developed no sustained competence or social connection in any of them, and arrived at the end of the year in a state of greater exhaustion and financial depletion than if I had maintained a single, moderate commitment throughout. A peer has argued that this is not simply poor planning but exemplifies a structural irrationality—specifically, a form of temporal inconsistency in which honoring each present preference allows one's overall position to deteriorate in a predictable, repeating fashion, much as if resources were being gradually extracted by the very mechanism of choice itself. I remain uncertain whether this criticism is valid. Must rationality demand consistency across time, even when one's circumstances and values genuinely shift? Or is it permissible to treat each decision as an isolated evaluation, provided that the local reasoning is sound? Furthermore, does the presence of tangible, cumulative costs—financial expenditure, lost opportunities for mastery, and disrupted social obligations to fellow members and organizers—strengthen the case for consistency across time, or are these empirical consequences irrelevant to the purely rational evaluation of the choice sequence? I seek your advice on how to analyze this situation philosophically, with particular attention to whether local justification can ever be undermined by global pattern, and whether the moral and practical burdens placed on others by each withdrawal alter the rational assessment of my behavior.
