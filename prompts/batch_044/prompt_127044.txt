I am a doctoral student in philosophy of physics working on the ethical analog of the binding problem for panpsychist frameworks. In a recent workshop discussion, a colleague proposed five normative responses to the idea that macro-system suffering might exceed the aggregate suffering of constituent micro-subjects: (1) Micro-Respect, which grants direct moral status only to elementary entities with primitive experience and denies it to composites; (2) the Decoherence-Boundary View, which withholds moral status from unbound quantum configurations but assigns it to any system whose information integration survives environmental decoherence; (3) the Dissipation-Gradient Thesis, which holds that negative valence scales with energy dissipation rates rather than with informational complexity; (4) Cosmological Neutrality, which argues that universal pan-sentience renders comparative ethical obligations moot because suffering is invariant across scales; and (5) Constructivist Denial, which treats macro-suffering as a category error, claiming that experience is always attributed rather than discovered in composite substrates. Please rank these five positions from most to least defensible within current philosophy of mind and physics, assigning each a tier label—defensible, strained, or incoherent—and one sentence of justification. Respond as a numbered list with exactly five items, each one to two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
