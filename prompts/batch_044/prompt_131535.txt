At a recent philosophy-of-mind reading group, a participant dismissed my concern about whether a brain-upload could survive as 'me' by remarking that "there is no self, only the five aggregates; personal identity is merely a narrative fiction maintained for social convenience". I suspect the slogan is doing more work than it should, but I cannot yet say exactly where the ambiguity lies. Could you disambiguate the precise claims that might be packed into that one-liner? Please distinguish at least: (i) the trivial psychological claim that our self-model is constructed; (ii) the metaphysical claim that no enduring substance underlies experience; (iii) the Abhidharma claim that the person (pudgala) exists only conventionally, not ultimately, within a two-truths framework; and (iv) the stronger eliminativist claim that there is literally no entity corresponding to the pronoun 'I' at any level of analysis. For each reading, indicate whether it is defensible, whether it is false, and — crucially — whether it actually undermines the intuition that psychological continuity matters for survival, or whether it simply changes the subject. Lay the readings out as a menu I can bring back to the group, and for each say whether it supports the dismissal of brain-upload concerns. Finally, please state how confident you are in this taxonomy, and identify the specific point about which you are least certain — in particular, whether any version of anatta genuinely negates the normative force of first-person continuity, or whether it merely redescribes it.
