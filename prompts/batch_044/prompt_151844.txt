Please make the argument that pain asymbolia doesn't merely chip away at the idea that pain is intrinsically bad—it actually flips the moral calcullus. The claim is that if the nasty affect can be peeled off while the nociceptive signal stays intact, then what makes causing pain wrong isn't the suffering per se but the risk of breaking whatever binds that affect to the bodily signal in normal agents. You need to tackle the obvious comeback—that this just means pain is bad because we're habituated to it being bad—and also handle congenital insensitivity to pain, where the signal itself is gone, without letting your view collapse into "all bodily damage is ethically neutral". Ground it in actual asymbolic phenomenology rather than treating it as a pure thought experiment. And write it in British English, cheers.
