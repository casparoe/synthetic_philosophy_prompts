I am preparing interview questions for a philosophy colloquium with an unnamed philosopher who defends a bifurcationist interpretation of Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment. On her view, when both the brain and the body retain full causal-functional continuity with the original psychology, personal identity does not relocate to either site but fractures into two partial centers of narrative gravity—neither fully identical to the pre-operative person, yet both genuinely continuous with him. Draft six to eight questions that avoid generic biography and instead press on the mechanics of her position: include one on how she distinguishes this fracture from standard psychological-continuity fission without collapsing into a four-dimensionalist account; one on why split-brain patients do not already trigger the same bifurcation on her criteria; one that anticipates her likely reply that narrative gravity can be genuinely shared across bodies, with a follow-up asking whether identity then comes in degrees; and one on first-person authority—if both descendants sincerely claim to be the original philosopher's successor, is there a fact of the matter about which is wrong, or is first-person judgment itself divided?
