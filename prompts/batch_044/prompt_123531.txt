I've bought into the etiological account of biological function — specifcally the version where a trait's function is whatever it was actually selected for in its ancestoral lineage, not just whatever causal role it happens to play right now. But I'm worried I've insulated it too well with all these "exaptation" and "malfunction" escape hatches. What would actually count against it? I want real defeaters, not just "hearts make thumping noises." For instance, how does it handle horizontal gene transfer where the selection history is totally fragmented? Or synthetic biology parts that work fine but have zero evolutionary past? And when a spandrel gets co-opted, is calling it an exaptation just renaming the problem away? Rank these objections — and any others you think matter — from fatal to merely awkward. Be honest about whether defenders of the view would concede each one, and whether the theory has been qualified into unfalsifiabilty. If it has, is that a problem for a theory that's supposed to tell us what functions actually are?
