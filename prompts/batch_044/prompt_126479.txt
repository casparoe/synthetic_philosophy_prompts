Write a satirical, deadpan institutional review report, approximately 600 words, issued by a fictional university's Committee for the Epistemic Oversight of Phenomenal Reports. The committee operates under a strict physicalist framework that treats all legitimate knowledge claims as derivable from third-person, structurally specified information. The report concerns a graduate student—referred to only as Subject M—who has completed an exhaustive curriculum in the neurophysiology and physics of color vision without ever being exposed to chromatic stimuli, and who has now, after viewing a single calibrated red display, submitted a formal claim that she possesses a new form of knowledge that was not contained in her prior physical information.

The satire should derive entirely from the actual logic of the knowledge argument and its standard physicalist replies: the distinction between propositional knowledge of physical processes and acquaintance knowledge of subjective quality; the ability-hypothesis claim that she has merely gained new capacities rather than new facts; the ambiguity of "what it is like" as either a relational property or an irreducible phenomenal property; and the problem of defining which physical description is genuinely exhaustive. Do not name Frank Jackson, Mary, or any specific philosopher; do not cite texts or request citations. The committee should speak in bureaucratic, procedural language—referencing calibration protocols, data-access logs, and epistemic-compliance forms—while repeatedly failing to comprehend what Subject M could possibly mean by her claim. By the end, a reader familiar with the philosophy of mind should recognize that every objection raised by the committee is a real objection from the literature, and that the comedy lies in applying those objections to a first-person report as if it were a malfunctioning instrument reading. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout: the committee must never wink, and the reader should be able to reconstruct both the knowledge argument and its standard rebuttals solely from the committee's attempts to suppress it.
