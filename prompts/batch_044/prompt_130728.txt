I take the DNA–Gödel isomorphism—what Hofstadter called typogenetics—to be structurally substantive rather than merely metaphorical: the genome encodes not only proteins but also the ribosomal machinery that interprets it, so replication involves a system containing a description of its own interpreter. This resembles a physical instantiation of Gödelian self-reference, with chemical syntax playing the role of logical syntax. What would genuinely count against treating this as a real isomorphism? Which of these would a committed defender have to concede, and has the view been insulated by retreating to an "informational abstraction" that applies only at a very high level?
