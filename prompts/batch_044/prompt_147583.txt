Following a long and unresolved argument with a friend about whether Dennett's three stances map onto real ontological differences or are merely practical heuristics, I want to formalize what we actually need from a theory of pattern-reality. Write the desiderata: what must any adequate account deliver if it respects the physical, design, and intentional stances without collapsing one into another?

Specify the requirements with the case behind each. Which predictive and explanatory phenomena must it cover—especially cross-stance cases where design-level functional patterns and intentional-level belief-desire patterns track the same behavior through different compressions, and interference cases where the stances seem to conflict? Which ontological distinctions must it preserve—stance-relative reality versus mere projection, predictive utility versus full ontological commitment, and the difference between having a pattern and having a particular amount of it? Which consistency conditions are binding—physical closure, no free ontological lunch, and empirical adequacy at each stance level?

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps. Identify whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether maintaining strict physical closure while granting full ontological autonomy to intentional patterns creates an unavoidable tension, and whether that tension is a result about the stances themselves. Then say which single requirement eliminates the largest share of existing views. Finally, score briefly how Dennett's own pattern-realism, eliminativist physicalism, and a strong emergentist alternative fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases.
