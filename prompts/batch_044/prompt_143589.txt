I'm on the grant committee for the Holloway Cultural Fund — we back these weird cross-disciplinary projects that mix art with sociology, sometimes brilliant, usually a mess. We just recieved a proposal called "Ghost in the Spreadsheet" from a historian and a UX desinger who want to study whether modern "hustle culture" among remote tech workers is actually caused by leftover Protestant-ethic vibes, like Weber argued about capitalism, but updated for algorithmic management and productivity apps.

They'd build an interactive archive where people log their daily work rituals and map them onto Weber's categories — value-rational action versus instrumentally rational action — then use that data to argue culture is a real causal force, not just a story we tell after material conditions do all the work. Half the board thinks this confuses correlation with causation and that values can't drive behavior, only describe it; the other half thinks Weber's elective affinity idea still holds, just in a digital form. I need to draft our decision memo by friday: should we fund it, reject it, or approve with conditions and a clear timeline? Please take both sides seriously — especially the strong argument that explaining with culture is just post-hoc rationalization — and reach an actual recommendation, not a maybe.
