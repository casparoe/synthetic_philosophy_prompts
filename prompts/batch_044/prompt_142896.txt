I have recently taken on a leadership role in a flat-hierarchy research cooperative whose members hold strong egalitarian norms, and I am trying to see whether my broader political commitments can survive that context. Here is my current package:

(1) Meritocratic desert is real but bounded: people are entitled to rewards that track their productive contribution, corrected for brute luck and inherited advantage.
(2) The American Dream is best understood not as a literal promise of upward mobility but as an instrumental cultural technology that sustains high-risk innovation; its normative value derives mainly from its social productivity.
(3) Jantelagen norms — the Scandinavian Law of Jante — are ethically appropriate within intimate, high-trust communities, because visible status differentiation corrodes the reciprocity required for robust collective provision.
(4) At the societal level, tall poppy syndrome that systematically diminishes exceptional achievers is harmful: it suppresses innovation, encourages brain drain, and undermines the surplus that funds collective goods.
(5) The ideal posture for a high achiever is public acknowledgment of specific accomplishments paired with private refusal to claim intrinsic moral superiority over others.
(6) Both our aspirational admiration for success and our envious resentment of it are deeply rooted in evolutionary psychology, which means neither the American Dream narrative nor Jantelagen norms can be fully eliminated by rational argument alone.

Go through these commitments and audit whether they hang together. For each pair, say whether the tension is real or only apparent, and if real, rank it by severity, state what the cheapest revision would be, and note what that revision costs. Then identify the single change that would resolve the most inconsistencies at once. I am not looking for a bibliography or a history of these views — just an internal consistency check before I present this stance to the cooperative.
