Two people at my local rationalist meetup got stuck arguing about quantum imortality and quantum suicide after someone mentioned an old LessWrong post. Dave said if MWI is right then quantum suicide is actually rational: you pull the trigger, and in the branches where the gun misfires or your neurons randomly don't fire you just wake up confused but alive. Since you never experience being dead, the subjective expectation is always "huh, I'm still here," so the risk is basically zero. Nina said he's ignoring the Born rule: those survivor branches have astronomically low measure, so your first-person expectation should be weighted toward the high-amplitude branches where you're dead or dying, not the freak ones where you live.

Dave came back that subjective expectation doesn't work like that. You don't have an experience of being dead; you either have an experience or you don't exist. So weighting by amplitude is the wrong frame -- it's like refusing to buy a lottery ticket because the winning world has low measure, but if you win you still collect the payout. From the inside, all that matters is that there's at least one branch with a living you.

Nina said fine, but apply it to aging instead of a discrete gun. Even without suicide, your cells decay in almost every branch. The branches where random quantum tunneling reverses that decay technically exist, but they're suppressed by factors that make them essentially impossible. So you'd spend subjective millenia in slowly decaying bodies, with only infinitesimal flashes of health. Quantum imortality isn't eternal youth; it's eternal decrepitude with occasional lottery-ticket recoveries.

Dave replied that this assumes consciousness tracks every decayed brain state equally. More plausibly, severe decay breaks the high-level pattern that counts as "you," so you'd only maintain strong subjective continuity in the healthy branches. The decaying ones are like branches where the pattern never stabilized in the first place, so you wouldn't actually experience all that rot.

Nina said that's just smuggling in a non-physical selection mechanism. There's nothing in physics that says consciousness fades with neural decay; if consciousness is physical, then decayed-Dave exists in those branches with the same structural claim to being him. So Dave is really saying quantum imortality only works if you add an extra-physical filter that picks out healthy branches, which kills the whole physicalist argument.
