Goodman's new riddle of induction shows that for every observed regularity supporting a familiar projection—say, that emeralds are green—there exists an equally observation-compatible but incompatible projection framed with a gerrymandered predicate such as grue, leaving historical entrenchment rather than any observable world-structure as the sole discriminator. Please describe the most important implications of this result for how we ought to design inductive biases in small neural architectures that lack the massive pretraining corpora through which statistical entrenchment is usually absorbed implicitly. Specifically, address whether explicit structural priors can rationally substitute for linguistic entrenchment, and what Goodman's argument implies about the legitimacy of feature learning when the available predicate space is not already constrained by a broad statistical tradition.
