Write a dialogue between a defender of the Lucas-Penrose anti-mechanist argument and a critic who accepts Gödel's incompleteness theorems but rejects the claim that they show human minds are non-mechanical. The defender should advance a refined version focused on what she calls "reflective consistency": not merely avoiding contradiction, but actively recognizing and repairing potential inconsistencies in one's own reasoning framework, which she claims requires stepping outside any fixed formal system that encodes that framework. The critic should respond that such self-revision can be mechanized via hierarchical architectures—systems that ascend to meta-level rules or use dynamic belief-revision to correct contradictions—so that "stepping outside" is just moving to a higher formal module, not a non-mechanistic leap. The defender should counter that any such hierarchy either generates an infinite regress of meta-systems or implicitly relies on an unformalized human judgment about which revisions are legitimate, thereby smuggling back in the very non-mechanical element the architecture was meant to eliminate. The critic should then ask whether "reflective consistency" differs in any substantive way from the consistency conditions addressed by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, or whether it is merely consistency paired with an unwarranted assumption of human rational transparency. Both interlocutors should address each other's actual points, avoid strawman representations, and maintain equal philosophical competence throughout. End without either party conceding.
