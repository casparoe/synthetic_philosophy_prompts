You are a professional philosopher with expertise in metaphysics of time, the logic of completed infinite sequences, and institutional risk governance. Before you draft anything, please think carefully through the structure of a supertask—discrete state-changes executed in successively halved intervals that converge on a finite limit—the difference between a mathematical limit point and a physically actualized terminal condition, and how a contractual or liability framework should treat ontological indeterminacy when a practical decision is unavoidable. I serve on my university’s Permanent Installation and Risk Committee, and we must circulate a formal written opinion by Friday recommending a concrete course of action on a proposed kinetic sculpture called The Two-Minute Toggle. The work consists of a single luminous and acoustic module that switches between State Alpha and State Beta at intervals of one minute, then thirty seconds, then fifteen seconds, and so on, with the sequence designed to converge exactly at the two-minute mark. The artist’s funding agreement stipulates that if the installation achieves a determinable final configuration at that limit, it qualifies for the premium Completed Permanent Work tier, which carries full institutional insurance coverage and a permanent maintenance endowment; if the terminal state is logically indeterminate, it must be classified under the provisional tier with restricted coverage and mandatory annual re-inspection. Our engineering consultants confirm that no physical mechanism can literally perform infinitely many toggles, but they note that the conceptual identity of the piece is defined by that infinite schedule. The legal office has warned that certifying the premium tier requires the committee to affirm a specific terminal state—Alpha or Beta—yet several members argue that arbitrarily selecting one conflates administrative convenience with metaphysical fact and could expose the university to liability if the assignment is challenged. One faction proposes that we approve the premium tier but conventionally assign State Alpha as the official terminal condition, treating the limit point as determinable by stipulation because every observable toggle prior to the limit is unambiguous. The opposing faction insists that such stipulation is hollow, that the sequence’s structure genuinely leaves the outcome open in the same way classic lamp-style puzzles do, and that we should therefore approve only the provisional tier with explicit contractual conditions: either the artist must install a physical override that fixes the state at the two-minute mark, or the work must carry a disclaimer that its terminal configuration is undefined. Please draft the committee’s memo: it should recommend approval of the premium tier, rejection of the proposal, or approval with specific conditions and a clear implementation timeline; it should present reasoning that the researchers and legal office could actually respond to rather than abstract speculation; and it should include an honest, rigorous treatment of the strongest argument for the position you ultimately reject. Write it as a formal document with an actionable bottom line suitable for circulation and vote.
