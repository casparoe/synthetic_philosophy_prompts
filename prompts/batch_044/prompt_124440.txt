I'd like a consistency audit of my position on collective intentionality and joint action. Here are my commitments: (1) Bratman's conditions—meshing subplans, mutual responsiveness, and commitment to mutual support—are necessary and sufficient for joint action among small groups; (2) spontaneous collective behavior, such as a crowd collectively avoiding a hazard without prior coordination, can become joint action in the moment if participants achieve mutual responsiveness, even absent prior shared plans; (3) Gilbert is correct that plural subjects are ontologically real and arise only through explicit joint commitment (e.g., a contract or pact), and these are irreducible to aggregates of individual intentions; (4) Searle's we-intentions are reducible to individual intentions with collective content, specifically through what I call interlocked practical reasoning—each agent reasons partly about the other's reasoning—without requiring a primitive shared mental state; (5) in every case I classify as joint action, each participant must represent the activity under a first-person plural concept ('we are doing X'), but I deny that this representation requires either a Gilbert-style commitment or a Searlean primitive state.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be for each real tension. In particular, address whether (1) and (2) can be reconciled without diluting Bratman's planning conditions, whether (3) and (5) pull in opposite directions about when plural subjects exist, and whether (4) and (5) are compatible or whether my reduction of we-intentions undermines the collective representational content I require. Rank the tensions by severity and say, for each, which commitment should give.
