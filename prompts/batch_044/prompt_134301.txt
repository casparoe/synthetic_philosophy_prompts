I'm writing a position paper for my philosophy of mind seminar on Hofstadter's proposal that selfhood is best understood as a strange loop: a self-referential symbol that arises from feedback patterns in a physical substrate rather than pointing to a Cartesian ego. Before I defend or attack this view, I want a precise specification of what an adequate theory of this kind must actually deliver.

Write the desiderata: the complete list of requirements a theory treating selfhood as a strange loop or self-referential symbol must meet. For each requirement, include the motivating case or conceptual pressure—such as explaining first-person authority without a homunculus; distinguishing a genuine strange loop from a merely recursive algorithm; accommodating the apparent unity of consciousness across temporal discontinuities or neural disruption; handling pathological cases like dissociative conditions and split-brain phenomena without either dissolving the self into fragments or reintroducing a substantial core; and accounting for the normative dimensions of agency and moral responsibility without circular appeal to the very self being explained. Also include a requirement about the causal architecture required—whether it must involve organizational closure or top-down causation, or whether bottom-up statistical correlation suffices—and about whether the loop can be instantiated in non-biological systems without losing the phenomenon of selfhood.

Then flag which pairs of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, particularly any tension between full physical reduction and the preservation of normative personhood. Identify the single requirement that does the most damage to rival accounts, and distinguish non-negotiable criteria from camp-specific preferences (for instance, biological naturalism versus functionalist openness to artificial instantiation).

Finally, briefly assess how Hofstadter's own framework fares against your list—focusing on specific gaps or strengths the criteria reveal—contrasting it with one competitor framework only to the extent needed to show where that competitor fails on one of your criteria. Keep the weight on the specification and its internal conflicts rather than on any literature survey.
