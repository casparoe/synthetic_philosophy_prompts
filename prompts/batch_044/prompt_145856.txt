I'm planning a session for a first-year philosophy seminar on whether generational labels—Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z—denote real kinds or are merely statistical artifacts. The students have some familiarity with essentialism-versus-social-construction debates but no background in sociology or demography. I want to teach them a more precise claim: that assessing ontological status requires first separating cohort effects from life-stage effects and period effects, and that even if generational groupings fail the conditions for natural kinds they can still function as interactive kinds through self-fulfillment and institutional reinforcement. Plan the explanatory arc: which of the three effect-types must be in place before the ontological question can even be posed, the single carrying example to build the session around—specifically, the popular claim that Gen Z shows uniquely high anxiety because of smartphones, and how that claim collapses the three effects into one—then the misconceptions the students will arrive with or form in sequence, such as that unreality entails statistical uselessness, that marketing demographics prove reality, or that denying real kinds licenses stereotyping, with the point in the arc where each should be headed off. Include a brief check for understanding that distinguishes genuinely grasping the dependency structure from merely repeating that generations are "made up," and state openly what formal demographic modeling and what metaphysics of interactive kinds you are leaving out at this level and why. Separate what is settled in the relevant philosophy of social science and sociology from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present any contested view as the consensus.
