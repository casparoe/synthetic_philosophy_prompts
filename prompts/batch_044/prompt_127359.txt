I am a doctoral candidate in epistemology, currently writing a seminar chapter on how analytic philosophy of knowledge is absorbed into institutional audit culture. For an exercise in sustained philosophical satire, please compose a deadpan internal audit memorandum—approximately 600 words, in British English—from the fictional Office of Epistemic Compliance (OEC) to the philosophy faculty of an unnamed research university. The memo should report the findings of a “Gettier-proof knowledge-certification audit” conducted on the department’s graduate curriculum, and its target should be the bureaucratic fantasy that procedural safeguards can eliminate accidental truth of the kind Edmund Gettier identified: specifically, the failure of justified true belief when justification rests on a false lemma that nevertheless entails a true conclusion. Rather than asking for citations, bibliographies, or references to obscure texts, I want you to inject a fictional but conceptually precise regulatory apparatus—the “Accidental Truth Shielding Protocol” (ATSP) and its three-tier “Justification Entailment Matrix”—so that the satire turns on ideas a well-trained model can handle without external lookup, yet feels specific enough not to read like generic pre-training repetition.

The voice must be relentlessly bureaucratic: numbered clauses, risk-assessment categories rendered in prose, references to “non-compliant belief-formation pathways,” and a formal “remedial action plan.” The auditors should earnestly recommend replacing open seminar discussion with a “pre-verified lemma pipeline” that screens out false premises before they can entail true conclusions—thereby inadvertently reproducing the very Gettier structure it claims to prevent, for instance by certifying beliefs whose justification has been laundered through an administratively approved but epistemically hollow intermediate step. The humour should arise from the auditors’ failure to recognise that their procedural fix generates new Gettier cases, not from invented philosophical positions; a specialist should see that you understand the real problem (the non-accidentality condition, the entailment relation between false premises and true beliefs, and the difficulty of specifying what makes justification genuinely knowledge-conducive). Keep the irony deadpan and legible—no winking asides—so that a reader can tell the auditors are the object of satire without being told so explicitly. Please ensure the piece remains internally consistent: the memo should apply its own ATSP standards to its own recommendations and still fail to notice the circularity. Write in British English.
