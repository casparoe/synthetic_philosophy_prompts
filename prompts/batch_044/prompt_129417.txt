I host a philosophy podcast that often touches on logic and artificial intelligence, and over the past month the topic has become personally urgent rather than abstract: I have been working closely with a reasoning system that attempts to evaluate its own outputs for consistency, and I watched it enter a loop in which its confidence in its self-check was itself subject to the same check, with no clear termination condition. The experience made me realize that my own confidence in my judgment operates differently—there is something pre-reflective about it that never fully enters the space of reasons—yet I have never been able to articulate why that should matter structurally. Next week I am interviewing a philosopher of logic and mind, an unnamed defender of a view I will call practical-grounded self-trust, who argues that the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents is not a technical glitch to be engineered around but a positive revelation: any rational agent that tries to formalize its own reliability entirely within its representational system will necessarily fall into circularity, and therefore genuine self-trust must be anchored in non-propositional, embodied capacities—skillful coping, affective attunement, practical engagement—that do not enter the formal calculus of belief and proof. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having him restate this picture. I need at least one question on why the standard modal-logic formulation of Löb’s obstacle is not merely an artifact of overly strong formalization, with a follow-up keyed to the reply that human reasoning is just bounded rationality with smaller domains; at least one on how this view distinguishes itself from ordinary externalist reliabilism, anticipating the answer that reliabilism still treats reliability as a property that could in principle be represented, and pressing what practical difference that distinction makes for AI design; one on whether artificial agents that lack bodies or affective lives are therefore permanently barred from genuine self-trust or merely from one kind of it, with a follow-up that targets the likely concession that they can have functional analogues; and one on what evidence or argument could actually dislodge the view, since he will likely say that counterexamples from formal epistemology miss the point by staying inside the representational frame. For each, include the follow-up that makes his standard deflection harder to deploy. The audience is intellectually serious but not specialist in modal logic, so the questions should be precise without requiring the listener to follow derivations in real time.
