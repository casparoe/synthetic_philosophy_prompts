i'm putting together prompts about hidden defaults in language and need one that pushes Hofstadter's Person Paper idea into a weird, modern bureaucratic form. Could you write a deadpan satirical memo — maybe around 600 words — from a fictional university unit called the Department of Universal Reason?

The setup: they've issued an "Inclusivity Audit Report" claiming to have eliminated all gendered and cultural pronouns and references. The twist is that they've replaced every specific subject with an abstract "neutral agent" defined as having no body, no emotional dependencies, no cultural location, and no temporal constraints — essentially a disembodied Cartesian reasoner — and they present this abstraction as the true universal human, not as another hidden default. Use actual admin jargon (invent committees like the Subcommittee on Unmarked Discourse), stay totally straight-faced with no winking, and let the discomfort build by accumulation. The reader should gradually realize that "neutral" language hasn't removed bias, it's just buried the default deeper in the architechture of "pure reason" itself. Keep it in character as an internal memo, don't cite any real philosophers or texts, and don't slip into lecture mode — the satire should come from the document's own logic. By the end it should feel like the same kind of unease Hofstadter was going for, but applied to the fantasy that abstraction equals inclusion.
