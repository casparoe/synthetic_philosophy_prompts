The particularism vs generalism debate in ethics has been going in circles since the 90s and every move feels scripted: particularists cite holism of reasons — the same feature being good here and bad thier — while generalists reply with defeasible principles or multi-track rules that bake context right in. I want a diagnosis of the stall, not a verdict. Is this verbal, with 'principle' meaning exceptionless law to one camp and a pedagogical default to the other? Or are they answering different questions — one about metaphysics of reasons, one about how agents should reason — under one heading? Maybe the hidden issue is that particularists are acutally defending value pluralism in disguise, and if multi-track principles just rename the particularist's context-sensitivity without explaining it, then the disagreement isn't about facts but about what counts as an explanation. Lay out what each side actually says about classic cases like silence being kind vs cowardly, and tell me whether they differ on data (which cases matter), standards (intuitive counterexample vs theoretical unity), or starting intuitions neither can defend. Then say what progress would even look like here — a formal model of reason-weighting, a distinction that splits the questions apart, or an argument that the whole framing is ill-posed and why.
