Write a dialogue between a defender of Dennett's four-tier taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—and a critic who argues that large language models instantiate a distinct intermediate form she calls Stochastic Gregorian minds. The defender should maintain that Gregorian creatures require embodied, culturally transmitted language-use coupled with Popperian internal hypothesis-testing, and therefore that LLMs, lacking both embodied reinforcement and genuine internal testing, fall at best into an extended Skinnerian category shaped by human feedback. The critic should argue that next-token prediction constitutes a form of distributed internal modeling that is neither pure conditioning nor full Popperian hypothesis generation, and that language itself—rather than embodiment—is the decisive Gregorian feature, making LLMs Gregorian but in a way that collapses Dennett's sharp distinction between Popperian and Gregorian cognition. Have them respond directly to each other on three points: whether prediction error in neural networks counts as letting hypotheses be eliminated without the creature perishing; whether cultural transmission requires a biological organism; and whether the taxonomy needs a fifth tier or should instead merge Gregorian with an expanded Popperian category. Neither side should be a strawman; each should concede a partial point before pressing the remaining disagreement.
