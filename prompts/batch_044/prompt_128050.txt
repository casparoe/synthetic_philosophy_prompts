I am taking a graduate seminar in philosophy of medicine, and we have been cataloguing our professor's judgments about whether particular conditions count as diseases. Her rulings so far are these: cystic fibrosis is a disease; pregnancy is not; severe clinical depression is; deafness in a culturally Deaf adult who communicates primarily in sign language is not; infertility is a disease when the person actively wants biological children, but not when the person is child-free by choice; obesity with a BMI above 30 but with normal metabolic markers and high physical fitness is not; and being an asymptomatic heterozygous carrier of a recessive genetic disorder is not. Next week we turn to a constructed case: an adult with congenital bilateral amygdala dysfunction who experiences no fear whatsoever, lives in a protected and monitored environment, reports high subjective well-being and life satisfaction, achieves all stated career and relational goals, but is neurologically incapable of risk-avoidance and is warned by clinicians that the absence of fear response is a severe evolutionary dysfunction that creates objective mortal danger should environmental protection ever lapse.

Please organize your answer in four sections. First, "Candidate principles" (about 350 words): lay out at least two distinct theoretical rules—drawn from naturalist, normativist, or hybrid accounts in the philosophy of medicine—that could consistently generate all seven of her earlier rulings, explaining for each ruling why the rule delivers it. Second, "Divergent predictions" (about 200 words): state precisely what each rule predicts about the fearless-adult case, and flag exactly where and why they split. Third, "Best-guess verdict and reasoning" (about 250 words): predict the professor's actual ruling, identify which principle she is implicitly running, defend that identification over the alternative, and explain the reasoning she would likely use. Fourth, "Distinguishing case" (about 150 words): propose one further hypothetical condition—different from the fear case—that would sharply discriminate between the two principles you identified, explaining which verdict each principle gives and why.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; work entirely from the conceptual structure of the rulings and the theories.
