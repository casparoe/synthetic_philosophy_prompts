Exam Question 3: Explain the Jain doctrine of anekāntavāda (many-sidedness) and its logical formulation in saptabhaṅgī (seven-fold predication). Apply it to the ontological status of the jīva, which Jain texts describe as eternal in substance (dravya) yet continuously modified in its modes (paryāya). Then assess whether anekāntavāda collapses into epistemic relativism, and distinguish the Jain position from both Buddhist śūnyatā (as articulated in Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi) and the Nyāya account of contradiction (virodha).

Student Answer:

Anekāntavāda teaches that reality possesses infinitely many attributes, so any single judgment captures only one facet. Its logical expression is saptabhaṅgī, which qualifies every assertion with syāt (perhaps, or conditionally). The seven modes are: (1) syād asti—perhaps it is; (2) syān nāsti—perhaps it is not; (3) syād asti-nāsti—perhaps it is and is not; (4) syād avaktavya—perhaps it is indescribable; (5) syād asti-avaktavya—perhaps it is and is indescribable; (6) syān nāsti-avaktavya—perhaps it is not and is indescribable; (7) syād asti-nāsti-avaktavya—perhaps it is, is not, and is indescribable together.

Applied to the jīva, this yields that the soul is eternal from the dravya-naya (substance-standpoint) but also changing from the paryāya-naya (mode-standpoint). Because these descriptions belong to different nayas, they do not genuinely conflict; the law of non-contradiction is therefore restricted to operate within a single standpoint rather than across them. This avoids the one-sided extremes (ekāntavāda) of eternalism and annihilationism.

Against Buddhist śūnyatā, the Jain view differs because Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi denies svabhāva to all entities, whereas Jainism affirms that both substance and mode have real, if partial, being. Compared to Nyāya, which treats virodha as an absolute failure of coherence, Jain logic allows apparently opposite predicates to coexist by assigning them to separate perspectives.

Finally, anekāntavāda does entail epistemic relativism: since every naya is equally valid and none can claim absolute priority, truth is distributed across standpoints rather than anchored in a single objective description. This is not skepticism—each viewpoint delivers genuine partial knowledge—but it does mean that no metaphysical claim can be advanced without the syāt qualification, rendering all assertions perspectivally indexed.
