Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on inside-view reasoning versus outside-view epistemic modesty. Camp One, the Inside-View Mechanists, holds that in domains marked by rapid structural change, sparse historical analogues, and accessible causal mechanisms, first-person model-building should dominate reference-class forecasting, provided the reasoner applies explicit, locally justified calibration adjustments. Camp Two, the Outside-View Modestists, holds that first-person reasoning is systematically overconfident across virtually all domains, that calibration adjustments suffer from the same bias they aim to correct, and that reference-class base rates should dominate unless there is independently verified, domain-specific track-record evidence.

Structure the deliverable in three parts.

Part I: Agreed Claims. Provide a numbered list of propositions both sides can sign. These should cover: (a) the formal decomposability of a forecast into a mechanistic component and a reference-class component; (b) the empirical regularity that uncalibrated human forecasters tend toward overconfidence in long-range, low-feedback predictions; (c) the logical consistency of Bayesian updating upon learning one's membership in a reference class; (d) the existence of domains where direct historical analogues are genuinely scarce; and (e) the conceptual coherence of asking whether epistemic modesty applies recursively to its own application. Do not invoke specific empirical studies, named datasets, or bibliographic authorities; keep the agreements conceptual and structural.

Part II: Disagreements Table. Present a table with four columns: Issue; Inside-View Mechanist Formulation (worded so that camp accepts its own side); Outside-View Modestist Formulation (worded so that camp accepts its own side); Status (substantive, partly verbal, or dependent on an empirical crux). The issues must include at least:
- Whether a reasoner can apply calibration adjustments to her own current argument without circularity, given that the assessment of her calibration is itself an inside-view judgment.
- Whether the scarcity of historical analogues in a domain undermines reference-class forecasting or merely forces a shift to a broader, more appropriate reference class.
- The normative weight of "epistemic modesty" when applied recursively: if modesty demands downgrading inside-view confidence, must it also demand downgrading the confidence in that very demand?

For each substantive disagreement, identify the crux: the precise sub-question whose resolution would move at least one side. At least one crux must turn on the self-reference problem of second-order calibration injected above.

Part III: Cruxes and Predictive Tests. For each substantive crux, provide:
- A concrete, designed scenario or logical structure—not a citation or reading list—that both sides agree bears on the crux. The scenario should feature a researcher who has built a novel causal model predicting an outcome in a domain with few direct precedents, and who simultaneously holds data about the historical failure rate of similarly novel, structurally analogous models constructed by peers in comparable reference classes.
- The explicit assessment or prediction each camp makes about that scenario.
- A brief statement of what evidence or argument both sides agree would genuinely update them, together with each side's prediction about whether the other side would in fact update upon receiving it.

Throughout, avoid requests for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or appeals to named authorities. Phrase residual disagreements in wording each camp would accept for its own position, and if any portion of the dispute is merely verbal, identify the precise formulation that generates the appearance of conflict and propose a neutral reformulation that dissolves it. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for philosophy of science or epistemology, ensuring that every claim can be evaluated by a reader without external search.
