Write a six-month interdisciplinary research proposal (approximately 3,000 words, for a philosophy-and-social-theory grant) on whether honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures can be ranked as morally superior or inferior without smuggling in the normative vocabulary of one of them. State the precise question: existing comparative work either treats the three as purely descriptive sociological types—making normative evaluation impossible—or assesses them through dignity-culture universalism, which begs the question by treating individual autonomy and emotional restraint as baseline norms. Propose a specific conceptual method: construct a minimal, cross-culturally valid criterion of recognition failure, defined as a systematic mismatch between a culture's own internal norms for appropriate grievance and its actual mechanisms for registering harm, then apply it to three hard constructed cases—intergenerational honor debts where no living agent can be addressed, dignity-culture demands for specific emotional performances (such as required displays of remorse or dignity-preserving protest) that may exclude those incapable of performing them, and victimhood-culture claims of diffuse structural harm with no identifiable perpetrator. Explain why this conceptual approach is preferable to empirical cross-cultural surveys, which measure social outcomes rather than internal normative coherence, and to standard moral theory, which imports external principles. Specify what success looks like—either a demonstrable asymmetry in recognition-failure vulnerability across the three cultures, or a rigorous proof that the failures are structurally incommensurable, with direct implications for how institutions should design grievance procedures. Specify honest failure conditions for the project itself: if the minimal criterion collapses into dignity-culture individualism, or if the case analyses yield perfect symmetry that renders comparison moot. Give a realistic scope assessment for six months: adequate for the conceptual architecture, the failure-criterion refinement, and the three case analyses; explicitly inadequate for empirical validation or full institutional design; and list the concrete deliverables (a working paper and a two-page policy brief on hybrid grievance mechanisms). Do not include a bibliography or citation list; refer to positions and arguments without attributing them to specific texts.
