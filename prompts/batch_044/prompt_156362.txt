At a rationalist meetup last tuesday two people got stuck arguing about prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection, and I genuinely can't tell who's winning. I want a move-by-move adjudication, not just vibes.

Maya said prioritarianism doesn't actually have the leveling-down problem at all — it's about weighting benefits more for worse-off people, not about equality for its own sake. If you could take 10 utils from someone rich and literally destroy them just to make the distribution flatter, prioritarianism says don't do it, since nobody is actually better off. So the classic "leveling down" scenario is a strawman.

Leo replied that in the real world there's always leakage — taxes, deadweight loss, admin costs — so he sketched a "leaky redistribution" case: take 10 from the well-off, 3 gets destroyed, 7 reaches the poor. If the poor are near some welfare threshold, prioritarianism might endorse this, but if they're already comfortably above it, the weighted gain might not justify the hit to the rich. Maya said that's just an efficiency concern, not the leveling-down objection proper.

Leo pushed back: the hidden issue is that prioritarianism's weighting curve can systematically justify heavy extraction from the middle class to deliver tiny weighted gains at the bottom, producing a flattened distribution that looks like leveling even if the mechanism is technically "help the worst off." The downward pressure on anyone above the bottom is a predictable feature, not a bug you can wave away. Maya said he's conflating "lower total welfare" with "equality as an independent goal" — prioritarianism never values equality per se, so calling it leveling is misleading.

Then Leo brought up "threshold prioritarianism" — only apply the extra weight below some line — and said it just pushes the arbitrariness to where you draw the line. Maya said at least it stops the middle-class squeeze.

Can you go through their exchange and rate each move? Who's right about what, and what's the core confusion? Also, is there something both are missing — maybe about ex-ante vs ex-post evaluation, or how this interacts with longtermist cause prioritization where the "worse off" might be future people?
