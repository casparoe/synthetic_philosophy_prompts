My roommate summarized Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique for me like this: they argued that lots of traits aren't adaptations but spandrels—structural byproducts of other evolutionary changes, like the triangular spaces between arches. He said this means adaptationist explanations are usually just-so stories, since you can always cook up a function for any spandrel after seeing it. He also claimed that spandrels can never get co-opted for a new adaptive use, so when evolutionary psychologists say language is an adaptation they're automaticly wrong—language is probably just a spandrel of bigger brains. Something feels off about jumping from "some traits are spandrels" to "functional-looking traits are probably spandrels", and also about saying spandrels stay useless forever. Is this a fair summary? Which parts are accurate to what Gould and Lewontin actually argued, where are the subtle misrepresenations—especially the bit about co-option being impossible—and does his dismissal of evolutionary psychology actually follow even if we grant the spandrels point?
