After a colleague was recently hospitalised following sustained, institutionally tolerated overtime that bordered on karoshi conditions, I have found myself unable to treat leisure merely as a policy preference. I want to understand whether our modern protections — the French thirty-five-hour-week framework, Japanese anti-karoshi statutes, and similar measures — actually rest on a philosophical foundation, or whether they are merely contingent, secular-humanist patches applied over a normative tradition that never recognised leisure as a right.

Please give me your actual credence — a number between 0 and 1 — in the following claim: that Weber's Protestant work ethic, reconstructed with philosophical rigour, contains no internal normative resources capable of generating an intrinsic right to leisure; that this absence explains why leisure-protective institutions depend entirely on values extrinsic to that ethic; and that this extrinsic dependence is the principal reason such institutions remain culturally fragile and vulnerable to economic erosion.

Decompose your reasoning into these four terms, each with a rough probability and one line of justification:

(a) That Weber's original formulation genuinely treats leisure solely as recuperative instrumentality or moral idleness, such that no charitable reconstruction can derive an intrinsic leisure-right from within the ethic's premises.

(b) That the institutionalisation of the thirty-five-hour week represents a normative rupture with, rather than an administrative secular extension of, the work-ethic tradition.

(c) That the persistence of karoshi-level overwork in economies historically shaped by this ethic is better explained by the absence of an internal leisure-right than by purely institutional or economic failure.

(d) That, assuming (a) through (c), the observed cultural fragility of modern leisure protections is genuinely caused by this normative gap rather than by independent political-economic dynamics.

Combine these into an overall credence. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, and specify what conceptual or historical evidence would most move your overall number.

I am not asking for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the reasoning exposed, with its weakest joint marked.
