He used this specifically to defend a scenario he called a "time-pocket"—a localized region of spacetime where the thermodynamic arrow reverses relative to the surrounding universe without violating global conservation laws—arguing that because he could vividly imagine such a pocket, cosmological models could not rule it out as metaphysically impossible. I feel that this one sentence is being made to carry too much weight, and that it might hide several very different claims, from an obvious point about logical consistency to an ambitious doctrine about imagination overriding physics. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing? For each reading, please clarify what kind of conceivability and what kind of possibility are involved, whether the claim is trivial, defensible, or overreaching, and—crucially—which version actually supports my classmate's inference from conceiving a backward-flowing pocket to its metaphysical possibility despite scientific objections. I want a disambiguated menu I can take back to class rather than a single interpretation.
