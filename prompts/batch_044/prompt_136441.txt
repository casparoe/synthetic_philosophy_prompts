I am attempting to move beyond the usual exchange of intuitions in the moral realism versus anti-realism debate and instead construct a rigorous specification sheet for any adequate metaethical theory. Write the complete desiderata: the phenomena it must explain — first-order moral disagreement, the phenomenological authority of obligation, and the appearance of moral progress over time — the distinctions it must preserve, particularly between possessing a genuine moral reason and merely having a motive that happens to align with morality, and the consistency conditions with established results in moral psychology, especially cross-cultural variation in normative judgment without collapsing morality into descriptive anthropology. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration, flag which pairs you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable — I am particularly concerned with whether preserving the full authority of moral claims can coexist with a thoroughgoing acknowledgment of deep, persistent disagreement — and distinguish non-negotiable conditions from preferences specific to particular camps.

The list must also incorporate a constraint that I do not believe is named in the standard literature, which I will call normative inertia: the tendency of agents to maintain specific moral commitments even after they have explicitly acknowledged that the original evidential or rational grounds for those commitments have been undermined. The theory must account for this inertia without treating it as either straightforward irrationality or as hidden rationality, and you should indicate whether meeting this desideratum naturally favors a realist, an anti-realist, or an orthogonal orientation. Finally, briefly evaluate error theory, a constructivist account, and a robust non-naturalist realist position against the full specification — but keep the weight on the criteria themselves, since my aim is to apply this list to a hybrid framework I am formulating rather than to adjudicate among existing schools.
