Then evaluate the argument that universalist epistemologies are self-undermining because dominant social positions systematically fail the ascription requirement for subaltern knowledge-claims. student answer:

Ascription universality is the principle that if S knows p, S must be able to attribute knowledge of p to every rational agent in a relevantly similar epistemic position. The dominance constraint says that when social power is uneven, dominant groups can satisfy this for their own experiences but not for those of marginalized groups. This comes straight from Sandra Harding's standpoint epistemology, and it demonstrates that universalism is incoherent.

The reasoning goes like this: universalism demands that anyone epistemically like me could know p if I know p. But dominant groups consistently refuse to grant that oppressed people know their own oppression. So the universalist has to either deny that oppressed groups have knowledge, or admit that universality doesn't apply across power differences. Either way universalism collapses.
