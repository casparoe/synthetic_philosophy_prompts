I've been asked to deliver the keynote address at the dedication of a new Center for Capital Justice Studies, an occasion that will bring together constitutional scholars, capital defense practitioners, and the family members of individuals who spent years on death row before being exonerated. The audience is fluent in the standard arguments—retribution, deterrence, and the statistical risk of wrongful execution—but I do not want to rehearse them. Instead, I want the speech structured around a specific conceptual framework I am calling the "irreversibility gap": the distinction between a punishment that can be corrected and compensated (wrongful imprisonment) and one that creates a moral debt the state can never discharge (execution of an innocent person). This idea must do real structural work, not serve as decoration.

For the scholars present, the framework should show that the risk of executing the innocent is not an external contingency that retributive or deterrence theories can simply accommodate through better procedure. Retributive justice demands a kind of epistemic finality—that the punishment be deserved with a certainty matching its severity—but capital sentencing pretends to that finality while operating within evidentiary practices that cannot guarantee it; meanwhile, deterrence logic treats human life as fungible and calculable, a premise that collapses once we recognize that an erroneous execution is not a miscalculation to be adjusted for, but an unrepairable rupture. For the family members in the room, the same framework should allow me to acknowledge the particular, non-statistical horror of wrongful execution without reducing their experience to data, and without offering the false comfort that procedural reform can close the gap—because on this view, the gap is structural, not contingent. The address should run about twelve minutes, be formal but not academic in tone, and must avoid claiming certainty where the argument denies it, or promising repair where repair is impossible. Please write it.
