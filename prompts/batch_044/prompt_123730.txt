Write a satirical internal memorandum, approximately 600 words, in the deadpan voice of a corporate wellness consultancy, addressed to clients who have adopted "Schlickian Play Protocols" to maximize existential meaning. The memo should propose a quarterly evaluation framework that quantifies pure play (Spiel) by tracking its absence of external Zweck (purpose), measures employee "meaning-per-hour," and offers tiered certification levels for "authentic non-instrumental activity." The satire must depend on an accurate understanding of Moritz Schlick's actual view—namely, that life acquires meaning only in activities done entirely for their own sake, where no further goal is sought, and that this meaning is destroyed the moment the activity is treated as a means to an end (including the end of "being meaningful"). The parody should therefore show the consultancy consistently trying to instrumentalize, measure, and optimize precisely that which Schlick argued must remain free of instrumentalization. Include at least one invented case study—e.g., a senior analyst who achieves "Level-III Play" during spreadsheet modeling—and one deadpan footnote explaining why failure to achieve certification is itself a form of play. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific editions of Schlick's works; write as if addressing an audience already familiar with the distinction between Spiel and Zweck-driven Arbeit, but do not rely on obscure secondary literature. The piece should be readable to someone who knows only that Schlick was a logical empiricist who wrote about meaning, and it should make a specialist recognize that the author understands why Schlick's view resists being turned into a productivity system.
