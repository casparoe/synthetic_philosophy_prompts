I serve on the competency-review board of a surgical training program, and after a recent incident in which a resident passed every written examination yet failed to adapt when a simulated procedure presented an atypical tissue plane, I have become personally convinced that our assessments confuse propositional mastery with genuine operative skill. I need a practical evaluation procedure—something my committee can actually apply in the simulation lab—that operationalizes the philosophical distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that without requiring evaluators to make speculative claims about internal mental states. The procedure should take the form of an ordered sequence of observational tests, with explicit criteria for what counts as passing or failing each stage, designated points where the evaluation must halt because the question exceeds behavioral evidence and becomes a matter of values or institutional risk tolerance, and clear instructions for how to weight verbal articulation against manual adaptation when the two come apart. Crucially, it must incorporate what I have been calling perturbation-response mapping: the idea that knowing-how reveals itself not in error-free execution but in the topology of recovery—how quickly, with what economy of motion, and toward what functional endpoint a practitioner corrects after an introduced anomaly—rather than in the absence of deviation itself. Please apply the procedure to two worked cases: a laparoscopic dissection in which sudden bleeding obscures the standard triangular landmarks near the cystic duct, requiring the resident to navigate by tactile and visual affordances rather than named anatomical structures; and a suturing task under time constraint where the needle angle is deliberately non-standard, testing whether the hands reconfigure grip and rotation without waiting for explicit problem-solving. Deliver the entire response as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold formatting, or tables, suitable for inclusion in a formal committee memorandum.
