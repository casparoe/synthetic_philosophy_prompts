Write an essay evaluating whether an institutional decision procedure should ever permit the aggregation of individually imperceptible harms to outweigh the prevention of a single, severe rights-violating harm. Ground your analysis in the philosophical comparison between torture and the aggregation of minute discomforts—sometimes illustrated with hypothetical dust-speck scenarios—but do not treat that comparison as a mere intuition pump. Instead, construct and analyze an intermediate case of your own design: a municipal regulatory agency with a fixed budget must choose between Program A, which eliminates a documented, high-probability risk of one instance of torture in its jurisdiction, and Program B, which reduces ambient noise exposure for two million residents by a margin that falls below any individual's perceptual threshold but that, summed across the population, yields a large aggregate disutility. Weigh the arguments that rights function as non-aggregative trumps, that sub-threshold harms lack the moral status required for aggregation, and that institutional legitimacy depends on treating severe harms as categorically distinct from distributed nuisances. Reach and defend a specific verdict about what the agency ought to choose, and justify whether your reasoning generalizes to the broader torture-versus-aggregation debate or whether the institutional context introduces a morally relevant distinction.
