Defend the view that the central ethical obstacle to sustained space colonization is not ecological harm to off-world environments, nor distributive injustice among Earth-bound populations, but the emergence of jurisdictional vacuums in interplanetary governance—situations in which decision-makers exercise coercive authority over settlements with whom they share no real-time deliberative link because of physical communication latency. Argue specifically that any legitimate normative framework for multi-planetary settlement must incorporate a latency-sensitive principle of democratic accountability: the exercise of sovereignty over a colony should be conditional on the physical possibility of synchronous, reciprocal deliberation between the governed population and the governing institution. Develop this claim by specifying at least two concrete institutional mechanisms—one that would satisfy the principle and one that would violate it—under conditions of substantial signal delay, such as that between Earth and Mars. Then address the objection that latency is merely an engineering problem to be solved through autonomous delegation and algorithmic administration, explaining why the substitution of automated decision-making for live deliberation fails to restore the normative conditions required for legitimate political rule. Conclude by examining whether this principle entails that fully autonomous off-world settlements must eventually claim full political independence, or whether they can remain in a permanently subordinate but legitimate relationship to Earth-based authority without violating the conditions of democratic accountability you have defended.
