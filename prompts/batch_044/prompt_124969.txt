Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the force of Arrow's impossibility theorem through philosophical reasoning rather than political voting. Each student receives a private card assigning them a strict ranking of three competing explanations for a single mental phenomenon — for instance, why people experience regret for choices they now endorse — evaluated along two separate dimensions: explanatory depth and empirical tractability. The cards are distributed so that the collective profile contains both a Condorcet cycle and one student whose pairwise choices match every majority outcome. The group must choose and apply an aggregation rule of their own design (e.g. Borda count, pairwise majority, or weighted scoring) to produce a single collective ranking. Structure the rules and a non-monetary incentive — say, collective recognition if they agree, withdrawn if they deadlock — so that they run into the failure themselves before it is named. Provide a precise timeline, the materials needed, and a debrief that maps each breakdown onto Arrow's conditions, particularly independence of irrelevant alternatives and non-dictatorship. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong on the first run (e.g. students merging the two evaluation dimensions or assuming a compromise must exist) and be explicit about which feature of the theorem — likely unrestricted domain over all logically possible orderings — the activity necessarily simplifies and how you will supply that in discussion. Please answer in British English.
