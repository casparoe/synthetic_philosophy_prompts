Write the full desiderata that an adequate philosophical theory of biological function must satisfy, with particular attention to cases where etiological (selected-effect) and causal-role accounts diverge. The specification should cover: (1) standard functional attributions (e.g., hearts pumping blood, kidneys filtering waste) and the distinctions they require between function, accident, and malfunction; (2) exaptations in which a trait's current contribution to fitness or system stability differs markedly from the selection history that established it; (3) dysfunction judgments where an organ or gene continues to perform its characteristic causal role yet undermines overall organismal viability or reproductive success; (4) function attribution in microbiome communities involving horizontally transferred genes, where evolutionary lineage is diffuse but metabolic contribution is specific and stable; and (5) synthetic or engineered biological constructs—such as minimal cells with designed metabolic pathways—that lack deep evolutionary histories yet exhibit robust, selectable causal contributions. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one theoretical camp, and identify any pair of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, especially those pitting historical etiology against present causal contribution. Finally, briefly indicate how a pure selected-effect account, a pure causal-role account, and a pluralist or hybrid view fare against the list, but keep the weight of your answer on the criteria themselves and on the conflicts among them rather than on an exhaustive comparative scorecard.
