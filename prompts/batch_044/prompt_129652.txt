I'm taking a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of science, and after a session on properties and powers a classmate offered me what he called a clean synthesis of the fink-and-mask debate. He summarized it like this: dispositions are fundamentally categorical properties that only behave dispositionally when nothing interferes; a fink is an interfering factor that removes the disposition right at the moment it would manifest, while a mask is one that blocks the manifestation even though the disposition itself remains fully present. Because both cases show that the categorical base and the behavioral output can come apart, he argued that the only defensible analysis is a conditional one restricted to normal conditions—where normal just means in the absence of finks and masks. From this he concluded that talk of an instrument being reliable is metaphysically on par with talk of a broken thermometer having a temperature-measuring disposition: there is no objective fact of the matter about reliability, only a counterfactual claim about what the instrument would do if it were not masked, and therefore the distinction between a genuinely well-calibrated device and a systematically distorted one is ultimately a matter of framing rather than metaphysical fact.

I suspect he is smuggling in an anti-realist conclusion about scientific instrumentation that does not actually follow from the metaphysics of dispositions, and I am also unsure whether he is describing the standard conditional analysis correctly or whether he is conflating it with the anti-conditionalist responses I half-remember being discussed. Could you audit this summary point by point? Please separate what is accurate about how philosophers distinguish finks from masks and about the role of categorical bases, what misrepresents the standard analyses or the objections raised against them, and whether his conclusion about instrument reliability survives once the metaphysics is corrected. I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list—just whether a careful defender of dispositional realism, or of a properly restricted conditional analysis, would accept his framing, and where exactly the reasoning slips from a modest metaphysical claim into the stronger skeptical claim he draws.
