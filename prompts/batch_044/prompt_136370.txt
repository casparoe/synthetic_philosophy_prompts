I cover science and ideas, and lately Ive been stuck on this historiography stall: microhistorians in the Ginzburg vein say elite/state history erases ordinary lives by treating people as data points inside big structures, while macrohistorians reply that zooming into one village or trial misses the trade networks, climate cycles, and bureaucratic capacity that actually drive change. Theyve been swapping these arguments since the 80s, pulling from different archieves, and neither side moves an inch. Frankly it's exausting.

My hunch is the deadlock isnt really about missing facts. Maybe its a hidden disagreement over scalar ontology—whether causation at the human scale is even the same kind of thing as causation at the state scale, a framework ecologists use but historians rarely name out loud. Or maybe theyre just answering totally different questions—one about lived meaning, one about structural mechanism—under the same heading. Diagnose it for me: is this verbal, substantive, a bedrock intuition about what explanation has to do, or an ill-posed question? Use what each side actually argues. And tell me what progress could look like here—some formal model of historical scale, a new archival practice, or should we just accept that agreement is impossible?
