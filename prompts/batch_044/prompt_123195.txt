A colleague summarized a line of research for me this way: "The WEIRD psychology literature shows that Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic subjects are psychological outliers: they reason more analytically and weight harm and fairness far more heavily than loyalty, authority, or sanctity, whereas most of the world reasons more holistically and distributes weight more evenly across all five moral foundations. Moral Foundations Theory treats these capacities as innate, universal modules—like language faculties—that culture merely parameterizes, not creates. From this empirical picture, moral objectivity is impossible: if morality were genuinely objective, these parameter settings would converge on a single correct distribution, but they do not. The only coherent view is a parameterized relativism in which each culture's weightings are internally consistent but incommensurable with those of other cultures, with no neutral standpoint from which to adjudicate them." I suspect this moves too quickly from data to metaphysics, but I am not sure exactly where the slips are. Could you audit the summary? Please separate what is accurate about the WEIRD findings and about Moral Foundations Theory from what misrepresents either; in particular, check whether the theory actually claims that culture only parameterizes universal modules, whether descriptive variation in weights undermines objectivity in the way claimed, and whether the notion of incommensurable parameter settings is a defensible inference or a conflation. Finally, evaluate whether the conclusion—parameterized relativism—actually follows if one accepts all the empirical premises, or whether a moral objectivist could consistently accept the cross-cultural variation and still maintain that some weightings are more correct than others.
