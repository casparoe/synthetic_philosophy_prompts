I've been working through the position advanced in Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander's Surfaces and Essences (2013)—that analogy-making is not a peripheral cognitive skill but the fundamental substrate of all cognition, with concepts functioning essentially as compressed analogies and perception operating through high-level analogical mapping rather than direct feature extraction. On this view, what we call categories are sustained analogical clusters kept in motion by continuous conceptual slippage, and even mathematical or logical reasoning depends on analogical bridges between formal structures and intuitive physical ones. I want to take this claim seriously and ask what would actually count against it: not just generic skepticism, but specific observations, arguments, or formal results that would damage or defeat it.

Specifically, I'd like a ranked map of its exposure. Which empirical findings—say, developmental evidence of non-analogical concept acquisition in very young children, or neuroscience identifying domain-specific modular architectures that resist analogical redescription—would be fatal versus merely awkward? Are there conceptual or formal results, perhaps from cognitive architecture or the philosophy of mathematics, showing that some reasoning proceeds by syntactic transformation or statistical pattern-matching without analogical mediation, and do these undermine the universal claim or only delimit its scope? There's also the risk of insulation: if every mental operation can be redescribed post hoc as an analogy, has the thesis qualified itself into unfalsifiability, and is that a fatal flaw for an empirically aspiring theory? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish which ones a committed defender of the analogy-as-core view would themselves concede as damaging—perhaps the modularity problem, or the difficulty of explaining systematic misanalogy without circularity—versus which they would dismiss as mischaracterizing the subtlety of analogical mapping, and assess honestly whether the position, as currently articulated, risks anything concrete or has been protected into untestability.
