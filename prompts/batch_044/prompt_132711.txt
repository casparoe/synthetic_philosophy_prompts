Student answer:
Hart's harm principle says law should only restrict liberty to prevent harm to others, not to enforce private morality. He rejects Devlin because Devlin wants to criminalize private immoral acts to protect social cohesion, but Hart argues there's no empirical proof that private vice makes society collapse. Devlin's disintegration thesis holds that shared morality acts like glue holding society together; if deep shared values are violated, even in private, the social fabric tears. Applying this, letting people suspend legal personhood for art weakens the inalienability of legal status and risks tearing that fabric.

As for competence drift, this means that when extreme consent gets accepted, people start thinking consent is easier to obtain in normal contexts. So allowing status suspension might make doctors or courts assume patients can consent to riskier procedures more easily. This undermines the autonomy defense because autonomy needs real competence, and drift confuses extreme artistic choice with ordinary decision-making. Therefore Hart's harm principle might actually support banning this, since the indirect harm from drift threatens other people's autonomy in medical settings.
