I'm organizing a working group on embedded agency and I'm trying to decide which conceptual bottlenecks deserve sustained attention versus which ones are merely decorative taxonomy. The framing I have in mind treats embeddedness not as a boundary failure but as the default condition: the agent's reasoning necessarily includes variables—its own hardware state, the causal pathways it can trigger, the epistemic position of anyone attempting to verify its outputs—that classical decision theory treats as external. Given that premise, which open lines of work actually reshape how we should think about rationality, self-modification, and normative commitment?

Specifically, I want a dependency-aware prioritization among three clusters for directing limited effort—say, a small research group over eighteen months. First, whether embedded agents can maintain coherent diachronic preferences when self-modification is causally available continuously rather than as a discrete, observable choice; this seems to determine whether long-term planning is even coherent in embedded settings. Second, how to formalize decision criteria that do not tacitly presuppose an external archimedean point from which an agent's reasoning can be evaluated—if this fails, normative claims about embedded agents may collapse into observer-relative description. Third, whether ecological psychology or enactive cognition can supply genuinely normative constraints rather than merely descriptive regularities, which might unlock practical design criteria but perhaps only if the first two are resolved. Which should come first, which are upstream of which, and which should be deprioritized because solving them would change little downstream? Defend the ordering by stating your criteria—whether unlocking further debates, tractability with current formal tools, or practical applicability to system design—and specify the trade-offs explicitly. I need a defended agenda with ranked priorities, not a survey or a reading list.
