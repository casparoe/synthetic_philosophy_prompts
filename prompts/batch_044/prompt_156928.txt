I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on historiographical method, and after our session on the Italian microhistorical school and the British Marxist tradition of history from below, I've become convinced that there's an unacknowledged contradiction running through both movements. Carlo Ginzburg and Giovanni Levi, in their well-known studies of local belief and kinship structures, appear to reject macro-structural explanation as an epistemic violence that dissolves the agency of ordinary actors into abstract forces. Meanwhile, E.P. Thompson's approach treats the absence of working-class voices in state archives not merely as a gap in evidence but as a structural feature that demands narrative redress. If both premises are taken together, they seem to entail what my study group has started calling scalar consistency: the principle that the evidentiary scale of one's sources must match the explanatory scale of one's conclusions, and that using micro-archival traces to account for macro-institutional change violates the ethical and methodological core of both approaches.

My roommate, who studies political science, thinks I'm overstating the case and that both traditions are united only in their opposition to elitist state-centered history, not in any shared ontology of scale. But I keep returning to a specific puzzle: if we accept that parish-level records reveal truths that state-level records systematically suppress, and if we also accept that macro-causal frameworks distort the lived experience captured in those parish records, then wouldn't any historian who uses local notarial documents to explain, say, the restructuring of a national tax code in the eighteenth century be guilty of a kind of methodological betrayal? They'd be deploying micro-evidence in service of macro-explanation—the very move both traditions condemn. Is this tension real, or am I importing a false coherence onto two traditions that never intended to form a unified epistemology? And if the tension is real, why do so many respected scholars combine granular archival work with large-scale institutional narratives without apparent theoretical discomfort?
