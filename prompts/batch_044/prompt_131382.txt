Having retired from a career in experimental physics and only recently begun serious study of the philosophy of history, I find myself troubled by the conventional tripartite division into the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution. The question of whether these labels track genuine discontinuities or are merely retrospective constructions strikes me as structurally analogous to debates in natural science about whether categories such as species or phase transitions denote mind-independent boundaries or useful conventions. But I wish to avoid the familiar analogies. Give me an example of a conceptual issue arising specifically at the boundary between the Middle Ages and the Renaissance—one that initially appears to parallel a subtle problem of classification or demarcation in physics or biology, yet upon closer inspection actually subverts that scientific analogy, showing why historical periodisation cannot be resolved by the same criteria used in natural science. I want the example to be precise and genuinely illustrative, not a restatement of the general problem. Please answer in British English, and do not provide citations, references, or a reading list; I am able only to evaluate the conceptual argument itself.
