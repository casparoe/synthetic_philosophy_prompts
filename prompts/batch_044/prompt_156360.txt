I want the spec first. In particular, I'm drawn to Strawson's framing that reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation, and the like—are the primitive data of moral responsibility, not symptoms of some deeper metaphysical desert. So write the desiderata for a theory that takes those attitudes seriously: what must it deliver?

Give me the full requirements list: the cases it must cover (including responsibility for harms mediated by fully transparent algorithmic decision systems, and for outcomes of collective action where no individual intended the result); the distinctions it must preserve (e.g., between being a proper object of reactive attitudes and being merely causally implicated, between reactive and objective attitudes); the empirical and conceptual constraints it cannot violate (psychology of moral emotion, the status of determinism if true, etc.); and whether any subset of these looks jointly unsatisfiable—especially if treating reactive attitudes as foundational conflicts with requiring determinism-compatibility. For each, say why it belongs.

Then briefly score Strawson's own view, a standard compatibilist desert-based account, and a hard determinist eliminationist view against your list.

One caveat: if my framing is confused—for example, if reactive attitudes can't actually do the normative work I'm assigning them, or if the algorithm-transparency case doesn't genuinely pressure the framework—say so plainly and don't humore the exercise.
