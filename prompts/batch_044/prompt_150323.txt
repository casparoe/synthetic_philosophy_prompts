I'm stuck on a passage from Kraepelin's old Lehrbuch (around 1899, I'm paraphrasing so don't worry about exact wording). He describes dementia praecox as showing an early emotional bluntting and a steady downhill coarse, while manic-depressive illness comes in episodes with full recovery between them. The diagnosis, he says, only becomes clear after tracking the patient for years to see which trajectroy they follow.

I'm trying to read this through Hacking's looping effects, but with a kind of reverse twist: once someone gets sorted into the 'progressive, bad-outcome' bucket, they might hide symptoms or drop out of care altogether, so their absence looks like further withdrawal and confirms the label. So the classification doesn't amplify behavior, it produces a silence that gets read as deterioration.

Does Kraepelin's framework leave any room for that? Or does he treat the symptoms as purely endogenous, meaning the patient's awareness of the diagnosis can't alter the biological course? There's also a circularity I can't shake: he uses long-term outcome to tell the two categories apart, but then uses the category itself to predict that same outcome. Is that just tautology, or is it a looping effect where the label shapes the very trajectory it's supposed to merely describe? Help me understand whether the passage's reliance on 'course over time' makes it vulnerable to this kind of self-confirmation, or whether it escapes because the doctor is just observing nature unfold.
