I'm writing from my philosophy of science seminar, where we've been examining demarcation criteria and the structure of scientific research programs. In our discussion section this week, a classmate raised a point about Lakatos's methodology that I haven't been able to resolve. We were analyzing how scientists protect a theoretical hard core by revising auxiliary hypotheses in the protective belt—for instance, adjusting local distance-ladder calibrations or dark-energy parameterizations to accommodate discrepancies in Hubble constant measurements—without altering the core lambda-CDM framework itself. My classmate argued that this is structurally identical to the ad hoc modifications Popper condemns, like adding epicycles to save geocentrism, because in both cases the core claim is shielded from direct empirical confrontation. I objected that Lakatos permits such adjustments only when they generate novel predictions, but she replied that this progressive problem shift condition is merely a sociological description of successful science, not a logical feature that restores falsifiability to the hard core itself.

So my question is this: if the hard core is, by methodological design, never directly exposed to refutation—since any anomaly can be absorbed by tweaking the protective belt—doesn't Lakatos's framework institutionalize the very immunization strategies that Popper's demarcation criterion was meant to exclude? Should we therefore conclude that research programs operating under Lakatosian rules fail Popper's falsifiability test by design, or is there a principled reason why protecting the hard core does not count as pseudoscientific evasion? I realize I'm probably conflating levels of analysis here, but I can't see exactly where the error lies, and I want to understand whether Lakatos is describing rational science or accidentally describing a system that Popper would classify as unfalsifiable.
