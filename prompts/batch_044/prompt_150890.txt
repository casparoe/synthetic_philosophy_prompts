Here is my opening move, delivered as a philosophy professor who has been pressing this tension with a graduate seminar. You maintain that consciousness is singular and its plural appearance illusory, yet quantum entanglement establishes joint probabilities between physical systems without requiring, or even implying, a single underlying observer—there is correlation without one mind measuring both terms—so entanglement undermines classical individualism without thereby vindicating your unitary consciousness. A second, clinical difficulty reinforces the problem: focal brain lesions—damage restricted to Broca's area, to hippocampal formation, or to specific cortical regions—produce precisely localized deficits in language, memory, or affect, rather than globally dimming one universal awareness. If consciousness were genuinely unitary in the Vedantic sense, should brain injury not diminish that single awareness uniformly, rather than carving out discrete functional capacities? Defend your monism without retreating to poetic metaphor; show specifically how the clinical localization of mental function can be reconciled with the claim that, in reality, there is only one perceiving subject.
