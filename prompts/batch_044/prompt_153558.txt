Write a take-home exam prompt for an upper-level epistemology seminar on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia — but skip the usual "define akrasia" setup. Present this case: a climate modeller recieves an internal audit showing she has accumulated "recursive calibration debt" — three successive rounds where she dismissed higher-order warnings about her own weigthing of recent temperature anomalies, each dismissal backed by fresh first-order arguments that later turned out flawed. She now faces a fourth round and still feels her current model is right. Ask students to evaluate whether rationality requires her to downgrade confidence now, using level-splitting vs level-bridging, and to say exactly where the epistemic akrasia is located — in holding the belief, in the refusal to integrate, or in the meta-belief about her own reliability? Then provide a model answer and marking scheme (40 marks, 1500 words max): 10 marks for correct use of split/bridge, 10 for locating the akrasia precisely, 10 for handling the recursive debt idea, 10 for clarity and for not treating the audit as mere first-order counter-evidence. In the scheme list specific mistakes that lose marks: conflating this with practical weakness of will (-4), treating the audit as ordinary data (-5), ignoring the recursive structure (-4). Keep it casual, dont ask for bibliographies or reading lists, and make sure a student cant answer by just summarising standard textbook summaries.
