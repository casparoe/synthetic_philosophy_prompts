There's this pattern in economic history where people ask why Britain and not China like it's a single switch that got flipped differently. They treat both paths as modular machines you can swap parts in and out of, which feels like counterfactual reasoning but applied to whole societies. What view of historical causation does that framing presuppose—does it need societies to have cross-world identity, or that culture is detachable from context? And is it consistent with the same historians saying development is path-dependant, since modularity seems to deny that? I'm not after a reading list, just whether there's a hidden philosophy of history in the question, and what breaks if we drop the modularity assumption.
