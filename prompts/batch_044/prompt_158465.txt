I sit on my university's Committee on Ethical Foundations of Discipline, and we must finalize our opinion on Case 2024-07 before next Thursday's vote. A tenured researcher was found to have fabricated data across a multi-year grant; she does not dispute the findings but appeals on metaphysical grounds, invoking Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. Because she did not create herself—neither her psychological constitution, her risk preferences, nor the structural incentives that shaped her choices—she claims she cannot be the causa sui that true moral responsibility requires. From this she infers that any sanction treating her as "deserving" punishment is conceptually incoherent, and that only forward-looking, non-retributive measures are legitimate. In response, a faculty working group has proposed adopting a "Causa Sui Audit Protocol" (CSAP): an amendment to our disciplinary code that would bar any sanction whose justification implicitly assumes ultimate self-authorship, permitting only "causally transparent interventions" (e.g., mandatory restructuring of lab oversight, suspension of grant authority, publication of correction notices) accompanied by explicit documentation that the measure is pragmatic rather than blame-based. Half the committee regards this as a category mistake—institutional accountability need not solve metaphysics—while the other half argues our current code smuggles in a libertarian conception of agency that Strawson's infinite-regress objection undermines.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate: it should recommend one concrete course of action—adopt CSAP university-wide, pilot it only for research-integrity cases with an 18-month sunset clause, reject the amendment but mandate "causal-transparency statements" for all sanctions, or another precisely specified option—with a clear implementation time frame. The memo must engage the Basic Argument seriously: explain whether the impossibility of being causa sui actually transfers into institutional design, distinguish pragmatic accountability from retributive blame without simply asserting the distinction, and treat with intellectual honesty the strongest consideration on the losing side. Do not restate employment-at-will policy, cite legal definitions of death or dismissal, or offer a bibliography.
