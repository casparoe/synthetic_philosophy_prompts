I sit on the funding advisory board for our institutes Center for Artificial Self-Modeling, and we've got a proposal thats splitting us right down the middle. The PI wants to build a neural architecture that embeds a Gödel-style self-referential loop inside the transformer itself—specifically, a formal sentence within the model's internal belief-state space that asserts its own unprovability, tied to a recursive self-monitoring module that updates weights based on that fixed point. They lean hard on Hofstadter's GEB analogy: just as Gödel's formula lets arithmetic talk about itself, this loop should let the system achieve genuine functional self-awareness rather than just simulating it. Half the board thinks this is a straight category error—Gödel's result is about syntactic incompleteness in formal systems, not about consciousness or subjective experience, and Hofstadter was being poetic, not engineering a blueprint. The other half thinks the analogy is structurally deep and that if we reject this we're being risk-averse about the exact kind of high-variance bet that could actually matter. I need a memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It should have a clear bottom line the board can vote on, honest reasoning for it, and a serious treatment of the best argument on the losing side—without just listing philosophers or asking for reading lists. Also, please dont treat the analogy as obviously wrong or obviously right; work through whether formal self-reference can actually do the work the PI claims.
