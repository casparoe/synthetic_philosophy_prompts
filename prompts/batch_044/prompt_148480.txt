For grading practice: please evaluate the following student response to a decision-theory exam question. The prompt was: Define expected-value fanaticism, and assess whether it can be resisted through risk-sensitive decision rules, through practical limits on accessing astronomically large payoffs, or through a justifiability constraint that counts only outcomes reachable by actions one could defend to affected parties—specifically, does that constraint block the fanatical conclusion without importing an arbitrary utility ceiling?

Student answer (paraphrased for grading):

Expected-value fanaticism holds that if one option offers any nonzero probability of an outcome with unbounded value, it dominates every option with a bounded best case, no matter how small the probability. This yields puzzles like Pascal's mugging, where an implausibly large promised payoff outweighs a near-certain modest gain. Risk-sensitive rules can escape the conclusion only by applying a nonlinear transformation that effectively caps utility, which is ad hoc unless independently motivated. The student then proposes a justifiability constraint: only outcomes producible by actions defensible within a cooperative scheme should enter the expectation. They argue this blocks fanaticism, since no cooperative scheme can sustain actions tied to infinite stakes, thereby excluding the relevant worlds without capping value itself. They also claim that, once adopted, this constraint eliminates ex post regret over forgoing the fanatical option, which demonstrates its rational necessity.

Please assign a specific grade (e.g., A–F or numerical) and provide point-by-point comments that justify it. Do not indicate whether you agree with the answer's substantive conclusion; your deliverable should be an external grader's assessment of logical rigor, conceptual clarity, and whether each step actually follows. In particular, check whether the student conflates filtering permissible actions with filtering worlds in the expectation, and whether ex post emotional responses are being used to justify an ex ante decision rule. Do not include a bibliography or reference list; assess the reasoning on its own terms.
