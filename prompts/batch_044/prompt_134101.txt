I recently received results from a clinical genetic screening indicating that I carry a structural variant in a non-coding intergenic region that appears to modulate lipid metabolism under caloric restriction, though it produces no transcript of its own. My physician described its effect as regulatory but emphasized that its evolutionary history is obscure: it may have spread through drift or as part of a larger haplotype rather than through direct positive selection for metabolic control. This personal context led me back to a philosophy of biology seminar I am taking, where we have been examining the debate between etiological accounts, which define function by evolutionary selection history, and causal-role accounts, which define it by present contribution to a system's capacity.

I have drafted an essay arguing that both frameworks fail to capture an intermediate category I want to call "stabilized contribution." On my view, a trait can possess a causal role that is systematically preserved not because the role itself was selected, but because it is structurally coupled to another trait that was. In the case of my variant, the metabolic influence might be a stabilized contribution: it is not merely an incidental biochemical correlation, since it is reliably reproduced and homeostatically integrated, yet it lacks the direct selection history required by the etiological account. At the same time, calling it a full causal-role function risks over-inclusion, because the region also binds transcription factors in ways that seem metabolically inert. I propose that we should reserve "function" proper for selected-for capacities, while treating stabilized contributions as a distinct, derivative status that explains why certain non-coding elements are maintained despite lacking an independent evolutionary rationale.

Could you comment on whether this conceptual distinction is coherent, and whether my essay risks circularity? I worry that by appealing to "structural coupling" with selected traits, I may be implicitly reintroducing etiological criteria into what is meant to be a causal-role analysis. I am also interested in whether the argument can be sustained at a purely conceptual level—without leaning on the specific molecular details of my variant—or whether it necessarily depends on empirical claims about non-coding regulatory architecture that I am not qualified to defend. Any suggestions for sharpening the boundary between a stabilized contribution and a mere evolutionary spandrel would be especially welcome.
