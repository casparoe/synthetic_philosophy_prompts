Having retired from experimental physics and recently begun studying epistemology in earnest, I keep returning to a calibration problem I knew intimately in the laboratory: two research groups can share every raw photon count from an interferometric array and still maintain incompatible parameter estimates if their priors about the instrument’s own noise-floor structure are not commensurable. Translating this into philosophy, I want your credence—precisely decomposed—on the following claim, which I will call the calibration-divergence thesis: in domains of complete empirical underdetermination (for example, rival interpretations of quantum mechanics, or competing ontologies of abstract objects), the failure of rational agents to converge on agreement is not primarily due to hidden private information or bounded computation, but to a structurally irreducible divergence in how they calibrate the evidential weight of theoretical reasoning itself, such that agreement-theoretic conditions are violated at the meta-level rather than at the object level.

Please provide a specific numerical credence in this thesis, decomposed into at least three terms with a rough probability and one line of justification for each: first, the applicability of agreement-theoretic results to philosophical belief revision as distinct from empirical prediction; second, the existence of genuinely framework-dependent meta-priors concerning the reliability of theoretical reasoning that cannot be reduced to hidden-information asymmetries; third, the irreducibility of any such divergence—that is, the absence of a non-question-begging arbitration procedure capable of reconciling the calibrations. Show how these terms combine to yield your overall figure, identify which term carries the greatest share of your uncertainty, and specify what kind of conceptual argument or non-empirical distinction—not a citation, bibliography, or reference list—would most shift your number and in which direction. I ask as someone who has watched instruments disagree despite perfect data-sharing, and I want to know whether philosophy possesses an analogous phenomenon that is substantive rather than merely apparent.
