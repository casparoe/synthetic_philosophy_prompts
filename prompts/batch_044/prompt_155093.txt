"The heterogeneity problem asks how hedonism can claim pleasure is the sole intrinsic good when pleasures seem utterly different—tasting chocolate, solving a proof, and feeling schadenfreude share no obvious feature. This was first raised by Jeremy Bentham, who worried that if pleasures have nothing in common, we cannot rank them with the felicific calculus. The standard hedonist reply, defended by J.S. Therefore, the heterogeneity objection fails, since once we identify this shared phenomenal property, pleasure is a unified kind after all."
