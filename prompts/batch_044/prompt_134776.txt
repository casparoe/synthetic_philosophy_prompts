I'm on my university's student affairs policy committee and we've got this proposal in front of us for Thursday's vote. The library wants to roll out a "smart assignment" system for study spaces after 8pm: instead of picking silent or collaborative zones yourself, the default puts you in a collaborative pod based on your registered course load and past check-in data, and you can switch but only after clicking through a two-question "reflection" screen about your study goals. The wellness office is backing it because pilot data recieved positive feedback on stress levels, but a student rep is arguing this isn't just a nudge—it's "architectural paternalism" that treats autonomy like a consumer preference rather than self-authorship, especially since the reflection screen adds friction to opting out. Please draft the committee memo recommending whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a concrete rollout timeline. It needs to take the student rep's worry seriously—not just restate that people can still switch—and it should address whether that reflection step actually respects autonomy or just disguises manipulation. Keep it actionable: what exactly should the library change, and by when? No bibliography needed, just reasoning we can vote on.
