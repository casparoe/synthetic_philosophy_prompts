hey, i'm a retired chemist who got into philosophy lately after retirement. i've been reading about dispostions and these weird cases called finks and masks — like a wire that's conductive but has a hidden switch that cuts it right when current hits? anyway, at my old company the materials lab ran a two-card system: every compound gets a "property card" listing things like solubility or brittleness based on standard tests, and then a separate "interference log" where techs write down any conditions — temperature spikes, contaminents, magnetic fields — that stopped the property from showing up. the card is treated as the real deal, and the log is just noise that masked it. but sometimes the log is twice as long as the card, and in our factory the stuff basically never behaves like the card says. so what view of properties does this practice actually presuppose? does it only hang together if you think dispositions are real powers that can be hidden, or could a straight Humean about laws run the same lab? and if we dropped that implicit theory and went with a flat categorical view — a property is just what actually happens, full stop — what would have to change in how we write the cards and train the technitians? also, is the practice even consistent, since we call something brittle even when our logs show it never breaks in real operating conditions? sorry for the ramble, hope that makes sense.
