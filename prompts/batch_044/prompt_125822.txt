Write a two-page research proposal for a philosophy dissertation chapter (one semester, approximately 8,000 words) investigating whether Hegel's master-slave dialectic implicitly assumes a dyadic structure of recognition—direct, face-to-face acknowledgment between two consciousnesses—that collapses once a third, mediating term such as an institution, a shared practice, or a material artifact is introduced. State the precise question: does the dialectic's resolution through mutual recognition depend structurally on there being exactly two parties, or can it survive triadic mediation without losing its normative force? Explain why standard interpretations, which treat recognition as purely intersubjective and binary, leave this structural dependency unexamined. Outline your method: first, reconstruct the logical steps of the dialectic using only its internal dynamics, without importing later system-level commitments; second, systematically vary the model by inserting a mediating third term and determine whether the asymmetry that drives the dialectic dissolves, relocates, or intensifies; third, evaluate whether a triadic model yields a different conception of freedom or merely redescribes the same outcome. Specify what a successful result looks like—for instance, demonstrating that mutual recognition is unstable without mediation, or that mediation necessarily undermines the immediacy required for self-consciousness—and state clearly what would count as failure, such as the dyadic-triadic distinction turning out to be purely descriptive with no consequence for the dialectic's normative conclusion. Provide an honest scope assessment: acknowledge that restricting the analysis to the dialectic's internal logic, rather than engaging the full Philosophy of Right or an exhaustive survey of secondary debates, is necessary to finish within one semester, and identify the precise boundary where the project risks expanding beyond that limit. Write the proposal in American English, and do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for specific source verification; refer to positions and arguments generally, noting that citations will be added separately.
