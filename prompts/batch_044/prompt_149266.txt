Exam question: Consider the classic problem of material constitution using the following scenario. At time t1, a sculptor forms a statue, call it Statue S, from a single lump of clay, call it Lump L. At t2, S is destroyed and L is reshaped into a ceramic bowl. At t3, the material of the bowl is melted and recast into an object with exactly the same macroscopic shape as S, using all and only the original atoms of L arranged in an identical spatial configuration. During this process, a preservation team had extracted a digital blueprint of S's formal properties at t1; this blueprint guided the recasting at t3, though the algorithm initially introduced a microscopic asymmetry in the left earlobe that was subsequently corrected by hand.

Question: Is the object present at t3 identical to the original Statue S? In your answer, address both the material-continuity criterion and the formal-structural criterion, and explain whether the intermediate existence of the bowl, together with the blueprint-guided reconstruction and its manual correction, affects either form of continuity.

Student's answer:

"The object at t3 is not identical to S. Material constitution requires spatiotemporal continuity of matter arranged statue-wise, not merely the presence of the same atoms elsewhere. Once L was molded into a bowl at t2, the statue-wise arrangement was destroyed, so S ceased to exist. At t3 the atoms are rearranged statue-wise again, but this produces a new object that is constituted by the same matter as L without being S itself. The digital blueprint does not preserve identity because it only stored formal properties, not the particular historical trajectory of L; the fact that a hand-corrected deviation was necessary shows that the original formal cause was not continuously present, but had to be restored through external intervention. This aligns with the standard view that a statue is essentially its particular clay arranged in a particular shape—when that shape is lost, the statue is lost, even if the clay survives in a different form. Therefore, the object at t3 is a perfect replica, not the original statue."
