I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science and formal epistemology, and I am trying to correct my own intuitions against more precise accounts. In a recent discussion group, a fellow participant summarized what she presented as the philosophical resolution of heuristic independence arguments—specifically, the "presumption of independence" that practicing scientists apply when treating separate measurements, instruments, or causal pathways as uncorrelated in the absence of explicit interaction terms. According to her account, this presumption began as a pragmatic rule of thumb in early experimental design, but has now been fully superseded by formal frameworks: in contemporary causal inference, independence is not assumed but derived from the structural absence of directed paths in a graph, and the old heuristic survives only as a pedagogical mnemonic for those who have not yet mastered the relevant formalism. She further argued that, once a causal model is fixed, judgments about independence become purely computational outputs with no residual normative or epistemic weight; consequently, any remaining disagreement about independence is merely disagreement about model specification, which formal learning algorithms can in principle resolve given sufficient data. I suspect there is an important elision here—namely, that selecting which variables enter the model, and which potential edges are excluded, still requires background scientific judgment that the formalism itself does not supply—but I am not yet able to say exactly where the summary overreaches. Is this a fair characterization? Please distinguish what accurately describes the relationship between heuristic independence assumptions and formal causal modeling, what counts as a defensible simplification for non-specialists, and what misrepresents the actual role of prior knowledge and expert judgment in applying these frameworks. In particular, does her conclusion—that the heuristic presumption has been rendered epistemically obsolete—actually follow from the formal achievements she describes?
