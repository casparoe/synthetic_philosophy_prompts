I am a high school student writing an independent essay on the possibility that conscious awareness reconstructs its own temporal sequence. I have been studying a visual phenomenon in which two small lights of different colors flash in rapid succession at two separate points in space; observers consistently report seeing one continuous object traveling between the points and shifting color at the midpoint, even though no physical stimulus ever occupied that intermediate location or displayed that intermediate shade. I have encountered arguments that draw an analogy between this kind of perceptual filling-in and the way people report the experience of making voluntary decisions—specifically, experiments in which neural activity associated with a movement begins measurably before the subject reports any conscious intention to move.

Within these discussions, I keep encountering what seems to be a fundamental but often unnamed structural distinction between two ways that an earlier neural event might relate to a later conscious report. On the first model, there is a genuine initial state—perhaps an unconscious motor command—that is subsequently altered or overwritten in consciousness, so that the conscious report is a revised edition of something that actually existed. On the second model, there is never any conscious-level predecessor at all; rather, consciousness constructs a complete narrative of prior intention, with the false temporal origin already woven into the fabric of the experience, comparable not to editing a document after it has been written but to forging a document that carries an incorrect date in its original printing.

My question has three parts. First, is there an established philosophical term for this specific distinction—between the retrospective alteration of an authentic experiential record and the ex nihilo generation of a record that lacks any authentic predecessor? Second, I have begun to wonder about a third logical possibility that I rarely see addressed: a parallel-track or merged-file model in which the original unconscious initiation and the backdated conscious narrative are both retained, but consciousness accesses only a blended composite that does not flag which elements derive from which temporal layer. Does this three-way framework—revision, fabrication, and hidden parallel preservation—correspond to any recognized taxonomy in the philosophy of consciousness, particularly in analyses that link perceptual phenomena like the traveling-color illusion to the temporal structure of voluntary action?

Third, if the first two models are indeed the standard ones, how exactly are they typically labeled, what are the boundary conditions that separate them, and has the third model been explicitly considered and rejected on empirical or conceptual grounds? I am not asking for a bibliography; I am asking whether the carving I have described matches an existing conceptual map, and if not, what the nearest established distinctions are and where my proposed third case fits or fails to fit among them.
