Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of philosophical positions on Buridan's ass — that is, on whether rational choice can occur in the absence of any preference ordering. Begin at the root with the question of whether the two options can be genuinely equiprobable and indistinguishable in every respect relevant to the agent's ends. From there, branch sequentially on: (a) whether an agent may legitimately break the tie by arbitrary selection without forfeiting rationality; (b) whether the introduction of an irrelevant but detectable asymmetry — say, a non-nutritive chromatic difference perceptible only on the left bale — dissolves the paradox or merely displaces it; and (c) whether imposing a hard deadline, such that inaction becomes itself a choice with fatal consequences, introduces a meta-preference or leaves the logical structure unchanged. Each terminal node should carry the name of a position (for example: strict preference-dependence; rational arbitrary-selection; libertarian volition; hidden-variable determinism; deflationary impossibility) together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Note any logical combinations that remain unoccupied — such as accepting genuine symmetry while denying that urgency alters the analysis — and briefly diagnose them as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, and specify what further diagnostic question would be required to distinguish them. The instrument should be usable by someone who knows the classic paradox but has not studied its secondary literature; do not request citations, bibliographies, or named scholars.
