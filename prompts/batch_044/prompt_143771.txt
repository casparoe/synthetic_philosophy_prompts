I have come to accept a reconstructed form of occasionalism inspired by Al-Ghazālī, but modified by an essentialist constraint. The view holds that no created being possesses any intrinsic causal power: every effect is immediately produced by God alone. The regularities we observe—fire accompanied by burning, medicine followed by healing—are not grounded in the natures of the created things, but in God’s consistent volition according to a stable pattern (the habitual course). I add, however, that this pattern is not arbitrary. It is constrained by the logical structure of the essences that God conceives in the divine intellect: fire is not essentially linked to burning, but the pairing of fire-with-burning belongs to the essential order of the world as God has ordained it. Consequently, scientific laws describe not the efficacy of secondary causes, but the invariant structure of divine action relative to those essences. I want an honest map of what would genuinely defeat this position—not merely the familiar objection that science presupposes real causal powers in nature, but specific conceptual results, empirical findings, or internal inconsistencies that would count against it. Would a complete neuroscientific reduction of mental states to brain states remove the need for direct divine action at each mental-physical junction, or could the occasionalist absorb that reduction? Do quantum-mechanical indeterminacies strengthen the view by undermining deterministic causal chains, or weaken it by making divine action appear redundant? Is there a fatal incoherence in claiming that essences constrain divine volition without themselves being causally efficacious? I want the potential defeaters ranked from fatal (the view collapses into contradiction or is rendered impossible) to merely awkward (it requires an ad hoc qualification that saves the core but damages elegance). I also want you to distinguish what a committed defender of this reconstructed occasionalism would concede as genuinely damaging, versus what they would dismiss as begging the question against theistic metaphysics. Finally, assess whether the position is well-insulated from refutation, and whether that insulation should be regarded as a philosophical virtue or as a methodological vice.
