Write an essay defending a verdict on whether geometric rationality — maximizing expected log utility, as formalized by the Kelly criterion — should be treated as the fundamental norm for rational choice under risk. Center the argument on a specific tension: the criterion’s elegance relies on a well-defined sample space and substitutable outcomes, yet serious philosophical decision problems involve both non-substitutable values (where losses violate categorical constraints rather than scaling smoothly) and ontological ambiguity (where the agent is uncertain not about outcomes within a space, but about which formal state-space description is correct — for instance, branching versus linear personal identity, or incommensurable interpretations of quantum mechanics). Assess whether geometric rationality can be preserved by extending it to hierarchical or meta-level betting strategies, or whether these extensions collapse its uniqueness and show it to be only a special case of broader expected-utility reasoning. Weigh both sides thoroughly, then reach and justify a definite conclusion — either that geometric rationality remains the core norm with disciplined supplements, or that the tension demonstrates its inadequacy as a general theory of rational choice.
