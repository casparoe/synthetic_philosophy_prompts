I'm on the arbitration panel for this open-source foundation I volunteer with, and we've got a messy case about broken commitments. Last year one of our senior maintainers said in a livestream that he was personally committed to keeping the legacy API stable for the next two release cycles so people could migrate safely. A bunch of small dev shops built tools around that assumption. Then he ghosted the project, didn't recieve any PR responses, and the API got broken in v3. Now the board is split: some want to formally censure him and bar him from maintainership for life, arguing he made a promise and broke it; others say it was just aspirational talk with no contract, and since open source is voluntary and anonymous there's no real binding force, so sanctioning is authoritarian overreach.

My co-chair brought up this idea that promises don't just create obligations out of thin air, but they lock in a normative expectation that other people can plan around - so breaking one doesn't just hurt folks, it actually corrupts the planning framework itself. But another member pushed back that without a practice of mutual recognition, like you have in anonymous volunteer labor, promises are more like weather forecasts than binding commitments. I don't know the jargon for any of this, but I need a decision memo for Thursday's meeting that takes both sides seriously and recommends something concrete - approve the sanction with conditions, reject it, or some middle path with a clear timeline. Definately don't include a bibliography or citations since I can't verify them before the meeting, and please make sure the reasoning honestly addresses the strongest point from whichever side ends up losing.
