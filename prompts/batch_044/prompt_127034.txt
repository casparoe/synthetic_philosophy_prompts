For my graduate seminar in value theory, I want students to conduct a comparative assessment of five responses to the repugnant conclusion in population axiology, structured not as an expository survey but as a tiered evaluation across two dimensions. The five conceptual schemes are: (i) a lexical-threshold variant of total utilitarianism that assigns zero or negative value to lives falling below a specified welfare level; (ii) an average-view revision incorporating a continuity constraint that requires smooth value transitions across different population sizes; (iii) critical-level utilitarianism modified by a dignity floor, such that welfare increments beneath that floor do not aggregate into social value; (iv) a person-affecting approach that weights the value of potential lives by relational proximity to existing moral agents; and (v) an asymmetrical welfare gradient, according to which welfare gains above a baseline are exponentially more valuable than equivalent gains below it, thereby inverting the monotonicity that generates the paradox. Please assign each strategy a tier—Outstanding, Adequate, Problematic, or Untenable—separately for logical consistency with the axioms that produce the repugnant conclusion, and separately for fidelity to pre-theoretic intuitions about whether large, flourishing populations are genuinely preferable to smaller populations of barely tolerable lives. For each assignment, supply exactly one sentence identifying the specific conceptual tension or virtue that determines the placement. Conduct this evaluation without bibliographic citation or appeal to specific published formulations; treat each position as a self-contained theoretical construct.
