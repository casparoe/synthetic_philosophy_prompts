I'm a senior analyst on an independent municipal oversight board that reviews proposed ordinances for liberty impacts before they reach the council floor. I see about a dozen proposals a month—zoning amendments, licensing rules, public-health mandates, subsidy conditions—and I have to sign off on a one-page assessment within ninety minutes. The board is legally literate but not philosophically trained, so my report has to translate conceptual distinctions into a reusable decision protocol that a non-specialist can audit. I want a procedure that classifies each ordinance's impact across negative liberty (absence of direct or structurally equivalent interference), positive liberty (the effective capacity for self-authorship, not merely the absence of chains), and republican liberty (non-domination, specifically the absence of arbitrary power even when that power lies dormant). To keep this from being a recitation of Berlin's two concepts plus Pettit's non-domination gloss, I need you to inject a practical distinction that standard treatments gloss over: for republican liberty, include a counterfactual vulnerability audit that asks whether affected parties could be subjected to arbitrary interference by a will that need not track their interests, even if no interference is currently occurring; and for positive liberty, include an adaptive-preference filter that checks whether the capacity being expanded is one the affected agents would endorse under conditions of non-manipulation, rather than counting every resource transfer as liberty-enhancing. The procedure should be a word-flowchart with ordered tests, explicit flag points where judgment cannot be eliminated, and a clear notation for when the three dimensions conflict rather than align. Apply it to three worked cases: a mandatory financial-literacy course tied to welfare eligibility; a discretionary noise-abatement ordinance enforced by a single unelected official with unreviewable authority; and a publicly funded apprenticeship program that requires participants to relocate to a region with few alternative employers. Include at least one case where the procedure yields a verdict I will find counterintuitive—specifically, where republican liberty is severely compromised but negative liberty looks untouched, or vice versa—and state plainly what the protocol cannot settle, such as how to weight incommensurable liberty losses when they trade off. Finally, say what specific argument or empirical finding about domination or adaptive preference would cause you to revise the procedure's structure, and identify the step in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to misapplication by a rushed practitioner.
