You are a senior methodology officer and philosopher of mind advising a multi-site cognitive neuroscience consortium that runs repeated consciousness studies—roughly forty sessions per quarter—staffed mainly by graduate students and technicians with uneven philosophical training. You need a reusable, operational protocol that translates heterophenomenology into a practical workflow: a step-by-step decision procedure, with explicit nodes and time constraints, that treats every subject’s verbal and behavioral report about conscious experience as a text within that subject’s heterophenomenological world, not as direct, authoritative access to private mental states. The procedure must remain ontologically neutral among competing theoretical frameworks (for instance, global workspace, higher-order, and integrated-information accounts), and it must be executable within fifteen minutes per case by a researcher with only a master’s-level background, with the principal investigator required to sign off solely at nodes where judgment genuinely cannot be eliminated.

Your deliverable should contain: (1) a preliminary screening checklist that decides whether a session’s data qualify for heterophenomenological treatment rather than for standard introspective or purely behavioral protocols; (2) an ordered sequence of no more than five interpretive tests—expressed as questions a non-expert can apply—to classify any given report as descriptive within the heterophenomenological world, confabulatory, theoretically loaded, or ambiguous; (3) clearly marked JUDGMENT REQUIRED points at every stage where the theory underdetermines the decision, specifying exactly what the PI must evaluate and sign; (4) explicit rules for conflicts between verbal report and concurrent behavioral or neurophysiological measures, distinguishing cases where discrepancy enriches the heterophenomenological narrative, cases where it invalidates the report, and cases where the data must be suspended; (5) worked applications to three concrete scenarios, including one split-brain patient who reports a unified visual field despite demonstrable contralateral neglect, one subject operating under adversarial incentivization who reports phenomenal-overflow experiences that align neatly with a rival theoretical framework, and one unwelcome case—an articulate subject describing meticulously detailed “micro-phenomenal shifts” during binocular rivalry whose reports your procedure must downgrade or reclassify despite their surface sophistication; and (6) a concise statement of what the protocol cannot do, especially regarding ontological commitment to consciousness and legitimate cross-subject comparison. Before constructing the procedure, begin with a brief conceptual map that distinguishes heterophenomenological neutrality from both eliminativism about consciousness and naive first-person authority, identifying which philosophical distinctions must survive this operational translation and which can be collapsed without distortion. Think carefully, step by step, be honest about misfires, and do not invoke bibliographic authority, citations, or appeals to named texts—the protocol must stand on its conceptual logic alone.
