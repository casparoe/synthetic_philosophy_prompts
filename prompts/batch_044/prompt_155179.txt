I am drafting my position for a philosophy of science seminar and want an internal consistency check before I present it. Here is my current package of commitments. First, I am an epistemic structural realist: I hold that mature scientific theories yield genuine knowledge of the structural relations they posit, but I remain agnostic about the intrinsic nature of whatever entities occupy the nodes of those structures. Second, I take the pessimistic meta-induction to be largely neutralized by the structural continuity observed across successive theories, so that radical ontological discontinuity at the level of particular entities does not undermine epistemic progress. Third, I want to claim that the mathematical structures in our best physical theories are not merely representational instruments but are literally instantiated in the world, which pulls me in an ontic structural realist direction. Fourth, despite that pull, I think there must be some intrinsic properties borne by the relata that ground or realize the structural relations; otherwise I fear we are left with an untenable ontology of pure relations lacking any relata whatsoever. Fifth, I invoke the no-miracles argument to motivate realism about structure, but I deliberately resist extending it to license belief in the intrinsic nature of the underlying entities. Sixth, I interpret quantum mechanics as showing that fundamental particles lack primitive identity conditions, which I take to favor a purely relational ontology at the fundamental level, though I allow that in classical regimes continuous trajectories can supply derivative identity conditions. Seventh, I maintain that structural continuity across theory change is fully compatible with the claim that the entities referred to by successive theories are entirely distinct, provided the relational architecture is preserved.

Please audit this set for me. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection—particularly whether my epistemic stance in the first commitment actually conflicts with my ontic leanings in the third, and whether my domain-restricted quantum claim in the sixth can genuinely coexist with my classical allowance in the seventh. Rank the real tensions by severity, specify for each what the least costly revision would be and exactly what it would sacrifice, and tell me whether there is a single commitment whose removal would dissolve the majority of conflicts without collapsing the rest of the package. I am not asking for a historical survey of structural realism or any bibliography; I want a logical audit of whether I am implicitly relying on incompatible motivations—such as using continuity arguments to defend agnosticism about entities while simultaneously demanding intrinsic properties for those same entities—to hold this particular combination together.
