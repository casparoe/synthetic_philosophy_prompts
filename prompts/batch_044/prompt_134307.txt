Below are draft notes for an essay titled Autonomized Derivation: The Two-Bitser and the Lost Origin of Intentionality. I am asking for your comments, but I want a committed evaluation: take a clear position on whether my central argument holds, identify the exact failure point if it does not, and do not simply catalog objections for me to weigh.

The essay starts from Searle's distinction between original intentionality—intrinsic to biological minds—and derived intentionality, which texts, symbols, and artifacts possess only by borrowing from minds that understand them. I then use Dennett's distributed-intentionality model, specifically the two-bitser idea in which two agents each hold one bit of a two-bit state so that neither individually possesses the full content but the system as a whole realizes it. My novel application is to what I call a cascade archive: a continuously maintained symbolic system in which every layer derives meaning solely from the layer above it, but the top layer was generated centuries ago by a stochastic natural process—wind-pattern encodings carved into stone by erosion—that possessed no intentionality whatsoever. Over generations, human and then artificial interpreters have added downward-deriving layers, yet the chain now extends so far that no living agent knows the origin, and the origin was never intentional anyway. I argue that Dennett's framework predicts the current maintainers experience the archive's contents as fully meaningful—functionally indistinguishable from original intentionality—because interpretive work is distributed across the system's nodes rather than anchored in one mind. Searle, by contrast, must either deny the archive even derived intentionality (since there is no original intentional source to borrow from) or concede that derived intentionality can float free of an original anchor, undermining his biological essentialism. I conclude there is a third category, autonomized derived intentionality, which is operationally real but metaphysically orphaned.

Please focus your response on two things. First, does this argument genuinely deploy Dennett's two-bitser against Searle, or have I misapplied the scope of distributed intentionality? If you think the cascade archive collapses back into standard derived intentionality—perhaps because the human interpreters along the chain supply the missing original source—state that clearly and pinpoint the step where the collapse occurs. Second, is autonomized derived intentionality conceptually coherent, or is it merely functionalism relabeled? Give your assessment in American English, be direct about errors, and avoid presenting balanced lists of considerations without a bottom-line verdict.
