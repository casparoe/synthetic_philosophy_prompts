I hold the following position on responsibility for autonomous systems. The responsibility gap that arises with advanced autonomous agents is not a contingent failure of institutional design or regulation but a structurally ineliminable feature. Specifically, when a system exhibits what I call open-loop teleological drift—meaning it revises its operative sub-goals in response to environmental feedback that was not specified in its original objective function, and it does so in ways that are not traceable to any discrete prior human decision—there is no longer a fact of the matter about which human agent intended the specific action taken. I further hold that attempts to close this gap through explainability mandates, audit trails, or mandatory human-in-the-loop veto structures fail because they confuse an epistemic gap (we lack information about who caused what) with a metaphysical gap (there is no single causal chain anchored in a prior human intentional state). I also claim this gap is categorically different from collective responsibility cases such as corporate action, because collective cases still involve distributed intentionality, whereas open-loop teleological drift involves none.
