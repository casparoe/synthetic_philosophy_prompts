Write a deadpan parody in the form of an internal corporate HR onboarding manual and compliance handbook (approximately 700 words, numbered sections 1.0 through 6.3, flat bureaucratic voice) for a fictional consultancy, "Narrative Gravity Consulting," that treats Daniel Dennett's claim that the self is merely a center of narrative gravity as literal corporate policy. The manual should address identity verification, legal liability, workplace wellness, and employee benefits. The satire must exaggerate real philosophical commitments—autobiographical coherence as a constructed fiction, the absence of a Cartesian inner owner, the functional utility of self-storytelling, the problem of competing sub-narratives, and the rejection of long-term unity by episodic-self theorists—without inventing straw-man positions. Include invented-but-plausible bureaucratic procedures: a "Narrative Continuity Audit" that quantifies the gravitational pull of competing life-stories; an "Episodic Self Exemption" form for staff who deny narrative unity across time; a "Branching Protocol" for identity splits after trauma or failed mind-upload attempts, specifying which narrative thread retains health benefits and pension rights; and a "Margin-for-Error" clause governing disputes over autobiographical memory. The piece should remain deadpan and legible: a specialist in philosophy of mind should recognize that the author understands what is being ironized, and should see the irony as fair rather than ignorant. Do not include citations, bibliographies, references to specific page numbers, or requests for further reading. The underlying point—that if the self has no Cartesian theater, then corporate "personal identity" is just a managed fiction—should be implicit in the manual's absurd seriousness, never stated outright.
