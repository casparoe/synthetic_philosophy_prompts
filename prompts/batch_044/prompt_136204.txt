Before I evaluate existing accounts, I want the conceptual specification up front.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of value-based social explanation must meet. For each requirement, give the motivating case or distinction behind it — for example: avoiding both reduction of values to material conditions and the fallacy of treating them as purely post-hoc rationalizations; handling Weber's elective affinity without collapsing it into functionalism; accounting for disenchanted value-causation, where values operate structurally rather than psychologically; respecting the distinction between value-rational and instrumentally-rational action; and preserving the historical specificity of the Protestant Ethic thesis without rendering it unfalsifiable. Flag which desiderata are non-negotiable for any serious account and which reflect preferences of particular camps (interpretivist philosophy of social science, rational-choice critics, methodological individualists, and so on). If any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — say, if explaining values structurally conflicts with preserving their causal efficacy at the individual level, or if predictive precision demands abandoning elective-affinity reasoning — state that explicitly; that tension is what I most want mapped. Keep the weight on the criteria and their interrelations rather than on scoring specific theories, though you may briefly indicate where Weber's own framework stands against the list. No bibliography or citation list is needed; I'm looking for the conceptual architecture.
