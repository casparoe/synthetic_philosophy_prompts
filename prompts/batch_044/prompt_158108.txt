Take Marx's core commitments: that capitalist exploitation consists in the extraction of surplus value through the wage relation; that alienation involves separation from the product, from the production process, and from one's species-being; and that machinery is typically deployed to intensify exploitation rather than to liberate labor. Apply these to a phenomenon that did not exist in his lifetime: the mass extraction of unpaid cognitive and communicative labor through digital platforms that harvest user-generated text, images, and code to train proprietary generative AI systems. These systems are then enclosed as fixed capital, licensed back to users and employers as automated services, and used to displace some of the very skilled labor that supplied the training data; value is realized through subscription rents and licensing fees rather than through direct commodity production. The data providers hold no wage contract with the platform, yet their accumulated outputs become capital that competes with them.

Would Marx most likely analyze this as (a) an extension of exploitation into circulation and social reproduction, in which unpaid training labor is indirectly exploited via the subsequent displacement of waged labor; (b) a rupture with the labor theory of value, since no quantifiable necessary labor-time is directly expended by the providers in the act of contribution; or (c) a new form of alienation—call it algorithmic dispossession—in which users are separated not merely from a finished product but from their own linguistic and creative patterns, which are converted into alien capital that returns to dominate them?

Select the option that best fits a rigorously Marxist analysis, defend it against the other two, and identify one specific element of Marx's nineteenth-century framework (for example, his distinction between productive and unproductive labor, or his treatment of machinery and fixed capital) that would have to be revised or stretched to make the analysis coherent. Be explicit about where the analogy holds and where it fractures.
