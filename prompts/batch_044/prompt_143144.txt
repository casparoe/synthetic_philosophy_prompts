After a long argument with a friend about whether skipping meat, reducing air travel, or voting in a national election can make any moral difference when climate systems and supply chains are massively overdetermined, I realized we never isolated the precise premises that divided us. I need a diagnostic taxonomy—not a bibliography—for the philosophical positions on individual contributions to collective harms, with climate-related consumption, dietary choice, and voting as the running examples. Construct a decision tree with exactly five binary branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete case question that a respondent could answer without already knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to. The cases should cover: whether a single vote in a large election can be said to cause a specific policy outcome; whether purchasing meat in a fixed-supply market causes harm; whether an action that is causally redundant but visibly signals opposition carries independent moral weight; whether moral obligations can exist even when no individual can single-handedly change the collective outcome; and whether participating in a harmful system corrupts one’s character regardless of causal impact. The tree should terminate in at least six named positions: pure inefficacy denial; probabilistic or expected-harm aggregation; collective-action or coordination-duty; structural-complicity or social-connection responsibility (in the vein of Young-style analysis); expressive or norm-constitution duty; and virtue-based or character-corruption avoidance. For each leaf, specify the path of yes/no answers that reaches it, name the position, and state in one sentence what practical recommendation it yields about meat or voting. Identify any two leaves that your branching questions fail to genuinely separate, explain why the diagnostic overlap occurs, and propose one further hypothetical case that would split them. Additionally, map out one logically coherent combination of answers—such as accepting zero individual causal efficacy, denying any collective duty, yet affirming a strict virtue-based prohibition—that corresponds to no prominent view in the current debate, and briefly diagnose whether its absence reflects incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or mere neglect by theorists. Frame everything for an audience that understands the basic inefficacy objection but does not have specialist knowledge of the literature; do not include reading lists, citation requests, or references to specific texts or page numbers.
