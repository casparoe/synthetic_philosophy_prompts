I'm preparing materials for an independent study I'm conducting with my school's philosophy club, where we've been reading about whether practical stakes can affect what we know. The standard classroom distinction—epistemic contextualism versus pragmatic encroachment—feels too tidy, and I want a diagnostic sorting instrument that forces people to decide what they actually think rather than which label they prefer. Could you design a decision tree or taxonomy—aimed at advanced high-school readers with some epistemology background—that sorts positions on stake-sensitive knowledge using concrete cases, not philosopher-names?

Please construct the tree from the logical space of answers first, then map actual positions onto the branches. Use these four diagnostic splits, each tied to a specific case: (1) A bridge-safety belief: if you suddenly learn that a school bus will cross based on your casual judgment, does that retroactively change whether you knew the bridge was safe, or only whether you may now assert it? (2) If knowledge itself shifts with stakes, is the shift semantic—the word "knows" expresses different conditions—or metaphysical, with a stable meaning but a stake-sensitive belief-relation? (3) Retrospective revelation: you formed a belief with moderate evidence while genuinely assuming stakes were negligible, then discover years later that catastrophic hidden stakes always applied. Is your prior belief now disqualified, intact, or is the judgment incoherent? (4) Distributed stakes: an analyst processes anonymized data where aggregate harm is enormous but individually imperceptible; do collective, unknown-to-her stakes affect her knowledge?

The instrument should contain about six to eight leaves. At each leaf, label the position by its common name if one exists in the literature, or mark it "unnamed/unoccupied." For empty combinations—say, a view accepting retrospective disqualification but denying any semantic shift—briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Also flag any pairs of positions that these four questions fail to separate, and state what further distinction would be required. Be sure to include classic contextualism, strict invariantism, pragmatic encroachment, and at least one hybrid or niche variant—such as a view that allows stake-sensitivity only for institutional roles but not private belief. Finally, please separate what is broadly settled—such as the conceptual distinction between semantic contextualism and metaphysical encroachment—from what specialists genuinely contest, including whether retrospective cases are coherent and whether distributed stakes can operate without individual awareness. Do not present any disputed interpretation, like the claim that encroachment collapses into contextualism, as if it were consensus.
