You are a professional philosopher of science with expertise in epistemology and inductive logic. Before delivering your verdict, please briefly outline the logical structure of Goodman’s original riddle and distinguish the parts that are settled from those that remain genuinely contested among specialists.

At a recent workshop on induction, two colleagues—Mara and Jonas—became stuck in a disagreement that went well beyond the textbook exchange about “grue” and “bleen.” Mara argued that Goodman’s riddle does not merely show induction needs a constraint on projectibility; she claimed it tacitly commits anyone who solves it to a modal metaphysics. Specifically, she said that treating “green” as projectible while rejecting “grue” requires us to already accept that there are genuine natural kinds whose modal behavior—how they persist or vary across possible worlds—differs systematically from that of gerrymandered predicates. Jonas replied that this overreads Goodman entirely: entrenchment is a syntactic and historical feature of linguistic practice, not a metaphysical discovery about natural kinds or modal structure, and importing modal realism is an unnecessary addition that Goodman himself never made. The exchange sharpened when Mara introduced a variant she calls “schmodal-grue”: a predicate applying to objects that are green in all worlds within a specified modal distance, but blue in worlds beyond that boundary, where “modal distance” is defined not metaphysically but purely through the similarity metrics internal to our current theoretical framework. She contended that if we reject this predicate on the same grounds we reject “grue,” we are not avoiding metaphysics but merely concealing our theoretical commitments inside our metric of world-similarity. Jonas countered that this variant actually proves his point—once you embed the similarity metric into the predicate’s definition, you have already done the work of selecting a projectible framework, so the case collapses into circularity and no independent riddle remains.

Please adjudicate their exchange point by point, rating each move as clearly right, partly right but overstated, clearly mistaken, or genuinely unsettled. (2) Is Mara’s “schmodal-grue” a legitimate extension of the problem, or does it collapse into circularity as Jonas claims? (3) What are both parties missing—especially regarding whether projectibility can be grounded in pragmatic, cognitive-scientific, or non-metaphysical considerations?
