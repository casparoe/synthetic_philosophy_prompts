In American and common-law courts, parties routinely stipulate to facts—agreeing that X occurred for the purposes of trial—even when both sides privately believe X is false, and judges treat these stipulations as binding. At the same time, witnesses swear an oath to tell the truth, perjury statutes punish false statements made under oath, and plea bargains allow conviction without any court ever establishing what actually happened.
