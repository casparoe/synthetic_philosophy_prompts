I'm working on a novel that keeps bumping into philosophy, and I wrote down my current takes on Bradley's regress and the unity of the propisition so I can see if I'm talking nonsense. Here they are, in no particular order:

1. I buy Bradley's point that if relations are real external things linking terms, you get an infinite regress—so they can't be fully real in that way.
2. I still think the unity of a propisition is genuinely real, not just a heap of subject and predicate.
3. I reject Bradley's Absolute as a mystical blob; I want individual things and thier properties to stay distinct.
4. I think Russell's fix works—relations are real but don't need another relation to hook them up, which stops the regress.
5. I think the same issue comes up in fiction: a plot isn't just events plus being-related-to-the-theme relations; if those relations are external, you get the same regress, but if they're internal or Russell-style real, the story holds together.
6. I don't think we need some mental act of synthesis—like a reader's consciousness—to glue either propisitions or narratives into one.

Can you go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly? For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd have to give up. Also tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess. And flag any pair that only looks like a problem but dissolves once you look closer—I'm pretty sure I'm mixing up a real contradiction with something that's just awkward wording.
