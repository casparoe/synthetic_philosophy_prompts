I have been thinking on my own about the idea that awareness might exist in some minimal form even in very basic physical things, and that our rich conscious lives are built out of those tiny pieces coming together. This raises a specific puzzle: if every particle or basic unit carries its own faint experience, there must be some account of how those countless micro-experiences merge into the single, unified stream I actually live through. Before I evaluate any particular theory, I want a clear specification of what any such account must deliver. Please write the desiderata: what must a theory of experiential combination get right — which cases it must cover, which distinctions it must preserve, which thought experiments it must survive — with the reasoning behind each requirement, and indicate which ones are non-negotiable and which reflect particular theoretical tastes rather than genuine constraints. Be concrete. For example, consider the scenario of gradual neural replacement: if biological neurons are one by one replaced by artificial components that preserve all functional and causal roles but are stipulated to carry no micro-experiential properties, should the overall consciousness fade slowly, disappear at a sharp threshold, or remain fully intact? An adequate theory must commit to one of these outcomes and explain away the other two. The theory must also distinguish true combination into one subject from mere aggregation — like many people in an auditorium, each with their own separate experience — and it must clarify whether successful combination requires specific spatial proximity, causal integration, or some other structural relation among parts, or whether it could in principle occur across physically disconnected systems. Most importantly, tell me whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and briefly assess how the two broad strategies people take — treating combination as governed by a fundamental law of nature versus treating it as emerging from structural relations among parts — stand against your list. I am not asking for a literature review, citations, or a bibliography; I want the criteria themselves, and an honest judgment about whether meeting all of them at once is actually possible.
