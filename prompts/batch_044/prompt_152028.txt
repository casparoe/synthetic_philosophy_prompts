As a retired physicist now studying moral philosophy, I am drafting an upper-level ethics exam. Write one 45-minute essay question on the paradox of deontology that cannot be answered by repeating the familiar one-versus-five scenario; instead, it should require students to examine whether the paradox arises from treating agent-relative prohibitions as linearly aggregable, analogous to the erroneous assumption that physical interactions always scale proportionally without saturation or interference effects. The question must ask students to state the paradox precisely, assess whether this scaling diagnosis resolves or merely relocates the tension, and defend a substantive position. Provide the exam question, a model answer of roughly 500 words, and a marking scheme delivered as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion, specifying the moves that distinguish an excellent answer from a competent one and naming the specific misunderstandings that should cost marks.
