I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun reading philosophy of psychiatry. At a local seminar, two regular participants—neither of them me—argued over whether Ian Hacking’s idea of looping effects undermines the scientific standing of psychiatric classifications. Their exchange ran approximately as follows.

First, Alex, a clinical psychologist, claimed that introducing prolonged grief disorder into a recent diagnostic manual generates a looping effect: bereaved individuals learn the criteria and begin to interpret their ordinary mourning through them, altering both their emotional course and their help-seeking behavior. This feedback, he said, reshapes the symptom profile that researchers measure and inflates prevalence figures, so the category functions as a moving target rather than a stable natural kind comparable to electron or hemoglobin.

Second, Blake, a philosopher of medicine, replied that looping is ubiquitous—categories such as teenager or genius also change those they describe—yet this does not erase their reality or social utility. She added that withholding a diagnostic framework does not prevent suffering; it merely denies people a conceptual tool for understanding their experience and accessing treatment. Thus the loop is practically necessary, not epistemically vicious.

Third, Alex conceded that looping occurs elsewhere but insisted psychiatric cases are distinctive: labeling someone diabetic does not alter pancreatic function, whereas in psychiatry the loop can generate or amplify the very symptoms being assessed. Because of this, he argued, psychiatric kinds lack the stability required for law-like scientific generalizations.

Fourth, Blake responded that Hacking himself regards interactive kinds as genuinely real, though historically mutable; instability is characteristic of human kinds, not a disqualification from them. She then pressed Alex to describe what a genuinely non-looping psychiatric classification would look like, suggesting that his demand for physical-science stability implicitly imposes an inappropriate metaphysics.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move, identifying who is right about what at each step and noting anything both overlook. Answer in one paragraph of no more than 250 words.
