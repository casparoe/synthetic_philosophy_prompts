I'm writing up my philosophical profile on introspection for a graduate reading group and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here are my commitments:

(1) Introspection is fallible and corrigible across the board: there is no domain of mental life in which first-person authority is absolute.

(2) Nevertheless, the fallibility is sharply asymmetrical: errors about occurrent phenomenal states are rare and correctable through finer-grained attention, whereas errors about standing propositional attitudes—beliefs, intentions, and desires—are pervasive and typically require external behavioural correction or long-term reflective equilibrium.

(3) Phenomenal states are self-intimating: if a state is phenomenal, its occurrence is necessarily available to the subject, even if its qualitative nature is mischaracterised.

(4) The qualitative content of phenomenal experience is conceptually mediated to a non-trivial extent; there is no "pure" phenomenal given that is entirely independent of conceptual framing.

(5) Consequently, introspection delivers authoritative knowledge of the occurrence of a phenomenal state, but not necessarily authoritative knowledge of its correct conceptual classification (e.g., that this is "pain" rather than "intense cold" or "a peculiar form of anxiety").

(6) I therefore reject both Cartesian infallibilism and eliminativism about first-person reports: introspection occupies an intermediate, domain-sensitive position with special but limited weight.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. I have not decided which commitment I would drop if forced—so do not assume I am protecting (3) or (5)—and I want to know whether any of these commitments silently commit me to something I claim to reject, such as a form of transparency about belief or an unmediated phenomenal given. Be specific about whether the problem lies between (2) and (4), (3) and (5), or elsewhere, and whether the tension can be resolved without abandoning the asymmetry in (2).
