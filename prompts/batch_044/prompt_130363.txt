Having spent a career in experimental physics and only lately turned to philosophy, I find the old parable of Death in Damascus taking on an unexpected shape when viewed through deterministic dynamics. I should be grateful if you could examine the following argument, which seems to prove far too much, and tell me where it fails.

(1) In the parable, the merchant's flight to Aleppo does not thwart fate; it is the very causal route by which death reaches him.
(2) If an outcome is fully determined, then any action taken to avoid it merely selects among pre-existing branches, none of which lie outside the determined outcome space.
(3) In chaotic physical systems, an evasive perturbation—say, a sudden change in trajectory—does not escape the attractor basin; with positive Lyapunov exponents, it only traverses a different orbit within the same invariant measure.
(4) Therefore rational precaution in any deterministic framework is structurally analogous to the merchant's flight: it reshuffles the causal sequence without altering the statistical destiny of the system.
(5) Yet we plainly regard vaccination, structural engineering, or diversification of risk as genuinely protective, not as self-defeating movements towards harm.
(6) So either determinism is false, or rational precaution is conceptually impossible.

I am reluctant to surrender (3), which seems physically sound, and I should prefer to keep (5) intact. Where exactly is the weakest link—between (2) and (3), or in the normative inference drawn in (4)? What does repairing it cost for a scientifically informed view of agency? Please reply in British English.
