Analyze the 1980 publication of DSM-III and the subsequent global dominance of categorical psychiatric diagnosis as a case study in path dependence. Treat the revision as a branching point: the shift to operationalized, checklist-based criteria was driven by demands for inter-rater reliability and by institutional pressure to distinguish psychiatry from psychoanalytic practice. Carefully map the mechanisms of lock-in—reimbursement coding, clinical training, pharmaceutical trial design, and international harmonization—distinguishing those that generate coordination-based path dependence from those that generate learning-based path dependence. Then assess the counterfactual: if the American Psychiatric Association had adopted a dimensional or phenomenological framework in 1980, would that framework have achieved global convergence regardless, given parallel developments in the ICD and national traditions? Pay particular attention to whether the later Research Domain Criteria initiative and the dimensional supplements in ICD-11 represent a genuine unlocking of the categorical path, or merely an overlay that leaves the core taxonomy intact. Finally, state whether this episode confirms the strong claim that psychiatric nosology is locked in irrespective of validity, or whether its persistence is better explained by active institutional defense than by the inertia of historical contingency—identifying precisely where the path-dependence model is silent or misleading.
