Here is an argument I have been turning over that seems to show that “the alignment problem” is either conceptually incoherent or irreducibly plural, yet I am suspicious because researchers clearly treat it as one field. (1) The alignment problem is standardly framed as ensuring an AI system pursues the goals its designers actually intend. (2) But “intended goals” requires a stable benchmark of designer values that can be formally specified or learned. (3) Normative terms like “justice” or “art” are what philosophers call essentially contested: they lack any shared substantive core and instead organize perpetual reasonable disagreement within a common vocabulary. (4) “Beneficial outcomes” and “human values” function the same way; deep, reasonable moral pluralism means there is no determinate benchmark independent of a particular normative framework. (5) So alignment cannot be a single technical problem of hitting a pre-existing target. Instead it fragments into as many distinct problems as there are irreconcilable normative frameworks, each requiring its own institutional settlement rather than a unified engineering solution. (6) If these fragments share no common structure, then technical results in one framework—say, scalable oversight trained on democratic preferences—do not advance alignment in another, say, one committed to deontological side-constraints. (7) Therefore the field of AI alignment lacks a unified object of study, and treating reward modeling, interpretability, and constitutional AI as solving “the” same problem is a category mistake. (8) Yet if we deny (4) and insist there is a determinable core of human values, we must claim that deep moral disagreement is largely erroneous or superficial, which seems implausible. I would prefer to keep (1) and (5) if possible, but I feel a slide between (4) and (6). Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost—especially regarding whether technical alignment research implicitly smuggles in a particular normative settlement under the guise of neutral engineering?
