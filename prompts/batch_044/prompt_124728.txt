Write a formal dialogue between two researchers—Dr. Aris Thorne, who holds that task decomposition in iterated amplification preserves both alignment and competence, and Dr. Bren Voss, who holds that in iterated distillation it systematically undermines them—debating a specific architecture in which a strategic-reasoning system is decomposed into an “intention-prediction” submodule and a “counterfactual-planning” submodule, evaluated only on the final plan rather than on the intermediate representations. Thorne should defend the view that alignment survives because the human evaluator’s reward is anchored to observable outputs and the decomposition is symbolically transparent; Voss should respond by arguing that the distilled student internalizes the decomposer’s hidden heuristic approximations, producing “representational drift” in which competence metrics remain high but alignment fails in out-of-distribution strategic contexts because the intermediate representations smuggle unexamined instrumental goals. Each speaker must directly address the other’s previous argument rather than talk past it. Conclude with both identifying the precise empirical comparison—say, performance on adversarial decomposition probes that perturb intermediate representations without altering final outputs—that would distinguish Thorne’s transparency thesis from Voss’s drift thesis, and briefly state what result each would take as confirmation.
