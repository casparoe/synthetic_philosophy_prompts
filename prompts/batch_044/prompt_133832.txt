I'm designing the final examination for an advanced undergraduate seminar on ontology and scientific explanation, and I want one essay question that moves well beyond a standard summary of the physical, design, and intentional stances. The question should center on Dennett's thesis that real patterns exist only relative to a predictive stance, but it should embed this within a specific conceptual puzzle that students cannot resolve by reciting labels: namely, the apparent conflict with mechanistic explanation in neuroscience and cognitive science, where identifying a mechanism typically requires that its parts be counterfactually manipulable and that the mechanism exhibit a kind of stability that seems independent of our predictive interests. Ask students to consider whether treating mechanisms as stance-dependent patterns undermines the interventionist rationale for mechanistic discovery, or whether the design stance can be reconceived so that counterfactual stability itself is a pattern recognized only from within that stance. The essay should require students to explain what each of the three stances commits us to ontologically, to articulate precisely why mechanistic explanation seems to demand more than predictive success, and to defend a position on whether the reality of patterns in mechanistic science is ultimately physical-stance bedrock, design-stance projection, or intentional-stance interpretation.

Please provide: (1) the exam question itself, formulated for a 75-minute written response; (2) a model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the specific argumentative moves expected; and (3) a detailed marking scheme totaling 20 marks, broken into five criteria, with each criterion specifying both the correct move (worth full marks) and the characteristic misconception that should receive partial or no credit. In particular, the scheme should penalize answers that conflate the design stance with mere instrumentalism, that treat the physical stance as automatically ontologically privileged without argument, or that fail to address whether counterfactual intervention requires stance-independent entities.
