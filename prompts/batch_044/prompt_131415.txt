There is this stall in the forecasting/policy world that I keep running into, and I want to know if it's even a real disagreement. One camp — call them the outside-viewers — builds reference classes from dead empires and hegemonic states (Rome, Qing, Soviet, etc.), computes base rates for systemic fracture, and applies them to modern bureacratic states. They say if you reject the base rate you're just telling yourself a just-so story with the inside view. The other camp — mostly comparative historians and some institutional economists — says the reference class is broken because modern states have nuclear deterrence, digital surveillance, and global capital flows that don't map onto any prior morphology. They talk about morphological reference classes — grouping by institutional form, not chronological age — and claim the forecasters are smuggling in a hidden similiarity judgment every time they pick 'empire' as the category.

The debate's been stuck for years. Every argument is well-known, nobody moves. My working theory: it's not about data, it's about whether 'similarity' in forecasting is an empirical discovery or a framing decision that comes before the numbers. But I could be wrong — maybe there's a hidden formal question about how to weight reference classes when base rates conflict, and neither side has solved it.

Can you diagnose this? Is it verbal (they mean different things by 'forecast'), substantive (they disagree about which institutional features are causally decisive), or are they answering different questions — one about betting odds under ignorance, one about causal explanation? Use what each side actually says about morphological indexing, about whether inside-view reasoning can ever override a base rate, and about what a forecast is for. Then tell me what progress would look like: a formal result on reference-class ambiguity, an agreement on indexing rules, or proof that the stall is permanent because the framing choice is normative?

And please — if my premise is confused, like if 'morphological reference class' isn't a real distinction people use or if I'm forcing two fields into one argument, call it out plainly. Don't need bibliographies, just the diagnosis.
