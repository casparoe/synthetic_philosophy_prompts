As a doctoral candidate in political philosophy with training in science and technology studies, I have developed the following set of commitments regarding John Dewey’s experimentalist framework and its bearing on democratic theory, and I want a consistency audit before I present them in a seminar. (1) I take Dewey’s central democratic thesis to be that democracy is best understood not as a constitutional form but as a mode of social inquiry—an ongoing, fallible experiment in collective problem-solving in which intelligence is distributed rather than centralized. (2) I reject algorithmic interpretations of Dewey: democratic inquiry is structurally analogous to scientific inquiry in being problem-driven and responsive to feedback, but political problems are irreducibly normative and conflict-laden in ways that laboratory problems typically are not, so the analogy has strict limits. (3) I hold that Dewey’s fallibilism extends fully to institutional design itself: constitutional arrangements, voting rules, and deliberative procedures should be treated as revisable hypotheses subject to empirical feedback, not as fixed frameworks within which inquiry occurs. (4) At the same time, I maintain what I call institutional fallibilism without meta-fallibilism: every specific democratic procedure is open to revision, yet the normative commitment to collective, experimental problem-solving functions as a transcendental precondition for revision rather than as one revisable item among others. (5) I also argue that contemporary participatory mechanisms integrating predictive modeling with iterative public feedback can be interpreted as genuine extensions of Deweyan experimentalism, provided the predictive instruments remain subordinate to collective judgment rather than substituting for it. Please trace the tensions in this package. Specifically, does (3) threaten the stability required by (4), and if so, is the tension severe or only apparent? Does my endorsement of predictive modeling in (5) conflict with (2)’s insistence on the irreducibly normative character of political problems? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest revision that would restore coherence for each, and tell me whether any apparent contradiction dissolves on closer inspection—particularly whether (4) can be defended without smuggling in a form of epistemic authoritarianism that (1) and (2) were meant to exclude.
