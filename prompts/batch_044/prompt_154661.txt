My current research position holds that Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument succeeds: being in a mental state does not entail being in a position to know that one is in it, given the margin-for-error principle applied to gradually changing conditions. Consequently, I treat the failure of the KK principle—if S knows p, then S knows that S knows p—not as a contingent epistemic limitation but as a structural feature of cognition that undermines any robust form of introspective transparency. Recently, however, I have been considering a hybrid amendment: the introduction of an internal 'reliability-tracking module'—a subpersonal mechanism that assigns confidence weights to first-order mental states and is itself subject to third-order monitoring—might restore a restricted, non-soritic luminosity for a specific subclass of cognitive states without reinstating the full KK principle. Before I integrate this amendment, I want a rigorous audit of what would genuinely count against my core acceptance of anti-luminosity and the associated rejection of KK. I am not asking for the standard catalogue of objections—such as the charge that the margin-for-error principle overgenerates scepticism about ordinary knowledge, or that knowledge is better analysed in terms of safety or sensitivity rather than luminosity. Rather, I want to know: which formal results in modal epistemic logic (for instance, regarding the consistency or inconsistency of KK with particular accessibility relations), which empirical findings in cognitive science concerning the accuracy of metacognitive monitoring, and which conceptual distinctions—say, between phenomenal access and epistemic access—would have to obtain for the anti-luminosity conclusion to be false? Furthermore, could a committed defence of anti-luminosity consistently concede that some second-order tracking is possible via such a module without collapsing into a qualified version of KK? I would like the potential defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones such a defence could absorb by further qualification rather than surrender.
