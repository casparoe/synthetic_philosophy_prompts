After a long argument with a friend about Arendt and political deception, I want to check a summary they insisted on. They claimed that Arendt treats every form of government secrecy as structurally identical to totalitarian propaganda: both replace shared factual reality with a fabricated narrative that the liar eventually comes to believe. They said she developed this specifically in her analysis of the Pentagon Papers, where bureaucratic self-deception is not a contingent failure but an inevitable feature of modern administrative lying, and that she therefore condemns even strategic deception—such as concealing a military vulnerability from an adversary—because it initiates the same reality-destroying spiral. They added that she distinguishes political lies from ordinary social falsehoods only by scale, not by kind, and concluded that her view makes democratic governance impossible since every state must keep some secrets. How fair is this? Please separate what she actually argued about traditional secrecy versus modern image-making, whether self-deception is inevitable or contingent in her account, and whether her critique applies to strategic deception at all or only to the bureaucratic substitution of narrative for fact. Also, does her position really undermine democratic governance, or does it preserve space for legitimate secrecy that this summary collapses?
