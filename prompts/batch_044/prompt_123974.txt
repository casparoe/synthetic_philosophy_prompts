As a retired physicist who has only recently begun working through philosophy of mind, I have become preoccupied with the color phi phenomenon—two discrete dots of different colors flashed in rapid succession, yet perceived as one continuous object that changes hue midway—and with the distinction between Orwellian revisions, in which the memory of an experience is rewritten after the fact, and Stalinesque revisions, in which the experience itself is constructed with false content in real time. The following argument, which I have assembled, seems to show that conscious experience has no determinate temporal order at all, a conclusion that strikes me as absurd; yet I cannot locate the error.

(1) In color phi, the reported midpoint color change never physically occurs.
(2) Its explanation must be either Stalinesque—the brain generates the intermediate percept before the second dot arrives—or Orwellian—the subject initially registers two separate dots and only later revises her memory to include continuous motion.
(3) Now suppose, as a nested-revision hypothesis, that a Stalinesque construction occurring at time t can itself become the target of an Orwellian revision at a later time t'.
(4) If such nesting is possible, then for any experience E apparently occurring at time T, there may be a higher-order revision that shifts E's apparent temporal location to T' without leaving any trace accessible to introspection.
(5) Because neuroscience can only measure neural correlates that are themselves equally subject to nesting, there is no independent third-person criterion to recover the true order.
(6) Hence there is no fact of the matter about the true temporal sequence of conscious states.
(7) But this undermines every ordinary judgment about temporal priority—whether a pain preceded a choice, whether two sounds were simultaneous, and so on.
(8) Therefore the nested-revision hypothesis proves too much.

Which premise should be rejected, and at what cost? Is step (3) conceptually incoherent, or does it merely force us to abandon the transparency of introspection about temporal order? If I must preserve the idea that experience is genuinely ordered in time, which of the other premises am I paying to keep?
