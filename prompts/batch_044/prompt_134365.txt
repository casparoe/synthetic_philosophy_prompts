There's this widespread practice in the modern stoicism revival—like the journaling templates in popular apps and those 'dichotomy of control' worksheets—where you list a worry, tag it either inside or outside your control, then commit to letting go of anything in the second column. What view of moral agency does that tagging ritual actually presuppose? Does it treat indirect influence—persuading a collegue, shaping a childs habits—as 'outside' in the same way wether is? And is it consistent with the stoic claim that virtue is the only good, given that these worksheets often obsess over career failures or social rejection, things that are technically exterals, yet frame them as serious losses worth tracking? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does the practice need a libertarian-style boundry between self and world, or can a compatabilist or even a relational account of agency save the split? If we replaced that binary with a gradient of influence, what part of the therapeutic practice would actually collapse?
