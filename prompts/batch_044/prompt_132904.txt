Write the full specification for an adequate theory of the comparative relation “better than” as it applies to spectrum arguments in value comparison. I want the criteria themselves, not a survey of the literature. List every desideratum the theory must satisfy: which pairwise cases it must get right (clear superiority, clear equality, borderline proximity, distant incommensurability); which structural properties it must preserve or deliberately abandon (transitivity, completeness, continuity, asymmetry, reflexivity); and which distinctions it must not collapse—especially between accumulation of marginal differences across a homogeneous quantitative dimension versus across a qualitatively shifting scale, and between “not better” due to equality versus due to incommensurability. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or paradox, centering on the sorites-style chain in which barely-noticeable improvements accumulate into a grossly implausible ordering. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable (e.g., asymmetry in clear cases, non-arbitrariness of boundaries) and which are camp preferences (e.g., preservation of full rational-choice axioms versus tolerance for cyclical preferences at distance). Crucially, identify whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: for instance, can a relation be locally transitive within a neighborhood of options yet globally non-transitive across distant ones without invoking an ad hoc cutoff? If you think a specific pair—say, unrestricted transitivity plus a principled, non-arbitrary boundary condition—is impossible to satisfy together, say so explicitly and explain the tension. Finally, without developing full theories, briefly indicate how a strict total-ordering view, an incommensurabilist view, and a vagueness-based graded-membership view each survive or fail against your list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on the scorecard.
