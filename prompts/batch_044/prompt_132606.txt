Provide an example of a conceptual tension within Harsanyi's aggregation theorem that arises when agents operate with structurally different state-space representations rather than merely disagreeing about probabilities within a shared frame. Specifically, construct a case in which Agent A's von Neumann–Morgenstern utility is defined over outcomes that depend on a variable X that Agent A can observe and condition upon, while Agent B treats X as an unobservable background parameter and defines utility only over observable outcomes Y that are probabilistically linked to X but never directly revealed. The social planner seeks to apply Harsanyi's linear aggregation rule but lacks a canonical translation between these representations—there is no agreed-upon joint distribution over (X,Y) pairs because the agents partition the world differently. Explain why this epistemic framing divergence, rather than a simple difference in risk attitudes or beliefs, threatens the shared-domain assumption required for assigning consistent aggregation weights. Identify the specific point in Harsanyi's argument—whether in the construction of the social preference relation or in the derivation of the weight vector—where the absence of a common state-space renders the aggregation ambiguous, and discuss whether any principled resolution can be achieved without imposing an external, non-neutral ontology onto one of the agents.
