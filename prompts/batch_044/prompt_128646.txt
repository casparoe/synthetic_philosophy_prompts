Following a recent collapse in my health and the dissolution of a long-term project, I have adopted what I take to be a strict tranquilist view: well-being consists solely in the absence of craving and suffering, with no positive contribution from pleasure, accomplishment, or meaning. Yet I am troubled by episodes of what I might call "unbidden positive affect"—sudden aesthetic arrest, unmotivated intellectual curiosity, or spontaneous warmth—that do not seem to respond to any prior deprivation. If tranquilism denies intrinsic value to all positive states, does it not follow that these episodes are disguised cravings, a kind of reaching-toward that corrupts pure tranquility? And if they are not cravings, how can they be compatible with the claim that only absence matters, without smuggling in a covert hedonism?
