I am a doctoral candidate in political philosophy, and I am preparing a one-year dissertation fellowship proposal. I need your help structuring a research plan around a specific conceptual tension in the ethics of voting that I have not seen addressed directly in the standard literature.

My working idea is this. Most debates about voting ethics frame the choice between expressive voting—casting a ballot to manifest one's values—and strategic or "lesser evil" voting—selecting the least bad viable candidate—as competing justifications. I want to examine a case where these justifications appear to conflict structurally: a voter ranks candidates by preference, but the ballot requires a single selection, and the leading viable option advocates policies that the voter regards not merely as suboptimal but as violating a deontological constraint (for instance, treating some group of persons merely as means). The central question is whether choosing that candidate can still be defended on expressive grounds, or whether the ballot functions as a speech act that necessarily expresses endorsement of the candidate's full platform, thereby implicating the voter in symbolic complicity.

More precisely, I want to ask: Is there a coherent account of expressive voting that permits strategic selection of a morally compromised candidate without the voter thereby expressing assent to the compromising policies? Does the answer depend on treating the ballot as a propositional speech act rather than as an anonymous input into an aggregation mechanism? And can any internal qualification—such as the voter's private intent—sever the link between the act of voting and the expressed endorsement?

For the proposal, please provide the following: (1) a precise statement of the research question and why existing frameworks (purely expressive versus purely instrumental) leave it unresolved; (2) the methodological approach—specifically, an analysis of the speech-act structure of ballot-casting combined with an account of moral complicity that distinguishes causal contribution from symbolic association, and an explanation of why this approach is preferable to standard consequentialist or rights-based analyses; (3) a description of what a successful result would look like (for example, a framework that distinguishes permissible strategic voting from impermissible symbolic endorsement under specified conditions) and what would count as failure (for example, if the speech-act analysis collapses back into outcome-based reasoning); (4) a realistic assessment of scope for a one-year project, noting that this is conceptual work requiring no empirical data collection. Please do not include a bibliography, citation list, or requests for specific readings; I will supply references myself. Write in formal American English.
