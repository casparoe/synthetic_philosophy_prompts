There's this institutional habit in philosophy of maths and logic where people bring up Frege's political diary — the one with the antisemitic and nationalist stuff he wrote near the end — and then immediately say but that doesn't touch his logicism or the sense/reference distinction, so we can bracket it. I'm wondering what philosophical commitments that bracketing actually presupposes. Does it assume formal reasoning is somehow imune to the character of the reasoner? Or that there's a hard boundary between context of discovery and context of justification that still holds even when the discovery involves explicit racial ideology? And if we take seriously the idea of epistemic contimination — that holding certain vicious beliefs can warp your judgement in apparently unrelated domains — does the practice of separating Frege's politics from his philosphy collapse? Or can a defender say logic is just different, and if so, what view of logic's autonomy does that commit them to? Is the practice even consistent with how we treat other figures, or is Frege special because his work is formal? I want to know what implicit theory of philosophical knowledge is being smuggled in when we call the diary biographically interesting but philosophically irrelevant.
