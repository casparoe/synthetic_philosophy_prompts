Distinguish Humean regularity theories of lawhood from anti-Humean dispositional essentialism, with particular attention to (i) the ontological status of laws, (ii) the explanation of counterfactual stability, and (iii) the charge that essentialism covertly reintroduces Humean constant conjunction at the level of property-identity. In your answer, address whether the governance metaphor employed by some essentialists can be made metaphysically substantive, or whether it collapses into a merely heuristic image of modal constraint.

The student wrote:

Hume's regularity theory treats laws as universal generalisations summarising constant conjunctions observed in nature. Because Hume denies necessary connections between distinct existences, laws carry no intrinsic modal weight; they are descriptive summaries, and counterfactual stability is explained entirely through psychological projection of observed habits onto hypothetical cases, without appeal to alternative worlds. Anti-Humean dispositional essentialism, by contrast, holds that laws are grounded in the essential natures of properties — for instance, the negative charge of an electron essentially includes the disposition to repel like charges. This grants laws genuine modal force, since the behaviour of particulars is metaphysically guaranteed by the properties they instantiate, not by patterns across worlds.

With regard to accidental regularities, the essentialist can accommodate them only by denying that they are lawlike. However, because the essentialist identifies a property's essence precisely through its regular co-occurrence with other properties, the boundary between law and accident is drawn by reference to observed constant conjunction after all. Thus essentialism risks circularity: it claims to ground regularity in essence, yet determines essence by regularity, effectively smuggling Humean constant conjunction back in at the level of property-identity.

As for governance, the metaphor implies that laws actively direct the behaviour of instances. This is not causal in Hume's sense — there is no impression of necessary connection — but it is precisely the relation Hume rejected and then sought to explain away psychologically. The essentialist therefore rehabilitates Hume's own concept of necessary connection, giving it an ontological rather than mental foundation. Governance is thus metaphysically substantive, though it operates through ontological dependence rather than efficient causation.
