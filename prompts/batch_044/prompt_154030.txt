There's this portfolio review norm in a lot of MFA programs: reviewers want to see range across at least three conceptually distinct themes, and if most of your work is just parameter tweaks of one idea—same compositional grid, different lighting; same melodic contour, different tempo—they mark you down as one-dimensional. Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity says that's basically all anyone ever does, sliding knobs on conceptual themes, there's no deep invention from scratch. So what theory of artistic value or creative identity does that review practice preseuppose? Does it need an ontological split between theme and varation, or can it be squared with the idea that every artist is just twiddling knobs? Reconstruct the implicit philosophy, say whether the practice strictly requires that split or is compatible with several views, and tell me what the rubric would have to look like if it took Hofstadter seriously—would they have to reward deep parameter exploration over superficial theme-hopping?
