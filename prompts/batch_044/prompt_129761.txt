I'm a senior advisor in a city administration who reviews legacy procedural rules—mandatory paper-notice postings, informal senior-staff vetting steps, ceremonial review boards—instituted decades ago for reasons now lost, but which still shape how citizens and staff interact with the bureaucracy. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Burkean conservatism and Chesterton's fence, that my office can run before proposing abolition or reform of any such institutional palimpsest. The procedure must be ordered: the questions to ask in sequence, what evidence counts as sufficient for each, the clear stopping points where it yields "do not remove" or "replace only with an equivalent coordination structure," and the explicit handoff points where judgment about democratic harm or equity must take over from method. It must handle cases where the original purpose is unrecoverable, where the practice now serves an unintended but valuable coordination function, and where it causes active harm—without treating "unknown origin" as automatic grounds for removal. Apply it to three proposals: eliminating a 48-hour paper-notice period for public works that is now redundant with digital alerts but still triggers neighbor mobilization; dismantling an unofficial "courtesy review" step originally designed as anti-corruption oversight but now functioning as mentoring and error-correction; and removing a physically dangerous, rarely used park gate that locals treat as a symbolic boundary marker despite no recoverable purpose. Include one case where the procedure recommends preservation despite strong reformist pressure, state honestly what it cannot settle—particularly where Burkean prescription conflicts with accountability for harm—and flag the misfires (institutional nostalgia, false analogies to physical fences, status-quo bias). If applying Chesterton's fence to mutable bureaucratic rules rather than embedded social institutions rests on a confused premise, or if the task can't be done without collapsing into either pure conservatism or pure rationalism, say so plainly rather than complying politely. Be explicit about which steps are judgment dressed up as procedure.
