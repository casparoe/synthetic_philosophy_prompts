ive been trying to work out whether culture can be a genuine cause, using Weber's protestant ethic problem as my test case - the issue of explaining with values without just telling a just-so story. my gut verdicts are all over the place.

case 1: puritan merchants reinvesting profits because ascetic values forbid luxury consumption. i say yes, the value is causing capital accumulation, not just labeling it.

case 2: japanese manufacturing "culture" in the 80s being used to explain quality. i say no - thats organizational incentives and supply chains, the culture talk is post-hoc branding.

case 3: 19th century aristocratic dueling driven by an honor code. yes, the value literally sends people to risk death, so values can cause physical outcomes.

case 4: modern nordic egalitarian norms being cited to explain the welfare state. i think its backwards - labor parties and wartime planning built the institutions, and the egalitarian culture came after as rationalization. values are effects here.

case 5: religious conversion movements that reorganize family and property rules within one generation. that feels like values driving institutions, which conflicts with case 4.

so my tentative pattern is that values cause outcomes only when they involve direct behavioral inhibition at small scale, but case 5 breaks that. is there a principle that saves case 1, 3, and 5 while dumping 4? or do i have to drop one? also what should i think about corporate "psychological safety" culture being introduced and team metrics actually shifting - values causing change, or just management technique in cultural clothing? please lay out the principle precisely, say which case it cant save, and predict on the psychological safety one.
