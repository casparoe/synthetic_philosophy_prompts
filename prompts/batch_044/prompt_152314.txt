I serve on the Interdisciplinary Ethics and Funding Review Board of the Meridian Institute for Foundational Research. At next Thursday's session we must vote on Proposal QF-2027, which seeks $2.4 million across thirty-six months to construct a macroscopic superconducting array sustaining high-density quantum coherence over roughly 10^23 degrees of freedom. The investigators, working within a framework they term valence topology, contend that if pan-sentience holds at the fundamental level, standard measurement procedures collapse coherence in ways that produce microphenomenal harm, and that their proposed coherence-preserving observation protocol could diminish such harm by orders of magnitude while yielding data on correlations between integrated phenomenal intensity and topological defect states. A significant minority on the board—physicists who grant panpsychism logical coherence but deny its empirical tractability—argues that the proposal illicitly embeds an unfalsifiable ethical ontology into funding criteria and that the Institute should not commit resources based on speculative suffering ascriptions to quantum fields. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame—full approval with conditions, a conditional moratorium, or denial—and it must engage seriously with the strongest consideration supporting the position you reject, rather than treating it as merely confused. The reasoning should weigh both the possibility that fundamental entities bear minimal valence and the objection that framing coherence collapse as harm is conceptually premature. Write the memo in British English; this request itself is composed in American English.
