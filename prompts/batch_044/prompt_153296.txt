Please rank the following five explanatory frameworks by their overall explanatory power for three cross-cultural patterns: the ceremonial destruction of accumulated goods in potlatch traditions; the erection of megalithic monuments lacking storage, defensive, or residential utility; and the modern luxury-market practice of deliberately destroying high-value goods to maintain scarcity premiums. For each framework, assign a tier—S, A, B, C, or D—with S indicating the strongest account and D the weakest, and provide exactly one sentence justifying that placement.

The frameworks are as follows.

First, Zahavi's handicap principle, interpreted for human social competition: wasteful expenditure serves as an honest signal of underlying fitness or resource access because it is too costly for low-status individuals to fake.

Second, Veblen's theory of conspicuous consumption, reconceptualized for positional goods: status is achieved through invidious comparison, and the visibility of waste, rather than its absolute magnitude, drives competitive escalation.

Third, a synthetic reputational escrow model: irreversible expenditure acts as a non-fungible social deposit that binds the spender to future cooperative obligations within the group, meaning the loss is not merely displayed but structurally committed.

Fourth, cultural group-selection accounts: collective ritual waste strengthens in-group solidarity and inter-group differentiation, with the fitness benefit accruing to the group rather than to the individual signaler.

Fifth, the abundance-error hypothesis: such behaviors are maladaptive cognitive spillovers from environments of resource surplus, carrying no reliable signaling value and persisting only because selection against them is weak.

In your evaluation, treat the escrow model as a serious theoretical contender rather than a strawman, and specifically address whether it offers explanatory leverage beyond what the handicap principle already provides for the three patterns listed.
