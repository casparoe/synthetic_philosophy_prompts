Write a poem of exactly sixteen lines, divided into four quatrains, structured as an alternating dialogue between two voices—call them A and B—so that A speaks lines 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, and 15, and B speaks lines 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16. Use an interlocked rhyme scheme across the voices: within each quatrain, A’s lines rhyme with B’s lines (for example, A1 rhymes with B2, B1 rhymes with A2), so that no single voice can complete a rhyme on its own; the sonic coherence depends on both being present. The philosophical subject is collective intentionality and joint action, rendered specifically as the tension between an irreducible plural-subject view—where two or more agents are jointly committed to an intention as a single body, generating obligations that are not decomposable into individual ones—and a reductionist view that treats collective action as merely individual actions with meshing subplans and shared beliefs. Have the poem begin with both voices describing the same physical task—say, lifting a table together—from strictly first-person singular perspectives, using singular pronouns and discrete sensory details. In the second and third quatrains, let the syntax blur: pronouns should slide from “I” and “you” toward an ambiguous “we,” and the images should become ones neither voice can claim exclusively (the weight distributed, the stairwell narrowing). In the final quatrain, either let the rhyme scheme require both voices to complete its closing couplet, or let it deliberately fail to close within one voice, so that the form itself enacts whether the collective intention holds together or collapses back into aggregation. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of action recognizes the distinction between joint commitment and meshed individual planning, but it should render these ideas through image, rhythm, and pronoun rather than through exposition or named doctrines. Do not include footnotes, citations, or references to specific texts; keep the tone formal and precise, and leave the resolution of the collective action—whether the table reaches the landing or is dropped—genuinely open.
