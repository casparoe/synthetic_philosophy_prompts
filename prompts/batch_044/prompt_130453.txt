Write a joke about exclusionary zoning and the philosophical idea of a right to the city. To work, the joke needs to turn on a specific conceptual tension: Henri Lefebvre framed the right to the city as a collective, non-proprietary entitlement to participate in producing urban space, not as individual property rights; meanwhile, NIMBY objections to upzoning often treat neighborhood character as a kind of private aesthetic entitlement; and YIMBY counter-arguments typically defend densification in purely market-efficiency terms that erase the collective, anti-commodified dimension of Lefebvre's idea. Make it funny and coherent to someone who knows both Lefebvre's distinction between habitat and inhabiting and the basic economics of zoning-induced supply constraints, but avoid generic jokes about people hating apartments near them.
