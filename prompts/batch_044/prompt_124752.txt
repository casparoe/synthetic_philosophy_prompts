I'm taking this phil of science / philosophy of mind course and we got onto this weird parallel between Darwin and Turing. My study partner summed up the comparison for our study group, and I'm not sure she's being fair to either thinker. She wrote:

"Darwin showed that biological competence—like an eye tracking light—can emerge without any comprehending designer behind it. Nature doesn't understand optics, but selection builds working eyes anyway. Turing made the miror-image move: he designed a test where a computer displays linguistic competence, holding a conversation, even though there's no internal comprehension or mental states inside the machine. So Darwin takes the mind out of natural achievement, and Turing takes it out of artificial achievement. Together they prove that comprehension is never necessary for competence—it's just an optional extra that can come from evolutionary pressure or from code. That's also why Searle's Chinese Room objection fails: Searle assumes you need comprehension for real language use, but Turing already demonstrated that linguistic behavior can happen without it."

I'm definatly not convinced the parallel holds up both directions, and I think she might be overreading Turing.
