Analyze South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission amnesty process—specifically the hearings before the Amnesty Committee in which individual perpetrators applied for immunity in exchange for full disclosure of politically motivated acts—through the conceptual lens of transactional forgiveness: the idea that forgiveness can be structured as a bilateral exchange in which one party offers truthful disclosure and the other offers release from punishment, with each party able to hold the other to the terms of the deal. First, map the institutional mechanics onto this model: identify the two parties, specify what counts as consideration on each side, and determine whether victims (or their representatives) had any standing to withhold the "forgiveness" side of the exchange if disclosure was inadequate. Second, show where the transactional framing breaks down: was the state acting as an agent for victims, could victims veto an amnesty grant, and what happens when disclosure is technically complete but morally hollow or strategically partial? Third, say what the TRC case reveals about the coherence of transactional forgiveness itself—whether it collapses into a unilateral state pardon disguised as exchange, or into an unconditional act of grace that happens to be accompanied by disclosure. Be disciplined about the mapping: name the actual institutional roles (the Amnesty Committee, the Committee on Human Rights Violations, the legal effect of a granted amnesty under the Promotion of National Unity and Reconciliation Act), and say explicitly where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
