ok so im a retired biologist who got into political philosophy lately and i keep noticing this pattern in debates about who belongs to the demos. say youre arguing that wild animals should be included in environmental decisions because theyre affected by them. but then to figure out what they want, you need a human mediator — maybe an ecologist or some algorithim — who translates the animals interests into policy language. and that translation changes the interest itself: the wolf's 'preference' becomes whatever the mediator frames as survival or welfare, which might not match what the wolf actually experiences. same thing with future generations in climate policy: we include them because theyll suffer from our choices, but we model their preferences using current economic tools, which shapes which future people actually get created. so theres a loop: you include X in the demos because X has interests, but the act of including X requires filtering those interests through a representational system that alters them, sometimes even altering who X is. is there an established name for this exact loop? its near the boundary problem and touches the non-identity problem, but its not either of those. if theres no term, can you name and define it precisely — and tell me where it breaks apart from proxy voting, trustee models, and plain old misrepresentation? i wont be satisfied with just a bare label; i want to know exactly what it is, what it isnt, and where its edges are.
