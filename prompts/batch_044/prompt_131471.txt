Write a joke about the axiom of choice. The punchline should turn on the conceptual tension between accepting non-constructive existence—specifically that AC licenses selecting an element from each set in an infinite family without supplying any rule for how the selection is made—and the intuitive resistance to the Banach-Tarski paradox, in which a solid ball is partitioned into finitely many pieces that can be rigidly moved to reassemble into two identical copies. Frame it around a constructivist being asked to demonstrate the decomposition at a dinner party, and make the refusal depend on the absence of an explicit choice function rather than on geometric implausibility alone. Keep it intelligible to someone with basic set-theoretic literacy but do not invoke named authorities, citation-style references, or specialized results beyond AC and Banach-Tarski.
