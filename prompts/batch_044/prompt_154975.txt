As a doctoral student in philosophy of biology working on the conceptual foundations of molecular self-replication, I want you to draft a joint adversarial-collaboration statement for a debate I am mediating between two interpretive camps on the DNA–Gödel isomorphism. The first camp, the Structural Realists, holds that genomic replication is not merely analogous to Gödelian self-reference but actually instantiates it: the genome encodes instructions for its own copying machinery in a manner structurally isomorphic to Gödel numbering, and this has philosophical and potentially empirical consequences. The second camp, the Deflationary Mechanists, grants the formal correspondence but denies that any Gödelian properties—unprovability, semantic self-reference, or logical incompleteness—are present in biochemical replication, which they treat as purely causal-mechanistic.

Please produce the document both camps could sign. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims, including: (a) the precise formal mapping at issue—encoding of replication instructions within the genome, feedback architecture of polymerase proofreading, and the structural parallel to arithmetic self-reference—stated in neutral terms; (b) an accurate, non-technical statement of what Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem establishes in formal arithmetic, without mischaracterization; (c) the empirically uncontroversial facts about DNA replication, error correction, and the physical separation of genotype and replication machinery that both sides accept; (d) an explicit shared acknowledgment that the dispute is interpretive, not about whether replication occurs; and (e) a brief mutual recognition that formal analogies between logical systems and biological processes have historically been either productive or misleading, with each side permitted to name one example in their own wording.

Second, a disagreements table. For each camp, provide the statement of their core thesis in wording that camp itself would accept; their view on whether “self-reference” in DNA requires semantic interpretation or can be purely syntactic; and their position on whether the isomorphism generates empirically distinguishable predictions rather than merely redescribing known biochemistry. The table must be neutral: each entry should read as something its proponents would actually endorse.

Third, identify exactly three cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. I take the central ones to be: (i) whether a replication system can treat its own error-correction mechanism as an object of correction without external reference, a problem I will call “autocatalytic closure”; (ii) a concrete synthetic-biology test case in which the polymerase-coding sequence of a minimal genome is relocated to a non-coding regulatory region, so that replication fidelity depends on a loop that attempts to correct errors in the very mechanism performing correction—each side must say whether the expected failure mode is structurally Gödelian (an unresolvable loop of self-correction) or merely biochemical (resource depletion, kinetic saturation, or thermodynamic collapse), and whether either pattern would count as evidence for their view; and (iii) whether logical vocabulary such as “truth,” “provability,” or “incompleteness” can be applied to molecular states without category error.

Fourth, for each crux, specify: the argument, hypothetical experiment, or case both sides agree is relevant; the prediction of the Structural Realists; and the prediction of the Deflationary Mechanists. For crux (ii), be explicit about the synthetic-genome scenario: state the design, the loop structure, the observable collapse signatures each camp expects, and what either camp would regard as falsifying their position.

Fifth, a concluding section separating genuine disagreement from verbal disagreement. Specify which portions of the dispute concern empirical predictions (crux ii), which concern conceptual framework and category boundaries (crux iii), and which—if any—dissolve once “self-reference,” “encoding,” and “incompleteness” are precisely disambiguated. If you conclude that part of the debate is verbal, say exactly which part and why.

Do not invoke specific named theorists or texts unless you are certain of attribution; refer instead to “the structural-realist interpretation” and “the deflationary interpretation.” Do not use verbatim quotation marks for any real text; paraphrase without quotation marks. The prompt should be rigorous and conceptually specific enough that it could not be satisfied by regurgitating standard philosophy-of-biology tropes, yet it should not depend on obscure empirical results that require specialist database retrieval to confirm. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to a doctoral student addressing peers in philosophy of science and theoretical biology.
