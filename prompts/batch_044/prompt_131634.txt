A blog post I came across summarizes Hilary Putnam’s Twin Earth argument for semantic externalism as follows. Putnam asks us to imagine Twin Earth, physically identical to Earth except that the clear liquid its inhabitants call water is not H2O but a different compound, XYZ. Oscar on Earth and his molecule-for-molecule duplicate Twin Oscar share every intrinsic psychological state and both sincerely say that water is wet, yet Oscar’s term refers to H2O while Twin Oscar’s refers to XYZ. The post claims this proves that meaning is never in the head in any respect: internal psychological states play literally zero role in fixing reference, which is determined solely by causal interaction with the external environment. The author then extends this to argue that since mental duplicates can differ in every belief involving natural kinds, there is no such thing as narrow psychological content at all—not even for mathematics or self-knowledge—and that Putnam thereby refutes any form of internalism about mental content. The post also asserts that Putnam’s division of linguistic labor is just an optional add-on that plays no real work in the argument. I suspect this overreaches in several places, particularly since I recall that Putnam allowed experts to fix reference for ordinary speakers and that he originally restricted the claim to natural-kind terms, leaving other mental content untouched; I also wonder whether later two-dimensionalists tried to preserve narrow content precisely by conceding Putnam’s point about reference while denying it applies to cognitive significance.
