Analyze the divergence between European statutory bans on commercial surrogacy and egg donation—notably in France, Germany, and Italy—and the American state-level commercial market through the lens of moral outsourcing: the claim that domestic prohibition coupled with permissive cross-border access does not eliminate moral complicity but spatially displaces it. Identify the key actors in both regimes, the options and incentives each faces, and what the framework predicts about cross-border reproductive travel and about the regulatory distinction between altruistic and commercial arrangements. Then identify precisely where the framework breaks: does it explain why some European states permit altruistic surrogacy, why American clinics actively market to European clients, or why prohibition persists despite clear displacement? Conclude by saying whether the episode supports, undermines, or transforms the outsourcing thesis, and whether the American market internalizes the moral problem differently rather than avoiding it.
