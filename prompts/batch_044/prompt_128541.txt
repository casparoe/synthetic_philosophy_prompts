Draft the joint statement that a committed Source Critic and a committed Archive Skeptic could both sign after arguing in good faith about how we know anything about the distant past, from Herodotus and Thucydides through modern archaeology. I want a numbered section of agreed claims—about what cross-checking testimony actually involves, about the kinds of gaps that survive in the record, about the difference between direct observation and inference across centuries—followed by a disagreements table with each side's exact position in wording they would accept. Then list the cruxes: I suspect one is whether the absence of contradictory sources counts as genuine corroboration or merely as an artifact of survival, and another is whether material traces can ever be interpreted without importing present-day categories. For each crux, name a concrete historical case or type of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and write down in advance what each camp predicts that case shows. Keep the framing focused on the methods—weighing physical remains, accounting for silence in the archive, ruling out bias—rather than on bibliographies or specialist theories.
