A colleague writes opinion pieces for a widely read online platform under a pseudonym. He maintains that, because his articles reach thousands of readers and generate collective discussion, he possesses a form of public authority; at the same time, he insists that anonymity is essential to his candour and refuses to defend his claims in any setting where he is personally identifiable. He treats aggregate engagement metrics—shares, comments, and algorithmic amplification—as a democratic validation of his views, describing himself as merely one node in a distributed conversation for which no individual bears responsibility. When I suggested that this allows him to wield influence without bearing the corresponding burden of accountability, he replied that this is simply how contemporary public discourse functions. I feel there is a deeper philosophical tension here, but I cannot articulate it precisely. Is his position genuinely defensible, or does it exemplify a particular kind of moral and existential problem?
