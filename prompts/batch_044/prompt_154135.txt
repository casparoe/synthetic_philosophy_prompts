I'm working with a small research group that operates at the intersection of historical methodology and the kind of epistemic hygiene common in rationalist forums, and I want a reusable decision procedure for classifying whether a legendary narrative contains a recoverable historical kernel, should be treated as a retrojected historical echo, or functions only as a structural analog to actual events. The procedure must be calibrated against three reference cases: the Trojan War tradition (where excavation at Hisarlik reveals a destroyed settlement that correlates imperfectly with the Homeric narrative), the Arthurian cycle (where near-total contemporary silence contrasts with massive later accretion), and the Exodus narrative (where direct archaeological correlation with the specific itinerary is absent, yet the textual tradition may encode collective memory).

I need the procedure structured as an ordered sequence of questions that a practitioner can run without specialist archival access. For each stage, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed, what answer forces a halt, and where the decision must be handed off to judgment rather than fact. Build in an explicit three-way disambiguation: a "historical kernel" requires evidence that a specific event generated the narrative core; a "historical echo" requires evidence that a later event was projected backward onto an earlier setting; a "structural analog" requires showing that the narrative pattern fits the era's conditions but describes no particular occurrence. Include concrete tests that distinguish these — for instance, how to evaluate anachronistic detail that could indicate retrojection versus mythologization versus analogical fitting, and how to weigh negative archaeological space against positive textual patterns.

The stopping points should classify outcomes into: probable kernel with recoverable distortions; unrecoverable kernel (the event may be real but the narrative is too corrupted); echo (a later event mislocated); analog only; and false-positive risk (why correlation with material remains might still fail to establish causal generation). Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed as procedure, particularly around weighing silence in the record versus absence of evidence, and flag where the procedure will misfire if applied to narratives with strong theological or national functions.

Then apply the procedure to two worked cases: first, the Arthurian cycle, where I expect it to halt at analog or unrecoverable kernel and want to see exactly which question stops it and why the echo test fails; second, the Exodus narrative, where the procedure should encounter ambiguity between echo, analog, and absent kernel, and I want to see how the disambiguation tests behave when archaeological correlation is missing but textual structure and collective-memory markers are present.

Finally, state clearly what this procedure cannot do — it should not generate bibliographies or reading lists, it should not settle theological claims about the narrative's meaning, and it should leave out source-critical philology and stratigraphic interpretation at this operational level — and include one check question at the end that would reveal whether a user has actually grasped the difference between identifying a kernel and identifying an echo, rather than merely labeling outcomes.
