So I'm a retired biologist who picked up Schopenhaur's World as Will and Representation after a friend mentioned his idea that music is the direct objectification of the Will — not like painting or poetry which copy Platonic Ideas. I've been testing this against my own listening and I'm getting contradictory gut feelings. Here are my verdicts. One, when I hear Beethoven's Ninth I feel it's expressing the structure of striving itself, not a picture of triumph or joy. Two, if I find out the composer was depressed during composition, that doesn't change what the music expresses; the emotional content seems independant of the creator's mood. Three, there's now AI that can generate a Bach-style fugue note-perfect; to me it sounds hollow, like it's copying the acoustic shape without the underlying thing. Four, film music that manipulates me into crying during a sad scene feels different — more like poetry or painting, using association rather than pure musical expression. Five, a cold algorithmic drone with no emotional contour actually strikes me as expressing the blind mechanical Will quite purely, maybe purer than Beethoven. But six, a rough folk recording made by someone with no theory, just raw grief, feels more genuinely Schopenhaurian than the AI fugue. So I'm torn between thinking the key is human consciousness behind the sound, or absence of representational content, or maybe emotional rawness. Is there a principle that captures all six, and if not which one should I drop? And what would it predict for a computer-generated piece designed specifically to have zero emotional contour and no human composer — just raw acoustic physics?
