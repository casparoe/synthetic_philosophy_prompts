I have been reading Augustine's argument that lying is never permissible—not even to save a life—because it corrupts the speaker's soul, and I am trying to map out exactly where I and others stand on the boundary cases that seem to challenge him. Please construct a decision tree (not a list of named schools) that sorts positions on this question: if telling the literal truth will directly cause severe, unjust harm to an innocent person, what options remain inside or outside Augustine's framework? The root node should be that very scenario. From there, branch by the claims that actually split people: whether saying something technically true but deliberately misleading—mental reservation—counts as a lie in Augustine's sense; whether a false statement told to shield someone from an aggressor is morally the same kind of act as a lie told for personal gain; whether the damage to one's own soul from lying is always weightier than the physical harm prevented; and whether Augustine's own allowance for joking falsehoods and hyperbole can be stretched to cover protective falsehoods without destroying his view. Each leaf should carry a name if one exists (for example, strict Augustinian, mental-reservationist, casuist exception, virtue-based revision) or be marked unnamed, plus one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—specifically what it must say about either the liar's soul or the duty to the victim. Please also note any combinations of answers that are logically open but apparently unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect; and flag any pair of leaves that the tree's questions cannot genuinely distinguish. I do not need bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—this is a conceptual map, not a literature review. Finally, please separate what is firmly established in Augustine's texts from what is genuinely contested among people who study him, and do not present a disputed reading—such as whether he permits mental reservation—as though it were the consensus view.
