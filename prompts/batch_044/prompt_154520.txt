I am an undergraduate philosophy student currently enrolled in an advanced seminar on practical reason and normative ethics, and I serve as the student representative on the University’s Undergraduate Research Ethics and Conduct Committee. The committee is divided over Case 2024-17, in which a student researcher in a cross-departmental survey study transmitted anonymized response data to an unaffiliated external analyst without securing a formal IRB amendment. The researcher does not deny the procedural breach; instead, she argues that an ideally rational deliberator—fully informed about the negligible re-identification risk, the scientific value of the collaboration, and the absence of harm—would have endorsed the data-sharing, and that sanctioning her for violating a direct procedural rule therefore applies the wrong normative framework. Some members propose that the committee adopt an indirect-normative standard: rather than applying rules directly to actions, we should determine what a suitably idealized agent would have done under conditions of full rationality and information, and treat that counterfactual endorsement as the governing norm for evaluating conduct. Others object that this collapses institutional accountability into speculative psychology and undermines the predictability required for ethical oversight.

Draft a formal policy memo addressed to the committee chair recommending whether we should (a) reject the indirect-normative framework and uphold the direct procedural sanction in Case 2024-17, or (b) adopt the framework and overturn or reduce the sanction, with reasoning that applies to both the immediate case and our charter. Your memo must reach an actionable bottom line—specifically, a clear recommendation on the case and on whether the committee should institutionalize the framework—while giving an honest and substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. In constructing your argument, draw on the distinction between direct and indirect normativity, the role of idealized deliberation in fixing reasons for action, and the institutional costs of substituting counterfactual endorsement for explicit rule-following. Write in a tone appropriate for an institutional review body, not as personal guidance to me.
