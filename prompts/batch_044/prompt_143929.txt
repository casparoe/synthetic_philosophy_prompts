I serve on my company's product ethics review board; my background is in software engineering, not philosophy. Our team proposes a conversational interface that detects user frustration through typing cadence and response-latency patterns, then collapses complex menus into a single prominent "quick resolve" option that defaults to upgrading the user to a premium tier. Proponents call this legitimate persuasion—reducing cognitive load precisely when the user is overwhelmed—while opponents argue it exploits a detected emotional vulnerability without explicit disclosure, amounting to manipulation. The board must issue a concrete recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—by Friday, with reasoning that the engineering team can actually act on, including a serious engagement with the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please deliver this as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: item one should state the bottom-line recommendation; item two should address autonomy and the disclosure obligation; item three should distinguish this practice from ordinary interface nudging; item four should present the best argument for the opposing view and why it does not prevail; and item five should specify any concrete conditions, safeguards, or timeline tied to the recommendation.
