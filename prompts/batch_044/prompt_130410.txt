Two philosophers debate whether counterpart theory or transworld identity better explains modal persistence for artifacts—specifically, a bronze statue that could survive being melted and recast. Adjudicate their exchange move by move: rate each step from clearly mistaken to clearly right, then say who is more right overall and what both miss.

(1) The counterpart-theory side says her view is superior because it permits the statue to exist in a world where the original bronze is melted and reshaped into a vase, as long as there is an appropriate counterpart with the correct artistic origin and form, without requiring the same bronze atoms.

(2) The transworld-identity side replies that counterpart theory fails for artifacts because the counterpart relation is arbitrarily stipulable: one could relate the statue to a marble replica of the same form, showing the theory smuggles identity conditions into the relation rather than explaining them.

(3) The first philosopher responds that transworld identity is worse: if the statue is strictly identical across worlds, then in any world where the bronze is melted, the statue is destroyed and only bronze remains, making it contradictory for the statue itself to survive reshaping.

(4) The second philosopher counters that this misrepresents her position. She can invoke modal parts: in the reshaping world the statue-modal-part is absent but the bronze-modal-part persists, so the object continues without contradiction.

(5) The first philosopher says this merely renames the problem—if a statue-part can be absent while the object persists, then the persisting object was never genuinely the statue, and modal parts do not solve the artifact case any better than counterparts do.

Please evaluate each of the five moves. Give an overall verdict on which framework handles this case better, and identify the deepest issue both parties neglect.
