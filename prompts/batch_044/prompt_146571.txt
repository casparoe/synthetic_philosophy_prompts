After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend last evening about Wittgenstein's rule-following remarks and the interpretation Kripke gives them, I have settled on a view I want to stress-test. I hold that the skeptical problem about rule-following is not genuinely solved by either the straight solution or the skeptical solution, but is instead dissolved once we recognize that the underlying demand—that there must be a fact that determines in advance every possible application of a rule—rests on a mistaken assimilation of normative practices to descriptive or predictive mechanisms. More precisely, I defend the claim that rule-following is essentially a matter of normative projection: the practitioner does not merely extrapolate from past dispositions or await communal correction, but actively extends a normative standard into genuinely open-ended future cases in a way that is irreducible to either private mental content or social sanction. On my view, Kripke's skeptical solution fails not because it abandons private mental states, but because it renders normativity essentially retrospective, leaving unexplained how any practice could genuinely innovate or extend itself into domains where no prior communal agreement has been established. I concede that this commits me to treating normative projection as a primitive feature of rational agency, and I am aware that this may look like an unexplained explainer; still, I think it is preferable to reducing normativity to something non-normative.

What, then, would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which arguments or intuitions—perhaps about the possibility of private rule-following in isolated agents, or about the explanatory priority of social correction, or about formal results in the logic of normativity—would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Are there conceptual results that show normative projection either collapses into a form of dispositionalism or requires a metaphysical commitment I have tried to avoid? I also want to know whether defenders of the skeptical solution would themselves concede that retrospective agreement cannot account for genuinely novel application, or whether they have resources to deny that novelty requires projection. Finally, is my position well insulated against falsification—for instance, by defining normative projection negatively, as whatever is left when reduction fails—and if so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a defect? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely uncomfortable, and indicate which ones I should take as personally compelling given that my commitment is primarily to the irreducibility of forward-directed normativity rather than to any specific metaphysics of rules.
