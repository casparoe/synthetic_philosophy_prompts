After an extended argument with a friend about Jain anekāntavāda—specifically whether syādvāda resolves contradiction by multiplying the aspects of reality or by multiplying the standpoints from which reality is accessed—I have realized we keep talking past each other because we have never mapped the actual logical space of positions. I want you to construct a taxonomy in the form of a decision tree or flowchart that sorts views on "many-sided truth" not by the labels people prefer but by the concrete answers they give to diagnostic cases. The tree should begin with a single starting commitment: that the claim "the self is permanent and also subject to change" can be genuinely asserted without contradiction. From there, each branch node should pose a question that a respondent can answer without knowing which philosophical position it corresponds to—questions about whether the resolution depends on partitioning the object itself into co-existing aspects, on partitioning the conditions of access or observation, or on treating the predicates as implicitly relativized to an unstated qualifier.

I want the leaves to include: a classical Jain view using the seven-fold predication structure, a modern perspectival-realist analog, an epistemic-humility view that denies any objective multifacetedness, and a semantic-contextualist position that treats the contradiction as dissolved by hidden indexicals. You must also inject and clearly separate a distinction I have been using informally in our debate: "aspect-pluralism," on which the object itself bears irreducibly many co-real faces, versus "modal-pluralism," on which there is a single face genuinely seen under irreducibly different conditions of access; make sure the tree distinguishes these even when they converge on identical verbal formulas, and flag any combination—such as aspect-pluralism paired with a strictly bivalent truth-valuation for every individual standpoint—that appears unoccupied, briefly diagnosing whether the gap is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or suggested readings, and phrase every node as a concrete case or claim rather than as an invitation to identify an author or text.
