The student’s answer reads thus. Dr. Reyes clearly violates Williamson’s knowledge norm because she admits she does not know that the temperature will exceed 1.5°C by 2035. Williamson holds that assertion expresses knowledge, so to assert without knowing is analogous to promising without the intention to fulfil the promise—both involve a kind of conventional misuse. The belief norm, by contrast, requires only rational belief, so under that norm Dr. Reyes is fully justified. However, a serious problem remains: if beliefs can change rationally over time in response to new evidence, then Dr. Reyes might assert p today and not-p tomorrow without contradiction, which would make her testimony unreliable. This is structurally similar to a money-pump in decision theory, where intransitive preferences allow an agent to be exploited in a cycle; to prevent this, diachronic rationality requires that beliefs remain consistent across time, and this consistency condition saves the belief norm from the reliability objection.
