I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy working on comparative epistemology and classical Chinese philosophy, and I have been trying to formalize an argument that came up in my dissertation seminar on skeptical structures in the Zhuangzi. A colleague proposed an extension of the butterfly dream that I think proves far more than the text licenses, yet I cannot pinpoint the exact failure. The argument runs as follows. (1) In the butterfly passage, Zhuangzi asserts a strict phenomenal symmetry: there is no first-person internal marker that distinguishes being Zhuangzi who dreamt he was a butterfly from being a butterfly now dreaming he is Zhuangzi. (2) If phenomenal symmetry holds, then any principle that privileges waking consciousness over dreaming must appeal to an external, post-hoc criterion—specifically, greater temporal coherence, causal continuity, and intersubjective stability. (3) But coherence, continuity, and stability are themselves features accessible only from within one of the competing narratives; they cannot serve as a neutral arbiter without begging the question against the dream-hypothesis. (4) Hence there is no non-circular justification available for the claim that one is currently awake. (5) Generalizing from this single case: for any present conscious state S, there exists a phenomenally identical dream-state S* embedded in a competing narrative of equal internal coherence. (6) Therefore, for any empirical belief B formed in S, there corresponds a belief B* formed in S* with identical phenomenal warrant but contradictory content—e.g., that I am sitting in a seminar room versus that I am a butterfly resting on a leaf. (7) If identical warrant supports contradictory propositions, then that warrant is undermined for both; consequently, no belief formed in S achieves genuine justification. (8) By recursive application, this undermines even the report of the butterfly dream itself, since Zhuangzi's narration is an empirical belief formed in some state that is itself subject to steps (5) through (7).

What makes the argument suspicious is that it does not merely reproduce Cartesian dream skepticism but collapses into a stronger, self-undermining form: it seems to dissolve the very textual ground from which it is launched. I have considered blocking it at step (3) by arguing that coherence is not assessed from a transcendent viewpoint but is constitutive of the state itself, or at step (7) by denying that contradictory contents automatically cancel warrant if the beliefs are indexically anchored to different narrative frames—what one might call narrative-relative realism. Yet I worry that either move saves empirical knowledge only by conceding that justification is pragmatic and frame-internal, which feels like abandoning the realist intuition that waking life is genuinely more authoritative, not merely more useful. Which step do you think is the weakest? If the argument is in fact valid, does the recursive self-undermining in (8) function as a reductio ad absurdum, or is there a coherent Zhuangzian position—perhaps a quietism about justification altogether—that accepts the conclusion without contradiction? I would particularly value an answer that addresses whether phenomenal symmetry alone can license the induction in (5) without smuggling in an unjustified assumption about the modal accessibility of dream-states.
