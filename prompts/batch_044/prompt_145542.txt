I'm working through my intuitions about temporal becoming versus the block universe, especially in light of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument, and I'd like to see if there's a single principle that captures them.

Here are my verdicts on concrete cases. First, standard special-relativistic simultaneity disagreement: observer A judges event X simultaneous with her typing, while observer B in motion judges X past. I think neither makes a physical error, which suggests "now" is frame-relative, not that X is both present and past. Second, Rietdijk's slide: if every spacetime point exists equally, then Caesar crossing the Rubicon is happening right now in the same ontological sense that I am typing. That feels wrong—like a category mistake between existence and presentness—but I can't formalize the mistake. Third, Putnam's Andromeda-paradox variant: walking toward Andromeda shifts my "now" slice over distant events I previously called future. I take this as an indexical shift, like "here" changing when I walk, not as proof those distant events are already actualized. Fourth, a constructed case: two observers sit in separate, non-interacting gravitational wells with no shared cosmic rest frame, each trying to define a local privileged foliation via her own well's geometry; they never agree on simultaneity, and there is no global frame to adjudicate. I want to say each still has a local fact about which events are genuinely present, but that sounds like multiplying ontologies without need. Fifth, the twin-paradox version with becoming: if one twin ages less, and becoming is real, then during separation there ought to be a fact about whose "present" is further along world-time, yet relativity denies absolute world-time. I think this undermines becoming, yet I still feel my own future pain is not already happening. Sixth, the unsettled case: in a Gödel universe with closed timelike curves, an observer can shake hands with her earlier self. If the block is true, this is just two points on a loop. If becoming is real, her past self should have "already become" past and be unavailable. I lean that the handshake is physically possible, hence the block view wins—but then I can't explain why I care more about my future suffering than about Caesar's.

Is there one coherent principle—maybe about indexicality, ontological parsimony, or normative asymmetry—that delivers all six verdicts without ad hoc patches? If the answer forces me to drop one, which should go? And what does the cleanest principle predict for my unsettled Gödel handshake case?
