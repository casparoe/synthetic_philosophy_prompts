My friend deployed it to claim that the choice is not merely hard but fundamentally arbitrary, akin to a coin flip, because no common measure could possibly adjudicate between them.

Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan might express, arranged from the trivial to the radical? In particular, distinguish the modest claim that values lack a shared scale from the stronger claim that no second-order consideration—such as narrative coherence, volitional commitment, or practical identity—can rationally structure the decision without first measuring the values against one another. I want a disambiguated menu I can bring back to the argument, not a single favored interpretation, and please indicate which readings my friend actually needs for his arbitrariness claim to go through.
