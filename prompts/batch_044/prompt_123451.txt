If we treat the hole argument not as a closed case against manifold substantivalism but as a live ontological constraint that applies to any diffeomorphism-invariant theory, then several open questions become urgent rather than historical. Some concern whether the argument retains its force in discrete or quantum-gravitational settings where smooth diffeomorphisms do not apply; others ask whether its conclusion depends on interpreting general covariance as a gauge redundancy rather than as a dynamical symmetry of the field equations; and still others probe how it interacts with relational structuralisms that identify spacetime not with a manifold of points but with an invariant relational architecture. For someone with about three years, training in metaphysics and logic rather than in theoretical physics, which two or three of these lines should take priority? Defend the ordering by upstream dependence—what must be settled before the others can be addressed—by tractability without requiring a full resolution of the problem of time in canonical quantum gravity, and by what kind of argument or conceptual clarification over the next few years would actually move a question up or down the list. I am less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda: in particular, is resolving the gauge-symmetry interpretation of the hole argument upstream of deciding the ontology of discrete spacetime, or does the dependency run the other way, and which of these, if any, is a dead end unless one first takes a stance on background independence?
