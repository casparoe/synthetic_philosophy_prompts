I've been reading about Ian Hacking's looping effects in psychiatric classification, and I keep running into what seems like a straightforward implication: if a diagnostic category like autism spectrum disorder actually reshapes how people understand and present themselves—so that being classified produces some of the very behaviors the category claims only to describe—then the condition can't be a genuine biological disorder but must be a purely social artifact created by the label itself. Given that, wouldn't the correct ethical and clinical response be to abandon such classifications altogether? After all, if the loop is what generates the symptoms, breaking it by withholding the diagnosis should eliminate the disorder rather than leaving patients without treatment. Where is the error in this reasoning?
