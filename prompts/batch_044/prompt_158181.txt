ok so for a seminar on moral responsibility i'm trying to lay out my view on standing and hypocrisy and want to see if it's coherent or a mess.

here's where i am:

(1) i think standing to blame is basically about authority — you can't blame someone for F if you're self-exempting from the same norm, because then you're not speaking from a position of commitment to it.

(2) but it's graded and domain-seperate: being sloppy with citations doesn't rob you of standing to blame plagarism, and minor hypocrisy only weakens rather than destroys it.

(3) i think a genuinely reformed wrongdoer — say someone who was addicted and is now sober for years — has full standing to blame current addicts, because authority tracks present commitment, not biography.

(4) at the same time i feel like clean hands means never having been in the same boat; if you lied in the past, your blame of a current liar always carries a whiff of self-distancing, so maybe reform doesn't fully restore it.

(5) i think unwitting hypocrisy — blaming for F while guilty of F but not realizing it — actually destroys standing more thoroughly than knowing hypocrisy, because it reveals you lack the self-monitoring that authority requires.

(6) i'm convinced evolutionary debunking can explain why hypocrisy feels specially bad — coalition policing — but i don't think that undermines the normative claim that it really does weaken standing.

(7) i also think blame can be warranted even without standing; standing just determines wheather the target can legitimately reject it, not wheather the blamer is justified.

so: which pairs are actually pulling against each other? specifically i'm suspicious of (3) vs (4) and (1) vs (5), but i might be wrong about where the damage is. rank the tensions by severity, say for each what has to give, and tell me if there's an apparent conflict that dissolves once you look closer. also flag the cheapest single revision that fixes the most.
