Consider the institutional practice in contemporary evolutionary genomics of annotating every non-coding DNA sequence as either 'functionally constrained'—and therefore presumed to be under purifying selection serving some adaptive or at least selectively maintained purpose—or 'neutral'—treated as evolutionary noise free to accumulate mutations without phenotypic consequence. What philosophical commitments about evolutionary explanation does this binary classification presuppose? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it comfortably, assess whether it is compatible with a robust spandrel-based view of genome architecture, and describe what would have to change in annotation protocols and funding criteria if evolutionary biology took Gould and Lewontin's critique seriously as an organising principle rather than as a marginal exception.
