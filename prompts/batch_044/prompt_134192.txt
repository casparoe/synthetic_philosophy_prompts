The deal is: they recruit clients from countries where commercial surrogacy is banned (mostly EU stautes) and fly them here for gestational surrogacy. The consultancy structures all payments as 'post-birth emotional labor stipends' paid to the surrogate, not fees for the baby, so the clients can claim back home that they never bought reproductive services. Our legal team says this skirts US law but might violate the spirit of European anti-commodification stautes, and there's a risk the clinic gets named in a future extradition or sanctions dispute. Meanwhile patient advocates on staff say we're just exporting a market that Europe rightly banned to protect surrogates from economic coercion, and that the 'stipend' framing is obvious fiction. Others argue that banning the partnership just pushes clients to less scrupulous clinics and denies surrogates here better protections. The board meets in two weeks. Please draft the memo I'll circulate: recommend whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions, include a concrete timeline for implementation or wind-down, and take seriously the argument that we're essentially laundering a banned market through linguistic tricks. Don't just cite policy — actually wrestle with whether the 'emotional labor' framing changes the moral picture or is pure euphemism. Keep it to about 600 words.
