I have no formal training in philosophy, but after listening to a podcast that tried to settle an old debate I have been turning it over on my own. From this Malcolm concluded that talk about dreaming is not really describing inner events that occur during sleep; it is closer to a learned manner of speaking upon waking that lacks proper verification conditions. Daniel Dennett then objected, according to the host, that Malcolm was still assuming a “Cartesian theater” — a private mental stage on which experiences must be displayed in order to count as real. Dennett offered his “multiple drafts” model instead: consciousness is just competing neural narratives being edited over time, with no single canonical stream. Because of this, the host said, Dennett showed that dreams are indeed real experiences after all — only they are distributed across brain processes rather than projected onto an inner screen — and Malcolm’s verification demand collapses because there is no one definitive “show” to check. The host finished by claiming that Dennett therefore dissolved Malcolm’s problem rather than solving it, leaving us free to treat dream reports as describing actual mental events.
