As a retired molecular biologist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science, I want to specify what an adequate theory of evolutionary explanation must deliver in light of the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique. I am not asking for a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by reference. Instead, write the desiderata: the full specification of requirements that any such theory must satisfy, with the motivating empirical or conceptual case behind each one. The theory must explain clear adaptations—such as the vertebrate eye—without licensing the inference that every trait is adaptive. It must accommodate genuine spandrels, structural byproducts that carry no present selective value, without reclassifying them as exaptations or as adaptations serving hidden functions. It must preserve the distinction between selection for a trait and selection of a trait. It must remain consistent with evolutionary developmental biology regarding deep homology and developmental canalization. In addition, include the requirement that the theory account for what I call epistatic spandrels: traits that are not directly selected but are developmentally locked to strongly selected regulatory networks, persisting despite being selectively neutral or even mildly deleterious. Identify which pairs of desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether demanding both strong predictive specificity for adaptive explanations and complete openness to non-adaptive alternatives renders the framework unfalsifiable. Then, briefly, indicate how strict pan-adaptationism, the Gould–Lewontin structuralist program, and a pluralist evo-devo synthesis fare against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than on the scorecard. Write formally, as a specification for philosophical discussion.
