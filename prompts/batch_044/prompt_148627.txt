I have just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about the metaphysics of Ubuntu and whether relational conceptions of personhood actually entail that social relations constitute the self, or merely nurture a pre-existing capacity. She defends a thick relationalist position—drawing on Ifeanyi Menkiti and Desmond Tutu’s interpretation of the proverb that a person is a person through other people—to claim that without sustained communal recognition a human being literally lacks full moral and ontological personhood. I defend a thinner relational view: that personhood is an innate potentiality that relational contexts actualize and recognize, not a status wholly generated by them. We have agreed to try an adversarial collaboration and want a document both of us could sign in good faith, without either of us feeling that our core commitments have been misrepresented.

Please draft our joint statement. Structure the response exactly as follows, with approximate lengths: (1) Agreed Claims (numbered list, about 300 words)—include shared commitments on the failure of Cartesian atomism, on the descriptive importance of communal interdependence in many African societies, on what developmental psychology reveals about severe social isolation, and on the ambiguity of personhood across normative and metaphysical registers; (2) Disagreements Table (about 250 words)—state each position in wording its own proponent accepts, specifying whether relations are constitutive or actualizing, whether moral status can diminish through social exclusion, and whether newborn infants possess full personhood prior to any social recognition; (3) Cruxes and Predictive Tests (about 350 words)—identify two cruxes: first, whether extreme social ostracism can extinguish personhood, with a concrete case both sides accept as relevant and each side’s explicit prediction about it; second, whether the direction of explanation runs from social structure to individual ontology or vice versa, with an empirical or conceptual test both agree bears on it and each prediction recorded; (4) Verbal Diagnosis (about 150 words)—note any disputes that dissolve upon clarifying terms, particularly around whether relationality is modal (counterfactual relations suffice) or purely actual (present relations are necessary); (5) Epistemic Note (about 75 words)—state your overall confidence in this framing of the disagreement, and explicitly single out the point you are least sure about.

In drafting, avoid bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the analysis should stand on conceptual grounds alone. Make sure the disagreement formulations are neither vacuous nor slanted, and treat the modal-versus-actual distinction in relational constitution as a live crux rather than a side issue, since that distinction, though rarely foregrounded in popular treatments of Ubuntu, seems to drive much of our underlying disagreement.
