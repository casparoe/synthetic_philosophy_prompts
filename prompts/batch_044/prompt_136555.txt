Write the desiderata that any adequate philosophical theory of well-being must satisfy if it is to defend tranquilism: the view that only the absence of craving and suffering has intrinsic value, while pleasure, achievement, knowledge, and relational goods are at best instrumentally useful and never constitutive of well-being. Before assessing particular formulations, I want the full specification of adequacy conditions.

Set out the requirements — the phenomena to explain, the distinctions that must not collapse, the consistency conditions with empirical science, and the conceptual puzzles that must be dissolved — with the motivating case or argument behind each. In particular, include:

- How the theory distinguishes authentic tranquility (the genuine cessation of craving and suffering) from merely suppressed, anesthetized, or ignorantly induced states, and whether it can draw that distinction without covertly assigning positive intrinsic value to awareness, autonomy, or self-knowledge that tranquilism officially denies.
- How it handles the structural asymmetry between suffering and craving: whether the absence of suffering alone is sufficient for well-being, or whether the underlying craving-structure must also be absent, and whether one can occur without the other (for example, a state of dull contentment that lacks acute suffering but retains latent appetitive orientation).
- What it says about cases in which the elimination of craving undermines the very motivational architecture needed to sustain long-term absence of suffering — whether this generates an internal paradox for tranquilism or merely marks a practical boundary.
- Which results from neuroscience and psychology about craving, reward-prediction error, and homeostatic regulation the theory must accommodate, and which it is permitted to treat as mechanistic descriptions lacking normative import.
- How it respects the distinction between having well-being (occupying a tranquil state) and having more or less of it, given that tranquilism denies gradations of positive value; and whether intersubjective comparison of well-being collapses entirely or survives in some reduced form.
- Whether the theory must preserve the agent’s capacity for reflective endorsement of her own tranquil state, or whether such a requirement illegitimately imports a desire-satisfactionist element.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, requiring both the complete elimination of craving and the preservation of the cognitive autonomy required to maintain that elimination, or requiring full consistency with affective neuroscience while denying that reward-circuit activity carries any normative weight.

Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and their internal conflicts rather than on grading existing theories, though briefly indicate how hedonism, desire-satisfactionism, and an objective-list account each violate at least one non-negotiable item on your list.

In your answer, state how confident you are in each major claim about these requirements, and single out the specific point — whether a particular conflict between desiderata, a proposed distinction, or a consistency condition — about which you are least sure.
