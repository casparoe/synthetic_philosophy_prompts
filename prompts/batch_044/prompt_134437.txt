As a high school student preparing for an advanced philosophy workshop, I want you to draft the joint statement that a committed Sartrean—who holds that radical freedom is inescapable and that bad faith is always culpable self-deception—could sign alongside a structuralist critic who argues that social and institutional constraints genuinely limit agency and that denying authorship can be an adaptive response to coercion rather than a moral failure. Rather than rehearsing the classic café-waiter example, center the collaboration on a synthetic framing: institutional bad faith within compulsory educational systems, specifically the case of a student who insists they hold no genuine beliefs because the curriculum mandates them. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides accept about the phenomenology of choice, the reality of external constraint, and the conditions under which self-deception is possible; (2) a disagreements table with each camp’s precise position phrased in wording its own members would accept, focusing especially on whether structural coercion can ever excuse inauthenticity and whether the notion of “situated freedom” is a coherent revision or a contradiction in terms; (3) the cruxes—above all, whether recognizing systemic limits necessarily undermines the normative demand for authenticity; and (4) for each crux, a concrete interpretation of the compulsory-education case, with both sides recording in advance what they predict about it and what argument or piece of evidence they agree would actually bear on the dispute. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and write the entire statement as if it were being composed for a seminar without access to external sources.
