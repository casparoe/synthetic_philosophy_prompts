You serve as a preliminary adjudicator for a transnational heritage tribunal that evaluates petitions for protected-status designation of collective rituals, commemorative calendars, and origin narratives submitted by communities seeking legal recognition of cultural continuity. Your office must render preliminary determinations within ten working days, using only the documentary record, limited ethnographic summaries, and internal conceptual frameworks; final sign-off requires a senior anthropologist, but your procedure must be reusable by junior staff without specialist training. Operationalize the analytical distinction between genuinely continuous practices and “invented traditions” (in the sense that present needs construct a formalized, ritualized response to new situations while claiming ancient lineage) together with the framework of “imagined communities” (in the sense that membership boundaries, shared temporal narratives, and collective self-images are configured through modern mediating practices rather than face-to-face continuity) into a stepwise decision procedure.

The procedure must include: (1) an ordered sequence of diagnostic tests, each with explicit evidentiary markers that can be checked against the petition dossier; (2) decision nodes that classify the petition as clearly continuous, clearly invented, or indeterminate, with the criteria for each; (3) a protocol for cases where the two frameworks yield conflicting signals—for example, where a ritual shows formal discontinuity characteristic of invention but serves an imagined community whose membership boundaries have remained stable for generations; and (4) explicit flag points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be escalated to the senior sign-off.

In addition, build in a specific safeguard against “retroactive institutionalization”: the risk that the petition process itself prompts a community to codify, standardize, or symbolically anchor an informal practice, thereby generating the very tradition the tribunal is asked to evaluate. Specify how your procedure detects this feedback loop and how it should affect the preliminary determination.

Apply the procedure to three worked cases: (a) a petition from a modern nation-state seeking protected status for a state-sponsored commemorative calendar created in the late twentieth century but narrated as millennia-old; (b) an ambiguous case from a diaspora community that reconstructed a ritual after displacement, blending documented pre-displacement fragments with innovations required by the new environment; and (c) one case where the procedure yields a classification that directly undermines the petitioners’ own origin narrative, and state exactly what the tribunal should record when the analytical result conflicts with the community’s self-understanding.

Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to external scholarship; ground every step in the conceptual logic of the two frameworks themselves. Flag precisely where the procedure will misfire or produce counter-intuitive results, and specify what your office should do in those instances.
