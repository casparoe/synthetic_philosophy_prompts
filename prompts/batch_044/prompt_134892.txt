I'd like a consistency audit of five commitments I've developed on archives, power, and historical method, before I present them in a working group. They are:

(1) The archive is not a passive repository but an active apparatus that produces the very subjects it records; visibility within an archive is an effect of power, not its precondition.

(2) The silences and omissions in archives are structurally positive—shaped by the same bureaucratic logic that generates presence—so an absence can be interpreted symptomatically rather than treated as a mere gap.

(3) There is an ethical obligation to recover the self-understandings of those rendered invisible by official records, and this recovery is best pursued through counter-archival practices that treat oral testimony, material traces, and administrative errors as authoritative sources rather than supplements.

(4) Historical interpretation ought to resist presentist translation: past subjects should be understood within the conceptual schemes available to them, because importing modern identity categories risks reproducing the very misrecognition that the archive originally enacted.

(5) The correct unit of archival analysis is not the content of a document but what I call the filing relation—the bureaucratic link between a body and its record—because power operates in the fact of being registered rather than in what is said within the record.

Please assess whether this package is jointly tenable. Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear to conflict and dissolve once the commitments are properly specified, and what is the least costly revision that would restore coherence? Rank the real tensions by severity, specifying for each what would have to be modified or abandoned and at what cost. In particular, I want to know whether my insistence on the filing relation in (5) undermines the content-sensitive recovery project in (3), and whether (4)'s prohibition on anachronism can survive together with (2)'s demand that we read structured silences through present interpretive frameworks.

Please state how confident you are in each claim you make about these commitments, and identify the specific point in your analysis about which you are least sure.
