As a member of my university's International Partnerships Ethics Board, I must help the board reach a binding decision by Thursday on whether to renew our ten-year strategic research collaboration with a partner university in an authoritarian state that has delivered rapid economic growth, low corruption, and large-scale poverty reduction, while sharply restricting academic freedom—most recently through a state-mandated "social harmony audit" that subjects international joint research to review of its social implications. The partner institution has submitted a defense of continued collaboration that relies on what its own scholars call "recursive performance legitimacy": the claim that authoritarian rule is provisionally justified not by prosperity alone, but by maintaining institutional mechanisms designed to automatically dissolve coercive controls once specific development targets are met. They argue that our joint urban-climate research has contributed measurably to those targets, so terminating the agreement would remove a prosperity-generating input without allowing the sunset mechanism to trigger, thereby harming the citizens whose eventual liberation the framework promises.

Some board members hold that no level of prosperity or order can justify suppressing open inquiry, and that maintaining the partnership lends moral cover to repression. Others argue that withdrawal mainly harms local scholars and eliminates a rare channel of external influence, with little effect on state policy. I need you to draft the formal recommendation memo I will circulate before Thursday's vote. The memo should be addressed to the board, approximately 350 to 400 words, confined to a single page, with the bottom-line recommendation—renew the agreement with explicit conditions, suspend it for twelve months with concrete benchmarks, or terminate it—stated clearly in the opening paragraph along with the timeline. Take a definite position and defend it with reasons; do not present a neutral survey of options or leave the choice to the board. You must engage the partner's "recursive performance legitimacy" argument honestly and on its own terms, identifying the strongest point in favor of renewal, but you must also treat the strongest counter-argument against your own recommendation with equal seriousness. Write in a tone appropriate for circulation before a binding institutional vote.
