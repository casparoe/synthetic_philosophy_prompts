hey, i've been going in circles on this argument about directness vs politeness norms and whether white lies are ever okay. here's the chain i keep running into:

(1) If a communicative practice is rational for agents with a given social ontolgy, then it's rationaly binding on anyone with that ontology.
(2) Dutch-style directness is rational when autonomy is primary and ambiguity counts as harm; Japanese-style indirectness is rational when relational harmony is primary and explicit refusal counts as harm.
(3) White lies work the same way - they're rational in ontologies where face-preservation outweighs information-transfer.
(4) Every actual human has autonomy, relational harmony, and information-transfer in their social ontology, just weighted differently.
(5) So every human is rationaly bound by directness, indirectness, and white-lying norms all at once.
(6) But these contradict in a single speech act: you can't be blunt, indirect, and deceptively kind about the same invitation refusal.
(7) Therefore no one can fully satisfy rational communicative obligations, which is absurd since people talk every day.
(8) So one of (1)-(4) has to go.

i really want to keep (4) - we all do care about all three, just in different proportions. but then i'm stuck with (6). is the slip that (5) falsely universalizes local weights? or that (6) assumes norms apply maximally rather than being defeasible? if i reject (1) and say "rational binding" is culture-local, does that save me from saying white lies are sometimes wrong but never required?

where's the actual error? and what's it cost me to fix it? i feel like something slipped between (4) and (5) but i can't name it.
