Here's an argument I cooked up for a seminar and now it feels sus. (1) If nudging is okay, it's because people still technically choise, so autonomy is preserved. (2) But choice is always shaped by archtecture — menus, defaults, framing — so there's no pure choice outside it. (3) So autonomy isn't absence of architecture, but whether it's transparent and revisable. (4) Now suppose there's "preference laundering": a nudge not only steers choice but rewrites the chooser's preferneces after the fact, so they come to endorse the steered option. (5) If that happens, the chooser's later endorsement makes the architecture transparent and revisable from their own view — they can revise it now that they like it. (6) So by (3) and (5), even hidden coercive architecture is justified, since the victim eventually prefers it and can revise it. (7) But that's absurd; hidden coercion shouldn't be okay just because brainwashing worked. Where does it slip? I'd rather keep (2) and (3) if I can, but tell me which step is the weakest link and what giving it up costs.
