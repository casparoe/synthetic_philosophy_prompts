In the course of preparing a graduate seminar on Keynes's A Treatise on Probability, I have been grappling with a reconstructive problem that sits precisely at the boundary of historical interpretation and formal epistemology. Keynes introduces the notion of weight of argument as a property that can increase—through the accumulation of relevant premises—without altering the first-order probability assigned to a conclusion, and he systematically contrasts this with the balance of evidence. Contemporary commentators remain divided: some read weight as an anticipation of modern imprecise-probability representations, in which an entire family of credence functions captures ambiguity without fixing a unique value; others take it to be an irreducibly logical relation between propositions, one that resists any quantitative expression whatsoever.

I want you to state, with explicit numerical values, your credence in the following specific and somewhat unconventional thesis: that Keynesian weight is best reconstructed neither as an imprecise-credence spread nor as a purely logical relation, but rather as a modal property of evidential structures—specifically, the stability of rational belief-revision paths under the addition of previously unconsidered premises. Please decompose your reasoning into three explicit sub-credences: first, the probability that Keynes himself intended weight to be quantifiable in any form; second, the probability that existing imprecise-probability frameworks genuinely capture the phenomenon he describes; third, the probability that this modal-revision-path interpretation is both conceptually coherent and textually defensible. Then combine them to produce your overall figure, identify which single component contributes the greatest variance to your final number, and specify what kind of textual discovery—perhaps an unpublished manuscript note or a clarifying passage from Keynes's discussion of weight and relevance—would most substantially shift your view. I ask this not for a bibliography or literature survey, but to see whether your reasoning can be made fully transparent.
