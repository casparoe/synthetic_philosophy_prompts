I'm serving on my university's student disciplinary appeals panel, and we are deadlocked over a case that has pulled us straight into the Hart–Devlin debate without anyone quite wanting to name it. A graduate student, entirely off-campus and with no university funding, organized a private "Stoic endurance circle": members signed voluntary contracts agreeing to ritual public shaming—for example, being read a list of their personal moral failures in front of the group—if they broke self-imposed rules about diet, study, or religious observance. No one was coerced; no physical harm occurred; but when the contracts leaked, dozens of students petitioned for expulsion, arguing that the practice "corrupts the shared moral environment" of the institution. Our student code explicitly permits sanctions only for harm, coercion, or fraud. Half the panel wants to expel him for legal-moralist reasons—that the university must defend its collective ethos—while the other half insists we are bound by the harm principle and have no jurisdiction over private vice. I have been asked to circulate the panel's formal written opinion before Thursday's vote.

Please draft the memo—no more than 450 words, roughly one concise page—recommending a concrete decision: uphold the expulsion, overturn it, or uphold it only under a narrowly defined condition (for instance, dissolution of the contracts plus a formal acknowledgment). Your reasoning should map the case onto Hart's distinction between positive and critical morality and his insistence that institutional discipline, like law, requires demonstrable harm rather than mere offense to shared values; it should also engage Devlin's claim that every society needs to enforce its shared moral bonds and that visible "immorality" erodes the social fabric, taking that argument seriously rather than dismissing it. Include an explicit correspondence table or list: what in this case plays the role of "the shared morality," "the harm," and "the private vice." Then state the strongest consideration against your own recommendation—if you reject expulsion, explain the genuine risk to institutional culture that Devlin would highlight; if you uphold it, explain the danger of empowering majoritarian moral policing. End with the actionable bottom line and a brief implementation note specifying a time frame or conditions. Do not include bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations; work only from the core conceptual structure of the debate.
