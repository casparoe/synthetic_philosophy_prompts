I'm writing up my position on the regress problem for a seminar and want a consistency check before I present it. Here is roughly where I've landed: I hold that foundationalism correctly describes sensory justification, since there are basic perceptual beliefs that can terminate inquiry without further support; that coherentism correctly describes scientific-theoretical justification, since observation is theory-laden and beliefs in that domain support each other holistically rather than resting on untouched foundations; and that infinitism correctly describes normative-ethical justification, since every moral principle can be questioned by a deeper one and an infinite linear chain of reasons is not vicious. I reject the idea that justification must have one uniform structure across all domains, so I do not treat these three theories as globally competing answers to a single problem. At the same time, I think a belief justified within one domain can migrate into reasoning in another domain without needing its original justification reconstructed—so I might invoke a perceptual belief about suffering in an ethical argument without restarting the foundational chain that originally supported it. I also think the classic regress argument fails because it tacitly assumes justification is linear and unidirectional, though I do not regard this as undermining infinitism, since infinitism precisely accepts linear infinite chains. Please audit these commitments: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each real tension? Rank them by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and say which single change would resolve the greatest number of problems.
