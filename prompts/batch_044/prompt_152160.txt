I am preparing a set of philosophical commitments on genetic information and the code metaphor for an advanced bioethics seminar, and I should like an audit of their coherence before I present them. Roughly, I hold that DNA is not literally a code in any semantically robust sense, since nucleotides lack intentional content and the mapping from sequence to phenotype is causally mediated rather than conventionally interpreted; that the language of genetic information is therefore a useful but ontologically misleading metaphor, and I reject the view that genomes represent their products in the way a map represents a territory; that despite this, I treat clinical genetic reports as carrying a distinctive normative weight, speaking of pathogenic mutations and normal reference sequences as though they prescribe what a patient's genome ought to contain; that gene expression is radically context-dependent, so that the same sequence may be functionally silent in one cellular environment and highly active in another, which undermines any simple deterministic reading; and that I support extending intellectual-property protections to wholly synthetic gene sequences, on the grounds that they are engineered informational artefacts analogous to software, with a design origin that distinguishes them from naturally occurring DNA. Do these hang together? I am particularly concerned that my rejection of semantic coding undermines both the normative language in clinical genetics and the software analogy in my property-rights view, while the radical context-dependency might dissolve the stability required for either privacy claims or patent protection. At the same time, I suspect that the normative clinical language and the context-dependency thesis are only apparently in tension, since the latter concerns expression rather than the sequence itself, and that one might distinguish ontological coding from functional design so that the first and last commitments survive together. Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate which pairs are genuinely incompatible and which merely appear to be so, state what the least costly revision would be, and say exactly what it costs me. Please write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in British English, as one might compose a careful letter or email.
