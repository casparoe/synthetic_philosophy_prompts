I've been working through the argument that qualia are not merely mysterious but positively incoherent—the strategy familiar from the quining-qualia literature, where one attempts to isolate an intrinsic qualitative character (the specific bitterness of quinine, the precise redness of an afterimage) by stripping away every relational, comparative, and functional property, only to find that no residue remains to be named. I'm particularly struck by the move that identifying any quale already requires placing it in a contrast space—this red against that orange, this ache against that pressure—so that the very grammar of "what it's like" presupposes the differences it claims to transcend, and by the super-blindsight scenario in which a subject behaves with perfect visual discrimination while reporting no experience whatsoever, leaving no functional trace of absence. I want a poem that doesn't mention consciousness in passing but actually enacts this dissolution: the feeling should come apart under the pressure of its own description.

Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the standard A1-b-A2 rhyme scheme and two refrains—where the form does the philosophical work. Let the first refrain insist on the pure intrinsic feel, something like "There is something it is like to taste this," and let the second deliver the quining result, "But quinine dissolves in comparison." Between them, rotate lines that accumulate the relational machinery: taste receptors, expectation effects, the inverted-spectrum possibility, and that super-blindsight case where behavior is intact and experience is allegedly gone. With each return of the first refrain it should sound more hollow, with each return of the second more overdetermined, until the closing quatrain brings both refrains into direct collision without resolving them—so the poem ends not with consciousness saved or destroyed, but with the incoherence of the original posit left exposed in the rhyme itself. Make it precise enough that someone familiar with the phenomenal-concept literature recognizes the contrast-space objection, but keep it a real poem, not a rhyme scheme with philosophy pasted on.
