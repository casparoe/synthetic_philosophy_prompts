She extends this framework to argue that in modern industrialized conflicts, widespread civilian economic participation—taxation, supply-chain labor, and resource extraction—creates a "distributed liability" that erodes the sharp combatant/non-combatant distinction, though she insists this tracks functional contribution and structural risk-allocation rather than individual moral culpability. She further applies the view to autonomous weapons, contending that an unmanned lethal system lacks moral agency of its own and should be evaluated solely through the liability profile of the diffuse human network that deploys, maintains, and authorizes it.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that do not merely ask her to restate these claims. For each question, include a follow-up keyed precisely to the reply she is most likely to offer—especially her tendency to answer that liability tracks functional role rather than mental state, and that autonomous systems inherit the moral structure of the network behind them.
