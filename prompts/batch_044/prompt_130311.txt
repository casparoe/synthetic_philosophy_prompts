Write a dialogue between a defender of Dennett's two-bitser view (all intentionality is derived from an interpreter's stance) and a defender of Searle's original-intentionality view (brains have intrinsic aboutness, everything else borrows it). Make both sides competent — no strawmen — and have them actually respond to each other's points, not just talk past one another.

To avoid another Chinese Room rerun, ground the debate in a specific hypothetical: a "Nested Interpreter" brain-computer interface. In this setup, a patient's biological neural network is coupled in real time to a synthetic predictive layer that generates interpretations of the patient's own mental states. The biological part might have original intentionality if Searle is right; the synthetic part is clearly just computing correlations. The characters should argue over whether the synthetic layer can aquire original intentionality through causal coupling, or whether it remains a mere two-bitser deriving all its aboutness from the biological host.

They need to adress three issues:
1) whether causal coupling can transfer original intentionality, or only let the synthetic part borrow derived content,
2) what misrepresentation in the synthetic layer means — if its predictions diverge from the patient's actual mental states, does that show derived intentionality can misrepresent in a way original intentionality can't, or does it just prove there's no intentionality there at all,
3) whether Dennett's design stance can even apply to a subsystem that was never designed for interpretation in the first place but was just implanted or evolved into the loop.

Keep the tone conversational but sharp, with real back-and-forth. A typo or two is fine — don't over-polish.
