I am preparing an exercise in metaphysics and should be grateful if you could draft the joint statement that a committed unrestricted universalist and a committed moderate restrictionist might both endorse after a rigorous adversarial collaboration focused specifically on the special composition question. I should like the document divided into a numbered section of agreed claims — including shared commitments about what the formalism of parthood entails, about the role of vagueness, and about which ontological costs are genuinely at stake — followed by a disagreements table in which each camp’s position is rendered in wording it would accept for itself. The table ought to identify at least three cruxes: first, whether causal overdetermination by parts and wholes is a genuine problem or a pseudo-problem arising from double-counting; second, whether there exists a principled, non-arbitrary boundary between those pluralities that compose something and those that do not; and third, whether accepting unrestricted composition forces one to deny the intuitive asymmetry between parts and wholes. For each crux, please include a concrete hypothetical case or argument that both sides agree is relevant — I have in mind something like a scattered object consisting solely of every brass doorknob manufactured in Sheffield between 1880 and 1890, existing only so long as at least two of its members remain within one metre of each other — and record, in advance, what each camp predicts about how that case should be analysed. Please conclude by stating whether any portion of their dispute is merely verbal, and if so which portion and why. Write in formal prose, using British spelling, and avoid any request for bibliographies, citations, or further reading lists, as this is intended for evaluation without external reference.
