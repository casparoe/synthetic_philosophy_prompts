As a high school student studying applied ethics, I have been reflecting on the classic aggregation dilemma that contrasts a single severe harm—such as torture—with an astronomical number of negligible harms, like dust specks causing imperceptible eye irritation. Rather than repeating the standard comparison, I want to understand how this framework applies to distributed moral responsibility in global supply chains, where billions of routine consumer choices each contribute an almost undetectable amount of suffering, set against one catastrophic factory disaster that causes concentrated, severe injustice. Please describe the most important implications of the torture-versus-dust-specks problem for how we should ethically weigh these accumulated micro-harms against singular, grave wrongs within large-scale economic systems.
