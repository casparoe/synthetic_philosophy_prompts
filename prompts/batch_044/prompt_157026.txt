I'm preparing a long-form feature for a science-and-ideas magazine on the procedural theater of defining the Anthropocene, and in conversations with colleagues we've kept returning to the same irony: the argument over whether the epoch begins with the mid-twentieth-century radionuclide signal, the late-eighteenth-century industrial marker, or the early-seventeenth-century biotic exchange has become less a stratigraphic dispute than a bureaucratic one, particularly after the International Commission on Stratigraphy's 2024 decision not to ratify the Anthropocene as a formal chronostratigraphic unit. For the piece, I need a deadpan satirical document—a mock Terms of Service agreement drafted by a fictional "Intertemporal Governance Board" that treats the Anthropocene as a licensed historical product whose users must agree to binding geological conditions.

The agreement should run about 450 to 500 words, written in the flat, impenetrable legalese of actual stratigraphic protocols, complete with references to Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Points, subcommission voting procedures, and retroactive liability clauses, while quietly applying that language to the fact that the "users"—human civilization—have already violated every term before signing. Please include specific "clauses" that address the competing start-date proposals not as scientific debates but as jurisdictional conflicts between legacy Holocene subscribers and new Anthropocene licensees, and ensure that the satire derives from exaggerating real procedural features—formal ratification votes, the authority of the ICS, the impossibility of governing an epoch that is still unfolding—rather than inventing false ones. The tone must remain entirely deadpan, with no winking asides, so that a reader familiar with the Anthropocene debate recognizes the parody as fair, yet a lay reader can still grasp that something is being ironized about humanity's attempt to govern its own destructive present through geological bureaucracy. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or suggested reading; I want the document to stand as a self-contained piece of mock-discursive prose that could sit alongside the feature without requiring external verification.
