(1) If a thought experiment yields knowledge about nature, then the imagined scenario must share the causal or structural features that actually make the conclusion true in reality.
(2) But we can only identify which features are "the relevent ones" by already knowing the correct theory.
(3) So thought experiments presuppose the very knowledge they're supposed to deliver.
(4) The only way out is to say our reasoning about imagined cases doesn't depend on prior theory at all — it's pure conceptual or rational intuition.
(5) But then there's no constraint on which imagined cases count, so I could "prove" gravity is repulsive just by imagining an elevator falling upward and applying the same intution.
(6) Science clearly rejects that kind of free-for-all, so (4) has to be false.
(7) Therefore thought experiments either beg the question or open the door to arbitrary conclusions.
(8) Yet physicists treat Einstein's elevator as genuinely revealing the equivilence principle, not just illustrating something they already knew.
(9) So either physicists are wrong about what they're doing, or one of the steps above is broken.
