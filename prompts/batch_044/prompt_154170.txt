I'm presenting at an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-mind and clinical-phenomenology seminar next Thursday. My thesis is that Sartre's doctrine of radical freedom should be read not as a libertarian claim about uncaused choice, but as the ontological thesis that consciousness necessarily fails to coincide with its own facticity—its embodied, historical, and social situation. I further argue that bad faith is not best modeled as intrapsychic self-deception but as an intercorporeal attention-modulation strategy: the subject distributes agency across an imagined social witness (the Sartrean Look) in order to treat its own body-schema as an external, causally determined object. This reading, I claim, makes Sartre empirically tractable—connecting to predictive-processing accounts of body ownership without reducing freedom to error minimization—while preserving the normative force of his critique of self-objectification.

They care about tractability, conceptual clarity, and therapeutic relevance. They will not ask for a bibliography.
