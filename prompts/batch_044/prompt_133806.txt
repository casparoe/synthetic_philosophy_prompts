I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and next Thursday I am presenting a twenty-minute position paper at an interdisciplinary seminar on autonomous systems and moral accountability. My thesis is that in distributed autonomous architectures—specifically multi-agent reinforcement-learning networks where nodes recursively update one another's reward structures—the standard “responsibility gap” is not merely an epistemic failure of traceability but an emergent structural feature. I argue that attempting to close the gap by inserting a “human-in-the-loop” at every decision node is conceptually incoherent once the system achieves what I call recursive goal interdependence, because in such loops no single agent, human or artificial, possesses the kind of independent practical reasoning that our moral and legal frameworks presuppose. I therefore propose that we recognize a category of distributed moral agency in which responsibility is genuinely shared but not decomposable into individual shares, rather than treating the gap as a temporary engineering problem to be solved by better logging or audit trails.
