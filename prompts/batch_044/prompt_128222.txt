Consider Arthur Schopenhauer's distinctive claim that music is not, like painting or poetry, a representation of Platonic Ideas mediated through the principle of sufficient reason, but rather the direct objectification of the Will itself—an immediate, non-conceptual copy of the inner nature of reality that expresses universal emotional conditions without reference to particular objects. Now suppose a contemporary system uses deep generative models trained on global musical corpora to produce continuous, non-repeating auditory streams that adapt in real time to an individual listener's biometric state—galvanic skin response, heart-rate variability, and cortical EEG patterns. There is no composer, no performer, no fixed score, and no reproducible work; each session yields a unique acoustic profile optimized for affective resonance with that specific nervous system, then vanishes entirely. If Schopenhauer were confronted with this technology, how would he classify its outputs? Would he deny that they are music at all, on the grounds that without a conscious subject whose inner Will is being expressed, the sounds can be only a mechanistic simulation—an acoustic shadow lacking the essential metaphysical relation to the Will? Or might he maintain that the Will objectifies itself indifferently through human genius and through statistical optimization, so that the biometric loop is merely a novel medium for the same direct expression? Additionally, given his insistence that music's universality—its capacity to speak to all human beings regardless of particular circumstance—is precisely what distinguishes it from the other arts, would he regard real-time biometric personalization as undermining the very feature that makes music metaphysically special? Construct the response he would most likely give, distinguishing between metaphysical, aesthetic, and ontological objections, and indicate where his system forces a choice between accommodating the technology and preserving music's privileged status.
