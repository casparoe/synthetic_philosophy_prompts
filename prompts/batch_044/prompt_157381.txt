I'm organizing a seminar that moves from Polybius's constitutional cycle through Ibn Khaldun's model of social cohesion and state decay, then to Spengler's morphological account of cultural birth, growth, and senescence, and Toynbee's challenge-and-response framework. Rather than comparing these figures directly, I want to step back and ask what a satisfactory macrohistorical theory must actually deliver if it is to accommodate both genuinely cyclical patterns—recurring institutional forms, the rise and fall of cohesive elites, apparent civilizational aging—and apparently linear, cumulative developments such as technological accumulation, scientific method, and irreversible changes in organizational scale. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve (for instance, internal decay versus external conquest, necessary phases versus contingent events, isolated cultural development versus cross-civilizational transmission), and the empirical or logical constraints it must satisfy, including the documented failure of strict periodicity predictions and the observed asymmetry between the reversibility of political forms and the irreversibility of knowledge growth. For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or reflects the preference of a particular camp—say, determinists who demand fixed cultural life-spans versus adaptationists who allow civilizations to rewrite their institutional DNA through contact and borrowing.

Crucially, check whether any subset of these criteria is jointly unsatisfiable. Can a theory maintain without contradiction that civilizations possess an organic life-span with predictable senescence, while also holding that they can adaptively transform their core structures through external challenge? If explaining the linear accumulation of science necessarily breaks every cyclical model of political history, or if deterministic periodicity and open-ended adaptive transformation are incompatible, I want that tension exposed rather than smoothed over. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; do not score the historical figures against them, since the conceptual architecture itself is what I'm after.
