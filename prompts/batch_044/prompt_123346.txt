Two things which, properly speaking, are really one and the same constitute this soul, this spiritual principle. One is the past, the other..." — the text cuts off, but he means the present consent he calls "a daily plebiscite."

I'm trying to understand this through Hobsbawm and Anderson — invented traditions, imagined communities — but Renan sounds like he thinks there's a real group soul, not just a story people tell. At the same time he says forgetting is essential. So is he a constructivist or a realist about collective identity?

That feels like presentism in metaphysics — only the present moment is real, and the past is just a memory. But if you read him as an eternalist, the nation's past and future are equally real parts of the "soul," and the plebiscite is just how we access it. Does Renan's view have a hidden commitment to one of these philosophies of time, or am I forcing physics-envy onto politcs?

Does his "spiritual principle" presuppose any particular social ontology, and where does it sit between realism and constructivism?
