Hey — I'm a retired marine biologist who got into philospohy late in life, so bear with me. I cooked up this argument for a local seminar and now it feels like it proves too much, but I can't spot the leak. It's about the meta-problem of consciousness — why we think physical descriptions leave out subjective experience. I call the puzzle the insulation dilemma: our judgments seem insulated from first-person correction on one side and from zombie replication on the other.

Here are the steps I wrote out:

(1) The meta-problem asks why physical processes seem unable to account for subjective experience.
(2) Any explanation of our judgment must either cite direct first-person acquaintance with qualia, or cite some observable feature like reportability or neural integration.
(3) If it cites direct acquaintance, the justification reaches only ourselves. We can't analogically extend it to other minds, because the hard problem is specifically about the gap between physics and experience, not about behavior — so we slide into a kind of solipsism where only I face a justified meta-problem.
(4) If it cites an observable feature, then a philosophical zombie that duplicates that feature will also judge there's a hard problem, even though it lacks experience. So the feature doesn't track real consciousness and can't ground our judgment.
(5) So every available explanation either collapses into solipsism or applies equally to zombies.
(6) That means there's no stable, non-arbitrary basis for thinking there's a meta-problem at all.
(7) Therefore the Chalmers-style framing is a phantom and we should just do neuroscience.

I realy want to keep (1) and (7) if I can, but I'm open to dropping (5) if that's the slip. Is there an equivocation in "observable feature" between steps (2) and (4)? Does (3) underestimate analogical reasoning? Please go through each step, say whether me or the imaginary objector is right there, and tell me what giving up that step costs. I'd rather not end up with solipsism, but I also don't want to save the meta-problem by cheating.
