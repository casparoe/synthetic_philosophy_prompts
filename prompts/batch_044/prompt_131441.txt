I sit on the review board for our universities consciousness-science center (yes we still dont have a proper logo). Weve got this proposal that wants to apply heterophenomenolgy to patients in minimal consciousness states, but with a twist. Rather than just bracketing caregiver interviews, the team wants to train an LLM on sparse eye-tracking and vocalization fragments to generate synthetic 'first-person texts,' then interpret those texts heterophenomenologically — treating them as narratives whose structure we study without asking whether they map onto an actual inner world. Half the board thinks this extends the method into a new space; the other half says it corrupts the core idea, since heterophenomenology requires a subject who can actually produce and own the report, not an algorithm stitching biometrics together. Our funding partner needs a decision by next Thursday.

Please draft the board's memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Give a concrete course of action with a timeline, take the 'this corrupts the method' objection seriously rather than waving it away, and include a note saying how confident you are in the recommendation and flagging the one point you're least sure about — probably whether synthesizing texts from minimal biometrics actually breaks heterophenomenology or just stretches it past recognition.
