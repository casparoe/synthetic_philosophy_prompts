Write a dialogue between two equally capable political philosophers, Mara and Jonas, debating the boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—through the concrete case of a permanent lunar settlement governed remotely by Earth-based democratic institutions. Mara should defend a recursive authorization view: anyone whose fundamental interests are systematically shaped by collective decisions belongs to the demos, regardless of present co-location or direct voting access, which includes future settlers and affected non-citizens on Earth. Jonas should defend a co-presence view: democratic membership requires actual participation in shared deliberative practices, so remote governance over a lunar population violates democratic legitimacy unless the settlers themselves constitute an independent demos. Have them engage directly with one another—no strawmen—through at least two rounds of reply, working through a specific tension: if the settlement's life-support decisions depend on Earth-based resource allocation, does that integrate Earth citizens into the lunar demos, or does it integrate lunar settlers into Earth's? They should converge on a precise conceptual disagreement about the conditions of democratic membership rather than a merely empirical dispute. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific scholars.
