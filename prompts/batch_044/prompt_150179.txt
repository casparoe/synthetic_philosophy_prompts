I'm giving the eulogy tomorrow for Dr. Elena Voss, my mentor, who died Tuesday at 87. She was a hardline modal realist—concrete worlds, no transworld identity, the full Lewisian package—and also a strict materialist with zero patience for afterlife talk. Roughly half the chapel will be philosphers who knew her work; the other half are grandkids and neighbors who just loved her. She left a note asking that nobody say she's "watching over us" or "in a better place" because she thought that was false, but she also wanted people genuinely comforted, not lectured.

I want the speech to use her modal realism as real structure, not decoration. The key idea: there are concrete worlds where a counterpart of Elena is alive right now, but those worlds are spatiotemporaly disconnected from ours, and the counterpart isn't identical to her—she's not there, and we're not there with her. The grief we feel is anchored to this actual world, and that's okay; it doesn't require pretending she's nowhere real, only recognizing that the "somewhere" isn't here and isn't her. I need the family to feel that emotional point without drowning in ontology, and I need the philosophers in the room to see I'm not smuggling in an indexical fallacy or treating counterparts as identical.

Write a five-minute eulogy in a casual spoken voice—like I'm at the podium, not publishing.
