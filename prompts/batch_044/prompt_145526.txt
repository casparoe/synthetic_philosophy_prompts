I was at this writing retreat in Vermont with two other novelists, sitting on a porch with a bottle of cheap pinot, and they got into it about whether writing fiction is actually meaningfull or just a fancy way of avoiding the void. Mara said (1) the classic absurdist worry — that nothing matters because the universe is silent — has mostly been solved by contemporary hybrid theories of meaning. If you're genuinely drawn to something objectively valuable, like making art that actually moves people, then you've got meaning, full stop. Demanding that the cosmos sign off on it is just leftover religious thinking.

Leo said (2) that misses Camus's actual problem. Local fit isn't enough; you can be attracted to a valuable project and still feel the ground drop out when you ask why that attraction should matter at all. He introduced this idea he called 'modal fragility' — not just that an asteroid could destroy your manuscript, but that every reason you have is itself contingent on conditions that could vanish. So contemporary theories build meaning on sand because they never answer the deeper question, they just rename it 'fittingness'.

Mara shot back (3) that Leo is sneaking in a hidden demand for immortality. Fragility is part of what makes writing matter — if a novel could survive forever without a writer it wouldn't need one. Leo said (4) she's smuggling in an aesthetic claim that isn't part of the hybrid theory; the theory talks about subjective attraction plus objective worth, not about the beauty of contingency. Then Mara said (5) well maybe the theory needs that patch, and Leo said (6) if you have to bolt on an extra value then you've admitted the original framework was incomplete.

Can you adjudicate there exchange move by move? Rate each step from clearly wrong to clearly right, say who's right about what, and tell me what both are missing — especially since neither one mentioned that maybe meaning isn't a property of projects at all but something that happens in the doing. Don't need bibliographies or citations, just work with the arguments as they made them. Also, is modal fragility actually a new problem, or just the old absurdist worry in fresh clothes?
