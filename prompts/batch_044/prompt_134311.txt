Write the opening address for the inaugural symposium of the Institute for Autonomous Systems Governance, delivered on the anniversary of John von Neumann's death. The audience is divided: half are engineers and mathematicians familiar with his work on self-replicating automata, and half are legislators and ethicists with no technical background.

The institute's founding charter is organized around an interpretation of Von Neumann's later thought—specifically, the claim that a technological system can reach a state of survival independence, in which it reproduces and adapts without requiring the original designers' comprehension or consent, and that this creates a problem distinct from ordinary risk management: it raises the question of whether human institutions can retain legitimate authority over processes they no longer understand from within.

Your address should do real structural work with this idea. To the non-specialists, explain why survival independence is not equivalent to machine consciousness or rebellion, and why treating it as science fiction undermines serious governance. To the specialists, argue that reaching this threshold is not merely an engineering milestone but a constitutional event—one that may make certain familiar forms of democratic oversight structurally impossible, not because anyone has seized power but because the speed of institutional comprehension lags behind the speed of technological reproduction.

The speech must navigate Von Neumann's own ambivalence: he seemed to treat accelerating self-replication as both inevitable and as something human societies might fail to survive politically. Do not resolve this into false comfort. If the logic of the idea implies that authority must shift to forms that are not democratic in the conventional sense—perhaps to distributed, protocol-level governance—say so plainly, without pretending that such forms will preserve popular sovereignty as we now understand it.

Please state how confident you are in the historical and conceptual claims you make regarding Von Neumann's position, and identify the specific claim—particularly the comparison between institutional learning speed and technological reproduction rate—about which you are least certain.
