Compare the partition-model of self-deception—according to which an agent is divided into a deceiver-part and a deceived-part, with information actively suppressed across an internal boundary—to the uniform-agent, selective-access model, which holds that a single reasoning system applies asymmetric evidential standards to belief-formation and belief-maintenance without any internal division. In particular, assess which model more naturally accommodates what we can call epistemic double-bookkeeping: the simultaneous maintenance of two contradictory representations of the same state of affairs, both in formats accessible to conscious deliberation, yet insulated from mutual revision. Does accommodating this phenomenon force the uniform-agent model to smuggle in something functionally equivalent to a partition? Conversely, does the partition model avoid reifying its parts into independent homunculi only by paying some other theoretical cost? Give your answer with specific attention to where each model stands on the possibility of genuine self-deception.
