I've been trying to systematize my position on the ethics-aesthetics boundary for a workshop paper, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate a draft. Here is where I stand:

(1) I am an autonomist about artistic value: the ethical defects of a work do not, in themselves, constitute aesthetic defects.

(2) Nevertheless, I think immoral art can be aesthetically superior to moral art precisely when its immorality generates structurally complex affective responses—say, the integration of moral horror with formal beauty—that morally upright works rarely achieve.

(3) I reject ethical criticism of art as a legitimate form of aesthetic evaluation; when we call a work "morally flawed," we are making a non-aesthetic judgment that should not enter into artistic appraisal.

(4) I maintain that the artist's moral biography is generally irrelevant to the work's value, with one exception: when the work's aesthetic structure manifestly expresses the artist's vicious psychology—for instance, a sadistic glee that is formally embodied in the rhythm or framing—then knowledge of the artist's character illuminates, without altering, an aesthetic property already present.

(5) I allow for a category I call "corrupted aesthetic value," in which a work's beauty is inextricable from its immoral framing; this does not diminish the work's artistic merit but places it in a distinctive evaluative category.

(6) I want to understand the separation of work and artist on the model of Gricean pragmatics: just as speaker meaning and sentence meaning diverge, with implicature depending on speaker intention but semantic content not, so some aesthetic properties depend on authorial intention but the work's core artistic value remains intention-independent.

My questions: Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, and which pairs only look tense? In particular, does (2) smuggle ethical evaluation back into aesthetics in a way that contradicts (3)? Does (4) violate (1) or can it be reconciled via the analogy in (6)? Is (5) compatible with (1), or does recognizing "corrupted value" concede that immorality can function as an aesthetic defect? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify what each revision would cost me, and identify the single change that would clean up the most inconsistencies. I am not asking for a literature survey or citation list—just an audit of whether this package hangs together.
