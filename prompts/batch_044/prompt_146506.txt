Write a two-page research proposal—suitable for a dissertation prospectus or a workshop pitch—on a structural problem in indirect normativity. The familiar idealized-deliberation framework holds that an agent’s practical reasons are fixed not by direct evaluation of actions but by what an improved version of that agent would advise or endorse under conditions of full information and rational coherence. The standard debate asks whether this collapses into direct normativity once the idealization is fully specified, or remains empty if left unspecified. Your proposal should shift attention to a more specific and under-explored question: whether indirect normativity requires a globally unified idealized deliberator, or whether it can tolerate domain-fragmented idealization, in which the structurally improved self that governs moral choice differs from the one governing prudential or epistemic choice. State the research question precisely and so that it admits a determinate answer—for instance, whether domain fragmentation renders indirect normativity extensionally equivalent to a cluster of direct, domain-specific theories, or whether it produces incoherence because there is no non-arbitrary criterion for partitioning domains. Explain why existing discussions of indirect normativity and internal reasons leave this open: they typically assume a single idealized agent without arguing that unity across practical domains is a substantive normative commitment rather than a hidden presupposition. Describe your method: a conceptual analysis of the constraints on idealization—rational coherence, information access, affective stability—applied separately to moral, prudential, and epistemic domains, followed by an argument about whether cross-domain unity can be justified or must be abandoned. Identify the two main possible conclusions and specify which considerations would decide between them. Say what the deliverable looks like—perhaps a taxonomy of fragmentation types together with a criterion for when indirect normativity remains genuinely indirect rather than reducible to direct norms—and include an honest assessment of scope: why the project is tractable for a single semester but risks failure if it attempts to solve the general problem of normative unity across all practical domains. Refer to positions and arguments by general description only; do not request a bibliography, citation list, or literature review, and indicate that specific references will be added separately.
