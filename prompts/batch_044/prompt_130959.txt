hey im stuck on this argument about anatta and personal identity that my study partner cooked up. it feels like it proves buddhism contradicts itself but i know thats too neat. anyway:

(1) Anatta says theres no enduring owner of experience, just the five skandhas in flx.
(2) So if we imagine copying someones mental stream onto a new body — not asking if its possible, just conceptually — there'd be no "soul" or "self" that either travels or stays behind. Only causal patterns replicate.
(3) That means the original person was never a singular thing; they were always just a temporary cluster. The copy is just another cluster with the same causal history.
(4) But then "my suffering" makes no sense, because "my" requires an owner and anatta denies owners exist at all, even conventionally.
(5) Still, buddhist texts constantly use first-personal language about "my dukkha" and "my path to liberation."
(6) So either anatta is false and there is a self that can suffer and be freed, or buddhism is incoherent because it denies the very subject that its ethics addresses.
(7) Therefore the doctrine of no-self undermines itself.

i think the problem might be in (4) — treating anatta as a metaphysical claim that wipes out indexical reference instead of a theraputic deconstruction. but im not sure what giving up (4) costs. could it also be (2), that replication shows nothing because anatta never needed a substrate? tell me which step is the weak link and what rejecting it actually commits me to. and please dont just say "two truths" unless you explain how that avoids being a cop-out.
