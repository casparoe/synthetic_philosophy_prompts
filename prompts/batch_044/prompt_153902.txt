I have recently begun working in a research group that is replacing an established theoretical framework with a newer one, and I have observed that experienced members can no longer restate the old core hypotheses in the new vocabulary without what feels like conceptual distortion. Before I commit to one philosophical interpretation, I want to know what an adequate account of incommensurability between scientific paradigms must be able to do. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain (loss of shared evaluative standards, persistence of empirical agreement, conditions for rational conversion), the distinctions it must respect (semantic versus methodological versus axiological incommensurability), and which requirements are non-negotiable versus camp-specific preferences. Specifically, flag any jointly unsatisfiable subset — for instance, can an account maintain both that rival paradigms lack common measures and that scientists can still rationally adjudicate between them? Also address whether the account must accommodate what I call localized incommensurability, in which only a subset of theoretical terms becomes untranslatable while observational protocols remain intact, and whether allowing this undermines the stronger Kuhnian thesis. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing theories.
