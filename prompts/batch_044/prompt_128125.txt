Analyze the Black Wednesday crisis of September 16, 1992 — when George Soros's Quantum Fund forced the British pound out of the European Exchange Rate Mechanism — through Aristotle's distinction in Politics I between oikonomia (household management oriented toward natural sufficiency) and chrematistike (wealth-getting that treats exchange as an end in itself). Focus specifically on Aristotle's claim that money (nomisma) is a conventional instrument that becomes unnatural (para physin) when it is made to breed more money (tokos) rather than to mediate the exchange of goods for use. Map the actors with discipline: does the UK Treasury, defending the peg, represent the oikonomos managing a collective household? Does Soros, shorting the pound for speculative profit, exemplify chrematistike? And what is the "money" in this case — the pound as a sovereign currency, the interest-rate hikes used to defend it, or the derivative positions through which the profit was realized? Then give an honest account of what Aristotle's framework explains about the crisis (for instance, whether treating a national currency as a commodity for self-reproducing profit illuminates the instability) and what it misses or misdescribes (for instance, that modern fiat money has no commodity backing to begin with, and that the "household" is a democratic state rather than an individual oikos). Finally, ask whether the episode reveals a conceptual gap: does chrematistike at the scale of sovereign currency and global capital require an ethical category that Aristotle's binary never anticipated, or does it ultimately confirm his warning that once exchange is detached from use-value and oriented toward unlimited accumulation, political order becomes fragile? Write in American English, and do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
