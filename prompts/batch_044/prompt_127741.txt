The second camp—call them the Threshold Collapse theorists—accepts that deontology captures something important about directness and agency, but they maintain that the paradox reveals an irreducible tension: any principled distinction between doing and allowing must either be arbitrarily rigid, failing to respond to magnitude at all, or implicitly smuggle in a consequentialist threshold at which the constraint dissolves and aggregate harm becomes decisive.
