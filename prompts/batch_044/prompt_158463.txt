As a journalist covering the conceptual architecture of archaeological science, I am preparing a long-form piece on what it actually takes to move from excavation data—broken vessels, foundation alignments, discard patterns—to defensible claims about institutional beliefs and collective norms. Before I speak with practitioners, I need the philosophical groundwork fixed. Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory of institutional and belief reconstruction from material traces must satisfy.

Consider specifically theories that aim to recover not merely individual mental states, but institutional norms—property obligations, ritual prohibitions, governance structures, or shared ontological commitments—from physical remains. For each desideratum, provide: the precise requirement; the case, logical tension, or empirical consideration that makes it mandatory rather than decorative; and whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular methodological camp. You must cover at least the following dimensions: (1) the equifinality constraint—how the theory distinguishes institutional logics that converge on formally identical material outputs; (2) the silence problem—how it accounts for norms that were widely practiced yet never materially expressed or symbolically marked; (3) the overreading safeguard—what prevents attributing intentional symbolic content to features better explained by taphonomy, ergonomics, or depositional accident; (4) consistency with formation processes—how it respects what sedimentology and post-depositional transformation actually permit us to know; (5) the aggregation gap—how it justifies inference from discrete artifacts or features to collective institutions without collapsing the distinction between individual action and shared rule; and (6) empirical tractability—what observable, intersubjective constraints must be available for a reconstruction to count as testable rather than hermeneutically open-ended.

Then, identify any subset of these desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable given current understanding of material culture dynamics, and state which single requirement does the most work in excluding the largest family of existing approaches—whether broadly processual, interpretive, or cognitive-archaeological. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions rather than on a scorecard of named theories; the goal is to know what adequate reconstruction demands before evaluating who delivers it. Write in a formal, polished register suitable for a feature on the philosophy of science.
