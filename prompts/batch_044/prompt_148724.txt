As a retired experimental physicist who has lately turned to philosophy of language, I have found myself drawn to epistemicism about vagueness—the view that predicates such as 'bald,' 'tall,' or even scientific classifications like 'species' possess precise, sharp boundaries of which we are merely ignorant, rather than the world itself lacking discrete joints. The analogy to an unknown systematic offset in measurement appeals to me: perhaps our linguistic categories have hidden cutoff points analogous to concealed parameters in an instrument. I want an honest assessment of what would genuinely count against this position. Please do not merely list the standard sorites-based objections; instead, rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicating for each whether a committed epistemicist should concede it as damaging or can reasonably dismiss it. In particular, I am interested in three lines of pressure: first, the intuition that there is simply no fact of the matter about whether a person with exactly 3,847 hairs is bald, which feels like an absence of joint in nature rather than mere ignorance of a hidden value; second, considerations from other domains—such as the continuity of evolutionary lineages or the absence of sharp phase boundaries in certain physical systems—suggesting that nature sometimes offers genuinely gradational structure without discrete joints for our predicates to latch onto; and third, whether epistemicism has been qualified into unfalsifiability, such that any apparent absence of sharp boundaries is automatically explained away as epistemic limitation, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a view of this kind. Which of these, if any, should move me away from epistemicism, and which are compatible with retaining it? I would also like to know what predictions or distinctive commitments this view makes that its rivals—particularly supervaluationism—do not, and whether there are conceivable observations or formal results that would render the epistemicist position genuinely risky rather than merely counterintuitive.
