If program equilibrium resolves one-shot prisoner's dilemmas by letting agents inspect each other's executable strategies before acting, then adapting it to AI safety debate—requiring debaters to submit reasoning as executable proof objects in a restricted formal language—should eliminate strategic deception, since the judge can verify every inference step syntactically. So why do researchers still maintain that debate protocols remain vulnerable to a stronger debater? Is the residual risk merely that the judge gets computationally overwhelmed by proof length, or is there a deeper mismatch between verifying inference rules and preventing selective framing of premises—one that makes source-code transparency in game theory fundamentally different from proof transparency in argumentation?
