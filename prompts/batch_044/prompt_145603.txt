(2) Incommensurability is best understood as divergence in shared standards for theory evaluation rather than as semantic untranslatability or total conceptual isolation.
(3) Despite (2), rational comparison across paradigms remains possible through paradigm-transcendent metrics such as predictive accuracy and problem-solving effectiveness.
(4) The thesis that there is no neutral observation language applies only to theoretical vocabulary, not to observational reports embedded in shared perceptual and instrumental practices.
(5) Consequently, there is no fully neutral standpoint permitting objective adjudication of competing paradigms, yet the historical convergence of scientists on a single paradigm does not require irrationality.
(6) This structure applies beyond classic historical cases to contemporary interdisciplinary divides—specifically, to conflicts between evolutionary psychology and cultural anthropology—where divergence lies in different ontologies of culture rather than in untranslatable vocabulary.
