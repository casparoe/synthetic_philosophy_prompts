(2) Turing showed that problem-solving behavior can be produced by formal computation without the system comprehending meanings or truths.
(3) so competence without comprehension isn't an exception; it's how complex success works in nature and machines.
(4) human reasoning — including Darwin's and Turing's own reasoning — is a biological and computational process.
(5) therefore human reasoning achieves its successes (predicting tides, building computers) through competence without comprehension: the reasoner doesn't actually grasp the truths involved.

(8) but if i reject (5), i'm saying human reasoning does involve real comprehension, which makes it an exception to the Darwin-Turing pattern.
(9) and if i keep (5), i undermine my own right to believe it.
(10) so either the Darwin-Turing insight collapses when turned on itself, or human cognition gets special treatment — which seems like exactly the pre-Darwin antropocentrism we wanted to avoid.
