Write a deadpan, bureaucratic "Frequently Asked Questions" document of approximately 500 words, presented as if printed on official letterhead by the fictional "Bureau of Liberty Boundaries" in a municipality that has adopted an extreme literalist interpretation of the harm principle associated with classical liberal political philosophy. The FAQ should address concrete citizen inquiries about speech and expression: whether delivering an honest negative review of a neighbor's public art installation constitutes other-regarding harm; whether remaining silent when a colleague proposes a wasteful collective project counts as tacit endorsement that damages group autonomy; and whether stating a dietary preference in a shared space creates an impermissible "atmosphere of judgment." The voice must remain entirely straight-faced and administrative throughout—no winking, no explicit acknowledgment that the framework is absurd. To satisfy the competing objectives of precision and liberty, invent at least one pseudo-quantitative administrative metric—such as a "Social Friction Coefficient" or an "Autonomy Impact Score"—that parodies the attempt to operationalize philosophical distinctions into enforceable rules. The target of the satire should be the conceptual difficulty of separating self-regarding from other-regarding conduct, especially in domains of speech and social influence, rather than any named living individual. Do not reference real-world legal cases, request citations, or mention contemporary public figures. By the conclusion, the document should have pushed the logic of harm-based regulation far enough that a reader recognizes the genuine philosophical tension: that defining harm narrowly enough to preserve meaningful freedom risks leaving serious social injuries unaddressed, while defining it broadly enough to cover them risks colonizing the entire sphere of private judgment.
