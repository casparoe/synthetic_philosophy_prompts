I've been working through my own understanding of Nāgārjuna's four-cornered denial—the tetralemma—and what Madhyamaka emptiness actually commits me to, mostly through independent reasoning rather than formal study. Before I discuss this with others, I want a consistency audit of where I've actually landed. Here are my commitments:

(1) Emptiness means nothing possesses an intrinsic, self-standing nature (svabhāva); everything exists only through dependent origination, conditions, and conceptual designation.

(2) This applies fully to the self: there is no enduring, hidden "I" that owns my experiences. What I call "me" is a conventional label for an interdependent process.

(3) Despite (2), I think moral responsibility requires more than convention. For praise or blame to be genuinely real—not merely useful social tools—there must be an agent with sufficient continuity and causal efficacy to have genuinely done otherwise.

(4) The tetralemma is correct: for any claim that something is ultimately real, all four positions collapse—yes, no, both yes and no, neither yes nor no. Each fails under analysis.

(5) Yet in practical reasoning I constantly rely on a layered "both/and" framework. A situation can be genuinely unjust and genuinely unavoidable; holding both does not feel like contradiction but like recognizing two valid levels of description.

(6) Emptiness itself is empty. The doctrine does not point to a hidden metaphysical reality behind appearances; it is only a conventional remedy for the habit of reification.

(7) Despite (6), grasping emptiness feels like an irreversible cognitive shift—similar to finally seeing that a stick in water is not actually bent—rather than merely adopting one conventional viewpoint among others.

(8) Conventional truths have genuine practical authority: one can be genuinely wrong about ordinary matters (a bridge being unsafe, a promise being broken) in a way that is not reducible to "violating social rules."

Do these eight hang together, or am I smuggling in commitments that cancel each other out? Please identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve once you distinguish conventional from ultimate levels, and for each real tension specify the least costly revision and what it costs me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all equally. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most problems at once, and exactly what I would lose by making that change.
