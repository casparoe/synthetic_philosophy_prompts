I've been in this historiography seminar and we're stuck on presentism — like, can historians actually escape the standards of their own time? The anti-presentists keep saying we need to recover the past on its own terms, but then someone always asks: whose terms? Past societies weren't monolithic; they had dissidents and internal critics, so "the standards of the time" is kind of a fiction we project back. The presentists say all history is selective anyway and we should just own our values. We've been going in circles for months. My hunch is this isn't really about history at all but about whether historiography should work like anthropology (understanding) or like ethics (judging). But maybe there's a hidden empirical question about how much internal moral disagreement actually existed in, say, 18th-century slaveholding societies? I want a diagnosis of the stall — is it verbal, substantive, bedrock intuitions about the purpose of history? — and honestly, is there anything that could end it, or is the question just badly formed?
