I'm retired from materials chemistry—spent thirty years looking at reactive compounds—and lately I've been poking around philosophy of science and metaphysics for fun, mostly the dispostionalist literature (Martin, Johnston, Lewis, that crowd). There's a pattern I keep running into in both old lab notes and in philosophical examples, and I can't find the right word for it, so I'm wondering if I'm just missing the term.

Take a compound that's reactive with water under standard conditions. But whenever water is present at the right concentration, it instantly forms a stable oxide layer that blocks the reaction. The layer only exists because the water is there; remove the water and the layer dissolves, leaving the compound reactive again. So the trigger condition causally produces its own blocker, and the blocker disappears exactly when the trigger is gone. The reactive property never gets destroyed—it's just suspended in a loop. I've seen the same structure in catalytic systems: substrate present activates the enzyme but also locks it into an inhibitory conformation, so the capacity is there, the conditions are met, but nothing happens because the meeting of conditions generates a stable intermediate that holds everything in abeyance.

In philosophy I've read there's finks, where the trigger destroys the disposition, and masks, where some independant factor blocks it. This feels different—the blocker isn't upstream or external, it's causally downstream of the trigger itself. Is there an established concept for this kind of self-suspending disposition? If it's just a strange variety of masking, I want to know exactly where the boundary is—does the blocker have to be causally independant for masking to apply? And if it's genuinely a third category, what should we call it, and how does it change the way we think about a disposition being "realized" versus merely potential? I'm not after a bibliography, just the conceptual carving: what it is, what it isn't, and whether philosophers have discussed it without giving it a specific name.
