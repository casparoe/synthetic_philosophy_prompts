Consider the published case judgments of an intergenerational ethics board. First, it approved a climate-mitigation plan costing 1.5 percent of current GDP to prevent severe ecological damage projected for 120 years hence, stating that pure temporal distance does not diminish moral weight. Second, it rejected a research initiative that would consume 8 percent of present health budgets to develop a lifespan-extension therapy whose benefits would be realized only after 350 years; the board held that obligations weaken sharply once predictive reliability and social identity across generations break down. Third, it permitted a deep-geological waste-containment project safeguarding populations for 10,000 years, but solely because the present cost was below 0.05 percent of GDP and no present lives were risked; it added that had the present burden exceeded 1 percent, the distant protection would not have justified it. Fourth, it endorsed applying economic-growth adjustments to future welfare comparisons but explicitly prohibited any pure rate of time preference.

The board now faces a proposal to build a continental direct-air-capture network requiring a sustained 3.5 percent GDP commitment for forty years. The network offers negligible present co-benefits but would prevent catastrophic warming in 200 years with 95 percent confidence. Predict the board's verdict if it seeks consistency with its precedents, giving your best guess and the reasoning that favors it over the main alternative. Identify the closest analog among the four prior rulings, and articulate at least two competing principles—such as a smoothly decaying epistemic-horizon function versus a two-tier lexical threshold with economic adjustments—that could each accommodate the full record. For each principle, say what the board would be committing itself to by deciding either way on this proposal. Finally, specify the one further case—perhaps a program costing 0.2 percent of GDP now to avert comparable harm in 600 years—that would best distinguish the two principles.
