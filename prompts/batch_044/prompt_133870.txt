The debate about Kierkegaards Abraham and the teleological suspension of the ethical has been stuck for ages, at least in the philosophy of religion seminars Ive sat in. One side reads Abraham as genuinely suspending the universal ethical for a singular relation to God — the knight of faith is incomprehensible, silent, outside ethics altogether. The other side keeps trying to rescue Abraham as still working inside some hidden ethical calculus — maybe a higher moral law, maybe a tragic conflict where ethics demands both killing and not killing. Nobody moves an inch. Is this just a verbal dispute about what 'ethical' means? Or are they actually disagreeing about whether religious experience can even be articulated in shared language — so one side is doing moral philosophy and the other is doing phenomenology of the unspeakable? I have a hunch its the latter disguised as exegesis, but I want a proper diagnosis: are they answering different questions, is there a hidden empirical claim about how people actually experience faith, or is it bedrock intuition about whether God can command something that breaks morality? And what, if anything, would shift it — a new distinction, a staged case that forces agreement, or should we accept its an ill-posed question and move on? Keep it concrete with what each side actually says about Abrahams silence and the ram.
