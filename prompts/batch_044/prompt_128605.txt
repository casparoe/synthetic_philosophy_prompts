I am designing a module on introspective knowledge for an advanced philosophy seminar, and I need a precisely engineered scenario to illustrate a non-obvious failure mode of self-access—one that does not rely on self-deception, pathological dissociation, or science-fictional brain scanning. Please construct a one-paragraph thought experiment set in an ordinary skilled practice, such as a sommelier evaluating a wine or an improvising musician, in which the subject clearly knows, via introspection, that she is undergoing a particular experiential state, yet she systematically misidentifies—or is genuinely unable to identify—a specific relational feature of that state, for example whether its qualitative character is being caused by the present stimulus or reconstructed from memory, or whether its intensity is calibrated to an absolute scale rather than relative to an immediately preceding contrast.

The case should be built so that a higher-order thought account of introspection predicts that awareness of the state's existence and awareness of that relational feature should come together, since on that view introspective knowledge consists in a second-order representation that takes the first-order state as its object with its salient properties represented; by contrast, a first-order self-monitoring account should allow that the monitoring mechanism can register the occurrence of the state without registering the causal or comparative architecture that determines the feature. After describing the scenario, please state the verdict each account gives, explain which structural property of the monitoring mechanism generates the split, and describe what happens to the disagreement if that property is removed—specifically, whether the case collapses into a standard introspection-failure example or remains distinctive.

Finally, please include an explicit defense on behalf of the higher-order theorist: show how she might argue that the subject's failure is not an introspective deficit but a post-introspective judgment error, and identify exactly which element of the scenario she must deny to preserve her view. Do not include citations, author names with publication years, or requests for further reading; I want the reasoning to be self-contained and evaluable without external lookup.
