Write a joke that applies Nāgārjuna's tetralemma and Madhyamaka emptiness (śūnyatā) to a mundane modern scenario—such as deciding whether a smartphone navigation app is genuinely trustworthy. The punchline should turn specifically on the actual logical structure of the tetralemma: the simultaneous rejection of all four positions (it is so, it is not so, both, neither) with respect to inherent existence (svabhāva), rather than on generic tropes about Eastern philosophy being obscure or mystical. The joke should remain coherent to someone who recognizes that emptiness is not simple non-existence but the absence of independent, intrinsic nature in the app, the user, and the route alike.
