I'm presenting a paper next friday in our history-philosphy seminar on why the 'man of his time' defence fails for elite figures like Jefferson, Churchill, and Columbus - not because presentism is okay, but because they actively curated which moral criticisms counted as knowledge. Jefferson read abolitionist tracts and structured his plantation anyway; Churchill saw Indian National Congress petitions and dismissed them; Columbus had Las Casas writing right beside him. So they weren't passive products of uniform norms, they were gatekeepers of normative legitimacy. The audience is half social historians who think moral judgment corrupts the archive, half analytic philosophers who want clear criteri for blame. I need the objections in damage-order, phrased in their actual vocab - epistemic closure, structural determinism, that kind of thing - with what to concede up front, what I should hold, the reply for the worst one, where my case is weakest (probably treating elite literacy as universal?), and the one question I'm praying they skip, likely whether this standard condemns every factory owner too.
