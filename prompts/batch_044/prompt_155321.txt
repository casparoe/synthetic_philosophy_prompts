I hold a hybrid, somewhat unorthodox position on the Anthropocene as a formal historical-geological period. First, on chronology: I think the boundary should be set at 1610—the point of globally synchronous biogeochemical disruption following the Columbian Exchange—rather than at 1950 (the Great Acceleration) or circa 1784 (the Industrial Revolution), on the grounds that sustained, intercontinental collective human agency first generated an irreversible Earth-system signal then. Second, on authority: I do not think the International Commission on Stratigraphy should have sole ratification power; instead, I propose that declaration should rest with a Polycentric Geohistorical Assembly that includes stratigraphers, Earth-system modelers, and representatives of affected indigenous communities, because epochal designation is as much a normative recognition of historical responsibility as it is a technical stratigraphic judgment. Third, on ontology: I reject the single-date model in favor of a Multi-Phase Irreversibility Framework, where Phase I (c.1610–1950) is defined by slow, distributed ecological exchange with low radiogenic intensity, and Phase II (post-1950) by rapid system perturbation marked by plutonium and synthetic-marker signals, with each phase requiring distinct evidentiary thresholds. What would genuinely count against this package? I am not asking for the familiar objections—that 1610 lacks a clear local stratotype, or that adding non-geologists politicizes science. I want an honest map of its exposure: which results in paleoecology or isotope geochemistry, which historical counterfactuals about pre-Columbian connectivity, and which institutional precedents from other scientific boundary bodies would be decisive rather than merely awkward? What predictions does this view make about evidentiary standards for epochal boundaries that rival single-date or pure-stratigraphic accounts do not, and has the framework already been insulated into unfalsifiability—say, by allowing Phase I and Phase II to be conceptually separated so that undermining one never threatens the other? If a high-resolution core sample showed that the 1610 shift was regionally patchy rather than globally synchronous, would that be fatal, or could the multi-phase structure absorb it? Which potential defeaters would a committed defender be forced to concede actually count, versus dismissing them as category mistakes? Rank them from fatal to awkward, and assess whether the position is well insulated—if so, say whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind.
