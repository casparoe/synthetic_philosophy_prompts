Design a 90-minute essay question for an upper-level philosophy of well-being seminar that deploys Nozick’s experience machine not only against hedonism, but to test whether “contact with reality” operates as an independent prudential value or merely as an instrumental condition for achievement, authentic desire-satisfaction, and objective goods. The prompt should present both Nozick’s original machine and a symmetric “reverse machine” in which a subject actually accomplishes valuable goals but experiences them as meaningless suffering due to neural interference. Ask students whether this symmetry strengthens or weakens the case for objective-list theories, and whether a hybrid view that weights both experience and reality-contact can survive without collapsing into one of the pure views. Provide the exam question itself; a model answer of approximately 700 words that distinguishes the instrumental from the intrinsic value of reality-contact and addresses the hybrid-collapse objection; and a marking scheme totaling 20 points that awards specific marks for: (i) accurate reconstruction of Nozick’s argument (4 points), (ii) clear analysis of the reverse-machine symmetry and what it reveals about desire-satisfaction theories (5 points), (iii) evaluation of objective-list theories’ dependence on reality-contact as intrinsic versus instrumental (5 points), (iv) a coherent defense or critique of a hybrid position that avoids collapse (4 points), and (v) precision in distinguishing hedonism, desire-satisfaction, and objective-list accounts throughout (2 points). For each category, name the specific mistake that should cost marks. Do not ask for citations or bibliographies; the answer must stand on conceptual analysis alone.
