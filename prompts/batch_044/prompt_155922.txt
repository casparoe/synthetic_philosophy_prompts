Write a final-exam essay prompt for an upper-division philosophy of religion seminar on divine hiddenness. The prompt should be unanswerable by reciting standard formulations of the problem; to achieve this, embed a novel conceptual framing in which hiddenness is analyzed not as an absence of direct experiential disclosure but as a structured epistemic asymmetry—specifically, a situation in which rational agents have sufficient inferential resources to construct a coherent theistic interpretation of natural phenomena but lack any non-circular mechanism for confirming that interpretation over its naturalistic rivals. Ask students to assess whether this asymmetry constitutes a genuine hiddenness problem or merely reflects an appropriate distribution of cognitive labor between human reason and putative revelation. Include a model answer of about 700 words and a marking scheme that awards marks across four specific criteria: (1) precise characterization of the asymmetry, (2) evaluation of whether hiddenness requires direct disclosure or permits mediated justification, (3) analysis of the cognitive-labor analogy and its limits, and (4) identification of one self-undermining consequence if the hiddenness objection is pressed too strongly. Specify that a passing answer must address all four, while an excellent answer must also resolve the tension between mediated justification and the demand for non-circular confirmation without collapsing into fideism or skeptical theism. Do not include requests for citations, reading lists, or textual references; evaluation should depend entirely on conceptual argumentation.
