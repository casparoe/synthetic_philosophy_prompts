Write a short story set in an arbitration chamber where binding institutional commitments—promises, authorizations, and apologies—are formed solely through spoken performative utterances subject to a real-time physiological sincerity audit. The protagonist is a mediator who must certify or reject these utterances based on whether conventional conditions are satisfied. The central conflict arises when a speaker engineers a nested performative: delegating a promise to a subordinate whose biometric profile passes the audit, while the principal harbors an undisclosed mental reservation. The mediator discovers that certifying the utterance creates a binding obligation, but rejecting it requires evidence of conventional failure that the system cannot detect. Include a scene in which the speaker attempts to annul the obligation through a second-order performative—declaring the original promise insincerely intended—and show whether this retroactive utterance succeeds or collapses under the chamber's rules. Keep the philosophical tension embedded in procedural dialogue and institutional mechanics rather than expository commentary, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or explicit philosophical attributions.
