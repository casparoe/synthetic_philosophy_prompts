There's a pattern i've noticed in recent politics that i don't have a word for. A public figure makes a claim that is obviously false to anyone who checks -- say, that an election was stolen despite every audit and court case saying otherwise. What's weird is that supporters don't actually defend the claim's literal truth; they treat repeating it as a loyalty test, and opponents get pulled into endless fact-checking that never resolves anything. The falsehood isn't hidden; its transparency is almost definately the point. I know Hannah Arendt wrote about lying in politics, especially about destroying shared reality, but i'm not sure if she named this specific variant where the lie is open and everyone knows it, yet it still reorganizes political life. Is there an established concept for this? How is it different from ordinary propaganda or from just being cynical about politics? And please separate what's settled about Arendt's view from what's genuinely contested -- don't present one reading as the consensus.
