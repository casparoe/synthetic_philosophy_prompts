Having retired from experimental physics and recently begun studying philosophy of history, I find the maxim "history is written by the victors" repeatedly invoked—notably, in a seminar where it was used to dismiss an archival study of industrialization on the grounds that the sources were produced by colonial administrators. Rather than treating it as self-evident, I want to know exactly what claim it makes. Could you distinguish the precise theses hidden in that sentence: the mundane point about archive control, the systematic-bias thesis about testimony, and the stronger metaphysical or relativist claim that victors actually constitute the historical facts themselves? For each reading, assess whether it is defensible, whether it genuinely undermines the archival study mentioned, and where it risks collapsing into an untenable historical relativism. Finally, please say explicitly where your analysis is weakest and what kind of argument or historical counterexample would lead you to revise your verdict on which readings are viable.
