I've settled on this view about statues and street names: call it the 'active-harm only' rule. If a figure's legacy is still baked into living institutions—laws, economic patterns, whatever—that actually hurt people now, then keeping the statue up is complicit. If the damage occured purely in the past, tearing it down is basically presentist vandalism. So rename the street if the policy's still enforced; leave the statue alone if it's just old crimes. What would count against this? I want the defeaters sorted—fatal to merely awkward—including cases where historical and present harm are entangled, or where repealed policies still leave structural scars. Which ones would someone holding this view have to concede actually hurt their case, and which could they brush off by tweaking what counts as 'active'?
