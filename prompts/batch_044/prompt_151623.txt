I'm writing an essay for my metaphysics of time course comparing presentism and eternalism, and I want to use the film Arrival (2016) as a case study. The narrative centers on Louise Banks, who learns a heptapod language and begins to experience future events—especially her daughter's illness and death—with the immediacy of present memories. Please read the film through this debate: does Louise's non-linear cognition support eternalism, or can a presentist reinterpret these experiences as modal representations of possible states rather than direct acquaintance with real future entities? Focus on the scene in which Louise knowingly chooses to conceive the child despite the grief she has already "remembered." Does this choice presuppose that the future is fixed and fully actual (the block universe), or could a presentist who adopts a growing-block ontology make sense of her foreknowledge without granting ontological status to future events? I also want you to address whether the film conflates epistemic access to the future with ontological commitment to it, and whether the emotional weight of the ending genuinely requires eternalism or can be sustained if only the present is real. Do not treat the film as a mere illustration; show where the philosophical framework illuminates a specific scene or character decision, and where the narrative logic resists being mapped onto either theory. Finally, suggest whether the film contributes anything to the debate about temporal experience or the ethics of foreknowledge that philosophical argument alone tends to miss.
