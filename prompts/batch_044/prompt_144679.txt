Consider a split-brain patient with a fully sectioned corpus callosum but intact anterior and hippocampal commissures, allowing limbic and affective cross-hemispheric signaling while blocking cortical semantic integration. The left hemisphere verbally denies awareness of stimuli confined to the right visual field, yet the right hemisphere produces consistent, adaptive non-verbal behavioral adjustments—processing that seems to exceed classic blindsight, since affective valence is transferred across hemispheres. Give your credence that, in this specific configuration, there are two distinct phenomenal subjects rather than one subject with a dissociated access pathway. Decompose the reasoning into: (a) the prior that phenomenal consciousness can exist without linguistic reportability; (b) the likelihood that intact subcortical and commissural pathways are insufficient to unify a single phenomenal field across hemispheres; (c) the likelihood that the observed adaptive behavior reflects conscious experience rather than sophisticated unconscious processing; and (d) any other factor you judge essential, each with a rough probability and one line of justification. Then combine these into an overall credence, identify the term in which you are least confident, and specify what empirical finding or conceptual argument would most shift that number.
