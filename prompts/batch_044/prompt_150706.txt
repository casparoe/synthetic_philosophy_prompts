Hey — retired biologist here, only been doing philosophy for about a year so go easy on me. I got pulled into Strawson's reactive-attitudes idea (that moral responsability is really about being a fitting target for resentment or gratitude, not about metaphysical freedom). My study group built this argument and now I can't see the exit.

(1) If Strawson is right, being morally responsible just is being an apropriate target of reactive attitudes.
(2) But reactive attitudes are evolved emotional-circuit responses — basically calibrated social-punishment and reward mechanisms.
(3) If a response is just an evolved circuit firing, then calling it "appropriate" can only mean it's functioning well for group coordination, not that it's tracking some independent moral fact.
(4) So on Strawson's view, "X deserves blame" just means "the social-punishment circuit activated correctly toward X."
(5) By the same token, every reactive attitude collapses into a description of brain-state coordination.
(6) That means there's no real distinction between saying "she is responsible" and saying "my resentment-module fired" — they're identical.
(7) But then moral status depends entirely on accidental exposure: a solitary being that never triggers anyone's circuits has zero status, while a random scapegoat who happens to trigger mass resentment is fully responsible.
(8) That proves too much — it makes moral responsibility a pure accident of social wiring.

Which step should I reject, and what's the cost? I'd like to hold onto (1) and (3) if I can, since they feel like the honest naturalist reading. Also please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested here, and don't present one view as the consensus.
