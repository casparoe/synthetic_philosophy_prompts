A small interdisciplinary group—three philosophers and one cognitive scientist—has secured three years of funding to pursue conceptual, not empirical, work on the architecture of meaning in life, specifically at the boundary where absurdist challenges meet contemporary naturalistic theories. Rather than asking for a survey, I want a defended research agenda: identify the three open conceptual questions that should dominate their time, rank them, and explain the ordering.

To keep the framing precise but not standard, distinguish between local teleology—the directedness or internal point carried by particular projects, commitments, or relationships—and global teleology—the background framework that licenses the judgment that those local points genuinely matter. Absurdism is conventionally interpreted as undermining global teleology, yet naturalistic accounts (objective-list, subjective-fulfillment, narrative-integrity) often attempt to preserve local teleology without clarifying whether global teleology is dispensable or merely relocated.

With that distinction in view:

First, which unresolved question about the dependency relation between local and global teleology should be prioritized, on the grounds that settling it restructures the other two? Second, among the practical implications—how one ought to approach life-planning, long-term commitment, or the ethics of grief—which follow genuinely from that resolution, and which are conceptually intriguing but practically inert? Third, which major line of work should be explicitly deprioritized—perhaps reconciling absurdist denial with religious meaning-structures, or formalizing subjective-fulfillment conditions—and by what criterion: tractability for philosophers without laboratories, neglect in the current literature, or downstream dependence on the first question?

Defend the ranking with explicit criteria, including upstream dependence, tractability within the group's composition, and what kind of argument or conceptual clarification over the next three years would cause you to reorder the priorities. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the reasoning and the agenda, not references to specific texts.
