You are advising a small interdisciplinary working group—philosophers, behavioral economists, and formal modelers—with three years of funding to resolve conceptual tensions in bounded rationality and satisficing. The group intends to avoid well-trodden descriptive territory, such as classic aspiration-adaptation models in static choice sets and fast-and-frugal heuristics evaluated in fixed environments, and instead focus on open questions that determine whether satisficing can serve as a normative framework rather than merely a descriptive account in contexts where aspiration levels are endogenous, the option space expands faster than evaluation capacity, and the search process itself degrades the quality of available information.

Identify the three to four open conceptual questions or lines of work you regard as most important for this specific agenda, and rank them. For each entry, defend the placement by addressing: (i) its upstream status—specifically which downstream debates about normativity, tractability, or institutional design depend on resolving it first; (ii) its tractability for philosophers and formal modelers working without large-scale empirical datasets, including what form of progress (a formal result, a conceptual distinction, a targeted counterexample) would count as meaningful within a three-year window; (iii) what kind of evidence or argument over the next few years would cause you to reorder the list.

In your reasoning, treat the distinction between local satisficing—stopping rules applied within a fixed framing of options—and framing satisficing—revising the threshold for good enough as the framing itself shifts under search—as an unsettled diagnostic rather than a settled taxonomy. Use that tension to distinguish genuinely foundational problems from derivative adjustments. The deliverable should be a defended agenda specifying where limited effort should go and why, not a survey or a bibliography.
