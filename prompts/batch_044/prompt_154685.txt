Disjunctivists maintain that in veridical perception the subject stands in a direct relational connection to an external object, and that hallucination lacks this relation entirely—hence there is no shared mental kind—while representationalists argue that because hallucination and veridical perception can be subjectively indistinguishable, they must share a representational content that fully determines phenomenal character in both cases. Despite successive refinements, neither side has yielded ground.
