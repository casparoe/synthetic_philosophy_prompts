I have been developing an argument for my graduate seminar on the epistemology of artificial intelligence, and I would welcome rigorous critical comments. I hold the view that demands for worst-case guarantees in AI alignment commit what I call the "specification circularity" error: any formal characterization of a worst-case misalignment scenario for a sufficiently general learner already depends on a prior semantic framework that itself requires alignment. Consequently, worst-case alignment is not an unattainable engineering target but a conceptually incoherent one; average-case statistical guarantees, combined with what I term "ontological containment"—restricting the learner's representational capacities rather than its outputs—are not merely more practical but philosophically prior. I take this to imply that much of the current safety literature misapplies the logic of safety-critical systems (where worst-case analysis is meaningful because the state space is closed) to open-ended learning architectures.

What would count decisively against this position? I am interested in the full map of its exposure: specific formal results showing that worst-case bounds can be derived without presupposing an aligned meta-language; empirical cases—whether historical or hypothetical—where a system with only average-case guarantees produced catastrophic misalignment despite ontological containment; arguments that my distinction between representational restriction and output restriction collapses; and whether defenders of worst-case alignment themselves concede conditions under which their demands become circular. Please distinguish evidence that would genuinely falsify the view from evidence that is merely awkward for it, and assess honestly whether I have qualified the position into unfalsifiability—particularly through the notion of "ontological containment." If the view is indeed well insulated, is that a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind? I personally find formal impossibility results and concrete failure modes more persuasive than broad analogies to evolutionary biology, so I would particularly value arguments directed at those points.
