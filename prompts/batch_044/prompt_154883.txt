Yet a colleague replied that nested simulations might multiply suffering risks across layers, so the argument could actually intensify far-future concern. Please argue that the simulation argument genuinely dampens fanaticism about the far future, but do so by engaging specifically with this epistemic-dampening mechanism: show how ancestor-simulation credence weakens the forecasting pipeline without collapsing into blanket skepticism, and explain precisely where the nested-suffering counter-argument fails to restore the original urgency. I want a rigorous conceptual argument, not a literature review—no bibliographies or citation requests—since I'm testing whether the reasoning stands on its own terms.
