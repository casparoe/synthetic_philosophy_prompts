I serve on the ethics review panel of a public funding body that receives proposals for interventions spanning human, animal, synthetic, and future-affected populations. We have no institutional resolution of which moral-status framework is correct, yet we must approve, reject, or refer proposals within a single session. I want an operational procedure that turns the principle of moral-uncertainty resolution—treating competing frameworks as generating interval-valued moral weights rather than point probabilities, and requiring explicit reversal-threshold analysis—into a checklist my panel can run in about twenty minutes per proposal.

The procedure should specify: the ordered questions to ask; what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage; the exact conditions under which it returns ‘approve’, ‘reject’, or ‘refer for human deliberation’; and the points where the question is irreducibly one of values rather than calculable fact. It must handle cases where frameworks disagree not only on the magnitude of weight but on whether an entity falls within the domain of moral consideration at all. Include a small set of test conditions the panel can apply without resolving underlying meta-ethical disputes, and flag where the procedure is likely to misfire—particularly where interval weights overlap across all plausible frameworks but the ordering of interventions still reverses.

Apply the procedure to three proposals: (i) a badger-vaccination programme that reduces severe suffering but alters predator–prey dynamics and may shift suffering to other species; (ii) a research grant to develop artificial systems with ambiguous sentience markers, where one framework assigns full moral status contingent on architectural features and another denies status entirely pending behavioural proof; (iii) a long-term geological carbon-storage scheme that confers substantial benefits on distant future generations but imposes localised risk on present communities with weak representation.

In at least one worked case, the procedure should yield a recommendation that conflicts with the intuitive judgement of most panel members, and you should note exactly which step produces the divergence. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do—especially whether it can ever justify selecting an option that is dominated under every single framework considered, and where the absence of a fully specified value function makes an ‘avoid side effects’ criterion inoperable. Write in British English, keep the deliverable reusable, and mark every point where judgment cannot be eliminated.
