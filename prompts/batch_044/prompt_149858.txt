Me and my friend have been going in circles for months about why dueling actually died out. I keep saying it was mostly institutional — once states monopolized violence and you could sue for defamation, risking your neck stopped being rational. She insists it was a real moral revolution, that people slowly argued themselves into seeing dueling as wrong, and she points to 19th century abolitionist-style rhetoric against it. Then our other friend brings in Bernard Williams and says we're both fooling ourselves, that moral change is just interests in disguise, and we shouldn't pretend reasons drove it. Meanwhile I came across Kwame Appiah's point about honor worlds — that dueling didn't become immoral so much as it became embarrassing, like you don't understand the rules of the game anymore. So now we have three people talking past each other: one causal story, one normative story, one conceptual story about honor.

The frustrating part is we've been stuck for a year in our reading group using the same examples — slavery, dueling, foot-binding — and nobody moves an inch. I started wondering if the stall isn't about evidence at all but about running seperate debates under one name. Maybe one of us is asking what caused the decline, another is asking what justification could have persuaded people, and another is asking whether honor reduces to status competition. Is that right? Or is there a hidden empirical disagreement lurking underneath, like whether honor codes are actually reducible to economic signaling? And if the diagnosis is that we're answering different questions, is there any distinction, historical case, or conceptual tool that could actually break the deadlock? Or should we just accept that no progress is possible because we're using the same words for different jobs?

My friend also threw in this comparison with professional mourning — those hired weepers at funerals — saying that practice faded not because anyone argued it was immoral, but because social signaling shifted and people stopped seeing it as necessary. She thinks dueling is the same. Is that analogy fair, or is it smuggling in the conclusion that moral reasoning never matters? I want an honest diagnosis of the whole mess: what kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, substantive, intuitive bedrock — and what, if anything, would count as moving it forward?
