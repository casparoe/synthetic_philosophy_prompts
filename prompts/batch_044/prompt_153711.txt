You serve on the Institutional Review and Funding Board for Emerging Cognitive Architectures at a research university. A multidisciplinary team — call them the Resonance Lab — has submitted a Phase-II proposal and preliminary data report for a substrate they term a “careenium-style architecture.” In this system, low-level physical components (“simmballs”) interact to produce high-level symbolic patterns (“simms”). The team’s central philosophical claim is that they have demonstrated downward causation: altering a symbolic constraint at the simm level produces immediate, measurable physical rearrangement of the simmball substrate without any intermediate algorithmic mediator, an effect they call “direct symbolic gravitation.” They argue this proves symbols literally push the substrate around, resolving symbol-grounding concerns by showing high-level patterns possess irreducible causal potency. They request approval to scale the substrate to human-scale reasoning tasks and to publish the philosophical interpretation in a general-science venue.

Half the board treats this as an empirical result that, if reproducible, establishes a genuinely new causal regime requiring ethical oversight before scaling — particularly because downward causation might imply the substrate possesses interests or suffering capacities at the simm level that do not reduce to simmball states. The other half regards the claim as a category error: the observed rearrangement is fully explained by bottom-up dynamics and standard feedback loops, and framing it as “symbols pushing substrate” is philosophical overreach that risks institutional credibility and misallocates review resources.

Your task is to draft the board’s formal decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — to be circulated before Thursday’s vote. The memo must include a concrete recommendation with a specific time frame (e.g., a 90-day reproducibility audit, a conditional approval pending independent replication, or a rejection with a path for resubmission), the reasoning behind it, and an honest engagement with the strongest argument from the side you reject. It should also specify criteria the researchers must meet in any follow-up, such that they can actually respond to your conditions rather than treating them as rhetorical dismissal. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the reasoning must stand on conceptual and institutional grounds accessible to board members without external literature searches.
