I am a doctoral student in cognitive science, working with hierarchical predictive-processing frameworks—generative models, precision weighting, and meta-level hyperpriors. Take Hofstadter’s distinction in Gödel, Escher, Bach between frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages, in which meaning is distributed rather than localized: the frame supplies the interpretive rules, the outer message directs how those rules apply, and the inner message is the content interpreted within that activated frame. Restate this tri-level architecture as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary—treating the frame message as a hyperprior that fixes model structure and precision profiles for lower-level inference, the outer message as a top-down signal that selects or revises that hyperprior, and the inner message as sensory-level prediction error being explained away. Then report the residue: what translates cleanly into differences of hierarchical depth and precision allocation; what requires distortion (for instance, does the frame message collapse into just another high-level prior, losing its special status as a non-interpreted rule-giver?); and what has no expression at all without either smuggling in a homunculus or triggering an infinite regress of meta-models. In particular, address whether the idea that a frame can be executed rather than interpreted survives in a system where every hyperprior is itself open to prediction-error revision and potential precision collapse. If something essential is lost, is that a limitation of the predictive-processing vocabulary, or does it show that Hofstadter’s framing was less precise than it seemed?
