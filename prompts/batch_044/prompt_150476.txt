Write a dialogue between two grad students, J and M, arguing whether Hobbes genuinely believed political philosophy could reach geometery-level certainty. J should defend the standard synthetic-method reading: Hobbes started Leviathan with defintions of human motion and the commonwealth, treating geometry as the one science God actually gave us, so civil philosophy should copy that rigor. M should attack this using Hobbes's actual practice—his failed quadrature proofs, his view that figures are built from motion not found in nature, and the idea that if geometric objects are just human constructions then you can't derive sovereign authority with triangle-level necessity. Make them respond to each other's actual points; no strawmen. Write casually—typos are fine. At the end, the speaker should say how confident they are in the exchange and point out the one claim they're least sure about, maybe whether Hobbes's bad math really undermines his politics.
