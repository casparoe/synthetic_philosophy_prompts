I'm drafting a long-form piece for the ideas section of my publication on the architecture of moral responsibility, and before I commit the argument to print I want a rigorous consistency check. Over the past year of covering debates at the intersection of philosophy of mind and ethics, I've settled into a position that modifies Strawson's reactive-attitudes framework, but I'm no longer sure whether the modifications cohere with the core or quietly undermine it. Here is my profile.

First, I hold that moral responsibility is anchored not in a metaphysical doctrine of libertarian free will but in our reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—understood as irreducibly interpersonal practices. Second, I maintain that these attitudes are rationally warranted only when directed at agents who possess what I have been calling dialogical standing: the capacity to enter into reason-giving and reason-receiving exchanges as a peer, rather than merely to behave in causally predictable ways. Third, I take hard determinism to pose no threat to responsibility on this view, since reactive attitudes presuppose only this dialogical standing and not any contra-causal freedom. Fourth, where dialogical standing is permanently absent—as in severe, irreversible cognitive disability—I believe we ought to adopt the objective attitude, treating the individual as an object of care and management rather than as a responsible interlocutor. Fifth, I also claim that young children, who clearly lack full dialogical standing, are nevertheless legitimate targets of genuine resentment (not merely instrumental correction) when they intentionally harm others, because the attitude serves an irreducibly educative, reason-constitutive function in developing that very standing. Sixth, and this is where I worry I am overreaching, I think institutional actors—corporations and algorithmic governance systems—can be appropriate targets of moral indignation and blame even absent consciousness, provided they are structured with normative feedback loops that functionally analogize dialogical exchange.

Do these commitments hang together, or am I smuggling contradictions under the guise of nuance? Specifically: does my gatekeeping condition in the second point conflict with my willingness in the fifth to direct full-blown resentment at children who lack that very standing? Does my determinism-friendly stance in the third point sit comfortably with my institutional-blame claim in the sixth, or does it force me either to abandon that application or to retreat on determinism? Is there a hidden tension between my fourth and sixth commitments—treating severely disabled humans with the objective attitude while directing reactive attitudes at deterministic, non-conscious algorithms—given that both seem to fall short of peer-level dialogical capacity? I also suspect there may be only an apparent contradiction between my determinism claim and my objective-attitude claim, since determinism applies universally and one might think it either justifies the objective attitude everywhere or nowhere; I think that dissolves once the grounds are separated, but I want confirmation. Please audit the package: rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and at what philosophical cost, flag any hidden consistency I have missed, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems without collapsing the whole view.
