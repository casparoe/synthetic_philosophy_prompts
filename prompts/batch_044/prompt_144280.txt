Compose a Shakespearean sonnet that stages the dispute between Lewisian counterpart theory and Kripkean transworld identity through an original scenario: a treaty ratified in 1847 between two river-basin confederations, where in every nearby possible world the river's course shifts and the confederations are populated by different individuals lacking shared origins. In the octave, develop the intuition that the treaty survives as the very same agreement—parallel to the claim that original parties might persist across worlds—using rhyme pairs that evoke fixity and discovery. At the volta (line 9), shift to Lewis's counter-analysis: what endures is not numerically identical signatories but a system of counterpart relations among role-occupants satisfying the treaty's structural conditions. In the sestet, let the rhyme register separate terms of structural correspondence from terms of essential sameness, so that the formal pattern mirrors the metaphysical distinction between recurrence and identity. The closing couplet should state precisely what Lewis denies—that the treaty in world W is the original document—and what Kripke affirms, leaving the conflict explicitly open. The framing should be original enough to avoid standard textbook examples, yet rendered with sufficient precision that a specialist in modal metaphysics recognizes both positions immediately.
