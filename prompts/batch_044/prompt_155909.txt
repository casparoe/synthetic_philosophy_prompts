Compare Weber’s notion of elective affinity between the Calvinist predestinarian ethic and the capitalist spirit with the framework of normative scaffolding, according to which cultural values do not merely correlate with institutional forms but actively restructure economic incentives through interpretive feedback loops that render certain practices rationally intelligible. In which framework is culture genuinely explanatory rather than epiphenomenal? Address specifically whether Weber’s methodological pluralism—his insistence that values enter causation only through the mediation of individual meaning-orientation—leaves adequate space for culture to function as an independent variable, or whether normative scaffolding collapses into the kind of idealist monocausality that Weber sought to avoid. Furthermore, evaluate whether either framework can survive the distinction between reasons and causes: does explaining an economic transformation by reference to the values that made it meaningful to actors conflate justification with causal explanation, and if so, which of the two frameworks is more vulnerable to that objection?
