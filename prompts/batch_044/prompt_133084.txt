Here is my opening challenge. I define a property Q as follows: in any given world, Q belongs to a being if and only if that being is the sole being in that world that instantiates every positive property actually instantiated there. Q appears to be a perfection—a form of maximal completeness relative to actual instantiation—so it should be positive. By your axiom that being God-like is positive, any God-like being must possess Q. But now consider world W, where only one positive property P is actually instantiated, and it is instantiated by exactly one being. That being automatically instantiates all actually-instantiated positive properties, so it has Q, and thus is God-like. Now consider world W*, where infinitely many positive properties are instantiated by infinitely many different beings, so no single being instantiates them all; there, no being has Q, and hence—if God-likeness requires Q—no being is God-like. This makes God-likeness contingent on how properties happen to be distributed across worlds, which conflicts with your claim that being God-like is necessarily positive. Resolve the tension: either show precisely why Q fails to be genuinely positive without merely asserting it by fiat, or explain how God-likeness can remain necessary despite this distribution-sensitive dependency.
