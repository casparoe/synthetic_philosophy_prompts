I've been trying to figure out what I actually think makes a mental state genuinely conscious, going by my gut on cases rather than any official theory. First, blindsight: someone navigates around obstacles but sincerely says they see nothing. To me that's clearly not conscious seeing — there's information use but no "what it's like." Second, a musician in deep flow with no inner monologue, no explicit "I am playing this chord," just pure immersion. I still call that fully conscious, maybe even more so. Third, a split-brain patient whose left hand grabs a pencil and draws, while the speaking side confabulates a reason ("I was doodling"). The action feels unconsciously driven, even though a narrative shows up after.

Fourth — and here's where I keep wobbeling — imagine a neural chip that constantly generates faint meta-labels for every percept ("this is red," "this is an itch"), but they feel like background noise the user doesn't own. The person can report the labels but says they don't feel like the ones experiencing the color. Are those percepts conscious? I flip-flop. Fifth, a meditator describing "pure awareness without an observer," no subject-object split, yet vividly present, not like sleep. I want to say it's conscious, but if consciousness needs some self-reference this seems to lack it. Sixth, an amnesiac learning a motor task, feeling present each session but retaining zero memory across weeks. Each session feels conscious in the moment, but there's no continuity of the knowing self. Does that break anything?

My hunch is that consciousness needs integration or ownership, not just information processing or even a higher-order representation sitting nearby. The chip has representation without ownership; the meditator might have ownership without explicit representation. So is the principle something like "endorsed integration into a self-model"? Or do I have to drop the meditator case, or the chip case, to keep it simple? What's your best principle, what does it predict for someone who recieves these meta-labels and slowly starts treating them as their own over time, and which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon? Please say how confident you are, and single out the point you're least sure of — probably whether the meditator and chip cases are actually in tension or just two descriptions of the same failure.
