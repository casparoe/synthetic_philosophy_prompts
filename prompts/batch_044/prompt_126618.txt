ok so i'm putting together my philosophical profile for our death-and-value seminar next week and i want to make sure i'm not being incoherent before i present. here's roughly where i've landed, and i need to know if the package hangs together or if i'm smuggling contradictions.

(1) i buy the epicurean core: death is nothing to us in the direct sense—once you're anihilated there's no subject left to suffer, so being dead can't be intrinsically bad.
(2) but i also think the deprivation account gets something important right: if i die young, that's a serious misfortune because it truncates the narrative arc of my life and frustrates diachronic interests i actually have now, not just because it removes future moments of pleasure.
(3) i reject lucretian symmetry—my pre-natal non-existence isn't a misfortune, but my post-mortem non-existence can be—because rational concern is asymetrically future-directed; only the future is open in a way my present self can stake claims on.
(4) i hold a strict no-subject-no-harm principle generally: posthumous harm to reputation or unfinished projects isn't harm to me, it's either harm to others or to no one at all.
(5) i think fear of death is rational only derivatively—fear of the dying process, fear of leaving dependants behind, fear of losing the open future—not fear of the state of being dead, since there's literally no being dead to fear.
(6) i also think this only applies to beings with a narrative self and future-directed interests; animals without a concept of their own future lives can't be harmed by death in the deprivation sense, even if they suffer while dying.

my worry is that (1) and (2) are pulling in opposite directions—if there's no me after death, who exactly is the subject of the deprivation?—and maybe (4) makes (2) impossible because if harm requires a present subject, then truncating a future that never happens can't harm anyone either. but then (3) and (5) seem okay together. also i wonder if there's an apparent tension between (5) and (2) that actually dissolves once you separate objective misfortune from rational fear. can you audit this? rank the tensions by severity, say for each what has to give and what the cheapest fix is, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess. please don't just list everything as equally bad—i need to know what's fatal and what's cosmetic, and whether i'm helping myself to something in (3) that (1) or (4) should rule out.
