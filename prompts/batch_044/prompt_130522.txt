Task: Construct a thought experiment meeting the specifications below.

Domain and framing: Mind uploading and gradual replacement of a human brain with synthetic neuronal equivalents, set in a realistic clinical context—specifically, an experimental treatment protocol for a progressive neurodegenerative disorder. Do not use teleportation, duplication, demons, perfect predictors, or exotic physics.

Scenario specifications (deliver in one paragraph):
- Over five years, a patient’s biological neurons are replaced one by one with synthetic equivalents that reproduce every functional property (firing patterns, synaptic weights, neurotransmitter dynamics) but are composed of non-biological substrate and linked via an artificial scaffolding rather than continuous biological tissue.
- The replacement proceeds in reverse hierarchical order: it begins with high-level prefrontal and associative cortices and moves downward, reaching the brainstem and core autonomic nuclei only in the final year.
- At the end of year three—when roughly 60% of neurons (mostly cortical) are synthetic—the patient is placed in a 72-hour medically induced coma with complete cessation of all measurable neural activity. Upon awakening, she reports no subjective gap, retains full autobiographical memory, and displays an unchanged personality.
- By year five, 100% replacement is complete. The patient is awake, reports continuous subjective experience with no felt interruption across the entire process, and behaves indistinguishably from her pre-treatment self.

Theoretical specifications:
- Engineer the case so that a psychological-continuity theorist—who holds that personal identity survives appropriate causal and psychological connections across substrate change and permits brief interruptions—must conclude that the original patient survives at year five.
- Engineer the case so that a biological-continuity (animalist) theorist—who holds that a person is essentially a living human organism and that identity requires the persistence of the same living biological brain—must conclude that the original patient was destroyed, and specify whether this destruction occurs at the point of 100% replacement, during the 72-hour coma, or at another point.
- The disagreement must fall out of the theories themselves, not be stipulated by fiat.
