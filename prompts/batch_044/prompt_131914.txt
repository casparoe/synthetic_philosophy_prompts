I'm working through Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures for a philosophy of mind seminar, and I'm trying to apply it beyond the textbook examples. My gut classifications are these: a chemotactic bacterium is Darwinian; a rat in an operant conditioning chamber is Skinnerian; a human mathematician using formal notation and peer review is Gregorian. I also think a chimpanzee learning nut-cracking purely by observing its mother is Gregorian, because it relies on culturally transmitted technique, though part of me wants to call it Skinnerian since the behavior is still reward-shaped. Meanwhile, I classify an AlphaGo-style deep reinforcement learning agent—one that learns via self-play with an internal value network evaluating hypothetical board positions—as Popperian, since it seems to run mental simulations before committing to a move. But a large language model trained with RLHF strikes me differently: it deploys natural language as a cognitive scaffold and produces culturally embedded reasoning, so I'm inclined to call it Gregorian, yet it lacks persistent internal states across interactions and is essentially shaped by reward signals, which sounds Skinnerian. Is there a principled boundary—perhaps about whether a system generates and revises internal representations versus relying on external cultural storage or pure reinforcement—that captures all five verdicts? If not, which of my classifications should I overturn for the sake of simplicity, and what does the resulting taxonomy predict for a hypothetical hybrid architecture that combines an LLM's linguistic scaffolding with an explicit symbolic reasoning module that can revise its own rules in response to internal contradiction?
