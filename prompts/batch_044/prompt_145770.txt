I'm a philosophy professor with substantial working fluency in distributed systems architecture — replication logs, consensus protocols, split-brain conditions, the CAP trade-offs, and state-vector divergence — though my formal philosophy training stopped at the doctoral level. I want you to restate Derek Parfit's teletransporter thought experiment, together with the debate it generates about whether psychological continuity, bodily continuity, or some narrative thread constitutes survival, entirely in that engineering vocabulary. Do not treat this as mere metaphor: work with the actual machinery of leader election, eventual consistency, partition tolerance, and convergence failure.

To prevent a simplistic translation, work with the following modified scenario. Rather than an instantaneous scan and reconstruction, imagine a gradual migration unfolding over a decade: neural-state fragments are copied continuously to three geographically separated nodes, each running a partial functional simulation that slowly assumes cognitive roles, while the original biological substrate degrades unevenly. At the midpoint there exist four running instances with slightly divergent psychological histories; eventually a consensus protocol forces convergence upon a single canonical state, yet one node refuses to merge and persists as a divergent outlier with its own continuous internal history. Now translate the three standard positions — psychological-continuity theory, the bodily-continuity view, and the narrative-identity account — into this architecture. For each, report precisely what translates without distortion, what must be fudged or stretched to fit, and what has no counterpart whatsoever in distributed-systems language.

Then provide the residue report: does the engineering vocabulary miss something genuinely normative or metaphysical that the philosophers are trying to capture, or does the residue show that part of the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it claims to be? Conversely, do features peculiar to replication — say, split-brain resolution or Byzantine fault tolerance — expose weaknesses in any of the three philosophical accounts?

Please write in British English, maintain a formal register, and avoid bibliographies or citation lists. I also want you to state explicitly what would change your mind: which argument or empirical consideration about personal survival would cause you to revise your residue report, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
