As a journalist who covers emerging science, I need a reusable editorial procedure for deciding whether a researcher's claim is assertible in a published story, grounded in the philosophy of assertion — specifically the debate over whether assertion requires knowledge, mere true belief, justified belief, or some intermediate norm such as reasonable belief. The deliverable should be an ordered checklist that an editor can run in minutes: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure stops with a clear rejection, and where it must defer to human judgment because the competing norms themselves diverge. The procedure must distinguish empirical claims, interpretive claims, and predictive claims; it must handle expert disagreement within a field and technically true assertions that mislead through framing; and it must state explicit stopping rules. Then apply the procedure to three cases: a climate scientist offering a specific regional temperature projection for 2050; a psychologist asserting a causal mechanism for a behavioral effect on the basis of a single pre-registered study; and a historian arguing that a recently discovered archival document requires revising a standard narrative. Be sure to include one case in which the procedure produces an answer a science editor would likely resist, and clearly flag every point at which judgment cannot be eliminated.
