I am a novelist drafting a scene in which an epistemologist character must revise her beliefs after receiving ambiguous testimony that seems to undermine not merely her prior probabilities, but the very categories through which she organized them. To make her reasoning philosophically credible rather than merely atmospheric, I need you to write an essay addressing the following controversy in Jeffrey conditionalization and uncertain evidence.

The standard Jeffrey rule applies when an observer receives uncertain evidence that shifts her direct probabilities over a fixed partition of possibilities, leaving her conditional probabilities within each cell unchanged. But consider a harder case—call it structural uncertainty—where the evidence is ambiguous about whether the partition itself remains appropriate: perhaps the categories she used to classify outcomes depend on theoretical commitments that the new evidence calls into question. One view holds that Jeffrey conditionalization is fundamentally a rigidity constraint on intra-partitional reasoning and therefore cannot accommodate revision of the partition without an entirely separate mechanism; the opposing view holds that any such revision can be represented as movement within a richer, hierarchical space of partitions, allowing Jeffrey's rule to apply recursively without essential amendment.

Weigh both positions carefully, but reach a definite conclusion: defend either that Jeffrey's original formulation can genuinely accommodate structural uncertainty through hierarchical expansion, or that it is essentially limited to fixed-partition cases and requires supplementation. Please separate what is widely settled—such as the formal structure of Jeffrey's update rule and its distinction from standard Bayesian conditioning on precise evidence—from what is genuinely contested, especially concerning whether observational autonomy extends to the choice of partition itself. Do not present a disputed interpretation as the consensus view, and do not provide bibliographies or citation lists; the scene requires conceptual clarity for a reader who will not check sources, not scholarly apparatus.
