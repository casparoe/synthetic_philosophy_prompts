I'm putting together my position for a philosophy-of-religion discussion and want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I've landed on Molinism and middle knowledge:

(a) God possesses middle knowledge: there are true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (CCFs) that are logically prior to the creative decree and not grounded in God's will.
(b) The grounding objection fails because these counterfactuals are grounded in the individual essences of possible creatures, present in the divine intellect through simple intelligence prior to any volitional act.
(c) Molinism genuinely preserves libertarian freedom because God's selection of a feasible world does not causally determine the free acts within it; causation operates intra-world, not across the world-selection boundary.
(d) Divine simplicity holds: there is no real distinction between God's knowledge of CCFs, God's will to actualize a particular world, and God's essence.
(e) Because CCFs constrain feasibility, there exist worlds God cannot actualize; this is not a limitation on omnipotence, since omnipotence extends only to what is logically and metaphysically possible, and the constraint is fixed by the counterfactual structure itself.
(f) I also hold a strict libertarian condition: for an act to be free, the agent must retain the power to do otherwise even holding fixed every feature of the actual world, including God's specific world-selection — freedom requires modal robustness inside the selected world, not merely variation across worlds.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently in conflict, and for each real tension what is the least costly revision? Rank the conflicts by severity rather than treating them as uniform. I do not know which commitment I would surrender if forced, so treat all six as initially on the table.
