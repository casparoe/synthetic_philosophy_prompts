Consider the standard taxonomy in philosophy of emotion: judgment theories in the line of Nussbaum or Solomon, perceptual or quasi-perceptual accounts associated with Prinz or Deonna and Teroni, and pure bodily-feeling or interoceptive theories in the tradition of James and Damasio. Now inject the following more specific, hybrid framing. Treat moral emotions—indignation, guilt, and shame—not as judgments about value, nor as perceptions of bodily change, nor as bare interoceptive feelings, but as hierarchical predictive-interoceptive updates that encode normative salience weights. On this view, the affective state is an error-correction signal within a generative model of social interaction; it restructures the agent’s predictions about social affordances, and the “normative” content is not a separable cognitive appraisal but is carried in the very tuning of the predictive dynamics through social learning.

State your actual credence, as a precise number between 0 and 1, that this predictive-normative hybrid is closer to the truth about moral emotions than any pure judgmental, perceptual, or feeling theory taken alone. Do not give me the number bare. Decompose it into at least four terms: (i) the prior that hierarchical predictive processing supplies the correct general framework for affective phenomena at all; (ii) the probability that normative content is genuinely embedded in interoceptive-predictive dynamics rather than being a post-hoc judgment appended to feeling; (iii) the probability that moral emotions specifically involve this normative encoding in a structurally distinctive way, separating them from non-moral affect; and (iv) the probability that the three pure theories collectively fail to accommodate the relevant phenomenological and functional data. For each term, provide a rough numerical probability and a single line of justification. Then explain how you combine these terms—whether multiplicatively, as weighted averages, or by some other rule—to reach your overall figure. Finally, identify which term you are least confident in, specify what kind of empirical or conceptual finding would most shift that term, and say whether your final number is more sensitive to movement in that term or to your choice of combination rule.
