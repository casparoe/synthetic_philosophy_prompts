As a novelist drafting a philosophical satire for my current manuscript, I require a deadpan document that a disillusioned protagonist might discover in the waiting room of a fictional consultancy. Write a polished, approximately 600-word FAQ brochure—formatted as numbered questions and answers in the voice of an administrative prospectus—for the "Web-of-Belief Ethics Clinic," a consultancy that applies W.V.O. Quine's epistemological holism to personal moral revision. The clinic should treat ethical commitments as ordinary nodes within a holistic web of belief, revisable for overall coherence with empirical science and lacking any special epistemic privilege; clients pay to have contradictory values pruned, re-weighted, or relocated to the periphery based on criteria of simplicity and predictive utility. The satire must exaggerate real features of Quine's position—specifically his rejection of any analytic-synthetic distinction that would shelter moral claims, his naturalist view that ethics is continuous with science, his claim that moral values tend to occupy peripheral rather than central positions in the web, and his willingness to revise any belief if adjustments elsewhere preserve coherence—rather than invent false caricatures. Include at least three named service descriptions (for instance, "Peripheral Value Pruning," "Analytic-Synthetic Divorce Mediation," and "Empirical Overhaul Packages") that map precisely onto these Quinean ideas. The tone should remain sustained, deadpan, and administratively sincere: the brochure must earnestly promote its services so that the absurdity emerges from the content itself, not from winking asides. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, page references, or attributions to specific published texts. Maintain internal consistency: the clinic's representatives should never slip into treating moral values as objectively binding or categorically distinct from empirical hypotheses. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with Quine yet richly legible to someone who recognizes the actual structure of his epistemology.
