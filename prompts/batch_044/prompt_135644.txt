I'm on this neighborhood planning comittee and we're deadlocked over a proposed developement near the creek. The enviro people have solid data about runoff and habitat loss, the housing folks have waiting lists and rent burden stats, and the small business owners say the traffic plan will kill foot traffic. Each side is convinced the others are either lying or missing the big picture. Last week I proposed that maybe all three descriptions are partly right — like, the development really does destroy habitat AND really does solve a housing crisis AND really does hurt local shops — and that the mistake is thinking we have to crown one narrative as "the real story" before we vote. The chair laughed and said that's just conflict avoidance with a philosphy degree. I kind of feel like there's a real idea there about truth being multi-sided, but I also worry I'm just saying that because I hate confrontation and want everyone to feel heard. To make it worse, one member pointed out that if "it harms the creek" and "it doesn't harm the creek" are both true in some sense, then we have a straight contradiction, not wisdom. So I'm definately stuck: how do I tell whether my belief that "no single viewpoint captures the whole thing" is an actual philosophical insight about the situation, versus just my personal preference for harmony? And if it IS an insight, does it mean we shouldn't take a yes/no vote at all, or does it change what kind of vote we should take — maybe one that lists conditions instead of picking a winner?
