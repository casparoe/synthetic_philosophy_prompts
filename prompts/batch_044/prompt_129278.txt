Compare the claim that DNA functions as a literal code—where nucleotide triplets denote amino acids only via the arbitrary, interpreter-dependent mapping performed by ribosomes and tRNA—with the claim that DNA is merely a causal template whose sequence correlates with protein structure through direct chemical affinity, without any semantic mediation. Specifically, discuss whether the presence of translation machinery makes DNA genuinely linguistic, or whether that machinery is just another biochemical link in a continuous causal chain. Finally, assess whether the near-universality of the genetic mapping, together with its documented exceptions (e.g., variant mitochondrial codons), supports the code metaphor, undermines it, or is neutral between these two accounts.
